Trump says US going 'low key' on Iran, shifts focus to economic pressure

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says the US is 'low keying it' on Iran, ruling out new strikes for now
  • Economic pressure takes precedence over military operations in current strategy
  • Reports suggest Trump is ready to end the war without a nuclear deal, but Tehran's demands complicate exit

President Donald Trump has signalled a sharp shift in US strategy towards Iran, saying Washington is now 'low keying it' and will lean on economic pressure rather than fresh military strikes. The remarks, reported by multiple outlets, come amid growing questions over the endgame of the conflict.

Speaking to reporters, Trump described the current approach as one of 'semi-negotiating' with Tehran, a phrase that has raised eyebrows given the scale of hostilities in recent months. The comments suggest a White House keen to dial down rhetoric even as the situation on the ground remains fluid.

A pivot from strikes to sanctions

The president's latest statement marks a notable departure from earlier posturing, when military action appeared to be the preferred lever. Now, the emphasis is on squeezing Iran economically, a tactic Trump has long favoured in foreign policy.

'We are low keying it,' Trump said, according to reports. The remark was interpreted as a direct signal that the US does not intend to escalate with new strikes, at least for now. Instead, the administration appears to be banking on economic pain to force a change in Tehran's calculus.

This pivot comes as reports suggest Trump is ready to end the war even without a comprehensive nuclear deal. According to a Times of India report, Tehran's demands are complicating any potential exit, leaving the administration in a bind.

Tehran's demands and the exit question

While the US may be ready to wind down, Iran is not making it easy. Reports indicate that Tehran is pressing for conditions that Washington finds hard to accept, though specifics remain unclear. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact nature of these demands.

Al Jazeera's coverage noted that Trump said the US is 'only semi-negotiating' with Tehran, a phrase that hints at talks happening at some level, but without full commitment. This ambiguity is typical of a president who often prefers to keep options open.

An NDTV report went further, suggesting that Trump started the war over nuclear issues but might be willing to give it up over the Strait of Hormuz. The strategic waterway, through which a significant share of global oil passes, remains a flashpoint in any potential deal.

What 'low key' means on the ground

For now, the practical implication of 'low keying it' is that US forces are unlikely to launch a new round of strikes. Instead, the focus will be on tightening sanctions and using economic tools to pressure Iran into concessions.

This approach carries risks. Economic pressure takes time to bite, and Tehran has shown resilience in the past. Meanwhile, the situation in the region remains volatile, with no clear timeline for a ceasefire or a formal end to hostilities.

The president's comments have not been met with uniform enthusiasm. Critics argue that a 'low key' approach could be read as weakness, while supporters see it as a pragmatic step towards de-escalation. The coming weeks will test whether this shift yields results.

What to watch next

The immediate question is whether Tehran will respond to the economic pressure with flexibility or further intransigence. All eyes are on the Strait of Hormuz and any diplomatic backchannels that may emerge in the coming days.

No formal peace talks have been announced, but the president's language suggests a window for negotiation is open. Whether it widens or closes will depend on both sides' willingness to move beyond their stated positions.
