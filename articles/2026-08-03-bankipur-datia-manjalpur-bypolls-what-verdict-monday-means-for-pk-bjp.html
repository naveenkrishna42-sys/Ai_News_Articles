Bankipur, Datia, Manjalpur bypolls: What verdict Monday means for PK, BJP

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bypolls in Bankipur (Bihar), Datia (MP), Manjalpur (Gujarat) see results on Monday
  • Prashant Kishor's Jan Suraaj Party faces first electoral test in Bankipur
  • Allegations of voter intimidation and police bias mark Bankipur campaign
  • BJP's Ashutosh Tiwari contests Datia; Manjalpur is a Congress stronghold test

Three assembly constituencies across three states go to counting on Monday, but the verdict will be watched far beyond their boundaries. Bankipur in Bihar, Datia in Madhya Pradesh and Manjalpur in Gujarat are holding bypolls that carry political symbolism disproportionate to their size.

The spotlight, however, is firmly on Bankipur, where poll strategist-turned-politician Prashant Kishor is contesting his first election. Kishor, who founded the Jan Suraaj Party (JSP) after years of backroom electoral consulting, has turned this urban Patna seat into a referendum on his political future.

A strategist's first electoral test

Kishor has spent months building a grassroots campaign in Bankipur, walking through lanes and holding small meetings — a marked departure from the data-driven, media-heavy campaigns he once designed for others. The JSP has positioned itself as an alternative to both the ruling NDA and the opposition Mahagathbandhan in Bihar.

But the campaign has not been smooth. Kishor has filed complaints alleging voter intimidation and police bias, specifically accusing the Patna Senior Superintendent of Police of arresting local JSP leaders. He has publicly demanded action against the SSP, claiming that police powers are being misused to favour rival candidates.

The Election Commission has not yet commented on the specifics of Kishor's allegations. Officials have confirmed receipt of the complaints but have not announced any inquiry. The opposition Rashtriya Janata Dal and the ruling Janata Dal (United) have both dismissed the charges as campaign rhetoric.

Datia: A BJP test in Bundelkhand

In Madhya Pradesh, the Datia seat in the Bundelkhand region is witnessing a direct contest between the ruling BJP and the Congress. The bypoll was necessitated after the sitting BJP MLA's election was set aside by a court, a rare judicial intervention that has given the Congress a talking point.

The BJP has fielded Ashutosh Tiwari, whose candidacy has energised the party's local cadre. Tiwari belongs to the dominant Brahmin community in the constituency, a factor that could prove decisive in a seat where caste arithmetic often trumps development discourse.

For the Congress, Datia represents an opportunity to break a losing streak in the state. The party has not won a significant electoral contest in Madhya Pradesh since its 2018 assembly victory, and a win here would provide a morale boost ahead of the next state elections.

Manjalpur: Congress's Gujarat test

In Gujarat's Manjalpur constituency, the bypoll is being watched as a bellwether for the Congress's revival prospects in a state where it has been reduced to a fringe presence. The seat, located in Vadodara, was held by the Congress before the vacancy arose.

The BJP is keen to reclaim Manjalpur, which has historically been a swing seat. The party has deployed senior leaders and central ministers for campaigning, signalling that it treats this bypoll as more than a routine electoral exercise.

The Aam Aadmi Party, which has been trying to expand in Gujarat, is also in the fray. While AAP is not expected to win, its vote share could cut into the Congress's base and alter the final arithmetic.

What the results will signal

Political analysts say the three bypolls, though localised, will be read as indicators of larger trends. In Bihar, a strong showing by the JSP could reshape the opposition space ahead of the 2027 assembly elections. In Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat, the results will test whether anti-incumbency is building against the BJP in its strongholds.

For Kishor personally, the stakes are existential. A defeat in Bankipur would raise questions about his ability to transition from strategist to mass leader. A victory, even a close second, would give his fledgling party a foothold and a narrative.

The counting of votes will begin at 8 am on Monday. District administrations in all three constituencies have made arrangements for security and electronic voting machine verification.

Exit polls have been banned in India, so the first definitive indication of the trend will come only when postal ballots are counted. Political parties have set up war rooms and deployed counting agents to monitor every round.

What to watch next

The immediate focus will be on the margin of victory or defeat in Bankipur. If Kishor loses by a narrow margin, he is likely to claim moral victory and continue his campaign. If he wins, the JSP becomes a serious player in Bihar politics overnight.

In Datia and Manjalpur, the BJP's ability to retain or regain seats will be scrutinised as a measure of its organisational strength outside its traditional belts. The Congress, meanwhile, will look for any sign of a turnaround to present as evidence of revival.

Monday's numbers will set the tone for the next round of electoral battles — including the high-stakes assembly polls due in several states over the coming year.
