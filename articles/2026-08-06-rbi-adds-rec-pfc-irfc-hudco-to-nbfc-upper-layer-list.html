RBI adds REC, PFC, IRFC, HUDCO to NBFC upper-layer list

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI released the updated NBFC upper-layer list for 2026-27, adding REC, PFC, IRFC, and HUDCO.
  • Tata Sons continues to feature in the list, with its deregistration plea still under review.
  • The classification brings stricter regulatory norms, including listing requirements for upper-layer NBFCs.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Thursday released its updated list of non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) in the upper layer, adding four new names to the regulatory spotlight. Power sector lenders REC, Power Finance Corporation (PFC), IRFC, and HUDCO have entered the list for 2026-27, according to the central bank's notification.

The move underscores the RBI's continued efforts to tighten oversight on large NBFCs, which are now subject to a more stringent regulatory framework. The upper-layer classification requires these entities to adhere to enhanced governance standards and, crucially, to list on stock exchanges within a specified timeline.

Who made the cut and who stayed

The four new entrants join an existing cohort of major NBFCs that the RBI deems systemically important. Their inclusion reflects their asset size and perceived risk to the financial system, given their significant lending portfolios in infrastructure and housing finance.

Notably, Tata Sons, the holding company of the Tata Group, continues to feature in the list for 2026-27. The conglomerate's deregistration plea remains under review by the central bank, according to reports. This ongoing classification has kept the question of Tata Sons' potential stock market listing alive, as the RBI's rules mandate that upper-layer NBFCs must be listed.

What the RBI's rule means

The RBI's framework for NBFCs, introduced in 2021, categorises entities into four layers—base, middle, upper, and top—based on their size, activity, and risk. The upper layer captures NBFCs with assets of Rs 1 lakh crore or more, or those that the RBI considers systemically significant.

For companies in this bracket, the listing requirement is non-negotiable. The central bank has set a deadline for these firms to get their shares listed, a move aimed at enhancing transparency and market discipline. For Tata Sons, this has been a contentious point, as the private holding company has historically resisted public listing.

Tata Sons under pressure

Tata Sons' presence on the list has been a subject of intense speculation in financial circles. The company had sought deregistration from the upper layer, arguing that its status as a core investment company should exempt it from the listing mandate. However, the RBI's latest list shows that no such relief has been granted yet.

The central bank's decision to keep Tata Sons in the list suggests that the review of its plea is still ongoing. Until a resolution is reached, the conglomerate faces the prospect of mandatory listing, which could reshape its ownership structure and governance.

Industry impact and next steps

The addition of REC, PFC, IRFC, and HUDCO to the upper layer will likely accelerate their compliance efforts. All four are already listed, so the immediate pressure is on meeting the RBI's enhanced norms on capital adequacy, risk management, and board composition.

For the broader NBFC sector, the RBI's list serves as a clear signal of its regulatory intent. Firms that grow beyond the threshold can expect closer scrutiny. Analysts will be watching for any further additions or removals in the coming quarters, as well as the RBI's final word on Tata Sons' deregistration plea.

The central bank is expected to review the list periodically, with the next update likely to reflect any changes in the financial health or structure of these entities.
