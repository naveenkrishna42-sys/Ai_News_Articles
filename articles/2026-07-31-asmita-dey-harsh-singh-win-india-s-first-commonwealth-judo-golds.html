Asmita Dey, Harsh Singh win India's first Commonwealth judo golds

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Asmita Dey and Harsh Singh became India's first judo gold medallists at the Commonwealth Games.
  • Yamini Mourya added a silver medal in her category.
  • Harsh defeated a vastly experienced Australian judoka in the men's -60kg final.
  • Asmita, daughter of a bicycle repairman, overcame tough competition to win her gold.

A historic double on the mat

India's judo contingent at the Commonwealth Games etched its name in the record books on Friday. Asmita Dey and Harsh Singh both claimed gold medals, marking the country's first-ever victories in the sport at these Games. The feat was further sweetened by Yamini Mourya, who added a silver to the tally.

The three medals transformed a modest campaign into a landmark one. For a nation that had never reached the top step of the judo podium at the Commonwealth Games, the double gold was a seismic shift.

Harsh Singh's upset over a veteran

In the men's -60kg final, Harsh Singh faced a vastly experienced Australian judoka. The bout was a test of nerve as much as skill. The Indian, younger and less decorated on the international stage, refused to be overawed.

His tactics were sharp, his throws crisp. When the referee's hand went up, Harsh had secured a historic win. The Australian, a seasoned campaigner, was left to rue a rare lapse against a rising star.

For Harsh, the victory was a culmination of years of relentless training. His journey, like many Indian athletes from small towns, was built on sacrifice and a single-minded focus.

Asmita Dey: A triumph against the odds

Asmita Dey's story adds a layer of poignancy to the day's success. The daughter of a bicycle repairman, she has often spoken about her family's modest means. Yet, on the mat, she displayed a wealth of composure.

Her final was a gruelling contest, requiring every ounce of her technical ability. She emerged victorious, her gold medal a testament to her perseverance. The image of a young woman from a humble background standing atop the podium will resonate far beyond the arena.

Her achievement is not just about the medal. It is about the barriers she broke, both on the mat and in the minds of those who doubted a girl from her circumstances could reach such heights.

Yamini Mourya's silver lining

While the golds grabbed the headlines, Yamini Mourya's silver was no less significant. She fought her way through a tough draw before falling in the final. Her medal adds depth to India's strongest-ever judo showing at the Commonwealth Games.

The trio's combined haul signals a new era for the sport in India. National coaches will hope this success translates into sustained investment and grassroots development.

For now, the focus remains on celebrating the moment. The athletes have given Indian judo a reason to dream bigger.

What lies ahead

The Commonwealth Games have often served as a springboard for Indian athletes to aim higher. With these historic golds, the judo federation will be expected to build on the momentum. Eyes will now turn to future international competitions, where Dey and Singh will carry the weight of new expectations.
