West Asia conflict prompts ONGC to plan India's first strategic gas reserve

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • ONGC plans India's first strategic natural gas reserve
  • Conflict in West Asia prompts energy security concerns
  • Reserve aims to buffer against supply disruptions and price volatility

State-owned Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) has proposed the creation of India's first strategic natural gas reserve, a move triggered by the escalating conflict in West Asia. The plan, reported by multiple sources including Livemint and Hindustan Times, marks a significant shift in India's energy security strategy.

Why a strategic gas reserve now

The ongoing war in West Asia has exposed the vulnerabilities of global energy supply chains. India, which imports over half of its natural gas needs, has felt the pinch. Prices have swung wildly, and supply routes have come under threat.

ONGC's proposal is a direct response to these pressures. The company wants to create a buffer that can cushion the economy against sudden disruptions. Officials familiar with the matter told Livemint that the reserve would be a first-of-its-kind facility for natural gas in the country.

How the reserve would work

Details remain sketchy, but the core idea is straightforward. The reserve would involve storing natural gas in underground facilities, likely depleted oil or gas fields. These caverns can hold large volumes of gas that can be tapped during emergencies.

ONGC is believed to be evaluating potential sites. The company has decades of experience in hydrocarbon exploration and production, giving it the technical know-how to repurpose existing infrastructure.

The size and timeline of the project have not been disclosed. ONGC has not issued an official statement beyond confirming that preliminary studies are underway.

India's growing gas dependence

India's natural gas consumption has risen steadily as the government pushes for a cleaner energy mix. Gas now accounts for about 6% of the country's primary energy use, and the target is to raise that to 15% by 2030.

Domestic production has not kept pace. Imports have filled the gap, making India vulnerable to global price shocks and supply disruptions. The West Asia conflict has underscored that risk, with Iran and other major producers caught in the crossfire.

Strategic reserves are common for crude oil. India already has a Strategic Petroleum Reserve with storage capacity for about 5.33 million tonnes of crude. But natural gas storage is more complex, requiring high-pressure underground facilities.

What other countries do

Several nations maintain strategic gas reserves. The European Union, for instance, mandates that member states fill storage to at least 90% capacity by November each year. The United States has the Strategic Petroleum Reserve but no equivalent for natural gas, relying instead on market forces and a robust domestic production base.

China has been building its gas storage capacity rapidly, with plans to reach 55 billion cubic meters by 2025. India's proposed reserve is likely to be far smaller initially, but it represents a crucial first step.

Challenges ahead

Building a strategic gas reserve is not cheap. It requires significant upfront investment in storage infrastructure and the purchase of the gas itself. The costs could run into thousands of crores.

There is also the question of who will pay. ONGC may need government support or a special tariff mechanism to make the project viable. The regulatory framework for such a reserve does not yet exist and will need to be created.

Another challenge is sourcing the gas. In a tight global market, securing long-term supply contracts for the reserve could prove difficult, especially with West Asian producers facing their own constraints.

The proposal is still in its early stages. ONGC is expected to submit a detailed feasibility report to the Petroleum Ministry in the coming months. A decision on funding and implementation will follow.
