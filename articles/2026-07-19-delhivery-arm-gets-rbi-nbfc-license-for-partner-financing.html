Delhivery arm gets RBI NBFC license for partner financing

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhivery's subsidiary has obtained an NBFC license from the RBI.
  • The move allows the company to expand captive financial services for its 30,000+ partners.
  • The license positions Delhivery to offer credit and other financial products within its logistics ecosystem.

RBI nod for captive NBFC

Delhivery, the logistics giant, has secured a non-banking financial company (NBFC) license from the Reserve Bank of India for one of its subsidiaries. The approval allows the company to formally scale up financial services targeted at its vast network of partners.

The subsidiary can now lend and offer financial products directly to the over 30,000 delivery partners, merchants, and small businesses that operate on its platform. This marks a shift from informal credit arrangements to a regulated structure.

Deepening the ecosystem play

For Delhivery, the NBFC license is a strategic tool. The company has long extended working capital advances and quick settlements to its network. With a formal license, it can now offer loans, insurance, and other products under regulatory oversight.

Industry analysts see this as a move to deepen partner loyalty and create a new revenue stream. The logistics sector in India runs on thin margins, and captive financing can improve cash flow for small partners while generating fee income for Delhivery.

Regulatory and operational shift

The RBI's approval comes with compliance requirements. The subsidiary must adhere to capital adequacy norms, reporting standards, and fair lending practices. Delhivery has not disclosed the exact capital it plans to deploy for this vertical.

Sources familiar with the matter indicate the company will initially focus on small-ticket working capital loans. Larger credit products may follow as the business stabilises. The NBFC will operate separately from Delhivery's core logistics business to maintain regulatory clarity.

What this means for partners

For the 30,000-plus partners, the formalisation means faster access to credit. Earlier, advances were discretionary and linked to delivery volumes. Now, partners can expect standardised loan products based on transaction history and repayment behaviour.

Merchants using Delhivery's services also stand to benefit. The NBFC can offer inventory financing and seasonal credit, helping small businesses manage cash flow during peak sale periods. This could reduce their dependence on expensive informal credit sources.

Delhivery has not yet announced specific interest rates or product launch timelines. The company is expected to roll out pilot programmes in select regions before a nationwide push.

The move comes as several Indian logistics and e-commerce firms explore embedded finance. By housing financial services within a regulated NBFC, Delhivery aims to avoid the regulatory pitfalls that have dogged unregulated lending in the past.

Watch for the first set of loan products to be announced in the coming quarter. The success of this venture will depend on how quickly Delhivery can onboard partners onto the formal credit system while maintaining asset quality.
