Bengaluru initiative offers snake charmers a fresh platform

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Bengaluru initiative called 'A 100 Charmers' gives snake charmers a platform to showcase their skills.
  • It aims to preserve the traditional art form while providing sustainable livelihoods.
  • The project seeks to change public perception and offer legal alternatives for the community.

A new initiative in Bengaluru is offering snake charmers a platform to perform, aiming to preserve their traditional art while addressing livelihood concerns. Called 'A 100 Charmers', the project provides these performers with a legitimate stage, moving them away from the fringes of society.

Reviving a fading tradition

Snake charming, a practice that has been passed down through generations, has faced increasing challenges. Legal restrictions under wildlife protection laws and shifting public attitudes have pushed many practitioners out of work. The initiative seeks to change that by creating a space where their skills can be showcased safely and legally.

Organisers behind 'A 100 Charmers' say the goal is not just entertainment. They want to document and celebrate the music and performance aspect of snake charming, which often involves the pungi, a wind instrument. The snakes themselves are not harmed, and the project emphasises ethical treatment.

Creating sustainable livelihoods

For many snake charmers, the loss of traditional income sources has been severe. Without a stage, they have had to look for odd jobs or abandon their craft entirely. This initiative provides a direct economic alternative, allowing them to earn a living through performances at events, festivals, and private functions.

The founders are working with local communities to ensure that the performers are compensated fairly. Early reports suggest a positive response, with several bookings already in place. The aim is to scale up the model across other cities in Karnataka.

Changing public perceptions

There is also a strong social component to the project. Snake charmers have often been viewed with suspicion or pity. 'A 100 Charmers' wants to flip that narrative, presenting them as skilled artists who are keepers of a unique cultural heritage.

By putting them on a stage alongside other performers, the initiative hopes to foster respect and curiosity. It also opens a dialogue about conservation, as many charmers have deep knowledge of snake behaviour and habitats.

Officials have not yet confirmed any formal government backing, but the project has drawn attention from cultural organisations in Bengaluru. The response from audiences has been encouraging, with many attending shows out of curiosity and leaving with a new appreciation for the art.

What comes next

The organisers plan to expand 'A 100 Charmers' by training more performers and developing a regular calendar of shows. They are also exploring collaborations with schools and museums to introduce the art to younger generations. The coming months will test whether this model can sustain itself financially while providing a dignified livelihood for the charmers.
