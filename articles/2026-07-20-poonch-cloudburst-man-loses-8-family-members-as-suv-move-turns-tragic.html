Poonch cloudburst: Man loses 8 family members as SUV move turns tragic

📅 2026-07-20 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • A man in Poonch lost eight family members when a cloudburst triggered a landslide after he stepped out to move his SUV.
  • The death toll from rain-related incidents in Jammu and Kashmir has risen to 24, with seven people still missing.
  • An Army helicopter retrieved a flash flood victim's body from a river in Rajouri district.

In a tragedy that has left an entire village in shock, a man from Poonch district lost eight members of his family after a cloudburst triggered a landslide late on Saturday. The survivor had stepped out for a few minutes to move his SUV to safer ground. When he returned, his home had been crushed under tonnes of mud and debris.

Rescue teams pulled out eight bodies from the rubble through the night. Officials confirmed that the family had been asleep when the hillside gave way. The man, who has not been named, is said to be in a state of severe trauma.

Death toll climbs across Jammu and Kashmir

The Poonch incident is part of a wider spell of devastation across the Union Territory. At least 24 people have died in rain-related incidents since Friday, with Doda and Rajouri districts also reporting casualties. In Doda, three people were killed in a separate landslide.

Seven people remain missing, according to disaster management officials. Search operations are continuing in multiple locations, though access remains difficult due to damaged roads and ongoing rainfall. The meteorological department has warned of more heavy showers in the region over the next 24 hours.

Videos show flash floods sweeping away vehicles

Videos circulating on social media show torrents of muddy water racing through streets and highways, carrying away cars and even a bus. In one clip, a bus is seen being swept downstream as onlookers shout in panic. The visuals have prompted authorities to issue fresh travel advisories for the region.

In Rajouri district, an Army helicopter was deployed to retrieve the body of a flash flood victim from a river. The operation was completed on Sunday morning. The victim's identity has not yet been released.

Rescue operations hampered by terrain and weather

Teams from the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF), State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) and local police are working in the affected areas. However, the mountainous terrain and continuous rain have slowed their progress. In some villages, roads have been washed away entirely, forcing rescue teams to travel on foot.

Authorities have set up relief camps in Poonch, Doda and Rajouri. Over 200 people have been moved to safer locations. The administration has announced an ex-gratia of Rs 4 lakh each for the families of the deceased.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total number of houses damaged or destroyed. Assessments are expected to begin once the weather improves and access is restored.

The monsoon season has historically brought risks of landslides and flash floods to the Jammu region. But the intensity of this weekend's rainfall has caught many by surprise. With more rain forecasted, the focus remains on rescue and relief.

What happens next: The meteorological department has predicted more heavy rainfall over Jammu and Kashmir for the next 48 hours. Authorities have advised residents in vulnerable areas to move to safer locations. The search for the seven missing persons continues.
