Chennai's 'route thala' culture and its role in student violence

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Route thala culture persists in Chennai colleges, leading to frequent clashes.
  • Authorities and institutions have struggled to curb the influence of these student leaders.
  • Experts call for a multi-pronged approach, including counselling and stricter enforcement.

In the bustling corridors of Chennai's colleges, a shadowy hierarchy often holds more sway than the official student council. Known as 'route thala' — a term for self-styled leaders who control specific bus routes and college territories — these figures have long been a fixture of the city's student life. But their influence, as recent incidents show, comes at a price: recurring violence that disrupts campuses and raises questions about the effectiveness of existing measures.

A culture entrenched in campus life

The term 'route thala' refers to individuals, often senior students, who command respect and fear among their peers, particularly along the bus routes connecting residential areas to colleges. They mediate disputes, offer protection, and sometimes extort money or favours. What began as informal student leadership has morphed into a parallel power structure, particularly in arts and science colleges across Chennai.

These leaders often operate with impunity, using their networks to mobilise students for protests or personal vendettas. Clashes between rival groups are not uncommon, with violent incidents occasionally spilling onto the streets. The culture is so entrenched that even after suspensions or police action, new leaders emerge to fill the vacuum, perpetuating the cycle.

Why enforcement has failed

Despite periodic crackdowns by police and college administrations, the 'route thala' system persists. One reason, observers note, is the lack of a sustained strategy. Punitive action against individuals, while necessary, does not address the underlying allure of such power among students. Many of these leaders come from politically connected families or have ties to local political parties, which often shields them from serious consequences.

Moreover, college authorities have been accused of turning a blind eye, either out of fear or because they find it convenient to have a single point of contact for student grievances. This tacit acceptance has only strengthened the culture, with some students viewing the role of 'route thala' as a stepping stone to a political career.

Human cost of the 'thala' phenomenon

The violence that stems from this culture has left a trail of injured students and disrupted academic schedules. In several instances, clashes have led to the stabbing of students, prompting police to file cases against dozens of individuals. The fear of retaliation often prevents victims from coming forward, making it difficult for authorities to build strong cases.

Parents, too, have expressed anxiety about sending their children to colleges known for such turbulence. The psychological toll on students who are caught in the crossfire — or pressured to align with one group or another — is significant, though rarely addressed by institutions.

What can be done

Experts suggest that colleges need to foster a more inclusive student governance model, where elected councils have real authority and are supported by faculty. This would reduce the appeal of informal power structures. Counselling services and conflict-resolution training for students could also help de-escalate tensions before they turn violent.

Police, for their part, need to be more proactive in monitoring known troublemakers, especially during college fests and admission seasons when clashes are more likely. A coordinated approach involving colleges, parents, and law enforcement is essential to break the cycle.

The challenge ahead is not just to punish offenders but to dismantle the very culture that glorifies them. Whether institutions are willing to take that long, hard look remains to be seen. As Chennai's colleges reopen for the new academic year, all eyes will be on how authorities handle the simmering tensions that 'route thala' culture continues to fuel.
