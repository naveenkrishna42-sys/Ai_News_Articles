10 Bruin inventions that shaped UCLA’s legacy of innovation

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Inventions Original source ↗
Key points
  • UCLA’s list spans breakthroughs in medicine, computing, and materials science.
  • Inventions include the first internet router, gene-splicing tools, and MRI contrast agents.
  • The university continues to file hundreds of patents each year, fueling industry partnerships.

UCLA has a long history of turning laboratory breakthroughs into real-world impact. A recent roundup by the university highlights ten inventions that emerged from its campus research, ranging from medical therapies to internet infrastructure.

The list, published by the UCLA Newsroom, showcases how faculty and student innovations have shaped fields as diverse as genetics, computing, and environmental science. Each invention represents years of work, often starting with a fundamental question about how something works.

From gene therapy to video compression

Among the most cited inventions is the discovery of the first gene-splicing technique, which later enabled the production of synthetic insulin. Developed by UCLA researchers in the 1970s, this method allowed scientists to cut and combine DNA from different organisms, laying the foundation for modern biotechnology.

Another key entry is the creation of the first internet router, built by UCLA professor Leonard Kleinrock and his team. That device, called the Interface Message Processor, sent the first message over ARPANET in 1969, a moment often called the birth of the internet.

The list also includes a video compression algorithm that made streaming video practical. Without it, platforms like YouTube and Netflix would struggle to deliver smooth playback over limited bandwidth.

Medical tools and materials science

In medicine, UCLA researchers developed a contrast agent for MRI scans that is now used in millions of procedures worldwide. The agent, a gadolinium-based compound, improves image clarity and helps doctors detect tumours and other abnormalities.

Another invention is a non-invasive method to monitor brain activity using light rather than electrodes. This technique, called functional near-infrared spectroscopy, is now used in studies of infant brain development and in brain-computer interface research.

Materials science also features prominently. UCLA engineers created a super-strong, lightweight composite material that is now used in aircraft and sports equipment. The material, a carbon-fibre reinforced polymer, offers high strength-to-weight ratios that reduce fuel consumption in aviation.

Powering progress across industries

The university’s inventions have led to the creation of dozens of spin-off companies. One example is a startup that commercialised a new type of battery electrolyte, promising longer life for electric vehicles. Another company uses a UCLA-developed algorithm to optimise energy use in data centres.

UCLA’s technology transfer office files hundreds of patents each year. Many of these licences generate royalties that fund further research, creating a cycle of innovation that benefits both the university and the broader economy.

The list is not exhaustive. UCLA researchers continue to work on projects ranging from quantum computing to personalised medicine. Each new invention adds to the university’s reputation as a hub for discovery.

What comes next will depend on how these technologies evolve. Some may take decades to reach the market; others could change daily life within a few years. UCLA’s track record suggests that the next breakthrough is already being tested in a lab somewhere on campus.
