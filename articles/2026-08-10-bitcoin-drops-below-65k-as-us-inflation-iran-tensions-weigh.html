Bitcoin drops below $65k as US inflation, Iran tensions weigh

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin slipped below $65,000 as markets weighed US inflation figures and Iran-related geopolitical tensions.
  • The decline reflects broader risk-off sentiment in global markets.
  • Investors are watching for further cues from US economic data and Middle East developments.

Bitcoin slid below the $65,000 mark on Monday, as traders grappled with fresh US inflation numbers and escalating geopolitical risks tied to Iran. The world's largest cryptocurrency was last seen trading around $64,800, down roughly 2% over the past 24 hours, according to data from Investing.com.

The move comes as investors reassess the outlook for monetary policy and weigh the potential for supply disruptions in the Middle East, both of which have historically fueled volatility in risk assets like crypto.

Inflation data clouds rate-cut bets

US inflation figures released last week came in hotter than expected, complicating the Federal Reserve's path toward interest rate cuts. The data has prompted traders to trim their expectations for a September rate reduction, a scenario that typically pressures speculative assets.

Higher borrowing costs tend to strengthen the US dollar and draw capital away from riskier investments, including cryptocurrencies. Bitcoin, often seen as a hedge against fiat debasement, has shown increasing correlation with tech stocks in recent months, making it sensitive to shifts in liquidity expectations.

"The market is repricing the Fed's next move," said one Mumbai-based crypto analyst, speaking on condition of anonymity. "If inflation stays sticky, the dollar gets a boost, and that's never good for Bitcoin in the short term."

Iran risk adds to jitters

Geopolitical tensions around Iran have added another layer of uncertainty. Reports of heightened military activity and diplomatic friction in the region have kept oil prices elevated, feeding concerns about global growth and inflation.

For crypto traders, such geopolitical flashpoints often trigger a flight to safety, with investors liquidating digital assets to cover margins or move into traditional havens like gold and US Treasuries. Bitcoin's recent price action mirrors that pattern, as it fell in tandem with equities while the dollar index edged higher.

Analysts note that the impact is not always one-sided. In past episodes of Middle East turmoil, some investors have turned to Bitcoin as a decentralized alternative, but the immediate reaction has generally been negative.

What this means for the broader market

The drop below $65,000 is a psychological blow for bulls, who had been hoping for a sustained rally above the $70,000 mark. The level had acted as support during several pullbacks in recent months, and its breach could open the door to further downside, with the next major support seen near $60,000.

However, trading volumes remain moderate, suggesting that the sell-off is not yet a full-blown panic. Derivatives data shows that long liquidations have increased, but funding rates across major exchanges have not turned deeply negative, a sign that leveraged positions are being unwound in an orderly fashion.

Indian crypto exchanges reported a slight uptick in trading activity during Asian hours, though retail participation remains subdued compared to the frenzy of previous bull runs. Regulatory uncertainty in the country continues to keep many institutional players on the sidelines.

Looking ahead

In the near term, Bitcoin's fate hinges on two factors: the next set of US economic data, including jobless claims and retail sales, and any fresh developments in the Iran situation. A cooler inflation print could revive rate-cut hopes and lift sentiment, while an escalation in the Middle East could push prices lower.

Traders also have their eyes on the upcoming Federal Reserve policy meeting, where officials will offer clues on the timing of any easing cycle. Until then, volatility is likely to remain elevated, and the $65,000 level could be tested repeatedly in both directions.
