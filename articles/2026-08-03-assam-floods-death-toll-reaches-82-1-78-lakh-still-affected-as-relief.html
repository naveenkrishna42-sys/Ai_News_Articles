Assam floods: Death toll reaches 82, 1.78 lakh still affected as relief work ramps up

📅 2026-08-03 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Death toll in Assam floods rises to 82, with 1.78 lakh people still affected.
  • Situation shows slight improvement as number of affected dips from previous highs.
  • Relief efforts include loan relief for borrowers in four worst-hit districts.
  • A Class 10 dropout rescues 2,000 people using a motorboat despite no formal training.

Flood waters recede but crisis persists

The flood situation in Assam is showing signs of improvement, with the number of affected people dipping to 1.78 lakh. However, the toll continues to mount, with 82 lives lost so far this season. Relief operations remain in full swing across the state, even as authorities warn that the danger is far from over.

Officials said the latest figures mark a decline from earlier peaks, when over 2 lakh people were hit by the deluge. Still, vast stretches of land remain submerged, and many families have yet to return to their homes. The Brahmaputra and its tributaries continue to flow above the danger mark in several districts.

Acts of courage amid the chaos

Among the many stories emerging from the crisis, one stands out. A Class 10 dropout from a remote village has become an unlikely hero, rescuing over 2,000 people using a motorboat. Despite having no formal training, he navigated treacherous waters to ferry stranded families to safety.

His efforts have drawn widespread praise, with locals calling him a symbol of resilience. Authorities have not yet confirmed whether he will be officially honoured, but his actions have already made a deep impact in the affected communities.

Relief measures and loan relief

The state government has announced a six-month loan relief for borrowers in Assam's four worst-hit districts. This move aims to ease the financial burden on farmers and small business owners who have lost their livelihoods to the floods. Banks have been instructed to implement the relief without delay.

Relief camps continue to provide shelter, food, and medical aid to thousands. However, officials admit that reaching every affected family remains a challenge, especially in remote areas where roads have been washed away. The army and paramilitary forces have been deployed to assist with rescue and distribution efforts.

Monsoon fury beyond Assam

While Assam bears the brunt, the monsoon has also wreaked havoc in other parts of the country. Gujarat, Jammu & Kashmir, and Maharashtra have reported flood-related disruptions, with several districts facing waterlogging and damage to crops. The India Meteorological Department has warned of more heavy rainfall in the coming days.

In Assam, the focus is now on rehabilitation and rebuilding. The state has sought additional central assistance, though no formal announcement has been made yet. Meanwhile, health officials are keeping a close watch on the spread of waterborne diseases, which often follow such disasters.

What to watch next

As the waters gradually recede, the task of recovery will take centre stage. The coming weeks will reveal how effectively the state manages rehabilitation, disease control, and the restoration of essential services. With forecasts of more rain, authorities are preparing for any fresh surge in the crisis.
