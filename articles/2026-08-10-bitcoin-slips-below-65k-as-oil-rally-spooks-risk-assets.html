Bitcoin slips below $65k as oil rally spooks risk assets

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin dropped below $65,000 on Monday.
  • Rising oil prices dampened sentiment for risky assets.
  • Investors are watching crude's impact on inflation and Fed policy.

Bitcoin slides as crude climbs

Bitcoin slipped below the $65,000 mark on Monday, as a sharp rally in oil prices weighed on appetite for riskier investments. The world's largest cryptocurrency saw its value erode in tandem with equities and other speculative assets, with traders recalibrating positions in response to a fresh surge in crude.

The move underscores how sensitive digital assets remain to macro shifts. With oil prices climbing, concerns over prolonged inflation have resurfaced, prompting investors to question the durability of the current risk-on mood.

Oil's ripple effect on crypto

Crude's ascent has historically been a double-edged sword for markets. On one hand, it signals robust demand; on the other, it stokes fears of cost-push inflation that could force central banks to keep policy tighter for longer. For Bitcoin, which has increasingly traded like a high-beta tech stock, that dynamic has translated into selling pressure.

Monday's decline was not isolated to crypto. Asian and European equity benchmarks also struggled, as energy costs threatened to squeeze corporate margins and consumer spending. The correlation between Bitcoin and traditional risk assets has strengthened over the past year, making it more susceptible to such external shocks.

Traders eye $64k support

Market participants are now watching the $64,000 level closely. A decisive break below that could trigger further downside, with some analysts pointing to $60,000 as the next psychological floor. However, others argue that the current pullback is a healthy correction within a broader uptrend, noting that institutional inflows remain steady.

Volatility has been a defining feature of crypto markets in recent weeks. Bitcoin has swung between gains and losses as investors juggle conflicting signals from the oil market, central bank commentary, and regulatory developments. The latest dip is a reminder that no asset class is immune to macro pressures.

What to watch next

Attention will turn to upcoming US inflation data and any fresh signals from the Federal Reserve on interest rates. A hotter-than-expected print could deepen the sell-off, while signs of easing price pressures might lure buyers back. Crude's trajectory will also be pivotal—if oil continues to climb, Bitcoin may face sustained headwinds.

For now, traders are bracing for more turbulence. The intersection of energy prices and digital assets is likely to remain a key theme in the coming sessions, as markets parse the implications for global liquidity and risk appetite.
