Yulu raises $93M to expand e-bike fleet, enter e-scooter segment

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Yulu raises $93 million in Series C funding led by GEF Capital
  • Funds to scale EV fleet and enter full-size electric scooter segment
  • Quick-commerce boom drives demand for e-bikes in India
  • Yulu joins Bounce in seeking funds as gig-EV rentals surge

Funding round led by GEF Capital

Bengaluru-based electric mobility startup Yulu has raised $93 million in a Series C funding round led by GEF Capital, with participation from existing investors. The fresh capital will be deployed to scale its electric two-wheeler fleet and mark the company's entry into the full-size electric scooter segment.

The announcement comes amid a sharp uptick in demand for e-bikes driven by India's booming quick-commerce sector. Delivery partners for platforms such as Zepto, Blinkit, and Swiggy Instamart are increasingly adopting electric vehicles to cut costs and comply with city-level emission norms.

Quick-commerce tailwinds

Yulu's battery-swapping network and docked e-bikes have made it a preferred partner for gig-economy riders. The company operates in multiple cities, offering subscription-based access to electric two-wheelers, which eliminates the high upfront cost of ownership for delivery workers.

Industry insiders say the quick-commerce segment's promise of 10-minute deliveries has accelerated the need for reliable, low-maintenance EVs. Yulu's expansion plans are expected to add thousands of vehicles to its fleet over the next year, though the company has not disclosed specific numbers.

Entry into full-size e-scooters

Until now, Yulu has focused on低速, compact e-bikes designed for last-mile connectivity. The move into full-size electric scooters signals a strategic shift to capture a broader customer base, including individual commuters and larger delivery fleets.

The new scooters are likely to compete with established players like Ola Electric, Ather Energy, and TVS Motor. However, Yulu's differentiator remains its integrated mobility platform, which combines hardware, software, and swapping infrastructure.

Investor confidence amidst funding winter

The Series C round stands out in a period when Indian startups have faced a funding slowdown. GEF Capital's leadership in the round underscores growing investor appetite for sustainable urban mobility solutions.

Yulu's rival Bounce is also reportedly seeking funds as gig-EV rentals gain traction. The simultaneous fundraising efforts highlight a sector poised for consolidation, with players racing to secure capital before demand peaks further.

Regulatory and operational challenges

Despite the optimism, Yulu faces hurdles including state-level EV policies, battery disposal concerns, and the need for dense swapping infrastructure. The company has not yet commented on how it plans to address these issues with the new funds.

Officials have also not confirmed whether the full-size scooters will be manufactured in-house or sourced from OEM partners. Analysts expect Yulu to announce partnerships or production timelines in the coming quarters.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on Yulu's deployment strategy for the fresh capital and how quickly it can scale its scooter segment. The company's ability to navigate regulatory approvals and infrastructure bottlenecks will determine whether it can maintain its lead in the gig-EV rental space.

With quick-commerce companies pushing deeper into tier-2 and tier-3 cities, Yulu's next moves could reshape last-mile logistics across India.
