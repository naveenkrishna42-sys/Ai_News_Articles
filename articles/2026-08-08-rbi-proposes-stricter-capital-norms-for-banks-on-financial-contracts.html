RBI proposes stricter capital norms for banks on financial contracts

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI draft rules require banks to hold more capital against risks from financial contracts.
  • The proposal aligns with global standards set by the Basel Committee.
  • Public comments invited until a specified deadline before finalising the norms.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Saturday proposed a new set of capital adequacy rules for banks to cover risks arising from financial contracts, a move that could require lenders to set aside more funds for complex derivative and repo transactions.

The draft framework, released for public consultation, seeks to align Indian banking norms with the latest global standards agreed upon by the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision.

What the new rules entail

Under the proposal, banks would need to calculate capital requirements for counterparty credit risk using a more sensitive approach, known as the standardised approach for counterparty credit risk (SA-CCR). This method replaces the current, less granular models, and is designed to better capture the volatility and interconnectedness of financial contracts.

The RBI has also outlined a revised treatment for credit valuation adjustment (CVA) risk, which accounts for the possibility of a counterparty's creditworthiness deteriorating. This includes a new basic CVA approach and a full CVA approach, depending on the sophistication of the bank's internal models.

Banks with significant derivative exposures would be more affected, while smaller lenders with limited activity in such instruments may see minimal impact.

Why the RBI is acting

The central bank's move comes amid growing complexity in India's financial markets, where banks are increasingly engaging in over-the-counter derivatives, interest rate swaps, and other structured products. The global financial crisis of 2008 highlighted how underestimated counterparty risks can amplify systemic shocks.

By adopting the SA-CCR, the RBI aims to bring Indian banks in line with international practices, making the domestic system more resilient to market stress. The regulator has stated that the proposed norms are intended to "ensure prudent risk management" and "promote financial stability."

Impact on banks

Banking analysts suggest that the new rules could lead to a modest increase in capital requirements for some lenders, particularly those with large trading books. However, the RBI has provided a transition period, allowing banks to phase in the changes over several quarters.

Public sector banks, which often have lower capital buffers compared to private peers, may need to raise fresh capital to comply. The RBI has not yet quantified the aggregate impact, but experts expect it to be manageable given the current health of the banking sector.

The draft rules also include provisions for recognising collateral and netting agreements, which can reduce capital charges where banks have legally enforceable netting arrangements.

Next steps

The RBI has invited comments from banks, market participants, and the public within a specified window, after which it will finalise the guidelines. The exact implementation date will be announced once the comments are reviewed.

This consultation is part of a broader regulatory push by the RBI to tighten oversight of financial markets, following similar measures on liquidity and market risk. Banks are expected to start aligning their systems and models well ahead of the final deadline.

As the financial landscape evolves, the central bank's proactive stance on capital adequacy will be closely watched by investors and policymakers alike.
