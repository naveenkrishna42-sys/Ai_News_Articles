40 Years of Karma: Subhash Ghai on the film's enduring legacy

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Subhash Ghai's 1986 film 'Karma' completes 40 years, and the director shares his reflections.
  • The film starred Dilip Kumar, Nutan, Anil Kapoor, Jackie Shroff, and Naseeruddin Shah.
  • Ghai talks about the film's themes of justice and revenge that still find relevance today.

A Milestone for a Cinematic Classic

Subhash Ghai, the celebrated filmmaker behind hits like 'Karz' and 'Ram Lakhan', is looking back at one of his most ambitious projects — 'Karma'. As the film marks its 40th anniversary, Ghai opened up about the movie's journey from script to screen, and why it continues to hold a special place in the hearts of moviegoers.

Released in 1986, 'Karma' was a multi-starrer that brought together the legendary Dilip Kumar, Nutan, and a young brigade of Anil Kapoor, Jackie Shroff, and Naseeruddin Shah. The film was a big-budget entertainer that blended action, drama, and a strong message about standing up against tyranny.

The Making of a Revenge Saga

Ghai, known for his grand storytelling, has often spoken about how 'Karma' was a turning point in his career. The story revolved around a retired freedom fighter who recruits three convicts to take down a corrupt and ruthless gangster. It was a classic good-versus-evil narrative, but with a distinct Indian flavor that resonated with the masses.

In his recent reflections, Ghai highlighted the casting coup — getting Dilip Kumar and Nutan on board was no small feat. He credited the actors for bringing depth to their roles, making the film more than just a masala entertainer. The director also recalled the challenges of shooting elaborate set pieces and the iconic climax, which remains one of the most remembered sequences in Hindi cinema.

Why 'Karma' Still Matters

Forty years later, Ghai believes the film's themes are still relevant. The idea of ordinary people rising against injustice, and the moral complexities of revenge, continue to be explored in contemporary cinema. Ghai noted that while the visual effects and action scenes may look dated to today's audience, the emotional core of the film remains intact.

The film's music, composed by the duo Laxmikant-Pyarelal, also contributed to its longevity. Songs like 'Naam' and 'Karma' became anthems, and Ghai fondly remembers how the soundtrack was a chartbuster in its time.

A Legacy of Stardom

'Karma' also served as a launchpad for its younger stars. Anil Kapoor and Jackie Shroff were already on the rise, but the film cemented their status as action heroes. Naseeruddin Shah, known for his art-house roles, proved he could shine in a commercial blockbuster as well. Ghai said he always believed in casting against type, and 'Karma' was a testament to that.

The film's success also marked a period when Ghai was at his creative peak, following up with 'Ram Lakhan' and 'Saudagar' in the coming years. His production house, Mukta Arts, would go on to become a major player in Indian cinema.

Looking Ahead

As 'Karma' completes four decades, Ghai remains active in the industry, mentoring young talent and exploring new storytelling formats. He has hinted at potential remakes or sequels of his classics, though nothing has been confirmed. For now, fans of the film can celebrate its legacy, and perhaps revisit the movie to appreciate how it paved the way for modern action dramas.

What remains to be seen is whether a new generation of filmmakers will draw inspiration from Ghai's brand of larger-than-life cinema, and how the industry continues to evolve in an era of OTT platforms and changing audience tastes.
