Glasgow gold quest: Neeraj Chopra, Mirabai Chanu lead India's medal hopes at CWG 2026

India's march for gold at the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow officially begins. Star athletes Neeraj Chopra and Mirabai Chanu headline a strong contingent carrying the nation's medal hopes.

The javelin thrower and the weightlifter are among India's best bets for podium finishes. Both have proven themselves on the world stage and will be expected to deliver in Scotland.

One medal already in the bag

India's first medal at the Games is already assured. The country will step into the arena with at least one guaranteed podium finish before the opening ceremony.

This unusual situation stems from a technicality in one of the events. Officials have not confirmed the exact discipline, but it is likely a draw-based or seeded competition where India has secured a top-three spot by default.

Top medal prospects to watch

Neeraj Chopra remains the biggest draw. The Olympic champion's presence alone raises expectations for a gold medal in javelin throw.

Mirabai Chanu, a former world champion in weightlifting, is another strong contender. She has consistently delivered at multi-sport events and will aim to add another Commonwealth gold to her collection.

Other athletes across athletics, boxing, shooting, and wrestling are also in the mix. But the depth of India's squad this year is thinner compared to previous editions.

Why the medal rush may slow down

Experts warn that India may not witness the same medal rush as in past Games. The absence of certain sports and tougher competition from other nations could limit the tally.

Several core disciplines where India traditionally dominates have seen changes in format or participation. This has narrowed the path to multiple golds.

Knowledge transfer over medals: Rohit Rajpal

Rohit Rajpal, a key figure in Indian tennis, has stressed that the Games are not just about medals. He said knowledge transfer is as important as winning at the Glasgow CWG.

Rajpal believes that sharing expertise and experience with younger athletes will have a longer-lasting impact than any single gold medal. His comments reflect a broader push within Indian sports to build sustainable excellence.

Coaches and support staff accompanying the contingent are expected to focus on learning from global best practices during the event.

The 2026 Commonwealth Games will test India's ability to compete under new conditions. With a guaranteed medal already in hand, the focus now shifts to how many more the team can bring home.

All eyes will be on Glasgow as Chopra, Chanu, and the rest of the squad begin their quest for glory.
