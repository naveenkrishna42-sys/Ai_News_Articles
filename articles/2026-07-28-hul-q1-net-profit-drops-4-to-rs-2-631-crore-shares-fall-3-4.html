HUL Q1 net profit drops 4% to Rs 2,631 crore; shares fall 3.4%

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • HUL's consolidated net profit fell 4% year-on-year to Rs 2,631 crore for the June quarter.
  • Revenue from operations grew marginally, but input cost inflation squeezed margins.
  • Shares of HUL declined 3.4% on the BSE following the results announcement.

Hindustan Unilever Limited (HUL) reported a 4% drop in consolidated net profit for the June quarter, missing street estimates. The FMCG major posted a net profit of Rs 2,631 crore, compared to Rs 2,741 crore in the same quarter last year. The results, released on Tuesday, sent the stock down 3.4% in a broadly negative market.

Revenue growth fails to offset cost pressures

Revenue from operations for the quarter stood at Rs 16,234 crore, up 2.5% from Rs 15,841 crore a year ago. However, the growth was not enough to counter the impact of sustained inflation in raw material prices. Input costs for palm oil, crude derivatives and packaging materials remained elevated, squeezing gross margins.

Operating profit (EBIT) declined 3.2% to Rs 3,420 crore, with margin contracting to 21.1% from 22.4% in the corresponding quarter last year. The company said it continued to invest behind brands and distribution, which added to cost pressures.

Volume growth muted in rural markets

Domestic consumer demand remained tepid, especially in rural India. HUL reported a 2% volume growth for the quarter, lower than the 3-4% that analysts had pencilled in. The management attributed the slowdown to uneven monsoon rains and a lag in rural recovery.

Urban demand held up relatively better, with premium and personal care segments seeing steady offtake. But staples and mass-market categories faced headwinds. The company noted that competitive intensity remained high, with local and regional players gaining share in some categories.

Board declares interim dividend

The board of directors declared an interim dividend of Rs 18 per share for the financial year 2026-27. The record date for the dividend has been fixed as August 8, 2026. This is in line with the company's practice of rewarding shareholders on a quarterly basis.

HUL also said that its board will meet again in October to consider the second quarter results. The company did not provide any specific guidance for the rest of the year, citing an uncertain demand environment.

Market reaction and analyst views

Shares of HUL closed at Rs 2,410 on the BSE, down 3.4% from the previous close. The stock has been under pressure over the past year, falling about 12% from its 52-week high of Rs 2,740. Analysts said the results were below expectations, particularly on the margin front.

Brokerages remained divided on the stock. Some maintained a 'hold' rating, pointing to the company's strong distribution network and brand portfolio. Others turned cautious, flagging that a recovery in consumption may take longer than earlier anticipated. Foreign institutional investors have trimmed holdings in the stock over the past two quarters.

HUL's results came on a busy day for corporate earnings. Larsen & Toubro, Ambuja Cements, Varun Beverages and Pfizer were among other large firms that reported their quarterly numbers. The broader market ended flat, with the Nifty 50 closing at 24,850 points.

The coming quarters will be crucial for HUL as it navigates a patchy demand recovery and persistent cost inflation. Investors will closely watch the trajectory of rural demand and commodity prices to gauge whether the worst of the margin compression is behind the company.
