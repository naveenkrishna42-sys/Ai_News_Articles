UPI Fees Shift to Merchants: No Customer Charges, No Extra GST

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-18 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • UPI transactions remain free for individual customers; the new Merchant Discount Rate applies only to large merchants above a turnover threshold.
  • No additional GST is levied on UPI transactions beyond existing service tax norms on payment services.
  • Petrol pumps and small traders have raised operational concerns, but the RBI expects no significant shift back to cash.

UPI's Free Era Ends Selectively, Not Universally

India's Unified Payments Interface has crossed a defining threshold. Sources confirm that the newly introduced Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on UPI transactions will not be passed on to customers, and merchants will not face an additional GST burden beyond existing norms. The change applies only to large merchants, leaving person-to-person transfers and small-ticket payments untouched.

For a payments ecosystem processing billions of monthly transactions, this is a structural shift. The question every buyer and business owner now asks is simple: who actually pays, and how much?

What the New UPI Fee Structure Actually Says

The MDR is a fee charged to the merchant's bank, which is then shared across the payment chain — the acquiring bank, the issuing bank, and the UPI app provider. Under the revised framework, this applies to merchants above a defined turnover threshold, not to individuals sending money to friends or family.

For a merchant processing ₹10 lakh a month in digital payments, a 0.2% MDR works out to roughly ₹2,000 — a cost that must be weighed against the convenience, speed, and traceability of UPI versus cash handling.

Why Petrol Pumps and Traders Are Worried

Madhya Pradesh petrol pumps have reportedly threatened to stop accepting UPI above ₹2,000, citing thin margins on fuel sales. Trade bodies argue that fuel retail already operates on fixed commissions, leaving little room for payment processing costs.

The concern is not irrational. For high-volume, low-margin businesses, even a fraction of a percentage point matters. However, the alternative — cash handling, reconciliation errors, and security risks — carries its own hidden costs that often exceed digital payment fees.

RBI's Position and Real-World Buyer Implications

The Reserve Bank of India has indicated that MDR apprehension is unlikely to spur higher cash usage. That assessment rests on a decade of behavioural change: consumers who have adopted UPI for daily transactions rarely revert to cash purely because a merchant absorbs a small fee.

For customers, the practical impact is zero. Your UPI transfer to a kirana store, a friend, or a utility biller remains free. For merchants, the calculus is different — and the decision to accept or decline high-value UPI payments will depend on individual margin structures.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Individual UPI users pay nothing extra; free transfers continue.
  • Large merchants absorb a modest MDR, typically under 0.3%.
  • No new GST layer applies specifically to UPI transactions.
  • Small merchants and P2P transfers stay outside the fee net.
  • Fuel retailers and trade bodies remain the loudest dissenters.

What This Means for the Digital Payments Market

India's UPI success was built on zero-cost ubiquity. Introducing MDR for large merchants tests whether that model can sustain itself without penalising consumers. Payment aggregators and fintech apps now face pressure to justify their share of the MDR through value-added services — analytics, credit access, and faster settlement.

For buyers, the takeaway is straightforward: your UPI experience does not change. For merchants, the era of fully free digital acceptance is narrowing, and pricing negotiations with payment providers will become a routine part of doing business.
