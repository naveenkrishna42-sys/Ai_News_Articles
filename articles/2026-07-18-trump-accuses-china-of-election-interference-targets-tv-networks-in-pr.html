Trump accuses China of election interference, targets TV networks in primetime speech

📅 2026-07-18 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump alleged China meddled in the 2020 election, without providing evidence.
  • He threatened TV networks over their coverage, signaling potential legal action.
  • Beijing rejected the accusations, calling them baseless and politically motivated.
  • The speech comes ahead of the US midterm elections, reigniting debate on election security.
  • Declassified files cited by Trump have not been independently verified to support his claims.

Former US President Donald Trump delivered a primetime speech on Thursday that revived unsubstantiated claims of foreign interference in the 2020 election, targeting China and threatening television networks. The address, seen as a precursor to the upcoming midterm elections, drew swift condemnation from Beijing and sharp reactions from media outlets.

Accusations Against China

Trump asserted that China had interfered in the 2020 US presidential election, a charge he has made repeatedly without presenting verified evidence. He claimed declassified files supported his allegations, but independent reviews have not confirmed such findings.

Beijing responded firmly. The Chinese foreign ministry dismissed the accusations as a 'nightmare' and a baseless attempt to shift blame. Officials in China reiterated their policy of non-interference in other nations' internal affairs.

The remarks have reignited debate over election security in the US, with critics arguing that Trump is leveraging unproven claims to energise his political base ahead of the midterms.

Threats Against TV Networks

In a notable part of the speech, Trump threatened television networks, accusing them of biased coverage. He suggested legal action against outlets he alleged were spreading misinformation, though he did not specify which networks or what steps he would take.

Media watchdogs have expressed concern over such threats, noting they could undermine press freedom. Network representatives have not yet commented on the specifics of Trump's remarks.

These threats mark an escalation in Trump's long-running feud with mainstream media, which he has often labelled as 'fake news'.

Election Security Claims Under Scrutiny

Trump's speech referenced declassified documents that he said proved his election security claims. However, multiple reports, including analyses by CNN and the BBC, have found no conclusive evidence of widespread fraud or foreign interference that could have altered the 2020 outcome.

Legal experts point out that dozens of court cases challenging the election results were dismissed due to lack of evidence. The former president's continued assertions have deepened partisan divides over trust in the electoral process.

Observers note that the midterm elections are likely to see heightened scrutiny of voting procedures, with both parties preparing for potential disputes.

Political Fallout and Reactions

The speech has drawn mixed reactions. Republican allies praised Trump for highlighting what they call unanswered questions about election integrity. Democrats and some independent analysts dismissed the address as a distraction from policy issues.

International reactions have been muted, though Beijing's sharp rebuttal underscores the diplomatic sensitivity of the claims. China has consistently denied interfering in US elections.

As the midterms approach, Trump's rhetoric is expected to remain a central theme in Republican campaigns, keeping election security at the forefront of political discourse.

What happens next: The impact of Trump's allegations on voter confidence and media regulation will become clearer in the coming weeks, as candidates and networks gear up for the November midterms.
