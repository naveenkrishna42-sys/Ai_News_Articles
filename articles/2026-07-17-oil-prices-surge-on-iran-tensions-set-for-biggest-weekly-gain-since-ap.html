Oil Prices Surge on Iran Tensions, Set for Biggest Weekly Gain Since April

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil prices rose more than 2% amid heightened US-Iran tensions.
  • Crude is set for its biggest weekly gain since April, driven by geopolitical risk.
  • Strait of Hormuz disruptions threaten commodity supplies beyond oil.
  • Analysts warn of continued volatility after latest Iranian attack on a Gulf ship.

Oil prices surged again on Friday, putting crude on track for its largest weekly gain since April as geopolitical tensions between the United States and Iran showed no signs of cooling. Benchmark crude rose more than 2% in morning trading, extending a rally that has added several dollars per barrel over the past five days.

Geopolitical Risk Premium Returns

The rally marks a sharp reversal from the relatively calm trading seen in recent months. Analysts say the market is once again pricing in a significant geopolitical risk premium, with every new incident threatening to disrupt flows from the Middle East.

"This is the biggest weekly jump we have seen since April," one market strategist noted. "The situation in the Gulf has deteriorated rapidly, and traders are reacting to every headline."

The latest leg higher came after reports of another Iranian attack on a commercial vessel in the Gulf. The attack, which targeted a ship near the Strait of Hormuz, has heightened fears that key shipping lanes could become unsafe for tankers carrying crude.

Strait of Hormuz in Focus

The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway between Iran and Oman, is a critical chokepoint for global oil supplies. Roughly 20% of the world's petroleum passes through the strait daily. Any disruption there sends shockwaves through commodity markets far beyond oil.

Analysts at Bloomberg warned that tensions in the strait now threaten supply chains for liquefied natural gas, petrochemicals, and other commodities. The risk of a broader blockade has pushed insurance and shipping costs sharply higher in recent days.

"The Strait of Hormuz is the most important oil transit chokepoint in the world," said a senior energy analyst. "If tensions continue to escalate, we could see supply disruptions that ripple across global markets."

Market Volatility Expected to Continue

The New York Times reported that oil prices are set for more volatility after the latest attack, with traders bracing for possible retaliatory strikes by the US or its allies. The situation remains fluid, and diplomatic efforts have so far failed to de-escalate the conflict.

The Wall Street Journal noted that US-Iran tensions remain high, with no clear off-ramp in sight. The Biden administration has vowed to protect freedom of navigation in the Gulf, but military options carry their own risks.

For now, the market is pricing in a prolonged period of uncertainty. The weekly gain is expected to be the largest in over three months, but many traders believe the rally could accelerate if the situation worsens.

What to Watch

All eyes will be on the Strait of Hormuz in the coming days. Any fresh attack on commercial shipping or military assets could push oil prices even higher. Traders are also watching for any signs of diplomatic progress, though none appear imminent. The coming week will likely determine whether this surge is a short-term spike or the start of a sustained rally.
