Kogilu Residents Step Up Push for Stalled Road Projects

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Residents of Kogilu are stepping up pressure on authorities to resume stalled road projects.
  • Poor road conditions have led to frequent accidents and traffic congestion in the area.
  • Local officials have acknowledged the issue but have not provided a timeline for completion.

Persistent Grievances

Residents of Kogilu, a rapidly growing suburb in north Bengaluru, are intensifying their demand for the resumption of several stalled road projects. The roads, which were supposed to be upgraded years ago, remain in a state of disrepair, causing daily hardships for commuters and locals alike.

The agitation has gained momentum over the past few weeks, with residents forming groups to petition civic authorities. Their primary demand is the immediate restart of work on roads that have been left incomplete, allegedly due to administrative delays and funding issues.

Safety and Traffic Woes

Commuters say the poor condition of these roads has led to frequent accidents, particularly during the monsoon season when potholes become hazardous. Two-wheeler riders are among the most affected, with several reporting near-misses on a weekly basis.

Traffic congestion has also worsened, as incomplete stretches force vehicles to navigate narrow detours. Residents point out that the situation is especially grim during peak hours, when office-goers and school buses add to the chaos.

Official Response

The Bangalore Mirror had earlier reported on the issue, and since then, local representatives have acknowledged the grievances. However, residents claim that assurances have not translated into action on the ground.

Authorities have cited procedural hurdles, including land acquisition and utility shifting, as reasons for the delay. They have not yet confirmed a revised timeline for completing the pending work.

Community Action

In a bid to expedite the process, residents have now started a sustained campaign, including submitting memorandums to the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) and the local corporator's office. They have also planned a series of public meetings to build pressure.

Some residents have taken to social media to document the state of the roads, sharing photographs and videos that have drawn attention from neighbouring communities facing similar issues. The campaign has found resonance among those who argue that infrastructure development has not kept pace with the area's rapid urbanisation.

What Lies Ahead

While officials have promised to look into the matter, residents remain sceptical, citing past inaction. The coming weeks are likely to see more coordinated protests if no concrete steps are taken.

Observers note that the issue reflects a broader pattern across Bengaluru's peripheral areas, where growth has outstripped civic infrastructure. For now, Kogilu's residents are determined to keep the pressure on, hoping that sustained public attention will finally get the roads they were promised.
