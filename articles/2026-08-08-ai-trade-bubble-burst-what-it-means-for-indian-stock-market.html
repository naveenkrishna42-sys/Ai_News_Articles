AI trade bubble burst: What it means for Indian stock market

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Global AI stocks, including Alphabet, Meta, and SK Hynix, have seen a sharp selloff, sparking fears of a bubble burst.
  • Indian IT and semiconductor-related stocks could face short-term volatility, but domestic-focused sectors may remain resilient.
  • Investors are increasingly viewing India as a relatively safer bet amid global tech uncertainties.
  • HSBC has identified ICICI Bank, Adani Ports, and Titan Company among top picks with up to 37% upside.

MUMBAI: The global artificial intelligence trade, which has powered stock markets from New York to Seoul for over a year, is showing signs of strain. Shares of Alphabet, Meta Platforms, and SK Hynix have tumbled in recent sessions, raising a pressing question for Indian investors: how far will the ripple effects reach Dalal Street?

The selloff marks a sharp reversal from the euphoria that had pushed tech valuations to dizzying heights. For Indian markets, the impact is likely to be mixed, with IT and semiconductor-linked stocks facing immediate pressure, while domestic-focused sectors may escape the worst.

Global AI stocks under pressure

Alphabet and Meta, two of the biggest advertisers on the planet, have seen their shares slide as investors question whether massive capital expenditure on AI infrastructure will translate into proportional revenue growth. SK Hynix, a key supplier of high-bandwidth memory chips used in AI servers, has also taken a hit, reflecting concerns over demand sustainability.

This is not a uniform crash but a recalibration. The market is beginning to price in a slower payoff from AI investments than earlier assumed. For Indian IT services firms, which derive a significant chunk of revenue from global tech spending, this could mean tighter budgets at client firms in the coming quarters.

Indian IT and semiconductor exposure

Indian IT stocks such as TCS, Infosys, and HCL Technologies have historically tracked global tech sentiment. A prolonged global AI selloff could lead to delayed decision-making on outsourcing contracts, especially in the US and Europe. The impact may not be immediate, but the sentiment overhang is real.

On the semiconductor front, India's nascent chip ecosystem is more about long-term positioning than immediate revenue. The immediate effect is likely to be felt through foreign institutional investor (FII) flows, as global funds reduce risk exposure across emerging markets, including India.

Yet, there is a counter-narrative emerging. Several global investors are now warming up to India as a relatively safer destination. Vallum Capital's Manish Bhandari has noted that global investors see India as a stable bet amid the AI wreckage, thanks to its domestic consumption story and policy continuity.

Domestic stocks: The safe haven argument

HSBC, in a recent note, has picked ICICI Bank, Adani Ports, and Titan Company among its top 10 stock ideas, with an upside potential of 37%. These picks are largely domestic-facing, underscoring the belief that India's growth is not solely dependent on global tech cycles.

Banks, ports, and consumer discretionary names are less exposed to AI capex cuts. The broader Nifty index, while not immune to global selloffs, has a strong undercurrent of local buying, particularly from domestic institutional investors (DIIs) and retail participants.

US import bans, meanwhile, are proving to be a double-edged sword. While they could spur local manufacturing in certain sectors, they also create uncertainty for companies with global supply chains. This adds another layer of complexity for Indian firms navigating the current environment.

What should investors watch now?

The immediate trigger would be the next round of earnings from US tech majors. If Alphabet and Meta signal further cuts in AI spending, the selloff could deepen, pulling Indian IT stocks down with it. Conversely, any stabilisation in global tech shares would provide a floor for Indian markets.

Also on the radar is the movement of FII flows. Any sustained outflow from Indian equities would pressure the rupee and bond yields, even if domestic sectors remain relatively insulated.

For now, the consensus is that India is better positioned than most to weather the AI storm, but it is not entirely decoupled. The coming weeks will reveal whether this is a temporary correction or the beginning of a longer cycle of de-rating for tech-heavy portfolios.

Investors would do well to focus on sectors with strong domestic demand and visible earnings growth, rather than chasing global tech narratives. The AI trade may be cooling, but India's fundamental growth story remains intact, albeit with a few bumps along the way.
