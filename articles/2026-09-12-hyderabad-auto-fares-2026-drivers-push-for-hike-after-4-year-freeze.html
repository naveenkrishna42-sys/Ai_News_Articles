Hyderabad Auto Fares: The 2026 Demand Explained

Autorickshaw drivers across Hyderabad have renewed their demand for a fare revision, pointing out that the last notified hike came in 2022 and that operating costs have since climbed roughly 25-30%. Driver unions argue that the current meter rate does not cover CNG, insurance, spare parts, or daily maintenance. Commuters, meanwhile, are wary of paying more in a city where app-based cabs already compete aggressively on price.

This is not a sudden flashpoint. The demand has been building for over a year, with unions submitting representations to the Transport Department and staging token protests at regional transport authority (RTA) offices. The core dispute is arithmetic: what does it actually cost to run an auto in Hyderabad in 2026, and what is a fair metered rate?

What Drivers Pay Today

An autorickshaw driver in Hyderabad typically spends Rs 450-550 per day on CNG for a full shift, assuming 100-120 km of running. Add Rs 150-200 for maintenance and tyre wear, Rs 100-150 for insurance and permit amortisation, and Rs 250-350 for vehicle EMI or daily rent if the auto is not owned outright. That puts daily operating costs at roughly Rs 950-1,250 before the driver's own earnings.

Against this, a driver doing 10 metered trips of 5 km each at current rates earns about Rs 700-750 gross — before fuel and maintenance. That gap explains why many drivers prefer flat-rate negotiations over the meter, a practice the RTA has repeatedly tried to curb.

The headline number drivers cite is a base fare of Rs 30-35 for the first 1.5 km and Rs 16-18 per km thereafter. That would raise a typical 5 km trip from about Rs 70 to roughly Rs 90-95. Unions argue this is still below Ola and Uber's non-surge rates for comparable distances.

How a Fare Hike Actually Happens in Telangana

Auto fares are not set by the market. The Transport Department issues a notification under the Motor Vehicles Act after consulting unions, consumer groups, and the RTA. That process includes a public objections window. In practice, notified increases have historically landed below union demands — a 2022 revision, for instance, raised the base fare to Rs 25 and per-km rate to Rs 13, which unions then called inadequate.

A negotiated outcome in 2026 is most likely to be a Rs 3-5 increase in the base fare and a Rs 2-3 increase per km, with stricter enforcement of rate cards and meter use. That would be a 12-20% effective increase — meaningful for drivers, but far short of the 30% demanded.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Drivers' pain point: CNG, insurance, and spare parts have risen faster than the metered fare since 2022.
  • Commuters' concern: Flat-rate demands and 'minimum fare' claims already make metered rides unreliable in many areas.
  • Enforcement gap: Rate cards are mandatory, but RTA checks are sporadic; overcharging complaints rarely result in penalties.
  • App-cab pressure: Ola and Uber's non-surge pricing caps how high auto fares can realistically go without losing riders.
  • Timeline: No notification has been issued as of September 2026; the demand remains at the representation stage.

What This Means for Hyderabad Commuters

Until a notification is issued, the 2022 rates remain legally binding. Commuters should insist on the meter, ask for a printed rate card, and note the auto's registration number. Overcharging can be reported to the RTA helpline or via the official transport department portal.

If a hike is notified, expect it to be modest and phased. The bigger issue is not the headline fare but enforcement — a higher notified rate means little if drivers continue to negotiate flat rates. For drivers, the real gain will come from consistent metered trips, not just a higher number on paper.
