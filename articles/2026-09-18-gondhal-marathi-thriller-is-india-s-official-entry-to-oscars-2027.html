Gondhal: The Marathi Thriller That Conquered India's Oscar Race

The Film Federation of India has selected the Marathi-language thriller Gondhal as India's official entry for the 99th Academy Awards, set to be held in 2027. The announcement, made on Friday, caps a fiercely contested race that included big-budget Hindi drama Dhurandhar and the animated mythological Hanuman Ansh. The jury's decision underscores a decisive tilt towards regional, genre-defying storytelling over mainstream spectacle.

Directed by an emerging voice in Marathi cinema, Gondhal weaves folk tradition, superstition, and unfulfilled desire into a taut thriller. Its selection is not merely a win for Marathi cinema but a strategic bet on content that resonates globally for its raw, uncompromising authenticity. For Indian audiences and diaspora viewers, this is a moment to rally behind a film that refuses to play safe.

Why Gondhal Beat the Heavyweights

The Oscar entry race is never just about box office. Dhurandhar, a period action drama, had the backing of a major studio and a popular lead, while Hanuman Ansh capitalised on the success of animated mythologicals. Yet Gondhal prevailed because it offers something rarer: a distinctly Indian story told through a universal genre lens. The film's use of the traditional Gondhal folk performance—a ritualistic art form from Maharashtra—as a narrative device gives it a cultural specificity that Academy voters often reward.

Critics have praised the film's ambition. The Times of India called it "an ambitious attempt to merge folk tradition, desire, superstition and thriller," a sentiment echoed across reviews. Unlike previous Indian entries that leaned on historical grandeur or social messaging, Gondhal thrives on atmosphere, psychological tension, and a fearless performance by its lead. That combination proved irresistible to the selection committee.

Real-World Implications for Indian Cinema

For the Indian film industry, Gondhal's selection is a validation of the regional-first strategy. Marathi cinema has quietly produced some of India's most inventive films over the past decade, but Oscar recognition has often eluded it. This entry could open doors for more regional filmmakers to aim for the global stage without diluting their voice. Distributors and streaming platforms are already eyeing the film for international release, anticipating a surge in demand.

For global audiences, Gondhal offers a window into a lesser-known Indian tradition. The Gondhal ritual involves storytelling through dance and music, often performed to invoke deities. By embedding this into a thriller, the film makes the exotic accessible while retaining its mystery. That balance is precisely what international awards circuits tend to celebrate.

Pros and Cons: What Buyers and Viewers Should Know

  • Pros:
    • Authentic representation of Maharashtrian folk culture, adding fresh flavour to Indian cinema's global profile.
    • Strong critical acclaim for direction, atmosphere, and performances.
    • Strategic advantage: a genre film that avoids the clichés of previous Indian Oscar entries.
    • Potential for increased international distribution and streaming deals.
  • Cons:
    • Limited mainstream awareness outside Maharashtra; may need aggressive marketing for global voters.
    • Folk-horror is a niche genre, which could polarise audiences expecting traditional Indian musicals.
    • No confirmed release date for overseas theatres yet, creating uncertainty for diaspora viewers.

The Road to the 99th Oscars

Gondhal now enters the gruelling Oscar campaign season. It will need to secure screenings for Academy members, build buzz through festivals, and possibly secure a US distributor. The film's team has not yet announced a strategy, but the selection itself is a major milestone. India has never won the Best International Feature Oscar, and while Gondhal faces stiff competition, its distinct voice gives it a fighting chance.

For now, the focus is on celebrating a win for Marathi cinema and for risk-taking storytelling. As the 99th Academy Awards approach in 2027, Gondhal carries the hopes of an industry eager to prove that its regional gems can shine on the world's biggest stage.
