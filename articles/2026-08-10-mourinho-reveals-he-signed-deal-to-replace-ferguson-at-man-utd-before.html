Mourinho reveals he signed deal to replace Ferguson at Man Utd before Chelsea U-turn

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mourinho says he agreed to succeed Ferguson at Manchester United in 2013.
  • A signed deal was in place before Mourinho changed his mind and returned to Chelsea.
  • The revelation comes 13 years after Ferguson's retirement, reigniting debate over what might have been.

A secret agreement that never came to pass

Jose Mourinho has finally broken his silence on one of football's most tantalising what-ifs. The Portuguese manager has revealed that he signed a deal to replace Sir Alex Ferguson at Manchester United in 2013, only to pull out at the eleventh hour and return to Chelsea instead.

The confession, made public this week, offers a rare glimpse into the chaotic negotiations that followed Ferguson's retirement announcement after 26 years at Old Trafford. Mourinho, then at Real Madrid, was widely considered the favourite to take over. But the story, as now told, was far more advanced than anyone knew.

The deal was done — then it wasn't

According to Mourinho, the agreement with United was fully signed and sealed. He was set to become the natural heir to the most successful manager in English football history. Yet, in a dramatic twist, he chose to walk away and return to Stamford Bridge, where he had already built a legacy between 2004 and 2007.

Why the U-turn? Mourinho has framed it as a decision driven by emotion rather than logic. He has described it as a choice from the heart, not the head — a rare admission from a manager known for his calculated, often ruthless approach to his career.

For United fans, the revelation cuts deep. David Moyes was eventually handed the poisoned chalice, lasting less than a season. The club has lurched from one manager to another since, with no one able to replicate Ferguson's dominance. Mourinho himself would eventually take the job in 2016, but by then the dynamic was different, and his tenure ended in acrimony in 2018.

Betrayal or a decision from the heart?

The question now being asked across football circles is whether Mourinho's decision was a betrayal of Ferguson's trust or a deeply personal choice. Ferguson had handpicked his successor, and the expectation was that Mourinho would carry the torch. Instead, he opted for the comfort of a club where he was already adored.

Critics argue that Mourinho's move to Chelsea set in motion a chain of events that destabilised United for years. Supporters of the Portuguese, however, point out that his return to Chelsea was a logical career move at the time — he knew the club, the league, and had unfinished business in England.

Mourinho's own words suggest a man still reflecting on the decision more than a decade later. He has not gone into exhaustive detail about the conversations that took place, but the admission itself is significant. It confirms what many had suspected: that the United job was his if he wanted it.

What this means for his legacy

The revelation adds another layer to Mourinho's complex relationship with Manchester United. He remains one of the few managers to have won a major trophy at the club since Ferguson, lifting the Europa League in 2017. Yet his time there was marked by tension, and he was sacked after a poor start to the 2018-19 season.

Had he taken the job in 2013, his legacy might look very different. He would have inherited a squad that had just won the Premier League title, with players like Rio Ferdinand, Nemanja Vidic, and Wayne Rooney still in their prime. Instead, he spent three more years at Chelsea, winning another league title, before the United job finally came around under less favourable circumstances.

For now, Mourinho's confession has reignited debate among fans and pundits alike. Was this the moment that defined United's decline? Or was it simply a manager making the right call for himself at the time?

What remains clear is that football's rumour mill will have plenty to chew on in the coming days. Mourinho has not indicated whether he regrets the decision, but the timing of this revelation — 13 years on — suggests it still weighs on him.

As the story continues to develop, one thing is certain: the question of what might have been at Old Trafford will not go away quietly.
