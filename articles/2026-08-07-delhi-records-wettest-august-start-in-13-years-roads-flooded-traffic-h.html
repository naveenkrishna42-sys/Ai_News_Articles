Delhi records wettest August start in 13 years; roads flooded, traffic hit

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi records wettest August start in 13 years
  • Non-stop rain causes waterlogging, traffic chaos across NCR
  • IMD issues red alert as more rainfall expected
  • Residents resort to makeshift rafts to ferry children through flooded streets

New Delhi: The national capital is reeling under its wettest August start in 13 years, with relentless rainfall since the beginning of the month throwing daily life out of gear. Roads across Delhi-NCR have turned into waterbodies, traffic has slowed to a crawl, and the India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued a red alert for the region, warning of more downpours in the coming days.

The city, which usually sees a brief respite from the monsoon by early August, has instead witnessed continuous showers that have overwhelmed drainage systems. Several arterial roads, including stretches near ITO, Minto Bridge, and the Ring Road, remain waterlogged, forcing commuters to wade through knee-deep water or abandon their vehicles altogether.

Life Grinds to a Halt

Cab services have reported a sharp drop in availability, with drivers refusing to venture into flooded zones. Auto-rickshaws, the lifeline of the city's last-mile connectivity, are few and far between, leaving thousands of office-goers stranded at bus stops and metro stations.

In several low-lying areas, residents have taken matters into their own hands. Videos circulating on social media show locals using makeshift rafts to ferry schoolchildren through flooded streets, a desperate measure that underscores the severity of the situation. The city's civic bodies, often criticised for their monsoon preparedness, have deployed pumps and rescue teams, but the sheer volume of water has stretched resources thin.

IMD Red Alert and Forecast

The IMD has categorised the rainfall as the heaviest for the first week of August in 13 years, a record that highlights the unusual intensity of this year's monsoon. The red alert, the highest warning level, signals the likelihood of extremely heavy rainfall, and the weather office has advised residents to stay indoors unless absolutely necessary.

Forecasters predict that the rain-bearing system, which has been stationary over northwest India, will persist for at least another 48 hours. This means more waterlogging, more traffic disruptions, and a continued strain on essential services like power supply, which has seen intermittent outages in several neighbourhoods.

Official Response and Public Concerns

The Delhi government has pressed emergency control rooms into round-the-clock operation, and teams from the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) have been kept on standby. However, officials have not yet confirmed any casualties or major damage, even as reports of submerged underpasses and fallen trees trickle in from across the city.

Residents, meanwhile, are voicing frustration over the lack of proactive planning. Social media is abuzz with complaints about poor drainage maintenance, unresponsive helplines, and the perennial problem of urban flooding that resurfaces every monsoon. The situation has also raised questions about the city's preparedness for climate-induced extreme weather, which scientists say is becoming more frequent.

What Lies Ahead

With the monsoon trough expected to remain active, Delhiites are bracing for more wet days. The IMD's forecast suggests a gradual reduction in intensity from mid-week, but the immediate concern is the next 24 to 48 hours, during which heavy spells are likely to continue.

All eyes are now on the city's drainage and disaster management systems to see how they cope, and whether the authorities can prevent a repeat of the chaos witnessed on the roads this week. For now, the capital remains at the mercy of the skies.
