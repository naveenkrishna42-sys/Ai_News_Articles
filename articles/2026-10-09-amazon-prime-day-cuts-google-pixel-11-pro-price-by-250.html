Amazon Prime Day Cuts Google Pixel 11 Pro Price by $250

Amazon Prime Deal Details

Amazon’s Prime Day event lists the Google Pixel 11 Pro at $899, a $250 reduction from its presumed launch price of $1,149. The discount appears on the 128 GB variant, with the 256 GB model seeing a similar proportional cut. The offer is available only to Prime members and runs for a limited window, after which the price returns to the standard rate.

What to Expect from the Pixel 11 Pro

Display and Design

Leaks suggest a 6.7‑inch LTPO OLED panel with a 120 Hz adaptive refresh rate, peak brightness around 1,600 nits, and a slightly thinner chassis than the Pixel 8 Pro. The device is expected to retain the familiar matte aluminum frame and glossy glass back, available in multiple color options.

Camera System

The rear camera suite is rumored to feature a 50 MP main sensor with larger pixel size, a 48 MP ultrawide lens, and a 48 MP telephoto offering 5× optical zoom. Front‑facing camera details point to a 12 MP sensor capable of 4K video at 60 fps. Software enhancements are likely to build on Google’s computational photography strengths.

Performance and Battery

Although the exact chipset has not been confirmed, industry sources indicate the phone will ship with Google’s next‑generation Tensor series processor, paired with 12 GB of LPDDR5X RAM. Battery capacity is anticipated to be around 5,200 mAh, supporting 30 W wired charging and 23 W wireless charging.

Pricing History and Value Proposition

Google’s flagship line has historically seen price drops of $100–$200 a few months after launch, making a $250 reduction notable. Compared to the Pixel 8 Pro’s launch price of $999, the Pixel 11 Pro’s discounted $899 brings it closer to the mid‑tier segment while promising flagship‑level features.

  • Strong Android update guarantee (minimum five years)

Should You Buy Now?

For users prioritizing timely software updates and a capable camera system, the $250 discount makes the Pixel 11 Pro an attractive option, provided the rumored specifications hold true. Those who require verified benchmark data or prefer established chipsets may wait for post‑launch reviews before committing.
