Microsoft denies Windows 11 spies on users every 15 minutes

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Microsoft denies Windows 11 secretly collects data every 15 minutes
  • Company says telemetry features are transparent and user-controllable
  • Privacy advocates remain skeptical despite assurances

Privacy Allegations Surface

Microsoft has officially denied claims that Windows 11 secretly collects user data every 15 minutes. The denial comes after reports circulated online suggesting the operating system runs hidden surveillance in the background, sparking fresh privacy concerns among users.

The controversy began when tech analysts pointed to certain diagnostic processes in Windows 11 that appear to activate at regular intervals. Social media posts amplified the claims, with some users describing the behavior as 'spying' and demanding immediate clarification from the company.

Microsoft's Response

In a statement, a Microsoft spokesperson dismissed the allegations as a misunderstanding of how telemetry and diagnostic features work. The company maintains that data collection is limited to system health and performance metrics, not personal files or browsing activity.

According to Microsoft, the 15-minute interval refers to a routine check for system updates and security patches, not a surveillance loop. The company also reiterated that users can adjust privacy settings or disable non-essential data sharing entirely through the Settings menu.

Telemetry Transparency Debate

This is not the first time Microsoft has faced scrutiny over Windows telemetry. Earlier this year, the company promised to improve transparency around data collection, following criticism from privacy groups and regulators. The latest denial suggests those commitments remain under the microscope.

Privacy experts note that Windows 11's default settings still share diagnostic data with Microsoft unless users manually opt out. While the company insists this data is anonymised and used only for product improvement, sceptics argue the line between helpful telemetry and invasive tracking can blur.

What Users Can Do

  • Open Settings > Privacy & Security > Diagnostics & Feedback
  • Choose 'Required diagnostic data' instead of 'Optional'
  • Turn off 'Tailored experiences' to limit targeted prompts
  • Review activity history and clear stored diagnostic data periodically

Microsoft has also pointed to its March commitment to simplify privacy controls, promising clearer explanations of what each setting does. The company says it has made progress but acknowledges more work lies ahead.

Industry Reaction

Tech reviewers have weighed in, with some defending Microsoft's stance while others urge caution. A recent test by PCMag highlighted that Windows 11 on 8GB RAM machines can feel sluggish, adding to user frustration over system overhead. ZDNET has also reported that Microsoft is aware of user annoyance and is exploring ways to make the OS less intrusive.

The timing of these denials is notable, coming just months after Microsoft pledged better memory efficiency and a smoother user experience. The company appears keen to avoid a repeat of past privacy controversies that damaged trust in its products.

What Happens Next

Microsoft has not announced any changes to Windows 11's telemetry practices, but the company says it will continue to review feedback. Users can expect further clarity in upcoming feature updates, likely in the next patch release. For now, the onus remains on individuals to review and adjust their privacy settings.

As the debate unfolds, regulators may also take a closer look at how operating systems handle diagnostic data. Microsoft's next move could set a precedent for the entire industry.
