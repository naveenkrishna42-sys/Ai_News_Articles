Gold Rate Today (October 10): what 18K, 22K and 24K really mean

Gold rates in India are usually quoted per gram for specific purity—24 carat (99.9%), 22 carat (91.6%) and 18 carat (75%). When you compare prices across cities like Chennai, Mumbai, Delhi and Kolkata, the headline number is only the starting point; jewellery billing typically adds making charges, GST and wastage/return policies that can change your “all-in” cost.

For buyers, the practical question is not just “how much per gram today,” but “what is my effective ₹/g cost after charges, and will it protect my resale value?” A strategy that ignores purity math and retail billing details can easily erase any perceived benefit from a lower displayed gold rate.

City-wise gold rates: how to compare across markets

Gold is traded in global benchmarks, but the price you see in different Indian cities reflects local supply, retailer pricing conventions, and currency moves. Even when the underlying gold market is stable, two shops can show materially different final prices because of making charge slabs, hallmark/assay handling fees and the way they estimate gold loss during making.

Today’s listings from major price-watching sources typically help you locate the base “rate” for your desired carat. Use those numbers to cross-check what retailers quote for jewellery, not just for bullion. If a retailer quotes a low rate but higher making and GST burden, your net outlay may be higher than a competitor offering a slightly higher base rate.

Purity math quick check (buyer’s benchmark)

Since 22K and 18K are purity-reduced, their “per gram” prices should generally scale down from 24K. A fast sanity check before you buy: if 24K is priced at P, then ideal purity-adjusted expectations are roughly 22K ≈ 22/24 of P and 18K ≈ 18/24 of P (actual spreads vary because of retailer pricing and demand). When the quoted 22K or 18K deviates sharply from this sanity band, ask for the calculation sheet and compare making charges across quotes.

What’s driving gold rates today (and what buyers should watch)

Daily gold movement in India is influenced by global price action and currency—especially the US dollar—plus risk sentiment. When the US dollar weakens, gold often becomes cheaper for buyers abroad, which can support local prices. Conversely, stronger dollar conditions can pressure gold despite steady domestic demand.

For a buying decision on Oct 10, your best “signal dashboard” is not predictions from a single headline, but the direction of USD and global yields, combined with domestic demand patterns (wedding season and festive buying tend to lift premiums). If you’re planning a purchase over multiple days, note that gold can swing with global cues even when local retail rates appear stable.

Value justification: jewellery vs bullion

If your priority is long-term value, bullion generally offers tighter alignment between purity and price. Jewellery, by contrast, carries design complexity and making charges that are not fully recovered on resale. When you’re comparing offers, calculate your effective ₹/g by adding making charges and GST to the base gold cost, then compare across retailers using the same carat and estimated weight-to-weight wastage rules.

Sell-on-rise vs buy-now: a practical framework for Indian buyers

Many buyers track “sell on rise” ideas when markets move up. The risk is that gold’s short-term rallies can reverse quickly, and jewellery resale typically discounts value due to buyer margins and purity verification costs. A more reliable approach is to align the purchase to your time horizon: if you need jewellery for an event, “timing the dip” is secondary to getting the correct carat and total pricing.

If you’re buying for investment, decide whether you want liquidity and minimal charges (bullion/coins) or cultural/usage value (jewellery). For both, consider staggered buying rather than a single decision—especially around periods of heightened global volatility.

  • Pros of buying jewellery: immediate utility, design flexibility, often clearer workmanship options.
  • Cons of jewellery resale: making charges and design premium are usually not fully recoverable.
  • Pros of bullion: closer purity-price correlation and usually lower non-gold costs.
  • Cons of bullion: no design/utility value; storage and liquidity costs still matter.

How to use today’s 18K/22K/24K rates without getting misled

Start with the base rate for your target carat from credible daily listings, then request a “net bill” estimate from the retailer in writing: gold price component, making charges, GST and any wastage policy. If a shop quotes a “discounted gold rate,” confirm whether it is discounting the base component only or bundling charges elsewhere.

Finally, keep your receipt, hallmark details and measurement method. For future resale, documentation and purity confirmation are often what determines how smoothly you can convert metal value back into cash—especially when comparing offers across cities.
