Gujarat Chandipura Virus: 22 Child Deaths, 7 Undergoing Treatment

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • 22 children have died of Chandipura virus in Gujarat this monsoon.
  • Seven children are currently under treatment for the infection.
  • Gujarat's health machinery has been placed on high alert following the outbreak.

Officials Confirm Rising Toll

Gujarat's health minister has confirmed that 22 children have died of Chandipura virus infection in the state this monsoon season. Seven more children are currently under treatment, with officials closely monitoring their condition.

The announcement came as the state's health machinery was placed on high alert following the outbreak. Medical teams have been deployed across affected districts, with a focus on early detection and prompt care.

What Is Chandipura Virus?

Chandipura virus is a mosquito-borne viral infection that primarily affects children, often leading to acute encephalitis. The disease progresses rapidly, with symptoms including sudden fever, convulsions, and altered consciousness.

Health experts note that the virus is known to cause seasonal outbreaks during the monsoon, when vector breeding peaks. Early medical intervention is considered critical to improving survival rates.

Government Response and Surveillance

State health authorities have intensified surveillance in the affected regions. Rapid response teams have been constituted to conduct door-to-door checks and refer suspected cases to designated treatment centres.

Officials have also ramped up vector control measures, including fogging and larvicide application in high-risk areas. Awareness campaigns are being run to educate parents on the early signs of the infection.

While the health department has not yet released a district-wise break-up of the deaths, sources indicate that the outbreak is spread across several districts in the state.

Challenges Ahead

The rapid progression of the disease poses a challenge for rural healthcare centres, which often lack paediatric intensive care facilities. Doctors are urging families to seek immediate medical help if a child develops fever with neurological symptoms.

Public health experts are also calling for enhanced laboratory capacity to confirm cases faster. Currently, diagnosis relies on clinical presentation and limited testing, which can delay response in remote areas.

As the monsoon continues, the state's health machinery remains on high alert. Officials say they are prepared to scale up resources if fresh cases emerge in the coming weeks.

All eyes will now be on the recovery of the seven children under treatment, and on whether the state's containment measures can curb further spread before the season ends.
