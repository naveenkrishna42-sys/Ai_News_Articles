US Imposes New Tariffs On 60 Countries Over Forced Labour Concerns

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • The US imposed 10% tariffs on goods from 60 countries over forced labour concerns.
  • India is among the nations affected, with the tariffs targeting key export sectors.
  • The policy aims to penalise imports linked to forced labour, raising trade tensions.

The United States has announced a sweeping new tariff regime, imposing a 10% levy on goods imported from 60 countries, including India, over concerns related to forced labour. The move, reported by multiple outlets including NDTV and The Guardian, marks a significant escalation in trade enforcement under the current administration.

Tariffs Aimed At Curbing Forced Labour Imports

The tariffs, described by officials as a punitive measure, target countries where labour practices have been flagged as problematic. The 10% rate applies to a broad range of goods, though specific industries are likely to be more heavily affected. The US government has framed the policy as a necessary step to protect human rights and ensure fair competition.

According to reports, the decision follows a review of trade practices and labour conditions in partner nations. The White House has not released a detailed list of products covered, but analysts expect sectors such as textiles, electronics, and agriculture to face the brunt of the new duties.

Impact On India's Export Sector

India is among the 60 nations hit by the tariffs, a development that could strain bilateral trade relations. The 10% levy on Indian goods is likely to affect key export items, including garments, leather products, and processed foods. Industry bodies in India have expressed concern over the potential loss of competitiveness in the US market.

Commerce ministry officials in New Delhi are assessing the impact. The move comes at a time when India has been pushing for stronger economic ties with the US, including a proposed free trade agreement. The tariffs could complicate those negotiations.

Broader Implications For Global Trade

The US action extends beyond India, covering a diverse group of nations from Asia to Africa. The Guardian reported that the tariffs apply to more than 80 countries, though other sources put the figure at 60. The discrepancy suggests that the policy may have multiple tiers or exemptions.

Trade experts warn that the move could trigger retaliatory measures from affected countries, potentially sparking a new round of trade disputes. The World Trade Organization has not yet commented, but member nations are expected to challenge the tariffs as protectionist.

The forced labour allegation is a recurring point of contention in global trade. The US has previously imposed similar restrictions on specific products, but this is the first time such a broad tariff has been applied across multiple nations.

The immediate effect on consumers in the US remains unclear. Importers may pass on the additional costs, leading to higher prices for goods ranging from clothing to electronics. The administration argues that the long-term benefit of ethical sourcing outweighs short-term price hikes.

As the policy takes effect, businesses on both sides are bracing for disruption. Indian exporters are expected to seek clarifications from the government on how to comply with the new rules. The coming weeks will reveal whether diplomatic channels can mitigate the economic fallout.
