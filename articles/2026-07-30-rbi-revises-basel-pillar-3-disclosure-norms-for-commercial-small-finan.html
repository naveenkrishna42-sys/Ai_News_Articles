RBI revises Basel Pillar 3 disclosure norms for commercial, small finance, payments banks

RBI tightens disclosure norms under Basel III framework

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has issued a revised Basel III Pillar 3 disclosure framework for commercial banks, small finance banks and payments banks. The move is aimed at enhancing transparency, market discipline and comparability of disclosures across the banking sector.

The revised framework aligns with the latest Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (BCBS) standards. It introduces new templates and tables for key risk areas including credit risk, leverage ratio and liquidity coverage ratio.

Banks will now be required to provide more granular data on their capital adequacy, risk exposures and risk management practices. The RBI said the revised norms will help market participants better assess a bank's risk profile and capital adequacy.

Key changes in the revised framework

The updated disclosure requirements cover several new areas. Banks must now disclose the composition of their capital instruments, including the features of Additional Tier 1 and Tier 2 instruments. They also need to provide detailed information on credit risk mitigation techniques and counterparty credit risk.

For leverage ratio, banks will have to disclose the total exposure measure and the leverage ratio itself, along with a reconciliation with the balance sheet. The liquidity coverage ratio disclosures now require banks to report the components of high-quality liquid assets and net cash outflows.

The framework also introduces new disclosure requirements for market risk, operational risk and interest rate risk in the banking book. Banks must disclose the capital requirements for each risk type and the methodologies used to calculate them.

Compliance timeline and applicability

The revised framework will apply to all commercial banks, small finance banks and payments banks operating in India. The new disclosure requirements will come into effect from the financial year 2025-26 onwards.

Banks will need to publish these disclosures on their official websites and in their annual financial reports. The RBI has said that banks must ensure the disclosures are consistent with the regulatory reporting submissions.

The central bank expects the revised framework to promote greater market discipline and improve the quality of risk information available to stakeholders. Banks have been given time to prepare their systems and processes to meet the new requirements.

Alignment with global standards

The RBI's move is part of India's commitment to adopt international best practices in banking regulation. The revised Pillar 3 framework is aligned with the BCBS standards issued in 2023, which aimed to improve the comparability and consistency of bank disclosures globally.

India has been progressively implementing Basel III norms since 2013. The central bank has been updating the disclosure framework periodically to keep pace with evolving global standards and domestic banking sector developments.

Banking experts say the revised norms will bring Indian banks' disclosure practices in line with those of their global peers. This could improve investor confidence and facilitate better risk assessment by analysts and rating agencies.

The RBI has not yet commented on whether the revised framework will be extended to other types of banks, such as cooperative banks or regional rural banks, in the future.
