Chennai disability activists to draft plan for accessible bus stops

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Activists will develop a comprehensive accessibility roadmap for bus stops in Chennai.
  • Current infrastructure often lacks ramps, tactile paving, and proper signage for persons with disabilities.
  • The plan aims to push for concrete timelines and implementation by civic authorities.

Activists take charge of accessibility push

Disability rights activists in Chennai are preparing to draft a detailed plan for making bus stops across the city fully accessible. The initiative comes after years of complaints about broken ramps, missing tactile tiles, and poorly placed signage that make public transport difficult for persons with disabilities to use.

The group includes members from several non-profits and advocacy organisations. They plan to survey existing bus stops, identify gaps, and propose standardised designs that meet Indian accessibility norms. The draft will be presented to the Greater Chennai Corporation and the Metropolitan Transport Corporation for implementation.

Current infrastructure falls short

Many bus stops in Chennai lack basic features such as kerb ramps, contrasting colours for visually impaired commuters, and audible announcements. Even those with tactile paving often have broken tiles or are blocked by parked vehicles and street vendors. This forces wheelchair users and people with visual impairments to navigate dangerous traffic or skip the bus altogether.

Rights groups have repeatedly pointed out that the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 mandates equal access to public infrastructure. Despite this, enforcement remains weak. The activists argue that a clear, costed plan with deadlines is necessary to turn legal obligations into real change.

Focus on universal design and enforcement

The proposed plan will include specifications for ramp gradients, width of pathways, height of signboards, and placement of tactile indicators. It will also recommend regular maintenance schedules and penalties for encroachments that block accessibility features. Activists want the final blueprint to be adopted as a standard for all future bus stop upgrades.

Some city councillors have expressed support for the move. However, officials point to budget constraints and the challenge of retrofitting old stops. The activists counter that incremental upgrades are possible and that accessibility cannot be treated as an afterthought.

What happens next

The group expects to finalise the draft within two months. They will then hold consultations with the corporation, transport department, and disability rights experts before submitting the final proposal. The success of the plan will depend on whether the authorities accept it as a binding roadmap rather than just another recommendation.
