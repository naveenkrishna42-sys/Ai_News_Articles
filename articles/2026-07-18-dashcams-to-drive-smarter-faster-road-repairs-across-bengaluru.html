Dashcams to drive smarter, faster road repairs across Bengaluru

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BBMP will install dashcams on garbage trucks, water tankers and other civic vehicles to monitor road conditions.
  • Footage will be analysed using AI to detect potholes, cracks and other damage in real time.
  • The system aims to reduce manual inspection time and speed up repair response across the city.

Bengaluru's civic body is turning to dashboard cameras to spot potholes and track road damage across the city. The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) plans to install dashcams on its fleet of garbage trucks, water tankers and other municipal vehicles.

The cameras will capture road conditions as these vehicles go about their daily rounds. Officials say the footage will be analysed using artificial intelligence to detect potholes, cracks, uneven surfaces and other problems in real time.

How the system will work

The dashcams will record video continuously while the vehicles are on the move. This footage will be fed into a central system where AI algorithms will scan for signs of road damage. The system can identify the exact location of a pothole or crack using GPS data embedded in the video feed.

Once a problem is flagged, the system will automatically generate a repair request and send it to the concerned BBMP ward office. The goal is to cut down the time between spotting a pothole and fixing it. Currently, road inspections are done manually by BBMP staff, which is slow and often misses many issues.

Faster response, better roads

BBMP officials believe this data-driven approach will make road repairs more efficient. Instead of waiting for complaints from the public, the civic body will have a constant stream of information about road conditions across the city. This could mean quicker repairs and fewer potholes left unattended for weeks.

The move comes as Bengaluru struggles with badly maintained roads, especially during the monsoon season. Potholes cause traffic jams, accidents and damage to vehicles. Residents have long complained about the slow response of the BBMP in fixing roads.

Pilot phase and rollout

The BBMP plans to begin with a pilot project covering a few wards. A small number of vehicles will be fitted with dashcams to test the system. If the pilot is successful, the civic body will expand the programme to cover the entire city.

Officials have not yet confirmed the cost of the project or the timeline for the full rollout. They are also working on data privacy guidelines, as the cameras will capture footage of public spaces and possibly people on the streets.

Other cities have tried similar tech

Using dashcams and AI for road maintenance is not new. Several cities in the United States and Europe have adopted similar systems. In India, some municipalities have experimented with the idea but none have implemented it on a large scale. Bengaluru could become the first Indian city to use this technology citywide.

The BBMP is also exploring whether the same footage can be used for other purposes, such as monitoring garbage collection routes or tracking the movement of civic vehicles. This could help improve overall efficiency of municipal services.

Residents and road safety experts have welcomed the plan. They say any effort to speed up road repairs is a step in the right direction. However, they caution that the system will only work if the BBMP acts quickly on the data generated. Simply collecting more information without fixing roads will not solve the problem.

The BBMP is expected to release more details about the pilot project in the coming weeks. If all goes as planned, Bengaluru's roads could soon be monitored by a network of cameras, with potholes getting fixed before most people even notice them.
