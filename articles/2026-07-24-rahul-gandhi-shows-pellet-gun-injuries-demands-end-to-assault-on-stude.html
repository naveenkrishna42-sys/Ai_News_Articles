Rahul Gandhi Shows Pellet Gun Injuries, Demands End to 'Assault on Students'

📅 2026-07-24 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rahul Gandhi displayed shrapnel injuries of a student protester in Parliament.
  • Medical reports confirm pellet gun use, with 'shards stuck to skin'.
  • RAF faces allegations of excessive force; HC has refused an NIA probe into the protest.
  • Opposition demands a parliamentary inquiry and immediate end to use of pellet guns on students.

Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi on Friday displayed graphic medical reports and images of pellet gun injuries sustained by a student protester during a crackdown on a July 20 march in New Delhi. Addressing Parliament, Gandhi held up a hospital report that described multiple metallic shards embedded in the skin of a young protester.

“Stop assaulting our students,” Gandhi demanded, directing his remarks at the central government. “This is not law and order. This is an attack on the future of the nation.”

Medical Evidence of Pellet Gun Use

Gandhi presented a document from a city hospital that listed injuries consistent with the use of pellet guns. The report, accessed by news agencies, noted that the patient had “multiple small, radiopaque foreign bodies” lodged in the subcutaneous tissue of the limbs and torso. Doctors recorded the condition as “shards stuck to skin” from what they described as probable pellet gun discharge.

The injured student was among hundreds who took part in the so-called “Cockroach March” to Parliament on July 20, protesting against alleged irregularities in the Common University Entrance Test (CUET) and other education policies. The march turned violent after police attempted to disperse the crowd, leading to several arrests and injuries.

RAF Faces Allegations of Excessive Force

The Rapid Action Force (RAF), a central paramilitary unit, has been accused of using excessive force during the crackdown. Reports from the scene indicate that in addition to pellet guns, personnel used batons fitted with nails and electric shock devices to break up the crowd.

Multiple protesters have come forward with injuries consistent with these weapons. Medical affidavits filed by lawyers representing the students describe “batons with nails” and “electric shocks” being used indiscriminately. The RAF has not yet issued a detailed response to the specific allegations, though officials have confirmed an internal inquiry is underway.

High Court Refuses NIA Probe

In a related development, the Delhi High Court on Friday refused to order a National Investigation Agency (NIA) probe into the July 20 protest. The court was hearing a petition from a student group that alleged the protest was being mischaracterised as a law and order problem. The bench observed that while the injuries were serious, the matter did not meet the threshold for an NIA investigation unless evidence of a larger conspiracy emerged.

“The court has left the door open for further legal recourse, but for now, the police will continue their own investigation,” said a lawyer representing the petitioners. The government has maintained that the protest was illegal and that force was used only after protesters turned violent and attempted to storm the Parliament complex.

Political Fallout and Demand for Inquiry

Gandhi’s strong words in Parliament have escalated the political confrontation. He demanded a joint parliamentary committee probe into the incident and called for an immediate ban on the use of pellet guns in crowd control. “You cannot treat students like enemies of the state,” he said.

The ruling party has dismissed Gandhi’s claims as an attempt to politicise a routine law enforcement operation. Union ministers have argued that the police were forced to act to protect public property and parliamentary security. No official statement has been issued by the Home Ministry on the specific allegations of pellet gun use.

The opposition has indicated it will raise the issue again when Parliament reconvenes next week.

All eyes are now on the RAF’s internal inquiry report, expected within a fortnight, which will determine whether the force used was proportionate.
