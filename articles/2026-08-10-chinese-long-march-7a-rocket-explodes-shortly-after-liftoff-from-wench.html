Chinese Long March 7A rocket explodes shortly after liftoff from Wenchang

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Long March 7A rocket explodes 90 seconds after liftoff from Wenchang spaceport
  • Mission was carrying a communications satellite; payload lost
  • China's state news agency confirms flight anomaly; investigation underway

Rocket explodes minutes into flight

A Chinese Long March 7A rocket exploded less than 90 seconds after lifting off from the Wenchang Satellite Launch Center on Monday, in a rare failure for one of the country's most frequently used launch vehicles. The rocket was carrying a communications satellite, but state media confirmed the mission failed after a flight anomaly.

Video footage circulating on social media shows the rocket climbing normally before a sudden bright flash, followed by a massive fireball. Debris fell into the surrounding area, though authorities have not yet reported any casualties or damage on the ground.

State media confirms anomaly

China's state news agency said the launch failed after the rocket experienced an unspecified anomaly. The Long March 7A is a variant of the Long March 7, a workhorse rocket used for both commercial and government missions. It has been launched successfully in recent years, but this failure marks a setback for China's rapidly expanding space program.

The satellite on board was intended for communications services, but officials have not disclosed its exact purpose or operator. The loss of the payload is expected to affect planned services, though no further details have been released.

Investigation underway

Chinese space authorities have launched an investigation into the failure. Past launch failures in China have led to temporary suspensions of related rocket models, and this incident is likely to prompt a review of the Long March 7A's systems and launch procedures.

The Wenchang spaceport, located on Hainan Island, is one of China's newest launch sites and has been used for high-profile missions, including crewed spaceflights and lunar probes. This is the first major failure at the site in recent years.

Impact on China's space ambitions

China has ramped up its launch cadence in recent years, aiming to rival the United States and other spacefaring nations. The Long March 7A is designed for geostationary orbit missions, a key capability for communications and weather satellites. A prolonged grounding of the rocket could delay several planned launches.

Global space analysts are watching the investigation closely. The failure is unlikely to derail China's broader space goals, such as its space station program or lunar exploration plans, but it highlights the risks inherent in rocket launches.

What's next

The investigation's findings will determine when the Long March 7A returns to flight. Meanwhile, China's space agency is expected to release more details in the coming days. For now, the focus remains on understanding what went wrong and ensuring future missions are safe.
