Cyclone Arnab Leaves Trail of Damage in Andhra Pradesh

Cyclone Arnab made landfall as a severe cyclonic storm near Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh on the morning of October 28, 2026, bringing with it fierce winds, torrential rain, and significant disruption. While the storm rapidly weakened post-landfall, the immediate human toll stands at three confirmed fatalities within the state. Initial assessments indicate considerable damage to coastal infrastructure, agricultural lands, and critical utility networks, necessitating a swift and coordinated response from state and central authorities.

The cyclonic event prompted widespread concern across the Bay of Bengal region, with neighbouring states also bracing for potential impact. However, the storm's trajectory and subsequent de-intensification primarily confined its most severe effects to specific coastal districts of Andhra Pradesh, allowing for a more focused relief effort.

Immediate Impact and Government Response

Fatalities and Displacement

The three reported deaths occurred in areas directly impacted by the cyclone's initial fury, underscoring the inherent dangers of such extreme weather phenomena. Beyond the tragic loss of life, thousands of residents were displaced from their homes, with many seeking refuge in government-operated relief camps. These camps, activated in anticipation of the cyclone, provided essential shelter, food, and medical aid to over a hundred thousand individuals evacuated from low-lying and vulnerable coastal communities.

Initial surveys by disaster response teams highlight extensive damage to kutcha houses, uprooted trees, and localized flooding in several mandals. Power supply disruptions were widespread across the affected districts, complicating early recovery efforts and communication. Road networks also suffered, with blockages due to fallen debris and waterlogging hindering immediate access for emergency services.

Chief Minister Seeks Central Aid

Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N. Chandrababu Naidu promptly communicated with Union Home Minister Amit Shah, formally requesting immediate Central assistance to support the state's extensive relief and rehabilitation efforts. The request underscores the significant financial burden that such a disaster places on state resources, particularly in the critical phases of rescue, relief, and subsequent long-term rebuilding. The state administration has mobilized its entire machinery, including the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) teams, for ongoing search, rescue, and restoration operations.

Meteorological Trajectory and Weakening

Cyclone Arnab's journey across the Bay of Bengal culminated in its classification as a severe cyclonic storm just before landfall. Meteorological data indicated sustained wind speeds upwards of 120 kmph at the time of impact near Narsapur. The precise timing and location of landfall were crucial in guiding pre-emptive measures, including the large-scale evacuations that likely averted a higher casualty count.

Post-landfall, the storm rapidly lost intensity, downgrading from a severe cyclonic storm to a cyclonic storm, and further into a deep depression over the subsequent 12 hours. This rapid weakening trajectory significantly mitigated the broader threat to eastern cities. Kolkata, for instance, which had been under a weather watch, experienced only moderate rain and gusty winds, rather than the severe conditions predicted for a direct hit. This de-intensification is a common phenomenon as cyclones lose their energy source (warm ocean waters) and encounter frictional forces over land.

Historical Context: Lessons from Hudhud

The arrival of Cyclone Arnab inevitably evoked stark comparisons with Cyclone Hudhud, which devastated Visakhapatnam and other parts of Andhra Pradesh in October 2014. While Arnab's immediate impact, particularly in terms of casualties, appears less severe, the memory of Hudhud's fury served as a critical impetus for heightened preparedness. Lessons learned from Hudhud, particularly regarding robust early warning systems, efficient large-scale evacuations, and pre-positioning of disaster response teams, played a crucial role in minimizing Arnab's human cost.
