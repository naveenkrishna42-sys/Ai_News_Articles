East Bengaluru gets mobile veterinary clinic for injured community animals

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • A mobile veterinary clinic has been introduced in East Bengaluru for injured community animals.
  • The vehicle will provide free first aid, emergency treatment, and basic medical care to street animals.
  • The service will cover multiple wards, aiming to reduce animal suffering and improve response times.

Residents of East Bengaluru now have a new resource to help injured street animals. A mobile veterinary clinic has been launched to provide on-the-spot treatment for dogs, cats, and other community animals in need.

The initiative is expected to cover several wards, bringing emergency care directly to animals that might otherwise go without. The vehicle is equipped to handle first aid and basic medical procedures, reducing the time injured animals spend waiting for help.

How the mobile clinic works

The ambulance-style unit will patrol designated routes and respond to calls from residents. Animal welfare volunteers and local NGOs have been coordinating with the civic authorities to ensure the service reaches the most vulnerable animals.

Treatment is free of cost. The clinic carries essential medicines, bandages, and equipment to stabilise injured animals before they can be moved to a full-scale veterinary facility if needed.

Community support and animal welfare impact

Animal lovers in East Bengaluru have welcomed the move. Many have reported incidents where injured street animals suffered for hours because no immediate help was available. The mobile clinic aims to close that gap.

Local activists say the service could also reduce the burden on existing animal shelters and government hospitals, which are often overcrowded and understaffed. The vehicle's mobility allows it to attend to multiple cases in a single day.

The Bengaluru civic body has not yet disclosed the exact number of animals expected to be treated each month, but officials indicated the service will operate daily during daytime hours.

What happens next

The mobile clinic will start with a trial run covering a few densely populated wards. Depending on the response and funding, the service may be expanded to other parts of the city.

Residents who spot an injured animal can contact the designated helpline number, which will be shared by the local animal welfare office. The vehicle's route and schedule will also be published online for easy access.
