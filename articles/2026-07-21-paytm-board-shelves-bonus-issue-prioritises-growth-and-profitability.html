Paytm board shelves bonus issue, prioritises growth and profitability

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Paytm board shelves proposal to issue bonus shares.
  • Company reports Q1 net profit of Rs 220 crore, up 79% year-on-year.
  • Revenue rises 28% to Rs 2,448 crore in the quarter.
  • Board approves Rs 100 crore investment in Paytm Money.

Paytm's board has scrapped a proposal to issue bonus shares, a move that would have rewarded existing shareholders. The decision was announced alongside the company's first-quarter results for the fiscal year, where it posted a net profit of Rs 220 crore.

The fintech major's revenue for the quarter stood at Rs 2,448 crore, a 28% increase from the same period last year. This marks the fifth consecutive quarter of net profitability for the company.

Profitability takes precedence

The board's decision to shelve the bonus issue signals a shift in strategy. Instead of distributing shares, the company will channel capital into growth initiatives. The management indicated that the move aligns with its long-term goal of sustaining profitability while expanding market share.

Paytm's focus on cost optimisation and high-margin businesses has paid off. The company has been trimming expenses and doubling down on revenue-generating segments like payments and financial services.

Investment in Paytm Money

In a separate development, the board approved a Rs 100 crore investment in Paytm Money, the company's wealth management arm. This infusion will support the platform's expansion into mutual funds, stocks, and other investment products.

Paytm Money has been a key growth driver for the group. The new capital will help it compete with established players like Zerodha and Groww in the fast-growing retail investment space.

A look at past bonus issues

Bonus shares are issued by companies to reward shareholders without paying cash dividends. They increase the number of shares outstanding while keeping the market capitalisation unchanged. Several Indian firms have used this tool in the past, especially during periods of strong cash flows.

For example, Reliance Industries has issued bonus shares multiple times, most recently in 2017. IT giant Infosys and consumer goods firm Hindustan Unilever have also done so. Paytm's decision to cancel its own plan is unusual but reflects its current capital allocation priorities.

The company did not specify a timeline for revisiting the bonus issue. Analysts say the move could be seen as prudent, given the need to reinvest earnings for growth.

Market reaction and outlook

Shares of Paytm's parent company, One97 Communications, traded flat following the announcement. Investors appeared to focus more on the profit numbers than the bonus issue reversal. The stock has recovered significantly from its lows last year, buoyed by improving financials.

The company is now eyeing the next leg of growth through new product launches and international expansion. With a strong cash position and a clear focus on profitability, Paytm is positioning itself as a mature player in the digital payments ecosystem.

What to watch: Whether Paytm can sustain its profit momentum in the coming quarters, and if the bonus issue plan resurfaces once growth stabilises.
