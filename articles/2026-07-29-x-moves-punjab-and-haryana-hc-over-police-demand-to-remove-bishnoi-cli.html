X moves Punjab and Haryana HC over police demand to remove Bishnoi clips

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • X filed a petition in Punjab and Haryana High Court against police notices.
  • Punjab Police demanded removal of content allegedly glorifying crime, including Lawrence Bishnoi clips.
  • The platform argues the notices are vague and violate freedom of speech.
  • The court is expected to hear the matter in the coming days.

Platform challenges police directive in court

Social media platform X has moved the Punjab and Haryana High Court after the Punjab Police asked it to take down posts that allegedly glorify criminal activity. The petition challenges multiple notices sent by the state police in recent weeks.

The police had flagged several clips and posts featuring gangster Lawrence Bishnoi, arguing they could inspire copycat behaviour or create a public nuisance. X contends the demand is overbroad and lacks legal basis.

Notices called 'vague and sweeping'

In its filing, X argued that the police notices did not specify which posts violated the law. The company said it cannot be expected to guess which content the authorities consider objectionable.

The platform also pointed out that some of the flagged material was news coverage or commentary, not direct glorification. It said the notices risked turning the company into a censor of legitimate speech.

Lawrence Bishnoi content under scrutiny

The dispute centres on posts related to Lawrence Bishnoi, a jailed gangster whose name has surfaced in several high-profile cases. Punjab Police have been cracking down on content that portrays him or other gangsters in a positive light.

Officials say such material can undermine law enforcement efforts and encourage young people to take up crime. The police have previously asked other platforms to remove similar content.

X has not disclosed how many posts were flagged or how many it took down before the legal challenge. The company maintains that it follows Indian law but needs clear, specific orders to act.

Freedom of speech versus crime glorification

The case raises questions about where to draw the line between free expression and content that could incite or glorify violence. Legal experts say courts have often held that mere depiction of crime is not illegal unless it directly threatens public order.

Punjab Police argue that the repeated sharing of gangster clips amounts to endorsement. X counters that its role is to host content, not to police opinions about criminals.

The High Court is likely to examine whether the notices satisfy the tests of proportionality and necessity under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution.

Previous clashes between X and Indian authorities

This is not the first time X has pushed back against government orders. The platform has had run-ins with Indian authorities over content takedown requests, including during the farmers' protests and the Delhi riots.

In 2023, X challenged several blocking orders in Karnataka High Court, arguing they lacked procedural compliance. The current petition in Punjab and Haryana High Court follows a similar pattern of seeking judicial clarity.

Next steps in the legal battle

The court is expected to list the petition for hearing later this week. X has sought an interim stay on the police notices until the matter is resolved.

Punjab Police will likely file a response, defending the notices as necessary to maintain law and order. The outcome could set a precedent for how Indian states can regulate content linked to organised crime on global platforms.
