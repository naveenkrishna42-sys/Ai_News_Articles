BJP passes resolution against Congress over Vande Mataram; announces nationwide protests

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP passes resolution against Congress on Vande Mataram controversy
  • Amit Shah claims song was never fully accepted after Independence
  • Party announces nationwide protests over the issue
  • Political leaders from both sides exchange sharp remarks

BJP Takes Strong Stand on Vande Mataram Issue

The Bharatiya Janata Party has passed a resolution against the Congress party regarding the ongoing Vande Mataram controversy, party sources confirmed on Saturday. The resolution marks a significant escalation in the political battle over the national song's place in India's civic life.

The BJP has vowed to take its protest against the Congress to the streets, announcing a nationwide campaign to highlight what it calls the Congress's historical stance on Vande Mataram. Party workers across multiple states are expected to participate in demonstrations in the coming days.

Amit Shah's Sharp Attack on Congress Legacy

Speaking at the party meeting where the resolution was passed, BJP leader Amit Shah accused the Congress of never fully accepting Vande Mataram even after Independence. Shah's remarks pointed to what he described as a long-standing reluctance within the Congress ecosystem to embrace the iconic composition by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay.

The BJP president drew a direct link between the Congress's past positions and the current controversy, framing the issue as one of national identity and cultural acceptance. His comments signals the party's intent to keep the Vande Mataram debate alive as a political weapon ahead of upcoming elections.

Congress Faces Backlash from Multiple Corners

The Congress has found itself on the defensive as voices from within the political spectrum and intellectual circles continue to question its historical approach to Vande Mataram. Senior Congress leader Kapil Sibal responded to the controversy by accusing those raising the issue of engaging in divisive politics.

Sibal described what he called the real anti-national activity as being done by those attempting to divide people over the national song. His remarks came as the party scrambled to contain the political damage from the controversy that has engulfed Parliament and state assemblies alike.

Historical Context of the Controversy

The Vande Mataram debate has resurfaced with renewed intensity, bringing back discussions about the song's complex history. The composition, first popularised by Rabindranath Tagore, has been at the centre of national identity debates since before Independence.

Tagore himself was known to be averse to the singing of the entire song, adding another layer to the historical complexity surrounding the national song. The literary and cultural dimensions of this debate have complicated the political narrative, with various interpretations of the song's significance emerging from different quarters.

Political Fallout Expected

The BJP's resolution and protest announcement are expected to intensify the political confrontation between the two major parties. Opposition ranks have shown signs of fracture over the issue, with some regional parties distancing themselves from the Congress position.

Political analysts suggest the controversy could reshape alliances and affect vote shares in several states. The Congress leadership faces the challenge of articulating a coherent position that neither alienates its traditional support base nor provides easy ammunition to the BJP's aggressive nationalist messaging.

Both parties are now preparing for an extended political confrontation on this issue, with the BJP's nationwide protest campaign set to kick off within the next few weeks. How the Congress responds and whether it can mount an effective counter-narrative will be closely watched in political circles.
