Karnataka Cabinet Portfolios Allocated Ahead of Monsoon Session

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • CM Siddaramaiah retains finance and cabinet affairs portfolios.
  • 19 ministers inducted into cabinet with portfolios allocated.
  • KH Muniyappa gets social welfare; Zameer Ahmed gets housing.

Bengaluru: The Karnataka cabinet finally has its full team in place. On the eve of the Monsoon Session, Chief Minister Siddaramaiah allocated portfolios to the 19 ministers inducted earlier, ending days of speculation. The Congress high command cleared the list late Tuesday night, and the allocation was announced Wednesday morning.

Key portfolios distributed

DK Shivakumar, the Deputy Chief Minister, has retained the crucial finance portfolio. This was widely expected, given his central role in the state's economic management. Along with finance, he also holds the cabinet affairs and Bengaluru city development portfolios, making him one of the most powerful ministers in the government.

KH Muniyappa, a senior Congress leader from Kolar, has been assigned the social welfare department. This is a significant responsibility, given the party's focus on social justice and welfare schemes. Zameer Ahmed Khan, another prominent face, has been given the housing portfolio, a key infrastructure ministry in the state.

Cabinet composition and expectations

The cabinet now comprises a mix of experienced leaders and fresh faces. The allocation reflects a careful balancing of regional and caste equations ahead of the upcoming elections. Several ministers who were inducted in the first round were given additional charges, while the new entrants have been assigned key departments.

Political observers note that the timing is crucial. With the Monsoon Session starting Thursday, the government will now be able to present a united front. The opposition BJP has been critical of the delay in portfolio allocation, accusing the government of indecisiveness.

What the session holds

The Monsoon Session is expected to be a stormy affair. The opposition is likely to raise issues like drought, inflation, and alleged irregularities in various schemes. The government, on its part, will seek to pass crucial bills and present its achievements.

With portfolios now allocated, all ministers are expected to hit the ground running. The coming weeks will show how effectively this team functions and whether it can meet the high expectations set by the Congress leadership.

As the session begins, all eyes will be on how the new ministers perform in their respective departments. The government's ability to deliver on its promises will be tested in the months ahead.
