Iran attacks vessel in Strait of Hormuz, UAE confirms

📅 2026-08-08 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • UAE confirms Iranian attack on vessel in Strait of Hormuz
  • IRGC says Hormuz will reopen after US fully accepts conditions
  • Iran rejects Oman mediation link, demands US meet conditions
  • US official expects a deal soon on Hormuz accord

Attack in the Strait

The United Arab Emirates has confirmed that Iranian forces attacked a vessel in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. The attack, reported on Saturday, adds a new layer of volatility to an already tense region.

Details of the vessel's identity and the extent of damage remain unclear. Officials have not yet confirmed casualties or the nature of the attack, though the incident marks a significant escalation in the ongoing confrontation.

IRGC's conditional reopening

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has stated that the Strait of Hormuz will reopen to normal navigation only after the United States 'fully' accepts its conditions. This statement comes amid reports that Iran is awaiting approval from its top council for a broader accord.

The IRGC's stance signals a hardline position, linking the free flow of shipping to political concessions from Washington. The US, for its part, has expressed cautious optimism, with an official saying, 'We expect a deal soon.'

Oman talks rejected

Iran has dismissed any connection between Omani-mediated talks and the reopening of the Strait. Tehran insists that any resolution must be based on the US meeting its conditions, rather than third-party mediation.

This rejection complicates diplomatic efforts, as Oman has historically served as a bridge between Iran and Western powers. The Iranian position appears to be a firm demand for direct US compliance, with no room for intermediaries.

Shipping restrictions sought

According to reports, Iran is seeking to bar US and Israeli vessels from transiting the Strait of Hormuz as part of any potential deal. This demand, if implemented, would represent a major shift in the region's maritime security dynamics.

The proposed restriction has raised concerns among global shipping stakeholders, who rely on the strait for about a fifth of the world's oil supply. The US has not publicly responded to this specific condition, but the expectation of a deal suggests active negotiations.

What to watch

As Iran awaits its top council's approval, the coming days will be critical. The world will be watching whether Washington's 'expectation' of a deal translates into concrete action, and whether the Strait of Hormuz remains open for all shipping.
