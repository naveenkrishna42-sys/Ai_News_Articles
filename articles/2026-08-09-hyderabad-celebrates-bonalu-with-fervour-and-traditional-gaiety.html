Hyderabad celebrates Bonalu with fervour and traditional gaiety

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bonalu festival celebrated across Hyderabad with traditional rituals
  • Devotees offer prayers to Goddess Mahakali at various temples
  • Processions and cultural events mark the festivities

Hyderabad is awash in colour and devotion as the city marks the annual Bonalu festival. Streets resonate with the rhythmic beats of drums, and the air is thick with the scent of incense and marigold. The festival, dedicated to Goddess Mahakali, sees thousands of devotees thronging temples across the city, offering prayers and seeking blessings.

A Festival of Faith and Tradition

Bonalu, a month-long celebration, holds deep cultural significance for the people of Telangana. The festival is marked by the offering of bonam, a special meal cooked in a pot, to the goddess. Women, dressed in vibrant sarees, carry the pots on their heads, often accompanied by traditional folk dances and songs.

The city's old quarters, particularly around the historic Golconda Fort and the Muthyalamma temple in Secunderabad, witness the largest gatherings. Devotees from all walks of life come together, transcending social barriers, to participate in the festivities.

Processions and Cultural Vibrancy

Elaborate processions form the heart of the celebrations. Decorated idols of the goddess are carried through the streets, accompanied by troupes of dancers and musicians. The Ghatam artists, balancing decorated pots on their heads, draw large crowds with their skill and devotion.

The atmosphere is electric, with the constant chants of "Amma, Mahakali" and the beating of dappu drums creating a trance-like state for many. The festival is not just a religious event but a grand cultural spectacle that showcases the region's rich heritage.

Community and Celebration

Bonalu is as much about community as it is about worship. Local residents set up makeshift stalls offering food and water to the pilgrims. Families gather to prepare the traditional bonam offering, which is later shared as prasadam. The festival fosters a sense of unity and shared identity among the city's diverse population.

Authorities have made extensive arrangements for crowd management and security to ensure a smooth celebration. Medical camps and drinking water facilities have been set up along the procession routes, and traffic diversions are in place to manage the heavy flow of people.

Looking Ahead

As the main day of Bonalu concludes, the festivities will continue over the coming weeks with the Rangam (oracle) ceremony and the final procession of the goddess to the river. For now, the city basks in the afterglow of a celebration that reaffirms its cultural roots and communal harmony.
