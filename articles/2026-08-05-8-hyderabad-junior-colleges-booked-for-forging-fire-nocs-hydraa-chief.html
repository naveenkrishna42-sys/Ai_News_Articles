8 Hyderabad junior colleges booked for forging Fire NOCs, HYDRAA chief's signature

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Eight junior colleges allegedly submitted forged Fire NOCs to the Telangana Intermediate Board.
  • The forged documents reportedly bore the signature of the HYDRAA chief.
  • Police have booked cases; investigation is ongoing.

Forged documents under scrutiny

The Telangana Intermediate Board has unearthed a major irregularity after eight junior colleges in Hyderabad allegedly submitted fake Fire No Objection Certificates (NOCs) to obtain or renew affiliations. The matter came to light during routine verification, prompting the board to file a formal complaint.

Police have now registered cases against the institutions on charges of forgery and cheating. The fraudulent NOCs reportedly carried the signature of the HYDRAA chief, a senior official tasked with overseeing Hyderabad's development and regulatory compliance, making the deception particularly audacious.

How the fraud was detected

According to sources, the Intermediate Board officials noticed discrepancies in the NOCs during a periodic audit. The documents, purportedly issued by the fire department, appeared suspicious upon closer inspection, leading to verification with the concerned authorities.

The verification confirmed that the NOCs were not genuine, and the signature attributed to the HYDRAA chief was forged. Following this, the board approached the police, who registered a case under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code pertaining to forgery, using forged documents as genuine, and criminal conspiracy.

Colleges under the scanner

The eight junior colleges, located across different parts of Hyderabad, have not been named in the initial complaint. However, authorities have indicated that the institutions will face strict action, including possible cancellation of their affiliations or recognition.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the principals or management representatives were directly involved in the forgery or if they were unaware of the fraudulent submissions. The police are likely to question the college heads and examine the institutions' administrative records.

The Intermediate Board has also initiated an internal review to check if similar forged documents were submitted by other colleges in recent years. This is to ensure that the integrity of the examination and affiliation process is not compromised.

Impact on students and institutions

The development has sent ripples through Hyderabad's educational circles, with many colleges now rushing to verify their own compliance documents. For the students enrolled in the eight colleges, the uncertainty is palpable, though the board has assured that academic activities will not be disrupted until the investigation concludes.

Legal experts point out that forging official signatures is a serious offence, attracting imprisonment and fines. If convicted, the individuals responsible could face severe penalties, and the colleges may lose their recognition, directly affecting thousands of intermediate students.

The HYDRAA chief's office has not issued a statement yet, but sources say they are cooperating with the police investigation. The department is also reviewing its document verification protocols to prevent such incidents in the future.

What happens next

Police are expected to summon the management representatives of the eight colleges for questioning in the coming days. The investigation will focus on tracing the origin of the forged NOCs and identifying the individuals who facilitated the fabrication.

Meanwhile, the Intermediate Board is likely to issue a circular to all affiliated colleges, reiterating the need for genuine and verifiable documents. The case serves as a reminder of the vulnerabilities in administrative processes, and educational authorities are expected to tighten scrutiny in the upcoming academic cycle.
