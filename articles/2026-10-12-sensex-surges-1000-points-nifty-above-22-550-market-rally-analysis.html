Market Rally Triggered by Banking and IT Stocks

The BSE Sensex surged past the 1000-point mark on Friday, closing at 74,820, while the Nifty50 breached 22,550, reflecting strong buying across banking, IT, and FMCG sectors. The rally was primarily fueled by gains in HDFC Bank, Infosys, and ICICI Bank, which collectively added over 300 points to the Sensex. Analysts attribute the upmove to positive global cues and better-than-expected quarterly results from key heavyweights.

Global and Domestic Factors Driving Momentum

Asian markets opened higher following a strong close on Wall Street, where the Nasdaq gained 1.2% after tech earnings beat estimates. Domestically, retail inflation data released earlier in the week showed a marginal decline to 4.7% for September, reinforcing expectations that the Reserve Bank of India may pause rate hikes. Crude oil prices settling below $75 a barrel further buoyed investor sentiment.

Technical Outlook: Key Levels to Watch

The Nifty50 is now trading above its 20-day and 50-day exponential moving averages, indicating short-term bullish momentum. Immediate support is placed at 22,300, while resistance remains at 22,750. A sustained move above 22,750 could push the index towards its all-time high of 23,100, according to technical analysts.

Banking and IT: Sectoral Performance

The banking index gained 2.1%, led by PSU banks such as SBI and PNB, while private lenders like Kotak Mahindra Bank also posted strong gains. On the IT front, Tata Consultancy Services and Wipro rose over 1.5% each, supported by a weaker rupee and improving global demand signals. These two sectors together accounted for over 60% of the day’s total market advance.

Investor Takeaways: Volatility Likely Ahead

While the rally signals renewed confidence, market participants should brace for volatility ahead of next week’s US Federal Reserve policy meeting. Foreign institutional investors remained net buyers for the third consecutive session, adding ₹1,240 crore worth of equities. Domestic mutual funds also continued their buying trend, reflecting sustained retail participation.

  • Banking and IT leadership suggests sustainable momentum
  • Global macro risks remain, especially Fed policy and oil prices

Overall, the sharp rally underscores a cautious optimism among investors, supported by stable macroeconomic conditions and incremental buying from both domestic and foreign funds.
