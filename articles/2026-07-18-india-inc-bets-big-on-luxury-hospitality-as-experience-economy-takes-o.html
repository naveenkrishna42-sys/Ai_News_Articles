India Inc bets big on luxury hospitality as experience economy takes off

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • India Inc is investing heavily in luxury hotels and resorts, moving beyond traditional room bookings.
  • The focus is now on creating immersive experiences, from curated tours to wellness retreats.
  • This shift reflects changing consumer preferences for memorable, personalised stays over standard luxury.

The new luxury: more than a room

India Inc is rethinking its approach to luxury hospitality. It is no longer just about selling a room for the night. Companies are now investing in creating entire experiences that guests can take home as memories.

This shift is visible across the sector. From heritage properties in Rajasthan to beachfront villas in Goa, the emphasis has moved to curated activities, local immersion, and personalised services. The room is just the starting point.

Why the change now?

Consumer behaviour is driving this transformation. Today's affluent traveller, especially the millennial and Gen Z cohort, values experiences over material possessions. They want stories to tell, not just a comfortable bed.

Hotel chains and corporate groups are responding. They are investing in art tours, cooking classes with local chefs, wellness programmes, and adventure activities. The goal is to make every stay feel unique and shareable on social media.

This is not a small trend. Industry insiders say the returns from experience-led hospitality are often higher than traditional models. Repeat bookings and word-of-mouth referrals are stronger when guests feel emotionally connected to a property.

Who is placing the bets?

Several large Indian conglomerates are leading this charge. They are not just building hotels but lifestyle destinations. These include partnerships with international luxury brands and standalone boutique properties.

For example, some groups are acquiring heritage palaces and converting them into experiential stays. Others are developing eco-resorts in remote locations, offering exclusivity and a connection to nature. The investment size runs into hundreds of crores for each project.

This is a long-term play. Companies are betting that the demand for premium experiences will only grow as disposable incomes rise and travel becomes more aspirational.

The role of technology

Tech is also playing a part. Hotels are using apps to let guests customise their itineraries before arrival. From booking a spa session to arranging a private dinner under the stars, everything is a tap away.

Data analytics helps properties understand guest preferences. This allows for hyper-personalisation, such as remembering a guest's favourite wine or suggesting local hikes based on past behaviour. The experience feels effortless and tailored.

However, the human touch remains critical. Staff are trained to anticipate needs and offer spontaneous gestures, like a welcome drink on arrival or a handwritten note. The blend of tech and warmth is what makes the experience memorable.

Challenges ahead

This shift is not without risks. Creating authentic experiences requires significant investment in training and local partnerships. There is also the challenge of maintaining consistency across multiple properties.

Competition is intensifying. As more players enter the space, differentiation becomes harder. Guests may start to expect these experiences as standard, raising the bar for everyone.

Yet, for now, the momentum is strong. India Inc's luxury hospitality bet is clearly evolving into an experience business. The room is just the beginning.

What to watch next: How smaller, independent hoteliers adapt to this trend and whether international luxury chains will follow India Inc's lead in crafting hyper-local experiences.
