Bitcoin ETFs See Best Week Since April Amid Coldcard Hack Fallout

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin ETFs logged their best weekly performance since April this year.
  • Inflows reached $853 million during the week.
  • The timing coincides with a reported Coldcard hardware wallet hack.
  • Analysts are split on whether the security incident drove investor flows.

Strongest Week in Months for Bitcoin ETFs

Bitcoin exchange-traded funds have just wrapped up their best week since April, pulling in a hefty $853 million in net inflows. The surge marks a notable reversal after weeks of tepid activity, and it has left market watchers scrambling for an explanation.

The weekly total, confirmed by multiple fund issuers, represents the largest single-week intake for the asset class in over three months. Trading volumes across major Bitcoin ETFs also climbed, signalling renewed interest from institutional players.

Yet the question on everyone's lips is whether a security breach at a popular hardware wallet maker played a role in this sudden rush.

The Coldcard Hack: A Catalyst or Coincidence?

Earlier this week, reports emerged of a vulnerability in Coldcard, a widely used hardware wallet among crypto enthusiasts. Details remain sketchy, but the incident has rattled a segment of the investor community that prizes self-custody above all else.

Some analysts argue that the hack may have nudged risk-averse holders toward regulated vehicles like ETFs. "When people lose faith in DIY security, they often look for intermediaries they trust," said one Mumbai-based fund manager, who asked not to be named.

Others, however, are more cautious. They point out that ETF inflows are typically driven by broader market sentiment, not isolated security events. Bitcoin's price remained relatively stable during the week, suggesting that fear wasn't the primary motivator.

Institutional Appetite Returns

The inflows come after a prolonged lull, during which Bitcoin ETFs saw sporadic outflows. The latest data suggests a renewed appetite among pension funds, family offices, and other large investors who have been waiting on the sidelines.

Issuers report that the bulk of the money came from new allocations rather than existing positions being topped up. This is a bullish signal, as it indicates fresh capital entering the ecosystem.

Notably, the weekly figure of $853 million is a sharp contrast to the modest numbers seen in June and July. Back then, daily inflows often hovered in the tens of millions, with occasional red days.

What the Market Is Watching Now

Regulatory clarity remains a key theme. With the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) yet to approve a domestic Bitcoin ETF, Indian investors are largely participating through overseas funds or via international brokerages. The current global momentum could, however, accelerate conversations in New Delhi.

Globally, the focus now shifts to whether this week's inflows mark the start of a sustained trend or a one-off spike. Historically, such bursts have often been followed by consolidation.

For now, the crypto market is watching two things: any further developments in the Coldcard investigation, and whether Bitcoin can break its recent trading range. If the ETF flows continue at this pace, the asset could see renewed upside pressure in the coming weeks.
