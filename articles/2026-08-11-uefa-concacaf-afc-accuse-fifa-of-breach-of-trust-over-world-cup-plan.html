UEFA, Concacaf, AFC accuse FIFA of 'breach of trust' over World Cup plan

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • UEFA, Concacaf and AFC jointly accused FIFA of a 'breach of trust' and 'deception' in an open letter.
  • The letter follows FIFA's proposal to expand the 2030 World Cup, which the confederations claim was not consulted.
  • The escalating dispute has led to calls for FIFA president Gianni Infantino to resign and talk of rival competitions.

Confederations strike back

Three of football's six continental governing bodies have launched an unprecedented attack on FIFA, accusing the world body of 'breach of trust' and 'deception' over its plans for the 2030 World Cup. In a joint open letter, UEFA, Concacaf and the AFC said FIFA's decision-making process had undermined the sport's governance.

The letter, addressed to the football family, does not hold back. It alleges that FIFA's leadership, under president Gianni Infantino, has repeatedly ignored the concerns of member associations and confederations while pushing through major reforms and competition changes without proper consultation.

What triggered the fallout

The immediate flashpoint is FIFA's proposal to expand the 2030 World Cup format, a plan that the three confederations claim was finalized without their input. They argue that such a significant change to the game's showpiece event required consensus, not unilateral action.

Sources suggest the plan would alter the number of teams and the tournament's structure, but FIFA has not yet publicly detailed the full proposal. The confederations say they were presented with a fait accompli, leaving them no room to negotiate or raise concerns.

Calls for Infantino to go

The open letter has escalated into open calls for Infantino to step down. Several member associations, particularly in Europe, have voiced frustration with his leadership style, which they describe as autocratic. The Guardian reports that UEFA, Concacaf and AFC are now pushing for Infantino's exit and are discussing the possibility of rival competitions outside FIFA's umbrella.

Such a move would be unprecedented in modern football history. The threat of breakaway tournaments, similar to the failed European Super League project, signals how deep the distrust has become.

Defence from an unexpected corner

Infantino has found support from an unlikely source: former US president Donald Trump. In a public statement, Trump said replacing Infantino would be a 'terrible mistake,' praising the FIFA president's leadership. The endorsement is notable given Trump's close ties to football stakeholders in North America, including Concacaf's own region.

Trump's intervention adds a political layer to a sporting dispute, but it is unlikely to soothe the anger among European and Asian federations. The BBC and other outlets have covered the growing rift, noting that Infantino's position, while not immediately threatened, is facing its most serious challenge since he took office in 2016.

What happens next

FIFA has not yet issued a formal response to the open letter. However, the confederations have set a clear deadline for dialogue, and if none is forthcoming, they may take concrete steps toward organizing alternative competitions. The next FIFA Council meeting, expected within months, will be a test of whether Infantino can bridge the divide or face a full-blown revolt.

For now, the football world watches as the sport's governance teeters on the brink of a historic split.
