10 AI Prompting Tips That Actually Improve ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini Output

📅 2026-08-13 📂 AI Tips & Tools Original source ↗
Key points
  • Use role, context, and constraints to frame prompts clearly.
  • Break complex tasks into smaller, sequential prompts.
  • Ask for output format and iterate based on the model's response.

Why Your Prompts Fall Short

You type a question, hit enter, and get a generic wall of text. The problem isn't the AI — it's the prompt. Vague inputs produce vague outputs, no matter which model you use.

These ten techniques work across ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. They're not magic spells; they're structured ways to communicate what you actually want. Each one is something you can test right now in your own chat window.

1. Assign a Role and Set Context

Start by telling the AI who it is and why you're asking. This frames every subsequent answer.

  • Example: "You are a senior data analyst. I'm a marketing manager with no coding background. Explain this spreadsheet's trends in plain English."
  • Check: Compare that with just pasting the spreadsheet and asking "What do you see?" — the role-based version will be far more targeted.

2. Specify the Output Format

Don't accept whatever format the AI chooses. Tell it exactly what you need.

  • Ask for "a bulleted list", "a table with three columns", "a JSON object", or "a 200-word summary" — and watch the response conform.
  • If you need code, specify the language and version. For prose, specify tone (formal, casual, persuasive).

3. Break Big Tasks into Smaller Steps

One massive prompt invites hallucinations and omissions. Split the task.

  • First prompt: "List the key arguments for and against remote work."
  • Second prompt: "Use those arguments to write a one-paragraph summary for a company newsletter."
  • This keeps each step simple, and you can correct course at any point without redoing everything.

4. Provide Examples (Few-Shot Prompting)

Show the model what good output looks like before asking it to produce its own.

  • Give two or three sample inputs with ideal outputs, then your real question.
  • For instance: "Here's a product description: [example]. Here's another: [example]. Now write one for this product: [your product]."

5. Use Constraints and Negative Instructions

Tell the AI what not to do. This is as important as telling it what to do.

  • "Don't use jargon." "Avoid listing more than five items." "Don't mention statistics you're not certain about."
  • Constraints force the model to stay within your guardrails, reducing unwanted tangents.

6. Ask for Step-by-Step Reasoning

When solving a problem, request a chain of thought.

  • "Walk through your reasoning step by step before giving the final answer."
  • This improves accuracy on math, logic, and planning tasks, and it lets you spot errors mid-stream.

7. Iterate with Follow-Up Questions

Treat the first response as a draft, not a final product.

  • Ask: "Make it more concise." "Add more detail on point two." "Rewrite this for a beginner."
  • Each iteration refines the output without starting from scratch.

8. Use Placeholders and Fill-in-the-Blanks

For templates, leave blanks for the AI to fill.

  • "Write a rejection email to a job candidate. Use [candidate name], [position], and [company name]."
  • This works brilliantly for recurring tasks like emails, reports, or social posts.

9. Ask for Multiple Options

Don't settle for one answer. Request alternatives.

  • "Give me three different headline options for this article."
  • "Propose two approaches to cut our cloud costs."
  • You get a menu to choose from, not a single guess.

10. Request a Self-Critique

Have the model review its own output against your criteria.

  • "Now check your answer for factual errors and missing points. Revise it."
  • This double-pass often catches mistakes the first response contained.

Putting It All Together

Here's a numbered, checkable sequence for your next prompt:

  1. Define the role and context in the first sentence.
  2. State the exact output format you want (list, table, code, length).
  3. List any constraints or exclusions.
  4. Provide an example if possible.
  5. Ask for a first draft.
  6. Review it, then iterate with specific follow-ups.
  7. Run a self-critique pass before you finalise.

Test these on a mundane task — writing a meeting summary or drafting an email — and you'll see the difference immediately.

When This Technique Is Right for You

These tips work best when you have a clear goal and some flexibility to iterate. They're overkill for a quick factual question like "What's the capital of France?" — just ask directly. But for anything that requires nuance, structure, or creativity, use them. They'll save you time and frustration across every major AI chatbot.
