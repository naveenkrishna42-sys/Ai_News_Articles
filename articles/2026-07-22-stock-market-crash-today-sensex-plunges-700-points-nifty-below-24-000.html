Stock Market Crash Today: Sensex Plunges 700 Points, Nifty Below 24,000

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex ended 700+ points lower, Nifty closed below 24,000
  • Banking, IT and auto stocks led the sell-off
  • Global market weakness and FII outflows were key triggers

Markets in Red: A Brutal Session for Indian Equities

Indian stock markets saw a sharp sell-off on Wednesday, with the BSE Sensex plunging over 700 points and the Nifty50 slipping below the crucial 24,000 mark. The benchmark indices ended deep in the red, wiping out gains from the previous session.

The Sensex closed at 79,200, down 710 points, while the Nifty50 settled at 23,950, falling 215 points. The sell-off was broad-based, with all sectoral indices ending in negative territory.

Banking and IT Stocks Take the Biggest Hit

Banking stocks were the worst performers. Heavyweights like HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and SBI dropped between 2% and 3%, dragging the Nifty Bank index down by over 800 points. Analysts attributed the selling to profit-booking after a recent rally.

IT stocks also faced heavy selling pressure. Infosys, TCS and Wipro fell 1.5% to 2.5% amid concerns over a potential slowdown in US demand. The Nifty IT index was among the top losers.

Global Cues and FII Outflows Weigh on Sentiment

Weak global cues added to the domestic gloom. US markets ended lower overnight on fears of prolonged high interest rates. Asian markets, including Japan and South Korea, also traded in the red, hurting investor confidence.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) were net sellers, offloading equities worth over Rs 1,500 crore in the cash market. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) stepped in but could not stem the fall.

Other Factors Behind the Crash

  • Rising crude oil prices, which stoked inflation worries
  • Uncertainty ahead of key US economic data due later this week
  • Weak rupee, which touched a fresh low against the dollar

What Analysts Are Saying

Market experts said the correction was overdue after the recent run-up. The Nifty had gained nearly 8% in the last three months. They advised investors to stay cautious and avoid panic selling.

Technically, the Nifty has broken its immediate support at 24,100. The next support is seen at 23,700. A close below that could trigger further selling.

The broader market also took a hit. The BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices fell 1.2% and 1.5%, respectively. Over 2,000 stocks declined on the BSE against 800 advances.

What to Watch Next

Traders will now focus on US GDP data and the Federal Reserve's policy statement due next week. Any hawkish signal could keep markets under pressure. On the domestic front, monthly auto sales numbers and corporate earnings will be in focus.
