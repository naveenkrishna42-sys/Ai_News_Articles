Zohran Mamdani Pushes for Netanyahu Arrest During Potential New York Visit

📅 2026-07-19 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Zohran Mamdani stated Netanyahu is a 'war criminal' and should be arrested in New York.
  • Mamdani is exploring legal options but will not rewrite laws to facilitate an arrest.
  • The move aligns with ICC warrants but faces diplomatic and jurisdictional hurdles.

Mamdani’s Stance on Netanyahu

New York State Assemblymember Zohran Mamdani has made it clear: if Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu sets foot in New York City, he wants him arrested. Mamdani, a Democrat representing parts of Queens, told reporters he considers Netanyahu a war criminal.

“He’s a war criminal,” Mamdani said. He confirmed he is actively looking into the legal mechanisms that could allow local authorities to detain the Israeli leader during a potential visit to the city. The comments come amid rising global tensions over Israel’s military operations in Gaza.

Legal Pathways Under Scrutiny

Mamdani acknowledged that the process is not straightforward. He said he is discussing the matter with legal experts but will not attempt to rewrite laws to make an arrest happen. “I’m discussing it but won’t rewrite laws,” he stated.

The assemblymember’s position is based on the International Criminal Court’s (ICC) arrest warrant for Netanyahu. The ICC issued the warrant in 2024, accusing the prime minister of war crimes and crimes against humanity. Mamdani believes that warrant gives New York the authority to act.

However, the United States is not a signatory to the Rome Statute, the treaty that established the ICC. This legal gap means U.S. authorities are not automatically bound to enforce ICC warrants. Legal experts have pointed out that any local arrest attempt would face significant federal and diplomatic hurdles.

Diplomatic and Political Fallout

Netanyahu has not confirmed any plans to visit New York. A trip to the city would typically involve high-level diplomatic engagements, including visits to the United Nations. The Israeli leader’s office has not commented on Mamdani’s remarks.

Mamdani’s stance has drawn both praise and criticism. Supporters of the Palestinian cause have hailed his move as a bold stand for international justice. Critics, including some Jewish and pro-Israel groups, have accused him of political grandstanding. They argue that targeting a foreign leader undermines U.S.-Israel relations.

New York City has a long history of hosting world leaders, including those accused of human rights abuses. Previous attempts to arrest foreign dignitaries have rarely succeeded, partly due to diplomatic immunity and federal jurisdiction. The State Department typically handles such matters, not local lawmakers.

What Happens Next

Mamdani has not introduced any legislation related to the arrest. He said he is still exploring options and declined to provide a timeline. If Netanyahu does plan a New York visit, the issue could escalate quickly.

For now, the matter remains in the realm of political rhetoric. The coming weeks will show whether Mamdani’s words translate into action or remain a symbolic stand. All eyes are on Netanyahu’s travel schedule and the legal advice Mamdani receives.
