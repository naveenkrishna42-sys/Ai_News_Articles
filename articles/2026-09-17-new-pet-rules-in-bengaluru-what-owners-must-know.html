Bengaluru's New Pet Rules: A Comprehensive Guide for Owners

The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has notified the Animal Welfare and Pet Ownership Regulations, 2026, effective from 1 October 2026. These rules aim to curb illegal breeding, reduce pet abandonment, and improve rabies control. For the city's estimated 2.5 lakh pet parents, the changes bring both responsibilities and costs. This guide breaks down the key provisions, compliance deadlines, and practical implications.

Mandatory Registration and Microchipping

Every pet dog and cat over three months old must be registered with BBMP. Registration requires a microchip implant, a rabies vaccination certificate, and proof of address. The annual fee is ₹500 for dogs and ₹300 for cats; lifetime registration costs ₹2,000 and ₹1,200 respectively. Owners can register online via the BBMP One app or at designated veterinary clinics. The deadline is 31 December 2026. Post-deadline, unregistered pets will be treated as strays and may be impounded.

Breeding and Sale Restrictions

Commercial breeding now requires a licence from the BBMP Animal Husbandry Department. Breeders must ensure each breeding pair is registered, microchipped, and screened for genetic disorders. The licence fee is ₹5,000 per annum, and renewals require a veterinary inspection. Pet shops must maintain records of every animal's origin and sale, and cannot sell unregistered pets. Violations attract penalties of ₹10,000 to ₹25,000. These measures target puppy mills and illegal online sales, which have surged in Bengaluru.

Penalties and Enforcement

Fines for non-compliance are steep. First-time offenders face ₹5,000 for unregistered pets, ₹10,000 for breeding without a licence, and ₹25,000 for abandoning an animal. Repeat offences can lead to cancellation of pet ownership privileges and seizure of animals. BBMP has appointed 50 animal welfare inspectors to enforce the rules, with surprise checks at pet shops and breeding facilities. Citizens can report violations via the BBMP helpline (1533) or the Animal Welfare Board of India portal.

Pros and Cons for Pet Owners

  • Pros:
    • Improved traceability reduces pet theft and illegal breeding.
    • Microchipping helps reunite lost pets with owners quickly.
    • Rabies control benefits public health and reduces stray dog conflicts.
  • Cons:
    • Additional annual costs (registration + microchipping) strain low-income households.
    • Breeding licence fees may push small breeders out of business.
    • Enforcement could lead to harassment if not implemented transparently.

What This Means for You

If you own a pet in Bengaluru, act before 31 December 2026. Start by getting your pet microchipped at a BBMP-approved centre (₹200) and then register online. Keep vaccination records handy. If you breed pets, apply for a licence immediately. The rules are strict but aim to create a safer city for animals and humans alike. For assistance, visit the BBMP website or call 1533. Non-compliance is not worth the risk—fines alone can exceed ₹25,000, and your pet could be taken away.
