Perovskite solar cells leap from 3.8% to 26.1%

📅 2026-08-25 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Perovskite solar cells efficiency jumped from 3.8% in 2009 to 26.1% in 2026
  • LONGi's silicon-perovskite tandem cells set new efficiency record
  • Broader impact on renewable energy and clean energy transition

Perovskite Solar Cells: A 17-Year Efficiency Leap

In 2009, scientists achieved a modest 3.8% efficiency with perovskite solar cells. Fast forward to 2026, and LONGi's silicon-perovskite tandem cells have shattered that record with a staggering 26.1% efficiency. This remarkable progress underscores the rapid advancements in solar technology, with significant implications for the renewable energy sector.

Understanding the Technology

Perovskite solar cells are a type of photovoltaic cell that uses a perovskite-structured compound, most commonly a hybrid organic-inorganic lead or tin halide-based material, as the light-harvesting active layer. These cells are known for their high absorption coefficient and low-cost production potential, making them a promising candidate for next-generation solar technology.

Silicon-Perovskite Tandem Cells

LONGi's breakthrough involves silicon-perovskite tandem cells, which combine the strengths of both silicon and perovskite solar cells. Silicon cells are the industry standard, known for their stability and long lifespan. Perovskite cells, on the other hand, are lightweight and can be produced using low-cost methods. By combining these two technologies, researchers have been able to achieve higher efficiencies than either technology alone.

The Efficiency Jump: Why It Matters

The leap from 3.8% to 26.1% efficiency is not just a numbers game. It signifies a significant step towards making solar energy more competitive with traditional energy sources. Higher efficiency means more power can be generated from the same amount of sunlight, reducing the overall cost of solar energy and making it more accessible.

Broader Impact on Renewable Energy

This advancement is a testament to the rapid pace of innovation in the renewable energy sector. As solar technology continues to improve, it is expected to play an increasingly larger role in the global energy mix. The reduction in cost and improvement in efficiency of solar energy can help accelerate the transition away from fossil fuels, contributing to a cleaner and more sustainable future.

Challenges and Future Outlook

Despite the impressive progress, there are still challenges to overcome. Perovskite solar cells, for instance, are not as stable as silicon cells. They degrade more quickly when exposed to moisture, heat, and light. Researchers are actively working on developing more stable perovskite materials and encapsulation techniques to address these issues.

Future Prospects

Looking ahead, the future of perovskite solar cells looks bright. With continued research and development, it is expected that their efficiency and stability will continue to improve. This could lead to a significant increase in the adoption of solar energy, further accelerating the transition to a renewable energy future.
