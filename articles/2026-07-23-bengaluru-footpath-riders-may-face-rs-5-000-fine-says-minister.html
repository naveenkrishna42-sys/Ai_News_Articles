Bengaluru footpath riders may face Rs 5,000 fine, says minister

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Transport Minister Krishna Byre Gowda suggests Rs 5,000 fine for footpath riding.
  • Proposal aims to curb rampant footpath riding in Bengaluru.
  • Pedestrian safety and traffic discipline cited as primary concerns.

Bengaluru's notorious footpath riding menace could soon attract a hefty penalty of Rs 5,000. Transport Minister Krishna Byre Gowda has proposed the steep fine as a deterrent against the widespread practice of two-wheelers and other vehicles encroaching on pedestrian walkways.

Proposal to curb footpath menace

The minister mooted the idea during a recent review meeting on traffic management in the city. Officials present confirmed that footpath riding has become a daily hazard for pedestrians, forcing them onto roads and increasing accident risks.

Currently, the penalty for riding on footpaths in Bengaluru is much lower, often ignored by riders. The proposed Rs 5,000 fine is intended to make violators think twice before taking shortcuts on walkways.

Why footpath riding persists

Traffic congestion on Bengaluru's roads is the primary driver behind footpath riding. Commuters, frustrated by gridlock, often mount the kerb to bypass jams, especially during peak hours. This behaviour is rampant near major junctions, market areas and IT corridors.

Pedestrians, particularly the elderly and children, bear the brunt. Many have complained of near-misses and harassment. The lack of dedicated cycling lanes and poor road design further compound the problem, forcing cyclists and two-wheeler riders to seek alternate paths.

Enforcement challenges ahead

While the proposal has drawn support from pedestrian rights groups, enforcement remains a significant challenge. Bengaluru Traffic Police already struggle with manpower shortages and a high volume of traffic violations.

Officials indicated that automated enforcement through CCTV cameras could be part of the solution. However, identifying and penalising footpath riders via cameras is technically difficult compared to red-light or speed violations.

The minister has asked the transport department and traffic police to submit a detailed feasibility report within a month. The report will examine legal provisions, implementation logistics and potential revenue from fines.

Public reaction mixed

Commuters in Bengaluru have reacted with a mix of support and scepticism. Many pedestrians welcomed the move, calling it long overdue. 'Footpaths are for walking, not for vehicles. This fine will finally teach riders a lesson,' said a resident of Indiranagar.

Two-wheeler riders, however, expressed frustration. 'We don't ride on footpaths for fun. The roads are so bad and traffic so heavy that sometimes there's no other way. First fix the roads, then fine us,' said a delivery executive who regularly works in the city.

Traffic experts caution that fines alone will not solve the problem without infrastructure improvements. Wider footpaths, dedicated cycling tracks and better traffic management are essential complementary measures.

What happens next

The proposal now moves to the state cabinet for approval. If cleared, the new fine will be incorporated into the Karnataka Motor Vehicles Rules. The transport department is expected to finalise the draft notification in the coming weeks, following which public objections will be invited before the rule is notified.
