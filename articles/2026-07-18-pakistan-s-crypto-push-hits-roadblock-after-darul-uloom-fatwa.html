Pakistan's Crypto Push Hits Roadblock After Darul Uloom Fatwa

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pakistan's government was moving to legalize cryptocurrency trading amid growing adoption.
  • Darul Uloom Deoband issued a fatwa declaring crypto trading un-Islamic and haram.
  • The fatwa has forced the government to pause its crypto-friendly policies and reconsider its stance.

Pakistan's ambitious plans to embrace cryptocurrency have hit a serious roadblock. The government was preparing to legalise and regulate digital assets, hoping to attract investment and modernise the financial system. But a religious decree from one of the country's most influential Islamic seminaries has thrown those plans into chaos.

Darul Uloom Deoband, a leading Islamic seminary, issued a fatwa declaring cryptocurrency trading as 'haram' or forbidden under Islamic law. The ruling has created a dilemma for the government, which was working on a regulatory framework for digital currencies.

Government's Crypto Push

For months, Pakistani officials had been signalling a pro-crypto stance. The State Bank of Pakistan was exploring a regulatory sandbox for digital assets. Lawmakers were drafting legislation to bring clarity to the sector. Crypto adoption was rising, with Pakistan ranking among the top countries for peer-to-peer trading volume.

The government saw cryptocurrency as a way to bypass traditional banking hurdles and attract tech-savvy youth. Officials hoped that clear regulations would curb illegal use while encouraging innovation. The mood was optimistic.

Fatwa Creates Confusion

The Darul Uloom fatwa changes the equation. The seminary's ruling is based on concerns that crypto trading involves speculation and uncertainty, which are prohibited in Islamic finance. The decree says that digital currencies lack intrinsic value and are not backed by any tangible asset.

The fatwa has created confusion among investors and policymakers. Many in the government now fear a political backlash if they proceed with legalisation. Religious parties have already started demanding a complete ban on crypto trading in the country.

Government in a Tailspin

The timing could not be worse for Pakistan's crypto enthusiasts. The government was finalising a draft bill to regulate digital assets. Now, officials are scrambling to assess the impact of the fatwa. Some ministries want to proceed, arguing that the fatwa is not legally binding. Others urge caution, pointing to the strong influence of religious authorities in Pakistan's society.

The finance ministry has not issued any official statement since the fatwa. Sources indicate that the government is reviewing its options. A complete reversal of policy is possible, though unlikely. Some officials are exploring a middle path — allowing only certain types of crypto transactions that comply with Islamic principles.

Investors Left in Limbo

Pakistani crypto investors are in a state of uncertainty. Many had poured money into digital assets, expecting legal clarity soon. The fatwa has sparked panic selling among some traders. Others are waiting for the government's next move.

Local exchanges report a drop in trading volumes. New sign-ups have slowed. The industry, which was on the verge of formal recognition, now fears a crackdown. Some startups are considering relocating to more crypto-friendly jurisdictions in the region.

What happens next depends on how the government navigates this religious and economic tightrope. The finance ministry is expected to hold consultations with Islamic scholars and industry stakeholders in the coming weeks. A compromise that satisfies both religious authorities and crypto advocates will be difficult to craft, but necessary if Pakistan wants to keep its digital finance ambitions alive.
