Fermented Chickpea Water: The New Health Trend Explained

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Fermented chickpea water, or chhole ka paani, is gaining popularity as a probiotic drink.
  • Advocates claim it aids digestion and boosts gut health, but scientific evidence is limited.
  • Experts advise caution due to potential sodium content and hygiene concerns.

The Rise of Chhole ka Paani

It is an unusual sight: people sipping the leftover water from boiled chickpeas, a staple of Indian kitchens. This fermented chickpea water, colloquially called chhole ka paani, has become the latest wellness fad on social media. Enthusiasts are swapping their morning kombucha for this humble, protein-rich liquid, claiming it works wonders for gut health.

The trend has sparked curiosity among nutritionists and home cooks alike. While some swear by its benefits, others are sceptical. The question is whether this kitchen by-product truly deserves a place in your daily health routine.

Why Are People Drinking It?

The appeal lies in its simplicity. Chickpea water, when left to ferment naturally, develops beneficial bacteria similar to those in yoghurt or pickles. Proponents argue that this probiotic-rich drink aids digestion, reduces bloating, and even boosts immunity. Some influencers have gone further, calling it a natural remedy for skin issues and inflammation.

However, these claims are largely anecdotal. Health experts point out that while fermented foods are generally good for the microbiome, there is no specific research on chickpea water. The sodium content, often high from cooking, could be a concern for those with hypertension.

What Does Science Say?

Fermentation is known to enhance the nutritional profile of foods. Chickpeas are rich in fibre, protein, and essential minerals. The process can also reduce anti-nutrients, making the water more digestible. Yet, the concentration of these benefits in the water alone remains unclear and unquantified.

Doctors advise caution. Uncontrolled fermentation can lead to harmful bacterial growth, especially if hygiene is compromised. For most people, a balanced diet with a variety of fermented foods like curd, idli, or kanji is a safer bet than relying on a single trendy drink.

How to Try It Safely

If you are curious, start small. Use organic chickpeas, soak them well, and discard the first rinse water. Cook fresh chickpeas, strain the water, and let it cool. Ferment it in a clean glass jar for 12-24 hours, depending on the ambient temperature. Store it in the fridge and consume within a day or two.

Begin with a tablespoon to see how your body reacts. Avoid it if you are on a low-sodium diet or have a compromised immune system. Pregnant women and children should consult a doctor before experimenting.

The Bottom Line

Chhole ka paani is not a miracle cure, but it is not harmful either, provided it is prepared properly. It can be a refreshing, tangy addition to your diet, but it should not replace medical treatment or a varied, nutrient-rich meal plan.

As with any trend, the key is moderation and informed choice. The wellness industry thrives on novelty, and this one is no different. Whether it becomes a lasting staple or fades away depends on how well it stands up to scrutiny.

For now, experts recommend treating it as a fun experiment rather than a health imperative. Watch this space for more evidence as researchers begin to study this age-old kitchen practice with modern tools.
