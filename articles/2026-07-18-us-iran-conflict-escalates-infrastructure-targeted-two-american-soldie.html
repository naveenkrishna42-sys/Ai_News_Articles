US-Iran conflict escalates: Infrastructure targeted, two American soldiers killed in Jordan

📅 2026-07-18 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two US service members killed in Jordan, one reported missing
  • US strikes on Iran continue for seventh consecutive night
  • Iran's supreme leader calls off interim peace agreement, warns of 'unforgettable lessons'

US Service Members Killed in Jordan

The United States has confirmed the deaths of two service members in Jordan, with a third reported missing. Officials have not yet disclosed the circumstances of the incident or the identities of those involved. The news comes as the US continues a seventh consecutive night of airstrikes against Iranian targets.

The Pentagon has not provided further details on the missing service member. Search operations are ongoing, according to a brief statement from US Central Command.

Infrastructure Targeted in Escalating Strikes

US airstrikes on Iran have shifted focus to infrastructure targets, according to reports from the region. The strikes, now in their seventh night, mark a significant escalation in the conflict between the two nations. Military analysts say the bombing campaign is aimed at disrupting Iran's logistical and supply networks.

No official casualty figures from the strikes have been released by Iranian authorities. Local media in Iran have reported damage to roads and bridges in several provinces, but these claims remain unverified.

Iran's Supreme Leader Breaks Off Peace Talks

Iran's Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, has called off an interim peace agreement with the United States, vowing that the country will teach Washington 'unforgettable lessons.' The decision came after weeks of backchannel negotiations reportedly brokered by Qatar and Oman.

In a televised address on Friday, the Supreme Leader accused the US of bad faith and aggression. 'The enemy will soon see the consequences of its miscalculations,' he said. The collapse of the interim deal removes the last diplomatic off-ramp from the current crisis.

Regional Fallout and International Reaction

The conflict has drawn in regional actors, with Jordan emerging as a flashpoint. The kingdom has long maintained a delicate balancing act between its alliance with the US and its proximity to Iran. The killing of US troops on Jordanian soil puts additional pressure on King Abdullah II's government.

Other Gulf states have called for restraint. Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates issued statements urging de-escalation, while Iraq has closed its airspace to military flights. The United Nations Security Council is expected to hold an emergency session in the coming days.

Oil prices have surged by nearly 12 percent since the start of the strikes, with Brent crude crossing $95 a barrel. Global markets remain volatile as traders assess the risk of a wider war.

The US State Department has advised American citizens to leave Iran and parts of the broader region immediately. Commercial flights out of Tehran and other major cities are heavily booked, with long queues at airports.

What happens next remains uncertain. Both sides have signalled they are prepared for a protracted conflict, but international mediators are exploring fresh ceasefire proposals. The coming week will be critical in determining whether the fighting expands or a new diplomatic push can take hold.
