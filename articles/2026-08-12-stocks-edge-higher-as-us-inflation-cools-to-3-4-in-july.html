Stocks Edge Higher as US Inflation Cools to 3.4% in July

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Consumer prices rose 0.1% in July, matching expectations
  • Annual inflation rate now stands at 3.4%
  • Cooling food and fuel costs drove the moderation
  • Wall Street near record high as AI stocks rally
  • Fed likely to hold rates steady at next meeting

Wall Street Opens Higher on Inflation Data

US stocks edged higher on Wednesday after the latest consumer price index report showed inflation cooling broadly in line with market expectations. The Dow Jones Industrial Average and the S&P 500 both opened in positive territory, building on a recent rally that has pushed major indices near record highs.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported that consumer prices rose 0.1% in July on a seasonally adjusted basis, matching what economists had forecast. On an annual basis, inflation now stands at 3.4%, down from 3.6% in June.

Food and Fuel Costs Ease

The moderation was driven largely by cooling food and fuel costs, which have been a persistent source of pressure on household budgets over the past year. Energy prices fell for the second consecutive month, while grocery prices showed only marginal gains.

Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy items, also came in softer than in recent months, though it remains above the Federal Reserve's 2% target. The report gives policymakers room to keep interest rates unchanged at their next meeting, a scenario that traders have largely priced in.

AI Stocks Lead the Rally

Technology stocks, particularly those tied to artificial intelligence, led the charge higher. Nvidia, Microsoft, and other AI-focused names extended their gains as investor appetite for the sector remains robust. The Nasdaq Composite outperformed the broader market, rising more than 0.5% in early trading.

Market observers noted that the combination of moderating inflation and strong corporate earnings from AI-related companies has created a favorable backdrop for equities. The S&P 500 is now within striking distance of its all-time closing high set earlier this year.

Fed Likely to Hold Rates Steady

Economists at ING said the cooling inflation data points to the Federal Reserve holding rates steady at its September meeting. Futures markets now imply a high probability that the central bank will keep its benchmark rate in the current range, with a rate cut not expected until later in the year at the earliest.

Fed officials have repeatedly said they need more confidence that inflation is on a sustainable path toward 2% before easing policy. Wednesday's report, while encouraging, is unlikely to shift that stance dramatically, especially with services inflation still running above pre-pandemic levels.

What to Watch Next

Investors will now turn their attention to upcoming retail sales data and quarterly earnings from major retailers, which will offer clues on consumer health. The Fed's annual Jackson Hole symposium later this month will also be closely watched for any signals on the timing of rate cuts.

For now, the market appears comfortable with the current trajectory. With inflation cooling and AI-driven earnings momentum intact, Wall Street is betting that the soft-landing scenario remains achievable. But a surprise in either direction could quickly alter that calculus.
