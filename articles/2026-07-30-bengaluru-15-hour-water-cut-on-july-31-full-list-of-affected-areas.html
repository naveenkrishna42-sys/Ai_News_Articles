Bengaluru 15-hour water cut on July 31: Full list of affected areas

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Water supply will be suspended from 10 am on July 31 to 1 am on August 1.
  • The disruption is due to repair work on a major water pipeline at the Thorekadanahalli pumping station.
  • Several areas in east and southeast Bengaluru, including Mahadevapura, Whitefield, and parts of KR Puram, will be affected.

Bengaluru residents in several parts of the city will face a 15-hour water supply disruption on Thursday, July 31. The Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board (BWSSB) has announced the shutdown in advance to allow for maintenance work on a key pipeline.

Timings and reason for the disruption

Water supply will be suspended from 10 am on July 31 until 1 am on August 1. The BWSSB said the disruption is necessary to carry out repairs on a major water pipeline at the Thorekadanahalli pumping station.

Officials stated that the maintenance is part of routine infrastructure upgrades. Residents are advised to store sufficient water in advance to manage during the cut-off period.

Full list of affected areas

The disruption will primarily hit areas in east and southeast Bengaluru. The BWSSB has released a list of localities that will be impacted.

  • Mahadevapura
  • Whitefield
  • Hoodi
  • KR Puram (parts)
  • Brookefield
  • Marathahalli
  • Kundalahalli
  • Varthur
  • Bellandur
  • Sarjapur Road (selected stretches)
  • Dommasandra
  • Devarabisanahalli

Residents in these areas should plan accordingly. Tanker water supply may be arranged for emergency needs, though the BWSSB has not yet confirmed details on that.

What residents should do

The BWSSB has urged residents to use water judiciously during the disruption period. Storing water in clean containers is recommended to meet basic needs for drinking, cooking, and sanitation.

Apartment complexes and residential layouts with overhead tanks are advised to fill them before 10 am on July 31. The board has not specified any alternate supply arrangements for individual households.

Officials have requested cooperation from the public. They said the work is essential to prevent future leaks and ensure long-term supply reliability in the affected zones.

Water supply is expected to resume in full capacity by early morning on August 1. Residents who face extended delays can contact the BWSSB helpline number for assistance.
