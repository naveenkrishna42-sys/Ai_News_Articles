Bengaluru experts weigh in on Ranbir Kapoor's Ramayana modern look

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Experts praise the film's fusion of traditional and modern design
  • Costume and production design seen as key to epic's appeal
  • Debate on authenticity versus creative liberty in mythological retellings

Bengaluru's fashion and film circles are abuzz with discussion over the recently unveiled look of Ranbir Kapoor in the upcoming Ramayana adaptation. The film, which has been in the making for years, finally offers a glimpse of its aesthetic direction, and city experts are eager to dissect every detail.

At the heart of the conversation is the film's 'modern aesthetics'—a deliberate choice that blends the epic's ancient roots with contemporary visual language. From the intricate embroidery on Kapoor's costume to the colour palette of the sets, the production seems to walk a tightrope between reverence and reinvention.

Fashion experts on costume design

Bengaluru-based designers note that the costumes in the Ramayana appear to draw from a wide range of influences. The use of rich fabrics, layered silhouettes, and subtle metallic accents gives the characters a timeless yet fresh appeal.

"There is a clear effort to make the epic relatable without losing its grandeur," says a city-based fashion consultant who has reviewed the stills. The choice of earthy tones mixed with jewel shades is being read as a nod to both tradition and modern minimalism.

Experts point out that such an approach is not new but has rarely been attempted on this scale. The challenge lies in ensuring that the modern touches do not overshadow the story's cultural weight.

Film industry perspective

Film critics and technicians in Bengaluru are equally engaged. They argue that the visual language of a mythological film often determines its success with younger audiences. The Ramayana team, they say, has taken a calculated risk by incorporating contemporary design elements.

"The audience today expects a certain polish and cinematic grandeur," observes a veteran art director who has worked on period dramas. The use of VFX and set design that blends real locations with digital backdrops is likely to be a talking point.

However, some experts caution against over-reliance on aesthetics. They stress that the narrative and performances, especially Kapoor's portrayal of Lord Ram, will ultimately decide the film's impact.

Balancing authenticity and creative liberty

This is where the debate gets interesting. Purists argue that mythological stories should adhere to traditional depictions, while others welcome creative interpretation. Bengaluru's cultural commentators see this as a healthy sign of evolving storytelling.

"Every generation reinterprets the epics in its own way," says a professor of cultural studies at a city university. "The key is to maintain the essence, not just the surface details." The response to the film's aesthetics, they add, reflects a broader shift in how Indians consume mythology.

The makers have not yet released detailed production notes, and the cast has remained tight-lipped about the creative process. But the early buzz suggests that the film's look will spark conversations well beyond the theatre.

As the release date approaches, the focus will now shift to the trailer and how the modern aesthetics translate into motion. Bengaluru's experts, for now, are watching closely—and taking notes.
