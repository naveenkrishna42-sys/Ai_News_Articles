US air defence munitions low? Trump shelved wider Iran strikes: Report

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Multiple reports indicate the US paused broader military action against Iran.
  • Concerns over low air defence munitions and ongoing peace talks in Oman are cited as reasons.
  • The Trump administration has not officially confirmed the munitions shortage, but advisors raised alarms.

New Delhi: The United States may have held back from escalating military strikes against Iran due to a shortage of air defence munitions, according to a series of reports from international media outlets. The development comes as the Trump administration navigates a delicate balance between military pressure and diplomatic negotiations.

Reports of a Pause in Strikes

Multiple news organisations, including The New York Times, The Guardian, and the Financial Times, have reported that the US paused its attacks on Iran for a second day. The pause coincides with ongoing peace talks brokered by Oman, which are also exploring a transit deal for the Strait of Hormuz.

A US official told NDTV that President Donald Trump paused the Iran strikes "to give peace talks some space." The official did not elaborate on the specific conditions for resuming military action.

Munitions Concerns

The Times of India, citing sources, reported that a key factor behind the decision to shelve wider strikes was the low supply of US air defence munitions. The report suggests that military advisors raised alarms about the sustainability of a prolonged campaign against Iranian air defences.

The Pentagon has not officially commented on the status of its munitions stockpiles in the region. However, the reports indicate that the issue was a major point of discussion in the White House Situation Room before the decision to pause was made.

Diplomatic Backchannel

Oman has emerged as a critical mediator in the conflict. The Financial Times reported that the Gulf nation is pushing for a transit deal that would ensure safe passage for oil tankers through the Strait of Hormuz, a vital chokepoint for global energy supplies.

The Guardian confirmed that the US has paused strikes as these talks gain momentum. The report did not specify a timeline for the pause, but noted that the situation remains fluid.

The New York Times, in its report titled "Trump Holds Off on Major War Escalation Against Iran as Advisers Raise Concerns," highlighted the internal divisions within the administration. Some advisors reportedly favoured a more aggressive posture, while others cautioned against overreach.

What to Watch

The coming days will be critical. The success of the Oman-mediated talks will likely determine whether the pause in strikes becomes a longer-term ceasefire or a temporary lull before a wider conflict. The status of US air defence munitions will also be a closely watched factor as the administration weighs its next move.
