At least 27 killed as Russia and Ukraine trade heavy attacks

📅 2026-08-04 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 27 people killed in cross-border attacks between Russia and Ukraine.
  • Russian officials report seven killed and 40 injured after Ukrainian drone hit a crowded beach.
  • Moscow says 320 Ukrainian drones downed; strikes hit warehouses in Moscow and St Petersburg.
  • Casualties and damage continue to rise as both sides intensify long-range strikes.

At least 27 people have been killed as Russia and Ukraine launched heavy aerial attacks against each other, with officials on both sides reporting civilian casualties and damage to infrastructure. The exchanges mark one of the deadliest 24-hour periods in recent weeks, raising fresh concerns about the conflict's trajectory.

Deadly drone strike on crowded beach

Russian officials said a Ukrainian drone struck a busy beach, killing seven people and injuring at least 40 others. The attack, which took place in a coastal area, sent holidaymakers scrambling for cover as explosions rang out. Emergency services were deployed to the scene, with the wounded rushed to nearby hospitals.

Separately, Russian authorities reported that 11 people were killed in Ukrainian strikes across several regions. The claims could not be independently verified, and Ukraine has not yet commented on the specific incidents.

Massive drone barrage over Russian cities

In a significant escalation, the Russian military said it had downed 320 Ukrainian drones overnight, including over Moscow and St Petersburg. The defence ministry said six people were killed in the attacks, and warehouses in both cities were damaged. Videos circulating on social media showed fiery explosions and plumes of smoke rising above residential and industrial areas.

The scale of the drone assault, if confirmed, would be among the largest since the start of the war. Russian air defence systems were reported to be active for hours, with residents in several districts hearing repeated blasts.

Ukrainian cities also under fire

Ukrainian officials reported similar attacks on their territory, with at least 20 people killed in Russian strikes on cities and towns. Residential buildings, power substations and water facilities were reportedly hit, leaving thousands without electricity or running water. Local authorities said rescue operations were ongoing, and the toll could rise as debris is cleared.

The attacks have intensified a pattern of long-range exchanges that have become a hallmark of the conflict, with both sides using drones and missiles to target infrastructure and civilian areas. Neither side has shown signs of de-escalation, and diplomatic efforts remain stalled.

International reaction and humanitarian toll

The United Nations and several governments have called for restraint, but no formal ceasefire talks are scheduled. Humanitarian agencies have warned that the repeated attacks are worsening conditions for civilians on both sides, with many families forced to flee or shelter for days. The true number of casualties remains unclear, as officials on both sides continue to tally the dead and wounded.

In Russia, the beach attack has drawn particular attention, as it struck a location associated with leisure and summer holidays. Local authorities have announced three days of mourning in the affected region. In Ukraine, officials have repeated calls for more air defence systems from Western allies.

What to watch

As both sides continue to trade strikes, the focus now shifts to whether the recent escalation will prompt a new round of diplomatic engagement or lead to further military escalation. Analysts will be watching for any changes in Western military aid, particularly air defence supplies to Ukraine, and for Russia's next moves in response to the drone attacks on its major cities.
