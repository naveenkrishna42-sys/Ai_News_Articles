VVS Laxman Defends BCCI CoE Amid India's Injury Crisis

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • VVS Laxman says CoE is not merely a rehab centre, counters criticism over India's injury crisis
  • BCCI secretary Devajit Saikia to visit CoE to take stock of the situation and meet Laxman
  • Injuries to Jasprit Bumrah and Sai Sudharsan have sparked questions about the facility's role

As India's injury list grows, the spotlight has turned to the BCCI's Centre of Excellence (CoE) in Bengaluru. With Jasprit Bumrah and Sai Sudharsan among those sidelined, questions are being raised about the facility's effectiveness. VVS Laxman, who heads the CoE, has pushed back against the criticism.

"The CoE is not merely a rehab centre," Laxman said, countering the narrative that the facility has failed in its primary role. His comments come at a time when the board is under pressure to explain the recurring fitness issues plaguing its top cricketers.

More Than Just Rehabilitation

Laxman's defence of the CoE is rooted in its broader mandate. The facility, he suggests, is designed to nurture talent and support players across their careers, not just patch them up after injuries. "We work on injury prevention, player development, and long-term fitness," he indicated, pushing back against the narrow framing of the CoE as a repair shop.

The head of the CoE also cautioned against using the word 'blame' in discussions about the injury crisis. He argued that injuries are an inherent part of a fast-paced sport like cricket and that the focus should be on solutions rather than assigning fault.

The Bumrah Factor

The absence of Jasprit Bumrah, arguably India's most important bowler across formats, has amplified the scrutiny. His recurring back issues have kept him out of action, and questions have been raised about whether the CoE's workload management protocols are adequate. Sai Sudharsan's setback has added to the concern, particularly with a busy international calendar ahead.

These injuries have forced the selectors to constantly reshuffle the squad. The management's reliance on the CoE to keep players match-fit has been tested, and the results have not always been convincing.

BCCI Steps In

Recognising the gravity of the situation, BCCI secretary Devajit Saikia is set to visit the CoE to take stock of the crisis. He is expected to meet Laxman and other members of the support staff to understand the root causes and explore ways to strengthen the system.

This high-level visit signals that the board is not taking the matter lightly. Saikia's assessment could lead to changes in how the CoE operates, from recovery protocols to training regimens. The meeting is likely to be a frank exchange on what is working and what needs a rethink.

What's Going Wrong?

Critics have pointed to a possible mismatch between the demands of international cricket and the CoE's monitoring systems. Some have questioned whether players are being pushed too hard in training or not given enough time to recover. Others have noted that the gap between domestic and international cricket has widened, making players more susceptible to breakdowns.

Laxman, however, insists that the CoE is not the problem. He argues that the facility has helped many players return stronger and has played a key role in India's bench strength. The challenge, he says, is managing the expectations that come with a packed schedule.

The coming days will be crucial. Saikia's visit and subsequent review will determine whether the CoE needs a structural overhaul or simply a better alignment with the team's needs. For now, Laxman's message is clear: the CoE is an essential part of Indian cricket's ecosystem, not a convenient scapegoat.

As the board and the CoE work to find common ground, the focus will be on ensuring that players like Bumrah and Sudharsan return to the field fitter and more resilient. The outcome of these discussions could shape India's approach to player fitness for years to come.
