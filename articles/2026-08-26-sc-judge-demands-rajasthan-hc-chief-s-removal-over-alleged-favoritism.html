SC Judge Demands Rajasthan HC Chief’s Removal Over Alleged Favoritism

Key points
  • Justice Sandeep Mehta alleges favoritism and roster misuse at Rajasthan High Court.
  • CJI Surya Kant has been urged to replace Acting Chief Justice SP Sharma.
  • Mehta's concerns stem from appeals being ignored despite clear evidence.

Justice Sandeep Mehta Raises Serious Allegations Against Rajasthan HC

Justice Sandeep Mehta, a Supreme Court judge, has publicly urged Chief Justice of India Surya Kant to replace Acting Chief Justice of Rajasthan High Court SP Sharma. Mehta's move comes after allegations of favoritism and misuse of office at the Rajasthan High Court. The Supreme Court has been approached with a plea seeking the transfer of Sharma, citing clear evidence of his alleged misconduct.

Allegations of Favoritism and Roster Misuse

Justice Mehta's concerns are rooted in the alleged favoritism and roster misuse at the Rajasthan High Court. He has stated that despite clear evidence of Sharma's favoritism, the appeals filed against him have been ignored. This has raised serious questions about the functioning of the judiciary in Rajasthan.

Impact on the Judiciary

The allegations against Sharma have significant implications for the judiciary. If proven true, they could set a dangerous precedent, undermining the integrity of the judicial system. It is crucial that such allegations are thoroughly investigated to ensure the fair administration of justice.

CJI Surya Kant’s Response

Chief Justice of India Surya Kant has been urged to take immediate action on Mehta's plea. The CJI has a crucial role in maintaining the independence and integrity of the judiciary. His response to this plea will be closely watched by the legal community.

Broader Implications

The situation in Rajasthan is not isolated. Similar allegations of favoritism and misuse of office have been reported in other high courts across India. This raises serious concerns about the functioning of the judiciary at the state level. It is essential that such issues are addressed promptly to restore public faith in the judicial system.
