Syria court sentences Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Syrian court issues death sentence against Bashar al-Assad in absentia
  • Former official Atef Najib also sentenced to death in the same case
  • Verdict relates to killings and torture during Syria's civil war

Damascus court delivers landmark verdict

A Syrian court has sentenced ousted president Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia, according to reports confirmed by multiple international media outlets on Tuesday. The verdict, issued by a Damascus criminal court, covers charges of killings and torture during the country's decade-long civil war.

The sentence is largely symbolic — Assad, who fled Syria in late 2024 after rebel forces seized the capital, remains at large, reportedly in Russia. Yet the ruling marks the first formal judicial action against the former ruler by Syrian courts since his ouster.

Co-defendant Atef Najib also sentenced

The court also handed a death sentence to Atef Najib, a former Syrian official who served as head of the political security branch in Daraa. Najib's role in the early crackdown on protests in 2011 is well documented; his actions are widely seen as a trigger for the uprising that spiraled into a full-blown conflict.

Both men were tried in absentia, with no legal representation present during the proceedings. Court officials have not disclosed whether either man has been formally notified of the verdict.

Charges of crimes against humanity

The charges against Assad include ordering military operations that killed tens of thousands of civilians, systematic torture in government-run detention centers, and use of chemical weapons. The court's ruling described these acts as crimes against humanity, aligning with findings by international bodies like the UN Commission of Inquiry.

Human rights groups have documented the use of barrel bombs, sieges, and chemical attacks during the war, which claimed over 500,000 lives according to UN estimates. The Syrian judiciary, rebuilt under the post-Assad administration, has vowed to pursue accountability for war crimes.

Legal and political implications

Legal experts note that in absentia death sentences are difficult to enforce, especially since Assad is believed to be in Russia, which has refused to extradite him. However, the verdict could complicate any future diplomatic negotiations involving Assad's status.

The Syrian government has said it will pursue international arrest warrants through Interpol, though cooperation from Moscow remains unlikely. Analysts suggest the ruling may also pressure other countries to reconsider their stance on Assad's exile.

Reactions and next steps

Reactions to the verdict have been mixed. Victims' families in Syria have welcomed the decision as a step toward justice, while some international observers question its legal legitimacy given the lack of due process in absentia trials.

Meanwhile, the Syrian government has indicated it will continue investigating other senior officials from the Assad era. The court's decision sets a precedent for future prosecutions.

What happens next is uncertain: Assad remains in exile, and the practical enforcement of this sentence hinges on international cooperation that has yet to materialize. The verdict, however, signals a clear break from the past and a commitment by Syria's new authorities to pursue accountability.
