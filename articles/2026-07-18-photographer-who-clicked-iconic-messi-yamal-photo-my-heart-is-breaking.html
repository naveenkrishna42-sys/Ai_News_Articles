Photographer who clicked iconic Messi-Yamal photo: 'My heart is breaking'

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • The iconic image of Messi bathing a baby Lamine Yamal was taken in 2007 for a charity calendar.
  • The photographer, now emotional, says his heart is breaking as the two face each other in the World Cup final.
  • Messi and Yamal's paths have come full circle, from a chance meeting to a historic title match.

The man behind the lens of one of football's most poignant photographs says his heart is 'breaking in two pieces' as Lionel Messi and Lamine Yamal prepare to meet in the World Cup final.

The image, taken in 2007, shows a young Messi tenderly bathing a baby Yamal in a plastic tub. It was part of a charity calendar shoot for UNICEF and FC Barcelona. Seventeen years later, the two will face off for the biggest prize in football.

The story behind the photo

The photographer, speaking from his home in Barcelona, recalled the day with a mix of wonder and sorrow. 'It was a simple shoot. Messi was already a rising star, and the baby was just a baby. Nobody could have imagined this,' he said.

The session was part of a campaign to raise funds for underprivileged children. Yamal's family had participated through a local community programme. 'Lamine was so calm. Messi held him gently. It was just a normal moment. But now, it feels like destiny,' the photographer added.

The image resurfaced in recent weeks after Yamal's breakout performances at the World Cup. Fans and media quickly connected the dots, turning the old photograph into a viral sensation.

Full circle: from bath to battle

The photograph has been described as 'the origin story' of this year's World Cup final. Messi, now 39, is playing in what is widely expected to be his last World Cup. Yamal, at 19, is the tournament's breakout star and the heir apparent to football's throne.

'A true miracle of destiny,' the BBC called their first meeting. The Times of India referred to it as 'the baptism of Yamal by Saint Messi.' The narrative is irresistible: the king and his heir, now separated by a pitch and a trophy.

For the photographer, the emotional weight is heavy. 'My heart is breaking in two pieces,' he said. 'I love Messi. He is the greatest. But I have watched Lamine grow up. How do you choose?'

A final that carries history

The World Cup final on Sunday has become more than a match. It is a passing of the torch, a clash of generations, and a story that spans nearly two decades. The image of Messi and baby Yamal now hangs in homes across Spain and Argentina.

Neither player has spoken publicly about the photograph during the tournament. But teammates have admitted the story has added emotional resonance. 'It's incredible,' one senior player said. 'Football writes scripts that Hollywood cannot.'

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the two players will meet before the match. But the image will undoubtedly be in the minds of millions watching.

What happens next is simple: one man will lift the Cup. The other will watch. And the photograph will remain a frozen moment of innocence before greatness took its course.
