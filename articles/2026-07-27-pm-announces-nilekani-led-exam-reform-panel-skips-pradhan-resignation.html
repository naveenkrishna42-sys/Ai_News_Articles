PM announces Nilekani-led exam reform panel, skips Pradhan resignation and student protests

📅 2026-07-27 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi announced a task force led by Nandan Nilekani for exam reforms.
  • No comment was made on Union Minister Dharmendra Pradhan's resignation.
  • Student protests and coaching class controversies were not addressed.
  • Opposition Congress dismissed the panel as a diversion tactic.
  • Task force includes tech experts, space scientists, and former spymasters.

Task Force to Overhaul Exam System

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday announced the formation of a high-powered task force to recommend reforms in India's examination system. The panel will be led by technology expert Nandan Nilekani, co-founder of Infosys and former chairman of the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI).

The task force includes a space scientist from ISRO, a former spymaster, and other domain experts. Its mandate is to suggest measures to make examinations more transparent, secure, and student-friendly.

Silence on Pradhan's Resignation

The Prime Minister, however, made no reference to the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan. Speculation has been rife in political circles about the reasons behind Pradhan's exit, but the PM's office has not issued any statement.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the resignation has been accepted. Sources in the government indicate that a formal announcement may be made later this week, but no timeline has been provided.

Students' Movement Ignored

Modi also did not address the ongoing students' movement demanding an end to the coaching class culture and a complete overhaul of the competitive exam system. Thousands of students have been protesting in Delhi and other cities, calling for a single national entrance test and an end to the high-pressure coaching industry.

Rediff.com reported that the slogan 'We Need To Stop Coaching Classes' has gained traction among student groups. The protests have been largely peaceful, but there have been sporadic clashes with police in some areas.

Congress Calls It a Diversion

The Congress party was quick to dismiss the task force announcement as a diversionary tactic. Party spokesperson said in a press briefing that the government is trying to shift attention away from the resignation of the education minister and the growing student unrest.

Congress leaders argued that the panel is a 'cosmetic measure' and that real reforms require political will, not just expert committees. They demanded a parliamentary inquiry into the exam system and the coaching industry.

Experts on the Panel

The composition of the task force has drawn attention for its mix of talent. Besides Nilekani, the panel includes a former director of the Intelligence Bureau, a senior scientist from the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), and an education policy expert from a leading university.

NDTV reported that the task force has been asked to submit its report within three months. It will examine issues such as paper leaks, standardisation of exams, and the role of private coaching centres.

What happens next depends on how quickly the government addresses the twin crises of the minister's resignation and the students' movement. The task force will meet for the first time next week, but without clarity on these issues, the panel's work may be overshadowed by political turbulence.
