Bitcoin Dips Below $65,000 as ETF Inflows Counter Fork Worries

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin fell below $65,000, pulled down by lingering concerns over a possible network fork.
  • Spot Bitcoin ETF inflows continued to provide support, cushioning the downside.
  • Traders remain cautious, watching for clarity on the fork and macroeconomic signals.

Bitcoin's Slide Under $65,000

Bitcoin slipped below the $65,000 mark on Sunday, extending a period of jittery trading as the market juggled fears of an impending network fork against steady inflows into spot exchange-traded funds (ETFs). The cryptocurrency's pullback comes after a volatile week, with prices swinging as traders weighed technical signals against broader macroeconomic cues.

Data from major exchanges showed Bitcoin trading hands at roughly $64,800 during early Asian hours, down about 1.2% from the previous close. The dip, while modest, underscores the fragility of current sentiment, with many investors choosing to sit on the sidelines until the fork question is resolved.

Fork Fears Linger

The spectre of a hard fork has been a recurring theme in crypto markets, and this time is no different. A potential split in the blockchain could create uncertainty around asset ownership and transaction processing, prompting some holders to trim positions. Historically, such events have led to short-term volatility, though outcomes have varied widely depending on community consensus and miner support.

Developers have not yet confirmed a definitive timeline, and conflicting statements from key figures have only added to the confusion. One prominent mining pool hinted at support for a code change, while another warned of network instability. This lack of clarity has left traders guessing, with options markets pricing in elevated implied volatility for the coming weeks.

ETF Inflows Provide a Cushion

Offsetting some of the bearish pressure, spot Bitcoin ETFs continued to attract capital. Recent filings show net inflows of roughly $250 million over the past five trading sessions, a sign that institutional appetite remains intact despite the price wobble. Fund managers note that these products offer a regulated avenue for exposure, which has proven popular among pension funds and family offices.

One analyst pointed out that the ETF bid has effectively created a floor under prices. "Every time Bitcoin dips, we see accumulation through the funds," he said. "It's not enough to spark a breakout, but it's preventing a freefall." This dynamic has kept the trading range relatively tight, with Bitcoin oscillating between $63,500 and $67,000 over the last fortnight.

Macro Factors in Play

Beyond the fork, traders are monitoring the broader economic environment. US inflation data released last week came in slightly above expectations, stoking fears that the Federal Reserve might delay rate cuts. Higher-for-longer interest rates typically weigh on risk assets, including cryptocurrencies, as they raise the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding investments.

Meanwhile, the dollar index has firmed, adding another headwind. A stronger greenback often correlates with weaker crypto prices, as it signals reduced liquidity in global markets. Indian investors, too, are keeping a close watch, with local exchanges reporting a slight uptick in trading volumes as some retail players look to buy the dips.

What Lies Ahead

For now, the market remains in a wait-and-see mode. The immediate trigger would be a clear statement from developers on the fork's status, which could either alleviate or amplify concerns. On the data front, upcoming US jobless claims and retail sales figures will offer fresh clues on the economy's trajectory.

Bitcoin's ability to hold above the $63,000 support level will be critical in the short term. A decisive break lower could open the door to further losses, while a rebound above $66,500 might signal renewed bullish momentum. As always, the crypto market's propensity for sudden shifts means traders should brace for anything.
