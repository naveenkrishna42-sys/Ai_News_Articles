FBI Probes Argentina FA Chief in $300 Million World Cup Money-Laundering Case

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • FBI is investigating a $300 million money-laundering case linked to Argentina's 2026 World Cup campaign.
  • Argentina FA president Claudio Tapia had his phone seized by FBI agents as he was leaving the United States.
  • The Argentine Football Association has denied reports that Tapia was formally summoned by US courts.

FBI Investigation Targets Argentina FA President

The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) has launched a massive probe into a $300 million money-laundering scheme linked to Argentina's campaign for the 2026 FIFA World Cup. The investigation has put the president of the Argentine Football Association (AFA), Claudio Tapia, squarely in the crosshairs of American law enforcement.

Tapia, who was in the United States for the World Cup, had his mobile phone seized by FBI agents at the last moment before he left the country. The dramatic move underscores the seriousness of the probe, which is believed to involve financial flows connected to the South American nation's bid and preparations for the tournament.

Phone Seized at Departure

According to multiple reports, the seizure happened just as Tapia was preparing to board a flight back to Argentina. The AFA president returned home without the coveted World Cup trophy and now faces a trio of major problems: the FBI investigation, internal federation politics, and the fallout from the probe.

The Argentine Football Association has issued a statement denying that Tapia was formally summoned by US courts. However, the denial does not address the FBI's seizure of his personal device or the broader money-laundering investigation.

World Cup Crisis Context

The probe comes on the heels of what has been described as a crisis surrounding the 2026 World Cup. Argentina, which won the 2022 tournament in Qatar, was one of the host nations for the 2026 edition, co-hosting with Uruguay and Paraguay. The financial dealings behind the bid and the preparations have now come under intense scrutiny.

The $300 million figure mentioned in the investigation suggests a significant scale of alleged financial impropriety. Investigators are reportedly looking at whether funds meant for the World Cup campaign were laundered through various channels.

The AFA has been forced to confront the truth about its president being called before the American judiciary, though the exact nature of the summons remains contested. The federation has not provided details on what Tapia discussed with US authorities or whether he is a target or a witness in the probe.

What Comes Next

Tapia's return to Argentina without the trophy and with his phone confiscated has raised questions about the stability of the AFA leadership. The federation now faces internal pressure from clubs and officials demanding answers about the investigation and its potential impact on Argentine football.

The FBI is expected to continue its probe, likely calling for more documents and testimony from AFA officials. Whether Tapia will return to the US for further questioning or face charges remains an open question. For now, Argentine football watches and waits as the legal storm gathers.
