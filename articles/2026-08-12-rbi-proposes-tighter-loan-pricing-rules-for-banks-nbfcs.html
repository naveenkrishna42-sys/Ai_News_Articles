RBI proposes tighter loan pricing rules for banks, NBFCs

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Draft norms propose external benchmark-linked pricing for floating rate loans.
  • Risk-based pricing framework under consultation to reflect borrower risk more accurately.
  • Banks and NBFCs have until mid-October to submit feedback.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has floated a discussion paper proposing stricter loan pricing norms for banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs). The move aims to bring greater transparency to how interest rates are set on retail and MSME loans.

The central bank has suggested that all floating-rate loans, including those to individuals and small businesses, be linked to an external benchmark such as the repo rate or a market-determined rate. Currently, many lenders use internal benchmarks like the marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR), which the RBI says may not transmit policy rate changes quickly.

Risk-based pricing in focus

The paper also outlines a risk-based pricing framework, under which lenders would be required to price loans according to the borrower's credit risk profile. This means that instead of a one-size-fits-all rate, borrowers with stronger credit histories could see lower interest rates, while riskier borrowers might face higher charges.

The RBI has argued that such a structure would improve credit risk assessment and reduce cross-subsidisation between borrowers. Lenders have been asked to ensure that loan pricing is not used to mask operational inefficiencies or to earn excessive spreads.

What the norms propose

Key proposals in the discussion paper include:

  • Mandatory linking of all new floating-rate loans to external benchmarks.
  • Clear disclosure of the spread charged over the benchmark, and a cap on how often this spread can be revised.
  • Restrictions on resetting interest rates at intervals shorter than one year for retail loans.
  • A requirement for lenders to explain the rationale behind any change in spread to borrowers.

The RBI has also proposed that lenders should offer borrowers the option to switch from an internal benchmark to an external one without additional charges. This, it says, would enhance competition and give borrowers more flexibility.

Industry concerns

Bankers and NBFC executives have expressed concerns that tighter norms could compress margins, especially for smaller lenders that rely on internal models to price risk. Some have pointed out that linking all loans to external benchmarks could make rates more volatile, affecting borrowers' repayment capacity.

There is also worry that the proposed risk-based pricing framework may require significant upgrades to data collection and analytics capabilities. Smaller NBFCs, in particular, may find it difficult to comply without substantial investment in technology.

The RBI has, however, clarified that the discussion paper is not a final directive. It has invited comments from stakeholders, including banks, NBFCs, and consumer groups, by mid-October. The central bank will finalise the rules only after reviewing the feedback.

What this means for borrowers

For the common borrower, the proposed norms could translate into faster rate cuts when the RBI lowers policy rates. Currently, many banks take months to pass on such cuts, and some do not transmit them fully. An external benchmark would make this transmission more direct.

At the same time, borrowers with weaker credit scores may see their rates rise, as lenders price in the actual risk. This could make borrowing more expensive for those with limited credit history or irregular income.

Analysts say the move is part of a broader regulatory push to make the financial system more transparent and customer-friendly. The RBI has been tightening norms on everything from loan recovery to digital lending over the past year.

The next few months will be crucial as the industry responds to the proposals. The final rules, once issued, could reshape how interest rates are set across the lending spectrum in India.
