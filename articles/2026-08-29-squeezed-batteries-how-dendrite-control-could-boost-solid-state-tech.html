Squeezed batteries: How dendrite control could boost solid-state tech

📅 2026-08-29 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Squeezing batteries redirects dendrite growth sideways, preventing short circuits
  • Thousands of cycles achieved in lab tests, a significant improvement over current tech
  • Broader implications for electric vehicles, smartphones, and renewable energy storage

What Are Dendrites and Why Are They a Problem?

Dendrites are microscopic, tree-like structures that form during the charging process of batteries. In solid-state batteries, these dendrites can grow uncontrollably, piercing the separator between the anode and cathode and causing short circuits. This limits the number of charge cycles a battery can undergo, reducing its overall lifespan.

How Squeezing Prevents Short Circuits

Researchers at SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory have developed a method to redirect dendrite growth sideways, away from the separator. By applying a gentle squeeze to the battery, they can control the direction of dendrite formation. This prevents the dendrites from piercing the separator and causing short circuits.

This breakthrough could significantly improve the lifespan of solid-state batteries, making them more viable for use in electric vehicles, smartphones, and renewable energy storage systems.

Lab Results and Broader Implications

In lab tests, batteries using this squeezing technique survived thousands of charge cycles, a marked improvement over current solid-state battery technology. This could mean longer-lasting batteries for consumers and more efficient energy storage for the grid.

The implications of this research are vast. If this technique can be scaled up and implemented in commercial batteries, it could lead to a new era of energy storage technology. This could have a significant impact on the automotive industry, where longer-lasting batteries could mean longer-range electric vehicles and reduced charging times.

Challenges and Future Directions

While this research is promising, there are still challenges to overcome. The squeezing technique needs to be refined and made more practical for commercial use. Additionally, the cost of solid-state batteries needs to be reduced to make them more competitive with traditional lithium-ion batteries.

Despite these challenges, the potential benefits of this research are significant. If successful, it could pave the way for a new generation of batteries that are safer, longer-lasting, and more efficient.
