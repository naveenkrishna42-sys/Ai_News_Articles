Bonalu festival begins in Hyderabad's Old City amid tight security

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bonalu festival commenced in Hyderabad's Old City with tight security measures in place.
  • Thousands of devotees are expected to participate in the celebrations over the coming days.
  • Authorities have deployed additional police forces and surveillance to ensure a smooth conduct.

Festive fervour grips Old City

The annual Bonalu festival kicked off in Hyderabad's Old City on Sunday, with the historic neighbourhood transforming into a sea of colour and devotion. The air was thick with the scent of incense and marigold as thousands of devotees gathered to offer prayers to Goddess Mahakali.

Security was visibly tightened across the area, with police personnel deployed in strength at key junctions and temple premises. Officials said the arrangements were made to ensure a peaceful celebration, given the large crowds expected over the next few days.

Security measures on high alert

Additional police forces, including women constables and home guards, have been stationed at major routes and sensitive spots. CCTV surveillance and drone monitoring have also been put in place to keep a close watch on the proceedings.

Traffic diversions have been announced in parts of the Old City to manage the influx of devotees. Commuters have been advised to plan their routes in advance, as several roads will remain closed or one-way until the festival concludes.

Devotees throng temples

From early morning, queues began forming outside prominent temples dedicated to the goddess. Women, dressed in traditional sarees, carried brass pots decorated with neem leaves and turmeric — a hallmark of the Bonalu tradition.

The rhythmic beat of drums and the chanting of hymns added to the charged atmosphere. Many devotees were seen performing the 'Rangam' ritual, where a woman, believed to be possessed by the goddess, predicts the year ahead.

A tradition steeped in history

Bonalu, which dates back to the 19th century, is celebrated annually in Hyderabad and parts of Telangana. The festival is believed to have originated after a plague outbreak in the region, when the goddess was invoked to ward off the disease.

Since then, the festival has grown into a major cultural event, drawing participants from across the state. The Old City remains the epicentre of the celebrations, with its narrow lanes echoing with devotion and song.

What to expect in the coming days

The festivities will continue over the next few weeks, with different neighbourhoods taking turns to celebrate. The grand finale, known as the 'Ghatam' procession, will see decorated pots carried to the River Musi, marking the end of the festival.

Authorities have urged the public to cooperate with security personnel and follow the designated routes. They have also advised against spreading rumours, stressing that all necessary precautions have been taken.

As the festival unfolds, all eyes will be on the seamless coordination between the police, civic bodies, and the devotees. The coming days will reveal whether the arrangements hold up under the pressure of the crowds — a test of both faith and administration.
