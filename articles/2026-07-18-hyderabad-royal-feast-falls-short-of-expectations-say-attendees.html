Hyderabad royal feast falls short of expectations, say attendees

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • The royal feast organized in Hyderabad failed to impress many attendees.
  • Critics pointed to a lack of authentic traditional elements and grandeur.
  • Organizers have not yet responded to the criticism.

Hyderabad: A much-hyped royal feast, organized to celebrate the city's culinary heritage, has left many attendees disappointed. The event, held at a historic venue, promised a taste of Nizam-era opulence but fell short of expectations, according to several guests who spoke to The New Indian Express.

Promises of grandeur fall flat

The feast was advertised as a lavish spread featuring traditional Hyderabadi dishes, including biryani, kebabs, and desserts. Attendees expected an experience reminiscent of the royal kitchens of the past. However, many said the execution lacked finesse.

One guest described the food as "average at best," noting that the spices and flavors did not match the rich standards associated with Hyderabadi cuisine. Another attendee said the presentation was underwhelming, with buffet-style service replacing the expected table-side serving.

Decor and ambiance draw criticism

The venue, though historic, was described as poorly lit and inadequately decorated. Guests said there were no cultural performances or traditional music to set the mood, which is typical for such events. The lack of attention to detail was a common complaint.

Several attendees pointed out that the event felt rushed and lacked the ceremonial touch that royal feasts demand. One person said it seemed more like a commercial gathering than a celebration of heritage.

Organizers had earlier claimed the feast would showcase authentic recipes passed down through generations. But many guests said the dishes tasted generic and did not stand out.

Mixed reactions from food connoisseurs

Food critics and local historians who attended the event also voiced their concerns. A prominent food blogger said the biryani was dry and the kebabs lacked the smoky flavor that defines Hyderabadi cuisine. The desserts, including the famous double ka meetha, were described as overly sweet and poorly prepared.

Another critic noted that the event failed to capture the spirit of the Nizam era, which is known for its attention to detail and love for fine dining. The disappointment was palpable among those who had high expectations.

Organizers have not yet issued a statement addressing the criticism. It remains unclear whether the event was a one-time affair or part of a series.

In the coming days, the organizers may face pressure to clarify their vision and address the feedback. For now, the feast will be remembered more for its shortcomings than its successes.
