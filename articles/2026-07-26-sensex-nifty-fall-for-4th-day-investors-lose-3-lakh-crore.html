Sensex, Nifty Fall for 4th Day; Investors Lose ₹3 Lakh Crore

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty 50 fell for the fourth consecutive session.
  • Investors lost ₹3 lakh crore in a single day.
  • Oil prices surged to $99 per barrel, stoking inflation fears.

Markets Extend Losing Streak

Indian equity benchmarks extended their slide for the fourth straight session on Thursday, as persistent global headwinds and rising oil prices weighed on sentiment. The BSE Sensex dropped over 800 points, while the Nifty 50 slipped below the 24,500 mark during intraday trading.

The sell-off wiped out nearly ₹3 lakh crore from investor portfolios in a single day, according to exchange data. The sustained decline has now pulled both indices into negative territory for the month, with the Sensex losing more than 3% since last week.

Oil at $99 a Barrel Stings Sentiment

Crude oil prices breached the $99 per barrel mark, inching closer to the psychologically important $100 level. The surge comes amid supply concerns and geopolitical tensions in the Middle East. For India, a net importer of crude, higher oil prices lead to greater import bills, widening the trade deficit and putting pressure on the rupee.

Analysts said the spike in crude could force the Reserve Bank of India to keep interest rates higher for longer, dampening hopes of a near-term rate cut. The rupee also weakened against the dollar, adding to the uncertainty in currency markets.

Broad-Based Selling Across Sectors

All major sectoral indices ended in the red, with banking, auto, and metal stocks bearing the brunt of the sell-off. The Nifty Bank index fell nearly 1.5%, dragged by declines in heavyweight lenders like HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank. Auto stocks such as Maruti Suzuki and Tata Motors also slipped on concerns over input cost inflation.

Midcap and smallcap stocks were not spared, with the BSE Midcap index falling 1.2% and the BSE Smallcap index losing 1.4%. The broader market weakness suggests that the selling pressure has spread beyond large-cap stocks.

Global Cues Turn Sour

Overseas markets also provided little support. Wall Street ended lower overnight as strong US economic data raised fears that the Federal Reserve may keep rates elevated. Asian peers like Japan’s Nikkei and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng traded in the red, tracking the negative sentiment from the US.

Foreign portfolio investors have been net sellers this week, pulling out over ₹6,000 crore from Indian equities. Domestic institutional investors have partially offset the outflows, but their buying has not been enough to stem the decline.

Market participants now await the monthly derivatives expiry next week, which could lead to heightened volatility. Analysts advise caution and suggest investors avoid making large bets until clarity emerges on the oil price trajectory and global central bank moves.
