Three Madhya Pradesh women arrested for jewellery thefts in Chennai's T Nagar

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Three women from Madhya Pradesh were arrested for thefts in T Nagar, Chennai.
  • They targeted shoppers, stealing jewellery and cash worth over Rs 12 lakh.
  • Police recovered stolen items and are investigating possible links to a larger gang.

Arrests in T Nagar

Chennai police have arrested three women from Madhya Pradesh for allegedly stealing jewellery and cash from shoppers in the busy T Nagar area. The arrests were made late Wednesday evening following a series of complaints from victims who reported losing their valuables in crowded markets.

The women were caught after a coordinated effort by local police and plainclothes officers who had been monitoring the area. Police have recovered stolen items worth over Rs 12 lakh, including gold chains, earrings, and cash.

Modus operandi

The trio, aged between 25 and 40, are believed to have targeted women shoppers in busy marketplaces such as Ranganathan Street and Pondy Bazaar. They would distract their victims before snatching jewellery or pickpocketing cash.

Police investigations revealed that the suspects would work in teams. One woman would engage the victim in conversation or create a commotion while the others would steal the valuables. They then fled the scene and divided the loot among themselves.

Recovery and investigation

Following the arrests, police conducted searches and recovered a substantial amount of stolen property. The recovered items include gold ornaments, diamond studs, and cash. Police have also seized mobile phones and other belongings from the accused.

Officials said the women had been staying in a rented room in a nearby locality and had been active for the past few weeks. Investigators are probing whether they are part of a larger gang that operates across cities. No prior criminal records have been confirmed yet.

Police appeal to shoppers

Chennai police have urged shoppers to remain vigilant, especially in crowded areas. They advise keeping valuables secure and reporting any suspicious activity immediately. A senior officer said, 'We are committed to ensuring the safety of citizens and will take strict action against those involved in such crimes.'

The accused have been remanded in judicial custody. Further investigations are ongoing to identify any accomplices or other victims.

What happens next: The women will be produced in court on Thursday. Police are also scanning travel records and call logs to trace their movements in the city.
