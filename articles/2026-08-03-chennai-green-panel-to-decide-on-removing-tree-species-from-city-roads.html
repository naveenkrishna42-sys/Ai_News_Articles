Chennai Green Panel to Decide on Removing Tree Species from City Roads

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chennai District Green Committee to deliberate on removing a specific tree species from city roads.
  • The decision could impact urban ecology and road safety in Chennai.
  • The committee's ruling will set a precedent for managing city trees.

Green Panel Takes Up Tree Removal Proposal

The Chennai District Green Committee is set to deliberate on a proposal to remove a specific tree species from the city's roads. The decision, which could significantly alter the urban canopy, comes amid growing concerns over the species' impact on pavements, underground utilities, and public safety.

The committee, which oversees environmental clearances and tree-related matters in the district, will examine the issue in its next sitting. While the exact species has not been officially named in public disclosures, sources indicate it is a fast-growing variety commonly planted along avenues in recent decades.

Why This Tree Species Is Under Scrutiny

Residents and civic bodies have long complained about the species' aggressive root systems, which have been known to crack sidewalks, block drainage lines, and even damage building foundations. In some stretches, the trees have also become a hazard during cyclones, with weak branches snapping under pressure.

Municipal workers have flagged several instances where roots have disrupted water pipelines and sewage networks, leading to frequent repairs. The committee's decision is expected to weigh these practical problems against the ecological benefits of mature trees, such as shade and carbon sequestration.

Balancing Ecology and Urban Infrastructure

Environmental activists have urged caution, pointing out that removing mature trees without a replacement plan could worsen air quality and heat island effects in Chennai. The city has already seen a decline in green cover due to rapid infrastructure projects, and any large-scale removal could be controversial.

The committee is likely to consider a phased approach, possibly recommending selective felling only where trees pose a clear danger or cause repeated damage. It may also suggest replacing the species with native, less invasive alternatives that are better suited to urban conditions.

What Happens Next

The District Green Committee's verdict will be crucial, as it could set a precedent for how other Indian cities manage problematic tree species. If approved, the removal process would require careful planning, including permits, public notices, and compensatory plantation drives.

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for the decision, but the matter is expected to be taken up within the next few weeks. The committee's ruling will be binding on local bodies, which will then execute the plan in coordination with the forest department.

For now, residents and environmentalists alike are watching closely, as the outcome could reshape Chennai's streetscapes for decades to come.
