US inks $58.6 billion deal for Patriot missiles amid Iran war stockpile concerns

📅 2026-07-30 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Lockheed Martin awarded $58.62 billion multiyear contract for PAC-3 MSE missiles
  • Deal aims to replenish US and allied stockpiles depleted by Iran war
  • Pentagon also committing over $120 billion for additional Patriot missiles and submarines

Massive missile contract signed

The US Department of War has awarded Lockheed Martin a $58.62 billion contract to produce PAC-3 MSE missiles. The multiyear deal is meant to replenish Patriot missile stockpiles that have run low due to the ongoing war with Iran.

The contract, announced by the Pentagon, is one of the largest single weapons deals in recent American history. Lockheed Martin described the award as a move to "strengthen the Arsenal of Freedom."

Stockpiles under pressure

The conflict with Iran has placed severe strain on US and allied missile inventories. Patriot systems have been used extensively to intercept ballistic missiles and drones, leading to rapid depletion of the interceptor missiles.

Military analysts have noted that the war has consumed munitions at a rate not seen since the height of operations in Iraq and Afghanistan. The new contract is intended to ensure a steady supply of missiles over several years.

Broader Pentagon spending spree

The Patriot missile deal is part of a larger Pentagon commitment. Reports indicate the Department of War is spending over $120 billion on Patriot missiles and submarines combined.

This includes the Lockheed contract as well as funding for submarine construction at shipyards across the United States. The spending reflects the Biden administration's push to rebuild military readiness amid a major ground and air campaign in the Middle East.

What the deal covers

The contract specifically covers the PAC-3 MSE (Missile Segment Enhancement) variant. This is the most advanced version of the Patriot interceptor, designed to hit incoming ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and aircraft.

Lockheed Martin will produce the missiles at its facilities in Alabama and Arkansas. The multiyear structure of the deal allows for bulk purchasing and lower per-unit costs.

Allied stockpiles also low

Several US allies, including Israel, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE, operate Patriot batteries. Their stockpiles have also been drained by the Iran conflict.

The new production contract will supply both US forces and allied nations under foreign military sales agreements. Pentagon officials have not confirmed exact delivery timelines or the total number of missiles to be produced under the deal.

Watch for further announcements on how the missiles will be allocated between domestic and allied forces, and whether additional contracts for other munitions are forthcoming as the war continues.
