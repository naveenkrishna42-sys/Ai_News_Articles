Hyderabad’s new urban park offers a quiet escape in the city centre

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • A new park opened in Hyderabad’s central business district, offering a quiet space amid urban bustle.
  • The park features native trees, walking paths, and shaded seating areas for visitors.
  • Local residents and workers have welcomed the initiative as a much-needed green lung in the area.

Hyderabad’s central business district has a new landmark, but it is not a glass tower or a flyover. A pocket park, tucked between office blocks and busy roads, is giving workers and residents a place to pause without leaving the city centre.

The park opened this week, and early visitors describe it as a welcome change. Instead of honking horns and crowds, there is the rustle of leaves and the occasional bird call. Benches are placed under the shade of native trees, and a gravel path winds through the small green patch.

Designed for calm

The park is not large — about half an acre — but its design is deliberate. The landscape architect chose local species such as neem and gulmohar, which require less water and provide dense shade. There are no food stalls or loudspeakers. The only structure is a small pergola covered with flowering creepers.

“You can sit here for ten minutes and feel like you are in a different city,” said a software engineer who works in a nearby high-rise. She was eating lunch on a bench, reading a book. “Earlier, I would just eat at my desk. Now I come here.”

Public response

Residents from surrounding apartment complexes have also started using the park in the evenings. Mothers with young children, elderly couples, and joggers — the space sees a steady trickle of people after 5 pm. The park stays open until 9 pm, with soft lighting along the path.

The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) developed the plot, which had been vacant for years. Officials said the park cost roughly ₹1.2 crore to build, including landscaping and irrigation. Maintenance will be handled by a local residents’ welfare association under a public-private partnership model.

One of many planned

This park is part of a larger GHMC plan to create 20 such pocket parks across the city by 2027. The idea is to turn small, unused municipal plots into green spaces. The first few parks have already been completed in Banjara Hills, Madhapur, and Ameerpet.

“We are focusing on areas with high foot traffic but very little greenery,” said a GHMC official involved in the project. “The idea is not to build big gardens — just small patches where people can breathe.”

The official added that the parks are designed to be low-maintenance, with drip irrigation and solar-powered lights. Native plants are chosen to attract local butterflies and birds, adding to the biodiversity.

What to watch

Two more pocket parks are expected to open in the next three months — one in Kukatpally and another in Secunderabad. If the current park remains well-used and well-maintained, the model could expand to other neighbourhoods. For now, Hyderabad’s office-goers have a new lunch spot, and it does not require a reservation.
