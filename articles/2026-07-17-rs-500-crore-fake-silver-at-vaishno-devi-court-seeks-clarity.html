Rs 500 Crore Fake Silver at Vaishno Devi? Court Seeks Clarity

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • A court has sought a response from authorities regarding allegations of fake silver offerings at Vaishno Devi.
  • The alleged fake silver is valued at Rs 500 crore.
  • The court's query follows a report or petition raising concerns about the authenticity of the offerings.

A court has stepped into a controversy surrounding the Mata Vaishno Devi shrine, seeking answers on whether fake silver worth a staggering Rs 500 crore was offered at the temple. The development has raised questions about the security and verification of donations at one of India's most revered pilgrimage sites.

Court seeks explanation on fake silver claims

The court has directed concerned authorities to provide a clear response to the allegations. The case stems from a report or petition that highlighted the possibility of counterfeit silver being deposited as offerings at the shrine. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact value or extent of the suspected fake items.

The shrine board, which manages the temple complex in Jammu and Kashmir, has not issued a detailed statement so far. The court's intervention signals the seriousness of the matter, given the large amount involved.

How the fake silver could have entered the system

Devotees often offer silver items, including coins, idols, and ornaments, at the Vaishno Devi shrine. These offerings are collected and processed by the shrine board. If fake silver was indeed deposited, it would have bypassed existing checks.

The court wants to know how such a large quantity of counterfeit material could have gone unnoticed. It has asked for details on the procedures followed to verify the purity of offerings.

Reactions and next steps

The news has caused concern among devotees and the public. Many are questioning the security and transparency of the donation process at the shrine. The court's query is seen as a step towards accountability.

Authorities are expected to file a response in the coming weeks. The court will then decide on further action based on the information provided.

What happens next will depend on the shrine board's explanation and any evidence it presents. The case could lead to tighter verification protocols at the temple.
