India Faces Elimination Gap: 2026 Measles‑Rubella Goal Likely Missed

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-28 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • 98% coverage yet elimination remains out of reach
  • Pockets of low coverage in remote communities drive persistence
  • Strategic interventions and funding need reinforcement

India’s public health system has achieved remarkable gains in routine immunisation, yet a critical milestone—eliminating measles and rubella by 2026—appears out of reach. This article dissects the current landscape, identifies persistent gaps, and outlines actionable steps for policymakers and stakeholders.

WHO’s 2026 Measles‑Rubella Elimination Target

Elimination is defined by WHO as fewer than one case per 100,000 population per year. The 2026 target for India is anchored in the Global Vaccine Action Plan, which sets a benchmark of complete coverage and sustained surveillance across all states. Achieving this requires not only high routine coverage but also robust outbreak response mechanisms.

India’s Vaccination Coverage Landscape

India’s Expanded Programme on Immunisation (EPI) reports a first‑dose measles coverage of 98% nationwide. However, coverage is uneven, with district‑level variances exceeding 10 percentage points. The national average masks significant disparities that undermine herd immunity.

Incidence Data: 2024–2025 Trends

In 2024, India reported approximately 6,200 measles cases, translating to an incidence of 0.5 per 100,000. Rubella cases remained below 1,000 with a similar incidence. Although these figures are below the elimination threshold, they represent a persistent reservoir that can seed outbreaks.

Pockets of Low Coverage in Remote Regions

High‑altitude Himalayan districts and tribal belt states consistently report coverage below 90%. In these areas, logistical constraints and vaccine hesitancy impede routine delivery. Targeted outreach is essential to bridge these gaps.

Impact on Marginalised Indigenous Communities

Marginalised groups, such as Adivasis and nomadic tribes, experience measles outbreaks at twice the national rate. Limited access to health facilities and cultural barriers contribute to higher susceptibility. Addressing these inequities is critical for national elimination.

Role of Gavi and International Funding

Gavi’s support has accelerated the procurement of monovalent measles and measles‑rubella vaccines. The partnership also funds training of community health workers and surveillance systems. Continued financial and technical support will be pivotal in 2026.

National Immunisation Programme Enhancements

Recent policy shifts mandate a second dose of measles vaccine at 15 months. The introduction of a combined measles‑rubella (MR) vaccine aims to improve coverage efficiency. Integration with the National Digital Health Mission will facilitate real‑time data capture.

Surveillance and Reporting Challenges

Under‑reporting remains a significant hurdle; estimated under‑ascertainment rates exceed 30% in several states. Strengthening passive surveillance and deploying rapid response teams can mitigate this issue.

Economic and Health Burden Analysis

Measles outbreaks cost the Indian economy an estimated ₹12.5 crore annually in healthcare spending and lost productivity. The societal impact is disproportionately felt in rural and low‑income households, where medical expenses can lead to catastrophic debt.

Path Forward: Policy Recommendations

Policymakers should prioritize state‑level coverage audits and allocate resources to high‑risk districts. Expanding community‑based surveillance and leveraging digital health tools will enhance outbreak detection. Finally, sustained funding from Gavi and domestic sources is essential to maintain momentum toward elimination.
