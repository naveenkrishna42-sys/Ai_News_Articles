Monsoon Travel: 5 Dengue Prevention Tips to Follow

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Apply EPA-approved mosquito repellent on exposed skin, especially during dawn and dusk.
  • Wear long-sleeved clothing and light-coloured clothes to minimise mosquito attraction.
  • Choose accommodation with air conditioning or mosquito nets and ensure windows are screened.
  • Avoid stagnant water near your stay—empty containers, flowerpots, and coolers.
  • Carry a rapid diagnostic kit or consult a doctor immediately if fever with headache or joint pain develops.

Why Monsoon Travel Demands Extra Care

The monsoon season brings relief from the heat, but it also creates ideal breeding grounds for the Aedes mosquito. This insect transmits dengue, a viral infection that can turn severe quickly. For travellers, the risk multiplies because they may visit areas with poor drainage or standing water.

Dengue cases typically spike between July and October in India. Health officials have not yet confirmed exact numbers for this year, but early reports indicate a rise in several states. Taking a few simple precautions can make the difference between a pleasant trip and a medical emergency.

1. Use Repellent Like It’s Your Second Skin

Mosquito repellent is your first line of defence. Look for products containing DEET, picaridin, or oil of lemon eucalyptus. Apply it on all exposed skin, and reapply after swimming or sweating.

Pay special attention to the hours around dawn and dusk—this is when the Aedes mosquito is most active. Don’t rely on wristbands or ultrasonic devices; they are not proven to work.

2. Dress to Protect

Clothing matters more than most realise. Wear long-sleeved shirts, full pants, and socks. Tuck your pants into your socks if you’re walking through grassy or wooded areas.

Light-coloured clothes are better because dark colours attract mosquitoes. If you can, treat your clothes with permethrin—a safe insecticide that stays effective through several washes.

3. Choose Your Stay Wisely

Before booking, check if the room has air conditioning or mosquito nets. AC keeps mosquitoes out because they prefer warm, humid air. If AC isn’t available, insist on a net over the bed.

Make sure windows and doors have intact screens. A single gap can let in dozens of mosquitoes. If you’re staying in a tent or a basic lodge, carry a portable mosquito net.

4. Deny Mosquitoes Breeding Grounds

The Aedes mosquito breeds in clean, stagnant water. A bottle cap, a clogged gutter, or a flowerpot tray can hold enough water for hundreds of eggs. Inspect the area around your accommodation.

Empty any containers you see. If you’re in a hotel, ask housekeeping to clear out coolers and plant saucers. Even a teaspoon of water can sustain mosquito larvae.

5. Know the Symptoms and Act Fast

Dengue often starts with a sudden high fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, and joint or muscle pain. Some people develop a rash or mild bleeding from the nose or gums.

If you develop a fever during or after travel, do not self-medicate with aspirin or ibuprofen—they can worsen bleeding. Paracetamol is safer, but consult a doctor quickly. Carry a rapid dengue test kit if you’re going to a remote area.

What to watch: With the monsoon still active, health authorities are likely to report more dengue clusters in the coming weeks. Travellers should check local advisories before heading out and stay alert even after returning home, as symptoms can appear up to two weeks later.
