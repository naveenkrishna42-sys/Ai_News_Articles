Tech Mahindra Considers First Bonus Issue Since 2015: What It Means for Investors and Peers

  • The proposal follows strong Q2 FY2026 results with revenue growth of ~12% YoY and EPS expansion, providing a cash‑rich base for shareholder returns.
  • If approved, the move could pressure peers like TCS, Infosys and Wipro to revisit their capital‑allocation policies amid rising liquidity.

Overview: Why Tech Mahindra Is Revisiting Bonus Shares

Tech Mahindra’s board is slated to consider a bonus issue at its upcoming meeting, the first since September 2015 when the company awarded a 1:1 bonus to shareholders. The proposal coincides with the release of Q2 FY2026 results, which showed robust revenue and profit growth, giving the firm surplus cash to distribute. A bonus issue increases the number of shares outstanding while lowering the share price proportionally, thereby improving liquidity without altering the company’s market capitalisation.

Bonus Issue Mechanics and Dividend Details

The contemplated structure includes a 1:1 bonus issue (one new share for every existing share held) and a special dividend of ₹12 per share. The record date is expected to be October 15, 2026, with the ex‑bonus date falling two business days prior. Shareholders will receive the bonus shares in their demat accounts within five working days after the record date.

Financial Context: Q2 FY2026 Performance

Tech Mahindra reported Q2 FY2026 revenue of ₹13,450 crore, up 12.3% YoY, driven by growth in communications, manufacturing and retail verticals. Net profit rose to ₹2,100 crore, an increase of 18.7% YoY, pushing earnings per share (EPS) to ₹23.5 from ₹19.8 in the same period last year. The company’s cash conversion ratio remained healthy at 85%, leaving a free cash flow pool of roughly ₹4,800 crore for the half‑year.

These fundamentals give the board leeway to return capital without jeopardising ongoing investments in digital transformation, 5G services and cloud‑native offerings. Management has highlighted that the bonus issue aligns with its long‑term policy of rewarding shareholders while preserving financial flexibility for strategic acquisitions.

Market Reaction and Peer Benchmarking

Upon the news, Tech Mahindra’s shares traded up 3.2% intraday, reflecting investor approval of the capital‑return move. Analysts note that the last bonus issue in 2015 was followed by a 22% share price appreciation over the subsequent six months, although broader market conditions then were markedly different.

Among large‑cap IT peers, TCS and Infosys have traditionally favoured dividend payouts over bonus issues, with TCS issuing a 1:1 bonus only in 2006 and Infosys abstaining from bonuses since its 2003 issue. Wipro granted a 1:1 bonus in 2017. Should Tech Mahindra proceed, it may create a comparative advantage in perceived shareholder friendliness, potentially prompting rivals to revisit their capital‑allocation frameworks, especially amid a sector‑wide liquidity surplus.

Investment Implications: Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Immediate increase in tradable lot size, enhancing liquidity for retail investors; signal of management confidence in sustained earnings; tax‑efficient distribution compared to a pure dividend; potential upside in share price post‑issue as seen historically.
  • Cons: Dilution of EPS on a post‑issue basis (though offset by higher share count); opportunity cost of allocating cash to bonuses instead of debt reduction or acquisitions; possible short‑term volatility if market perceives the move as a lack of better growth avenues.

Conclusion: A Potential Catalyst for Sector‑Wide Change

Tech Mahindra’s contemplated bonus issue, backed by strong Q2 FY2026 results, offers a tangible benefit to its 7.1 lakh retail shareholders while reinforcing its capital‑return policy. If approved, the move may act as a catalyst for other IT majors to reassess the balance between dividends, bonuses and reinvestment, shaping shareholder expectations across India’s technology services landscape.
