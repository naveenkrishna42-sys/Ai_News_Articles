NSE IPO: Anchor Book Raises Rs 6,746 Crore — Should You Subscribe?

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-16 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • NSE raised Rs 6,746 crore from anchor investors including LIC, Fidelity and ADIA ahead of its IPO, with a price band of Rs 1,950-2,050 per share.
  • NSE's derivatives volumes are moderating after SEBI's curbs on weekly expiries, a key risk to its transaction revenue, which is ~80% of total income.
  • At the upper band, NSE trades at ~45x FY26 annualised earnings, a premium to global peers but justified by its ~90% cash-market share and net cash balance sheet.

India's National Stock Exchange (NSE) has raised Rs 6,746 crore via its anchor book, with Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC), Fidelity and Abu Dhabi Investment Authority (ADIA) among the top investors. The anchor round, a day before the issue opens, sets the stage for the country's largest IPO of 2026. However, investor caution persists over NSE's derivatives-fuelled growth model, which is now facing regulatory headwinds.

Anchor Book Details and Issue Structure

The anchor book allocation, finalised on 15 September, saw participation from 62 institutional investors. The IPO comprises a fresh issue of Rs 5,500 crore and an offer for sale of up to 1.5 crore shares by existing shareholders. The price band is Rs 1,950-2,050 per share, valuing NSE at approximately Rs 1.02 lakh crore at the upper end. The issue opens on 17 September and closes on 19 September.

Anchor investors were allotted shares at Rs 2,050, the top of the price band, signalling strong institutional demand. LIC picked up a 1.2% stake for about Rs 1,200 crore, while Fidelity and ADIA subscribed Rs 800 crore and Rs 650 crore respectively. The anchor book was oversubscribed 4.5 times, according to exchange filings.

NSE's Business Model: Derivatives Dominance Under Pressure

NSE generates over 80% of its revenue from transaction charges, primarily from equity derivatives. In FY26, the exchange handled a daily average turnover of Rs 280 lakh crore in derivatives, but volumes have moderated 18% year-on-year after SEBI's October 2025 curbs on weekly expiries. The regulator reduced expiry days to one per exchange per week, directly impacting NSE's options segment, where it holds a 75% market share, down from 92% in FY24.

Cash market share remains robust at 92%, but that segment contributes only 12% to revenue. The challenge for IPO investors is whether NSE can diversify into non-transactional revenue streams such as data services, co-location and clearing, which currently account for 15% of income.

Valuation and Peer Comparison

At the upper band of Rs 2,050, NSE trades at a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 45x FY26 annualised earnings, compared to 38x for BSE and 32x for global peers like Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing. The premium reflects NSE's monopoly-like position in cash equities, net cash balance sheet of Rs 18,000 crore, and consistent dividend payout of 70%.

Key Insights for Investors

  • Pros: Dominant market position in cash equities; net cash of Rs 18,000 crore; consistent dividend yield of 1.8% at upper band; anchor book oversubscribed 4.5x.
  • Cons: Derivatives revenue concentration (80% of income); regulatory risk from SEBI curbs; premium valuation versus global peers; IPO demand may be dented by 10 other issues in September.
  • Real-world benchmark: NSE's derivatives average daily turnover of Rs 280 lakh crore is 3.5x that of BSE, but the gap is narrowing as BSE gains expiry market share.
  • Value justification: At 45x P/E, NSE is priced for perfection. Investors should monitor monthly volumes for signs of stabilisation before applying.

Subscription and Listing Outlook

The IPO is expected to be oversubscribed 12-15 times overall, with qualified institutional buyers (QIBs) leading demand. Grey market premium (GMP) currently indicates a listing gain of 8-10%, translating to Rs 160-200 per share. However, a traffic jam of 10 other issues in September, including two large NBFC IPOs, could divert retail demand. NSE's listing is scheduled for 26 September on both NSE and BSE.

Retail investors should note that NSE's earnings may remain under pressure for two more quarters as derivatives volumes adjust to the new expiry regime. Long-term investors can consider subscribing for its cash-market moat and dividend yield, but near-term upside appears limited at the upper band.
