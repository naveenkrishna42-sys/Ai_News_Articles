8 Indian Colleges That Became Bollywood Movie Backdrops

Key points
  • St. Stephen's College in Delhi featured in 'Dil Chahta Hai' and 'Rang De Basanti'.
  • Film City in Mumbai doubled as St. Xavier's College for 'Kuch Kuch Hota Hai'.
  • Mithibai College in Mumbai was the setting for 'Student of the Year'.
  • Elphinstone College in Mumbai appeared in 'Yeh Jawani Hai Deewani'.
  • Doon School in Dehradun was used in 'Taare Zameen Par' and '3 Idiots'.
  • Bishop Cotton School in Shimla was the location for 'Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge'.

Real campuses, reel stories

Bollywood has long borrowed real architecture to build its fictional worlds. Some of the most memorable college films were shot on actual campuses, not on sets. These locations became characters in their own right.

Architectural Digest India recently highlighted eight Indian colleges that served as backdrops for popular Bollywood movies. Each campus brought its own history and atmosphere to the screen.

St. Stephen's College, Delhi

St. Stephen's College in Delhi University appears in multiple films. It was the setting for parts of Dil Chahta Hai (2001) and Rang De Basanti (2006). The red brick buildings and sprawling lawns gave the movies an authentic academic feel.

The college's gothic-style architecture, built in 1941, offers a timeless look. Filmmakers often use it to evoke a sense of tradition and prestige.

Film City, Mumbai — doubling as St. Xavier's

For Kuch Kuch Hota Hai (1998), the production team recreated St. Xavier's College in Mumbai within Film City. The actual St. Xavier's campus was not used, but the sets captured its essence. The film's iconic college sequences were shot here.

This approach is common when logistics or permissions make real locations impractical. Film City's controlled environment allows for repeated takes and crowd management.

Mithibai College, Mumbai

Mithibai College in Vile Parle, Mumbai, was the primary location for Student of the Year (2012). The film's glossy, competitive college world was built around the campus. Its modern architecture and large open spaces suited the film's high-energy vibe.

The college became instantly recognisable to audiences. Many students from surrounding areas visited the campus after the film's release.

Elphinstone College, Mumbai

Elphinstone College, one of Mumbai's oldest institutions, featured in Yeh Jawani Hai Deewani (2013). The campus's heritage look matched the film's flashback sequences set in a Delhi college. The stone facade and arched corridors added character.

The film's production team chose Elphinstone for its photogenic, old-world charm. It helped establish the nostalgic tone of the college scenes.

Doon School, Dehradun

The Doon School in Dehradun served as a key location for Taare Zameen Par (2007) and 3 Idiots (2009). Its vast, green campus and colonial-era buildings made it ideal for boarding school and engineering college settings.

In 3 Idiots, the campus doubled as the fictional Imperial College of Engineering. The school's disciplined environment matched the film's academic themes.

Bishop Cotton School, Shimla

Bishop Cotton School in Shimla appeared in Dilwale Dulhania Le Jayenge (1995). The school's British-era architecture was used for the college scenes featuring Raj and Simran. The hill station location added to the romantic feel.

The film's team used the school's chapel and main building. These shots became some of the most iconic in the movie.

Other notable mentions

Other colleges on the list include Sophia College in Mumbai, seen in Devdas (2002), and Hindu College in Delhi, used in Rockstar (2011). Each location contributed to the narrative through its unique design.

Some of these campuses have since become tourist attractions for film fans. The connection between real architecture and cinematic storytelling remains strong.

Bollywood continues to scout new campuses for future projects. As filmmakers look for fresh backdrops, more Indian colleges may find themselves on the big screen.
