Trump says Iran war could end 'pretty soon', claims US talks underway as Tehran denies direct negotiations

📅 2026-08-07 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says Iran war could end 'pretty soon'
  • US claims talks underway with Iran; Tehran denies direct negotiations
  • Iran and Oman agree on Hormuz route coordinates; deal close but conditional

War of words over peace

US President Donald Trump has said the war with Iran could end 'pretty soon', while claiming that Washington is engaged in talks with Tehran. The remarks come amid escalating conflict in West Asia, with fresh maritime incidents reported in the Red Sea and the Strait of Hormuz.

Tehran, however, has denied any direct negotiations with the United States. Iranian officials maintain that no bilateral channel is open, even as diplomatic efforts intensify behind the scenes.

Hormuz deal inching closer

In a separate development, Iran and Oman have agreed on the coordinates of a route through the Strait of Hormuz, according to Iran's Foreign Ministry. The strategic waterway, through which a significant share of global oil passes, has been a flashpoint in the ongoing conflict.

Iranian officials say a deal on Hormuz is close, but insist that Washington must first meet its commitments. The exact nature of these commitments has not been disclosed, though analysts point to sanctions relief and security guarantees as likely sticking points.

Red Sea incident raises stakes

Meanwhile, Yemen's naval forces reportedly thwarted a Houthi attack on an oil tanker in the Red Sea using an explosive-laden boat. The incident underscores the widening geographic scope of the conflict, which has drawn in multiple regional actors.

The attack was foiled before it could cause significant damage, according to reports. However, the episode highlights the persistent threat to commercial shipping in one of the world's busiest maritime lanes.

Mixed signals from Washington and Tehran

Trump's optimistic assessment of the war's trajectory contrasts sharply with Tehran's public stance. The gap between the two sides' statements suggests that while back-channel efforts may be underway, formal negotiations remain elusive.

Observers note that the 'pretty soon' timeline is vague, and the conditions for ending the war are far from settled. The coming days are likely to test whether the Hormuz deal can be finalised and whether broader de-escalation follows.

Officials have not yet confirmed the specifics of any US-Iran talks, leaving room for speculation about the true state of diplomatic engagement.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on the Strait of Hormuz in the coming weeks. If the Iran-Oman agreement translates into a formal deal, it could pave the way for a broader ceasefire. But without clear commitments from Washington, Tehran's insistence on preconditions may keep the war raging.

The Red Sea remains a volatile zone, and any further attacks on commercial vessels could derail the fragile momentum towards peace.
