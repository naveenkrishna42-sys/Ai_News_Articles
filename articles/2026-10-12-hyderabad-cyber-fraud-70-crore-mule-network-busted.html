Hyderabad’s Biggest Cyber Fraud Bust: ₹70 Crore Network Unmasked

Hyderabad police have dismantled one of the largest cyber fraud syndicates in recent memory, recovering evidence of a ₹70 crore network that operated through over 150 mule bank accounts. The investigation, led by the Cyber Crimes Wing of Hyderabad City Police, exposed a sophisticated operation that preyed on citizens through digital arrest scams and phishing tactics. What makes this case particularly shocking is the revelation that an old ₹1 note was allegedly purchased for ₹45 lakh as part of a hawala-style credibility test used by the gang.

How the Mule Account Network Functioned

The fraudsters recruited hundreds of individuals—mostly from economically vulnerable sections—by promising easy money for opening and operating bank accounts. These accounts were then used to receive stolen funds transferred from victims’ accounts. The money was quickly siphoned off or converted into cryptocurrency before being moved abroad. According to sources, each mule account was rotated every few days to avoid detection, and the gang used encrypted messaging apps and fake identities to coordinate operations.

The ₹45 Lakh ₹1 Note: Symbol of a Larger Scheme

Investigators say the bizarre transaction involving an old ₹1 note was part of a ritualistic verification process where fraudsters tested the trustworthiness of new recruits. By paying an absurd sum for a seemingly worthless note, they assessed whether the person could be trusted with larger sums. This tactic reflects a deeper understanding of psychological manipulation, leveraging greed and desperation among recruits. Officers believe such symbolic gestures helped build loyalty within the inner circle of the syndicate.

Operational Tactics and Victim Impact

Victims were contacted via spoofed numbers mimicking senior police officers or government officials. They were falsely accused of being involved in money laundering or terror funding and coerced into transferring money to 'safe' accounts—which were actually controlled by the gang. Many victims lost anywhere between ₹50,000 and ₹5 lakh. Some were even forced to hand over property documents or gold. The emotional toll was severe, with several cases reporting depression and anxiety among survivors.

What Authorities Are Doing Now

Following the arrests, authorities have frozen all identified mule accounts and are working with nationalized banks to track remaining beneficiaries. A special task force has been constituted to monitor similar patterns nationwide. Police have also urged the public to verify any unsolicited calls claiming to be from law enforcement agencies and to report suspicious activity immediately. Financial institutions have been advised to flag transactions above ₹50,000 originating from unknown sources or flagged entities.
