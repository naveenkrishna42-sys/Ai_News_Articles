RBI's New EMI Rules for Laptops and Mobiles: What Borrowers Must Know

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has issued new guidelines for EMI defaults on consumer durables like laptops and mobiles.
  • Defaulting can lead to late fees, higher interest, and negative credit score impact.
  • Lenders may repossess the financed product if default persists, as per RBI norms.

Understanding the New RBI Guidelines

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has come out with fresh norms that clarify what happens when you fail to pay EMIs for items like laptops and mobile phones. These consumer durables are often bought on equated monthly instalments, and the new rules aim to bring more transparency and fairness to the process.

Under the latest RBI directives, lenders are now required to follow a structured approach before declaring a loan as a default. This includes sending multiple reminders and giving borrowers a reasonable window to clear overdue amounts. The central bank has also stressed that repossession of the financed asset should be a last resort, not the first step.

What Happens When You Miss an EMI?

If you miss a payment, the first thing you can expect is a late payment fee, which varies from lender to lender. Along with this, the interest on the outstanding amount may increase, making the total repayment heavier over time. The RBI norms state that these charges must be clearly disclosed at the time of signing the loan agreement.

More critically, a missed EMI gets reported to credit bureaus, which can pull down your credit score. A lower score affects your ability to get future loans, credit cards, or even rental agreements. According to the new guidelines, lenders must inform borrowers about the credit score impact before reporting a default to the bureau.

The Repossession Clause

If the default continues for a specific period, typically 60 to 90 days, the lender has the right to repossess the laptop or mobile phone. However, the RBI has mandated that lenders must give a prior notice of at least 15 days before taking such an action. This gives the borrower a final chance to clear the dues and retain the product.

In case the asset is repossessed, the lender is required to sell it at a fair market value and adjust the proceeds against the outstanding loan. If the sale amount is less than the due, the borrower remains liable for the remaining balance. Conversely, if there is a surplus, it must be returned to the borrower.

What Borrowers Should Do

Financial advisors suggest that borrowers facing temporary cash crunch should contact their lender immediately. Many lenders offer restructuring options, such as extending the loan tenure or reducing the EMI amount for a few months. The new RBI norms encourage such flexibility, provided the borrower communicates proactively.

It is also advisable to read the loan agreement carefully before signing, especially the clauses related to late fees and repossession. The RBI has made it mandatory for lenders to highlight these terms in simple language, so there is no fine print confusion.

Impact on the Consumer Durable Market

The new norms are expected to bring more discipline to the consumer finance sector. With clearer rules, lenders may become more cautious in approving loans, but they also have a transparent recovery process. For buyers, this means better protection against sudden seizure of their devices, but also stricter accountability for timely payments.

Industry watchers say the guidelines could reduce the number of disputes between borrowers and lenders. The RBI's push for standardised procedures is likely to benefit both parties in the long run.

As the norms come into effect, borrowers should keep track of their EMI schedules and maintain a buffer for unexpected expenses. The RBI has also set up a grievance redressal mechanism for those who feel they have been unfairly treated by a lender.

Going forward, the central bank is expected to release more detailed FAQs and operational guidelines for lenders. Borrowers are advised to stay informed through official RBI updates and their bank's communication channels.
