AFC joins UEFA, Concacaf in rejecting FIFA's World Cup plan

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • AFC has formally rejected FIFA's World Cup plan, joining UEFA and Concacaf.
  • The proposal faces mounting opposition from three major confederations.
  • FIFA President Gianni Infantino's push for the plan has suffered a significant setback.

AFC's Rejection Adds to Growing Opposition

The Asian Football Confederation (AFC) has formally rejected FIFA's latest World Cup proposal, aligning itself with UEFA and Concacaf in opposing the plan. The decision, announced on Friday, marks another significant setback for FIFA President Gianni Infantino, who has been championing the proposal.

The AFC's stance was confirmed through an official statement, though details of the vote or the specific reasons for rejection have not been fully disclosed. This move brings the number of confederations against the plan to three, leaving FIFA with limited regional support.

What the Plan Entails

The proposed World Cup plan, which has not been publicly detailed in full, is believed to involve a major restructuring of the tournament's format and calendar. Infantino has reportedly been pushing for the changes to expand the competition's global appeal and revenue potential.

However, the plan has drawn sharp criticism from national associations and leagues, who argue it would disrupt domestic calendars and increase player workload. The exact provisions remain under wraps, but the widespread rejection suggests deep-seated concerns across multiple regions.

UEFA and Concacaf's Earlier Objections

UEFA, representing 55 national associations, was the first to voice its opposition, issuing a joint statement that highlighted the risks to women's football and the broader game. The statement warned that the plan could have 'first casualties' in the women's game, a point that has resonated with critics.

Concacaf, which includes 41 member associations, followed suit with its own official statement rejecting the proposal. The confederation's leadership cited similar concerns about competitive balance and the welfare of players, adding to the chorus of dissent.

A Coordinated Front?

The synchronized rejections from UEFA and Concacaf, now joined by the AFC, suggest a coordinated effort to block the proposal. Some observers see this as a rare display of unity among confederations that often have competing interests.

Infantino, who has faced criticism for his leadership style and centralization of power, now finds himself isolated in his push for the plan. The AFC's decision is particularly notable given Asia's growing influence in world football, both as a market and a host region.

What This Means for FIFA

With three confederations formally against the plan, FIFA's ability to push it through appears increasingly uncertain. The proposal would likely require approval from a majority of FIFA's member associations, and the combined weight of UEFA, Concacaf, and the AFC represents a substantial bloc.

Infantino may attempt to revise the plan to address concerns, but the coordinated nature of the opposition suggests that compromise will not come easily. The coming weeks are likely to see further discussions, with all eyes on whether FIFA can broker a deal or face a deadlock.

Officials have not yet confirmed the next steps, but the pressure on Infantino is mounting. The outcome of this standoff could redefine the future of the World Cup and test the unity of global football governance.

As the debate continues, the focus now shifts to whether other confederations, such as CAF or OFC, will side with FIFA or join the opposition. Their decisions could prove decisive in determining whether the plan survives or is shelved indefinitely.
