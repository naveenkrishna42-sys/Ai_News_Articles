Man City reject Barcelona's €45m Rodri bid, demand €70m

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Barcelona's initial €45 million bid for Rodri has been rejected by Manchester City.
  • City have set a €70 million asking price for the Spanish midfielder.
  • Rodri has reportedly agreed personal terms with Barcelona, snubbing Real Madrid.
  • Transfer uncertainty persists as both clubs remain apart on valuation.

Manchester City have rejected Barcelona's opening bid for midfielder Rodri, with the Premier League champions holding firm on a €70 million asking price. The Catalan club's initial offer, believed to be in the region of €45 million, was dismissed as City look to maximise the value of one of their key assets.

The news, first reported by Barca Blaugranes and later confirmed by The Athletic, sets the stage for a protracted negotiation between the two European heavyweights. Barcelona, eager to reinforce their midfield, now face a decision: increase their offer significantly or walk away from the table.

Barcelona's Pursuit Intensifies

Barcelona's interest in Rodri is not new, but the timing has raised eyebrows. With the summer transfer window in full swing, the Blaugrana have identified the Spanish international as a priority target to bolster their engine room. Reports from Spain suggest that Rodri has already agreed personal terms with Barcelona, snubbing a late push from Real Madrid.

That development, first flagged by Managing Madrid, adds a layer of intrigue to the saga. If Rodri has indeed given his word to Barcelona, the onus is on the club's hierarchy to bridge the gap with City. However, Barcelona's well-documented financial constraints could complicate matters, making a €70 million outlay a significant stretch.

City's Stance Remains Unchanged

Manchester City, for their part, have shown no willingness to entertain cut-price offers. The club's valuation of €70 million reflects Rodri's importance to Pep Guardiola's setup, where he has been a linchpin in midfield since his arrival. City's rejection of the €45 million bid signals that they are prepared to play hardball, even if it means keeping an unsettled player.

Rodri's contract situation adds another wrinkle. With his current deal running until 2027, City hold considerable leverage. The club is not under immediate pressure to sell, and any departure would likely require a fee close to their asking price. Barcelona, meanwhile, are banking on the player's desire to move, but City have shown in the past that they are not easily swayed by player power.

What This Means for Both Clubs

For Barcelona, the rejection is a setback but not a fatal blow. The club's sporting director, Deco, is known for his persistence in the transfer market, and a revised offer could be imminent. However, Barcelona must also consider their wage bill and La Liga's financial fair play rules, which have forced them into creative deal structures in recent windows.

For City, the situation is more straightforward. They have depth in midfield, but Rodri's departure would leave a void that would be difficult to fill at short notice. Guardiola has consistently praised Rodri's tactical intelligence and work rate, and losing him would require a significant reinvestment.

Rodri's Position in the Middle

Rodri's future remains uncertain, but the player himself has stayed silent publicly. The 30-year-old has not commented on the speculation, leaving fans and pundits to parse reports from various outlets. What is clear is that a move to Barcelona would represent a homecoming of sorts for the Madrid-born midfielder, who has spent the prime of his career in England.

The coming days will be crucial. Barcelona are expected to return with an improved offer, but whether they can meet City's valuation remains to be seen. With the transfer window closing at the end of the month, time is not on their side.

All eyes will be on the negotiation table at the Etihad Campus, where City's hierarchy will weigh their options. For now, the ball is in Barcelona's court — and the price tag is unlikely to drop.
