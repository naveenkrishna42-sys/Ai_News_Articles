NASA's $30 million mission to save Swift telescope fails

NASA's efforts to save the Swift gamma-ray observatory have come to an end. The $30 million mission to boost Swift into a higher orbit has failed, and the telescope is now in a decaying orbit. Swift's orbit is expected to decay over the next few months, and the telescope may re-enter the Earth's atmosphere soon.

Swift's contributions to astronomy and astrophysics

Swift has made significant contributions to astronomy and astrophysics since its launch in 2004. The telescope has observed over 2,000 gamma-ray bursts, providing valuable data for understanding the universe's most powerful explosions. Swift has also conducted extensive surveys of the sky, discovering new objects and phenomena.

NASA updates next steps for Swift

NASA has announced that it will be exploring commercial options for a potential Swift boost mission. The agency is assessing the feasibility of a commercial mission to boost Swift into a higher orbit. NASA has not yet confirmed the specifics of the commercial mission, but officials have expressed interest in working with private companies to extend Swift's life.

Challenges and setbacks in the Swift rescue mission

The Swift rescue mission has faced several challenges and setbacks. The mission was initially planned for 2025, but was delayed due to technical issues and budget constraints. The mission has also been affected by the ongoing COVID-19 pandemic, which has impacted NASA's operations and delayed the launch of the mission's primary payload.

Looking ahead: NASA's plans for Swift

NASA is currently assessing the feasibility of a commercial Swift boost mission. The agency is working with private companies to explore the possibility of a commercial mission to extend Swift's life. NASA has not yet confirmed the specifics of the commercial mission, but officials have expressed interest in working with private companies to ensure Swift's continued operation.
