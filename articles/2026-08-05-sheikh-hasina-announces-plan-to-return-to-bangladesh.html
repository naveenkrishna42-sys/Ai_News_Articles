Sheikh Hasina announces plan to return to Bangladesh

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sheikh Hasina announces intention to return to Bangladesh
  • She calls for lifting the ban on her party, Awami League
  • She claims students were emotionally manipulated during protests

Dhaka, August 5: Ousted Bangladesh Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina has announced her intention to return to her homeland, declaring, "Whatever fate awaits me, I will return to my people." The statement, made in a message to supporters, marks her first public commitment to go back since she fled the country in the wake of mass protests.

Return amidst political turmoil

Hasina's announcement comes at a time of heightened uncertainty in Bangladesh. The interim government has banned her party, the Awami League, and several of its top leaders face legal proceedings. In her address, Hasina directly appealed for the ban to be lifted, framing the party as an essential part of the nation's democratic fabric.

She did not provide a specific timeline or details about how she plans to enter the country. Security analysts suggest her return could trigger fresh political confrontation, given the volatile mood on the ground.

Claims of manipulation

In her remarks, Hasina also addressed the protests that led to her ouster, claiming that students were "emotionally manipulated" by external forces. She alleged that the demonstrations, which began as a quota reform movement, were hijacked to destabilise her government. She did not name any specific group or country behind the alleged manipulation.

Her comments have drawn sharp reactions from student leaders and civil society groups, who have dismissed the allegations as an attempt to rewrite history. The interim government has not yet responded officially to her claims.

Regional implications and India's stance

The announcement has also stirred diplomatic ripples. A day after Dhaka issued a warning over her planned address, India denied any official involvement in facilitating or supporting her statement. New Delhi has maintained a cautious public posture, balancing its historical ties with Hasina's government and the need to work with the current administration in Dhaka.

Observers note that any move by Hasina to return will be closely watched in Delhi, given the strategic importance of Bangladesh in India's neighbourhood policy. The two countries share a long border and deep economic linkages, making political stability in Dhaka a direct concern for Indian policymakers.

What happens next

Hasina's return plan remains shrouded in practical uncertainties. The interim government has not indicated whether it would allow her entry, and legal hurdles could complicate any attempt. Her party, the Awami League, remains banned, and its activists face a crackdown.

For now, her statement has energised her loyalists, who have been demoralised since her ouster. But it has also raised the stakes for the interim government, which must decide how to respond to a former leader who refuses to fade away. The coming weeks will reveal whether her words translate into action, and how Dhaka and New Delhi navigate this delicate moment.
