Hyderabad Students March Over NEET Row, Seek Minister's Resignation

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Students marched through central Hyderabad over the NEET row.
  • Protesters demanded the Union Education Minister's resignation.
  • The protest highlights growing discontent over alleged exam irregularities.

Hundreds of students marched through the heart of Hyderabad on Friday, their voices rising against the ongoing NEET controversy. The protest, which snaked through central areas of the city, saw demonstrators demanding the resignation of the Union Education Minister. The crowd, largely comprising medical aspirants, accused the minister of failing to address alleged irregularities in the exam process.

Protest Route and Demands

The march began in the afternoon, with students gathering at a central location before moving through key thoroughfares. Chants of 'We want justice' and 'Minister resign' echoed through the streets. Police maintained a watchful presence but did not intervene. The protesters held placards that read 'No to NEET scam' and 'Fair exams for all'.

The core demand was clear: the Union Education Minister must step down. Students argued that the minister had lost credibility over the handling of the NEET row. They claimed that the exam's integrity had been compromised, affecting the futures of thousands of aspirants.

Allegations of Irregularities

The protest is the latest in a series of demonstrations across the country. Students have alleged that the NEET exam this year was marred by question paper leaks and unfair grading practices. The government has denied these claims, but the protesters remain unconvinced.

One student leader, speaking on condition of anonymity, said, "We have been waiting for answers. The minister has not taken responsibility. Our careers are at stake." The crowd expressed frustration over what they see as a lack of transparency and accountability.

Calls for a Judicial Probe

Beyond the demand for resignation, protesters called for a Supreme Court-monitored investigation into the entire NEET process. They argued that only an independent probe could restore faith in the examination system. Some students also demanded the cancellation of this year's results and a fresh test.

The protest remained peaceful throughout. Local shopkeepers in the area reported minor disruptions, with traffic being diverted for a few hours. The Hyderabad police confirmed that no untoward incidents occurred and that the march concluded without any arrests.

The NEET row has become a political flashpoint, with opposition parties backing the students' demands. However, Friday's protest was largely apolitical, driven by student groups and civil society organisations.

What happens next depends on the government's response. With more protests planned in other cities, the pressure on the Education Ministry is mounting. The minister has so far not commented on Friday's march. Students have vowed to continue their agitation until their demands are met.
