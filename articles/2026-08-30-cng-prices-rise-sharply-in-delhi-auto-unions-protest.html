CNG prices rise sharply in Delhi; auto unions protest

📅 2026-08-30 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • CNG prices in Delhi rise by ₹3.89/kg due to West Asia crisis
  • Auto unions demand fare hike, threaten strike over price increase
  • Experts warn of potential economic impact on public transport
  • BJP spokesperson predicts price drop after US-Iran situation stabilizes

CNG Prices Surge in Delhi

The price of compressed natural gas (CNG) in Delhi has increased by ₹3.89 per kilogram, citing the ongoing West Asia crisis. This price hike has sparked protests from auto unions and taxi operators, who argue that the increase is unjustified and will burden the public. The Indian Gas Limited (IGL) has attributed the rise to global supply chain disruptions caused by the conflict in the region.

Auto Unions Demand Fare Hike

Auto unions and taxi operators have demanded a fare hike to compensate for the increased cost of CNG. They argue that the price increase will directly impact their operational costs and, consequently, the prices of auto and taxi services. The unions have threatened to go on strike if their demands are not met, which could disrupt public transport services in the city.

Economic Impact of CNG Price Hike

Experts warn that the rise in CNG prices could have broader economic implications. CNG is a key fuel for public transport, and any significant increase in its price could lead to higher operational costs for auto and taxi services. This, in turn, could result in increased fares, making public transport less affordable for the common man. Additionally, the disruption in public transport services could have ripple effects on the economy, affecting businesses and individuals alike.

Government and Industry Reactions

The Indian government has been quick to respond to the situation. A BJP spokesperson, Shahnawaz Hussain, has predicted that CNG prices will come down after the US-Iran situation stabilizes. However, the spokesperson has not provided a specific timeline for this price drop. The government is also likely to be in touch with international partners to ensure a steady supply of CNG and other essential commodities.
