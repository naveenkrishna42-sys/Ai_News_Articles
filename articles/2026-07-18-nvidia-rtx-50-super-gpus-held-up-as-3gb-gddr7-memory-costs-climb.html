Nvidia RTX 50 Super GPUs held up as 3GB GDDR7 memory costs climb

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nvidia's RTX 50 Super GPUs have been delivered to board partners for testing.
  • The launch is on hold due to unexpectedly high pricing for 3GB GDDR7 memory modules.
  • Nvidia is reportedly negotiating with memory suppliers like Samsung and Micron to reduce costs.
  • The delay affects potential mid-cycle refreshes of the RTX 5090, 5080, and 5070.

Super Series Sample Delivery Confirmed

Nvidia has shipped early samples of its GeForce RTX 50 Super series to major board partners, according to reports from multiple hardware outlets. The move signals that the chips are physically ready for integration.

However, insiders indicate that the rollout has stalled. Sources familiar with the matter say the graphics cards are now sitting in testing labs, awaiting a commercial decision that hinges on one key component: memory.

The GDDR7 Price Problem

The core issue is the cost of 3GB GDDR7 memory modules. These next-generation chips, produced by Samsung and Micron, are reportedly priced significantly higher than Nvidia's internal targets. The premium is eating into the margins Nvidia expects for a mid-cycle refresh.

Industry analysts point to early production yields and high demand from the AI sector as drivers of the price surge. Unlike conventional GDDR6X, the 3GB GDDR7 modules offer higher density and bandwidth, but the manufacturing complexity has kept unit costs elevated.

Board Partners Caught in the Middle

Nvidia's Add-in-Board (AIB) partners, including Asus, MSI, and Gigabyte, have received the RTX 50 Super samples. But they have not received a timeline for mass production. The uncertainty is creating planning headaches for these firms, who typically rely on Super refreshes to boost holiday sales.

One source told Club386 that partners are ready to build cards, but Nvidia has not yet approved the final bill of materials. The company is reportedly pushing back on the memory pricing, demanding reductions before it greenlights the launch.

What the RTX 50 Super Lineup Was Supposed to Be

The RTX 50 Super series was expected to include refreshed versions of the RTX 5090, RTX 5080, and RTX 5070. These cards were meant to feature more CUDA cores and higher clock speeds than the standard models, alongside the new 3GB GDDR7 memory modules.

For consumers, the Super refresh typically offers a performance bump without a generational architecture change. The delay means that gamers hoping for a mid-2026 upgrade may have to wait longer, or settle for current-generation cards at current prices.

Nvidia's Options

Nvidia has a few paths forward. It could absorb the higher memory costs and sell the Super cards at a lower margin, passing some cost to consumers. Alternatively, it could delay the launch until memory prices drop, which might not happen until late 2026 or early 2027.

The company could also revise the specifications, perhaps using cheaper 2GB GDDR7 modules instead. But that would reduce the memory capacity of the flagship cards, diluting the Super value proposition.

No official statement has been issued by Nvidia. The company typically does not comment on unannounced products.

What to watch next: Nvidia's next quarterly earnings call, expected in August, may provide clues. If executives mention memory supply constraints or pricing headwinds, it will confirm the hold. If not, the silence will speak volumes about the internal deadlock.
