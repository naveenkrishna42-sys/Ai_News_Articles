Maha Kumbh 2025: Travel Guide to 9 Mega Melas Across India

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Maha Kumbh 2025 in Prayagraj is the largest of India's mega melas, but many others offer unique experiences.
  • The guide covers nine major fairs, including Kumbh Mela, Pushkar Fair, and Gangasagar Mela.
  • Travel tips include best times to visit, accommodation options, and cultural highlights for each event.

Maha Kumbh 2025: The Crown Jewel of Indian Fairs

The Maha Kumbh Mela in Prayagraj, scheduled for 2025, is expected to draw millions of pilgrims and tourists. This event, held once every 12 years, is the largest peaceful gathering on Earth. Visitors can witness the ritual bathing at the confluence of the Ganga, Yamuna, and the mythical Saraswati rivers.

Organisers have planned extensive infrastructure upgrades, including temporary bridges and sanitation facilities. Authorities recommend booking accommodation months in advance, as hotels and tent cities fill up rapidly.

Seven Other Kumbh Melas: Where Faith Meets Tradition

Beyond the Maha Kumbh, India hosts several other Kumbh Melas at regular intervals. The Ardh Kumbh Mela, held every six years in Prayagraj and Haridwar, attracts fewer crowds but offers a similarly spiritual experience. The Kumbh Mela rotates among four locations: Prayagraj, Haridwar, Nashik, and Ujjain.

For those seeking a less crowded experience, the Magh Mela in Prayagraj occurs annually, with a significant turnout during the month of Magha. Similarly, the Simhastha Kumbh in Ujjain, held every 12 years, draws devotees to the banks of the Shipra River.

Pushkar Fair: A Blend of Camels and Culture

The Pushkar Fair in Rajasthan, held each November, is one of India's most vibrant cattle fairs. It features camel races, cultural performances, and a bustling market. This event attracts both domestic travellers and international photographers.

Visitors can stay in desert camps or guesthouses in Pushkar. The fair coincides with the Kartik Purnima festival, adding a religious dimension to the spectacle. Travel tips include dressing modestly and bargaining at the market.

Gangasagar Mela: Pilgrimage at the Bay of Bengal

The Gangasagar Mela, held on Sagar Island in West Bengal, is a major pilgrimage where devotees bathe at the confluence of the Ganga and the Bay of Bengal. This event, occurring on Makar Sankranti in January, sees over a million participants.

Access to the island requires a ferry from Kakdwip, which can be crowded during the mela. Authorities have improved safety measures in recent years, but travellers should plan for long queues and limited amenities.

Hemis Festival: Ladakh's Monastic Marvel

The Hemis Festival in Ladakh, held in July, celebrates the birth of Guru Padmasambhava. This colourful event at Hemis Monastery features masked dances, traditional music, and a vibrant market. It is a must-see for those interested in Tibetan Buddhist culture.

Travel to Ladakh requires acclimatisation to high altitudes. The best time to visit is during the festival, but permits are needed for certain areas. Accommodation in Leh is available, but booking ahead is advisable.

Other Notable Melas: From Goa to Tamil Nadu

The Goa Carnival, held in February, showcases Portuguese-influenced culture with parades, floats, and music. The Mahamaham festival in Kumbakonam, Tamil Nadu, occurs once every 12 years and involves ritual bathing in a sacred tank.

The Kumbh Mela in Nashik and Ujjain also draw large crowds. Each mela has unique customs and local delicacies. Travellers should research specific dates and logistics, as these can vary annually.

What to Watch Next

Authorities are expected to release detailed schedules for the Maha Kumbh 2025 in the coming months. Travellers should monitor official channels for updates on accommodation and transportation. Early planning remains the key to a smooth pilgrimage experience.
