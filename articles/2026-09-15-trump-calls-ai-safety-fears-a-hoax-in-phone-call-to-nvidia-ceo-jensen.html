Trump Calls AI Safety Fears a 'Hoax' in Phone Call to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-15 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump used a live phone call to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang at the All-In Summit to dismiss AI safety guardrails as a 'hoax', signalling lighter regulation ahead for US AI firms.
  • Nvidia's data centre GPU dominance (H100, H200, Blackwell) makes it the biggest commercial beneficiary of any regulatory rollback in Washington.
  • For Indian buyers, the policy shift could lower AI compute costs and accelerate cloud GPU availability, but also raises vendor lock-in and safety-compliance risks.

A Live Call, a Blunt Message

At the All-In Summit, former US President Donald Trump was put on speakerphone with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, turning a tech conference into a political statement. Trump dismissed AI safety fears as a "hoax" and described organised opposition to data centres as a "sick conspiracy", while praising Huang and backing American leadership in AI over China. The moment was choreographed, but the commercial signal is real: Washington's most influential voice on the right is now openly aligned with the AI build-out.

For buyers of AI hardware and cloud compute, this is not abstract politics. It directly shapes how fast GPUs reach the market, how they are priced, and what compliance obligations sit on top of them.

Why Nvidia Sits at the Centre of This Fight

Nvidia controls the overwhelming majority of the market for AI accelerators. Its H100 and H200 chips remain the default for large-model training, while the Blackwell generation (B100, B200) is ramping into data centres globally. Any political shift that weakens proposed AI safety guardrails in the US benefits Nvidia first, because it reduces the risk of export restrictions, mandatory safety audits, or licensing hurdles on its fastest-selling products.

Huang has consistently argued that AI safety should be handled through industry standards rather than heavy-handed regulation. Trump's public endorsement of that view gives Nvidia political cover as it expands capacity and lobbies against state-level data centre opposition.

Real-World Buyer Implications

For Indian enterprises, startups, and cloud providers, the practical question is cost and availability. Nvidia GPUs are already scarce and priced in dollars; a single H100 server can run into tens of lakhs of rupees, and full training clusters cost crores. If US regulatory pressure eases, Nvidia can expand supply faster, which could soften rental prices on platforms like AWS, Azure, and CoreWeave over the next 12-18 months.

The flip side is compliance. Indian firms serving US or European clients still face EU AI Act obligations and sectoral rules, regardless of what Washington says. A "hoax" framing in the US does not remove liability for an Indian bank deploying an unvalidated AI model.

  • Pro: Lighter US regulation may accelerate GPU supply and reduce cloud AI compute costs.
  • Pro: Stronger US government backing for AI data centres supports long-term infrastructure investment.
  • Con: Weaker safety guardrails increase reputational and legal risk for enterprises exporting to regulated markets.
  • Con: Political volatility around AI policy makes multi-year procurement planning harder.
  • Watch: Nvidia's Blackwell allocation to Asian cloud providers will determine near-term availability.

What to Watch Next

Track three signals: Nvidia's data centre revenue guidance, US state-level data centre approvals, and any new export-control language covering Blackwell-class chips. If the regulatory freeze holds, expect aggressive pricing on AI instances and more Nvidia-certified partners in India. If it reverses after the next election cycle, buyers locked into US-only stacks may need contingency plans.

Huang's willingness to take that call in public tells you where Nvidia believes its growth lies: in speed, scale, and political access, not in waiting for global consensus on AI safety.
