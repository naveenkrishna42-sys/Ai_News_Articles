Pakistan takes SCO chair, Modi warns on terror tactics

Key points

Pakistan assumes SCO chairmanship

Pakistan has taken over the chairmanship of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO), a move that has sent ripples through regional politics. This transition is significant as it places Pakistan at the helm of a bloc that includes both India and China, two of the world's most populous countries. The SCO, founded in 2001, aims to promote peace, security, and cooperation among its member states.

PM Modi's sharp criticism of Pakistan

During the SCO summit, Prime Minister Narendra Modi has delivered a scathing critique of Pakistan's use of terrorism as a strategic tool. Modi's remarks come amid a backdrop of escalating tensions between India and Pakistan, particularly over the Kashmir dispute. Modi accused Pakistan of using terror attacks to achieve its political and strategic objectives, a claim that Pakistan has vehemently denied.

Pakistan's response and diplomatic fallout

Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has retaliated against Modi's accusations, hitting back at India's stance on the Indus Waters Treaty. The treaty, signed in 1960, outlines the sharing of water resources between India and Pakistan. Sharif's remarks have further escalated the diplomatic tensions between the two nuclear-armed neighbors.

Broader regional and global implications

The SCO summit has highlighted the complex geopolitical dynamics at play in South Asia. Modi's call for an end to wars and conflicts has been seen as a powerful message of peace. However, the ongoing tensions between India and Pakistan, as well as other regional conflicts, have a significant impact on global energy and trade.
