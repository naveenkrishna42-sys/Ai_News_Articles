BJP Opposes Bengaluru’s Second Airport in Shivakumar’s Constituency

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP opposes second airport in Kanakapura, citing environmental concerns
  • Kanakapura is the constituency of Karnataka CM Shivakumar
  • Proposed site faces opposition over land acquisition and ecological impact

Political Opposition to Bengaluru’s Second Airport

The Bharatiya Janata Party has formally opposed the proposed second airport for Bengaluru, which is planned in Kanakapura — the home turf of Karnataka Chief Minister Shivakumar. The party has raised concerns over environmental impact, land acquisition costs, and the viability of the location.

State BJP leaders argue that the proposed site in Kanakapura, about 60 km from the city, is ecologically sensitive. They claim it could damage nearby forests and water bodies. The party has also questioned the need for a second airport when the existing Kempegowda International Airport can still be expanded.

BJP's Stance on Feasibility

Senior BJP functionaries have stated that the project appears driven by political considerations rather than genuine aviation demand. They point out that Kanakapura is Shivakumar’s assembly constituency, and the decision lacks thorough technical assessment.

“We are not against development, but this location is unsuitable,” a state BJP spokesperson said. The party has demanded a fresh feasibility study and public consultation before any land acquisition begins.

Government’s Rationale

The Congress government in Karnataka has defended the project, citing the rapid growth of Bengaluru’s air traffic. Officials note that the existing airport is nearing saturation, with passenger numbers crossing 40 million annually. A second airport, they argue, is essential to handle future demand.

The government has also pointed to the economic benefits for the Kanakapura region, which is largely rural. The proposed airport is expected to generate thousands of jobs and boost local infrastructure.

Environmental and Local Concerns

Environmental activists have joined the opposition, warning that the project could destroy wetlands and wildlife habitats. Farmers in the area have also protested, fearing loss of agricultural land without adequate compensation.

The state government has promised to conduct an environmental impact assessment and ensure fair rehabilitation for affected families. However, no timeline for the assessment has been announced.

What Happens Next

The state government is expected to submit a detailed project report to the Union Ministry of Civil Aviation within months. The BJP’s opposition could delay approvals, especially if the central government demands additional studies. The final decision will hinge on the outcome of the feasibility and environmental reviews.
