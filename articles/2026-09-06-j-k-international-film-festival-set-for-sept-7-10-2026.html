J&K International Film Festival Set for Sept 7–10, 2026

Key points
  • Festival runs Sept 7–10, 2026, featuring 45 Indian and international films across three venues.
  • Ticket prices range from INR 300 to INR 2,500, with early‑bird discounts for students and seniors.
  • Expect 15,000‑20,000 attendees and a INR 5–crore budget supporting local filmmakers.

About the Festival

The International Film Festival of Jammu and Kashmir (IFFJ) will open on September 7, 2026, and conclude on September 10, marking its fourth consecutive year of celebrating cinema in the region. Organized by the J&K State Film Development Corporation in partnership with the Indian Film Festival Federation, the event showcases both established and emerging filmmakers from India and abroad. With a INR 5–crore budget, the festival aims to boost regional tourism and provide a platform for Kashmiri storytellers.

Venues include the Sher-e-Kashmir International Conference Centre in Srinagar, the Cultural Centre of Excellence in Jammu, and the historic Maharaja’s Summer Palace grounds. Each location offers modern projection equipment and acoustic systems capable of screening 4K content, ensuring a premium viewing experience for audiences. The festival’s opening night will feature a gala screening of the award‑winning Indian drama “The Last Letter,” followed by a live musical performance by Kashmiri folk artists.

Program Highlights

Feature Films Competition

The competitive section presents 20 feature films, half of which are Indian productions and the remainder international entries. Films are judged on narrative depth, technical execution, and cultural relevance, with cash prizes of INR 2‑lakhs for winners. Notable entries include “Mountain Echoes” from Nepal and “Desert Dawn” from Egypt, both making their South Asian premieres at IFFJ.

Directors such as Anurag Kashyap and Lijo Jose Pellissery will attend master‑class sessions, offering insights into contemporary filmmaking techniques. Audience participation is encouraged through post‑screening Q‑A panels, fostering dialogue between creators and viewers.

Documentary Section

Ten documentaries focusing on social issues, environmental concerns, and cultural heritage will be screened in a dedicated pavilion. These films range from short pieces on Himalayan wildlife to in‑depth looks at the region’s textile industry. The documentary block is free for students with ID, promoting accessibility and education.

Filmmakers will also conduct workshops on digital editing and distribution platforms, equipping local creators with practical skills. The section aims to generate awareness and potential funding for community‑based projects.

Logistics & Visitor Guide

Ticket pricing is structured to attract diverse demographics: INR 300 for general admission, INR 500 for premium seatings, and INR 2,500 for VIP packages that include meet‑and‑greet sessions with directors. Early‑bird discounts of 20% are available for bookings made before August 31, with additional 10% off for senior citizens and students. All tickets can be purchased online via the festival’s official portal or at the venue boxes.

Transportation is facilitated by state‑run buses and authorized taxi services, with special shuttle routes from Srinagar’s Sheikh Khalifa Airport to the conference centre. Accommodation options range from budget guesthouses to five‑star hotels, many offering festival‑specific rates. Visitors are advised to carry ID proof and follow COVID‑appropriate guidelines, as per the union health ministry’s latest directives.
