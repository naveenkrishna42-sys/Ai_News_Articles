Hyderabad Traffic Chaos: Overnight Rain Wreaks Havoc on Roads

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Overnight rain led to waterlogging and traffic snarls in Hyderabad
  • Commuters faced long delays on major roads and junctions
  • Authorities are working to clear water and restore normal traffic

Hyderabad woke up to a watery mess on Tuesday after overnight rain lashed the city, throwing traffic out of gear across several key corridors. The downpour, which continued into the early hours, left roads waterlogged and commuters battling long delays during the morning rush hour.

Major junctions hit

Areas such as Begumpet, Ameerpet, and Khairatabad reported significant waterlogging, with vehicles crawling through knee-deep water. The situation was particularly bad at underpasses and low-lying stretches, where cars stalled and buses had to be rerouted.

Traffic police were deployed in large numbers to manage the chaos, but the sheer volume of water overwhelmed drainage systems in several localities. Commuters took to social media to share photos and videos of submerged roads, with many reporting hour-long delays on stretches that usually take minutes.

Commuters bear the brunt

Office-goers and students faced the worst of the ordeal, with many choosing to walk part of the way after their vehicles got stuck. Autos and cabs were scarce, and those available charged inflated fares, adding to the frustration.

"I left home at 8 am but reached office only by 11 am," said a software professional travelling from Kukatpally to Hitec City. "The road near Madhapur was completely jammed, and there was no way to move."

Public transport disrupted

Telangana State Road Transport Corporation (TSRTC) buses reported delays of up to 45 minutes on several routes, with some services diverted due to waterlogging. The Hyderabad Metro, however, ran largely on time, offering some relief to commuters.

Authorities have not yet confirmed the total rainfall recorded, but weather officials had earlier forecast heavy spells for the city. The Indian Meteorological Department has warned of more rain in the coming days, which could prolong the disruption.

Officials respond

Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) teams were seen clearing blocked drains and pumping out water from affected areas. Officials said they were working on priority to restore normalcy, but admitted that the city's ageing drainage network was struggling to cope.

"We are on the ground and attending to complaints on a war footing," a GHMC official said. "Some areas may take a few more hours to clear completely."

The traffic police have urged commuters to avoid non-essential travel and to use alternative routes where possible. They have also advised against driving through flooded stretches, as water levels can be deceptive.

What to watch

With more rain predicted, Hyderabadis are bracing for another difficult day on Wednesday. The focus will be on how quickly authorities can clear the backlog and whether the city's infrastructure can withstand another spell of heavy downpour.
