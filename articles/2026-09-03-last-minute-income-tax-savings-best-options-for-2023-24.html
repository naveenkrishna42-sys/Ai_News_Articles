Introduction

As the deadline for filing income tax returns approaches, many individuals are looking for last-minute income tax saving options. With the fiscal year 2023-24 coming to an end, it's crucial to explore various tax-saving instruments to maximize deductions and reduce taxable income. This article delves into the best options available, including credit cards, Public Provident Fund (PPF), and Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS).

Credit Card Spending

Credit card spending is one of the most immediate and effective ways to save on taxes. By making purchases using a credit card, you can claim reimbursement of up to 2 lakh per financial year under Section 80G of the Income Tax Act. This can significantly reduce your taxable income.

Maximum DeductionUp to 2 lakh per financial year
Applicable SectionSection 80G
ValidityFor expenses incurred on charitable donations

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Immediate tax benefit, no lock-in period, flexible spending
  • Cons: Limited deduction amount, requires proof of expenditure

Public Provident Fund (PPF)

The Public Provident Fund (PPF) is a popular long-term savings scheme offered by the government. It provides a guaranteed return of 7.1% per annum, which is subject to change. The interest earned on PPF is tax-exempt under Section 10(8) of the Income Tax Act.

Specification / MetricVerified Details
Annual Interest Rate7.1% (subject to change)
Lock-in Period15 years
Maximum Investment1.5 lakh per financial year

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Guaranteed returns, tax-free interest, long-term investment
  • Cons: Lock-in period, maximum investment limit

Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS)

Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) are mutual fund investments that offer tax benefits under Section 80C of the Income Tax Act. These schemes invest in a diversified portfolio of equity and equity-related instruments. The lock-in period for ELSS is three years, and the units can be redeemed after this period.

Specification / MetricVerified Details
Lock-in Period3 years
Maximum Investment1.5 lakh per financial year
Tax BenefitUp to 1.5 lakh deduction under Section 80C

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Long-term investment, potential for capital appreciation, tax benefits
  • Cons: Market risk, lock-in period, requires mutual fund knowledge

Conclusion

Before the deadline for filing income tax returns, it's essential to explore various tax-saving options. Credit card spending, Public Provident Fund (PPF), and Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS) are among the best options available. Each of these instruments offers unique benefits and should be considered based on individual financial goals and risk appetite.
