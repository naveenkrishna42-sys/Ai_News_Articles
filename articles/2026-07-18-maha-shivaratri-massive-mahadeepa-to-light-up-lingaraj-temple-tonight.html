Maha Shivaratri: Massive Mahadeepa to Light Up Lingaraj Temple Tonight

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Mahadeepa, a giant lamp, will be lit atop the Lingaraj Temple at 10 pm on Maha Shivaratri.
  • The tradition is linked to a legend of King Lalatendu Keshari, who sought Lord Shiva's forgiveness.
  • Thousands of devotees are expected to gather for the ritual, a highlight of the festival.

Bhubaneswar is set for a spectacle tonight. As Maha Shivaratri reaches its peak, the 11th-century Lingaraj Temple will be bathed in the glow of a massive lamp known as the Mahadeepa. The ritual is scheduled for 10 pm, drawing thousands of devotees to the ancient shrine.

The Legend Behind the Mahadeepa

The lighting of the Mahadeepa is not merely a ceremonial act. It is rooted in local lore. According to tradition, the practice was started centuries ago by King Lalatendu Keshari of the Somavamsi dynasty.

The story goes that the king, after a grave mistake, sought Lord Shiva's pardon. In his penance, he vowed to light a colossal lamp atop the temple every Maha Shivaratri. The flame, visible for miles, was meant to symbolize the cosmic light of the deity and serve as a beacon of devotion.

Preparations and Rituals at the Temple

Preparations for the Mahadeepa began days in advance. Priests and temple staff have been busy cleaning the sanctum and preparing the giant wick. The lamp, made of brass and cloth, is filled with ghee and camphor.

The lighting will follow a series of rituals that have been performed throughout the day. Devotees have been offering prayers, chanting hymns, and observing fasts. The temple premises are expected to remain open late into the night to accommodate the crowds.

What Makes This Year Special

Officials have not confirmed any specific changes for this year's ceremony, but security has been tightened. Additional police personnel have been deployed to manage the expected rush. Live broadcasts on local channels will allow those unable to attend to witness the event.

The Mahadeepa is a key highlight of the five-day Maha Shivaratri festival at Lingaraj Temple. The temple, dedicated to Lord Shiva, sees its highest footfall during this period.

The Symbolism of the Giant Lamp

For the faithful, the Mahadeepa is more than a light. It represents the triumph of good over darkness, and the eternal presence of Shiva. The act of lighting it is seen as an offering of the self—a way to burn away ego and ignorance.

Devotees believe that witnessing the Mahadeepa brings blessings and washes away sins. The ritual has been passed down through generations, surviving centuries of change.

The temple administration expects the Mahadeepa to burn through the night, its flame a silent prayer in the sky. For the thousands gathered, it will be a moment of collective devotion and quiet wonder.

What happens next: The Mahadeepa will be extinguished at sunrise, marking the end of Maha Shivaratri. The temple will then prepare for the final day of festivities, including a grand procession of the deity.
