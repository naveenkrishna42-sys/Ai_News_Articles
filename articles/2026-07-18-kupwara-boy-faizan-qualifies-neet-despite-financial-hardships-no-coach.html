Kupwara boy Faizan qualifies NEET despite financial hardships, no coaching

📅 2026-07-18 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Faizan, a resident of Kupwara, cleared NEET UG 2026 without private coaching due to financial constraints.
  • He relied on free online resources, school lessons, and self-study to prepare for the exam.
  • His achievement highlights the challenges faced by students from remote and economically weaker backgrounds.

A dream without a price tag

Faizan, a young man from Kupwara in north Kashmir, has cleared the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET) UG 2026. He did it without the cushion of private coaching that most successful candidates depend on. His family could not afford the high fees charged by coaching centres.

For Faizan, the path to medicine was lined with obstacles. Kupwara, a district known for its scenic beauty but also for its remoteness and economic struggles, does not offer easy access to expensive test-prep institutes. Many students from the region migrate to Srinagar or other cities for coaching. Faizan stayed back.

Self-study and free resources

Faizan prepared using free online materials, textbooks from his school, and notes shared by friends. He spent long hours at home, often studying by the light of a kerosene lamp during power cuts, which are frequent in the area. He did not have a smartphone until late in his preparation, relying on a shared device at home to access YouTube lectures.

“I knew I had to work twice as hard because I didn’t have a teacher guiding me step by step,” Faizan told Greater Kashmir. He followed the NCERT syllabus rigorously and solved previous years’ question papers repeatedly. His routine was simple: wake up early, study, help his father with odd jobs, and study again late into the night.

Family sacrifices and community support

Faizan’s father works as a daily-wage labourer. His mother manages the household with whatever little they have. The family’s priority was to ensure Faizan could continue his education, even if that meant cutting back on other expenses. A local teacher, impressed by Faizan’s dedication, lent him some reference books.

Neighbours and relatives chipped in with small amounts of money to cover his exam fees and travel to the test centre in Srinagar. Faizan’s success, therefore, is not just a personal triumph but a collective effort by a community that believes in the power of education.

A broader trend of resilience

Faizan is not alone. This year’s NEET results have thrown up several stories of students from rural and economically disadvantaged backgrounds cracking the exam. Candidates from Bihar and Uttar Pradesh, states with a history of educational disparity, have also performed strongly in the 2026 re-exam. Multiple Resonance students, a coaching chain, secured top ranks, but Faizan’s story stands out because he did it without any institutional support.

The highest-ranked female candidate, Shravani Kudale, who secured All India Rank 5, told The Times of India that she stayed away from her phone and social media for a full year. Faizan’s discipline mirrored that — he had no smartphone to distract him, only the focus to succeed.

What lies ahead

Faizan now needs to navigate the NEET UG counselling process, which is set to begin soon. He will need financial help for college fees and living expenses if he secures a seat in a government medical college. The family is hopeful but anxious. Local civil society groups have already started raising funds to support his medical education.

For now, Kupwara has a new hero. Faizan’s journey is a reminder that talent and determination can break through the hardest of circumstances. The real test, however, will be whether he gets the support to complete what he has started.
