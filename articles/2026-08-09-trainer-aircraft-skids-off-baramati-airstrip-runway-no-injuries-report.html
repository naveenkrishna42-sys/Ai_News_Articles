Trainer aircraft skids off Baramati airstrip runway; no injuries reported

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trainer aircraft of a private company skidded off the runway at Baramati airstrip in Maharashtra on Sunday.
  • No casualties or injuries have been reported in the incident.
  • This is the second aircraft mishap at the airstrip, raising safety concerns.
  • The airstrip has been in the news after a previous incident involving Deputy Chief Minister Ajit Pawar.

Incident at Baramati airstrip

A trainer aircraft belonging to a private company skidded off the runway at the Baramati airstrip in Maharashtra on Sunday. The aircraft veered off the tarmac under circumstances that are yet to be officially detailed. Emergency response teams reached the spot soon after the incident.

Officials have confirmed that there were no casualties. The condition of those on board, if any, has not been officially stated. The aircraft is reported to have suffered damage, though the exact extent is unclear.

Second mishap in recent weeks

This is the second aircraft-related incident at the Baramati airstrip in a short span. The previous one involved Deputy Chief Minister Ajit Pawar, whose aircraft had met with an accident. That incident had drawn significant attention, given Pawar's political stature.

The repeat of such an event has inevitably raised questions about the safety protocols at the airstrip. Local aviation experts and residents are now asking whether adequate measures are in place to handle emergencies at this facility, which has seen increased activity in recent months.

Safety concerns mount

The airstrip, located in the agricultural heartland of western Maharashtra, is used for both private and official travel. Its proximity to the Pawar family's political base makes it a point of interest, especially after the earlier incident involving the senior leader.

While authorities have not yet commented on the cause of Sunday's skid, preliminary observations suggest a possible technical snag or pilot error. Investigators are expected to examine the aircraft and the runway conditions to determine what went wrong.

What lies ahead

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) is likely to initiate a probe into the incident. A formal report may take days to emerge, and until then, questions about the airstrip's safety standards will persist.

For now, the focus remains on clearing the runway and restoring normal operations. The incident serves as a reminder that even routine training flights carry risks, and the authorities may need to revisit their safety audit procedures for such airstrips across the state.

As investigations proceed, all eyes will be on whether any systemic lapses are identified and rectified. The airstrip's future usage, particularly for political and private aviation, could hinge on the findings of this probe.
