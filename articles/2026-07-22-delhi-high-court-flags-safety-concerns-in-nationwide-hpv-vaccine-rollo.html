Delhi High Court flags safety concerns in nationwide HPV vaccine rollout

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi High Court has termed safety issues in the HPV vaccine rollout a 'very serious issue'.
  • The court issued a notice to the Centre and the ICMR on a PIL challenging the vaccination drive.
  • The plea argues the vaccine was rolled out without adequate clinical studies in India.
  • The nationwide programme targets girls aged 9-14 to prevent cervical cancer.

The Delhi High Court on Wednesday flagged safety concerns in the nationwide rollout of the Human Papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine, calling it a 'very serious issue'. The court was hearing a public interest litigation that challenges the mass vaccination drive, arguing it was launched without sufficient clinical trials on the Indian population.

Court issues notice to Centre and ICMR

A bench of Justices Rajiv Shakdher and Amit Bansal issued a formal notice to the Union Ministry of Health and the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), seeking their response. The court has asked the authorities to explain the scientific basis and safety data behind the decision to vaccinate millions of schoolgirls across the country.

The petitioner, represented by advocate Ashok Agarwal, argued that the vaccine was cleared for a mass programme without a proper phase 4 post-marketing surveillance study in India. The bench noted the gravity of the matter and directed the respondents to file a detailed affidavit within three weeks.

Plea questions lack of Indian studies

The PIL contends that the HPV vaccines currently in use were primarily tested on populations in developed countries, with little data on their long-term effects on Indian girls. The petitioner has demanded that the government release all available trial data and conduct a fresh, independent study before proceeding further.

Lawyers for the petitioners pointed out that while cervical cancer is a major health concern, rushing into a massive immunisation programme without addressing safety questions could put healthy children at risk. They cited reports of adverse events following immunisation in other countries as a reason for caution.

Government's vaccination targets

The Union government had earlier announced plans to vaccinate girls aged 9 to 14 years under a national programme, aiming to reduce the burden of cervical cancer. The drive was initially piloted in select districts before being expanded. However, the court's intervention has cast a shadow over the timeline.

The ICMR and the health ministry are expected to present their safety dossier and explain why they consider the vaccines safe for mass use. The court has scheduled the next hearing for August, giving the government time to prepare its case.

Broader concerns over vaccine policy

The case has reignited a debate in medical circles about the adequacy of drug and vaccine trials in India. Critics argue that regulatory approvals sometimes rely on foreign data, overlooking genetic and environmental differences that could affect safety and efficacy. The court's remark that the issue is 'very serious' signals that the judiciary is closely watching the matter.

Health activists have welcomed the court's intervention, saying it will force a transparent review of the vaccine's safety record. They have also called for public hearings where affected families can share their experiences.

The next hearing will be closely watched by public health experts, vaccine manufacturers, and parents across the country. The court's decision could set a precedent for how new vaccines are introduced in India's national immunisation schedule.
