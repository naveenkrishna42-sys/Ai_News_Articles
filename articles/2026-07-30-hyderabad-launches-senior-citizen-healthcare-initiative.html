Hyderabad launches senior citizen healthcare initiative

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hyderabad municipal corporation launched a senior citizen healthcare programme.
  • Initiative includes subsidised health screenings and free home visits for bedridden elderly.
  • Programme targets early detection of age-related conditions like diabetes and hypertension.

The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) has rolled out a healthcare initiative specifically for senior citizens. The programme, announced on Thursday, aims to make preventive and primary care more accessible for the city's elderly population.

What the programme offers

Under the initiative, residents aged 60 and above can avail subsidised health screenings at designated municipal clinics. The package includes checks for blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, and common age-related vision and hearing issues.

For senior citizens who are bedridden or have mobility challenges, the corporation will arrange free home visits by paramedical staff. These teams will conduct basic health assessments and advise caregivers on managing chronic conditions.

Focus on early detection

Officials said the programme's primary goal is early detection of non-communicable diseases such as diabetes and hypertension. These conditions are prevalent among the elderly and often go undiagnosed until complications arise.

The initiative also includes counselling sessions on nutrition, medication adherence, and fall prevention. GHMC will partner with local geriatric specialists to conduct quarterly awareness camps in each zone.

Registration and rollout

Senior citizens can register at their nearest ward office or through the municipal corporation's mobile app. A dedicated helpline number has been set up for enquiries and to schedule home visits.

The programme will first launch in three pilot zones — Secunderabad, Kukatpally, and LB Nagar — before expanding to the entire city over the next three months. The corporation has allocated an initial budget of Rs 2 crore for the first year.

Why this matters

Hyderabad's population of senior citizens has grown steadily, with estimates suggesting over 15 lakh residents are aged 60 and above. Many live alone or in households where younger members work during the day, making regular clinic visits difficult.

Public health experts have long flagged the lack of geriatric-focused services in municipal healthcare. This initiative is a step toward addressing that gap, though officials acknowledge it is not a substitute for comprehensive geriatric care.

The programme will be reviewed after six months based on enrolment numbers and health outcomes. If successful, the model may be replicated in other urban local bodies across Telangana.
