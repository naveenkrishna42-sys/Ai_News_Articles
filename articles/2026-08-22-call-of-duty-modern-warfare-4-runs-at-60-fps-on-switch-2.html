Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 runs at 60 fps on Switch 2

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Early video footage demonstrates 60 fps gameplay on Switch 2 hardware.
  • The port follows Microsoft's 10-year commitment to bring the franchise to Nintendo consoles.
  • Technical testing remains in the early stages as users report varied performance experiences.

Performance Benchmarks on New Hardware

Leaked footage circulating online this week provides the first look at Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 running on Nintendo's successor to the Switch. The video highlights a consistent 60 frames per second (fps) output, a significant technical hurdle for mobile-hybrid hardware. This development signals a major shift in how high-fidelity shooters are optimized for portable systems.

The performance demonstration appears to align with the hardware capabilities hinted at by industry leaks over the past year. While earlier reports suggested potential compromises in graphical fidelity, the current footage shows the game maintaining fluid motion during active combat sequences. Industry analysts have long waited for evidence that the upcoming console could handle AAA titles with demanding technical requirements.

The Microsoft and Nintendo Agreement

This port is a direct result of the 10-year partnership agreement signed between Microsoft and Nintendo. The deal was finalized following Microsoft's acquisition of Activision Blizzard, aiming to expand the reach of the Call of Duty franchise to a wider user base. Nintendo fans have historically lacked access to the series, making this release a significant milestone for both companies.

The integration process has not been without friction. While the 60 fps footage has impressed some early observers, other reports indicate that optimization remains a work in progress. Some testers have encountered software bugs, including update loop errors that prevent the game from launching correctly on the new hardware.

Technical Hurdles and Beta Testing

As the beta phase for Modern Warfare 4 officially opened on August 21, users across different platforms have begun stress-testing the servers. On the Switch 2, the primary concern remains the balance between frame rate stability and visual resolution. Achieving 60 fps often requires dynamic resolution scaling, which can lead to blurriness in complex environments.

  • Early access for the beta began on August 21 across major platforms.
  • Technical support forums are currently tracking reports of 'checking for updates' errors.
  • Developers are expected to release patches to stabilize the experience before the global launch.

The transition of a franchise as computationally intensive as Call of Duty to Nintendo hardware marks a turning point for mobile gaming. Whether the platform can sustain this level of performance across larger, more demanding maps remains to be seen. Developers are likely prioritizing stability and frame rate consistency to meet the high expectations of the core player base.

Industry observers will continue to monitor performance updates and patch notes as the beta progresses toward the final retail release.
