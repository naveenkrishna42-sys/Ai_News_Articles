Bitcoin holds above $65k as US inflation, Iran risks loom

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin trades above $65,000, holding steady amid market uncertainty.
  • US inflation figures and Iran-related geopolitical risks are key market drivers.
  • Analysts say crypto remains sensitive to macro and geopolitical news.

Bitcoin steadies above $65,000

Bitcoin held its ground above the $65,000 mark on Monday, as traders balanced hopes of easing U.S. inflation against fresh geopolitical concerns involving Iran. The world's largest cryptocurrency showed resilience, though gains remained capped by cautious sentiment across risk assets.

Market participants are now fixated on upcoming U.S. inflation data, which could shape the Federal Reserve's next policy move. A softer reading might bolster rate-cut bets, a scenario that tends to favour speculative assets like Bitcoin. Conversely, sticky inflation could reinforce the Fed's higher-for-longer stance, pressuring digital currencies.

Iran tensions add to market jitters

Adding to the mix, reports of heightened tensions around Iran have kept investors on edge. Any escalation in the Middle East typically fuels a flight to safe havens, pulling money out of volatile assets, including cryptocurrencies. While Bitcoin has often been touted as a hedge, in practice it has traded more like a risk-on asset in recent months.

Geopolitical shocks have previously triggered sharp, albeit short-lived, sell-offs in crypto markets. Traders are now weighing whether the current situation could lead to a similar bout of volatility, or if the market's growing institutional base will absorb the shock more smoothly.

Inflation data as the next catalyst

The immediate focus for crypto traders is the release of U.S. inflation numbers later this week. The data will provide fresh clues on whether price pressures are cooling enough for the Fed to begin cutting interest rates. Lower rates typically reduce the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like Bitcoin, making it more attractive to investors.

Analysts note that Bitcoin's recent price action has been tightly correlated with expectations for U.S. monetary policy. A dovish surprise could push prices toward recent highs, while a hot print might drag it below the psychological $65,000 support level.

Support and resistance levels

Technical charts show Bitcoin finding support around $64,500–$65,000, a zone that has held firm over the past week. On the upside, resistance sits near $67,000, a level that has capped rallies in recent sessions. A breakout above that could open the door for a test of $70,000, a milestone that has eluded the market for weeks.

Trading volumes remain moderate, suggesting that large institutional players are waiting for clearer signals before committing fresh capital. Retail interest, meanwhile, has stayed steady, with search activity for Bitcoin-related terms holding at average levels.

Global crypto markets in sync

The broader cryptocurrency market mirrored Bitcoin's cautious tone. Major altcoins traded within narrow ranges, with Ethereum holding above its own key support level. The total market capitalisation of all cryptocurrencies remained largely unchanged, reflecting the wait-and-see mood among investors.

In India, where crypto regulation remains a work in progress, traders are keeping a close watch on global developments. The domestic market has shown resilience, but volumes tend to spike only when global prices break out of their current range.

What to watch next

With the inflation report and geopolitical headlines set to dominate the week, Bitcoin's next major move could come swiftly. A clear break above $67,000 would signal renewed bullish momentum, while a drop below $64,000 might invite fresh selling pressure.

Traders are also monitoring any new statements from U.S. officials on Iran, as well as the Fed's communication in the wake of the inflation data. For now, the market remains in a holding pattern, with the $65,000 level acting as the key battleground for bulls and bears alike.
