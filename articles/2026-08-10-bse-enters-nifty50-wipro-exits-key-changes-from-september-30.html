BSE enters Nifty50, Wipro exits: Key changes from September 30

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • BSE will replace Wipro in the Nifty50 index starting September 30, 2026.
  • The change is part of NSE Indices' semi-annual review based on six-month average free-float market capitalisation.
  • BSE's inclusion is expected to attract passive fund inflows of approximately $250-300 million.

Index Rejig: A New Player in the Benchmark

In a significant reshuffle of India's benchmark equity index, the National Stock Exchange (NSE) announced that BSE Ltd will enter the Nifty50, replacing IT major Wipro. The change will take effect from September 30, 2026.

The move, part of NSE Indices' semi-annual review, reflects the shifting weight of India's financial sector. BSE's market capitalisation has surged in recent years, driven by strong volumes in derivatives and a booming SME listing pipeline.

Wipro, once a heavyweight in the IT pack, has seen its stock underperform amid a weak global demand environment for technology services. The company's free-float market cap has lagged peers, making it an automatic candidate for exclusion.

What Triggers the Change

NSE Indices uses a rules-based methodology to select Nifty50 constituents. The criteria include six-month average free-float market capitalisation, liquidity, and trading frequency. Stocks ranked below 50th position are dropped, while those moving up enter the index.

According to the latest review, BSE's average free-float market cap has comfortably exceeded the threshold, while Wipro's has slipped. The exchange also meets the liquidity norms, with robust daily turnover in its shares.

Analysts note that the shift is not a commentary on Wipro's fundamentals but rather a reflection of market dynamics. The IT sector's weight in the index has been declining, while financial exchanges have gained favour with investors.

Impact on Fund Flows and Trading

Index changes typically trigger forced buying and selling by passive funds that track the Nifty50. Exchange-traded funds (ETFs), index funds, and institutional portfolios will need to realign their holdings by September 30, the effective date.

Market estimates suggest that BSE's entry could attract passive inflows of around $250-300 million. This is based on the average daily turnover and the weight BSE is likely to command in the index.

On the other hand, Wipro is expected to see outflows of a similar magnitude. However, the IT major remains a constituent of other indices like the Nifty IT, which could soften the impact.

Broader Market Implications

The reshuffle underscores a broader trend: the growing dominance of financial markets infrastructure in India's equity story. BSE's rise has been meteoric, with its stock price multiplying several times over the past few years.

For retail investors, the change means their index funds will now hold a stake in the country's oldest stock exchange. This adds a new dimension to passive investing, which has traditionally been tilted towards banking and IT stocks.

Wipro's exit, meanwhile, is a reminder of the cyclical nature of index composition. IT stocks had a golden run during the pandemic, but the sector has since faced headwinds from global slowdowns and AI disruption fears.

What to Watch

Between now and September 30, traders will watch for any last-minute adjustments in fund flows. The NSE may also announce other changes in the broader indices, including the Nifty Next 50, which could see entries from mid-cap names.

For BSE, the inclusion is likely to boost its visibility among global investors. The exchange has been expanding its product suite, including in the derivatives segment, which has seen record volumes.

As always, investors should focus on the long-term fundamentals of any stock, rather than reacting to index moves alone. The next semi-annual review is scheduled for early 2027, and further changes are expected.
