Sensex faces resistance at 76,300; market under persistent pressure

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex struggles to breach 76,300 mark, indicating strong resistance
  • Market remains under pressure from global and domestic factors
  • Investors cautious as volatility continues in Indian equities

Sensex stalls at 76,300 as bears hold ground

The BSE Sensex faced stiff resistance at the 76,300 level on Monday, unable to push past a wall of selling that has kept the benchmark under pressure for several sessions. Traders reported that every attempt to rally above this mark was met with fresh sell orders, particularly from institutional investors.

The index opened marginally higher but quickly ran out of steam. By mid-session, it had given up most of its early gains and was trading flat to negative. The 76,300 level has now emerged as a key psychological barrier.

Why the market is struggling

Multiple factors are weighing on sentiment. Globally, concerns over interest rate trajectories in the US and Europe have made foreign portfolio investors cautious. On the domestic front, mixed quarterly earnings from some large-cap firms have dampened enthusiasm.

Rising crude oil prices and a depreciating rupee have added to the unease. The rupee slipped past the 83.50 mark against the dollar, stoking fears of imported inflation and putting additional pressure on rate-sensitive sectors like banking and auto.

Broader market shows fatigue

The Nifty 50 mirrored the Sensex, hovering near its own resistance zone. Mid-cap and small-cap indices also struggled, indicating that the selling pressure is broad-based. Analysts said that valuations remain elevated in many pockets, leaving little room for error.

Volatility, as measured by the India VIX, edged higher. This suggests that traders are bracing for sharper swings in the near term. Options data showed maximum open interest at the 76,300 strike, confirming it as a strong resistance level for the Sensex.

What to watch for

All eyes are now on the next set of corporate earnings and global macroeconomic data. The US Federal Reserve's policy meeting minutes, due later this week, could provide clues on the pace of rate cuts. Any hawkish signal could trigger further selling in emerging markets like India.

Domestic institutional investors have been net buyers, but their buying has not been enough to offset foreign outflows. Market participants will closely track foreign portfolio investment flows in the coming sessions to gauge the direction of the trend.

For now, the 76,300 level remains the line in the sand. A decisive close above this mark could open the door for a rally towards 77,000. But if selling intensifies, the next support for the Sensex lies near 75,500.
