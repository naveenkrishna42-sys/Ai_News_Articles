RBI flexi-loan curbs may push NBFCs to costlier bullet loans: Kotak

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's recent curbs on NBFC flexi-credit products may push lenders towards bullet loans.
  • Kotak report suggests this shift could increase the cost of funds for NBFCs.
  • Borrowers may face higher interest rates or reduced flexibility in repayment options.

The Reserve Bank of India's recent restrictions on flexible credit products offered by non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) may inadvertently push lenders towards bullet loans, potentially raising the cost of funds and, in turn, the cost for borrowers, according to a report by Kotak Institutional Equities.

The RBI's move, aimed at curbing potential risks in the unsecured lending space, has created a regulatory gap that could alter the lending landscape for NBFCs, the report said. With flexi-loan structures now under tighter scrutiny, lenders are likely to recalibrate their product offerings, and bullet loans—where the principal is repaid in a lump sum at maturity—are emerging as a viable alternative.

Flexi Loans Under Scrutiny

Flexi-credit products, which allow borrowers to draw and repay funds within a sanctioned limit, have grown in popularity among Indian NBFCs, particularly in the personal loan and small business segments. These products offer borrowers the convenience of withdrawing money as needed, with interest charged only on the amount utilized.

However, the RBI's recent guidelines have imposed stricter norms on such products, citing concerns over over-leveraging and potential asset quality deterioration. The central bank's move is part of a broader effort to tighten oversight in the consumer credit space, which has seen rapid growth in recent years.

The Bullet Loan Alternative

According to the Kotak report, NBFCs may shift towards bullet loans to comply with the new regulations while maintaining product appeal. In a bullet loan structure, the borrower pays interest periodically but repays the entire principal at maturity, which can be a less flexible option compared to flexi loans.

This shift, however, comes with trade-offs. Bullet loans carry higher credit risk for lenders, as the principal is exposed for the entire tenure without periodic amortization. To compensate for this risk, NBFCs may need to price these loans higher, leading to increased borrowing costs for consumers and small businesses.

Cost Implications for Borrowers

The report suggests that the transition could raise the overall cost of funds for NBFCs, which are already grappling with elevated borrowing costs in the current interest rate environment. These incremental costs are likely to be passed on to borrowers, making credit more expensive at a time when demand for retail and MSME loans remains robust.

For borrowers, this could mean higher equated monthly instalments (EMIs) or reduced access to flexible credit lines, which were particularly attractive for managing working capital needs. Small businesses, in particular, may feel the pinch, as flexi loans were a popular tool for managing cash flow fluctuations.

Industry Response and Outlook

Industry experts note that while the RBI's intent is to safeguard financial stability, the unintended consequence could be a reduction in credit off-take or a shift in product mix. Some NBFCs may explore hybrid structures or innovate within the regulatory framework, but the near-term impact is likely to be a period of adjustment.

The Kotak report underscores the need for NBFCs to reassess their product strategies and pricing models. It also highlights the importance of maintaining asset quality, as the shift towards bullet loans could increase the risk of defaults if not managed prudently.

As the industry adapts to the new norms, all eyes will be on the RBI's next steps and whether it provides further clarity on the flexibility of credit products. For now, borrowers and lenders alike will need to navigate a changing landscape with higher costs and altered product dynamics.
