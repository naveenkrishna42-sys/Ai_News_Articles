China Plans Asteroid Collision Test, Targeting Smaller Space Rocks Than NASA's DART

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • China is planning a kinetic impact test to alter the trajectory of an asteroid.
  • The target is a smaller and less predictable asteroid than the one hit by NASA's DART mission.
  • A second spacecraft will observe the impact to study its effects.
  • The projectile will travel at approximately Mach 26 (9 km/s).

China is preparing to launch its first kinetic impact test for planetary defense, a mission designed to slam a spacecraft into an asteroid at hypersonic speed. The effort targets smaller and less predictable asteroids than the one hit by NASA's Double Asteroid Redirection Test (DART) mission in 2022.

Chinese state media and multiple science outlets have reported the plan, which involves a projectile moving at roughly Mach 26 — nine kilometres per second. A second observation spacecraft will trail the impactor to measure changes in the asteroid's orbit.

Smaller, Faster Targets

NASA's DART mission successfully altered the orbit of Dimorphos, a 160-metre-wide moonlet orbiting the larger asteroid Didymos. China's test aims at a different class of threat: asteroids that are smaller, move faster, and are harder to track.

Scientists consider these smaller objects more dangerous because they are more numerous and often go undetected until they are close to Earth. A collision with a city-sized asteroid could cause regional devastation, but even a 50-metre object could level an urban area.

Mission Design and Observers

The Chinese mission will involve two spacecraft. The first is the kinetic impactor, designed to crash into the target asteroid at extreme velocity. The second will remain at a safe distance, observing the collision and collecting data on the resulting debris cloud and trajectory shift.

State-run media outlet Global Times first reported the plan, and other outlets including Interesting Engineering and the South China Morning Post have confirmed the broad outlines. The China National Space Administration has not yet released a specific target asteroid or a launch date.

Why Kinetic Impact Matters

Kinetic impact is currently the most mature technology for deflecting an asteroid on a collision course with Earth. The method relies on sheer momentum: a fast-moving spacecraft transfers its energy to the asteroid, nudging it into a different orbit.

NASA proved the concept works with DART. China's test aims to validate the technique for a wider range of asteroids, including those with irregular shapes, loose surfaces, or unpredictable rotation.

What Happens Next

Chinese scientists are expected to select a target asteroid within the next year. The mission will likely launch before the end of the decade. Success would make China the second nation to demonstrate planetary defense via kinetic impact, and the first to test it on a smaller, more challenging object.

International planetary defense experts are watching closely. The results could shape how global space agencies prepare for the next real asteroid threat.
