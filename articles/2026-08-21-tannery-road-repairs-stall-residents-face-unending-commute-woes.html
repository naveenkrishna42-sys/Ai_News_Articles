Tannery Road repairs stall, residents face unending commute woes

Key points
  • Constant repair work has significantly hampered traffic flow on Tannery Road.
  • Commuters report long delays and hazardous driving conditions daily.
  • Lack of a clear completion timeline continues to frustrate the local community.

Infrastructure Grind on Tannery Road

Bengaluru’s Tannery Road has effectively become a no-go zone for daily commuters. What began as essential infrastructure repair has morphed into an indefinite ordeal, with heavy machinery and excavated earth blocking major parts of the carriageway.

Motorists navigating the area report that the road surface is barely navigable. Deep trenches remain open for days, often left without adequate barricades or safety warnings. The result is a chaotic bottleneck that stalls traffic for several kilometres during peak hours.

Commuters Struggle with Daily Delays

Local residents have voiced significant frustration over the lack of progress. Many claim that the work proceeds at a snail’s pace, with workers appearing on-site only sporadically. The dust from the construction site has also become a major health concern for shopkeepers and pedestrians living along the stretch.

The current state of the road is forcing drivers to take long detours through narrow residential lanes. These alternative routes are ill-equipped to handle the sudden surge in traffic, leading to frequent gridlock and safety risks for local children and the elderly.

Safety Risks and Lack of Oversight

Authorities have not yet provided a firm timeline for the completion of the project. The absence of clear signage or traffic diversion plans suggests a breakdown in project management. Without a dedicated traffic marshal presence, the intersection points have become dangerous zones where vehicles often collide or come to a standstill.

Business owners on Tannery Road state that their footfall has dropped significantly since the work began. Customers are choosing to avoid the area entirely rather than brave the muddy, uneven terrain. Despite the growing public outcry, the situation remains largely unchanged as of this week.

  • Open trenches remain a constant threat to two-wheeler riders.
  • Dust pollution is affecting local businesses and households.
  • Traffic diversion signage remains largely absent or confusing.

The city administration is expected to face mounting pressure to expedite the repairs in the coming days. Observers will be watching to see if officials release a revised project schedule or deploy additional manpower to clear the site before the upcoming monsoon showers worsen the road conditions.
