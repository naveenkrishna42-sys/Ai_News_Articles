Former Congress MP Sajjan Kumar dies at 80

Key points
  • Sajjan Kumar died at the age of 80 while serving a life sentence.
  • He was convicted for his role in the 1984 anti-Sikh riots.

A Life Marked by Legal Controversy

Sajjan Kumar, the former Congress Member of Parliament, has passed away at the age of 80. His death comes while he was serving a life imprisonment sentence handed down by the Delhi High Court in connection with the 1984 anti-Sikh riots.

For years, Kumar remained a central figure in the long-drawn-out legal proceedings concerning the violence that erupted in the national capital. The riots, which followed the assassination of then-Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, claimed thousands of lives and left deep scars on the city’s social fabric.

Conviction and Imprisonment

In 2018, the Delhi High Court overturned a lower court's acquittal of Kumar, sentencing him to life imprisonment. The bench had found him guilty of criminal conspiracy, promoting enmity, and acts against communal harmony.

The court's verdict was a significant moment for survivors and legal activists who had campaigned for accountability for over three decades. Kumar surrendered before a Delhi court in December 2018 to begin his sentence at Tihar Jail, where he remained incarcerated until his health declined.

Reflections on the 1984 Riots

The death of the former MP has reignited memories of the trauma experienced by victims during the November 1984 violence. Survivors have frequently recounted the chaotic scenes that unfolded across various parts of Delhi, describing instances of arson and targeted attacks.

  • The 2018 High Court judgment specifically cited the failure of state machinery during the riots.
  • Legal proceedings against several other accused individuals in related cases continue to be monitored by various judicial bodies.
  • Public discourse surrounding the riots remains sensitive, often highlighting the struggle for justice for the families affected.

While political reactions have been limited, the passing of Kumar brings a final, if controversial, chapter to one of the most significant legal cases in post-independence India. The legal process, which spanned decades, serves as a testament to the persistent efforts of those seeking closure.

Authorities are expected to follow standard legal and medical protocols regarding the transfer of custody following his death. Observers will now monitor whether the conclusion of this specific legal matter influences the trajectory of ongoing cases related to the 1984 riots.
