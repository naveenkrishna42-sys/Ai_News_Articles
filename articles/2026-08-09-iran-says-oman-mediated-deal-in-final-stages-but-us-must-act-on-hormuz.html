Iran says Oman-mediated deal in final stages, but US must act on Hormuz

📅 2026-08-09 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran says Oman-mediated deal is in 'final stages'
  • Tehran demands US action to reopen Strait of Hormuz
  • Talks with US contingent on interim deal compliance

Oman channel nears completion

Iran announced on Sunday that a deal brokered by Oman to ease tensions in the region is in its final stages. The statement comes amid a widening standoff over the Strait of Hormuz, a vital waterway for global oil shipments.

Tehran's remarks signal a cautious opening, but officials have tied any progress to concrete action from Washington. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has bluntly stated that the strait will not reopen unless the US meets Iran's demands.

US action, not words

Iranian authorities have repeatedly stressed that the onus is on the United States to demonstrate goodwill. A senior Iranian minister said talks with Washington will not happen while the interim deal continues to be breached, a reference to previous nuclear-related understandings.

The precise nature of the breaches has not been detailed, but Tehran has framed them as a failure to uphold commitments. US officials have not yet commented publicly on the latest Iranian statements.

Strait of Hormuz at the centre

The Strait of Hormuz remains the flashpoint. Roughly a fifth of global oil consumption passes through these narrow waters, making any disruption a matter of international concern. Iran has in the past threatened to close the strait in response to sanctions or military pressure.

Reports from regional media suggest the conditions for reopening the waterway are complex. The Times of India has outlined a set of demands from both sides, but no official list has been confirmed by either government.

Talks conditional, not imminent

Iran's foreign ministry has clarified that any direct dialogue with the US is contingent on the interim deal being honoured. This conditionality suggests a diplomatic path exists, but it is narrow and fragile.

The Omani initiative, if finalised, could provide a framework for de-escalation. However, analysts caution that the gap between rhetoric and action remains wide, particularly given the history of mistrust between Tehran and Washington.

What to watch next

Diplomatic observers will now watch for a clear US response to Iran's latest overture. Whether Washington moves to address Tehran's stated grievances could determine if the Oman deal becomes a reality or fades into another round of stalemate.
