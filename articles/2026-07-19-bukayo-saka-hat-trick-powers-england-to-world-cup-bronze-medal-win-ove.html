Bukayo Saka hat-trick powers England to World Cup bronze medal win over France

Key points
  • Bukayo Saka scored a hat-trick to lead England to a 6-4 victory over France in the World Cup third-place playoff.
  • The match was a 10-goal thriller, with Kylian Mbappe breaking the all-time World Cup goal record despite France's loss.
  • England secured their first World Cup medal in 60 years, with manager Thomas Tuchel hoping the players can be proud of the achievement.

Bukayo Saka delivered a stunning hat-trick as England defeated France 6-4 in a breathless World Cup third-place playoff on Saturday, securing the bronze medal in the 2026 tournament. The match, played in front of a capacity crowd, was a 10-goal spectacle that saw records tumble and emotions run high.

England manager Thomas Tuchel, who took charge of the national team earlier this year, said he hoped his players could be proud of their achievement. It is England's first medal in a major tournament in 60 years, since they won the 1966 World Cup.

Saka steals the show

Bukayo Saka was the star of the match, scoring three goals in a devastating attacking display. The Arsenal winger tormented the French defence with his pace and finishing, becoming the first English player to score a hat-trick in a World Cup match since Geoff Hurst in the 1966 final.

Saka's first goal came in the 12th minute, a clinical finish from inside the box. He added his second just before half-time, and completed his hat-trick in the 67th minute with a powerful strike that left the French goalkeeper with no chance.

His performance drew praise from fans and pundits alike, with many calling it one of the finest individual displays in a World Cup match.

A 10-goal thriller

The match was an end-to-end affair from the first whistle. Both teams attacked relentlessly, resulting in a combined 10 goals that kept spectators on the edge of their seats. England took the lead early, but France fought back repeatedly, ensuring the result was in doubt until the final minutes.

England's other goals came from a mix of team play and individual brilliance. Jude Bellingham and Harry Kane also found the net, while an own goal from a French defender added to England's tally. France responded through goals from Kylian Mbappe, Antoine Griezmann, and two other scorers.

Mbappe breaks record

Despite the defeat, France's Kylian Mbappe etched his name into World Cup history. The forward scored a goal in the match, taking his all-time World Cup goal tally to a record-breaking number. The previous record was held by Miroslav Klose of Germany.

Mbappe's goal was a moment of individual brilliance, a trademark run and finish that brought the French fans to their feet. However, it was not enough to secure a win for his team, as England's superior finishing proved decisive.

The 24-year-old's achievement adds another chapter to his remarkable career, though he will likely be disappointed with the team's fourth-place finish.

A milestone for England

For England, the bronze medal represents a significant achievement. The team had not reached the podium in a World Cup since their triumph in 1966, and this result provides a boost for Tuchel's project. The German manager has brought a pragmatic approach to the squad, focusing on defensive solidity while allowing attacking players like Saka and Bellingham to flourish.

Tuchel said after the match that he wanted the players to feel proud of their accomplishment. He acknowledged the disappointment of failing to reach the final, but stressed that winning a medal was a major step forward for English football.

Players embraced on the pitch after the final whistle, with Saka receiving a standing ovation from the crowd. The team will now return to England with the bronze medal, a tangible reward for their efforts over the past month.

Both teams will now turn their attention to the future. France will look to rebuild and challenge for the 2030 World Cup, while England will aim to build on this success under Tuchel's guidance.
