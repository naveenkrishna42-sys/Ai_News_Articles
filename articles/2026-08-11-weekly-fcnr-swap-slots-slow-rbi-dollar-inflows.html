Weekly FCNR swap slots slow RBI dollar inflows

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's weekly auction slots for FCNR (B) swaps slow dollar inflows
  • Banks face operational constraints in bidding for swap windows
  • Impact on forex reserves and rupee liquidity management

The Reserve Bank of India's decision to allot weekly slots for FCNR (B) deposit swaps is delaying the central bank's dollar receipts, according to bankers familiar with the matter.

The swap facility, which allows banks to convert foreign currency non-resident (bank) deposits into rupees, has seen slower than expected participation due to the weekly bidding structure. Banks have flagged that the current frequency of auction slots is not aligned with their deposit mobilisation cycles.

How the swap mechanism works

Under the FCNR (B) swap scheme, banks can place their dollar deposits with the RBI and receive rupees at a pre-determined exchange rate. This helps lenders manage their foreign currency liabilities while the central bank adds to its dollar reserves.

However, the RBI's practice of opening swap windows only on specific days of the week has meant that banks must wait for the next available slot to execute transactions. This lag is particularly pronounced when deposit inflows peak mid-week and the next auction is several days away.

Bankers cite operational mismatch

Treasury officials at two large private sector banks said the weekly slot system creates a bottleneck. "If we receive a large FCNR deposit on a Tuesday and the swap window opens only on Thursday, the funds remain idle in nostro accounts," one treasurer said. This idle period also exposes banks to currency fluctuation risks.

Some banks have resorted to hedging in the forward market to cover the gap, but that adds to costs. Smaller lenders, which rely more heavily on the swap route to manage their FCNR books, are finding it particularly cumbersome.

Impact on RBI's dollar accretion

The delayed participation means the RBI's dollar receipts from these swaps are trickling in rather than arriving in a steady stream. This has implications for the central bank's forex reserve management, especially when it is looking to build buffers against external shocks.

Bankers point out that the weekly slot system was introduced to bring predictability to the central bank's operations. But the current design has inadvertently slowed the pace of conversions. The RBI has not yet commented on whether it plans to review the frequency of these auctions.

Rupee liquidity and market signals

The timing of dollar inflows also affects rupee liquidity in the banking system. When swaps are executed, the RBI injects rupees into the system; when they mature, it absorbs them. A staggered schedule can lead to uneven liquidity conditions, making it harder for banks to plan their short-term funding.

Market participants are watching whether the central bank will shift to a more flexible auction calendar, perhaps offering daily windows or allowing banks to book swaps in advance. Any such move would likely be welcomed by the banking community.

For now, the RBI has not indicated any change to the weekly slot mechanism. Bankers say they are making do with the current system, but the friction is evident in the slower pace of dollar conversions.

Going forward, the central bank's stance on swap frequency will be a key indicator of its comfort with the current pace of reserve accretion. If dollar inflows remain lumpy, the RBI may have to recalibrate its approach to ensure smoother reserve management.
