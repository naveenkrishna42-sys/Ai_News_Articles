Bengaluru Traffic Police proposes regulated carpooling to ease congestion

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru Traffic Police proposes regulated carpooling to reduce congestion.
  • System would require registration with traffic police and compliance with guidelines.
  • Proposal aims to improve air quality and reduce vehicle count on roads.

Bengaluru Traffic Police has proposed a regulated carpooling system to tackle the city's worsening traffic congestion. The move aims to reduce the number of private vehicles on the road during peak hours. Officials believe a structured approach can make carpooling safer and more effective.

What the proposal entails

The proposed system would require carpool users to register with the traffic police. This registration would include vehicle details and passenger information. The police would then monitor compliance through a digital platform.

Guidelines under consideration include limits on the number of passengers and routes. The goal is to prevent misuse and ensure that carpooling does not become a commercial transport service. Officials are also studying how to integrate this with existing ride-hailing apps.

Addressing congestion and pollution

Bengaluru consistently ranks among the most congested cities in India. The traffic police say regulated carpooling could reduce vehicle numbers by 15-20% during rush hours. This would also help lower vehicular emissions, which contribute to the city's air pollution.

The proposal comes after several pilot projects by private companies showed mixed results. The police believe a government-backed system would have better enforcement and public trust. However, they have not yet announced a timeline for implementation.

Challenges ahead

Implementing a regulated carpooling system will not be easy. Bengaluru's traffic is chaotic, and many commuters prefer driving alone for convenience. The police will need to address safety concerns, especially for women passengers.

Another hurdle is the lack of infrastructure for dedicated carpool lanes. Without such lanes, carpool vehicles will still get stuck in the same traffic jams. The proposal will also require coordination with the transport department and the city's civic body.

The police are now inviting feedback from the public and stakeholders. A final plan is expected after consultations with experts and citizens. The state government's support will be crucial for any rollout.

What happens next? The traffic police will hold a series of meetings with commuter groups and transport operators. A detailed framework could be ready in the next few months. If approved, Bengaluru could become one of the first Indian cities to enforce regulated carpooling.
