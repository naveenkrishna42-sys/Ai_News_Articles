Jharkhand Ministers Meet Protesting Students; Agitation to Continue

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Jharkhand ministers met protesting students for the first time since the JPSC agitation began.
  • The government said it will consider the students' demands, but no concrete resolution was announced.
  • Protesters have vowed to continue the agitation until their demands are met.
  • The talks were described as inconclusive by multiple sources.

Ranchi: Fourteen days into the protest, Jharkhand ministers finally sat down with the agitating students on Friday. The meeting, described as a first step by both sides, ended without a breakthrough. The agitation, led by Jharkhand Public Service Commission (JPSC) aspirants, will continue.

A Long-Awaited Dialogue

The talks took place at the state secretariat, where a delegation of protesting students met with senior ministers. The government side said it would "consider" the demands raised by the aspirants. However, no formal assurance was given on the core issues that have brought thousands to the streets.

Students have been demanding changes to the JPSC exam pattern, including a revision of the syllabus and a re-evaluation of the recruitment process. They argue that the current system is flawed and puts candidates from certain backgrounds at a disadvantage.

No Breakthrough, No Surrender

Multiple reports from the meeting suggest that the discussions were cordial but inconclusive. The ministers listened to the students' grievances but stopped short of committing to any specific action. The protesters, for their part, made it clear that they would not call off the agitation until their demands were met in writing.

"The government has promised to look into our demands," one of the student leaders said after the meeting, speaking on condition of anonymity. "But we have heard such promises before. We need action, not words."

What the Students Want

  • Revision of the JPSC syllabus to align with current academic standards.
  • A transparent and merit-based selection process.
  • Immediate release of pending exam results.
  • Formation of a committee with student representation to oversee reforms.

The protest, which began as a small sit-in, has now swelled into a major movement, drawing support from various student organisations and political groups. The demonstrators have been camping at a central location in Ranchi, braving monsoon rains and police barricades.

Government's Stance

Officials familiar with the development said the government is keen to resolve the impasse but is treading carefully. "We are open to dialogue, but we cannot accept demands that go against the rules of the commission," a senior official said, requesting anonymity. The ministers have reportedly asked the students to submit a written charter of demands, which will be examined by a sub-committee.

No fresh date for the next round of talks has been announced. The students, however, have indicated they are willing to meet again if the government shows sincerity in addressing their concerns.

What's Next

The coming days will be crucial. The students have announced plans to intensify their protest if no progress is made by next week. The government, on its part, faces the challenge of balancing the aspirants' demands with administrative feasibility. All eyes are now on the promised written response from the state. Until then, the sit-in at Ranchi continues.
