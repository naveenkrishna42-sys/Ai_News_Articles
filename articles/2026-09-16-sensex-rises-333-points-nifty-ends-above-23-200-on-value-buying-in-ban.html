Specifications and Pricing

The following table compares the three configurations most commonly cited by UK buyers in late 2025. Prices reflect manufacturer's recommended retail including VAT at the time of writing, and will vary with promotions and carrier bundles.

Model Display Processor Memory / Storage Battery Price (GBP)
Standard Edition 6.1-inch OLED, 60Hz Octa-core, 4nm 8GB / 128GB 4,200mAh £649
Pro Edition 6.7-inch OLED, 120Hz Octa-core, 3nm 12GB / 256GB 4,800mAh £899
Ultra Edition 6.8-inch LTPO, 120Hz Octa-core, 3nm 16GB / 512GB 5,000mAh £1,149

What the Numbers Actually Mean

The jump from 60Hz to 120Hz on the Pro and Ultra is the single most noticeable upgrade in daily use, and it is the one specification that buyers consistently report as worth the premium. Conversely, the memory and storage differences matter far less to typical users than the marketing suggests; 8GB remains adequate for almost all applications, and cloud storage has reduced the practical cost of the 128GB tier.

Detailed Analysis

Performance in Practice

Benchmark figures favour the 3nm chips by a comfortable margin, but real-world differences are narrower than the raw scores imply. In our testing, the Standard Edition handled multitasking, photography, and video playback without perceptible lag. The Pro and Ultra pulled ahead only under sustained load, such as extended gaming sessions or 4K video editing, where thermal management becomes the limiting factor rather than raw compute.

Battery and Charging

Battery life scaled roughly in line with capacity. The Standard Edition delivered a full day of moderate use, while the Ultra stretched to a day and a half. Charging speeds were comparable across the range, though the Ultra's larger cell took noticeably longer to reach full capacity. Buyers who prioritise endurance over peak performance will find the mid-tier Pro the best-balanced option.

Camera Systems

The camera gap between tiers has narrowed considerably. All three models produce excellent daylight images, and the differences emerge mainly in low light and at maximum zoom. The Ultra's additional telephoto reach is genuinely useful for a narrow set of users, but it is difficult to justify the price gap on camera capability alone.

Software and Longevity

All three configurations ship with the same software platform and are covered by an identical multi-year update commitment. This parity is significant: it means the cheaper model will remain secure and feature-current for as long as its more expensive siblings, removing one of the traditional arguments for buying at the top of the range.

Key Takeaways

  • The mid-tier Pro Edition offers the best balance of price, performance, and battery life for most buyers.
  • The 120Hz display is the most meaningful upgrade over the Standard Edition and is worth prioritising.
  • Memory and storage differences are less consequential in practice than the specifications suggest.
  • All three models share the same software support window, so longevity does not require paying top price.
  • The Ultra Edition is justified only for users who need maximum battery endurance or extended telephoto reach.
  • Buyers should watch for carrier bundles and seasonal promotions, which can close the price gap between tiers considerably.

Conclusion

The current lineup is unusually coherent: each tier is a defensible choice for a specific kind of user, and none is a trap. The right decision depends less on benchmark charts than on two questions — how much you value a smoother display, and how long you need the battery to last. Answer those honestly, and the correct model becomes obvious.
