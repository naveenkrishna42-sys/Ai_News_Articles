North West India Witnesses Rare Solar Eclipse

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • North West India witnessed a rare solar eclipse on Wednesday.
  • Schools and science clubs organized viewing events with safe equipment.
  • Astronomy enthusiasts highlighted the importance of using proper eye protection.

A Celestial Spectacle

Skywatchers across North West India turned their eyes skyward on Wednesday as a rare solar eclipse unfolded, casting a surreal twilight over the region. From bustling city terraces to quiet village fields, people gathered in groups, armed with special glasses and pinhole projectors, to catch a glimpse of the celestial phenomenon.

The eclipse, which began mid-morning and peaked around noon, saw the moon gradually slide in front of the sun, blocking out a significant portion of its light. In some areas, the sky dimmed noticeably, and temperatures dipped slightly, adding to the eerie atmosphere.

Community Viewing Events

Schools, colleges, and amateur astronomy clubs across the region organised public viewing sessions to ensure safe observation. In Jaipur, a local science centre set up telescopes with solar filters, drawing hundreds of eager visitors, including families with young children. Similar events were reported in Chandigarh, Amritsar, and smaller towns like Hisar and Bikaner.

Teachers used the occasion as a live lesson in astronomy, explaining the mechanics of eclipses to students. "It's not every day that you get to see something like this," said a science teacher at a government school in Jodhpur, who had arranged for her students to observe the eclipse using cardboard boxes and mirrors.

Safety First, Always

Astronomy enthusiasts and health officials alike stressed the importance of never looking directly at the sun without proper protection. Regular sunglasses, they warned, are not sufficient. Only certified eclipse glasses or solar filters, which block out harmful ultraviolet and infrared rays, should be used.

Despite the warnings, some people were spotted using improvised methods, such as welding masks or exposed photographic film. Experts cautioned that these can still pose a risk to eyesight, urging everyone to follow recommended guidelines.

A Shared Moment of Wonder

The eclipse also sparked a flurry of activity on social media, with users across the region posting photos and videos of the event. Many expressed awe at the natural spectacle, while others shared tips on how to capture the eclipse safely with smartphone cameras.

For many, the event was more than just a scientific curiosity. It was a moment of collective wonder, a reminder of the vastness of the universe and our small place within it. As one observer in Udaipur put it, "It makes you feel connected to something bigger than yourself."

What's Next?

While this eclipse has passed, astronomers note that another partial solar eclipse will be visible from parts of India in the coming years. For those who missed this one, or want to relive the experience, local science museums and planetariums are expected to host follow-up sessions and exhibitions on solar phenomena.

As the moon slowly retreated, revealing the sun's full brilliance once again, the crowds dispersed, carrying with them memories of a rare and beautiful event. For now, the skies have returned to normal, but the excitement lingers—and the next opportunity to witness such a spectacle may be closer than we think.
