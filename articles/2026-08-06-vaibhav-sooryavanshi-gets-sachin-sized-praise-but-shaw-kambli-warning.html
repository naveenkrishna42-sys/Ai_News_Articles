Vaibhav Sooryavanshi Gets Sachin-Sized Praise, But Shaw-Kambli Warning Follows

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vaibhav Sooryavanshi has been touted to have a bigger impact than Sachin Tendulkar after his international debut.
  • Robin Uthappa cited Vinod Kambli and Prithvi Shaw as cautionary tales of wasted talent.
  • Ajinkya Rahane advised the youngster that international cricket is not the IPL.
  • The hype around Sooryavanshi has triggered warnings about distraction and complacency.

The hype machine has already shifted into overdrive for Vaibhav Sooryavanshi. After a debut that has set the cricketing world abuzz, the young batter is being compared to the greatest India has ever produced. One former cricketer has gone so far as to suggest his impact could eclipse that of Sachin Tendulkar.

But with that soaring praise comes a chorus of caution. Several veterans are pulling the brakes, pointing to the cautionary tales of Vinod Kambli and Prithvi Shaw — two immensely gifted cricketers whose careers derailed before they could fulfil their enormous promise.

The burden of the Tendulkar comparison

It is not every day that an Indian cricketer is mentioned in the same breath as Tendulkar. Yet, Sooryavanshi's early performances have drawn exactly that kind of reaction. The sheer audacity of his strokeplay and the composure he has shown at the crease have prompted bold predictions about his future.

One former player has claimed that Sooryavanshi's influence on Indian cricket could be bigger than Tendulkar's. That is a statement loaded with expectation, and one that could weigh heavily on a player who has just begun his journey at the highest level.

Tendulkar carried the hopes of a billion for over two decades. Comparisons of this magnitude, while flattering, can also become a psychological burden that crushes even the most talented of careers.

Rahane's reality check

Ajinkya Rahane, who has seen the highs and lows of international cricket, has offered a brotherly word of advice. He has told Sooryavanshi, in no uncertain terms, that international cricket is not the IPL.

Rahane's message is simple: the IPL is a platform where reputations are built, but the Test arena demands a different kind of temperament. The white-ball extravaganza rewards aggression, while the longer format punishes recklessness. Sooryavanshi will need to adapt his game if he is to succeed across all formats.

Rahane's words carry weight. He has been through the grind, been dropped, and fought his way back. His advice is not about technique alone; it is about mindset and the ability to handle failure at the highest level.

The Shaw-Kambli warning

Robin Uthappa has been more direct. He has invoked the names of Vinod Kambli and Prithvi Shaw, two batters who promised so much but delivered so little in the long run.

Kambli was a prodigy who once outshone Tendulkar in their youth. Shaw was a U-19 World Cup-winning captain who was tipped for greatness. Both had the talent, but their careers were derailed by off-field distractions and a failure to adapt.

Uthappa's warning is stark: talent alone is not enough. The transition from domestic cricket to the international stage is brutal, and those who do not treat it with the seriousness it demands will be left behind. The message to Sooryavanshi is clear — do not let success get to your head.

The distraction of fame

Another veteran has added a different dimension to the warning, pointing out that Sooryavanshi will now have to deal with 'female attention' and the trappings of stardom that come with international recognition. It is a crude but real reminder of the pressures that young cricketers face.

Social media, endorsement offers, and public adulation can easily derail a young player's focus. The cricketing graveyard is full of players who could not handle the fame that came with early success. Sooryavanshi will need a strong support system to navigate this treacherous terrain.

The Indian team management and senior players will have a role to play in keeping him grounded. The board's support staff will also need to monitor his workload and mental health as he adjusts to the demands of international cricket.

What lies ahead

Sooryavanshi's talent is undeniable, and the praise he is receiving is a testament to his potential. But potential is a fragile thing; it must be nurtured with patience and discipline. The road ahead is long, and every innings will be scrutinised.

The coming months will reveal whether he can handle the pressure of being India's next big thing. For now, the cricketing fraternity watches with bated breath, hoping that this prodigy does not become another cautionary tale.
