Crypto platform Knaken bankrupt, €7 crore in customer funds missing

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Crypto trading platform Knaken has been declared bankrupt by a Dutch court.
  • Approximately €7 million (₹62 crore) in customer funds are missing or unaccounted for.
  • The bankruptcy highlights ongoing risks in the cryptocurrency exchange sector.

Dutch cryptocurrency trading platform Knaken has been declared bankrupt, leaving a trail of missing customer funds worth €7 million (approximately ₹62 crore). The court ruling has sent shockwaves through the crypto community and raised fresh concerns about the safety of assets held on such exchanges.

The platform, which was registered in the Netherlands, had been under scrutiny for several weeks before the bankruptcy order was issued. According to court documents, a significant portion of customer deposits — around €7 million — cannot be located, prompting investigators to dig deeper into the company's financial records.

What led to the collapse

Knaken had been operational for over three years and had built a modest but loyal user base in Europe. However, a liquidity crisis began to emerge earlier this year when several large withdrawal requests could not be fulfilled.

Customers reported delays of weeks in processing withdrawals. Social media channels were flooded with complaints of frozen accounts and unresponsive support teams. The company's management had cited technical glitches and a surge in trading volume as reasons for the delays, but the problems only worsened.

An internal audit reportedly revealed discrepancies between the funds held in the exchange's wallets and the liabilities it owed to users. That mismatch eventually led to the bankruptcy filing.

Missing funds raise alarm

The €7 million gap is the most troubling part of this case. Investigators are now trying to trace where the money went — whether it was lost in bad trades, stolen by insiders, or siphoned off through opaque transactions.

Dutch authorities have not yet made any arrests. Officials have confirmed that the company's directors are cooperating with the probe, but no clear explanation for the missing funds has emerged so far. Users who had money on the platform are now left in limbo, unsure if they will ever see their investments again.

This is not an isolated incident. Crypto exchanges across the globe have faced similar crises — from the collapse of FTX in 2022 to smaller platforms vanishing overnight. The Knaken case underscores a pattern: when user funds are not held in segregated accounts or insured, they become vulnerable to mismanagement or outright theft.

What happens to customers now

Under Dutch insolvency law, affected customers are treated as unsecured creditors. That means they stand in line behind secured creditors, tax authorities, and administrative costs. Recovering even a fraction of the lost money could take years.

Legal experts say the chances of full recovery are slim. Some customers have already formed a group to explore collective legal action, but the costs and complexity of cross-border litigation may prove prohibitive for many retail investors.

The case also raises questions about regulatory oversight. Knaken was registered with the Dutch central bank, but registration does not amount to full supervision. Critics argue that current regulations are too lax to protect consumers from such failures.

Authorities in the Netherlands are now reviewing whether to tighten rules for crypto custodians. Similar discussions are underway in other European capitals, as regulators grapple with the rapid growth of digital assets. The European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation (MiCA) is expected to bring more stringent requirements later this year, but for Knaken's customers, that help comes too late.

What to watch: The investigation into Knaken's missing funds will determine whether criminal charges are filed. Customers are advised to monitor official announcements from the court-appointed trustee, who will manage the liquidation process.
