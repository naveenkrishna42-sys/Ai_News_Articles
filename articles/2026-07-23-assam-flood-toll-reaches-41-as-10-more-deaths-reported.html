Assam flood toll reaches 41 as 10 more deaths reported

📅 2026-07-23 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • 10 more deaths raise Assam flood toll to 41
  • 872 villages submerged across the state
  • Congress MP Gaurav Gogoi alleges severe delay in relief delivery
  • IMD debunks cloudburst claims, points to infrastructure gaps

The flood situation in Assam continues to worsen, with 10 more deaths reported in the last 24 hours, pushing the official toll to 41. The deluge has submerged 872 villages, leaving thousands homeless and dependent on relief camps.

Relief efforts under scrutiny

Congress MP Gaurav Gogoi has alleged a severe delay in the delivery of relief materials to flood-affected areas. He stated that many affected families have not received essential supplies even days after the waters rose.

Government officials, however, maintain that rescue operations are ongoing and that relief distribution is being scaled up. The National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and state disaster response teams have been deployed in the worst-hit districts.

Weather alerts and landslide risks

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued red, orange, and yellow rain alerts for several states, including Assam. The heavy rainfall has not only triggered floods but also worsened the Himalayan landslide crisis in neighboring regions.

Down To Earth reported that entire villages have gone underwater, with residents describing the sudden onset of flooding as devastating. Many have lost their homes, livestock, and means of livelihood.

IMD debunks cloudburst claims

The IMD has officially debunked claims that cloudbursts were responsible for the recent flooding. Instead, the meteorological agency has pointed to sustained heavy rainfall over several days as the primary cause.

The Assam Tribune reported that the IMD's statement has shifted the focus to infrastructure gaps. Experts argue that inadequate drainage systems, encroachments on floodplains, and poor river management have exacerbated the crisis.

Political fallout

The flood situation has become a political flashpoint. Opposition leaders, including Gaurav Gogoi, have demanded a thorough review of the state's disaster preparedness. They accuse the government of being slow to respond.

The ruling party has dismissed these allegations, stating that all available resources are being mobilized. Officials have urged citizens to cooperate with evacuation orders and avoid venturing into flooded areas.

As the monsoon season continues, authorities are bracing for more rain and potential landslides. The next 48 hours are critical as water levels in major rivers remain above the danger mark.
