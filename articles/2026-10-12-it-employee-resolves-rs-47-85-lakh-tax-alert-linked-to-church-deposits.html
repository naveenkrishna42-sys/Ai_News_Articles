PAN-Linked Deposits Spark Tax Scrutiny

An IT employee in Hyderabad received an income tax alert after his Permanent Account Number (PAN) was linked to deposits amounting to Rs 47.85 lakh made into a local church’s savings account. The bank had flagged the transactions under the Reserve Bank of India’s know-your-customer (KYC) norms, triggering an automated tax intelligence unit (TIA) alert.

The deposits were made over six months by multiple donors during a fundraising campaign for a community kitchen project. However, because the employee had previously assisted the church with digital payment integrations, his PAN was inadvertently captured in the transaction metadata.

Why PAN Linkage Matters

Under India’s tax framework, any cash or cheque deposit exceeding Rs 10 lakh in a financial year is reported to the I-T Department via Form 26AS. When a PAN appears in such high-value transactions, the system assumes potential tax liability unless proven otherwise.

Resolution Through Documentation

The employee responded to the tax notice by submitting audited financial statements of the church, itemized donation receipts, and a sworn affidavit clarifying his non-financial role. He also provided audited IT returns showing no personal benefit from the deposits.

Within 45 days, the I-T officer closed the case, citing insufficient evidence of taxable income. The incident underscores how easily legitimate contributions can trigger false positives without clear records.

Key Takeaways for Taxpayers

Linking Aadhaar with PAN reduces identity mismatches, but individuals involved in third-party financial activities must maintain robust documentation. Banks and taxpayers alike should ensure transparent audit trails for all high-value transactions.

Broader Implications for Donors and NGOs

This case sets a precedent for religious institutions and non-profits receiving large donations digitally. Experts recommend maintaining separate PANs for organizational accounts and digitizing donor records to prevent future confusion.

For individuals, especially those working in finance or tech roles for NGOs, it’s critical to avoid being named on accounts unless directly involved. A simple mismatch can lead to months of scrutiny and stress.

  • Proactive Record-Keeping: Keep dated receipts and audited statements for all third-party financial involvement.
  • PAN Hygiene: Avoid unnecessary association with high-volume accounts not under your direct control.
  • Transparency Tools: Use digital ledgers and cloud-based accounting to support audit trails.
  • Legal Safeguards: File affidavits early when responding to tax notices to expedite resolution.

Lessons for Financial Institutions

Banks must refine their anomaly detection systems to distinguish between donors and facilitators. Poor tagging during KYC processes can wrongly implicate innocent parties, creating avoidable friction in India’s growing digital economy.
