Viral Unboxing Fuels Speculation Ahead of September 9 Event

Social media is abuzz with a leaked iPhone Fold unboxing video that has captured global attention just hours before Apple formally announces its first foldable device at the 'Surprise and Shine' event. The 90-second clip, shared across TikTok and Twitter, showcases the device's hinge mechanism, dual-screen setup, and packaging design, reigniting speculation about Apple's late entry into the foldable smartphone market. Unlike Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold series, which has dominated since 2019, this unboxing suggests Apple is prioritizing novelty over refinement.

Face ID Omission Confirmed by Supply Chain Sources

Multiple reports from credible analysts, including Ming-Chi Kuo, confirm that the upcoming iPhone Fold will notably exclude Face ID facial recognition technology. The decision stems from engineering challenges integrating sensors within the ultra-thin foldable display architecture. Instead, Apple is expected to rely on a traditional front-facing camera with software-based biometric authentication, potentially compromising security standards users associate with flagship iPhones. This omission places the device at a competitive disadvantage against Samsung's under-display fingerprint scanner and ultrasonic sensors already in Galaxy Fold models.

iPhone 18 Pro Features Missing in Foldable Variant

Leaked renderings and early firmware scans reveal the iPhone Fold will lack several premium features anticipated for the iPhone 18 Pro lineup, including periscope telephoto cameras and titanium-grade durability coatings. The device's camera bar design appears to use a single 48MP main sensor with basic computational photography, falling short of the 5x optical zoom and 48MP ultrawide capabilities confirmed in Pro models. Battery life is also expected to trail, with a 3,800mAh cell offering just 12 hours of mixed usage compared to the 15-hour endurance of the iPhone 18 Pro Max.

Pricing Context and Market Implications

Industry estimates suggest the iPhone Fold will launch at ₹1.35 lakh (approximately $1,600) for the base 256GB model, positioning it above Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 6 but below the hypothetical 'iPhone Ultra' tier. This pricing gap leaves room for Apple to introduce a premium variant later in the year, likely named 'iPhone Ultra' or 'iPhone Fold Pro,' targeting enterprise and power users. Analysts warn that a limited initial rollout—potentially restricted to select markets like the US and China—will cede early-mover advantage to Samsung, which has built a robust ecosystem for foldable apps and accessories since 2019.

Key Real-World Implications for Buyers

  • Compromised Security: Lack of Face ID or under-display fingerprint tech raises privacy concerns compared to Samsung's ultrasonic sensors.
  • Feature Trade-offs: Missing Pro-grade cameras and titanium build may disappoint power users expecting flagship parity.
  • Premium Pricing Without Premium Specs: ₹1.35 lakh entry price lacks justifiable value against similarly priced foldables with superior hardware.
  • Samsung's Early Edge: Limited availability and truncated features give Android's Galaxy Z series a clear advantage in 2026.
