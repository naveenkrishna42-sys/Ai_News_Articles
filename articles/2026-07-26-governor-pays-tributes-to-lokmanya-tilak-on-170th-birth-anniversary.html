Governor pays tributes to Lokmanya Tilak on 170th birth anniversary

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Governor paid tributes to Lokmanya Tilak on his 170th birth anniversary
  • Ceremony held at Raj Bhavan, Mumbai
  • Tilak remembered as a key figure in India's independence movement

Governor pays homage to Tilak

The Maharashtra Governor on Thursday paid tributes to Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak on his 170th birth anniversary. The ceremony took place at Raj Bhavan in Mumbai.

Officials from Raj Bhavan said the Governor offered floral tributes to the portrait of Tilak. The event was a simple but solemn affair, reflecting the respect for the leader often called the 'Father of Indian Unrest'.

Tilak's enduring legacy

Lokmanya Tilak was born on July 23, 1856. He was a scholar, mathematician, and a towering figure in the Indian independence movement. He popularised the Ganesh Chaturthi festival as a public event to unite people against British rule.

Tilak's famous slogan, 'Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it,' continues to inspire generations. He was also a journalist who used newspapers like Kesari and Maratha to spread nationalist ideas.

Raj Bhavan ceremony details

The event at Raj Bhavan was attended by a small group of dignitaries. The Governor was seen paying his respects with folded hands, standing before Tilak's framed image decorated with garlands.

No other prominent political leaders were reported present at the ceremony. The Raj Bhavan has not released a detailed statement beyond the official announcement.

A day of remembrance

Various organisations across Maharashtra also observed the birth anniversary with events. Schools and colleges held lectures and competitions on Tilak's life. Local political parties issued statements praising his contributions.

Tilak was a founding member of the Indian National Congress and later a leader of the extremist faction. He spent six years in Mandalay prison for sedition charges, a period that cemented his status as a martyr for freedom.

His birth anniversary comes a month after the state government announced new initiatives to preserve heritage sites linked to him. Details of those projects are still awaited.

The Governor's tribute underscores the continued reverence for Tilak in Maharashtra's political and cultural life. His legacy remains a touchstone for debates on nationalism and education.

No further events are scheduled at Raj Bhavan for this anniversary. The state government is expected to hold a larger commemoration next year for the 150th birth anniversary of another freedom fighter.
