UEFA members vote to boycott FIFA over Infantino's World Cup plans

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • UEFA and its 55 national associations voted to boycott FIFA if World Cup plans proceed
  • FIFA President Gianni Infantino is pushing to sell stakes in the World Cup to private equity
  • UEFA official Laura McAllister accused FIFA of 'blackmail' over the proposal
  • The threat marks an escalation in the dispute over FIFA's financial restructuring plans

European football's governing body UEFA has taken a hardline stance against FIFA. Its 55 member associations voted unanimously to boycott the world governing body if FIFA President Gianni Infantino pushes ahead with plans to sell stakes in the World Cup to private investors.

Unanimous vote against FIFA's proposal

The decision was announced in a joint statement from UEFA and its national associations on Thursday. The statement did not mince words. It said the member associations are prepared to stay away from any FIFA competition if the plan goes through.

The vote took place during a UEFA meeting in Switzerland. Officials described the atmosphere as tense. The proposal under dispute would allow private equity firms to buy stakes in future World Cup tournaments.

Accusations of blackmail

Laura McAllister, a UEFA executive committee member, did not hold back. She accused FIFA of using 'blackmail' to push the plan through. Speaking to the BBC, McAllister said the approach from Zurich has been aggressive.

She claimed FIFA has tried to pressure European associations into accepting the deal. McAllister said the move would fundamentally change how football's flagship tournament is run. She argued it would hand control of the sport's most valuable asset to outside investors.

What Infantino wants

Gianni Infantino has been exploring ways to unlock more revenue from the World Cup. Reports in The Times and CNBC indicate he wants to sell minority stakes in the tournament to private investment funds. The idea is to raise immediate cash for FIFA's development projects.

But critics say the plan would compromise the integrity of the competition. They argue that selling stakes to profit-driven investors could lead to commercialisation at the expense of the sport. FIFA has not yet responded to the boycott threat.

What a boycott would mean

If the boycott is carried out, it would be unprecedented. European teams form the backbone of the World Cup. Without them, the tournament would lose its biggest stars, its largest television audiences, and a significant portion of its revenue.

The 55 UEFA associations include heavyweights like England, Germany, France, Spain and Italy. Their absence would effectively cripple any World Cup. The move would also deepen the rift between FIFA and European football bodies, a divide that has been growing for years.

UEFA has not specified what form the boycott would take. It could mean national teams refusing to play in World Cup qualifiers or tournaments. It could also extend to clubs refusing to release players for international duty.

What happens next

The ball is now in FIFA's court. Infantino must decide whether to push ahead with the plan or back down. UEFA has made its position clear. The next few months will determine whether the world's most popular sport faces its biggest institutional crisis in decades.
