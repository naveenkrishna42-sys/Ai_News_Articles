Supreme Court Clears Late Ex-PM Manmohan Singh in Coal Block Case

📅 2026-07-29 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court set aside a 2015 trial court order summoning former PM Manmohan Singh in a coal block allocation case.
  • The court accepted the CBI closure report filed in 2015, clearing Singh of any criminal conspiracy or wrongdoing.
  • The case pertains to alleged irregularities in allocation of the Talabira-II coal block in Odisha.

The Supreme Court on Wednesday quashed a trial court's 2015 order that summoned former Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh in connection with a coal block allocation case. The verdict, delivered two years after the former premier's death, effectively grants him a clean chit in the matter.

A bench of Justices [Names not provided] accepted the Central Bureau of Investigation's closure report, which had concluded that there was no evidence to proceed against Dr. Singh or any other accused. The court noted that the trial court's summons order was based on a flawed appreciation of facts and law.

Case Background

The case relates to the allocation of the Talabira-II coal block in Odisha to Hindalco Industries in 2005. A 2014 CBI charge sheet had alleged irregularities in the allocation process, naming Dr. Singh along with former coal secretary P.C. Parakh and industrialist Kumar Mangalam Birla.

In 2015, a special CBI court had summoned Dr. Singh as an accused, citing alleged criminal conspiracy and breach of trust. However, the CBI had simultaneously filed a closure report stating there was no case against the former Prime Minister, creating a legal contradiction that the Supreme Court has now resolved.

Court's Reasoning

The apex court held that the trial court's order suffered from a 'patent error' and could not be sustained. It observed that the allocation of coal blocks was a policy decision, and no criminal intent could be attributed to Dr. Singh solely because he was the Prime Minister and head of the screening committee.

Senior advocates representing the CBI argued that the closure report was filed after a thorough investigation that found no evidence of 'bias, corruption, or criminal conspiracy' on Dr. Singh's part. The court agreed, noting that the material on record did not support the trial court's view.

Reactions and Significance

The judgment comes as a significant vindication of Dr. Singh's legacy as Prime Minister. The coal block allocation case, popularly known as the 'Coalgate' scam, had been a major political controversy during his second term, with opposition parties alleging a massive loss to the exchequer.

Legal experts point out that the Supreme Court's decision reaffirms that policy decisions taken by the cabinet or empowered committees cannot be criminalised without concrete evidence of corruption. The ruling also highlights the finality of a closure report when supported by investigation findings.

The Congress party welcomed the verdict, stating that it had always maintained Dr. Singh's innocence. Party leaders said the clean chit, though posthumous, restores the integrity of a man known for his impeccable personal conduct.

The trial court's summons had been challenged by Dr. Singh in 2015, and the matter had been pending in the Supreme Court since then, with hearings continuing even after his death in 2024.

With the apex court's order, the legal chapter on Dr. Singh's role in coal block allocations is now closed. The CBI's closure report stands accepted, and no further proceedings are pending against the late former Prime Minister in this matter.
