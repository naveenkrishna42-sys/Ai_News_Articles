Why Xi Jinping Skipped PM Modi's BRICS Dinner: Full Analysis

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-13 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Xi Jinping skipped the BRICS gala dinner hosted by PM Modi, with reporting pointing to an awkward moment during Modi's speech.
  • The snub unfolded alongside Xi's calls for an 'open ecosystem' in AI and his criticism of 'might-makes-right' logic.
  • Modi reiterated that peace and tranquillity on the border remain the foundation for normal India-China relations.

The BRICS summit's first day ended with a diplomatic puzzle: Chinese President Xi Jinping did not attend the gala dinner hosted by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, even as both leaders struck sharply different notes from the same stage. Multiple outlets, including NDTV and Times of India, reported that Xi appeared uncomfortable during Modi's address, with the Prime Minister briefly pausing mid-speech. Xi then skipped the dinner, an unusual move at a summit where informal meals often do the real diplomatic work.

What Actually Happened on Day 1

Modi used his plenary remarks to anchor India-China ties to border peace, calling tranquillity along the Line of Actual Control essential for normal relations. Xi, in his own address, denounced what he termed "might-makes-right logic" and pitched an "open ecosystem" for artificial intelligence, positioning China as a rules-based global actor. The Hindu reported the exchange as a defining moment of the summit's opening day.

The dinner snub matters because multilateral summits are choreographed to the minute. A leader's absence from a host's gala dinner is read by foreign ministries as a deliberate signal, not a scheduling accident.

The Protocol Angle: Why Dinner Skipping Is a Signal

At BRICS and similar forums, the leaders' retreat and gala dinner are where unscripted conversations happen. Skipping them reduces the chance of bilateral contact and denies the host a photo-op that signals unity. For India, which hosted the evening, the absence undercuts the optics of consensus.

Analysts tracking India-China relations will watch three things next: whether a bilateral meeting is scheduled, whether the joint declaration softens language on border de-escalation, and whether military-level talks resume on the same calendar as before.

Real-World Implications for India

For Indian businesses with China exposure, the signal is caution rather than rupture. Trade flows, supply chains and border trade depend on stable political channels. A visible snub raises the risk premium on bilateral clearances, from visas to customs inspections.

  • Pro: India's public framing on border peace keeps the onus on Beijing without escalating rhetoric.
  • Pro: Xi's AI and anti-hegemony pitch gives India room to push for technology governance rules in BRICS.
  • Con: A skipped dinner reduces chances of back-channel de-escalation at a critical juncture.
  • Con: Optics of disunity can dilute BRICS' collective bargaining power on trade and payment systems.

What to Watch Next

Watch for the joint declaration's language on terrorism, border management and AI governance. A bilateral readout from either side, or its absence, will confirm whether the dinner snub was tactical or structural. For buyers and investors, the practical benchmark remains the same: border stability first, commercial normalisation second.
