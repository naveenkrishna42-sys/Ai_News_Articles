PM Modi Says AI Can't Replace Skilled Workers: What It Means for ITIs

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-22 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi told skill-trainee graduates in Lucknow that AI substitutes repetitive tasks, not certified trades with physical or compliance components
  • Trades such as electrical, welding, HVAC, CNC operation and healthcare assistance remain protected; screen-based entry-level work is the exposed end
  • The Prime Minister argued IITs alone cannot build Viksit Bharat, pushing the policy focus onto ITIs and PMKVY-certified technicians

PM Modi in Lucknow: The Core Message

Prime Minister Narendra Modi addressed the 5th Kaushal Deekshant Samaroh in Lucknow, a convocation-style ceremony for young people completing formal skill training. His central assertion — that AI cannot take the jobs of skilled workers — was delivered as a direct rebuttal to the anxiety now running through campuses, coaching centres and family drawing rooms. 'Times are changing,' he said, framing the shift as a filter rather than a flood.

The setting matters. A Kaushal Deekshant Samaroh is not a technology summit; it is a graduation for welders, electricians, technicians, healthcare assistants and machine operators trained inside India's vocational ecosystem. Speaking to that audience, the Prime Minister tied the argument to two familiar themes: the dignity of labour and the limits of degree-centric education. The remarks were reported by NDTV and carried in the Prime Minister's Office release on the Lucknow address.

Why 'Skilled' Is the Operative Word

The distinction he drew is technically defensible. AI systems substitute tasks, not trades. A model can draft a quotation, summarise a service report or flag an anomaly on a production line, but it cannot torque a bolt on a live gas line, trace an intermittent fault in a three-phase motor, or manage a patient who cannot articulate their own symptoms. Those roles are protected less by sentiment than by unstructured physical environments, safety liability and the cost of a wrong move.

Where the Protection Is Thinner

That protection is not universal, and pretending otherwise would be poor advice to a 19-year-old choosing a course. Entry-level data entry, basic bookkeeping, first-draft content and routine visual quality inspection are precisely the rungs where AI deployment is cheapest. A trainee certified only in repetitive, screen-based tasks sits on the exposed end of the market. The Prime Minister's claim holds strongest for trades with a physical or compliance component — electrical, plumbing, CNC operation, welding, HVAC, nursing assistance and automotive service.

The IIT vs ITI Argument

Modi's second strand was blunter: IITs alone will not build Viksit Bharat. India graduates a very large cohort of engineers every year while industry bodies report persistent shortages of certified technicians. The gap shows up in wages. A skilled CNC or HVAC technician with five years of field experience frequently earns on par with, and in some metros above, a fresh generalist engineering graduate — because the technician's skill is verifiable and immediately deployable.

What Trainees and Employers Should Take Away

  • Course selection is now a risk decision: prefer NSQF-aligned programmes with a mandatory on-site apprenticeship over classroom-only modules.
  • Certification beats duration: a Sector Skill Council certificate plus six months of supervised shop-floor work outranks a longer course with no assessment.
  • Employers gain leverage: apprentices trained under structured schemes arrive with documented competencies, cutting onboarding time and rework costs.
  • Gap remains: the speech signals intent, but trainer shortages and uneven ITI lab infrastructure still limit outcomes in several states.
  • AI literacy is not optional: even protected trades increasingly involve digital diagnostic tools, so trainees should add basic data and device literacy.

The practical reading is narrow and useful. Skill alone is not a shield; certified, verifiable skill in a trade that requires hands, judgement and accountability is. That is the segment of the workforce the Prime Minister was addressing in Lucknow, and it is the segment where India's skilling investment has the clearest return.
