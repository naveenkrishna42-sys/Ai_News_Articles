Bitcoin Slips as S&P’s New Crypto Index Excludes It, Crash Fears Loom

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • S&P’s new crypto index does not include Bitcoin, raising questions about its market standing.
  • Crash fears intensify as Bitcoin prices slide, with some analysts predicting a fall to $20,000.
  • The exclusion may reflect a broader shift toward other digital assets, though Bitcoin remains dominant.

S&P’s New Index Leaves Out Bitcoin

Bitcoin was conspicuously absent from S&P Global’s newly launched cryptocurrency index, a move that has sent ripples through the digital asset market. The index, designed to track the performance of leading cryptocurrencies, instead features a selection of altcoins, including Ethereum and Solana.

This exclusion comes at a time when Bitcoin is already under pressure, with its price sliding in recent weeks. Analysts suggest the omission could further undermine investor confidence in the world’s largest cryptocurrency.

Crash Fears Return

Market observers are now warning that Bitcoin could be headed for a steep decline, with some forecasts putting the price as low as $20,000. The fear is not unfounded—Bitcoin has a history of volatile swings, and the current macroeconomic environment, marked by rising interest rates and regulatory uncertainty, is adding to the jitters.

“The exclusion from the S&P index is a symbolic blow,” said one analyst, speaking on condition of anonymity. “It signals that institutional players may be looking beyond Bitcoin for long-term growth.”

What the Index Means

S&P’s crypto index is expected to be used as a benchmark for investment products, such as exchange-traded funds. Bitcoin’s absence could mean that fund managers and institutional investors will allocate capital to other coins, potentially diverting billions away from Bitcoin.

However, Bitcoin proponents argue that its sheer market capitalisation and liquidity make it indispensable. At press time, Bitcoin still commands over 40% of the total crypto market, far ahead of its nearest rival.

Market Sentiment Turns Sour

The broader crypto market has been in a downturn, with total market capitalisation dropping by nearly 15% over the past week. Bitcoin’s price has fallen below $30,000, a level that had previously acted as strong support. If the sell-off intensifies, a drop to $20,000 is not out of the question, according to technical analysts.

Regulatory developments are also weighing on sentiment. In India, the government has yet to clarify its stance on crypto taxation, while globally, central banks are tightening monetary policy. These factors are creating a perfect storm for risk assets.

What to Watch

Investors will be closely watching Bitcoin’s ability to hold the $25,000 mark in the coming days. A break below that could trigger a cascade of stop-loss orders, accelerating the decline. Meanwhile, the S&P index’s performance will be a test of whether altcoins can sustain investor interest without Bitcoin’s backing.

The coming weeks will reveal whether Bitcoin can reclaim its status as the bellwether of crypto, or if the market is truly moving on.
