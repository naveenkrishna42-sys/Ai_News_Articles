CAF unanimously backs Infantino as Africa rallies behind FIFA chief

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • CAF's unanimous vote backs Infantino's continued leadership
  • Support comes as European federations push for World Cup reform
  • Infantino faces calls to resign from Norway's Lise Klaveness

Africa's decisive show of support

The Confederation of African Football (CAF) has unanimously voted in favour of Gianni Infantino, throwing the weight of the continent's 54 member associations behind the FIFA president. The decision, announced on Friday, marks a significant boost for Infantino as he navigates growing dissent from European quarters.

African nations have long been seen as a crucial voting bloc in FIFA's internal politics, and this collective endorsement signals a clear alignment with Infantino's leadership. The vote came during CAF's general assembly, though details of the deliberations were not immediately disclosed.

European opposition intensifies

The CAF decision stands in stark contrast to the mood in Europe, where several federations have voiced open discontent with Infantino's tenure. UEFA has stood by its plans to boycott the World Cup, even after an apology from Infantino, according to reports from espn.in. The rift highlights a deepening divide between the global body and its European arm.

Adding to the pressure, Norway's football federation president Lise Klaveness has called on Infantino to resign, stating bluntly: "There is no trust now." Klaveness has emerged as a vocal critic, and her comments reflect a broader unease among Northern European federations over FIFA's direction.

World Cup privatisation concerns

At the heart of the discord is a contentious proposal to privatise the World Cup. The New York Times reported that FIFA executives were given an ultimatum to approve the plan, a move critics say could prioritise commercial interests over the sport's integrity. Infantino has denied any impropriety, but the controversy has fuelled calls for greater transparency.

Meanwhile, Mexico has defied Concacaf's position by joining Argentina in supporting Infantino, according to a BBC report. This split within regional confederations underscores the complex alliances shaping FIFA's power structure.

What this means for FIFA's future

With Africa firmly in his corner, Infantino now commands a substantial base of support that could prove decisive in any future votes. The continent's unanimity gives him political cover against European criticism, though it does little to bridge the growing chasm between the two blocs.

For now, the focus shifts to how UEFA responds to this African endorsement. The threat of a World Cup boycott remains on the table, and Infantino's apology appears to have done little to soothe tensions. The coming months will test whether the global game can hold together its fractious coalition.

Observers will be watching closely as FIFA's next council meeting approaches, with all eyes on whether Europe's defiance or Africa's loyalty shapes the governing body's trajectory. The battle for football's soul is far from over.
