Sensex Slips 455 Points, Nifty Dips Below 24,600; Bajaj Finance Falls 6%

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex closed 455 points lower, Nifty settled below 24,600.
  • Bajaj Finance was the top loser, falling 6%.
  • Broader market sentiment remained weak amid selling pressure.

Indian stock markets closed the week on a sombre note on Friday, with benchmark indices extending losses. The BSE Sensex ended 455 points lower, while the NSE Nifty slipped below the crucial 24,600 mark, reflecting cautious investor sentiment across sectors.

Market breadth remained negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on both exchanges. The sell-off was led by financial heavyweights, with Bajaj Finance emerging as the standout loser, plunging 6% during the session.

Bajaj Finance Drags Financials

Bajaj Finance shares came under severe pressure, eroding investor wealth significantly. The stock's sharp fall pulled down the overall indices, as the non-banking financial company (NBFC) carries substantial weight in both Sensex and Nifty.

While the exact trigger for the decline was not immediately clear, analysts pointed to profit-booking after recent gains. The stock had been on a strong run in the past few weeks, and Friday's slide suggests some investors chose to lock in gains.

Other financial stocks also traded weak, though none matched the intensity of Bajaj Finance's drop. The broader financial sector index ended the day in the red, adding to the market's downward momentum.

Broader Market Under Pressure

Midcap and smallcap stocks also witnessed selling, though the decline was relatively milder compared to largecaps. The broader market indices fell in tandem with the benchmarks, indicating that the corrective phase was widespread.

Sectoral indices presented a mixed picture, but most key sectors including IT, auto, and energy ended lower. Only a few defensive pockets managed to stay afloat, offering limited support to the market.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have been net sellers in recent sessions, and Friday's data is expected to reflect continued outflows. Domestic institutional buying, however, has partly cushioned the fall, according to market observers.

Volatility and Global Cues

Global cues remained weak, with Asian markets trading mixed and European indices opening lower. Concerns over global growth and monetary policy have kept investors on edge, impacting risk appetite across emerging markets.

The volatility index, India VIX, rose during the day, signalling heightened anxiety among traders. Options data suggested that traders were building positions for further downside, though some support was visible near lower levels.

Market participants are now awaiting clarity on domestic inflation data and global central bank moves. Any unexpected developments could dictate the near-term trajectory, analysts said.

What to Watch Next Week

Investors will closely track crude oil prices, rupee movement, and monthly auto sales numbers in the coming sessions. The RBI's policy stance and commentary from the US Federal Reserve will also be key drivers.

The market's ability to hold above key support levels will be crucial. If selling pressure persists, further downside cannot be ruled out, but any positive surprise on the macro front could trigger a rebound.
