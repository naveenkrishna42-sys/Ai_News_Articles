12 Killed in Russia in One of Deadliest Ukrainian Attacks Since War Began

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 12 people were killed in a Ukrainian drone attack in Russia's Tatarstan region.
  • The strike targeted oil refining facilities, according to reports.
  • This is described as one of the deadliest attacks on Russian soil since the war began in 2022.
  • Separate overnight exchanges between Russia and Ukraine left seven dead and many injured.

At least 12 people were killed in a Ukrainian drone attack on Russia's Tatarstan region, officials said on Monday, marking one of the deadliest strikes on Russian soil since the war began four years ago.

The attack targeted oil refining facilities in the region, which lies hundreds of kilometres from the Ukrainian border. It was the latest in a series of long-range Ukrainian drone offensives aimed at disrupting Russia's energy infrastructure.

Deadliest Strike in Recent Memory

Russian officials confirmed the death toll on Monday morning, though full details of the attack were still emerging. The strike hit residential areas near the targeted industrial sites, according to local reports.

This attack is being described as one of the most lethal Ukrainian strikes inside Russia since the conflict began in February 2022. Previous attacks have often caused damage to fuel depots and refineries, but rarely have they resulted in such a high number of civilian casualties.

Targeting Oil Infrastructure

Ukraine has increasingly used long-range drones to strike Russian oil refineries and storage facilities, aiming to cut off fuel supplies to the Russian military and reduce revenues from energy exports. The Tatarstan region is home to several major refining complexes, making it a key target.

The scale of the attack suggests a shift in Ukraine's strategy, with a greater focus on inflicting economic damage deep inside Russian territory. Western analysts have noted that such strikes are part of a broader effort to weaken Russia's war machine.

Overnight Exchanges Leave More Dead

In a separate escalation, Russia and Ukraine exchanged overnight strikes that left at least seven people dead and many others injured, according to reports from both sides. The cross-border attacks have become a near-daily occurrence, but the latest round appears to have been particularly intense.

Ukrainian officials reported that Russian missiles and drones hit residential areas in several cities, while Russian authorities reported similar attacks on border regions. The full extent of the damage is still being assessed, with rescue operations underway in some areas.

War Enters Fourth Year with No End in Sight

The war has now entered its fourth year, with no diplomatic resolution in sight. Both sides continue to trade strikes, and the civilian toll keeps climbing. The attack in Tatarstan is a stark reminder that the conflict is not confined to the front lines, but is being felt deep within both countries.

International calls for a ceasefire have grown louder, but neither Moscow nor Kyiv has shown willingness to compromise. The United Nations and other global bodies have repeatedly condemned attacks on civilian infrastructure, but such appeals have had little effect.

Russian authorities have not yet released a full list of the deceased, nor have they specified how many of the casualties were civilians versus military personnel. Ukrainian officials have not commented directly on the Tatarstan attack, in line with their usual policy of neither confirming nor denying specific strikes.

As the war grinds on, the likelihood of more high-casualty attacks remains high. Both sides are believed to be stockpiling drones and missiles for further strikes.

What happens next will depend on whether either side escalates further or seeks a path to negotiations. For now, the people of Tatarstan and other regions on both sides of the border are left to count their dead and brace for what comes next.
