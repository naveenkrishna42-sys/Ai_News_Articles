India's 7.8% GDP Print: A Standout Performer in Asia

India's latest gross domestic product (GDP) expansion at 7.8% has positioned the country as the fastest-growing major economy in Asia. The figure, which outpaces China's estimated 4.7% and the 3.2% average across advanced Asian economies, reflects resilient domestic consumption, a rebound in private capital expenditure, and improving manufacturing output. For Sensex and Nifty investors, this headline number signals underlying corporate earnings strength, particularly in financials, capital goods, and consumer discretionary segments that historically benefit from accelerating GDP momentum.

However, growth alone does not dictate equity market direction. Capital flows, currency dynamics, and relative regional valuation matter equally. This is where the parallel rallies in the KOSPI, Nikkei, and Taiwan TAIEX introduce a layer of complexity for Indian benchmark indices.

The Asian Rally Paradox: KOSPI, Nikkei, and TAIEX Hit New Highs

While Indian macros remain supportive, neighbouring markets have entered overbought territory. The KOSPI has surged past the 4,200 mark, the Nikkei 225 has reclaimed levels above 41,000, and the Taiwan TAIEX broke through 22,000, driven largely by semiconductor and artificial intelligence (AI) hardware demand. These rallies are not merely sentiment-driven; they are anchored by earnings upgrades at bellwethers like Samsung Electronics, TSMC, and Tokyo Electron.

For global allocators, the question is simple: when peer markets trade at stretched valuations, why rotate into Indian equities trading near all-time-high price-to-earnings (P/E) multiples of 24-26x on the Nifty 50? This valuation gap is the central concern flagged by foreign portfolio investor (FPI) desks.

Why Asian Peers Outperform Could Pressure Sensex and Nifty

Three structural factors make the KOSPI-Nikkei-TAIEX rally a direct risk for Indian benchmarks.

1. Capital Reallocation Pressure

FPIs managing regional mandates increasingly favour Korean and Taiwanese tech exposure as AI capex accelerates. Indian markets, lacking a comparable semiconductor bellwether, face relative outflows. Tracking data shows FPIs have pulled a net Rs 12,400 crore from Indian equities over the past four weeks even as Korean and Taiwanese bourses absorbed record inflows.

2. Currency-Adjusted Returns Favour East Asia

The Korean Won and Japanese Yen have appreciated against the US dollar in 2026, enhancing dollar-based returns on KOSPI and Nikkei investments. The Indian rupee has remained range-bound with mild depreciation pressure, eroding the currency-adjusted return advantage for Sensex and Nifty buyers.

3. Valuation Premium Without Earnings Catch-Up

The Nifty 50 trades at a 40-60% premium to Asian peers despite delivering lower quarterly earnings upgrades. If corporate Q2 FY27 results disappoint relative to elevated expectations, even a strong GDP backdrop will not shield indices from valuation compression.

What Indian Investors Should Monitor This Quarter

  • Pros for Indian Equities: Strong 7.8% GDP supports earnings; domestic institutional flows remain positive with Rs 28,000 crore SIP inflows in August; RBI's stable policy stance reduces macro volatility.
  • Cons for Indian Equities: FPI selling intensifies as Asian tech rallies offer cheaper AI exposure; rupee weakness caps currency-adjusted gains; elevated Nifty P/E leaves limited margin of safety.
  • Watch Trigger: Q2 FY27 earnings season beginning mid-October 2026 will determine if India can sustain premium valuations.
  • Sector Implication: IT services and pharma may face relative underperformance versus global tech-heavy Asian indices.

Bottom Line: Strength at Home, Competition Abroad

India's 7.8% GDP print is genuinely constructive for the structural bull case supporting Sensex and Nifty. Yet in a globally interconnected market, capital chases relative momentum. As long as KOSPI, Nikkei, and Taiwan TAIEX continue delivering AI-led earnings surprises at cheaper valuations, Indian indices will struggle to attract incremental FPI flows. Traders and long-term investors should brace for heightened volatility and a possible consolidation phase unless domestic earnings decisively outperform regional peers in the upcoming results cycle.
