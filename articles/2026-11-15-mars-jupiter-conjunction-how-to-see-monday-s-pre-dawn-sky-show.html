Mars-Jupiter Conjunction: How to See Monday's Pre-Dawn Sky Show

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-15 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mars and Jupiter make a close pre-dawn approach on Monday, November 16, 2026, visible worldwide in the east-southeast sky.
  • Jupiter will dominate at roughly magnitude -2.5 while Mars stays faint and low in brightening twilight.
  • Naked eyes suffice, but 7x50 or 10x50 binoculars frame both planets together and reveal Jupiter's four Galilean moons.

What You Will See Monday Morning

Mars and Jupiter will make a close approach in the pre-dawn sky on Monday, November 16, 2026, appearing to nearly touch low in the east-southeast before sunrise. Reports from Forbes, SNJ Today, Oregon Coast Beach Connection and The Peninsula Qatar confirm the event is visible from both hemispheres, so no travel is required.

The two worlds are not physically close. A conjunction is a line-of-sight alignment, with the planets sitting at vastly different distances from Earth. The visual effect is still striking because the pair will fit inside a single binocular field of view.

Why This Pairing Is Worth the Alarm Clock

Jupiter will be unmissable, shining around magnitude -2.5, outclassed only by the Moon and Venus. Mars, by contrast, will be a faint orange point struggling against brightening twilight. That brightness contrast is the practical story: find Jupiter first, then drop to binoculars and sweep nearby for Mars.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pro: completely free, worldwide, and bright enough to see from city skies despite light pollution.
  • Pro: binoculars turn a two-dot sight into a small telescopic scene with moons visible.
  • Con: Mars may be swallowed by dawn glare; the earlier you look, the better.
  • Con: a low eastern horizon is mandatory — high-rises or hills can hide the pair entirely.
  • Photography: use a tripod, brace the binoculars, and keep exposures to a few seconds.

Safety and Expectations

Never point binoculars or a telescope anywhere near the sun, and stop observing once sunrise begins. Treat this as a beautiful photo opportunity rather than a once-in-a-lifetime event: planetary conjunctions occur routinely. This one earns attention purely because Jupiter's glare anchors the view and Mars adds colour contrast beside it.
