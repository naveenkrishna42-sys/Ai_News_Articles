Bengaluru Officer Loses Rs 10 Lakh After Downloading WhatsApp Wedding Invite

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Bengaluru-based officer downloaded a wedding invitation file sent on WhatsApp.
  • The file contained malware that gave scammers remote access to her phone.
  • Rs 10 lakh was siphoned from her bank account in multiple transactions.

Officer Targeted by Malware-Laced Invite

A Bengaluru-based officer lost Rs 10 lakh after downloading a wedding invitation sent via WhatsApp. The incident highlights the growing threat of malware-based financial fraud.

The victim, whose identity has not been disclosed by authorities, received the invite from an unknown number. She downloaded the file, which triggered a hidden malware payload.

How the Scam Unfolded

Once downloaded, the malware gave scammers remote access to her smartphone. They used this access to intercept one-time passwords (OTPs) and banking credentials.

According to police sources, the scammers initiated multiple small-value transactions over several hours. The total loss amounted to exactly Rs 10 lakh.

The officer only realised the fraud when she checked her bank balance the next morning. She immediately approached the Cyber Crime police station in Bengaluru.

Police Investigation Underway

Cyber crime officials have registered a case under the Information Technology Act and relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code. They are tracing the fraudulent transactions through bank records.

Preliminary investigations suggest the scam originated from a phone number registered in a different state. The malware used is believed to be a variant of a known remote access trojan.

Law enforcement officials have not yet confirmed if other people were targeted by the same number.

Rise in WhatsApp-Based Cyber Fraud

This case adds to a growing list of WhatsApp-based frauds in India. Scammers increasingly use social engineering tactics to trick users into downloading malicious files.

Common lures include fake job offers, loan approvals, and now wedding invitations. Experts warn users to never download files from unknown senders.

Banks and digital payment platforms have repeatedly advised customers to not share OTPs or banking details with anyone.

What's next: Police are working with telecom and banking authorities to trace the money trail. The victim is expected to file a formal complaint with her bank for possible reimbursement. Officials urge citizens to verify any unsolicited WhatsApp messages before interacting with them.
