Global stocks fall as semiconductor sell-off deepens, oil heads for weekly gain

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures dropped sharply as chipmaker sell-off accelerated.
  • Netflix shares slid 11% following earnings, adding to tech sector pressure.
  • Crude oil prices set for weekly gain on supply disruption fears.
  • Markets trimmed losses after consumer sentiment data but tech slide persisted.

Global stock markets tumbled on Friday as a deepening rout in semiconductor stocks sent shockwaves through equity indices. The sell-off, which began earlier in the week, intensified during Asian and European trading hours before spilling over into Wall Street futures.

Investors fled risk assets amid growing fears that the chip sector's boom cycle is peaking. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite was set to open lower, extending losses from the previous session.

Semiconductor rout deepens

The slide in chip stocks accelerated after major manufacturers flagged softening demand and inventory buildup. The Philadelphia SE Semiconductor Index, a key benchmark for the sector, hovered near multi-month lows.

Analysts pointed to oversupply concerns and weakening end-user demand for consumer electronics. The sell-off dragged down broader indices, with the S&P 500 futures dropping in pre-market trading.

Netflix slides 11%, weighs on tech

Adding to the tech sector's pain, streaming giant Netflix saw its shares plunge 11% in after-hours trading following its quarterly earnings report. The company missed subscriber growth targets, raising doubts about the streaming industry's growth trajectory.

The sharp drop in Netflix shares amplified the negative sentiment already weighing on technology stocks. The broader communication services sector also came under pressure.

Oil set for weekly gain

In contrast to equities, crude oil prices were on course for a weekly gain. Brent crude hovered near $85 per barrel, supported by ongoing supply disruptions and geopolitical tensions in key producing regions.

Traders cited output cuts from major producers and steady demand as factors keeping prices elevated. The energy sector remained a bright spot amid the broader market gloom.

Markets trim losses after consumer data

Wall Street trimmed some of its early losses after the release of better-than-expected consumer sentiment data. The University of Michigan's consumer confidence index came in above forecasts, offering a brief reprieve to risk-averse investors.

However, the tech slide remained the dominant driver of market direction. The gains from the consumer data were short-lived as traders refocused on the semiconductor rout.

European and Asian indices also closed lower. Japan's Nikkei 225 fell over 2%, while Germany's DAX and France's CAC 40 each dropped more than 1%.

The sell-off underscores growing anxiety that the technology sector, which has powered much of the market's rally over the past year, is now facing headwinds.

All eyes will be on next week's earnings from other major tech firms, which could either stabilise or further unsettle markets.
