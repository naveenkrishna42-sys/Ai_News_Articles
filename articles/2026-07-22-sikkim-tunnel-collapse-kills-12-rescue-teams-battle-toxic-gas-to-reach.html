Sikkim tunnel collapse kills 12, rescue teams battle toxic gas to reach trapped workers

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • 12 workers confirmed dead after tunnel collapse at a hydropower project in Sikkim.
  • 13 workers remain trapped; rescue operations are hampered by toxic gases.
  • The incident follows an explosion inside the tunnel; cause not yet officially determined.

Tunnel Collapse at Hydropower Site

A tunnel collapse at a hydropower project in Sikkim has left 12 workers dead and several others trapped, officials said on Wednesday. Rescue teams are racing against time to reach 13 workers who remain stuck inside the debris-filled tunnel.

The incident occurred at a construction site in the mountainous state. Details of the exact location and the name of the project have not yet been officially released.

Toxic Gases Hamper Rescue Efforts

Rescue operations are being severely hampered by the presence of toxic gases inside the tunnel. The gases, believed to have been released after an explosion, have forced teams to work in shifts with limited oxygen supplies.

Emergency crews are using heavy machinery to clear the rubble. However, progress has been slow as they must stop periodically to ventilate the tunnel and check air quality levels. Officials have not confirmed the composition of the gases.

Conflicting Reports on Casualties

While authorities have confirmed 12 deaths, other reports suggest the toll could be higher. Some news agencies have put the number of dead at 11 or 20, with the number of missing varying between 5 and 14. The state government has not yet issued a consolidated statement clarifying the exact figures.

Family members of the trapped workers have gathered near the site, anxiously waiting for updates. The workers were employed by a contractor working on the hydropower project. Their identities have not been released pending formal notification of next of kin.

What Happened

The collapse is believed to have been triggered by an explosion inside the tunnel. The blast may have been caused by a build-up of methane or other gases, but investigators have not confirmed the cause. The National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) has been deployed to assist local teams.

Sikkim, a state prone to landslides and seismic activity, has seen several infrastructure accidents in recent years. This is one of the deadliest tunnel incidents in the region in recent memory.

Rescue teams are expected to continue operations through the night. Officials have said that the priority remains extracting the trapped workers safely. The state government has promised a thorough inquiry into the incident once rescue efforts are complete.
