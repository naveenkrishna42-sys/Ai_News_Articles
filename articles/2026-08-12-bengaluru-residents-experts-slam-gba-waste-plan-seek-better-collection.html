Bengaluru residents, experts slam GBA waste plan, seek better collection

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Residents and experts have criticised the GBA's latest waste management decision.
  • They argue the focus should be on improving the current waste collection system.
  • The GBA has not yet responded to the criticism.

Backlash over GBA's waste plan

Residents and waste management experts in Bengaluru have strongly criticised the latest move by the GBA, saying it fails to address the city's core problem — a broken collection system. They have demanded that the authority fix the basics before introducing any new initiatives.

The GBA's recent decision, announced earlier this week, was meant to streamline waste processing. But those on the ground say it sidesteps the daily chaos of door-to-door collection, segregation, and transportation that plagues most wards.

Collection system is the real issue

Municipal solid waste handling in Bengaluru rests on a fragile chain, and the weakest link, residents argue, is collection. In several neighbourhoods, garbage bins overflow for days, while in others, collection is erratic and unannounced.

Experts point out that without a reliable collection network, any downstream investment in processing or treatment is wasted. "You can't compost what never reaches the plant," said a city-based waste consultant who has worked with multiple wards.

The GBA's move, they say, appears to prioritise large-scale infrastructure over the unglamorous but essential work of picking up trash from every doorstep.

Residents demand accountability

Residents' welfare associations across the city have voiced similar frustrations. Many have submitted formal complaints to the GBA, asking it to publish ward-wise collection schedules and enforce penalties on contractors who skip routes.

"We see new projects announced every few months, but our street still looks the same," said a resident of an east Bengaluru layout, who did not wish to be named. "Fix the collection first. That is all we ask."

Some have also questioned the transparency of the GBA's decision-making process, alleging that residents were not consulted before the move was announced.

Experts call for a ground-up approach

Waste management specialists argue that Bengaluru's problems are not unique, but they are solvable with consistent execution. They point to cities like Pune and Indore, where collection systems were overhauled before large investments were made.

The key, they say, is to map every household, fix timings, and ensure that waste is segregated at source. The GBA, they contend, must focus on these fundamentals rather than chasing headline-grabbing projects.

One expert suggested the GBA conduct a pilot in a few high-density wards, measure the results, and then scale up. "But that requires patience and political will," he added.

What happens next

The GBA has not yet responded to the criticism. It is expected to hold a review meeting later this month, where the waste collection issue is likely to be on the agenda.

Residents say they will continue to press the authority for a concrete timeline. Whether the GBA relents or sticks to its plan remains to be seen, but the pressure is clearly mounting.
