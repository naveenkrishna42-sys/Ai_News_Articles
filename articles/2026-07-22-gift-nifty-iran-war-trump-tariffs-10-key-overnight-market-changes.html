Gift Nifty Signals Weak Start

Indian stock markets are set for a cautious opening on Wednesday, with Gift Nifty trading around the 22,450 mark, down over 100 points from Tuesday's close. The negative bias comes as global investors reassess risk amid escalating geopolitical tensions and renewed trade war fears.

The Nifty 50 and Sensex are expected to open lower, tracking weakness in Asian peers and overnight losses on Wall Street. Analysts expect a volatile session as traders digest multiple headwinds.

Iran War Escalation Rattles Global Markets

Crude oil prices surged more than 3 percent overnight after reports of fresh military strikes in the Iran conflict zone. Brent crude climbed past $82 per barrel, raising concerns about inflation and input costs for Indian companies.

Defense and oil marketing stocks are likely to see heightened activity. Traders are closely watching diplomatic moves, as any further escalation could trigger a broader sell-off in emerging markets.

Trump Tariff Threats Return

Former US President Donald Trump has renewed threats of imposing steep tariffs on Indian goods, citing trade imbalances. The announcement, made late Tuesday US time, has spooked export-oriented sectors such as IT, textiles, and pharmaceuticals.

The Nifty IT index could face pressure, with companies like Infosys and TCS seen as vulnerable. The Indian government has not yet responded, but officials indicate they are prepared for negotiations.

Kospi Rally Offers Rare Positive Cue

South Korea's Kospi index rallied over 1.5 percent in early Asian trade, driven by strong semiconductor exports and a weaker won. This provided a rare bright spot for Indian markets, given the correlation between tech-heavy indices.

However, analysts caution that the Kospi move is sector-specific and unlikely to offset broader global headwinds. Indian market breadth is expected to remain negative.

Other Key Overnight Developments

The US dollar index strengthened to 104.5, putting pressure on the Indian rupee, which opened weaker at 83.80 against the dollar. Foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) sold equities worth Rs 1,200 crore in Tuesday's session, extending their selling streak.

Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) bought shares worth Rs 850 crore, providing some support. Gold prices also rose 1 percent, reflecting safe-haven demand.

On the macroeconomic front, India's GDP growth forecast for Q1 FY26 was trimmed by a global brokerage, citing weak manufacturing data. This has added to concerns over earnings momentum.

Asian markets were mixed, with Japan's Nikkei down 0.8 percent while China's Shanghai Composite traded flat. European and US indices ended lower overnight, with the S&P 500 losing 0.6 percent.

All eyes are now on the weekly F&O expiry and any fresh commentary from the RBI regarding inflation management. Traders are advised to remain cautious and avoid aggressive bets until clarity emerges on the geopolitical and tariff fronts.
