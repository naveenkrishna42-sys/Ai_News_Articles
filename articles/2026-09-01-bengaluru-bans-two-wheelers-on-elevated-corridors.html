Bengaluru bans two-wheelers on elevated corridors

📅 2026-09-01 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru's new traffic rules ban two-wheelers from Electronics City and Airport Road elevated corridors.
  • The ban aims to improve safety and reduce congestion on the elevated roads.
  • The ban has been met with mixed reactions from commuters and two-wheeler owners.

Bengaluru's Elevated Roads: A Two-Wheeler Free Zone

Bengaluru's traffic authorities have announced a ban on two-wheelers from the elevated corridors of Electronics City and Airport Road. The decision comes as part of the city's ongoing efforts to improve road safety and reduce congestion. The ban, which is set to take effect from next month, has been met with mixed reactions from commuters and two-wheeler owners.

Why the Ban?

The ban is primarily aimed at improving safety on the elevated roads. Two-wheelers are often involved in accidents on these roads, which are designed to accommodate faster-moving vehicles. The ban is also expected to reduce congestion, as two-wheelers are known to cause bottlenecks on these roads.

Impact on Commuters

The ban is expected to have a significant impact on commuters who rely on two-wheelers to travel to work. Many of these commuters live in areas that are not well-connected by public transport, and the ban could force them to seek alternative modes of transportation. The city's traffic authorities have promised to improve public transport in these areas to mitigate the impact of the ban.

Reactions from Two-Wheeler Owners

Many two-wheeler owners have expressed their displeasure with the ban. They argue that the ban is unfair, as they are not the ones causing the accidents. They also claim that the ban will not significantly reduce congestion, as many of the commuters are already using public transport.

Looking Ahead

The ban is part of a broader effort by Bengaluru's traffic authorities to improve road safety and reduce congestion. The city's traffic police have promised to enforce the ban strictly, and have also announced plans to improve the elevated roads to make them safer for all types of vehicles.
