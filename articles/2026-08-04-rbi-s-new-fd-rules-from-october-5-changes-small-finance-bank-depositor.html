RBI's new FD rules from October: 5 changes small finance bank depositors must know

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI introduces new FD rules for small finance banks starting October.
  • Five key changes affect depositors' interest rates and deposit terms.
  • Depositors need to review their FD portfolios to adapt to new norms.

New FD norms for small finance banks from October

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has announced a fresh set of fixed deposit (FD) rules specifically for small finance banks (SFBs), set to take effect from October. These changes are aimed at harmonising regulations and enhancing depositor protection, but they also bring practical implications for customers who park their savings in these institutions.

Small finance banks, which cater largely to underserved segments, will now operate under revised guidelines that touch everything from interest rate setting to premature withdrawals. Depositors, especially those with significant sums in SFBs, would do well to understand what shifts are coming.

1. Interest rate recalibration

One of the most awaited changes involves how SFBs calculate and offer interest rates on fixed deposits. The new rules are expected to bring more transparency in rate setting, potentially aligning them closer to prevailing market benchmarks. This could mean slightly different returns for depositors compared to earlier slabs.

While the central bank has not specified exact percentages, the move is seen as a step to reduce arbitrage between SFBs and larger commercial banks. Customers should watch for updated rate cards from their respective banks as October approaches.

2. Premature withdrawal terms

The revised framework also tweaks the conditions under which depositors can break their FDs prematurely. Historically, SFBs had their own penalty structures; the new rules aim to standardise these across the sector. This means the penalty for early withdrawal may change, affecting net returns for those who need liquidity before maturity.

Depositors are advised to check their bank's updated penalty slabs, as some may see a marginal increase or decrease depending on the tenor and the remaining period.

3. Deposit insurance coverage clarity

Another key change relates to the clarity around deposit insurance. While the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) covers up to Rs 5 lakh per depositor, the new rules are expected to streamline how SFBs communicate this coverage. Banks will now have to display insurance details more prominently in passbooks and statements.

This is a consumer-friendly move, but it also means depositors should verify their total exposure across multiple accounts in the same bank, as the insurance cap applies collectively.

4. Renewal and maturity procedures

The RBI has also revised the process for auto-renewal of FDs. Under the new norms, SFBs must obtain explicit consent from depositors before renewing an FD on maturity, unless the depositor has given a standing instruction. This prevents unwanted auto-renewals at lower rates, a common complaint among customers.

In practice, banks will need to send reminders and seek confirmation, which could add a step to the renewal process. Depositors should keep an eye on their maturity dates and respond promptly to bank communications.

5. Nomination and joint account rules

Finally, the new guidelines tighten the rules around nomination and joint accounts. SFBs will have to ensure that nomination facilities are offered at account opening, and in the absence of a nomination, the bank must inform the depositor in writing. This is aimed at reducing disputes in the event of the depositor's demise.

Joint account holders will also see clearer definitions of survivorship rights, which could affect how funds are transferred. Existing depositors may want to update their nomination details to avoid any hiccups later.

What this means for you

For the average small finance bank depositor, these changes are largely procedural but carry financial consequences. Interest rates and penalty structures directly impact returns, while the new consent norms give depositors greater control over their money. It is advisable to review your existing FDs and bank communications over the next few weeks.

As the October deadline approaches, banks are expected to roll out detailed circulars. The RBI has not yet published the complete notification, but the broad contours are clear. Watch for official updates from your bank and the central bank's website to stay ahead of the curve.
