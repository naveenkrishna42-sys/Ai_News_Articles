Maha Shivratri 2026: Date, Puja Timings, Rituals and Significance

When is Maha Shivratri in 2026?

Maha Shivratri, one of the most significant Hindu festivals dedicated to Lord Shiva, falls on Saturday, March 14, 2026. The festival is observed on the 14th day of the dark fortnight (Krishna Paksha) in the month of Phalguna as per the Hindu calendar.

The Chaturdashi tithi begins at 2:17 PM on March 14 and ends at 4:28 PM on March 15. Devotees across India and around the world will mark the occasion with fasting, night-long vigils, and special prayers.

Exact Puja Timings and Nishita Kaal

The most important time for performing the Maha Shivratri puja is during the Nishita Kaal, which is the midnight hour. This year, the Nishita Kaal puja time is from 12:07 AM to 12:56 AM on March 15, 2026.

Other significant time slots include the early morning puja (04:17 AM to 05:26 AM on March 15) and the evening puja (06:29 PM to 08:54 PM on March 14). Devotees typically perform four pujas during the night, each corresponding to a specific part of the night.

Rituals and Observances

Devotees observe a strict day-long fast, consuming only fruits, milk, or water. Many undertake a nirjala (without water) fast. The fast is broken the next day after the morning puja and after offering food to a Brahmin.

The main ritual involves bathing the Shiva Linga with water, milk, honey, and bel leaves. Devotees offer sandalwood paste, rice, flowers, and fruits. Chanting the Panchakshari mantra 'Om Namah Shivaya' and the Mahamrityunjaya mantra is considered highly auspicious. A night-long vigil, known as jagran, is kept.

Spiritual Significance

Maha Shivratri is believed to be the night when Lord Shiva performed the Tandava, the cosmic dance of creation, preservation, and destruction. It also marks the night he married Goddess Parvati.

Spiritually, the festival symbolises the victory over darkness and ignorance. It is a time for introspection, self-discipline, and seeking the blessings of Lord Shiva for peace, prosperity, and liberation.

What to watch: Temples across India, especially the twelve Jyotirlingas, will see heavy footfall. Check local timings and darshan arrangements if you plan to visit.
