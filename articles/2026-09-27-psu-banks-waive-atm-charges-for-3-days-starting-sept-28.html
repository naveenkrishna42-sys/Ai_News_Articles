PSU Banks Extend Free ATM Limit Through Strike Duration

On September 28, a series of Public Sector Unit (PSU) banks have publicly announced they will temporarily suspend all ATM service charges once customers exceed their predetermined free withdrawal allowance. This three-day measure represents an immediate customer relief initiative aimed at mitigating frustration among millions who depend on automated teller machines for routine cash access.

Customer Benefit Analysis

The existing free ATM threshold remains intact for standard usage patterns. However, any transaction amount surpassing this baseline triggers zero-cost processing for the duration of the strike period. For frequent withdrawals, this means significant savings without altering the underlying banking infrastructure or requiring new account provisions.

Pros

  • Direct cost reduction for regular savers and small enterprises relying on ATM visits
  • Immediate relief offered during an ongoing financial disruption caused by workforce action
  • Clear, transparent application based solely on transaction volume thresholds

Cons

  • The benefit expires after three days with no extension mechanism until the strike concludes
  • Delayed salary payments for government employees compound financial stress for many households
  • Limited scope—only affects ATM-based withdrawals; other banking services continue normally

Government Employee Compensation Delay

A parallel development underscores the broader economic impact of the strike: central government employees were provisionally scheduled to receive advance salary disbursements on September 25 to partially compensate for the expected three-day cessation of banking services. This measure was crafted as a stopgap while fiscal negotiations continue regarding a potential transition to a five-day workweek framework, leaving workers caught between wage uncertainty and service disruptions.

Union Negotiations and Policy Uncertainty

Current reports indicate that no definitive commitment has been secured by the Finance Ministry concerning union demands related to a possible five-day workweek implementation. Bank unions remain in active discussions to establish work schedules and compensation parity prior to any escalation in labor unrest, creating ambiguity for both consumers and financial institutions about the true duration of banking disruptions and associated fees.
