After Modi Era: What Next for India's Foreign Policy?

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • NBR report analyzes India's foreign policy trajectory post-Modi era.
  • Focus on how domestic political shifts could impact strategic alliances.
  • Experts assess continuity and change in India's global role.
  • Report highlights key challenges and opportunities for future leadership.

Report Examines Post-Modi Foreign Policy

A new analysis from The National Bureau of Asian Research (NBR) is generating discussion in diplomatic circles. The report, titled 'After Modi: Political Leadership and the Future of Indian Foreign Policy,' looks at how a change in leadership could reshape India's approach to the world.

The timing is significant. India has emerged as a key player in global affairs under Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The NBR study does not predict a specific successor but instead explores the structural and political factors that will influence future decisions.

Domestic Politics and Global Strategy

The NBR report argues that domestic political calculations will remain a major driver of foreign policy. Any future government will have to balance strategic autonomy with the need for strong partnerships.

Key relationships with the United States, Russia, and China are under the microscope. The report suggests that while the broad contours of India's non-alignment may persist, the emphasis could shift depending on the ruling coalition's ideology.

Challenges Ahead for New Leadership

A future prime minister will inherit several complex files. The border standoff with China, energy security, and the push for a permanent UN Security Council seat are long-standing issues that require deft handling.

The NBR analysis points out that India's growing economic heft gives it more leverage. But it also warns that internal pressures—such as unemployment and regional disparities—could limit how much attention a new leader can pay to foreign affairs.

Continuity or Change?

Experts quoted in the report see a high probability of continuity in core areas. India's 'Act East' policy, its partnership with the Quad, and its focus on the Indo-Pacific are likely to endure regardless of who is in power.

However, the style of diplomacy could change. The Modi government has been known for a personalised, assertive approach. A future leader might revert to a more institutional, consensus-driven method.

The report also flags the rising influence of Indian diaspora groups and business lobbies. These stakeholders are expected to push for policies that protect trade routes and investment flows.

What to Watch

The NBR study serves as a primer for policymakers and analysts tracking India's trajectory. With general elections due in the coming years, the debate over foreign policy priorities is only expected to intensify. How the next government navigates between strategic ambitions and domestic realities will shape India's role for a generation.
