Congo Ebola outbreak becomes second-largest ever as deaths cross 2,000

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ebola outbreak in DRC is now second-largest ever recorded
  • Death toll crosses 2,000, according to health officials
  • WHO and Africa CDC call for urgent, community-led response
  • Outbreak began months before official alert, WHO says

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has become the second-largest in history, with the death toll crossing 2,000. The grim milestone underscores the challenges of containing the virus in a region plagued by conflict and mistrust.

Health officials confirmed the figure on Wednesday, though the exact count remains fluid as new cases are reported. The outbreak, concentrated in North Kivu and Ituri provinces, has now surpassed the 2018-2020 outbreak in the same region, which killed over 2,200 people.

A crisis that escalated quietly

The World Health Organization has revealed that the outbreak actually began months before the first official alert was raised. This delay allowed the virus to spread silently across communities, complicating containment efforts.

By the time authorities declared an emergency, the disease had already taken hold in areas where armed groups operate. Health workers have faced attacks, making it nearly impossible to reach affected populations in some zones.

Community-led action the only way forward

Both the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention and the WHO are now pushing for a shift in strategy. They argue that top-down measures have failed and that local communities must be empowered to lead the response.

This means working with traditional leaders, religious figures, and local health workers to build trust. Vaccination teams have been able to reach some areas, but insecurity remains a major hurdle.

In recent weeks, the number of new cases has shown signs of plateauing in certain hotspots. However, officials warn that any relaxation in vigilance could trigger another surge.

Regional and global implications

The outbreak has already spilled into neighbouring Uganda, though cross-border transmission has been limited so far. Regional governments have stepped up screening at border posts, but porous frontiers make complete prevention difficult.

Globally, the response has been hampered by funding shortfalls. Aid agencies say they need more resources to deploy mobile laboratories and support community outreach. The WHO has reiterated that the outbreak remains a public health emergency of international concern.

What lies ahead

Efforts are now focused on accelerating vaccinations and tracing contacts in the most affected areas. Officials are also ramping up awareness campaigns to counter misinformation that has fuelled resistance to medical help.

The coming weeks will be critical. If community engagement improves and security allows, health workers believe the outbreak can be brought under control. But as one official put it, the virus has shown time and again that it exploits every gap in the response.

All eyes are on the DRC as the world watches whether the lessons of past outbreaks are finally applied.
