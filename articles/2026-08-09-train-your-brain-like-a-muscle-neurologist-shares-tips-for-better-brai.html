Train your brain like a muscle: Neurologist shares tips for better brain health

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Brain can be trained like muscles, says neurologist
  • Exercise habit linked to lower Alzheimer's and dementia risk
  • Simple daily habits can rewire the brain at any age

The brain as a muscle

Can you really train your brain the way you train your biceps? According to a neurologist, the answer is yes. Just as physical exercise builds muscle strength, mental and lifestyle habits can reshape the brain's wiring, improving memory, focus, and resilience against age-related decline.

The concept, known as neuroplasticity, means the brain is not fixed but adaptable. Every new skill, habit, or challenge creates and strengthens neural pathways, much like lifting weights builds muscle fibres.

Exercise: The brain's best friend

One of the most powerful tools for brain health is physical activity. The exercise habit could help lower Alzheimer's and dementia risk, according to recent reports. Aerobic exercise increases blood flow to the brain, promotes the growth of new neurons, and reduces inflammation.

The neurologist emphasises that you do not need to run marathons. Even brisk walking, cycling, or swimming for 30 minutes a day can make a measurable difference. The key is consistency, not intensity.

Beyond crossword puzzles

Crossword puzzles and Sudoku are popular, but experts warn that they are not enough. Doing the same puzzles repeatedly trains a narrow set of skills. The brain needs variety and novelty to stay sharp.

Learning a new language, picking up a musical instrument, or trying a new hobby forces the brain to create new connections. This kind of challenging mental activity is what really boosts cognitive reserve.

Simple daily habits that rewire the brain

Neuroscience-backed habits can be surprisingly simple. For instance, doing a task with your non-dominant hand, like brushing your teeth, activates fresh neural circuits. Practising mindfulness or meditation for a few minutes daily can also strengthen attention and emotional regulation.

Social interaction is another vital habit. Engaging in meaningful conversations, whether with family or friends, stimulates the brain and reduces stress. Combining mental, physical, and social activities gives the best results.

What about sleep and diet?

Sleep is when the brain consolidates memories and clears toxins. Skimping on sleep undermines all other brain-boosting efforts. A diet rich in vegetables, fruits, whole grains, and omega-3 fatty acids also supports cognitive function.

Hydration matters too. Even mild dehydration can impair concentration and short-term memory. Small, consistent choices add up over time.

Start small, start today

You do not need a complete lifestyle overhaul to see benefits. New research suggests that just 5 to 10 minutes of focused brain training per day can improve cognitive performance at any age. The trick is to make it a daily routine.

The neurologist advises picking one habit and sticking with it for a month. Whether it is a daily walk, a new language app, or a few minutes of meditation, consistency beats intensity.

As research continues to evolve, the message is clear: your brain is never too old to change. With the right habits, you can keep it fit for life.
