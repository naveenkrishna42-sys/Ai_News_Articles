Trip Delay Insurance: What Indian Cardholders Actually Get

Key points
  • Most Indian premium credit cards cover trip delays only after 6-12 hour waits, not the 4-hour trigger common on US cards.
  • Reimbursement is capped per ticket and usually covers meals, lodging and local transport, not missed-event losses.
  • Claims require written airline delay proof, original boarding passes and receipts filed within 30-90 days of return.

What Trip Delay Insurance Really Covers

Trip delay insurance reimburses incidental expenses when a common carrier — an airline, train or bus operator — delays you beyond a defined threshold. It is not compensation for the delay itself. It pays for meals, overnight hotel stays, airport transfers and essential phone calls while you wait for the next available service. The payout is capped, typically per ticket or per traveller, and sits separate from any compensation the carrier may owe you under its own conditions of carriage.

On Indian credit cards, this benefit is almost always bundled into a broader travel protection package alongside baggage loss, trip cancellation and personal accident cover. The insurer is a third-party general insurer, not the card issuer, so the terms follow the master policy wording rather than the bank's marketing brochure.

Coverage Thresholds at a Glance

The single most important number is the waiting period before cover activates. Most Indian issuers set this at 6 to 12 hours, compared with the 3 to 4 hour triggers common on US premium cards. A six-hour gap between flights may leave you entirely uninsured.

Buyer Implications: Does It Justify the Fee?

Evaluate this benefit against your actual travel pattern, not the headline cover amount. A traveller who flies twice a year on domestic routes gains little from a Rs 25,000 cap that needs a 12-hour delay to activate. A frequent international flyer transiting through congested hubs in winter or monsoon season extracts far more value.

Compare the incremental annual fee against the realistic probability of a qualifying delay. If your card charges Rs 5,000 more than a basic variant and you face one qualifying delay in three years, the expected payout is thin. The benefit makes commercial sense mainly when it arrives bundled with lounge access, lower forex markup and air accident cover on a card you already hold for other reasons.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Cover activates only after the waiting period ends; the first 6-12 hours are on your own account.
  • Reimbursement is expense-based, so unspent cover is never paid out in cash.
  • Weather, air traffic control and mechanical faults are usually covered; airline strikes and your own late check-in typically are not.
  • Claims need an airline-issued delay certificate, which is difficult to obtain after leaving the airport.
  • Sub-limits often apply separately to meals and lodging, so a single large hotel bill may not be fully reimbursed.

How to File a Claim Without Rejection

Collect the delay certificate at the airline counter before departing the airport. Preserve every boarding pass, even for segments you completed. Keep itemised bills rather than card slips, since insurers reject summary receipts. File through the insurer's portal within the stated window, which is usually 30 to 90 days from your return date, and retain copies of everything submitted.

Declare the claim accurately. Inflated or duplicate claims across multiple cards for the same delay are treated as fraud and can lead to policy cancellation. If the airline later compensates you for the same expense, disclose it, because most policies operate on an indemnity basis and will not pay twice.

Verdict for Indian Cardholders

Trip delay insurance is a genuine but narrow benefit. It softens the cost of long, carrier-caused delays and is worth factoring into your card comparison, provided you read the trigger hours and per-ticket cap rather than the promotional summary. Treat it as a secondary reason to hold a travel card, never the primary one.
