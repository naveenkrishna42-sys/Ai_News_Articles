Iran, Oman Make Progress on Strait of Hormuz Reopening Deal

📅 2026-08-04 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran and Oman report progress on a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Reports claim Iran's president was given a 'final ultimatum' by Khamenei over resignation.
  • Iran's president denies threatening to resign during talks on the strait.

Talks Advance on Key Waterway

Iran and Oman have made headway in negotiations over reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. The development comes as regional tensions remain elevated, with both sides signalling a willingness to reach an agreement.

Diplomatic sources indicated that the talks, which have been ongoing for several weeks, have moved past preliminary stages. The specifics of the deal, including timelines and verification mechanisms, have not been publicly disclosed.

Leadership Tensions Surface

Amid the diplomatic progress, reports have emerged of internal friction within Iran's leadership. The Times of India cited a report claiming that Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei has issued a 'final ultimatum' to President Masoud Pezeshkian over his potential resignation.

Iran International reported that the president met Mojtaba Khamenei, the Supreme Leader's son, in the back seat of a car, a meeting that has fuelled speculation about the nature of the discussions. The Jerusalem Post also noted that Khamenei has warned Pezeshkian over resignation threats.

President Denies Resignation Threat

The Guardian, however, quotes Iran's president as denying that he threatened to resign during talks over the Strait of Hormuz. This contradiction highlights the uncertainty surrounding the internal political situation, with official channels offering little clarity.

Observers note that the timing of these reports, coinciding with sensitive negotiations, could complicate efforts to project a unified front. Analysts suggest that internal discord, if real, could weaken Tehran's bargaining position.

Global Implications

The Strait of Hormuz is a vital artery for roughly a fifth of the world's oil supply. Any prolonged disruption would have severe consequences for global energy markets, making the reopening deal a matter of international concern.

India, which relies heavily on Gulf oil imports, is closely watching the developments. A stable reopening would ease supply concerns, while any delay could push up fuel prices in the domestic market.

What to Watch

In the coming days, the focus will be on whether the reported ultimatum leads to any concrete political change in Tehran. The final shape of the reopening deal, including any role for international monitors, will also be a key indicator of the talks' success.

Neither Iranian nor Omani officials have confirmed a timeline for a formal agreement, but the reported progress suggests that a breakthrough could be within reach.
