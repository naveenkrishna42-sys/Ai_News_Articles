Indian copyright office rules AI can't be author

📅 2026-09-02 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • AI-generated works can be copyrighted under Indian law.
  • AI systems cannot be authors under Indian law.
  • The ruling may impact AI-generated art and content creation.

AI-Generated Works Can Be Copyrighted

The Indian Copyright Office has ruled that works generated by artificial intelligence can be copyrighted, provided they meet the necessary criteria. This means that artists and creators using AI tools to produce their work can still protect their intellectual property. The ruling is a significant development in the evolving landscape of AI and copyright law.

AI Systems Cannot Be Authors

However, the Copyright Office has made it clear that AI systems themselves cannot be considered authors. This distinction is crucial, as it sets a precedent for how AI-generated content is treated under the law. The ruling ensures that human creators retain the necessary authorship to claim copyright, even when their work is partially or fully generated by AI.

Broader Implications

The ruling by the Indian Copyright Office has broader implications for the AI industry and content creation. It provides a legal framework for artists and creators to protect their work, even as AI tools become more sophisticated. The distinction between AI-generated works and AI systems as authors is a key aspect of this ruling, and it may influence how copyright law is interpreted in other jurisdictions.

Legal Challenges and Future Developments

The ruling is not without its challenges. The distinction between AI-generated works and AI systems as authors may be subject to legal challenges, particularly as AI technology continues to evolve. The Indian Copyright Office's decision may also prompt further developments in copyright law, as the legal community grapples with the implications of AI on authorship and intellectual property.

Expert Opinions

Legal experts have weighed in on the Indian Copyright Office's ruling. They note that the distinction between AI-generated works and AI systems as authors is a significant development, as it provides a clear legal framework for the protection of intellectual property. However, they also caution that the ruling may not be without its complexities, particularly as AI technology continues to evolve.
