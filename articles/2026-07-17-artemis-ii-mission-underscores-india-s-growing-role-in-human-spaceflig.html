Artemis II mission underscores India's growing role in human spaceflight

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • Artemis II, NASA's first crewed lunar flyby since Apollo, is set for launch, highlighting complex human spaceflight engineering.
  • ISRO's Gaganyaan programme and its human rating of the LVM3 rocket show India's steady progress in manned missions.
  • Collaboration opportunities between NASA and ISRO on Artemis and beyond are being discussed at technical levels.

Artemis II prepares for lunar flyby

NASA's Artemis II mission is now less than a year away from its planned launch. The flight will carry four astronauts around the Moon and back, the first crewed mission to lunar orbit since Apollo 17 in 1972.

The mission puts a sharp focus on the engineering challenges of human spaceflight. Life support, radiation shielding, and crew safety systems are being tested to their limits. Every component must work perfectly for the astronauts to return safely.

ISRO's parallel path

Across the globe, the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is marking its own progress. The Gaganyaan programme, India's first human spaceflight initiative, has moved from design to hardware testing.

ISRO recently completed the human rating of its LVM3 launch vehicle. This certification means the rocket is now considered safe enough to carry a crew. The agency is also testing crew escape systems and life support modules at its facilities in Bengaluru and Sriharikota.

Engineers at ISRO have studied Artemis mission data closely. The lessons from NASA's work on heat shields, parachutes, and emergency abort systems are directly relevant to India's plans.

Collaboration opportunities

Officials from both space agencies have held technical exchanges in recent months. Areas of discussion include docking systems, astronaut training protocols, and deep space communication networks.

India is also a signatory to the Artemis Accords, the international framework for peaceful lunar exploration. This opens the door for Indian experiments and possibly Indian astronauts on future Artemis missions.

Engineering challenges remain

Both agencies face hard problems. The Artemis II capsule must protect its crew from deep space radiation for over a week. ISRO's Gaganyaan capsule, designed for lower orbits, will need upgrades if it is to go beyond Earth.

Thermal management is another hurdle. The spacecraft must handle temperature swings of over 200 degrees Celsius. Indian teams are developing multi-layer insulation and active cooling systems to tackle this.

The human factor is equally demanding. Astronauts need reliable food, water, and waste management for missions lasting days or weeks. ISRO has built a compact life support system that recycles air and water, a technology critical for longer flights.

What to watch next

Artemis II is expected to launch no earlier than late 2026, pending final safety reviews. ISRO plans its first uncrewed Gaganyaan test flight by mid-2026, with a crewed mission targeted for 2027.

The coming months will reveal how quickly India can close the gap with established spacefaring nations, and whether the two agencies can turn technical talks into joint missions beyond Earth's orbit.
