Bengaluru's voter recognition crisis: 54.87% unrecognised

📅 2026-08-25 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Only 54.87% of Bengaluru voters recognised in SIR Draft Roll
  • 45.13% marked ASDD, raising concerns about electoral integrity
  • Election Commission of India to verify the data

Bengaluru's Voter Recognition Crisis

Bengaluru's recent SIR Draft Roll has revealed a startling statistic: only 54.87% of the city's voters were recognised, with 45.13% marked as 'Addressed but Suspicious for Deletion and Discrepancy' (ASDD). This revelation has raised serious concerns about the integrity of the city's electoral process.

Understanding the SIR Draft Roll

The SIR Draft Roll is a crucial document in India's electoral process, prepared by the Election Commission of India (ECI). It lists all eligible voters, their addresses, and other relevant details. The recent data suggests a significant number of voters in Bengaluru may not be eligible to cast their ballots.

Why This Matters

This statistic is not just a technical glitch; it has significant implications for the upcoming elections. If a large number of eligible voters are being excluded, it could lead to a skewed representation of the city's demographics. This could potentially influence the outcome of the elections, as the voices of a significant portion of the population may not be heard.

Concerns About Electoral Integrity

The high number of voters marked ASDD has raised serious concerns about the integrity of the electoral process. It suggests a possible lapse in the voter identification process, which could be exploited by those with malicious intent. This could lead to a loss of public trust in the electoral system, which is the backbone of a democratic society.

Election Commission's Response

The Election Commission of India has acknowledged the issue and is currently verifying the data. However, the process could take time, and in the meantime, the city's electoral integrity remains at risk. This delay could potentially be used by political parties to spread misinformation and undermine the electoral process.

Broader Implications

This issue is not isolated to Bengaluru. Similar concerns have been raised in other cities as well. The ECI needs to address this issue promptly and transparently to restore public trust in the electoral process. This could involve a thorough review of the voter identification process, the introduction of stricter guidelines, and the implementation of technology to streamline the process.
