Samsung Galaxy Glasses launched at Unpacked event

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Samsung Galaxy Glasses revealed at Unpacked event
  • Designed in collaboration with Gentle Monster and Warby Parker
  • Integrates with Galaxy ecosystem, competing with Meta's smart glasses

Samsung has officially entered the smart eyewear race. At its Unpacked event on Thursday, the company revealed the Samsung Galaxy Glasses, a pair of smart spectacles designed to blend everyday style with digital functionality. The announcement marks Samsung's most ambitious push yet into wearable tech beyond watches and earbuds.

Design and collaborations

The Galaxy Glasses are not a solo effort. Samsung partnered with Gentle Monster, the South Korean eyewear brand known for bold fashion frames, and Warby Parker, the popular American online retailer. This dual approach suggests Samsung is targeting both style-conscious users and practical, everyday wearers.

Early hands-on reports from attendees describe the glasses as surprisingly lightweight, with frames that look indistinguishable from standard prescription eyewear. The Verge noted that the design is far more subtle than earlier smart glasses prototypes, with no obvious bulges or visible cameras from the front.

Features and ecosystem integration

The glasses are built to connect seamlessly with Samsung's Galaxy ecosystem. Users can make calls, listen to music, and access notifications through discreet speakers built into the temples. A small touchpad on the side allows for gesture controls, including swipes and taps to change tracks or answer calls.

CNBC reports that the device includes a built-in camera for capturing photos and short videos, activated by a long press. This puts it in direct competition with Meta's Ray-Ban Stories and the newer Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses. Samsung has not yet disclosed the camera's resolution or storage capacity.

Battery and charging

The Galaxy Glasses come with a charging case, similar to the approach used for true wireless earbuds. Samsung claims the case provides multiple full charges, though exact battery life figures for the glasses themselves have not been confirmed. Officials said more detailed specifications will be shared closer to the launch date.

Market positioning and competition

Samsung enters a market that Meta has been aggressively building over the past two years. Meta's Ray-Ban collaboration has seen steady sales, and the company recently added AI-powered features like real-time translation and object recognition. Samsung is expected to offer similar AI integration through its Galaxy AI platform, though the company did not detail any specific AI features at the event.

Analysts see the move as a strategic play to lock users into the Galaxy ecosystem. Smart glasses could serve as an always-on accessory that keeps users connected to Samsung phones, tablets, and smartwatches, creating a tighter product loop. Pricing has not been announced, but industry watchers expect the glasses to be priced competitively with Meta's offering, which starts around Rs 15,000.

GSMArena's eyes-on report highlighted the clarity of the audio and the comfort of the frames during extended wear. The publication noted that the glasses felt more polished than earlier iterations of smart eyewear from other brands.

Samsung has not yet announced an official release date for the Galaxy Glasses. The company is expected to share launch details and pricing in the coming weeks, as it prepares to ship the product before the end of the year. All eyes will be on how well the glasses integrate with the Galaxy ecosystem—and whether consumers are ready to wear a computer on their face.
