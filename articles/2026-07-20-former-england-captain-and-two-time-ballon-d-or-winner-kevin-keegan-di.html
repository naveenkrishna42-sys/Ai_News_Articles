Former England captain and two-time Ballon d'Or winner Kevin Keegan dies at 75

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kevin Keegan died at 75 after a battle with cancer.
  • He was a former England captain and two-time Ballon d'Or winner.
  • Tributes poured in from clubs including Liverpool, Man City, and Newcastle United.

Football legend Kevin Keegan passes away

Kevin Keegan, the former England captain and one of the most iconic figures in British football, has died at the age of 75. He passed away after a battle with cancer, according to reports from The Times of India and other outlets.

The two-time Ballon d'Or winner leaves behind a legacy as both a player and manager. Keegan's death has prompted an outpouring of grief from fans and clubs across the country.

A stellar playing career

Keegan rose to prominence at Liverpool, where he won three First Division titles and two UEFA Cups. His explosive pace and goal-scoring ability made him a fan favourite at Anfield.

He later played for Hamburg, Southampton, and Newcastle United. In 1978 and 1979, he won the Ballon d'Or, cementing his status as one of the world's best players. He also captained England 31 times, earning 63 caps in total.

Managerial highs and lows

After retiring as a player, Keegan managed Newcastle United, Fulham, and the England national team. He led Newcastle to two top-three finishes in the Premier League and was known for his attacking philosophy.

His tenure as England manager was shorter-lived, ending in 2000 after a disappointing Euro 2000 campaign. He later managed Manchester City, though results were mixed. Despite the uneven record, his passion for the game never dimmed.

Tributes from across the football world

Liverpool fans paid tribute to a legend who helped define an era. Manchester City supporters also remembered his time at the helm. Newcastle United issued a statement honouring his contributions both as a player and manager.

BBC Sport and Telegraph India reported on the widespread reaction. Many recalled his famous quote: “I would love it if we beat them,” said during a heated title race with Manchester United in 1996. The quote remains a staple of football culture.

Keegan's death marks the end of an era for English football. He was a symbol of determination and flair, a player who gave everything on the pitch.

What comes next

A memorial service is expected to be announced in the coming days. Clubs across the Premier League will likely hold a minute of silence in his honour. The football world will continue to reflect on the life of a man who inspired generations.
