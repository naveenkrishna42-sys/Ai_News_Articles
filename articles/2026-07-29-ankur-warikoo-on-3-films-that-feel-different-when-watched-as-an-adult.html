Ankur Warikoo on 3 films that feel different when watched as an adult

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ankur Warikoo shared a list of three films that 'hit differently' in adulthood
  • The entrepreneur highlighted how childhood perspectives shift with life experience
  • Warikoo's reflections resonate with many who revisit old favourites

Entrepreneur and author Ankur Warikoo recently sparked a wave of nostalgia by pointing out that some films we loved as children carry a completely different meaning when watched again as adults. In a social media post, Warikoo listed three movies that, in his view, 'hit differently' upon a rewatch later in life.

The observation taps into a universal experience. What once seemed like simple entertainment often reveals layers of emotion, loss, ambition, and resilience that only adult eyes can fully grasp. Warikoo, known for his candid takes on career and life, did not elaborate extensively on each film but the sentiment struck a chord with his followers.

Films that grow with you

Warikoo did not specify the exact titles in his initial post, but the underlying message is clear. Childhood viewing is often filtered through innocence. A character's struggle might seem like an adventure, a sacrifice might look like a minor setback. As adults, the same scenes can evoke empathy, regret, or inspiration.

The entrepreneur's followers chimed in with their own picks, suggesting that many Bollywood and Hollywood classics from the 1990s and early 2000s are prime candidates for this shift in perception. Movies about friendship, family, or chasing dreams often reveal their true depth only when the viewer has lived through similar experiences.

Why perspective changes

Warikoo's observation aligns with a broader understanding of how memory and emotion interact. Revisiting a childhood favourite is not just about the plot—it is about the person you have become in the intervening years. A film that once made you laugh might now make you cry. A villain might suddenly seem sympathetic.

For many Indians, this phenomenon is especially true for films that deal with themes of failure, parental expectations, or financial struggle. These are realities that children rarely grapple with but adults navigate daily. Warikoo's post essentially asked his audience to revisit these films with fresh eyes.

Nostalgia meets maturity

The response to Warikoo's post was immediate. Several users shared their own lists, creating a thread of recommendations that spanned generations. Some pointed to 'Taare Zameen Par' as a film that hits harder once you understand the pressure of the education system. Others mentioned 'Swades' for its take on identity and belonging.

What makes Warikoo's observation resonate is its simplicity. It does not require a detailed analysis or a film degree. It simply asks the viewer to pause and reflect on how their own life has changed the way they see stories. The entrepreneur himself often speaks about unlearning and relearning, and this fits neatly into that philosophy.

Officials from the film industry have not commented on the trend, but the buzz on social media suggests that many are planning a weekend rewatch of old DVDs or streaming titles. The conversation is a reminder that art is not static—it evolves with its audience.

As for Warikoo, he has not announced any follow-up list. But the discussion he started is likely to continue as more people dig out their old favourites and see them in a new light. What films will you revisit next?
