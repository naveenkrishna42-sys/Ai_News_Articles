Imran Khan’s Protest Participation Raises Legal Questions

UK-based actor Imran Khan, known for his work in British and international cinema, has found himself at the center of a legal debate after participating in the October 2026 Committee for Joint Action (CJP) protest in Mumbai. The demonstration, which focused on issues related to civil liberties and press freedom, drew significant attention when Khan was photographed among the demonstrators. His involvement has now triggered a review of his Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) card status, with several legal experts questioning whether foreign nationals on such permits are permitted to engage in politically charged activities within India.

What Are the OCI Rules on Political Activity?

The OCI scheme, governed by the Citizenship Act, 1955, grants foreign nationals of Indian origin a lifelong visa along with certain rights such as property ownership and visa-free travel. However, the rules explicitly restrict OCI cardholders from participating in political activities, including protests, rallies, or campaigns. According to Section 8(3) of the OCI Rules, 2006, any act deemed prejudicial to the sovereignty and integrity of India may lead to the cancellation of the OCI card. Legal experts interpret this clause broadly, often encompassing public demonstrations that touch on sensitive socio-political matters.

Implications for Foreign Nationals Holding OCI Cards

For individuals like Imran Khan, the stakes are significant. A violation could result in the cancellation of his OCI card, potentially affecting his ability to visit India freely and own property there. While enforcement of these rules varies, recent years have seen increased scrutiny of OCI holders engaging in public discourse, especially on contentious issues. Legal advisors recommend that OCI cardholders avoid participating in protests or political gatherings to maintain compliance with Indian immigration laws.

Broader Impact of the Mumbai CJP Protest

The October 2026 CJP protest in Mumbai was one of the largest demonstrations in recent months, with over 600 people detained and later released. Among those named in the FIR was veteran activist Tushar Gandhi, grandson of Mahatma Gandhi, highlighting the protest's resonance across civil society. The involvement of international figures like Imran Khan adds another layer of complexity, as it raises diplomatic and legal questions about the extent to which foreign nationals can influence domestic discourse through public demonstrations.

Public and Legal Reactions

Following the news of Khan’s potential OCI violation, reactions have been mixed. Some legal experts argue that participation in peaceful protests should not warrant punitive action unless directly linked to acts of violence or sedition. Others maintain that the OCI framework demands strict adherence to its terms, regardless of intent. As of now, no official statement has been issued by the Ministry of External Affairs or the Foreigners Division regarding Khan’s case. Meanwhile, advocacy groups continue to call for clearer guidelines on the rights and limitations of OCI cardholders in India.
