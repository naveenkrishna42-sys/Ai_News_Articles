Gulf shipping traffic dips after Houthis claim tanker attack

📅 2026-08-06 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Houthis claim attack on Saudi oil tanker in Red Sea and Gulf of Aden
  • Gulf shipping traffic has declined following the reported strike
  • Trump says Strait of Hormuz reopening 'soon'; crude oil prices under watch

Houthi claim rattles Red Sea shipping

Gulf shipping traffic has dropped noticeably after Yemen's Houthi rebels said they attacked a Saudi oil tanker in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden. The claim, reported by Reuters on August 5, 2026, has added fresh uncertainty to one of the world's busiest maritime corridors.

The Houthis have also threatened to expand their attacks beyond the Red Sea, raising concerns among shippers and insurers. Vessel tracking data suggests a slowdown in transits, though exact numbers have not been independently verified.

Trump's assurance on Hormuz

Former US President Donald Trump has said the Strait of Hormuz will reopen 'soon', offering a glimmer of reassurance to markets. The strait handles a significant share of global oil shipments, and any disruption tends to spike crude prices.

His comments, reported by CNN, come amid heightened tension in the region. However, no specific timeline has been provided, and officials have not yet confirmed any breakthrough in negotiations.

Oil markets on edge

Crude oil prices have been volatile since the attack claim surfaced, with traders weighing the risk of supply disruptions. The Red Sea route is critical for tankers moving between the Gulf and Europe, and any prolonged slowdown could tighten supplies.

Analysts point to the Houthis' stated intention to broaden their campaign as a key factor. The group has previously targeted Saudi infrastructure, but strikes on moving tankers mark an escalation that could have wider implications.

What is being monitored

Shipping companies are reportedly rerouting some vessels around the Cape of Good Hope, adding days to journeys and raising costs. Insurance premiums for Red Sea transits are also said to be climbing, though underwriters have not disclosed revised rates.

Saudi authorities have not yet issued a detailed statement on the alleged attack, and the extent of damage to the tanker remains unclear. Maritime security firms are urging caution in the region.

Regional fallout

The Houthi claim comes against a backdrop of ongoing regional tensions. The Gulf of Aden and Red Sea have seen sporadic incidents in recent years, but the latest development suggests a possible shift in tactics.

International naval forces operating in the area have not announced any new deployments. Yet, the drop in traffic indicates that commercial operators are not waiting for official guidance before adjusting their plans.

For now, the immediate focus is on whether the Houthis follow through on their threat to expand attacks. The coming days will show if shipping lanes stabilise or if the slowdown deepens.
