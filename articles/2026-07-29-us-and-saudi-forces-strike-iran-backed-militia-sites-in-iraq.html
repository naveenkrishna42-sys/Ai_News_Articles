US and Saudi forces strike Iran-backed militia sites in Iraq

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US and Saudi forces struck sites used by Iran-backed militias in Iraq
  • Iran's IRGC fired missiles at Jordan in response
  • Multiple casualties reported in the strikes as conflict escalates

The United States military has confirmed that American and Saudi forces carried out coordinated strikes on positions used by Iran-backed militias in Iraq. The operation marks a significant escalation in the widening West Asia conflict.

According to a statement from the US military, the strikes targeted facilities that were actively being used by Iranian proxy groups to launch attacks. The joint action with Saudi Arabia signals a deeper involvement of regional allies in the ongoing confrontation with Tehran.

Coordinated strikes across Iraqi territory

The strikes hit multiple locations across Iraq, though officials have not yet disclosed the exact number of targets or the full extent of damage. Initial reports suggest that the operation involved both air and ground-based assets.

Witnesses in several Iraqi provinces reported hearing loud explosions late Tuesday night. Local security sources confirmed that at least one ammunition depot and a command center were destroyed in the bombardment.

Iran retaliates with missile fire at Jordan

In a swift response, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) launched a barrage of missiles toward Jordan. The attack appears to be aimed at disrupting US and allied operations from Jordanian territory.

Jordanian air defense systems intercepted several of the incoming projectiles, but some landed near military installations near the border. Casualty figures from the missile strikes remain unconfirmed at this hour.

Regional escalation threatens wider war

The exchange of fire has raised fears of a broader regional war. The US-Saudi operation in Iraq represents the first time Riyadh has openly joined American forces in striking targets inside Iraqi territory.

Iran-backed militias in Iraq, which operate under the umbrella of the Popular Mobilization Forces, have vowed retaliation. In a statement, a spokesman for Kata'ib Hezbollah said the group would "respond with full force" to the attacks.

The strikes also threaten to destabilize Iraq's fragile political landscape. The Iraqi government has not yet issued an official response, but several lawmakers condemned the violation of national sovereignty.

Impact on global energy markets

News of the strikes sent oil prices surging in early Asian trading. Brent crude crossed the $95 per barrel mark as traders priced in the risk of supply disruptions from the Strait of Hormuz.

Reports indicate that Iran has also targeted commercial shipping near Hormuz, with at least two vessels reporting near misses from missile fire. The US Navy Fifth Fleet has increased patrols in the vital waterway.

The situation remains fluid, with diplomatic channels reportedly active but showing little progress. UN Secretary-General António Guterres has called for an immediate de-escalation, but neither Washington nor Tehran has signaled a willingness to step back.

What to watch: Whether Iraq's government can assert control over its territory and prevent its soil from becoming a permanent battleground between US-led and Iranian forces.
