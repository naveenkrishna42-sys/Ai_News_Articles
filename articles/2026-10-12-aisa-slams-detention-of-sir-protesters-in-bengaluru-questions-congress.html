AISA Condemns Detention of SIR Protesters in Bengaluru

The All India Students' Association (AISA) has strongly condemned the detention of students who staged a protest against the implementation of the Single National Internal Examination (SIR) system at Bangalore University. The protest, which took place on the university campus, turned tense after police intervened to disperse the demonstrators, leading to several arrests.

Protest Against SIR Implementation

The students, supported by AISA and other student groups, voiced their dissent against the newly introduced SIR model, arguing that it undermines the existing semester-wise internal evaluation system. They claimed the change was imposed without sufficient consultation with students or faculty, and that it reduces flexibility in academic performance assessment.

Police Action and Detentions

According to eyewitness accounts, police used baton charges and tear gas to disperse the crowd after the protesters attempted to march towards the university administrative block. AISA alleged that over 30 students were detained, including several female activists. The organization accused the state government of adopting an authoritarian approach reminiscent of BJP-ruled states.

Congress Government Under Scrutiny

AISA’s sharp critique raises questions about the Congress party’s commitment to protecting student rights, especially after coming to power in Karnataka on a platform of democratic values and youth empowerment. The group posed pointed questions about how the Congress differs from the BJP when it comes to suppressing dissent on university campuses.

Police Justification

In response, the Bengaluru Police stated that the protest was unauthorized and posed a threat to public order. A senior officer claimed that action was taken only after repeated warnings went unheeded. The police also denied using excessive force, asserting that baton charges were a standard protocol to control unruly mobs.

  • Police Response: Authorities justified the crackdown as necessary to maintain law and order.
  • Broader Implication: The incident highlights ongoing tensions between student bodies and state governments over educational reforms.
