Sensex drops 456 points, Nifty slips as bank stocks weigh on markets

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 456 points, Nifty slipped as banking stocks dragged.
  • Selling pressure was broad-based, with financials leading losses.
  • Analysts cite global cues and profit-booking as key factors.

Indian equity benchmarks ended lower on Friday, August 7, as selling pressure in banking and financial stocks pulled the markets down. The BSE Sensex fell 456 points, while the Nifty 50 also slipped, closing in the red for the session.

The decline was led by heavyweights in the banking sector, which dragged the indices despite some resilience in other pockets of the market. Traders cited weak global cues and profit-booking after a recent rally as key reasons for the downturn.

Banking stocks lead the slide

Banking and financial services shares were the biggest losers, with major lenders witnessing sharp cuts. The Nifty Bank index underperformed the broader market, as investors trimmed positions amid concerns over valuations and rising bond yields.

Private sector banks and public sector lenders both came under pressure, contributing significantly to the Sensex's fall. The weakness in banking stocks also weighed on other rate-sensitive sectors, including realty and auto.

Broader market shows mixed trend

While the headline indices fell, the broader market showed a mixed trend. Midcap and smallcap stocks were relatively resilient, with some counters managing to close in the green despite the overall negative sentiment.

However, the overall market breadth remained negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. Sectors like IT and FMCG provided some support, but it was not enough to offset the losses in banking.

Global cues and domestic factors

Weak global signals, including subdued Asian markets and uncertainty over US interest rates, added to the cautious mood. Investors also kept an eye on domestic inflation data and monsoon progress, which could influence the Reserve Bank of India's policy stance.

Analysts said the correction was largely profit-taking after recent gains, with no major negative domestic trigger. They noted that the markets could remain volatile in the near term, awaiting fresh cues from global and domestic data points.

What to watch next

Market participants will now focus on upcoming economic data, including industrial production and inflation numbers, due later this month. The movement of global oil prices and the dollar index will also be monitored for their impact on foreign fund flows.

With the earnings season largely behind, traders are likely to take cues from global markets and policy announcements. The direction of banking stocks will remain a key factor in determining the market's near-term trajectory.
