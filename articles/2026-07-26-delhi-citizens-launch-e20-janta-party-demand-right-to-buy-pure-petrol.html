Delhi Citizens Launch 'E20 Janta Party', Demand Right to Buy Pure Petrol

📅 2026-07-26 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Citizens launch 'E20 Janta Party' after the Cockroach Janta Party, demanding undiluted petrol.
  • Delhi taxi and transporter associations plan a march to Parliament on August 4.
  • Protesters reject mandatory E20 fuel, citing damage to older vehicles and engines.
  • The movement follows the exit of former Petroleum Minister Hardeep Singh Poonia (CJP).

New Delhi: A new citizen-led movement has emerged on the streets of the national capital. Borrowing a page from the theatrical politics of the 'Cockroach Janta Party', a group of commuters and vehicle owners has launched the 'E20 Janta Party'. Their singular demand: the right to buy 100 percent pure petrol.

The group is gearing up for a major protest on August 4. Delhi's taxi unions and transporters associations have announced a march to Parliament. They are demanding an urgent policy review of the government's mandatory ethanol blending program.

The E20 Mandate and Its Discontents

The central government has been pushing for a 20 percent ethanol blend in petrol, branded as E20 fuel. While the policy aims to reduce crude oil imports and cut vehicular emissions, a vocal section of users says it has caused unforeseen problems.

Drivers of older cars, two-wheelers, and commercial taxis claim E20 fuel leads to engine knocking, reduced mileage, and higher maintenance costs. A recent study by an automotive research body flagged that vehicles not designed for high ethanol content face corrosion and fuel system damage.

The 'E20 Janta Party' argues that the government should offer a choice. They want petrol pumps to stock both E20 and pure petrol, allowing consumers to decide based on their vehicle's compatibility.

Political Echoes: From Cockroach to E20

The name 'E20 Janta Party' draws inspiration from the 'Cockroach Janta Party', a satirical political outfit that recently gained attention for mocking the government's sanitation drives. Sources within the new group say the moniker is deliberate — to highlight what they see as a pestilent policy forced on the public.

Former Petroleum Minister Hardeep Singh Poonia had been the face of the ethanol push. After his recent exit from the ministry, the opposition has sharpened its attack. Transport unions now question whether his successor will continue the hardline stance on blending.

August 4: The March to Parliament

The Delhi Taxi Association has confirmed a large demonstration on August 4. They expect hundreds of cabs and auto-rickshaws to converge near Parliament House. The protest is likely to cause major traffic disruptions in central Delhi.

Organisers have submitted a memorandum to the Prime Minister's Office. Their key demands include a three-year moratorium on mandatory blending, compensation for vehicle repairs, and a transparent study on the long-term impact of E20 on Indian roads.

Several opposition MPs have extended support. They argue that the policy was rolled out without sufficient public consultation. The government, however, maintains that ethanol blending is critical for energy security and farmer incomes.

What Happens Next

The August 4 protest will test the government's resolve. With the transporters' lobby being a powerful vote bank in Delhi, the pressure is mounting.
All eyes are now on the new petroleum minister. A policy rollback or a phased implementation could be on the cards if the protest gathers steam.
