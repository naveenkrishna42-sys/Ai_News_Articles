Vivek Oberoi on Sonam Wangchuk hunger strike: 'Main abhineta hun, neta nahi'

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vivek Oberoi said 'Main abhineta hun, neta nahi' when asked about Sonam Wangchuk's hunger strike.
  • The actor has been criticised for dodging the question and for his past political statements.
  • Sonam Wangchuk's hunger strike continues, drawing attention to regional issues.

Bollywood actor Vivek Oberoi on Friday sidestepped a question about climate activist Sonam Wangchuk's ongoing hunger strike, saying he is an actor, not a politician. Oberoi, who was interacting with the media at a promotional event, was asked to share his thoughts on Wangchuk's protest.

"Yaar main abhineta hu, neta nahi hu. Toh main political cheezon pe dhyaan nahi deta hun," Oberoi replied, dismissing the query with a casual shrug. The response drew immediate criticism on social media, with many pointing out that Oberoi has not shied away from political issues in the past.

Actor vs Activist

Sonam Wangchuk, the Ladakh-based engineer and education reformer, has been on a hunger strike for over a week, demanding official recognition for his region's ecological concerns and greater autonomy for educational institutions. His protest has garnered support from various quarters, including celebrities and politicians.

Oberoi's refusal to comment comes at a time when several Bollywood figures have spoken out in support of Wangchuk. The actor's remark, "I am an actor, not a politician," was seen by critics as a convenient way to avoid taking a stand.

Past Political Stances

Oberoi's claim of staying away from politics does not align with his public record. In 2019, he openly endorsed Prime Minister Narendra Modi and campaigned for the Bharatiya Janata Party in the general elections. He also made headlines for his remarks on the Citizenship Amendment Act (CAA) and the National Register of Citizens (NRC), expressing support for the government's policies.

His recent comment, therefore, struck many as disingenuous. "Vivek Oberoi had no problem being political when it suited him. Now he is suddenly apolitical?" one user tweeted, echoing a sentiment that trended briefly on social media.

The Hunger Strike Continues

Meanwhile, Sonam Wangchuk remains on hunger strike, now in its ninth day. He has been camping at a protest site in Delhi, demanding that the government address his list of demands, which include a separate 35-acre land parcel for a university he founded and a special status for Ladakh to protect its fragile ecosystem.

Wangchuk's health has been a cause for concern, with medical teams monitoring his vitals. The protest has drawn attention to the region's unique challenges, including climate change and cultural preservation. Several student groups and environmental activists have joined the protest in solidarity.

Oberoi's non-answer has not changed the trajectory of Wangchuk's movement, but it has added a layer of debate about celebrity responsibility. When stars choose to speak on some issues and not others, the public notices.

What happens next depends on whether the government responds to Wangchuk's demands. For now, the activist's resolve remains unshaken — and the actor's evasion is a footnote in a larger story.
