Trump's $1.4 Billion Crypto Gain May Threaten His Own Bill

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump made $1.4 billion from crypto investments, creating a potential conflict of interest.
  • His proposed crypto bill faces scrutiny as critics say it benefits him personally.
  • The situation could stall or sink the legislation in Congress.

A Billion-Dollar Conflict

President Donald Trump has made $1.4 billion from cryptocurrency investments, according to financial disclosures. That windfall now threatens to derail his administration's biggest legislative priority on digital assets.

The proposed crypto bill, designed to regulate the industry, faces growing criticism from lawmakers who say Trump's personal stake creates an unavoidable conflict of interest. Critics argue the rules could be tailored to protect his holdings, not the public.

The Numbers Behind the Controversy

Trump's crypto portfolio includes major holdings in Bitcoin, Ethereum, and several altcoins. The $1.4 billion figure represents gains from purchases made before and during his presidency.

White House officials have not commented on whether Trump will divest his crypto assets or place them in a blind trust. Ethics experts say the lack of transparency undermines the bill's credibility.

Congressional opponents have already filed motions to delay the legislation. They want a full audit of Trump's crypto trades before any vote.

What the Bill Proposes

The draft legislation aims to create a federal framework for crypto exchanges, stablecoins, and decentralized finance. It includes consumer protections and anti-fraud measures.

However, industry insiders suggest certain provisions—like tax breaks for long-term holders—would disproportionately benefit wealthy investors like Trump. The bill's supporters deny any special treatment.

Political Fallout

Democrats have seized on the issue, accusing the president of using his office for personal enrichment. Even some Republican senators have expressed unease.

"We can't have the president writing rules that make him richer," said Senator Lisa Murkowski during a committee hearing. The White House dismissed the concerns as "baseless attacks."

Lobbying groups on both sides are mobilizing. Crypto advocates worry the controversy will kill the bill entirely, leaving the industry without clear regulation.

What Happens Next

The bill faces a critical vote in the Senate next month. If it stalls, Trump's $1.4 billion crypto empire may become the reason his own signature legislation collapses.
