DR Congo Ebola workers threaten strike over unpaid salaries

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Health workers in DR Congo have threatened to quit over unpaid salaries.
  • The Ebola outbreak has killed 1,700 people in eastern Congo.
  • Unpaid wages and deadly risk have pushed workers to the brink.
  • The outbreak is spreading faster than previous epidemics.

Unpaid wages push Ebola responders to the brink

Frontline health workers battling the Ebola outbreak in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo have threatened to walk off the job, citing months of unpaid salaries. The warning comes as the region struggles to contain what is already the deadliest outbreak in the country's history.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of workers affected or the total amount owed. But the threat has raised fears that a mass exodus of medical staff could cripple an already fragile response.

Deadly toll and a faster-spreading virus

The outbreak has now killed at least 1,700 people, according to reports from multiple news agencies. The virus, caused by the Bundibugyo virus strain, has spread across several provinces in eastern Congo, a region plagued by armed conflict and community mistrust.

Health experts say this outbreak is spreading faster than previous epidemics. The combination of dense population movement, ongoing violence, and weak healthcare infrastructure has made containment especially difficult.

Why health workers are quitting

For many frontline workers, the decision is one of survival. They face daily exposure to a virus with a high fatality rate, often without adequate protective equipment or hazard pay. Now, with salaries unpaid for months, many say they can no longer afford to continue.

Some workers have not received their wages for several months, according to reports. The financial strain has forced many to choose between their own livelihoods and the lives of the people they are trying to save.

A response on the brink

The threat of a mass resignation comes at a critical juncture. The World Health Organization and other international agencies have been working alongside Congolese health authorities to curb the outbreak. But without local staff on the ground, those efforts could stall.

Community engagement, contact tracing, and vaccination campaigns all depend on local health workers. If they walk away, the virus could spread unchecked into new areas, potentially overwhelming health systems across the region.

What happens next

The Congolese government and international partners have not yet issued a formal response to the workers' demands. Negotiations are reportedly underway, but no timeline has been announced for resolving the pay dispute.

In the coming weeks, all eyes will be on whether authorities can address the grievances quickly enough to prevent a full-scale collapse of the response. For now, the workers' threat remains a warning — one that could determine the trajectory of the outbreak.
