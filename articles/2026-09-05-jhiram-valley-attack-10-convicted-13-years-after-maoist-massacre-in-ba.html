Jhiram Valley Verdict: A 13-Year Wait Ends in Bastar

On September 5, 2026, an NIA Special Court in Chhattisgarh convicted 10 accused in connection with the Jhiram Valley massacre of May 25, 2013, a calculated ambush that killed 17 people, including senior Congress leader Mahendra Karma, former minister Uday Mudliyar, and several party workers and security personnel. The attack took place in the Darbha Valley region of Bastar, roughly 40 km from Jagdalpur, when Maoist cadres triggered an improvised explosive device and opened fire on a convoy returning from a Parivartan rally. The conviction marks the first major judicial closure in one of the deadliest political killings in independent India’s Maoist conflict.

According to NDTV and The Hindu, the NIA took over the probe from state agencies in 2014, filing a comprehensive chargesheet against 31 individuals, 10 of whom have now been found guilty. Charges were framed under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, the Indian Penal Code for murder and conspiracy, and the Explosive Substances Act. Sentencing is scheduled in subsequent hearings, while chargesheet proceedings against 15 absconding accused remain pending.

How the Attack Unfolded

The Congress convoy was returning from a Parivartan Yatra rally addressed by then-party president Sonia Gandhi and Rahul Gandhi. Maoist cadres detonated a landmine targeting the lead vehicle carrying Mahendra Karma, followed by indiscriminate gunfire. The blast destroyed the vehicle, and subsequent firing killed nearly the entire leadership of the Congress’s Bastar unit in a matter of minutes.

Why the Case Took 13 Years

Investigators cited extreme terrain, witness reluctance due to Maoist intimidation, and the sheer volume of digital and ballistic evidence as key delays. Forensic teams had to reconcile IED components recovered from the site with weapons traced to multiple Maoist divisions operating across Dandakaranya. The NIA also recorded depositions from surrendered Maoist cadres, which formed the evidentiary backbone of the prosecution case.

Reactions From Families of the Convicted

The Hindustan Times reported emotional scenes outside the court, with relatives of the convicted claiming that the accused were falsely implicated. One family member argued that her husband "didn’t know how to shoot" and had been working as a daily-wage labourer during the attack. Defence counsel indicated plans to challenge the verdict in the High Court, citing inconsistencies in witness identification and alleged procedural lapses during evidence collection.

Broader Implications for India’s Maoist Conflict

The Jhiram Valley verdict carries significant weight in the legal and security landscape of central India. It is among the largest convictions secured under UAPA for a Maoist-linked political killing, and it sets a precedent for prosecuting mass-casualty attacks orchestrated by banned organisations. Security analysts note that convictions of this scale, achieved more than a decade after the incident, reinforce the Centre’s policy of pursuing judicial outcomes alongside operations such as Operation Prahar.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Conviction milestone: First major verdict against senior Maoist operatives for a mass political killing in Chhattisgarh.
  • Pending trials: 15 absconding accused, including alleged masterminds, remain to be tried in absentia.
  • Political impact: Reinforces the legal accountability framework for Left-Wing Extremism under UAPA.
  • Witness protection gap: Long delays highlight vulnerabilities in securing testimony in conflict zones.
  • Legal precedent: May serve as a template for prosecuting other Bastar-era massacres such as the Sarkeguda and Burkapal incidents.
