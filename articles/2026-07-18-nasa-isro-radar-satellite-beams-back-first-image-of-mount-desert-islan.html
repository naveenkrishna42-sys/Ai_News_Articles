NASA-ISRO Radar Satellite Beams Back First Image of Mount Desert Island

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • NISAR satellite captured its first radar image of Mount Desert Island, Maine.
  • Joint mission between NASA and ISRO aims to monitor Earth's land and ice surfaces.
  • Image shows forests, wetlands, and urban areas in unprecedented detail.

A New Eye in the Sky

The NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar (NISAR) satellite has delivered its first radar image of Mount Desert Island off the coast of Maine, marking a significant milestone for the joint mission. The image, released on September 24, 2025, by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, showcases the island's rugged terrain, dense forests, and coastal wetlands with striking clarity.

NISAR, equipped with both L-band and S-band radar systems, can see through clouds and darkness. This capability makes it a powerful tool for monitoring changes in Earth's surface, from deforestation to glacial movement.

What the Image Reveals

The radar image of Mount Desert Island highlights the island's diverse geography. The bright areas indicate urban centers like Bar Harbor, while darker patches represent dense forest cover. Wetlands and water bodies appear in muted tones, reflecting their smoother surfaces.

Scientists at JPL noted that the image's resolution allows them to distinguish between different types of vegetation and even detect subtle changes in soil moisture. This level of detail is crucial for tracking environmental shifts over time.

How NISAR Works

NISAR uses synthetic aperture radar to bounce microwave signals off the Earth's surface. By measuring the time and strength of the returning signals, it builds a high-resolution map of the terrain. The satellite orbits Earth every 12 days, providing consistent coverage of the planet's land and ice sheets.

The mission is a collaboration between NASA and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Each agency contributed one of the two radar systems, with NASA providing the L-band and ISRO supplying the S-band. This dual-frequency approach gives scientists a more complete picture of surface structures and movements.

Implications for Science and Policy

NISAR's first image is more than a technical achievement. It demonstrates the satellite's potential to monitor environmental changes in real time. For regions like Mount Desert Island, which is part of Acadia National Park, the data can help track the impact of climate change on coastal ecosystems.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact timeline for when NISAR data will be available to the public. However, the mission aims to provide open access to its observations, enabling researchers worldwide to study land subsidence, ice dynamics, and natural hazards.

What Comes Next

The NISAR team plans to release more images in the coming weeks, covering a range of landscapes from the Amazon rainforest to the Himalayas. These will serve as calibration tests for the satellite's instruments.

The next major milestone will be the release of NISAR's first global map of Earth's land surfaces, expected within six months. Scientists are already planning studies on how the satellite's data can improve flood forecasting and earthquake response.
