‘Dowry Death a Serious Crime’: Court Denies Bail to Man, Mother

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Delhi court denied bail to a man and his mother accused of dowry death.
  • The court termed dowry death a 'serious social evil' and a crime against society.
  • The accused allegedly harassed the woman for additional dowry, leading to her death.

A Delhi court has denied bail to a man and his mother accused of causing the death of a woman over dowry demands. The court described the offence as a 'serious crime' and a 'social evil' that strikes at the foundation of marriage.

The accused, identified as the husband and mother-in-law, were arrested after the woman died under suspicious circumstances. Prosecutors argued that she was subjected to relentless harassment for additional dowry.

The court noted that granting bail in such cases would send a wrong message to society. It observed that dowry deaths are not merely personal disputes but crimes that undermine the institution of marriage.

Court’s Observations on Dowry Death

The judge remarked that dowry death is a 'serious social evil' that needs to be tackled with an iron hand. The court emphasised that such cases require a strict approach to deter others from similar conduct.

“The sanctity of marriage is being eroded by demands for dowry. This court cannot turn a blind eye to the suffering of women,” the judge stated in the order.

The court also pointed out that the evidence prima facie indicated the woman’s death was not accidental. It said the accused had a lot to answer during trial.

Prosecution’s Case

The prosecution told the court that the woman was married in 2022. Soon after the wedding, her in-laws began demanding a car and cash as additional dowry.

When her family failed to meet these demands, the harassment intensified. The woman was allegedly locked in a room and deprived of food. She died in July 2024.

The post-mortem report indicated injuries consistent with a struggle. The police filed charges under Section 304B (dowry death) and Section 498A (cruelty) of the Indian Penal Code.

Defence Arguments Rejected

The defence counsel argued that the woman died by suicide due to mental illness. They claimed there was no evidence of dowry demands or harassment.

The court rejected these arguments, saying the medical evidence and witness statements pointed to a different story. It noted that the woman’s family had testified about repeated demands for dowry.

The court also observed that the accused had not cooperated with the investigation. Their conduct after the woman’s death raised further suspicion.

The judge said the seriousness of the offence and the need for a fair trial outweighed the accused’s right to bail at this stage.

What Comes Next

The accused remain in judicial custody. The trial is expected to begin in the coming months. The court has directed the prosecution to produce all witnesses within six weeks.

This case adds to the growing list of dowry-related deaths in India, where thousands of women lose their lives every year to greed and patriarchy. The legal system continues to grapple with balancing the rights of the accused and the demand for justice for victims.

The next hearing is scheduled for October 2024, when the court will take up the framing of charges.
