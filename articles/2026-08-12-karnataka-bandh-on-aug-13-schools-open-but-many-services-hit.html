Karnataka bandh on Aug 13: Schools open, but many services hit

📅 2026-08-12 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Karnataka bandh called for August 13 over Cauvery water-sharing dispute.
  • Schools likely to remain open as Karnataka Associated Managements of Schools (KAMS) withdraws its closure call.
  • Essential services like hospitals and pharmacies to function; transport and commercial establishments may be affected.
  • The bandh has been called by pro-Kannada outfits and farmer groups.
  • Authorities have not yet confirmed the full impact on public transport and offices.

Bandh call and the Cauvery context

Karnataka is set to observe a statewide bandh on August 13, called by pro-Kannada outfits and farmer groups over the long-standing Cauvery water-sharing dispute with Tamil Nadu. The protest aims to pressure the state government to take a firm stand in the ongoing legal and political tussle over river water allocation.

The bandh call has received support from various political and social organisations, though the extent of the shutdown remains unclear. Authorities have not yet confirmed which services will be fully impacted, but the call has already led to confusion among residents and businesses.

Schools to remain open

In a significant relief for parents and students, schools across Karnataka are likely to function normally on August 13. The Karnataka Associated Managements of Schools (KAMS), which initially backed the bandh, has withdrawn its support, allowing educational institutions to operate as usual.

The decision comes after talks with the state government, which assured school managements of adequate security. Officials have urged parents to check with their respective schools for any last-minute changes, though no widespread closures are expected.

What will remain open?

Essential services are expected to function despite the bandh. Hospitals, pharmacies, and emergency medical services will operate round the clock, as per standard practice during such shutdowns. Milk booths, vegetable markets, and ration shops may also open in several areas, depending on local conditions.

Government offices, banks, and courts are likely to function, though attendance may be thin. The state government has not declared a holiday, and employees have been advised to report to work. However, some district administrations may choose to remain partially closed, especially in regions with a strong bandh presence.

Services likely to be affected

Public transport, including buses and auto-rickshaws, is expected to be hit hard. The bandh supporters have indicated they will block roads and stage protests at major junctions, which could disrupt commuters across Bengaluru and other cities. Private bus operators and cab services may also stay off the roads, citing safety concerns.

Commercial establishments, including malls, shops, and restaurants, are likely to remain shut in many areas, though this may vary by locality. The hospitality sector, particularly hotels and eateries, may see reduced operations. Online delivery services, including food and e-commerce, could face delays due to the lack of transport and potential protests.

What to watch for

As the bandh unfolds, all eyes will be on Bengaluru, where tech parks and IT firms may see reduced attendance. Many companies have already asked employees to work from home or come prepared for disruptions. The state government has deployed additional police forces to prevent any untoward incidents.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the bandh will lead to any shutdown of the Bengaluru Metro, though suburban rail services are expected to run. Commuters are advised to plan their travel accordingly and stay updated on local news.

The coming hours will determine the actual impact of the bandh. With schools open and essential services functioning, the shutdown may be partial, but the Cauvery issue is unlikely to be resolved soon. Watch for any further statements from the state government or the organisers, which could alter the situation.
