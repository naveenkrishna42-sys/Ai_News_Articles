Early detection and robotic surgery reshape prostate cancer treatment, says expert

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Prostate cancer cases are increasing beyond metro cities in India.
  • Half of patients are diagnosed at a late stage, reducing treatment options.
  • Robotic surgery offers precision and faster recovery for prostate cancer patients.
  • Common myths and lack of awareness delay diagnosis in Indian men.

Prostate cancer on the rise beyond metros

Prostate cancer is no longer a disease confined to India’s metropolitan cities. Experts report a steady increase in cases from smaller towns and rural areas, driven by better diagnostic access and changing lifestyles. However, awareness remains a major hurdle.

According to urologist Dr Sashiraj Singh, half of all prostate cancer patients in India are diagnosed too late. This significantly limits treatment options and reduces survival rates. The doctor emphasises that early detection can dramatically change outcomes.

Robotic surgery: a precision tool

Robotic-assisted surgery is emerging as a powerful weapon in the fight against prostate cancer. The technology allows surgeons to remove cancerous tissue with greater accuracy, preserving surrounding nerves and muscles. This leads to fewer side effects like incontinence and erectile dysfunction.

Patients who undergo robotic surgery often experience shorter hospital stays and faster recovery times. Dr Singh notes that the procedure is becoming more available in Indian hospitals, though cost and infrastructure remain barriers for many.

Three everyday symptoms men ignore

Dr Sashiraj Singh lists three common symptoms that men often dismiss: frequent urination, especially at night; a weak or interrupted urine stream; and blood in urine or semen. These signs can indicate prostate issues, including cancer.

The doctor advises men over 50, or those with a family history of prostate cancer, to seek medical advice promptly if they notice any of these symptoms. Early screening through a simple blood test for prostate-specific antigen (PSA) can catch the disease before it spreads.

Myths that delay diagnosis

Several myths prevent Indian men from seeking timely care. A common belief is that prostate cancer only affects older men, but cases are now being seen in men in their 40s and 50s. Another myth is that treatment always leads to impotence or incontinence, deterring many from getting checked.

Some men also fear that a PSA test is painful or unnecessary. Doctors stress that these misconceptions are costing lives. Public health campaigns and community outreach are needed to break these taboos and encourage regular screenings.

What happens next

With prostate cancer cases rising across India, health authorities are likely to push for wider screening programs and greater access to robotic surgery. Awareness drives targeting rural and semi-urban areas will be critical to catching the disease early and improving survival rates.
