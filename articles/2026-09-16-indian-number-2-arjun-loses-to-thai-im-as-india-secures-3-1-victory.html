Arjun’s Shock Upset and India’s Overall Win

In day‑two play at the 2026 FIDE Chess Olympiad, India’s number‑two player, Adhish Thirunavukkarasu Arjun (2258 ELO), was defeated by a Thai International Master in a 3‑1 match, a result that surprised commentators and fans alike. The loss came after India’s top trio—Gukesh, Erigaisi, and Arjun—had delivered a dominant 3‑1 overall result against Thailand, wiping out a potential ripple effect on the team’s standings.

What the Scores Reveal

While the Indian men ticketed a 3‑1 win against Thailand, the decisive board included a 1‑2 Ø by the Thai IM in the first game, followed by back‑to‑back losses for India’s top two at the board. The scoreline reflects a rating gap of roughly 600 points: Arjun (2260) versus a Thai 2050 IM. In the fourth game, Arjun managed a draw, but the three‑point deficit proved decisive.

Thai IM – A Rising Contender

The Thai International Master (rating 2054) is one of the fastest‑rising talents from Bangkok’s chess scene. He has repeatedly outperformed lower‑rated foreign opponents in the past season and is now considered a threat to the traditional home‑grown treasurers. His play in the first round came with 3–2 in his favour in the tournament pool, proving his ability to create problems for stronger pairs.

Women’s Sweep: A Big Boost

While the men’s result captured headlines, India’s women swept through the Dominican Republic with a 5‑0 score. Their flawless run secured a 7‑0 win in a round versus a European field, strengthening team’s overall points tally and positioning India at the top of the award table for best team performance.

The result also opens the door for other reserve players to demonstrate their resilience in high‑pressure matches.
