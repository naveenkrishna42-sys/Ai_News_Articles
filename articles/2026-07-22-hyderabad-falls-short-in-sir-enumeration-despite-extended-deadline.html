Hyderabad Falls Short in SIR Enumeration Despite Extended Deadline

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hyderabad has only completed 60% of SIR enumeration despite a two-month deadline extension.
  • The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) cites manpower shortages and resident unavailability as key challenges.
  • The SIR database is critical for property tax assessment and urban planning.

Hyderabad is falling behind in the Systematic Identification and Registration (SIR) of properties, even after the state government extended the deadline by two months. Officials from the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) report that only about 60% of the targeted properties have been enumerated so far. The original deadline was set for June-end, but the state pushed it to August 31 to allow more time for the exercise.

Ground-Level Hurdles

GHMC field staff cite several reasons for the slow pace. Many residents are not at home during working hours, making it hard to verify occupancy and collect details. In some areas, property owners are reluctant to share documents due to privacy concerns.

Manpower shortages have also hit the enumeration drive. The corporation deployed around 3,000 personnel for the task, but the sheer volume of properties—over 18 lakh in the city—has stretched resources thin. "We are trying to cover as many as possible, but it is a slow process," a GHMC official said, speaking on condition of anonymity.

What Is SIR and Why It Matters

The SIR process involves collecting detailed data on every property, including ownership, built-up area, land use, and tax history. This information feeds into a centralised database that will be used to rationalise property taxes and plug revenue leaks.

Hyderabad's property tax collection has been inconsistent, with many properties either under-assessed or not on the tax rolls at all. The SIR database is expected to give the GHMC a clearer picture of the city's real estate, helping it levy taxes more accurately and plan for infrastructure needs.

Extension Hasn't Helped Much

When the state government announced the extension in June, officials hoped it would give enough time to complete the enumeration. But the pace has not picked up significantly. In some zones, particularly the Old City, the exercise has been especially slow due to narrow lanes and dense construction.

Residents in these areas complain that surveyors are not visiting regularly. "They came once, asked a few questions, and left. I haven't seen them since," said a resident of Hussaini Alam. GHMC officials acknowledge the issue and say they are trying to improve coverage by deploying additional teams to such pockets.

The corporation has also run into technical glitches with the mobile app used for data entry. Field staff report that the app sometimes crashes or fails to sync data, forcing them to re-enter information. This has added to the delays.

What Happens Next

The GHMC is now considering a fresh drive to cover the remaining properties, possibly involving weekend surveys and evening visits to catch residents at home. The state government has warned that failure to complete the enumeration on time could affect the next property tax revision cycle.

With the August 31 deadline now a little over a month away, the corporation has its work cut out. Whether it can pick up the pace and meet the target remains to be seen.
