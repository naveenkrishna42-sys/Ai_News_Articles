International Moon Day 2026: India joins global celebration of lunar exploration

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • International Moon Day was observed on July 20, marking the anniversary of the first human Moon landing.
  • Events in India included exhibitions at BITM in Kolkata and activities at other science centres.
  • The day focused on past achievements and future goals in lunar exploration, including India's upcoming missions.

India marks International Moon Day with exhibitions and talks

Science centres and planetariums across India observed International Moon Day on July 20, joining a global celebration of lunar exploration. The day marks the anniversary of the first human landing on the Moon in 1969.

In Kolkata, the Birla Industrial and Technological Museum (BITM) hosted a special programme. Visitors explored model displays and attended lectures on India's lunar missions. The event aimed to spark interest in space science among students and the general public.

Global events honour past missions

Internationally, the day was marked by events ranging from NASA's public programmes to smaller community gatherings. The United Nations recognised the day to promote sustainable lunar exploration and international cooperation.

In China, state media highlighted the country's lunar ambitions. A China Daily article described Moon Day as a moment to "chase dreams" and celebrate progress. The Chinese space agency shared updates on its Chang'e programme, which has successfully landed rovers on the Moon.

In the United States, groups such as Soldiers' Angels used the occasion to honour military veterans who contributed to the Apollo programme. The University of Colorado Boulder's Fiske Planetarium also held a public viewing night with telescopes and talks.

Why International Moon Day matters

International Moon Day was established by the UN General Assembly in 2021. It falls on July 20 each year, the anniversary of Apollo 11's lunar landing in 1969. The day is intended to educate the public about lunar science and inspire the next generation of explorers.

For India, the day carries special significance. The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has successfully placed two missions on the Moon: Chandrayaan-1 in 2008 and Chandrayaan-3 in 2023. The latter made India the fourth country to achieve a soft landing on the lunar surface.

Looking ahead: India's lunar future

ISRO is preparing for Chandrayaan-4, a sample return mission expected in the coming years. The agency is also collaborating with Japan on the LUPEX mission to explore the Moon's south pole. These projects are part of a broader push to establish a sustained human presence on the Moon.

As nations race to return astronauts to the lunar surface, India's role in space exploration continues to grow. The coming decade will likely see more international partnerships and ambitious missions aimed at unlocking the Moon's secrets.
