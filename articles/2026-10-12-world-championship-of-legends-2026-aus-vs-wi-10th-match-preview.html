Likely match-ups: how veterans can swing phases

Powerplay (overs 1–6): Look for whether Australia’s top order prioritises wicket preservation or early boundary density. West Indies’ best spells usually start by attacking stumps and forcing batters into uncomfortable cut/inside-edge decisions.

Middle overs (7–15): Legends games often feature “managed aggression”. The key is whether Australia’s batters rotate strike effectively against seam/swing variations, especially when the ball becomes easier to hit but harder to time perfectly. For West Indies, the challenge is keeping line consistent while still creating enough dot balls to build pressure.

Death overs (final phase): If a team holds its nerve, totals become chaseable even when scoring slows. The difference typically shows up in economy and wicket timing: one wicket at the wrong moment can trigger a rapid collapse, while a well-timed breakthrough can freeze batters into singles.
