ISRO Releases First NISAR S-Band Images, Reveals Antarctic ‘Hummingbird’

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • ISRO shared the first NISAR S-band SAR data through its Bhoonidhi portal.
  • The images include a radar view of an Antarctic ice feature nicknamed the 'Hummingbird'.
  • NISAR is a joint US-India mission to monitor Earth's land and ice surfaces.
  • The S-band radar can see through clouds and darkness, aiding year-round observation.

First Look at NISAR's S-Band Radar Data

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has released the first S-band synthetic aperture radar (SAR) images captured by the NISAR satellite. The data is now publicly accessible through ISRO's Bhoonidhi portal, marking a significant step in the joint US-India Earth observation mission.

NISAR, short for NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar, launched in early 2025. It carries both an L-band radar from NASA and an S-band radar from ISRO. Until now, only L-band imagery had been shared.

An Icy 'Hummingbird' in Antarctica

Among the released images is a striking radar view of an ice formation in Antarctica that scientists have nicknamed the 'Hummingbird'. The feature, captured by the S-band radar, shows a symmetrical pattern of ice and crevasses that resembles the bird in flight.

The image was taken over the Getz Ice Shelf in West Antarctica. Researchers at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) noted that the radar's ability to penetrate ice and reveal subsurface structures made the feature visible. The hummingbird shape is the result of ice flow dynamics and underlying bedrock topography.

Why the S-Band Matters

The S-band radar operates at a wavelength of about 10 centimetres. It can see through clouds, smoke, and darkness, making it ideal for monitoring polar regions where cloud cover is persistent. The data will help scientists track ice sheet movement, glacial melt, and changes in sea ice extent throughout the year.

ISRO officials said the S-band payload will also be used for agricultural monitoring, disaster management, and mapping of surface deformation. The combination of L-band and S-band data gives researchers a more complete picture of Earth's changing surface.

Public Data Through Bhoonidhi

ISRO has begun releasing NISAR data through its Bhoonidhi portal, a platform that provides free access to satellite imagery and products. Users can download S-band data products for scientific research and applications.

The first batch covers several regions including Antarctica, parts of the Himalayas, and agricultural zones in India. More data releases are planned as the satellite continues its five-year mission.

Collaboration Across Continents

NISAR is a landmark collaboration between ISRO and NASA. The satellite orbits Earth every 12 days, scanning the entire land and ice-covered surface. Its data will be used to study earthquakes, landslides, volcanic activity, and changes in forests and wetlands.

The US and Indian space agencies have committed to making the data freely available to the global scientific community. The release of S-band imagery fulfils a key milestone in that promise.

The 'hummingbird' image has already generated interest among glaciologists. It offers a rare glimpse into the intricate patterns carved by moving ice. Scientists hope further analysis will reveal how the Getz Ice Shelf is responding to warming ocean waters.

ISRO has not yet set a schedule for the next batch of S-band data. But with the portal now active, researchers around the world can begin downloading and analysing the first publicly available images from India's radar payload.
