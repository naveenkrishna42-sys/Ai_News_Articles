Venezuela confirms 3 hantavirus deaths, 2 more suspected

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Three confirmed deaths from hantavirus in Venezuela
  • Two more deaths are suspected to be linked to the virus
  • Hantavirus is a rodent-borne disease that can affect the brain and other organs

Outbreak Confirmed

Venezuelan health authorities have confirmed that a hantavirus outbreak has killed at least three people. Officials are investigating two additional deaths suspected to be linked to the same virus.

The announcement comes as health workers in affected regions step up surveillance and public awareness campaigns. The virus, carried primarily by rodents, can cause severe respiratory and neurological complications.

What is Hantavirus?

Hantavirus is a zoonotic disease transmitted through contact with rodent urine, droppings, or saliva. Infection can occur when contaminated dust is inhaled, or through direct contact with broken skin.

The disease can progress into Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome (HPS), which affects the lungs, or Haemorrhagic Fever with Renal Syndrome (HFRS). In some cases, the virus can also affect the brain, leading to neurological symptoms.

Early symptoms include fever, muscle aches, fatigue, and dizziness. As the illness progresses, patients may experience coughing and shortness of breath. The incubation period typically ranges from one to eight weeks.

Response and Investigation

Venezuelan health officials have not yet disclosed the exact locations of the confirmed cases. Investigations are underway to determine the source of the outbreak and to identify any additional cases.

Local medical teams have been dispatched to affected areas to conduct testing and provide treatment. Public health messaging is focusing on rodent control and avoiding contact with rodent habitats.

The two suspected deaths are being examined to confirm whether hantavirus was the cause. Officials have urged residents to report any symptoms and to seek medical attention immediately if they suspect exposure.

Regional Context

Hantavirus outbreaks are rare in South America but have been documented in several countries, including Argentina, Chile, and Brazil. Venezuela has not reported a significant outbreak in recent years, making this cluster noteworthy.

Experts note that environmental factors, such as increased rodent populations due to seasonal changes or habitat disruption, can contribute to the spread of the virus. Health authorities are monitoring the situation closely.

What to Watch

Officials are expected to release more details as the investigation progresses. The focus will remain on containment and preventing further transmission. Residents in affected areas are advised to take precautions against rodent exposure.
