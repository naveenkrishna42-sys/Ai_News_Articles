Banks Anticipate 50 bps Funding Cost Cut Amid Liquidity Surge

Key points
  • Liquidity surge lets banks shave up to 50 bps off funding costs.
  • Lower funding costs translate into modest cuts to credit‑card APRs.
  • Cashback programmes may tighten as banks balance margin pressure.

Why Funding Costs Are Falling

Recent RBI data shows a 12% rise in net banking system liquidity since June 2026. The surge stems from higher corporate deposits, a slowdown in inter‑bank borrowing, and the RBI’s open‑market operations that have injected excess reserves.

With more cheap money available, banks’ cost of funds—measured by the weighted average cost of capital (WACC)—has slipped. Industry analysts estimate a reduction of 40‑50 basis points (bps) for major lenders.

Direct Impact on Credit‑Card Pricing

Credit‑card interest rates in India typically track banks’ funding costs plus a risk premium. A 50 bps dip in the underlying cost gives issuers room to trim annual percentage rates (APRs) by roughly 0.3‑0.5% for standard cards.

However, the benefit is not uniform. Premium cards that bundle travel insurance and lounge access retain higher APRs to cover ancillary services, while mass‑market cards see the most noticeable reductions.

What Consumers Should Look For

Lower APRs improve the effective cost of carrying a balance, but only if you maintain a revolving balance. For pay‑in‑full users, the real value lies in how banks adjust annual fees and cashback structures.

Many issuers are expected to offset margin pressure by tightening cashback caps or introducing tiered spend thresholds. Scrutinise the fine print: a 1.5% cashback that applies only after ₹30,000 monthly spend may be less attractive than a flat 1% on all purchases.

1. Evaluate the Net Effective Rate

Calculate the net cost by combining APR, annual fee, and cashback. For example, a card with a 13.5% APR, ₹500 annual fee, and 1% cashback on ₹20,000 monthly spend yields an effective rate of roughly 12.8%.

2. Watch for Introductory Offers

Some banks launch limited‑time 0% APR periods to attract new users. These promos often coincide with the funding cost dip, but they may revert to higher rates after six months.

Key Real‑World Highlights

  • Liquidity surge provides banks a rare cost‑saving window.
  • Standard credit‑card APRs could fall by up to 0.5%.
  • Cashback percentages remain stable; caps may tighten.
  • Annual fees are likely to stay flat or see modest reductions.
  • Consumers should compute net effective rates before switching cards.
