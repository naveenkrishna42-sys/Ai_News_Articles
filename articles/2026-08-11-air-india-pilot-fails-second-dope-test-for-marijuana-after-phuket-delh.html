Air India pilot fails second dope test for marijuana after Phuket-Delhi scare

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Captain's second dope test confirms marijuana use
  • Flight suffered rare flight-control event and 300-foot altitude drop
  • Government orders AAIB investigation into the incident

Confirmatory test confirms initial finding

The captain of the Air India flight that plunged 300 feet over the Bay of Bengal has failed a second, confirmatory dope test for marijuana, according to sources. The initial screening test had already flagged the substance, and the follow-up lab analysis has now upheld that result.

The pilot, whose identity has not been officially released, was operating the Phuket-Delhi service when the aircraft experienced a rare flight-control event. The incident led to a sudden altitude drop that alarmed passengers and crew, though the plane landed safely at Delhi airport.

Flight-control event under scrutiny

Details of what exactly went wrong in the cockpit remain sketchy. The Hindu reported that the flight suffered a rare flight-control event before the 300-foot descent, a phrase that aviation experts say points to something beyond routine turbulence or pilot error. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has already de-rostered the captain, and the co-pilot has been taken off flying duties pending investigation.

The government has now asked the Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB) to conduct a formal probe into the incident. This move signals that the authorities are treating the matter with exceptional seriousness, given that a pilot testing positive for a banned substance raises questions about fitness and decision-making at a critical moment.

What the dope test means for the pilot

Under Indian civil aviation rules, any crew member found to have consumed a prohibited substance can face suspension of licence, fines, and even criminal proceedings. The confirmatory test result essentially removes any ambiguity about the initial screening, which had already triggered an automatic grounding.

Sources told The Times of India that the captain's confirmatory test also came back positive for marijuana, corroborating the first result. The airline has not issued a detailed statement beyond confirming that the pilot has been removed from all duties and that it is cooperating with investigators.

Airline's response and industry impact

Air India has so far declined to comment on the specific findings, citing the ongoing investigation. However, the incident has put the spotlight on cockpit safety protocols and substance-abuse monitoring in Indian aviation. Airlines typically conduct random dope tests, but this case is likely to trigger a review of how frequently and rigorously such checks are carried out.

Industry observers note that a single lapse, if proven, can tarnish the reputation of an entire carrier. Air India, which has been undergoing a major overhaul since its privatisation, can ill afford a scandal of this nature. The airline will now have to demonstrate that it has robust systems in place to prevent a recurrence.

What happens next

The AAIB investigation will examine the flight data recorder and cockpit voice recorder to establish the sequence of events on that day. The pilot's medical records, training history, and any prior disciplinary actions will also be reviewed.

For now, the focus remains on whether the marijuana use contributed to the flight-control event or was merely a coincidence. The aviation regulator is expected to take a strict view, and the pilot's licence could be suspended or revoked if the probe establishes a direct link. The industry will be watching closely, as this case could set a precedent for how India handles substance abuse among flight crew.
