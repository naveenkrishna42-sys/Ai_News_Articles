Beyond Frontiers Trust launched in Chennai to aid marginalised communities

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Beyond Frontiers Trust was officially launched in Chennai
  • The trust will focus on education, skill development and livelihood support
  • Initial programmes will target underserved neighbourhoods in the city

Chennai has a new non-profit organisation. Beyond Frontiers Trust was formally launched in the city on Thursday, with a stated mission to extend support to marginalised communities across the region. The initiative brings together professionals from education, social work and corporate backgrounds, signalling a multi-pronged approach to community development.

The trust's founding members said the organisation will begin with a focus on education, skill development and livelihood generation. Rather than a single flagship project, the group plans to run several small, community-specific interventions that can adapt as needs evolve.

Grassroots-first approach

Speaking at the launch, the trustees emphasised that their work will be rooted in direct engagement with local communities. The initial phase will involve mapping the requirements of underserved neighbourhoods in Chennai, followed by tailored programmes in spoken English, digital literacy and vocational training.

"We are not here to impose solutions," one trustee said. "We want to listen first, understand what people actually need, and then build programmes with them, not for them." The trust has also indicated it will collaborate with existing NGOs and government schools rather than duplicate services already available.

Skill development at the core

A significant portion of the trust's early work will centre on employability. Plans include short-term certification courses in fields such as retail, hospitality and basic computer applications, aimed at youth who have dropped out of formal education. The trust will also run mentorship circles where working professionals guide young adults through resume writing, interview preparation and workplace etiquette.

For women in low-income households, the trust is exploring home-based income generation models, including tailoring cooperatives and food-processing units. The organisers believe that sustainable change requires economic independence, not just charitable handouts.

Governance and transparency

Beyond Frontiers Trust has registered under the Indian Trusts Act and has committed to publishing annual impact reports. A board of advisors, comprising academics and industry leaders, will review the trust's work every quarter to ensure accountability.

The trust has also said it will keep administrative costs below 15 per cent of its annual budget, with the remainder going directly to field programmes. This pledge is aimed at attracting donors who are increasingly wary of overhead-heavy charities.

Funding for the initial phase has come from private contributions and a small corpus set aside by the founding members. The trust is now in talks with corporate partners for CSR support, though no agreements have been finalised yet.

What lies ahead

The next few months will be crucial as the trust hires its first batch of field coordinators and begins community outreach in two identified zones in northern Chennai. A formal project timeline and budget allocation are expected to be released before the end of the year, the trustees said.

For now, Beyond Frontiers Trust is a small ship setting sail. How well it navigates the complex waters of urban poverty will depend on its ability to stay nimble, listen closely and deliver measurable outcomes.
