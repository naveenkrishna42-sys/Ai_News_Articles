US H-1B fee hike: Impact on Indian IT workforce

📅 2026-08-27 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US proposes $100k H-1B visa fee, likely to take effect in 2027
  • Indian IT firms and workers face potential financial and operational challenges
  • NASSCOM criticizes the fee proposal, calling it a 'short-term fix'

US Proposes $100k H-1B Visa Fee

The US Department of Homeland Security (DHS) has announced plans to impose a $103,265 fee for H-1B visas, a move that could significantly impact Indian IT workers and firms. The proposal, which is part of a broader immigration reform package, is expected to take effect in 2027. The fee is intended to reduce the number of H-1B visas issued each year, which has been a contentious issue in recent years.

Impact on Indian IT Workers

Indian IT workers, who form a significant portion of the H-1B visa holders, are likely to be affected by the fee hike. The fee is significantly higher than the current $1,000 annual fee, and it could make it more difficult for Indian workers to obtain H-1B visas. This could lead to a shortage of skilled workers in the US, particularly in the IT sector, which is already facing a labor shortage.

Financial Impact

The fee hike could also have a financial impact on Indian IT firms. These firms often rely on H-1B visas to hire skilled workers, and the fee could increase their hiring costs. This could make it more difficult for them to compete with other firms, particularly those based in countries with lower visa fees.

Operational Impact

In addition to the financial impact, the fee hike could also have operational impacts on Indian IT firms. These firms may need to adjust their hiring strategies, potentially increasing the use of other visa categories or looking to hire locally. This could lead to a shift in the geographic distribution of IT work, with some jobs moving to other countries.

NASSCOM's Response

The National Association of Software and Services Companies (NASSCOM), a leading industry body representing Indian IT firms, has strongly criticized the fee proposal. In a statement, NASSCOM called the fee a 'short-term fix' and argued that it would not address the root causes of the H-1B visa shortage. NASSCOM also expressed concern that the fee could lead to a brain drain from the US, as skilled workers may choose to stay in India rather than take the risk of obtaining an H-1B visa.

Broader Implications

The proposed H-1B fee hike is part of a broader immigration reform package that the Trump administration has been pushing for. The package includes a number of other proposals, such as increasing the number of visas available for skilled workers and making it easier for employers to transfer workers between companies. The outcome of these proposals is uncertain, and it is unclear whether the fee hike will be implemented as currently proposed.

Global IT Workforce

The H-1B visa program is a key part of the global IT workforce. Indian workers, in particular, have been a significant component of this workforce, providing skilled labor for US IT firms. The proposed fee hike could disrupt this dynamic, potentially leading to a shift in the geographic distribution of IT work.

US Labor Market

The proposed fee hike could also have implications for the US labor market. The H-1B visa program is a key source of skilled labor for the US economy, particularly in the IT sector. A reduction in the number of H-1B visas could lead to a shortage of skilled workers, potentially driving up wages and making it more difficult for firms to find and hire workers.
