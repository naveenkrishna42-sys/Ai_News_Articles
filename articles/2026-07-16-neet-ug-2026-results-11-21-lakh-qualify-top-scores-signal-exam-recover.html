NEET-UG 2026 results: 11.21 lakh qualify, top scores signal exam recovery

📅 2026-07-16 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • 11.21 lakh candidates qualify for medical admissions out of over 24 lakh who appeared
  • Only 19 candidates scored above 700, with two achieving 715 marks
  • Punjab's Aryan Gupta and Haryana's Pranshul Bansal share the top rank
  • NTA extends deadline for Allen fee refund again; candidates asked to submit bank details

NEET-UG 2026 results show sharp drop in top-end scores

The National Testing Agency (NTA) declared the NEET-UG 2026 results on Thursday, with 11.21 lakh candidates qualifying for medical admissions. This marks a significant recovery for the exam after the controversies that plagued the 2024 edition.

A total of 24.3 lakh candidates appeared for the test conducted on May 3, 2026. The qualifying number — 11.21 lakh — represents roughly 46% of test-takers, consistent with previous years' averages.

The most striking feature of this year's results is the compression at the top. Only 19 candidates crossed the 700-mark, with two students — Aryan Gupta of Punjab and Pranshul Bansal of Haryana — sharing the top score of 715. Last year, 67 candidates had scored above 700, raising questions about paper leaks and inflated scores.

Background: The road back from 2024 crisis

The NEET-UG 2024 exam was marred by allegations of a paper leak, leading to a Supreme Court-monitored investigation and a re-test for over 1,500 candidates. The controversy forced the NTA to overhaul its question paper distribution system and introduce stricter protocols for 2025 and 2026.

The 2025 results had already shown a correction — 44 candidates scored above 700. The 2026 numbers suggest the trend is continuing downward, bringing the score distribution closer to pre-2024 patterns when 10-20 candidates typically crossed 700.

Educational analysts attribute this to the NTA's decision to reintroduce a single question paper set for all candidates, replacing the multi-set system that had been introduced in 2024. The single-set approach, combined with tighter logistics, has reduced the scope for irregularities.

Why the score compression matters for medical admissions

The concentration of top scores has direct implications for admission to premier medical colleges. With only two candidates at 715, there will be virtually no tie-breaking disputes for the All India Quota seats. In 2024, 67 candidates above 700 had created a bottleneck at the top, forcing the Medical Counselling Committee to use complex tie-breaking criteria.

For the 11.21 lakh qualifying candidates, the cut-off marks for government medical colleges will likely remain stable. The qualifying percentile for general category candidates is fixed at the 50th percentile, while OBC/SC/ST candidates have relaxed thresholds.

The NTA has also extended the deadline for the Allen Career Institute fee refund. Candidates who had paid for test preparation and now seek refunds must submit their bank account details by July 31, 2026. This follows multiple extensions since January, when Allen announced a partial refund scheme following parent protests over alleged misleading advertisements.

Regional performance and gender divide

Among the top performers, Punjab's Aryan Gupta and Haryana's Pranshul Bansal represent a shift from the traditional dominance of candidates from Kerala and Tamil Nadu. However, the overall state-wise qualifying percentages are yet to be released by the NTA.

The gender gap in qualifying numbers remains narrow. Of the 11.21 lakh qualifiers, approximately 5.6 lakh are female candidates — a 50% share that has remained consistent over the past three years. The number of female candidates appearing for NEET has also grown steadily, crossing 13 lakh in 2026.

For reserved category candidates, the qualifying marks are determined by relaxed percentile requirements. The NTA has confirmed that category-wise cut-offs will be published along with the final answer key, expected within two weeks.

What to watch next

The release of the NEET-UG 2026 results sets the stage for the counselling process, which the Medical Counselling Committee will begin in August. The first round of All India Quota counselling will open for registration on August 5, with seat allocation based on merit-cum-choice.

A key development to watch is whether any legal challenges arise over the results. The Supreme Court is still monitoring the implementation of the NTA's new security protocols, and any petition alleging irregularities could delay the counselling schedule.
