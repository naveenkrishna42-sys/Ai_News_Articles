NASA to launch telescope to detect 100,000 new worlds

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • NASA's new telescope, named after Nancy Grace Roman, is set to launch on August 30.
  • The telescope could detect around 100,000 new worlds, more than all previous planet-hunting observatories combined.
  • The telescope's camera captures 100 times more sky than Hubble in a single shot.

NASA's New Telescope Set for Launch

NASA is preparing to launch its new telescope, named after Nancy Grace Roman, on August 30. The telescope, known as the Roman Space Telescope, is set to revolutionize the search for new worlds beyond our solar system.

Mission to Detect 100,000 New Worlds

The Roman Space Telescope is expected to detect around 100,000 new worlds. This is more than all previous planet-hunting observatories have found combined. The telescope's advanced camera will capture 100 times more sky than the Hubble Space Telescope in a single shot.

Advanced Camera Technology

The telescope's camera is a significant upgrade from previous models. It will allow astronomers to study the universe in unprecedented detail. The camera's wide field of view will enable the telescope to capture vast areas of the sky, making it an invaluable tool for discovering new exoplanets.

Backstory of the Roman Space Telescope

The Roman Space Telescope has a rich history. It was named after Nancy Grace Roman, often referred to as the 'Mother of Hubble'. Roman played a crucial role in the development of the Hubble Space Telescope. Her legacy continues with the Roman Space Telescope, which aims to build on the success of Hubble.

Preparations for Launch

NASA has completed the launch dress rehearsal for the Roman Space Telescope. This is a critical step in ensuring the telescope's safe and successful launch. The team has been working diligently to prepare the telescope for its mission. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact launch time, but the telescope is set to lift off on August 30.
