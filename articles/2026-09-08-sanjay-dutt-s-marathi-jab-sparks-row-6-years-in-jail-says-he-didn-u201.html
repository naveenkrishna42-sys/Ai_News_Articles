Dutt’s Marathi Snub at Dahi Handi Event

At a recent Dahi Handi celebration in Mumbai, actor Sanjay Dutt was invited to speak on stage. When a Maharashtra minister asked him to address the gathering in Marathi, Dutt responded with a joke: “Main chhe saal Yerwada Jail mein raha, tab bhi Marathi nahi seekh paya” (“I spent six years in Yerwada Jail, yet I couldn’t learn Marathi”). The quip, laced with humor, was met with silence by parts of the audience and swift backlash online.

Political Fallout and Public Reaction

The video of the exchange circulated widely across social media platforms, drawing sharp criticism from several regional leaders. Shiv Sena and BJP functionaries expressed displeasure, calling the remark disrespectful to the state language. Former Pune police commissioner and prison officials also weighed in, recalling Dutt’s incarceration period between 2013 and 2019 for possession of illegal arms.

Language, Identity, and Celebrity Influence

The controversy underscores tensions around language pride in Maharashtra, where Marathi holds cultural and administrative significance. While some defended Dutt as exercising free speech, others argued that public figures bear responsibility when addressing linguistic sentiments. The incident adds to ongoing debates about celebrity conduct and regional identity in Indian cinema and politics.

Background: Sanjay Dutt’s Legal Trajectory

Dutt was sentenced in 2013 under the Arms Act for acquiring weapons from Pakistan-trained operatives during the 1993 Mumbai attacks case. He served over five years in Yerwada Central Prison before being released on parole and later granted remission. His time in jail has remained a subject of public discourse, particularly in interviews and autobiographical reflections.

Meanwhile, cultural organizations are expected to weigh in on the broader debate around language use by public personalities. Whether this becomes a lasting controversy or fades into pop culture memory depends on how stakeholders engage moving forward.
