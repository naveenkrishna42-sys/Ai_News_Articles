Mumbai AC local cooling failure hospitalises one, affects two others

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cooling system malfunction in an AC local train near Borivali
  • One passenger hospitalised, two others suffered breathing difficulties
  • Western Railway officials investigating the incident

A malfunction in the cooling system of an air-conditioned local train on Mumbai's Western Railway line left one passenger hospitalised and two others struggling to breathe on Monday morning. The incident, which occurred near Borivali station, has once again raised questions about the maintenance of the city's suburban fleet, particularly as the monsoon season sets in.

Sudden failure mid-commute

The train, part of the AC local service that runs between Churchgate and Virar, was approaching Borivali when passengers reported a sudden drop in air quality inside the coach. Several commuters complained of a lack of fresh air and a stifling sensation.

Railway officials said the cooling system appeared to have failed, leading to a build-up of heat and humidity inside the sealed coach. Unlike non-AC locals that have open windows and fans, AC trains rely entirely on their climate control systems for ventilation.

"The coach became uncomfortably warm within minutes. Some passengers started feeling dizzy," a commuter who did not wish to be named told reporters. The guard was alerted, and the train was stopped at Borivali station for inspection.

Three passengers affected, one in hospital

At least three passengers reported breathing difficulties and required medical attention. One of them was taken to a nearby hospital for observation. The other two were treated at the station and later discharged.

"We immediately arranged for medical assistance at Borivali station. The affected passengers were attended to by railway doctors," a Western Railway spokesperson said. The spokesperson confirmed that the cooling system had malfunctioned but did not elaborate on the exact cause.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether a technical glitch or a maintenance oversight led to the failure. The train was taken out of service for repairs, and passengers were advised to board the next available local.

Recurring concern for Mumbai's suburban network

Mumbai's AC local trains, introduced to provide relief from the city's heat and humidity, have faced occasional technical issues since their launch. The fleet, which operates on both Western and Central Railway lines, is popular among commuters but requires consistent upkeep.

This is not the first time a cooling system failure has caused distress. In recent years, similar incidents have been reported, prompting the railways to emphasise preventive maintenance. However, with the monsoon increasing humidity levels, any failure in ventilation can quickly become uncomfortable.

The Western Railway has announced an internal inquiry into Monday's incident. "We are looking into why the cooling system failed and what steps can be taken to prevent such occurrences in the future," the spokesperson added.

Commuters have urged the railways to conduct thorough checks before trains are pressed into service. For now, the affected passengers are recovering, and the railway authorities are focused on restoring confidence in the AC local network. The inquiry's findings are expected in the coming days, and Western Railway will likely issue a detailed report on the corrective measures to be implemented.
