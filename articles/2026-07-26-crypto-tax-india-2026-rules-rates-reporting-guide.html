Crypto Tax India 2026: Rules, Rates & Reporting Guide

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Crypto gains in India are taxed at 30%, with no deduction for expenses or losses.
  • The 1% TDS on crypto transfers above Rs 50,000 remains in effect.
  • Losses from crypto sales cannot be set off against other income.
  • All crypto transactions must be reported in the income tax return.

How Crypto Is Taxed in India for 2026

The tax treatment of cryptocurrency in India has not changed dramatically for the financial year 2026. Gains from the transfer of virtual digital assets (VDAs) continue to be taxed at a flat 30% under Section 115BBH of the Income Tax Act. This rate applies to all types of crypto transactions — selling, swapping, or spending crypto for goods or services.

No deductions are allowed for the cost of acquisition or any expenses incurred during the transaction. This means an investor cannot deduct mining costs, transaction fees, or even the purchase price when calculating gains. The entire sale value is treated as income.

Key Rules Every Crypto Investor Should Know

One of the most important rules is that losses from crypto transactions cannot be set off against any other income. If you lose money on a crypto trade, you cannot use that loss to reduce your tax liability on salary, business income, or capital gains from other assets.

The 1% Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) on crypto transfers continues. Any buyer transferring more than Rs 50,000 in a financial year to a seller must deduct 1% TDS. For non-specified persons, the threshold is Rs 10,000. The TDS must be reported and deposited with the government.

Reporting Requirements for Crypto Holdings

Taxpayers holding or trading crypto must declare their holdings in the income tax return. The tax department expects a separate schedule for virtual digital assets, detailing each transaction. Failure to report can lead to notices and penalties.

Exchanges are required to report all transactions above a certain threshold to the tax authorities. The government has been tightening compliance, and several exchanges have implemented automated reporting systems. Investors are advised to maintain records of every trade, including dates, values, and counterparties.

What Has Not Changed

The government has not introduced a lower tax rate for long-term holding. Whether you hold crypto for a day or a year, the 30% rate applies. There is no indexation benefit, and no distinction between short-term and long-term capital gains for VDAs.

Gifts of crypto are also taxable. If you receive crypto as a gift, and the value exceeds Rs 50,000, it is treated as income in the hands of the recipient. There are exceptions for gifts from relatives, but the rules are strict.

What to Watch in the Coming Year

The tax landscape for crypto in India remains stable for now. But industry bodies are lobbying for a reduction in the TDS rate and for allowing loss offsets. The government has not signalled any changes. Investors should expect the current rules to apply for the full financial year 2026 and plan their transactions accordingly.
