Court declines interim relief in family feud

The Bombay High Court has refused to allow access to a family estate, stating that the property must remain locked until all ownership disputes are resolved. The order came in response to a plea seeking interim access, which the court dismissed citing the risk of altering the status quo.

Disputes over ownership remain unresolved

The estate, which has been a subject of contention among family members, is currently under the court's supervision. The petitioners had argued that they needed access for maintenance and to prevent deterioration. However, the respondents opposed this, claiming that any entry could be misused.

The court noted that the disputes over the estate's ownership are complex and require a thorough examination. It said that granting access at this stage would not be in the interest of justice, as it could prejudice the rights of any party.

Legal experts weigh in

Legal observers say the decision underscores the judiciary's cautious approach in family property matters, where emotions and legal claims often intersect. The court's priority, they say, is to ensure that no party gains an unfair advantage before the final hearing.

This is not the first time the estate has been in the news. Previous orders had already restrained any transaction or transfer of the property, and the latest ruling extends that protection to physical access as well.

What happens next
