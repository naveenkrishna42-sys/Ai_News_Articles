Centre Deploys National Team to Probe Chandipura Virus Outbreak

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Union Health Ministry deploys National Joint Outbreak Response Team (NJORT) to Gujarat and Rajasthan
  • Chandipura virus has killed 22 children in Gujarat this monsoon, says state health minister
  • Multi-institutional investigation launched to contain the critical public health threat

NEW DELHI: The Union Health Ministry has deployed the National Joint Outbreak Response Team (NJORT) to Gujarat and Rajasthan, where a Chandipura virus outbreak has emerged as a critical public health concern. The move comes as Gujarat's health minister confirmed that 22 children have died from the virus this monsoon alone.

The central team, comprising epidemiologists and public health experts, will support state authorities in containment and investigation. The deployment marks a significant escalation in the government's response to the viral encephalitis outbreak.

Multi-Institutional Probe Intensifies

The investigation is being conducted on a multi-institutional level, with several agencies pooling resources to trace the virus's spread. Officials said the NJORT will coordinate with state health departments to conduct door-to-door surveillance and vector control measures.

Chandipura virus, transmitted by sandflies, causes rapid-onset encephalitis, primarily affecting children. The case fatality rate is high, and early intervention is critical. Health teams are now racing to identify hotspots and implement preventive measures before the monsoon peak worsens the situation.

Gujarat and Rajasthan on Alert

Gujarat has reported the maximum number of cases, with 22 deaths confirmed by the state health minister. Rajasthan has also recorded cases, prompting the central government to broaden its response. The NJORT will work alongside local medical teams to strengthen surveillance and ensure timely treatment.

District hospitals in affected areas have been put on high alert, with paediatric intensive care units being scaled up. The health ministry has also issued advisories to neighbouring states to remain vigilant, given the virus's potential to spread through vector movement.

What Is Chandipura Virus?

Chandipura virus belongs to the Rhabdoviridae family, related to the rabies virus. It was first isolated in 1965 in Maharashtra, but outbreaks have been sporadic since then. The virus is known to cause acute encephalitis syndrome, with symptoms including sudden fever, convulsions, and altered consciousness.

There is no specific antiviral treatment or vaccine for Chandipura virus. Management is supportive, focusing on reducing brain swelling and controlling seizures. Public health experts stress the importance of vector control, particularly the use of insecticide-treated nets and indoor residual spraying in affected villages.

Community Awareness Campaigns

Local authorities have launched awareness campaigns to educate families about the symptoms and the need for immediate medical attention. Parents are being urged to bring children with high fever and neurological symptoms to hospitals without delay.

Health workers are also distributing insect repellents and advising communities to eliminate sandfly breeding sites, such as cracks in walls and damp organic matter. The response is being closely monitored by the central government, with regular updates expected.

Government Response and Next Steps

The Union Health Ministry has assured full support to the affected states, including the deployment of rapid response teams and supply of essential medicines. The National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC) is providing technical guidance to the field teams.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total number of cases across both states, and the investigation is ongoing. The focus now shifts to the effectiveness of containment measures in the coming weeks, particularly as the monsoon season continues to create favourable conditions for sandfly breeding.

The coming days will be crucial in determining whether the outbreak can be brought under control, with the NJORT's findings expected to shape a national response strategy for future vector-borne viral threats.
