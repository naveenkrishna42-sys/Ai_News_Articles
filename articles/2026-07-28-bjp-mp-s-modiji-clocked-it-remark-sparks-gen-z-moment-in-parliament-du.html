BJP MP's 'Modiji clocked it' remark sparks Gen Z moment in Parliament during anti-paper leak debate

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP MP used Gen Z slang 'clocked it' to praise PM Modi during anti-paper leak Bill debate in Lok Sabha.
  • Opposition leaders criticized the Bill as an 'admission of failure' and a 'showpiece'.
  • Trinamool Congress MP Abhishek Banerjee highlighted recurring paper leak scandals affecting students.
  • Congress MP Gaurav Gogoi questioned the government's use of pellet guns in Kashmir during the debate.

Lok Sabha debate takes a Gen Z turn

A BJP Member of Parliament injected a dose of Gen Z slang into the Lok Sabha on Tuesday, praising Prime Minister Narendra Modi with the phrase 'Modiji clocked it' during a debate on the anti-paper leak Bill. The remark drew a mix of chuckles from the treasury benches and sharp retorts from the Opposition, who saw it as an attempt to deflect from serious issues.

The MP, whose name has not been officially confirmed in reports, used the colloquial term meaning 'understood' or 'noticed' to suggest that the Prime Minister had grasped the gravity of exam paper leaks. The moment quickly became a talking point, with some calling it a refreshing break from parliamentary formality and others dismissing it as a publicity stunt.

Opposition calls Bill an 'admission of failure'

The anti-paper leak Bill, introduced to curb the rising number of exam paper leak incidents, faced fierce criticism from opposition parties. Several leaders termed the legislation an 'admission of failure', arguing that the government should focus on preventing leaks rather than just punishing offenders after the damage is done.

Trinamool Congress MP Abhishek Banerjee took a sharp dig at the government, stating, "Every year, students get a new paper leak scandal. This Bill is not a solution—it is a sign of how little has been achieved." He demanded concrete measures to overhaul the exam system rather than reactive legislation.

Congress MP raises pellet gun issue

Congress MP Gaurav Gogoi used the debate to question the government on a separate but emotive issue. "Who ordered you to use pellet guns?" he asked, tearing into the government over the use of non-lethal ammunition in Kashmir. Gogoi termed the anti-paper leak Bill a 'showpiece', arguing that the government was more interested in optics than in solving real problems.

The Opposition's strategy appeared to be linking the Bill to broader grievances, including student unrest and regional security. Gogoi's intervention shifted the focus momentarily, with several members demanding a detailed response from the government on the pellet gun question.

What the anti-paper leak Bill proposes

The Bill aims to introduce stricter penalties for those involved in leaking exam papers, including fines up to Rs 1 crore and imprisonment of up to 10 years. It also proposes the creation of a national testing authority to oversee the conduct of major entrance and recruitment exams.

Government officials defended the Bill as a necessary step to restore trust in the examination system. They argued that the legislation would act as a deterrent, though critics pointed out that enforcement would remain a challenge without systemic reforms.

The debate continues, with both sides expected to push for amendments. The Bill is likely to be sent to a parliamentary committee for further scrutiny before a final vote.
