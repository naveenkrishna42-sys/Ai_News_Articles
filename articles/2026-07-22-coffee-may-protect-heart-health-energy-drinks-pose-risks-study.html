Coffee may protect heart health, energy drinks pose risks: Study

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Moderate coffee consumption linked to lower risk of heart disease and arrhythmias
  • Energy drinks associated with increased blood pressure and heart rhythm disturbances
  • Experts recommend limiting energy drink intake, especially for those with existing conditions

Coffee's Heart Benefits

A new study published in the Journal of the American Heart Association adds to the growing body of evidence that coffee can be part of a heart-healthy diet. Researchers found that moderate coffee consumption — up to five cups a day — was associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular disease and arrhythmias.

The findings align with previous recommendations from the American Heart Association, which has said that for most adults, five cups of coffee a day is fine. The key, experts note, is what you add to it. Black coffee appears to offer the most benefits, while added sugar, cream, or syrups can negate those effects.

Energy Drink Dangers

The same study painted a starkly different picture for energy drinks. These beverages, often loaded with caffeine, sugar, and other stimulants, were linked to a higher risk of heart problems. Researchers observed that energy drink consumption was associated with increased blood pressure and a greater likelihood of heart rhythm disturbances.

Unlike coffee, which is typically consumed slowly and in moderate amounts, energy drinks are often gulped down quickly, leading to a rapid spike in caffeine levels. This can be particularly dangerous for young adults and those with underlying heart conditions.

  • Energy drinks contain high levels of caffeine, often exceeding 200 mg per serving.
  • They also include taurine, guarana, and other stimulants that compound caffeine's effects.
  • Studies have linked energy drinks to emergency room visits for palpitations and chest pain.

Context Matters

Cardiologists stress that the way caffeine is delivered matters. Coffee contains antioxidants and anti-inflammatory compounds that may counteract some of caffeine's negative effects. Energy drinks, on the other hand, are highly processed and loaded with sugar, which can contribute to inflammation and insulin resistance.

Dr. Jennifer Wong, a cardiologist at the University of California, San Francisco, told reporters that the findings reinforce the idea that not all caffeine is created equal. “Coffee, when consumed in moderation, appears to have a protective effect. Energy drinks, however, introduce a lot of variables that can stress the heart,” she said.

What This Means for You

For most healthy adults, moderate coffee consumption is safe and may even be beneficial. The American Heart Association recommends limiting added sugars and opting for black coffee or with a splash of milk. For energy drinks, the advice is more cautious: avoid them if you have high blood pressure, heart disease, or are pregnant.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether regulatory bodies will update guidelines based on this study. However, the research adds weight to existing calls for clearer labeling on energy drinks regarding their caffeine content and potential risks.

As more studies emerge, the line between friend and foe in the world of caffeinated beverages is becoming clearer. For now, coffee drinkers can enjoy their morning cup with confidence, while energy drink consumers may want to reconsider their habit.
