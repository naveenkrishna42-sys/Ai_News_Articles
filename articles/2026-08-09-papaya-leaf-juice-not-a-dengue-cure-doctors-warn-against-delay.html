Papaya leaf juice not a dengue cure, doctors warn against delay

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rising dengue cases have led to increased use of papaya leaf juice as a home remedy.
  • Doctors clarify that papaya leaf juice is not a cure for dengue.
  • Experts warn against delaying medical treatment in favour of home remedies.

As dengue cases climb across several Indian cities, a familiar home remedy is seeing a resurgence: papaya leaf juice. From roadside vendors to social media posts, the bitter green extract is being touted as a quick fix for the mosquito-borne viral infection. But doctors are pushing back, warning that the juice is not a cure and that relying on it could cost precious time in seeking proper medical care.

The appeal of a home remedy

Papaya leaf juice has long been a part of folk medicine in many parts of the country. It is often recommended by neighbours and relatives, and increasingly shared in WhatsApp forwards, as a way to boost platelet counts. With dengue cases rising, especially during the monsoon season, many families are turning to this remedy out of fear and desperation.

In cities like Delhi, Bengaluru and Pune, local health centres have reported an uptick in patients who have tried papaya leaf juice before coming in for treatment. Some admit to having consumed it for several days, hoping it would prevent the disease from worsening.

What doctors say

Medical professionals are clear: papaya leaf juice has no proven antiviral effect against dengue. While some studies have suggested it may help in raising platelet counts in certain patients, these findings are far from conclusive and do not support its use as a treatment. Dr. Meera Sharma, an internal medicine specialist at a city hospital, said, "Papaya leaf juice is not a cure. It does not kill the dengue virus, and it does not prevent complications."

More importantly, doctors are concerned about the delay in seeking proper care. Dengue can progress rapidly, and severe cases may require hospitalisation, fluid management and close monitoring. Time lost in trying home remedies can make the difference between a mild illness and a life-threatening one.

The risk of self-medication

Apart from the lack of efficacy, papaya leaf juice is not without risks. It can cause stomach upset, nausea and, in some cases, allergic reactions. For people with underlying health conditions, it may interact with other medications. Self-medicating with unproven remedies can also mask symptoms, leading patients and families to underestimate the severity of the illness.

Health authorities have repeatedly urged the public to rely on established medical advice. The focus should be on early diagnosis, adequate hydration and monitoring of warning signs such as persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, bleeding or difficulty in breathing.

What to watch

With the dengue season far from over, public health officials are expected to intensify awareness campaigns about prevention and early treatment. Hospitals are bracing for more cases, and vector control measures are being stepped up in affected areas. The key message from doctors remains simple: do not substitute proven medical care with unverified home remedies, and seek help at the first sign of complications.
