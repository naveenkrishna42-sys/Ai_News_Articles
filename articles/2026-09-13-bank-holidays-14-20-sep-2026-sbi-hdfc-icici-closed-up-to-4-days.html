Bank Holidays 14-20 Sep 2026: SBI, HDFC, ICICI Closed Up to 4 Days

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-13 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Branch closures between 14 and 20 September 2026 are state-specific, not nationwide, with up to four non-working days depending on your city.
  • Digital channels, UPI, NEFT, IMPS and ATMs continue to function on bank holidays, but cheque clearing and physical loan processing stall.
  • Time-sensitive work such as EMI mandate changes, demand drafts, forex and locker access must be completed before the closure window.

Why This Week's Bank Holiday Calendar Matters

The Reserve Bank of India's September 2026 holiday list places up to four non-working days for bank branches between 14 and 20 September, depending on the state. SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and Punjab National Bank follow the same RBI calendar, so the closure pattern is identical across lenders in a given city.

These are not uniform national holidays. A branch in Mumbai may operate on a day when branches in Kochi or Ranchi are shut. Customers must match the holiday to their own city and branch, not to a national list.

What Actually Stops Working

Real-world impact is narrower than the headline suggests. UPI transfers, card payments, ATM withdrawals and online NEFT/IMPS run through the RBI's 24x7 rails and do not pause for branch holidays.

What does stall: cheque presentation and clearing, demand draft issuance, cash deposits above ATM limits at the counter, locker operations, forex cash, new account opening and physical verification of loan documents. A cheque dated on a holiday is typically processed on the next working day, which can delay salary credits or vendor payments by 24 to 48 hours.

Buyer and Customer Implications

  • EMI and mandate dates: If your auto-debit falls on a holiday, the debit usually executes on the next working day. Confirm your balance to avoid a bounce charge of ₹400–₹750 plus GST.
  • Cheque clearing: Add one to two extra working days for outstation cheques during a cluster of holidays.
  • Cash-heavy businesses: Retailers and traders should arrange cash before the weekend rather than relying on counter deposits.
  • Loan disbursal: Sanction letters may be issued digitally, but disbursal into a new account often needs a working branch day.
  • Branch-dependent work: Locker access, KYC re-verification and forex notes are the services most likely to be pushed to the following week.

How to Verify Your City's Holiday

Three checks take under five minutes. First, open the RBI's official holiday list for your state and cross-reference the date. Second, check your bank's app or website notice board, which mirrors RBI regional circulars. Third, call the branch landline rather than relying on a forwarded WhatsApp list, which frequently mixes up state holidays.

Practical Planning Window

Schedule high-value transactions for 14–18 September where your state is open. Complete locker visits, demand drafts and forex requirements before the second Saturday. For routine payments, digital rails remove most of the risk, but keep a buffer of one working day for anything that needs a human signature or a physical instrument.

Verdict

The 14–20 September 2026 window is a planning inconvenience, not a banking shutdown. Customers who use UPI, cards and net banking face almost no disruption. The real cost lands on cheque-based transactions, cash businesses and branch-dependent paperwork. Check your city's specific dates, pre-fund accounts tied to EMI mandates, and move physical banking tasks into the working days.
