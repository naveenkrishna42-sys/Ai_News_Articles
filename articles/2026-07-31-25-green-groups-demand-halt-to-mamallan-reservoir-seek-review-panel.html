25+ green groups demand halt to Mamallan reservoir, seek review panel

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • More than 25 environmental groups have jointly demanded a halt to construction work at the Mamallan reservoir.
  • The groups are seeking the constitution of an independent review panel to evaluate the project's environmental impact.
  • The demand comes amid growing concerns over the reservoir's potential ecological consequences.

More than 25 environmental organisations have jointly called for an immediate halt to construction work at the Mamallan reservoir, demanding that authorities set up an independent review panel to scrutinise the project. The groups, in a collective appeal, have argued that the reservoir's ecological impact has not been adequately assessed before the commencement of work.

The demand, made public on Friday, reflects a growing unease among civil society actors over the pace of the project's execution. The organisations have urged the government to pause all activities at the site until a thorough and transparent evaluation is conducted by an independent body.

Concerns over ecological damage

The environmental groups have cited potential risks to local biodiversity and water systems as key reasons for their demand. They argue that the reservoir could disrupt the natural flow of water in the region, affecting both flora and fauna, as well as the livelihoods of communities dependent on the local ecosystem.

While the specific details of the project's design and location have not been disclosed in the appeal, the groups have insisted that a comprehensive environmental impact assessment should have been completed before any construction began. They have also expressed concerns over the adequacy of existing studies, calling for an independent review to verify their findings.

Demand for transparency and accountability

The appeal stresses the need for transparency in the project's planning and implementation. The groups have asked the authorities to make public all relevant documents, including feasibility studies and environmental clearances, to allow for a meaningful public consultation.

They have also called for accountability, noting that any decision to proceed with the reservoir should be based on scientific evidence rather than administrative urgency. The organisations have urged the government to engage with local communities and experts before taking any further steps.

Reactions and next steps

Officials have not yet responded to the demand, and it remains unclear whether the authorities will consider the request for a halt. The project's status is currently uncertain, with no official confirmation on whether construction has been paused or will be paused in response to the appeal.

The environmental groups have indicated that they will continue to press their case through formal channels, including petitions and representations to the relevant government departments. They have also hinted at the possibility of legal action if their concerns are not addressed.

As the debate over the Mamallan reservoir intensifies, all eyes will be on the government's next move. The coming weeks are likely to see further deliberations, with the independent review panel demand emerging as a key test of the authorities' commitment to sustainable development.
