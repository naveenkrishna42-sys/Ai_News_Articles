Justin Langer slams two-day Tests after Australia rout Bangladesh

  • Starc became the leading wicket-taker in World Test Championship history, surpassing Cummins and Lyon.

Mackay, Aug 23: The Mackay Test will be remembered for its alarming brevity. Australia completed a crushing victory over Bangladesh inside two days, with the match lasting a mere 750 balls—among the shortest in Test history. But the swift result has sparked a fierce debate, with former Australia coach Justin Langer calling the trend 'unacceptable'.

A rout that raised eyebrows

The hosts needed less than two full days to dismantle Bangladesh, bundling them out for a paltry total in the first innings before chasing down a modest target. Mitchell Starc was the wrecker-in-chief, tearing through the visiting batting line-up with raw pace and swing. His figures were the standout of the match, but the speed of the contest left many questioning the state of the longest format.

Langer, never one to mince words, did not hold back. 'Two-day Tests are unacceptable,' he said. 'We are seeing too many matches finish early. It is not good for the game, not good for the fans, and certainly not good for the future of Test cricket.' His comments come as cricket boards worldwide grapple with shrinking attention spans and packed calendars.

Starc's milestone in the middle of the mayhem

Amid the chaos, Starc reached a personal landmark. He surpassed both Pat Cummins and Nathan Lyon to become the leading wicket-taker in World Test Championship history. The left-arm quick's consistency in the WTC across cycles has been formidable, and his performance in Mackay underlined his value to the side.

Captain Pat Cummins was effusive in his praise for Starc. 'We know how good he is,' Cummins said. 'He has been doing it for years. On a pitch like this, he is almost unplayable.' The allrounder's ability to extract movement from docile surfaces has long been his hallmark, and in Mackay, he made the ball talk from the first over.

The shortest Tests: a worrying trend

The Mackay Test is now among the shortest in the history of the game, a dubious distinction that has reignited discussions about pitch preparation and the balance between bat and ball. Some experts argue that result-oriented pitches are the culprit, while others point to the gulf in quality between the top sides and the rest.

Langer, who coached Australia to the 2021 T20 World Cup title, believes the issue is systemic. 'We need to look at the bigger picture,' he said. 'If we keep producing pitches that are impossible to bat on, or if teams keep being this mismatched, we will lose the essence of Test cricket.'

The International Cricket Council has not yet commented on the match's duration, but the governing body is expected to review the pitch rating. A 'poor' rating could result in demerit points for the venue, though officials have not confirmed any action.

What lies ahead for Test cricket

The debate over the format's future is unlikely to subside soon. With the World Test Championship final scheduled for next year, teams are already jostling for points, but the quality of contests remains uneven. Bangladesh's early exit in Mackay will do little to silence critics who argue that two-tier Test cricket is inevitable.

For now, Australia will savour the victory, but the questions Langer raised will linger. The next Test in the series is expected to draw more scrutiny, and all eyes will be on the pitch curator. The cricketing world will be watching to see whether the response is a more balanced surface or another quick finish.
