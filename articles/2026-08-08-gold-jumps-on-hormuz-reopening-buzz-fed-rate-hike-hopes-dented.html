Gold jumps on Hormuz reopening buzz, Fed rate-hike hopes dented

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gold posted its best week since January, with prices jumping on Hormuz reopening buzz.
  • The development dents US Fed rate-hike hopes, boosting bullion's appeal.
  • Analysts suggest buying on dips, with some eyeing $5,000 as the next target.

Gold prices have surged, marking their best week since January, as speculation over the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz dampened expectations of a US Federal Reserve rate hike. The buzz around the crucial shipping lane's reopening has shifted market sentiment, prompting investors to reassess their positions in the precious metal.

The rally comes amid a complex interplay of geopolitical signals and monetary policy bets. With the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint for global oil supplies—showing signs of easing tensions, traders have grown less convinced that the Fed will need to aggressively raise rates to combat inflation. That shift has given gold a fresh tailwind, as lower rate expectations reduce the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets.

Why gold is climbing now

The latest price action reflects a broader market recalibration. Reports of the strait's reopening have alleviated some supply-side fears, but they've also muddied the Fed's policy path. Investors now see a less hawkish Fed, which typically supports gold prices.

According to market data, gold's weekly gain is its strongest since January, underscoring the momentum. The metal has been buoyed by a combination of factors: persistent inflation worries, geopolitical uncertainty, and now a shift in rate expectations. Analysts note that the market is pricing in a softer stance from the US central bank, which could keep bullion bid in the near term.

What analysts are saying

Market observers are divided on the next move. Some see this as a breakout moment, with gold potentially heading toward the $5,000 mark. Kitco's latest commentary highlights that investors who waited for September may have missed the initial surge, and the current momentum could carry prices higher.

Others advocate a more cautious approach. The Times of India's outlook suggests that buying on dips remains the right strategy, given the volatility around geopolitical events. The key, analysts argue, is to time entries carefully rather than chase the rally at current levels.

Technical and fundamental signals

From a technical standpoint, gold has shown resilience, holding above key support levels. TradingView's analysis notes that the metal is steady as traders weigh the impact of the Hormuz flare-up on interest rates. The market seems to be in a wait-and-see mode, with the next catalyst likely to come from US economic data or Fed commentary.

Fundamentally, the case for gold remains intact. Inflation is still a concern, and central banks continue to diversify reserves. The reopening of the strait may reduce some geopolitical risk premium, but it hasn't erased the underlying demand for safe-haven assets.

Should you buy gold now?

The question on every investor's mind is whether this is the right time to enter. The answer, according to experts, depends on your risk appetite and investment horizon. For long-term holders, gold's role as a hedge against uncertainty remains compelling. For short-term traders, the volatility around the Hormuz situation could offer opportunities, but also risks.

One thing is clear: the market's focus has shifted. The buzz around the strait's reopening has dented rate-hike hopes, and that's a positive for gold. But as always, investors should stay vigilant, as any new developments could quickly reverse the trend.

Looking ahead, all eyes will be on the Fed's next moves and any further updates on the Strait of Hormuz situation. If rate-hike expectations continue to fade, gold could extend its gains. If not, a pullback may be on the cards, making the buy-on-dips strategy all the more relevant. Watch for US inflation data and central bank speeches in the coming weeks for clearer direction.
