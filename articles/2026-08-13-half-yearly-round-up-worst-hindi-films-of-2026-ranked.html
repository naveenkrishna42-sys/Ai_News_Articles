The first half of 2026 has come and gone, and Bollywood has already served up a mixed bag at the box office. While some films managed to strike a chord with audiences, others have been relegated to the dustbin of critical and commercial failure. A new half-yearly round-up from Hindustan Times has ranked the worst Hindi films of the year so far, and the list is as telling as it is brutal.

Among the titles that have earned a spot on this unenviable list are Hai Jawani To Ishq Hona Hai and The Kerala Story 2. The ranking, which has sparked conversations across social media, offers a snapshot of what went wrong in the first six months of 2026—and why some of these films will likely be forgotten by the time the year ends.

The Criteria Behind the Ranking

Ranking the worst films is never a straightforward task. It involves a combination of critical reception, audience response, and box office performance. The Hindustan Times round-up appears to weigh all three, though the exact methodology has not been disclosed. What is clear is that the films on the list failed to meet even basic expectations, whether in storytelling, execution, or entertainment value.

The inclusion of Hai Jawani To Ishq Hona Hai comes as little surprise to those who followed its release. The film, which aimed to capture the youthful energy of modern romance, reportedly suffered from a weak script and lackluster direction. Similarly, The Kerala Story 2 has been criticised for its narrative inconsistencies, despite the commercial success of its predecessor.

What the List Says About Bollywood

The ranking is more than just a list of failures; it is a mirror held up to the industry. The fact that two such different films—one a light-hearted romance, the other a controversial drama—both landed on the list suggests that the problems are not genre-specific. Rather, they point to a systemic issue of prioritising star power over substance.

Audiences in 2026 are more discerning than ever. They have access to a global array of content, from OTT platforms to international cinema, and they are quick to abandon films that do not offer something fresh. The films on this list, according to critics, relied too heavily on formulaic tropes and failed to innovate. That approach, once a safe bet for Bollywood, is now a fast track to the bottom of the rankings.

The conversation, however, is a healthy one—it forces the industry to confront uncomfortable questions about quality and audience trust.

As the second half of the year begins, all eyes are on the upcoming releases. Can Bollywood redeem itself with a strong lineup of films, or will the trend of underwhelming movies continue?
