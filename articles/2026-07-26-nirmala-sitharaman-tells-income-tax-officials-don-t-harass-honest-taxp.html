Nirmala Sitharaman tells Income Tax officials: Don't harass honest taxpayers

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • FM Sitharaman asks tax officials not to make honest taxpayers run from pillar to post
  • Directs department to crack down on evaders while ensuring convenience for compliant citizens
  • CBDT asked to explore ways to clear backlog of pending tax appeals

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has sent a clear message to Income Tax officials: stop harassing honest taxpayers and focus on those evading their dues. Speaking at a recent review meeting, she urged the department to simplify processes so that compliant citizens don't have to run from pillar to post for routine matters.

Focus on convenience, not harassment

Sitharaman stressed that the tax department must prioritise the convenience of the common person. She said honest taxpayers should not be subjected to unnecessary scrutiny or delays. Instead, the department should make compliance seamless and hassle-free.

The minister directed officials to adopt a taxpayer-friendly approach. She emphasised that technology should be leveraged to reduce human intervention in routine assessments and refunds.

Go after evaders, protect the honest

While calling for leniency towards compliant taxpayers, Sitharaman was firm on tax evaders. She instructed the department to intensify action against those who deliberately avoid their tax obligations. The message was clear: the system must distinguish between honest taxpayers and wilful defaulters.

This dual approach is aimed at improving voluntary compliance. Tax experts say such signals from the top can help rebuild trust between citizens and the tax administration.

Clear the appeal backlog, explore new ways

Another key directive from the Finance Minister was to clear the massive backlog of pending tax appeals. She asked the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) to explore innovative ways to resolve these cases quickly. Long-pending appeals often cause anxiety for taxpayers and clog the system.

The backlog has been a persistent issue for years. Sitharaman's push for faster resolution could bring relief to thousands of taxpayers whose cases have been stuck in legal loops.

Honest taxpayer is the true architect of India

The minister's remarks echo a broader government narrative that honest taxpayers are the backbone of the nation's development. Without naming anyone, she praised those who pay their taxes diligently, calling them the true architects of India's progress.

This sentiment was also reflected by Minister of State for Finance Shiv Pratap Shukla, who recently stated that the honest taxpayer is the real builder of the country. The government appears keen to shift the narrative from tax collection as a burden to a patriotic duty.

Industry bodies have welcomed these directions. They hope the department will now focus more on data-driven enforcement rather than targeting small taxpayers unnecessarily.

What happens next depends on how quickly the CBDT implements these instructions. Taxpayers will be watching closely to see if the rhetoric translates into real change on the ground.
