Allahabad HC: Police Can't Freeze Full Bank Account Without Linking Entire Balance to Crime

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Allahabad High Court held police cannot freeze entire bank account without linking total balance to crime
  • Court ruled freezing entire account violates Article 300A and 21 of Constitution
  • Decision sets precedent for account freeze cases in India

The Allahabad High Court has ruled that police cannot freeze an entire bank account unless they can show the full balance is linked to a suspected crime. The judgment came on a petition challenging the freezing of a current account by Uttar Pradesh Police's Cyber Crime Cell.

The court said freezing an entire account without establishing the entire amount is connected to an offence violates constitutional rights. The ruling provides important clarity on the limits of police powers in financial investigations.

What the Court Said

Justice Rajiv Gupta and Justice Vikas Budhwar of the Lucknow bench made the observation while hearing a petition filed by a businessman. The petitioner's current account was frozen by the Cyber Crime Cell during a probe into a cheating case.

The court noted that freezing an entire account amounts to deprivation of property without authority of law, violating Article 300A (Right Against Deprivation of Property) and Article 21 (Right to Life) of the Constitution.

The bench stated that an order freezing a bank account must clearly specify the amount linked to the crime. It cannot be a blanket freeze of the entire account.

The Case Background

The petitioner's account was frozen by the Cyber Crime Cell of Uttar Pradesh Police. The petitioner argued that the freezing was illegal because the whole balance could not be linked to the alleged crime.

The petitioner's counsel argued that the freezing order did not specify any particular amount. It simply directed the bank to freeze the entire account, causing severe hardship to the petitioner's business operations.

Government counsel argued that freezing entire accounts is necessary during investigations to prevent dissipation of funds. They submitted that courts should not interfere with investigation processes.

Key Observations

The court observed that freezing an entire account without linking the whole balance to the suspected crime is disproportionate. It said such blanket orders cannot be sustained in law.

The bench directed that the bank account be de-frozen but only to the extent of the amount not linked to the alleged crime. The amount linked to the crime was to remain frozen pending investigation.

The court also directed the investigating officer to comply with the legal requirements while passing any freezing order in future. It said the officer must specify the exact amount suspected to be involved in the crime.

What This Means

The judgment sets an important precedent for bank account freezing cases across India. It clarifies that investigating agencies cannot use blanket freezes as a tool to pressurise accused persons.

Legal experts say this ruling protects the fundamental rights of account holders while still allowing investigations to proceed. The court has balanced the needs of investigation with individual rights.

The judgment applies to all police and investigating agencies in Uttar Pradesh and could be cited in other states as well.

What remains to be seen is how strictly investigating agencies will follow this ruling in future cases. The court has made clear that blanket freezes will not be tolerated.
