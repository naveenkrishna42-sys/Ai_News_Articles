Nirmal Purja Among Seven Climbers Recovered After Pakistan Avalanche

📅 2026-08-03 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nirmal Purja and six other climbers' bodies recovered after Broad Peak avalanche.
  • Three more climbers remain missing; search operations continue.
  • King Charles and Prince William pay tribute to Purja, a former British soldier.
  • Chinese Embassy confirms a Chinese climber missing, activates emergency response.

Rescuers in Pakistan have recovered the bodies of celebrated mountaineer Nirmal Purja and six other climbers, days after a massive avalanche swept through Broad Peak, the world's 12th-highest mountain. The recovery brings a sombre end to a dramatic search operation that had gripped the global climbing community.

Purja, a former British soldier who shot to fame for his record-breaking climbs, was part of a team of ten climbers caught in the avalanche. Officials confirmed the recovery on Sunday, though the identities of the other deceased climbers have not all been officially released. Three climbers remain unaccounted for, and search efforts are still underway in treacherous conditions.

A Record-Breaking Career Cut Short

Nirmal Purja was no ordinary climber. The Nepali-born mountaineer, who served in the British Army's Gurkha regiment, became a global icon in 2019 when he climbed all 14 of the world's 8,000-metre peaks in just six months and six days — a feat previously thought impossible. His exploits were chronicled in the acclaimed documentary 14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible, which brought him international fame.

Purja's death has sent shockwaves through the mountaineering world. Tributes have poured in from around the globe, with many remembering his grit, humour, and relentless pursuit of the impossible. His team, Elite Exped, which he founded to guide climbers on the world's highest peaks, has not yet issued an official statement.

Royal Tributes and International Response

In a rare joint tribute, King Charles and Prince William honoured Purja's service and achievements. The British royals highlighted his military background and his contributions to mountaineering, calling him an inspiration to many. Purja had become a British citizen after his army service, and his death has resonated deeply in the UK.

The Chinese Embassy in Pakistan has also confirmed that a Chinese climber is among the missing, activating a consular emergency response. Officials are coordinating with Pakistani authorities to assist in the ongoing search. The incident underscores the inherent dangers of high-altitude climbing, where even the most experienced mountaineers are at the mercy of nature.

The Search and Recovery Effort

Rescuers battled harsh weather and difficult terrain to reach the victims. The avalanche struck Broad Peak, a mountain known for its steep faces and unpredictable conditions, on the border between Pakistan and China. The recovery operation involved helicopters and ground teams, with officials describing the conditions as extremely challenging.

While seven bodies have been recovered, the search for the remaining three climbers continues. Pakistani authorities have not yet provided a timeline for when the operation might conclude. The families of the victims have been informed, and repatriation efforts are expected to begin in the coming days.

What Happens Next

As the climbing community mourns, questions are being raised about safety protocols on commercial expeditions. The full details of what triggered the avalanche are still unclear, and an investigation is expected. For now, the focus remains on recovering the missing and honouring the legacy of a man who proved that nothing is impossible — until the mountain said otherwise.
