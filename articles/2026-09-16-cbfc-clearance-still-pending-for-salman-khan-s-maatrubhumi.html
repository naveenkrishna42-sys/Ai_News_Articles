No CBFC Relief for Maatrubhumi: Exclusive Ground Report

Contrary to a flurry of reports claiming that Salman Khan's upcoming film Maatrubhumi has received a clearance from the Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC), authoritative sources confirm that no certificate has been issued as of September 15, 2026. The film, directed by Farhad Samji and produced by Sajid Nadiadwala, was submitted to the CBFC's Examining Committee in Mumbai last week. However, the committee has neither recommended any cuts nor granted a 'U/A' or 'A' certificate. This delay directly impacts the film's scheduled theatrical release on October 2, 2026, and its pre-sale deals with OTT platforms and satellite broadcasters.

For exhibitors and distributors, the absence of a CBFC certificate means that advance bookings cannot open, promotional campaigns remain on hold, and the risk of a last-minute postponement looms large. The film's budget is estimated at Rs 120 crore, with Salman Khan commanding a fee of Rs 60 crore. Any delay beyond the Dussehra window could erode 20-30% of the opening weekend revenue, which is critical for recovering investment.

Why the CBFC Certification Is Still Pending

Content Under Scrutiny

Sources within the CBFC indicate that the Examining Committee has flagged certain scenes depicting communal violence and political rallies. The board typically takes 3-7 working days to certify a film, but Maatrubhumi has now been in the queue for over 10 days. This is not unusual for big-ticket films with sensitive themes; in 2024, Article 370 faced a similar delay before receiving a 'U/A' certificate with minor cuts. The producers are reportedly willing to comply with suggested modifications, but no formal communication has been received yet.

Impact on Release Date and Buyer Confidence

Exhibitors in key circuits like Delhi-UP, Mumbai, and Punjab are hesitant to block screens for Maatrubhumi without a confirmed certificate. A multiplex chain executive, requesting anonymity, stated that if the certificate does not arrive by September 20, they will allocate prime screens to holdover releases or regional films. This uncertainty also affects OTT buyers: Netflix and Amazon Prime Video are in advanced negotiations for post-theatrical streaming rights, but the deal is contingent on a clean certification. A delay could push the digital premiere to December 2026, reducing the film's overall valuation.

What This Means for Buyers and Exhibitors

For theatre owners, the lack of certification is a red flag. Advance bookings, which typically contribute 15-20% of a Salman Khan film's opening day collection, cannot commence. This directly impacts cash flow and footfalls. For OTT platforms, the delay introduces a risk of content obsolescence if the film's theme becomes dated. The estimated OTT deal of Rs 90 crore is already 10% lower than initial quotes due to the certification uncertainty. If the film releases after October 15, the digital rights value could drop by another Rs 10-15 crore.

Historical Precedents and Expert View

In 2023, Adipurush faced CBFC delays due to mythological depictions, resulting in a postponed release and a 25% drop in opening day collections. Trade analyst Komal Nahta notes that for a Salman Khan starrer, a delay beyond the announced date erodes audience excitement, especially among single-screen audiences. The producers have not yet issued an official statement, but industry insiders suggest that a revised release date may be announced by September 18 if the certificate is not received.

Final Assessment

The CBFC certification for Maatrubhumi remains pending, and reports of a clearance are premature. Buyers should monitor official CBFC updates and be prepared for a possible shift in release strategy. With Rs 120 crore at stake, the next 72 hours are critical for the film's box office fate.
