Ayodhya Ram Navami: Sacred Sites, Light Show, Visit Guide

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Sacred Places Original source ↗
Key points
  • Signify's lighting project illuminates Ayodhya's sacred sites ahead of Ram Navami
  • Ram Janmabhoomi, Hanuman Garhi, and Saryu ghats are key pilgrimage spots
  • Best visited October to March; Ayodhya is 6 km from Ayodhya Dham Junction

The Radiance Before Ram Navami

On the eve of Ram Navami, Ayodhya is set to glow. Signify, the lighting company, has switched on a new illumination scheme across the city's sacred heritage sites, brightening temples, ghats, and corridors for the millions of devotees expected to throng the birthplace of Lord Ram. The festival, which marks Ram's birth, falls in the Hindu month of Chaitra, and this year the city is preparing for a spectacle of light and devotion.

But the lights are only the latest layer on a city that has been a pilgrimage destination for centuries. Ayodhya's religious geography is dense, and the new lighting is designed to guide visitors through it. This guide covers the core sites, their spiritual weight, and the practical details you need to plan your own darshan.

Ram Janmabhoomi: The Heart of the City

The Ram Janmabhoomi complex is the focal point of Ayodhya's sacred map. The site, where a grand temple now stands, is believed to be the exact birthplace of Lord Ram. The temple architecture follows the Nagara style, with carved pillars, domes, and a sanctum that draws thousands daily. The new Signify lighting accentuates the temple's facade, making it visible from afar, especially at dusk.

Devotees typically queue for hours. The complex includes the Ram Lalla idol, the child form of Ram, and the surrounding courtyard has been landscaped with walkways and gardens. Photography inside the sanctum is prohibited, but the exterior and the adjoining museum offer ample opportunity for reflection. The atmosphere here is one of continuous chant and prayer, a living tradition that predates any modern renovation.

Hanuman Garhi and Other Landmarks

Just a short walk from the Janmabhoomi stands Hanuman Garhi, a hilltop temple dedicated to Hanuman, Ram's greatest devotee. The temple is a fortress-like structure with 76 steps leading to the main shrine. Locals say no visit to Ayodhya is complete without paying respects here first, as Hanuman is believed to guard the city. The temple's oil lamps and the new accent lighting make it a warm beacon at night.

Other significant sites include the Kanak Bhawan, a temple known for its gold-plated idols of Ram and Sita, and the Saryu ghats, where pilgrims perform ritual bathing. The ghats, especially Ram ki Paidi, are now lined with new light fixtures that reflect off the river water, creating a serene evening view. Aarti at the ghats, held each evening, is a sensory experience of fire, bells, and chanting.

Practical Visiting Information

Best time to visit: The winter months, from October to March, offer pleasant weather, with temperatures ranging from 10°C to 25°C. Ram Navami itself, usually in March or April, is the most crowded but also the most vibrant. If you prefer fewer crowds, visit on a regular weekday, avoiding major festivals.

How to reach: Ayodhya is well-connected by rail. The Ayodhya Dham Junction railway station is about 6 km from the temple complex, with regular trains from Lucknow, Varanasi, and Delhi. The nearest airport is Ayodhya's own Maharishi Valmiki International Airport, which has flights from major cities. From the station or airport, auto-rickshaws and taxis are readily available.

Nearby attractions: Combine your visit with a trip to the nearby Nageshwarnath Temple, said to have been built by Kush, Ram's son. Also consider the Ram Katha Museum, which houses artefacts and paintings depicting the Ramayana. The city of Faizabad, just 7 km away, offers the grand Gulab Bari, a mausoleum with lush gardens.

For a comfortable visit, allocate at least two days. Start early at the Janmabhoomi to avoid queues, then walk to Hanuman Garhi before the midday heat. The evening aarti at the Saryu ghats is the perfect end to your pilgrimage, especially with the new lights reflecting on the water.
