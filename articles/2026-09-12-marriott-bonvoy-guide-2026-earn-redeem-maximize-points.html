Why Marriott Bonvoy Still Matters for Indian Travellers

Marriott Bonvoy is the loyalty programme covering 30-plus brands, from budget-friendly Fairfield and Aloft to luxury flagships like St. Regis and Ritz-Carlton. For Indian members, the appeal is straightforward: over 150 hotels across the country, plus thousands more abroad where a single points balance can cover a family holiday. Unlike airline miles that expire in 24-36 months, Bonvoy points stay alive as long as your account shows activity every 24 months, which is an unusually generous rule for infrequent travellers.

NerdWallet's latest guide reaffirms the core mechanics, but the practical question for buyers is simpler: how much is a Bonvoy point actually worth, and which earning route gives the best return?

How Marriott Bonvoy Points Are Earned

Hotel Stays

Members earn 10 base points per US dollar spent at most brands, 5 points at longer-stay properties such as Residence Inn and TownePlace Suites, and 2.5 points at Marriott Vacation Club. Elite tiers stack bonuses on top: Silver adds 10%, Gold 25%, Platinum 50%, Titanium 75%, and Ambassador 75% plus dedicated support. A Gold member spending Rs 1 lakh annually on stays therefore banks roughly 1.25 lakh points, enough for several off-peak nights in a mid-tier Indian property.

Credit Cards and Transfers

Indian members typically earn through co-branded cards issued on the Amex, Visa and Mastercard networks, or by transferring points from American Express Membership Rewards at a 1:1 ratio, often with periodic 30% transfer bonuses. US-issued cards such as the Marriott Bonvoy Brilliant and Boundless offer far larger welcome bonuses, which is why many Indian frequent flyers maintain a US card alongside a domestic one.

Redeeming Points: Where the Real Value Sits

Marriott moved to dynamic award pricing, which means the same hotel can cost 20,000 points on a quiet Tuesday and 60,000 during Diwali week. The sweet spot remains high-category luxury properties during off-peak dates, where cash rates of Rs 40,000-60,000 per night translate into redemption values near Rs 0.90 per point. Budget properties and peak dates often drop below Rs 0.35 per point, at which stage paying cash and saving points is the smarter play.

Cash plus points redemptions let members offset part of a paid stay, but the value is usually weaker than a full award night. Points Advance allows booking before you have the full balance, provided you top up before check-in, a useful tool for locking in low off-peak rates early.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Fifth night free on award stays effectively cuts the per-night cost by 20% on five-night bookings.
  • Elite status unlocks late checkout, lounge access and suite upgrades that frequently outweigh the points themselves.
  • Amex transfer bonuses of 30% materially shift the value equation in favour of transferring rather than paying cash.
  • Dynamic pricing punishes peak-season redemptions; flexibility of two to three weeks can double your value per point.
  • Points expire after 24 months of inactivity, so a small transfer or a single stay resets the clock.

Maximisation Strategy for Indian Members

Stack a co-branded card for everyday spend with Amex Membership Rewards transfers during bonus windows. Concentrate stays within Marriott to hit Gold or Platinum, since the elite bonus compounds every future earning. Book five-night award stays wherever possible to trigger the fifth-night-free benefit, and redeem at luxury properties during off-peak dates rather than burning points on budget hotels. Finally, track dynamic pricing across a two-week window before confirming, as the same room can swing by 20,000 points depending on the date.

Treat Bonvoy as a long-horizon asset: earn aggressively, redeem selectively, and never redeem below roughly Rs 0.50 per point unless you need to clear a balance before expiry.
