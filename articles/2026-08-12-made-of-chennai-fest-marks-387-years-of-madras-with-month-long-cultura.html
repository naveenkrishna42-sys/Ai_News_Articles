Made of Chennai fest marks 387 years of Madras with month-long cultural events

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Month-long cultural festivities celebrate 387 years of Madras
  • Events include talks, performances, and heritage walks
  • The Hindu's Made of Chennai initiative curates the programme

Chennai is set to mark the 387th anniversary of its founding as Madras with a month-long cultural celebration, curated by The Hindu under its Made of Chennai banner. The festivities, which begin this month, will bring together artists, historians, writers, and residents in a series of events spread across the city.

A city older than its name

Madras was founded in 1639, when the British East India Company acquired a strip of land along the Coromandel coast. Over nearly four centuries, it grew from a fishing village into a bustling metropolis, later renamed Chennai in 1996. The anniversary, observed every 22 August, is a moment for the city to reflect on its layered past and evolving identity.

The Made of Chennai programme, now in its third year, has become a fixture in the city's cultural calendar. It aims to tell Chennai's stories through the voices of those who live and shape it — from veteran Carnatic musicians to young street-food vendors.

What the month holds

The schedule includes curated walks through heritage neighbourhoods like George Town and Mylapore, where participants can trace the city's architectural and social evolution. Evening sessions will feature conversations with historians and urban planners, while performance spaces will host classical dance, theatre, and independent music.

One highlight is a series of food trails spotlighting the city's iconic eateries, from century-old filter coffee stalls to modern kitchens reimagining Chettinad cuisine. Organisers say the aim is to make the celebration accessible, with several events free and open to all.

Local voices, shared memories

The festival also opens a digital archive where residents can contribute photographs, anecdotes, and family histories tied to the city. This crowdsourced memory bank, organisers hope, will grow into a living record of Chennai's everyday life.

School students have been invited to participate in storytelling workshops and heritage quizzes, encouraging younger generations to engage with the city's past. A special segment will honour long-standing community institutions — from bookshops and clinics to music sabhas — that have anchored neighbourhoods for decades.

A city in constant motion

Chennai today is a city of contrasts: ancient temples stand beside IT parks, and bustling markets coexist with quiet residential lanes. The anniversary comes at a time when the city is grappling with rapid infrastructure changes, coastal vulnerability, and the pressures of urban growth. Yet its cultural resilience remains a defining trait.

As part of the month's events, a public art project will transform underpasses and walls in select areas with murals inspired by Madras's history. The works, created by local artists, will depict everything from the old Buckingham Canal to the Marina's fishing communities.

The celebrations are expected to draw thousands of participants across the month, with a special finale planned for the founding day itself. Officials from the Greater Chennai Corporation have extended support, though a full list of partner organisations is yet to be announced.

As the city blows out 387 candles, the Made of Chennai festival offers a chance to look back — and to ask what the next chapter holds for a metropolis that has never stopped reinventing itself.
