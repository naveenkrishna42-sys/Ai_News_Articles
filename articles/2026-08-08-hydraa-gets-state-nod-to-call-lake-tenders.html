HYDRAA gets state nod to call lake tenders

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • HYDRAA receives state government approval to call tenders for lake restoration.
  • The move is expected to speed up the revival of lakes across Hyderabad.
  • Officials say the tenders will cover desilting, beautification, and boundary demarcation.

In a significant step towards the revival of Hyderabad's dying water bodies, the Hyderabad Disaster Response and Asset Protection Agency (HYDRAA) has received the state government's nod to call tenders for lake restoration. The approval, granted this week, empowers the agency to initiate the tendering process for the rejuvenation of several lakes under its jurisdiction.

The decision comes amid growing concerns over the deteriorating condition of lakes in and around the city, with many encroached upon or polluted. HYDRAA officials said the tenders will cover a range of activities, including desilting, removal of encroachments, beautification of lake beds, and demarcation of boundaries.

What the approval means

Until now, HYDRAA had to seek separate permissions for each lake-related work, often causing delays. With the blanket approval, the agency can now float tenders for multiple lakes simultaneously, cutting down on bureaucratic red tape.

Officials familiar with the development said the move is aimed at expediting the restoration process, which has been slowed by procedural hurdles. The tenders are expected to be floated within weeks, with work likely to begin in the current financial year.

Scope of work

The restoration plan includes scientific desilting to increase water storage capacity, removal of invasive weeds, and construction of walking tracks and fencing around the lakes. In addition, the agency plans to set up sewage treatment plants (STPs) at select locations to prevent untreated effluents from flowing into the water bodies.

HYDRAA has identified several lakes across the city, including those in the western and northern peripheries, for the first phase of restoration. The agency has also sought the help of the Hyderabad Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board (HMWS&SB) and the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) for coordinated action.

Challenges ahead

While the approval is a positive development, experts point out that the actual restoration will depend on the timely execution of projects and sustained funding. Many lakes in the city have been reduced to sewage-filled pits, and reversing the damage will require long-term commitment.

Encroachments remain a thorny issue, with several structures built on lake beds and buffer zones. HYDRAA has been conducting surveys and demolition drives, but the process is often contested in courts. Officials said the agency will take a firm stance on illegal structures to ensure the lakes are restored to their original extent.

Public support and next steps

Residents living near the lakes have welcomed the move, hoping it will improve the local environment and groundwater levels. Citizen groups have been demanding faster action from the authorities, and the tender approval is seen as a response to those calls.

The agency is expected to publish the tender documents online, inviting bids from qualified contractors. Evaluation criteria will include technical capability, past experience in similar projects, and cost-effectiveness. A monitoring committee will oversee the progress of the work.

As the tenders are floated, all eyes will be on how quickly the restoration translates into visible results. For a city that has lost a significant number of its lakes over the decades, this could be a turning point — provided the momentum is maintained.
