A New Chapter in Israel's Iran Policy?

The political landscape in Israel often sees its core national security doctrines debated and redefined. With the potential rise of figures like Gadi Eisenkot, a former Israel Defense Forces (IDF) Chief of Staff, the nation's long-standing strategy towards Iran faces scrutiny. Benjamin Netanyahu's tenure has been characterized by an uncompromising, often aggressive posture, viewing Iran's nuclear ambitions and regional proxies as an existential threat requiring direct and preemptive action.

Eisenkot, while equally committed to Israel's security, represents a potentially different strategic philosophy rooted in military pragmatism and a calibrated approach to regional power dynamics. His ascendancy could signal a significant pivot, moving away from unilateral, overt confrontation towards a more nuanced strategy focused on deterrence, containment, and strategic cooperation.

Eisenkot's Strategic Doctrine: A Pragmatic Shift?

From Military Chief to Political Challenger

Gadi Eisenkot's background as IDF Chief of Staff from 2015 to 2019 provides a crucial lens through which to understand his potential approach to Iran. During his military leadership, Eisenkot was instrumental in refining the 'war between wars' (MABAM) doctrine. This strategy emphasizes continuous, low-intensity military operations to degrade enemy capabilities and deter escalation, primarily targeting Iranian arms transfers and proxy infrastructure without triggering full-scale regional conflict.

This doctrine contrasts with Netanyahu's more politically charged rhetoric, which often framed Iran as an immediate, existential threat demanding decisive, and sometimes overtly public, action. Eisenkot's approach is typically less about grand pronouncements and more about sustained, intelligence-driven operations designed to maintain Israel's qualitative military edge and strategic initiative.

Divergent Approaches to the Iran Threat

Netanyahu's strategy has consistently prioritized preventing Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons at all costs, often advocating for preemptive strikes and robust international sanctions. His public campaigns against the Iran nuclear deal (JCPOA) and his direct appeals to the US Congress underscored a willingness to challenge international consensus for what he perceived as Israel's vital security interests.

Eisenkot, while sharing the fundamental concern about Iranian nuclearization, might favour a more multi-faceted approach. This includes strengthening regional missile defense systems, enhancing cyber capabilities, and leveraging intelligence assets to monitor and disrupt Iranian activities. His military experience suggests a preference for calculated risk-taking and a clear-eyed assessment of the costs and benefits of direct military engagement, prioritizing effective deterrence over high-profile, potentially destabilising actions.

Regional Implications and Alliance Dynamics

Impact on Abraham Accords and Arab Relations

A shift in Israel's Iran strategy under Eisenkot could have significant implications for its burgeoning relationships with Arab states, particularly those signatory to the Abraham Accords. Many Gulf nations share Israel's concerns about Iranian regional hegemony, but they often prefer a quieter, more coordinated approach to counter-proliferation and regional stability. A less overtly aggressive Israeli stance might foster greater trust and deeper security cooperation with these partners, allowing for a more unified front against Iranian influence.

Conversely, an overly cautious approach could be perceived by some regional allies as a weakening of resolve. Eisenkot would need to skillfully balance deterrence with reassurance, ensuring that a pragmatic shift is not mistaken for appeasement by either allies or adversaries.

The U.S. Factor: A Return to Alignment?

Netanyahu's often confrontational approach to Iran frequently led to friction with successive US administrations, particularly regarding the Iran nuclear deal. His perceived interference in US foreign policy created diplomatic strain. Eisenkot, with his extensive military background and experience working with US defense officials, might foster a more aligned and less contentious relationship with Washington.

A strategy that emphasizes coordinated intelligence sharing, joint military exercises, and a shared understanding of regional threats could restore a more harmonious dynamic between Israel and its primary strategic ally. This alignment would be crucial for navigating complex issues like Iranian nuclear development, Hezbollah's capabilities, and the broader stability of the Middle East.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Eisenkot's military background suggests a focus on intelligence-driven, covert operations and deterrence over large-scale, overt military action against Iran.
  • A potential shift could lead to improved coordination with Abraham Accords signatories and other Arab states, fostering a more unified regional security posture.
  • Eisenkot's pragmatic approach may facilitate better alignment and communication with US foreign policy and defense establishments, reducing diplomatic friction.
  • The 'war between wars' doctrine aims to degrade Iranian capabilities and deter escalation without triggering a full-scale regional conflict.
  • The core objective remains preventing Iranian nuclearization, but the methods employed are likely to evolve under new leadership.
