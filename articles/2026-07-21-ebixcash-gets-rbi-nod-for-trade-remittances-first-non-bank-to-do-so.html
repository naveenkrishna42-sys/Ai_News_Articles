EbixCash gets RBI nod for trade remittances, first non-bank to do so

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • EbixCash secures RBI authorization for outward trade remittances under Section 7 of FEMA.
  • It is the first non-bank entity to receive such approval, previously limited to banks.
  • The move is expected to streamline foreign payments for small and medium enterprises.

RBI grants first non-bank trade remittance license

The Reserve Bank of India has authorized EbixCash to handle outbound trade remittances, making it the first non-bank entity to receive such approval. The license, granted under Section 7 of the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), allows the company to process payments for imports and other trade-related transactions on behalf of its clients.

Until now, only scheduled commercial banks could facilitate outward trade remittances. The RBI's decision opens the door for non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and payment aggregators to enter a space dominated by traditional lenders.

What this means for importers and exporters

For businesses engaged in cross-border trade, the approval means faster and more competitive remittance services. EbixCash can now directly handle foreign currency payments without routing them through a bank, potentially reducing transaction times and costs.

Small and medium enterprises, which often struggle with cumbersome bank procedures for small-value trade payments, stand to benefit the most. The company can offer digital platforms for initiating remittances, cutting down paperwork and manual intervention.

How EbixCash plans to use the license

EbixCash intends to integrate the remittance service with its existing suite of financial products, including forex cards, travel insurance, and cross-border payment solutions for education and healthcare. The company has a network of over 2,500 physical outlets across India, alongside digital channels.

Industry watchers say the move could pressure banks to lower their remittance fees and improve turnaround times. The RBI has been gradually easing norms to allow non-bank players into segments like forex and trade finance, aiming to increase competition and financial inclusion.

Regulatory context and future outlook

The authorization comes under the RBI's broader push to liberalize foreign exchange regulations for non-bank entities. In recent years, the central bank has permitted NBFCs to act as authorized dealers in foreign exchange for certain categories of transactions.

EbixCash must comply with know-your-customer (KYC) norms, anti-money laundering guidelines, and reporting requirements set by the RBI. The company will have to submit periodic statements on the volume and value of remittances processed.

Other non-bank players, including large fintech firms and payment aggregators, are likely to apply for similar licenses in the coming months. The RBI has not indicated a timeline for processing further applications, but the precedent set by EbixCash could accelerate approvals.

Analysts expect the trade remittance market in India to grow as digital infrastructure improves and more entities enter the fray. The immediate focus for EbixCash will be to onboard clients and demonstrate operational capacity under the new regulatory framework.
