Hyderabad-FAST seizes 25,300 kg adulterated spices

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 25,300 kg of cloves, fennel seeds and other spices seized
  • Spices were adulterated with dyes, synthetic colours and talcum powder
  • Hyderabad-FAST conducted the operation; further details awaited

Hyderabad's Food Safety Task Force (FAST) has seized more than 25,300 kilograms of spices, including cloves and fennel seeds, found to be adulterated with dyes, synthetic colours and talcum powder. The operation, details of which emerged on Tuesday, marks one of the largest such crackdowns in the region in recent months.

Scale of the seizure

The confiscated stock includes a significant quantity of cloves and fennel seeds, along with other spices commonly used in Indian households. Officials said the total weight crossed 25,300 kg, though a full itemised breakdown has not yet been released.

The adulterants — dyes, synthetic colours and talcum powder — are typically used to enhance the appearance of spices, making them look fresher or more vibrant. Such practices not only cheat consumers but also pose serious health risks, as synthetic colours and talcum powder are not meant for human consumption.

How the operation unfolded

FAST teams carried out raids at multiple locations, acting on specific intelligence. The exact number of premises searched and the identities of the owners have not been officially confirmed yet.

Officials are now investigating the supply chain, trying to determine where the adulterated spices were sourced from and which markets they were destined for. Forensic analysis of the seized material is expected to establish the exact composition of the adulterants.

Health concerns

Consumption of spices mixed with talcum powder or synthetic dyes can lead to digestive problems, allergic reactions and, in chronic cases, more severe organ damage. Public health experts have repeatedly flagged the need for stricter enforcement, as adulteration remains a persistent issue in the spice trade.

Hyderabad, a major hub for spice processing and export, has seen several such seizures in the past. However, the sheer volume in this case has drawn attention to the scale of the problem.

What happens next

The seized spices will be sent for laboratory testing to confirm the presence and concentration of adulterants. Based on the results, legal action will be initiated against those responsible, which could include fines and imprisonment under the Food Safety and Standards Act.

FAST is also expected to issue a detailed report in the coming days, shedding light on the full extent of the racket. Consumers are advised to buy spices from certified brands and report any suspicious products to the authorities.

As the investigation progresses, more raids are likely in the coming weeks, targeting wholesalers and retailers who may be part of the same supply network.
