Bombay High Court upholds co-op court’s power to add developer in society case

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bombay High Court upheld the co-operative court’s jurisdiction to add a developer as a party in a housing society dispute.
  • The ruling reinforces the principle that developers can be impleaded when their interests are directly affected.
  • The decision provides clarity on the scope of powers under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act.

The Bombay High Court has upheld the authority of a co-operative court to implead a developer as a party in a dispute involving a housing society. The judgment, delivered on Tuesday, clarifies the procedural powers of such courts under the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act.

The case arose from a conflict between a housing society and its members. The developer, who was not originally a party, sought to be impleaded, arguing that the outcome of the dispute would directly affect its interests. The co-operative court allowed the plea, a decision later challenged before the High Court.

High Court’s reasoning

Justice Abhay Ahuja, who presided over the single-judge bench, held that the co-operative court has the inherent power to add or remove parties to ensure a just and effective resolution. The court noted that the developer’s presence was essential to avoid multiplicity of proceedings and to fully adjudicate the matter.

The judgment cited Section 91 of the Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, which grants co-operative courts wide jurisdiction to decide disputes touching the constitution, management, or business of a society. The court ruled that impleading a developer falls within this ambit when the developer’s rights or liabilities are intertwined with the dispute.

Implications for housing societies

Legal experts say the ruling provides much-needed clarity. Developers are often involved in redevelopment or construction projects linked to society disputes. The decision ensures that all stakeholders can be brought before the same forum, reducing delays and conflicting rulings.

Advocate Rohan Shah, who appeared for the developer, said the judgment reaffirms that co-operative courts are not constrained by rigid procedural rules. “The court recognised that the developer’s presence is necessary for a complete and fair hearing,” he said.

Background of the case

The dispute originated from a redevelopment project in suburban Mumbai. The society had entered into an agreement with the developer, but disagreements arose over the terms. Some members filed a case in the co-operative court, seeking to restrain the developer from proceeding. The developer then moved to be impleaded, arguing that it was a necessary party.

The co-operative court allowed the impleadment, but the petitioners—members of the society—challenged this in the High Court. They contended that the co-operative court lacked jurisdiction to add a developer who was not a member of the society.

Court’s response to the challenge

Justice Ahuja dismissed this argument, stating that the co-operative court’s powers are not limited to disputes between members alone. The Act allows the court to decide disputes involving any person who claims an interest in the subject matter, provided that interest is directly affected.

The judge also noted that the developer had a subsisting contract with the society. Excluding the developer from the proceedings could lead to a piecemeal resolution, potentially harming both the society and the developer.

What this means going forward

The ruling is likely to influence how co-operative courts handle similar applications. It sets a precedent that developers with a direct stake in a society’s affairs can be impleaded, even if they are not formal members. This could streamline dispute resolution in redevelopment cases, which are common in Mumbai’s real estate landscape.

Observers point out that the judgment also serves as a check against frivolous objections to impleadment. Parties will now find it harder to argue that a developer should be kept out of a dispute that clearly concerns them.

The matter will now return to the co-operative court for further hearing on the merits of the original dispute. All eyes will be on how the lower court proceeds with the developer now formally a party.
