Indian-American Astronaut Anil Menon Captures Auroras from ISS

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Anil Menon, Indian-American NASA astronaut, observed auroras from the ISS.
  • Simultaneous ground-based visuals from Earth complemented the space imagery.
  • The event underscores increased solar activity and its visual impact on polar regions.

A Cosmic Spectacle from Orbit

Indian-American NASA astronaut Anil Menon has shared striking visuals of auroras as seen from the International Space Station (ISS), offering a rare celestial treat for space enthusiasts. The imagery, captured during a recent pass over the polar regions, shows shimmering curtains of green and purple light dancing across the Earth's limb.

Menon, who is part of the current ISS expedition, took to social media to post the photographs, which quickly garnered attention for their vivid detail. The visuals were not limited to orbit alone—skywatchers on the ground also managed to capture matching auroral displays from high-latitude locations, creating a unique side-by-side perspective of the same phenomenon.

Solar Activity Drives the Display

Auroras occur when charged particles from the Sun interact with Earth's magnetic field and atmosphere. The recent uptick in solar activity has led to more frequent and intense auroral displays, visible even at lower latitudes than usual. Scientists are closely monitoring this period, as the Sun approaches the peak of its 11-year cycle.

The synchronised capture from space and Earth provides valuable data for researchers studying the dynamics of these light shows. By comparing the orbital perspective with ground-based observations, scientists can better understand the spatial extent and intensity of the auroral oval.

A Milestone for Indian-American Representation

Anil Menon's journey to the ISS is itself a notable achievement. Born to Indian parents, he was selected as a NASA astronaut in 2017 and has since become a role model for aspiring spacefarers in India and the diaspora. His recent aurora visuals have been widely shared on Indian social media, with many celebrating his heritage and accomplishments.

Menon's work on the station includes a range of scientific experiments, but it is moments like these—capturing the raw beauty of our planet from above—that resonate most with the public. His images serve as a reminder of the fragile and awe-inspiring world we inhabit.

What's Next

As solar activity continues to rise, more auroral displays are expected over the coming months. Astronauts aboard the ISS are likely to capture even more spectacular imagery, while ground-based observatories and amateur photographers will keep their cameras trained on the skies. For now, Menon's photographs stand as a testament to the wonders of space exploration and the shared experience of looking up at the same night sky.
