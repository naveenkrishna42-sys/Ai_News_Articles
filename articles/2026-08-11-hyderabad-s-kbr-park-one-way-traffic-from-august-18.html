Hyderabad's KBR Park one-way traffic from August 18

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • One-way traffic system will be implemented around KBR Park from August 18.
  • Move aims to ease congestion and improve traffic flow in the area.
  • Commuters are advised to follow new routes and signage.
  • Traffic police will deploy personnel to manage the transition.

Hyderabad's KBR Park will see a new traffic arrangement starting August 18, with authorities implementing a one-way system around the park. The decision, announced on Tuesday, is aimed at easing congestion in the area, which has long struggled with heavy vehicular movement during peak hours.

The move comes as part of ongoing efforts to streamline traffic flow in the city's central zones. The park, a popular spot for morning and evening walkers, sees significant footfall, and the surrounding roads often get choked, especially on weekends.

What the new system entails

Under the one-way scheme, vehicles will be allowed to move in a single direction along the roads encircling KBR Park. The exact route details and entry/exit points have been outlined by the traffic police, who have urged commuters to familiarise themselves with the changes before the implementation date.

Signage and road markings are being put in place to guide motorists. Authorities have also indicated that the system will be monitored closely in the initial days, with adjustments made if necessary.

Impact on daily commuters

Residents and daily commuters in the Jubilee Hills and Banjara Hills areas are likely to feel the impact. Those who regularly use the roads around KBR Park to cut through the city may need to plan alternate routes.

The traffic police have advised commuters to factor in extra travel time during the first few days of the new arrangement. While the one-way system is expected to reduce bottlenecks, the transition period could see some confusion as drivers get used to the changed flow.

Authorities' preparations

Additional traffic personnel will be deployed at key junctions around the park to assist motorists and ensure smooth movement. The police have also said that they will be using social media and local channels to spread awareness about the new rules.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the system will be permanent or subject to review after a trial period. However, similar measures in other parts of the city have reportedly helped in reducing travel times.

For now, the focus is on making the transition as seamless as possible. Commuters are encouraged to follow the new signage and instructions from traffic personnel on the ground.

As August 18 approaches, all eyes will be on how the one-way system performs in real-world conditions. The traffic police have indicated they will assess the impact over the following weeks, and further tweaks cannot be ruled out if the need arises.
