IHG One Rewards 100% Bonus: The Core Offer

IHG One Rewards has rolled out a 100% bonus on purchased points, a recurring promotion that effectively doubles every point you buy. The standard rate is 1 US cent per point, so a 100% bonus brings the effective cost down to 0.5 cents (roughly ₹0.42) per point. That is the number every buyer must anchor to before swiping a card.

This is not a flash sale on a single property. It is a straight points purchase promotion, meaning the points land in your IHG One Rewards account and can be redeemed across the entire portfolio, from Holiday Inn Express to InterContinental and Six Senses. The value depends entirely on how you redeem, not on how you buy.

Buyers should confirm the exact cap and bonus ceiling in their own account, as IHG varies these terms by member and region. Indian cardholders will also see a foreign exchange markup if the transaction is billed overseas, which quietly adds 2-3.5% to the true cost.

Where the Math Actually Works

High-Category Redemptions

InterContinental and Six Senses properties routinely price at 70,000 to 100,000 points per night. At 0.5 cents per point, that is $350 to $500 (roughly ₹29,000 to ₹42,000) in effective cost. If the cash rate for the same night is ₹55,000 or higher, buying points is a clear win.

Low-Category and Cash-Plus-Points Stays

At Holiday Inn Express or Crowne Plaza properties, award rates often hover near 20,000 to 30,000 points. That translates to $100 to $150 in effective cost, which frequently loses to a discounted cash rate or a corporate code. Run the comparison night by night rather than assuming points always win.

Point Transfers and Top-Ups

IHG allows members to top up an existing balance to reach an award threshold. This is the strongest use case for a small purchase, because you are buying the final few thousand points to unlock a redemption rather than speculatively stockpiling.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Effective rate: 0.5 US cents per point, one of the better recurring IHG purchase offers.
  • Sweet spot: InterContinental, Regent, and Six Senses redemptions during peak pricing.
  • Weak spot: Low-category hotels where cash rates are already discounted.
  • FX drag: Indian cards billed in USD attract a 2-3.5% markup, raising the real cost.
  • Cap risk: Bonus points are usually capped per account per year, limiting bulk buying.
  • Flexibility: Purchased points carry no expiry while the account remains active.

Buyer Implications and Verdict

This promotion rewards travellers with a specific redemption already in mind. Buying points blindly to hold as a speculative asset is poor value, because IHG periodically devalues award charts and the effective 0.5-cent floor is not guaranteed to beat future cash rates.

For a family booking a peak-season InterContinental stay, the savings can run into tens of thousands of rupees. For a solo traveller booking a midweek Holiday Inn, the same purchase is likely a loss. The bonus is real, the math is simple, and the discipline required is entirely yours.
