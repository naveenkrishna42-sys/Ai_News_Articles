Pat Cummins returns as Australia announce Test squad for Bangladesh series

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pat Cummins, Josh Hazlewood, and Nathan Lyon return for the Bangladesh Test series.
  • Opener Jake Weatherald has been recalled to the squad.
  • The selection reignites the debate around Australia's bowling attack with all frontline quicks available.

Cummins, Hazlewood, Lyon back in the fold

Australia have announced a formidable Test squad for the upcoming home series against Bangladesh, with captain Pat Cummins, Josh Hazlewood, and Nathan Lyon all returning to the side. The trio had been rested or managed in recent assignments but are now set to lead the bowling attack against the visiting Tigers.

The selection marks a significant boost for the hosts, who are looking to consolidate their position in the World Test Championship standings. Bangladesh, known for their spin-friendly conditions, will face a pace-heavy Australian attack on home soil.

Boland debate reignites

The return of the full set of frontline quicks has reignited the debate around Scott Boland's place in the side. With Cummins, Hazlewood, and Mitchell Starc all fit, the question of who makes the final XI has become a talking point among selectors and pundits.

Boland has been a consistent performer in recent Tests, but the availability of the 'big three' could push him to the sidelines. Cricket Australia's selection panel will have a tough call to make when the first Test begins.

Weatherald earns recall

Opener Jake Weatherald has been recalled to the Test squad, adding depth to the batting order. The left-hander has been in solid domestic form and provides an option at the top of the order alongside Usman Khawaja.

Weatherald's inclusion suggests the selectors are looking at long-term planning, with an eye on both the current series and future assignments. His ability to counter spin could be crucial against Bangladesh's slow bowlers.

The squad also includes several all-round options, giving the team flexibility in conditions that may assist both pace and spin.

What to watch

The series is scheduled to begin later this month, with the first Test expected to be played at a venue that traditionally offers pace and bounce. All eyes will be on the final XI announcement and how the selectors balance experience with form.
