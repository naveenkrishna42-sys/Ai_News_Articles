Chennai: Woman and infant daughter killed in container lorry accident

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A woman and her infant daughter died in a road accident in Chennai.
  • The accident occurred near Perambur when a container lorry hit their two-wheeler.
  • The lorry driver has been arrested by the police.

Mother and child killed in Perambur collision

A woman and her six-month-old daughter were killed on Sunday after a container lorry hit the two-wheeler they were riding in Chennai. The accident took place near the Perambur area, a busy stretch known for heavy vehicle movement.

The victims were identified as residents of the local neighbourhood. The woman was riding pillion with her infant in her lap when the lorry struck them from behind, according to preliminary police reports. Both died on the spot.

The driver of the container lorry has been arrested. Police are investigating whether the driver was speeding or negligent. Officials have not yet confirmed if the driver was under the influence of alcohol.

Eyewitness accounts and immediate response

Eyewitnesses told the police that the lorry was moving at a high speed and appeared to lose control before hitting the two-wheeler. The impact threw the woman and the infant off the vehicle. Locals rushed to the spot but could not save them.

Police arrived shortly after the incident and sent the bodies to a government hospital for postmortem. The two-wheeler was badly mangled. A case has been registered under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code and the Motor Vehicles Act.

Rising concerns over road safety in Chennai

This is the latest in a series of fatal accidents involving heavy vehicles in Chennai. In recent months, several accidents have been reported on arterial roads where container lorries and trucks frequently ply. Activists have repeatedly called for stricter enforcement of traffic rules and better road design.

According to Chennai Traffic Police data, over 300 people died in road accidents in the city last year. A significant number of these involved two-wheeler riders and pedestrians. The police have announced special drives to target overspeeding and drunk driving, but accidents continue to occur.

Road safety experts point out that the lack of dedicated lanes for heavy vehicles and poor lighting on certain stretches contribute to the risk. They urge authorities to implement immediate measures such as speed governors on lorries and more frequent patrolling.

What happens next

The arrested driver will be produced before a magistrate on Monday. Police are examining the lorry's mechanical condition and checking if it had valid permits. Meanwhile, the victims' family members have been informed. The case is likely to be fast-tracked given the involvement of an infant's death, but a timeline for trial has not been set. Traffic police are expected to review safety measures on the stretch where the accident occurred.
