Russia floats rail link to India via Iran, Afghanistan, Pakistan

📅 2026-08-09 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Russia has floated a proposal for a direct rail link to India via Iran, Afghanistan, and Pakistan, calling it 'viable'.
  • The route is seen as a way to reduce dependence on the Strait of Hormuz for trade.
  • The proposal comes amid an energy crisis and shifting geopolitical dynamics.
  • No official agreements or timelines have been announced yet.

Moscow's latest connectivity pitch

Russia has floated a proposal to build a direct railway link connecting it to India, routing through Iran, Afghanistan, and Pakistan. Moscow has described the plan as 'viable', though it remains at a conceptual stage with no formal agreements or timelines announced.

The proposal, reported by multiple outlets including The Times of India and NDTV, is part of a broader push by Russia to expand its transport corridors amid Western sanctions and shifting trade patterns. If realised, it would allow passengers and freight to travel overland from Russia to the Indian subcontinent, bypassing sea routes entirely.

Why the route matters

The proposed corridor would traverse Iran, Afghanistan, and Pakistan before reaching India. Its strategic significance lies in bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway through which roughly a fifth of global oil consumption passes. For Russia, which is increasingly looking east and south for trade partners, a rail link to India would shorten transit times and reduce exposure to maritime chokepoints.

Energy considerations also play a role. With Europe cutting back on Russian gas imports, Moscow is seeking alternative markets for its hydrocarbons. India, a major energy importer, has emerged as a key buyer of Russian crude in recent years. A direct rail route could facilitate not just energy trade but also the movement of goods, machinery, and agricultural products.

Geopolitical complications

However, the proposal faces significant hurdles. Afghanistan remains politically volatile, with no universally recognised government since the Taliban's return to power in 2021. Pakistan and India have a fraught relationship, with rail connectivity between the two nations suspended for decades. Iran, meanwhile, operates under heavy international sanctions, complicating any large-scale infrastructure investment.

Despite these challenges, Moscow appears to be pushing the idea as part of a larger vision for Eurasian connectivity. The route would complement the existing International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC), a multi-modal project that links Russia, Iran, and India via ship and rail. A dedicated railway would be a more direct and potentially faster alternative.

What officials have said

Russian officials have not publicly detailed the technical feasibility, estimated costs, or financing mechanisms for the project. No bilateral agreements have been signed with Iran, Afghanistan, or Pakistan regarding the rail link. Indian authorities have also not issued an official response to the proposal.

The Times of India report suggests that Moscow's framing of the plan as 'viable' is aimed at generating interest among partner nations. However, experts note that such mega-infrastructure projects in the region have historically faced delays, funding shortfalls, and political friction. The absence of a stable transit route through Afghanistan remains the single biggest obstacle.

What to watch

Observers will look for signals from Tehran, Islamabad, and New Delhi in the coming months. Any concrete movement would likely begin with a feasibility study or a trilateral memorandum of understanding. For now, the proposal remains an ambitious idea — one that could reshape regional trade if political will and funding align.
