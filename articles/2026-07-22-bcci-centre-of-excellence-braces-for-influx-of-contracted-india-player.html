BCCI Centre of Excellence braces for influx of contracted India players

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • BCCI's Centre of Excellence expects a large number of contracted players to arrive for training and recovery.
  • The influx is linked to a packed international calendar and targeted fitness programs.
  • Logistical arrangements are being scaled up to handle the increased player traffic.

Centre of Excellence gears up for player surge

The BCCI's Centre of Excellence (CoE) in Bengaluru is bracing for a significant influx of centrally contracted India players in the coming weeks. Sources indicate the facility is preparing for a near-full house as the national team's schedule intensifies.

The sudden spike in player traffic is directly linked to a combination of recovery from recent tours and pre-series conditioning camps. The CoE, which replaced the National Cricket Academy (NCA), is now the central hub for player management.

Targeted programs and packed calendar

Each player arriving at the CoE will be on a targeted program designed by the support staff. These programs range from injury rehabilitation to strength and conditioning work, tailored to individual needs before the next assignment.

With the Indian team playing across formats almost year-round, the CoE has become a revolving door. Players not in the playing XI often report here for intensive work rather than returning to their home states.

Logistical challenge for the board

Managing such a large group of elite athletes under one roof presents a logistical challenge. The CoE has expanded its accommodation and training facilities in recent years, but the upcoming volume is expected to test those limits.

Support staff, including physiotherapists, trainers, and nutritionists, are being rostered in shifts to ensure round-the-clock availability. The board has not officially commented on the exact numbers expected, but insiders say it will be the highest concurrent attendance since the facility's upgrade.

The situation highlights the BCCI's growing emphasis on centralised player oversight. By bringing all contracted players under one roof, the board aims to standardise fitness benchmarks and reduce injury risks.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on how the CoE manages this traffic jam without compromising the quality of individual programs. The effectiveness of this centralised model will be tested in the coming series.
