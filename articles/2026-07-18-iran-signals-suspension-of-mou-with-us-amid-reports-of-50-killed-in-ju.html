Iran Signals Suspension of MoU With US Amid Reports of 50 Killed in July Strikes

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran indicates it is suspending ceasefire commitments under a MoU with the US.
  • Reports claim 50 people were killed in US strikes in July, though official figures remain unconfirmed.
  • Two US service members were killed, one is missing after an Iranian missile strike in Jordan.
  • Iran warns Gulf states hosting US troops of a 'corresponding response' as tensions widen.

Iran Signals Shift in Ceasefire Stance

Iran has suggested it is suspending a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with the United States, a move that effectively puts the ceasefire agreement on hold. The announcement comes amid a week of intensified American strikes, with reports emerging that 50 people were killed in US operations in July.

An Iranian official, quoted by state media, described the MoU as 'suspended' without providing a timeline for its potential reinstatement. The development marks a significant deterioration in the fragile understanding that had briefly de-escalated hostilities between the two nations.

Casualty Reports and Unconfirmed Figures

Reports from the region claim that US strikes in July have resulted in at least 50 deaths. These figures have not been independently verified, and officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of casualties. The strikes were part of a broader American campaign targeting Iranian-linked positions in the Middle East.

The lack of official confirmation has left room for conflicting narratives. Iran has alleged higher numbers, while US military spokespersons have declined to comment on specific operational details.

Escalation on the Ground: US Casualties in Jordan

The conflict has exacted a toll on American forces as well. Two US service members were killed, and one remains missing following an Iranian missile strike in Jordan on Friday. The attack, which targeted a logistics hub, represents one of the deadliest single incidents for US troops since the escalation began.

Search operations for the missing service member are ongoing, with US Central Command stating that 'every effort is being made to locate and recover' the individual. The incident has prompted a review of force protection measures across the region.

Iran Warns Gulf States of ‘Corresponding Response’

In a parallel development, Iran has issued a stark warning to Gulf states that host US military personnel. Tehran's foreign ministry stated that any nation permitting its soil to be used for offensive operations against Iran would face a 'corresponding response'.

The warning specifically targets countries like Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, and Bahrain, which maintain significant American military installations. Analysts see this as an attempt to widen the conflict's psychological front, pressuring Gulf nations to distance themselves from Washington's campaign.

Diplomatic Fallout and Rhetoric Intensifies

The diplomatic language has grown sharper. An Iranian envoy, Mojtaba, reportedly described the US approach to the nuclear deal as 'worthless, devoid of credibility'. This rhetoric mirrors the hardening of positions on both sides, with little room for negotiation visible.

The US State Department has not yet responded directly to Iran's MoU suspension, but a spokesperson reiterated Washington's commitment to 'defending its interests and allies' in the region. The coming days may see further retaliatory actions as both sides test each other's red lines.

The situation remains fluid. What to watch next: whether Iran follows through on its threat to target Gulf state infrastructure, and if the US escalates its air campaign in response to the Jordan attack.
