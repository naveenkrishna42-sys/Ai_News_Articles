Mumbai Drivers Scale the 'Marathi Mile' on New Flyover

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The 'Marathi Mile' flyover has opened in Mumbai, connecting key arterial roads.
  • Drivers face steep climbs and sharp turns, requiring careful navigation.
  • The flyover aims to reduce travel time, but some find the gradient challenging.

A New Test of Nerves on Mumbai's Roads

Mumbai's newest flyover, colloquially dubbed the 'Marathi Mile', has thrown a fresh challenge at the city's drivers. The elevated corridor, which opened to traffic this week, connects two of the city's busiest arteries. But it is not just the promise of shorter commutes that has commuters talking; it is the sheer steepness of the structure.

The flyover rises at a sharp gradient, demanding a steady foot on the accelerator and a firm grip on the steering wheel. For motorists used to Mumbai's flat, long stretches, this incline is a new kind of hurdle. The structure's curves, too, have been drawing attention, with some sections requiring drivers to reduce speed drastically to maintain control.

Why the Steep Design?

Engineers explain that the gradient was dictated by the limited land available at the junction. To achieve the required height while spanning the existing road network, a steeper than usual incline was inevitable. The design, they say, prioritises structural integrity over driver comfort, a trade-off that is common in dense urban environments.

Local commuters, however, are divided. Some praise the flyover for cutting travel time by nearly 15 minutes during peak hours. Others, particularly those on two-wheelers, express nervousness about the climb, especially during the monsoon when the surface can get slippery. "It feels like climbing a hill," one rider said, speaking to Mumbai Mirror.

Early Days, Mixed Reactions

In the first 48 hours since opening, traffic police have reported no major incidents, though minor slowdowns have been noted at the crest where vehicles tend to lose momentum. The Mumbai traffic police have deployed additional personnel at the base and exit points to guide drivers unfamiliar with the structure.

Some motorists have already started seeking alternative routes to avoid the flyover altogether. Auto-rickshaw drivers, in particular, have been vocal about the difficulty of hauling their three-wheelers up the slope with a full load. "It puts extra strain on the engine," said one driver, waiting at the base for a fare, "but if it saves time, we have to use it."

What Lies Ahead

The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) has said it will monitor the flyover's performance over the next month. Officials have not yet confirmed whether any design modifications are being considered, but they have assured the public that regular maintenance checks are in place. For now, the 'Marathi Mile' stands as a testament to Mumbai's willingness to push boundaries—literally and figuratively—in its quest to keep the city moving.

As more drivers become accustomed to the incline, the initial anxiety may well fade. But the flyover's real test will come during the full force of the monsoon, when rain and wind will challenge even the most seasoned drivers. The city will be watching closely.
