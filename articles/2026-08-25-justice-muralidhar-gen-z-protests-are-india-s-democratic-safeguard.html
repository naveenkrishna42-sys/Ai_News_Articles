Justice Muralidhar: Gen Z Protests Are India's Democratic Safeguard

Key points
  • Justice S Muralidhar dismisses state-sponsored labels intended to delegitimize youth dissent.
  • Gen Z's political engagement signals a shift from passive observation to active rights-based advocacy.
  • The persistence of student-led protests suggests traditional political silencing tactics are losing effectiveness.

The Resilience of Dissent

Former Justice S Muralidhar has struck a chord in the current political climate by framing the ongoing Gen Z-led protests as the ultimate guarantor of Indian democracy. In an era where the state frequently deploys rhetoric like 'Dimaghi Naxal'—a term used to discredit intellectuals and student activists—Muralidhar argues that such labels are failing to achieve their intended purpose of intimidation.

The veteran jurist suggests that these movements are not merely transient expressions of anger. They represent a fundamental shift in how the younger generation perceives its relationship with the state. By resisting categorization as 'anti-national' or 'urban naxal,' these protesters are effectively reclaiming the narrative of patriotism.

Beyond the 'Dimaghi Naxal' Rhetoric

The label 'Dimaghi Naxal' is a calculated political tool designed to paint dissent as a form of intellectual subversion. However, its overuse has led to a noticeable decline in its potency. When an entire demographic is labeled for questioning policy, the label loses its sting and instead serves as a badge of critical inquiry.

Muralidhar’s analysis highlights a critical tension: the state’s reliance on fear-based governance versus the youth's reliance on rights-based protest. History suggests that when the state attempts to delegitimize legitimate grievances through name-calling, it inadvertently galvanizes the very groups it seeks to suppress.

Why This Matters for India

The political significance of this trend cannot be overstated. As Gen Z moves into the workforce and voting booths, their skepticism toward state narratives will likely shape policy debates for decades. This generation is not asking for favors; they are demanding institutional accountability.

The following factors define the current friction between the establishment and the youth:

  • Information Decentralization: State-controlled media narratives are being bypassed by peer-to-peer digital communication.
  • Institutional Skepticism: There is a growing demand for transparency in educational and legal systems.
  • Resistance to Stigma: The 'Naxal' label no longer forces students into silence, but rather encourages them to document their protests more thoroughly.

Analytical Outlook

If the state continues to rely on binary labeling, it risks complete alienation of the youth vote. Muralidhar’s observation serves as a warning: democracy survives not through the silence of its people, but through their active participation. As long as the youth continue to challenge the 'Dimaghi Naxal' trope, the democratic fabric remains intact, albeit strained under the weight of excessive securitization.
