International Moon Day Celebrations at BITM Set for July 20

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • BITM will celebrate International Moon Day on July 20 with a series of science programmes.
  • Events include model rocketry demonstrations, lunar exhibits and a sky observation session.
  • The museum aims to educate visitors about lunar exploration and India's Chandrayaan missions.

Kolkata's Birla Industrial and Technological Museum (BITM) is gearing up to mark International Moon Day on July 20. The museum has lined up a day-long programme to celebrate humanity's lunar achievements and inspire the next generation of space enthusiasts.

Programme Highlights

The celebrations will kick off with a special exhibition featuring lunar imagery, models of spacecraft and detailed panels on the Moon's geology. Visitors can expect to see scale models of India's Chandrayaan orbiters and landers, alongside international missions like Apollo and Chang'e.

A series of talks by scientists and educators will cover topics ranging from the Moon's formation to the future of lunar bases. BITM officials said the sessions are designed for both students and general visitors.

Interactive Activities for All Ages

Children can participate in hands-on workshops where they will build and launch simple model rockets. Museum educators will guide them through the basics of propulsion and orbital mechanics.

Another highlight is a lunar surface simulation zone, where visitors can try their hand at operating a rover over a mock-up of the Moon's terrain. The activity aims to explain the challenges of remote exploration.

Sky Observation and Telescope Viewing

Weather permitting, BITM will set up telescopes on its premises after sunset. Visitors will get a chance to observe the Moon's craters, maria and highlands up close. Museum staff will be on hand to point out features like the Sea of Tranquility and the Tycho crater.

This segment is expected to draw the largest crowd. BITM has advised early arrival to avoid long queues.

Chandrayaan and India's Lunar Legacy

The museum has dedicated a corner to India's lunar missions, including Chandrayaan-1 and Chandrayaan-2. Information boards detail the discovery of water molecules on the Moon by Chandrayaan-1 and the lessons learned from the Chandrayaan-2 lander's hard touchdown.

A short documentary on the upcoming Chandrayaan-3 mission will also be screened at regular intervals throughout the day.

Entry and Registration Details

All activities are included in the regular museum entry ticket. However, some workshops require prior registration due to limited capacity. Interested visitors can register online through BITM's official website.

The museum will remain open until 8 pm on July 20. Additional security and crowd management measures will be in place.

International Moon Day was proclaimed by the United Nations General Assembly in 2021, with July 20 chosen to mark the anniversary of the Apollo 11 lunar landing in 1969.

BITM's celebration is part of a global push to promote public engagement with lunar science and exploration. The museum expects to host several thousand visitors across the day-long event.
