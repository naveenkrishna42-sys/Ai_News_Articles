Why 'Green Prescriptions' for Hormone Balance Are Gaining Ground

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Green prescriptions involve structured outdoor activities like walking or gardening to improve health.
  • Doctors cite stress reduction and circadian rhythm regulation as key mechanisms for hormone balance.
  • The trend is growing in India as clinics and wellness programmes adopt nature-based interventions.

The Rise of Nature-Based Health Advice

A quiet shift is underway in Indian clinics. Doctors are increasingly writing 'green prescriptions' — not for pills, but for time spent outdoors. The goal: better hormone balance.

These prescriptions are not vague advice to 'go outside'. They are structured recommendations. A patient might be told to walk for 30 minutes in a park, tend a garden twice a week, or sit under a tree for 15 minutes daily.

The trend is catching on. Wellness centres and even some corporate health programmes are integrating green prescriptions into their protocols. Patients are taking them seriously.

What Doctors Say About the Mechanism

Physicians point to two main reasons for the boom. First, stress reduction. Chronic stress raises cortisol, a hormone that disrupts the balance of oestrogen, progesterone, and testosterone. Time in green spaces lowers cortisol.

Second, natural light exposure regulates the body's internal clock. This circadian rhythm controls the release of melatonin and other hormones. Disrupted sleep patterns are common in urban India, and green prescriptions offer a simple fix.

Doctors also note that physical activity, even light movement like walking, improves insulin sensitivity. This helps prevent metabolic disorders linked to hormonal imbalances, such as PCOS.

Why Now? The Urban Context

The surge is tied to urbanisation. More Indians live in cities with limited access to nature. Screen time is high, outdoor time is low. People are reporting fatigue, mood swings, and irregular cycles.

Green prescriptions are a low-cost intervention. They require no expensive equipment or medicines. They also address the root cause — lifestyle — rather than just symptoms.

Some hospitals in Delhi and Mumbai have started pilot programmes. Patients receive a 'nature plan' along with their regular treatment. Early feedback indicates better compliance and improved outcomes.

Evidence and Caution

The evidence base is growing. Studies from Japan and Europe show that 'forest bathing' — mindful walks in woods — reduces cortisol and blood pressure. Indian researchers are now studying similar effects in local parks.

But doctors caution that green prescriptions are not a replacement for medical care. They work best as a complement. Patients with severe hormonal issues still need traditional treatment.

Another concern is accessibility. Not everyone has a park nearby. In dense cities, finding safe, clean green space can be a challenge. Some doctors prescribe indoor plants or window views as alternatives.

What Happens Next

The trend is expected to grow as more clinics adopt the approach. The government is also taking note — some states are considering including green prescriptions in public health guidelines.

For now, the message from doctors is simple: step outside. Even 20 minutes a day can make a difference. And for many patients, that small change is already starting to balance their hormones.
