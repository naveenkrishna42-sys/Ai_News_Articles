Ethanol boom raises poultry feed costs in South India

📅 2026-08-31 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ethanol production is diverting maize from poultry feed, raising costs
  • Poultry farmers in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka are facing financial distress
  • Government and industry efforts to address the issue are underway

Maize Shortage Hits Poultry Farmers

Maize, a staple feed for poultry farmers in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, is in short supply due to its diversion for ethanol production. This has led to a significant increase in feed costs, putting poultry farmers in financial distress.

Impact on Poultry Industry

The poultry industry in these states is a significant economic contributor. According to the Indian Poultry Association, the industry employs over 1.5 million people and contributes around 1.2% to India's GDP.

However, the current situation is threatening this livelihood. Farmers are reporting a 20-30% increase in feed costs, making it difficult to sustain their operations.

Government and Industry Response

The government and industry bodies are aware of the issue. The Tamil Nadu and Karnataka governments have announced measures to support the poultry industry. These include subsidies on feed and electricity, and incentives for farmers to switch to alternative feed sources.

The Indian Poultry Association has also called for a coordinated effort to address the issue. They have proposed a national policy to regulate the diversion of maize for ethanol production.

Broader Implications

This issue is not just about poultry farmers. It has broader implications for the food security and economic stability of South India. The poultry industry is a key player in the region's food supply chain, and any disruption to it can have cascading effects.

Moreover, the issue highlights the need for a balanced approach to biofuel production. While ethanol is a renewable energy source, its production should not come at the cost of food security and livelihoods.

Looking Ahead

Looking ahead, the poultry industry in Tamil Nadu and Karnataka needs to diversify its feed sources. This can be achieved by promoting the cultivation of alternative feed crops, such as sorghum and broken rice.

Additionally, there is a need for a more transparent and regulated approach to maize diversion for ethanol production. This can help ensure that food security and livelihoods are not compromised for the sake of biofuel production.
