Activists Press for Sterilisation-First Approach in Bengaluru

Formal Plea Submitted to CM Office

Animal welfare organisations in Bengaluru have formally urged Chief Minister Siddaramaiah to prioritise mass sterilisation of street dogs over permanent confinement. The joint submission, backed by over a dozen NGOs, calls for scaling up the existing Animal Birth Control (ABC) programme rather than resorting to long-term kennelling of strays.

Why Sterilisation Over Confinement?

Activists argue that sterilisation offers a humane and sustainable solution to control the stray dog population. They cite data from the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) showing that areas with regular sterilisation drives report fewer dog bites and lower community complaints. Permanent confinement, they warn, leads to overcrowded shelters and increased aggression among canines.

Rising Concerns Over Dog Incidents

The appeal comes amid growing public concern over dog bite incidents and territorial conflicts in residential areas. According to BBMP health records, over 1,200 dog bite cases were reported in the city between January and September this year. Activists stress that proactive sterilisation can gradually reduce such incidents without resorting to reactive capture-and-confine measures.

State Plans to Expand ABC Programme

The Karnataka Animal Husbandry Department is reportedly considering an expansion of its ABC programme, which includes vaccinating, sterilising, and releasing street dogs. Officials are evaluating partnerships with private veterinary clinics and mobile sterilisation units to increase outreach. The initiative aligns with guidelines issued by the Animal Welfare Board of India and directions from the Supreme Court in earlier petitions.

Legal Backing for Sterilisation Strategy

Legal experts tracking animal welfare law say the activists’ stance is supported by Supreme Court rulings that emphasise sterilisation as the primary method for managing stray dog populations. They note that while permanent confinement may be legally permissible in exceptional cases, routine capture of healthy dogs is discouraged under current judicial precedents and central policies.
