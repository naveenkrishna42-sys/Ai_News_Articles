Greater Bengaluru Authority Issues E-Khatas for Irregular Properties

In a significant development for Bengaluru's real estate sector, a Special Committee (SC) within the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) has initiated the issuance of e-khatas for flats previously constructed without full adherence to urban planning norms. This decision, reported by The New Indian Express, marks a pivotal shift in how the metropolitan authority is addressing the long-standing issue of unauthorized constructions, aiming to integrate these properties into the formal revenue system.

The move, while promising to bring thousands of properties into the tax net and offer some semblance of legality to their owners, has sparked considerable debate among urban planners, legal experts, and potential homebuyers. Understanding the nuances of this policy is critical for anyone involved in Bengaluru's property market.

Understanding E-Khata and Property Regularization Context

An e-khata is a digital record of property ownership, crucial for paying property taxes, obtaining building licenses, and availing utility connections. Traditionally, properties with significant deviations from approved plans or built on unapproved layouts were often denied 'A' khatas, leading to a parallel 'B' khata system that restricted property owners' rights and market liquidity. The GBA's current directive appears to be an attempt to formalize properties that have existed in a legal grey area, potentially under a regularization scheme.

Historically, Karnataka has grappled with regularization efforts, most notably the 'Akrama-Sakrama' scheme, which aimed to regularize unauthorized constructions and land encroachments by levying fines. While previous attempts faced legal hurdles, the current GBA initiative suggests a refined approach to addressing urban sprawl and unplanned development. This scheme likely targets specific categories of violations, such as minor setback deviations or FSI (Floor Space Index) infringements, rather than fundamentally illegal constructions on government land or ecologically sensitive zones.

Implications for Homebuyers and the Real Estate Market

For current owners of properties now eligible for e-khatas, this move offers a path towards legitimization, enabling them to pay property taxes and access basic municipal services more easily. However, for prospective buyers, the situation demands careful scrutiny. An e-khata, while essential, does not automatically confer complete legality or immunity from future challenges, especially if the underlying construction deviation is significant.

The value proposition for buyers hinges on the extent of regularization. Properties with minor deviations that are now 'e-khata ready' might see improved marketability. Conversely, properties with substantial violations, even with an e-khata, could still face hurdles in resale, securing housing loans from major financial institutions, or obtaining occupancy certificates that denote full compliance. Buyers must conduct thorough due diligence, including verifying the specific nature of the original deviation and the precise terms of the regularization under which the e-khata is being issued.

Navigating the Legal and Financial Landscape

The issuance of e-khatas for irregular properties will likely involve significant penalties or regularization fees levied by the GBA. These fees, which could be a percentage of the property's guidance value, represent a new revenue stream for the authority but also add to the overall cost for property owners. Buyers should factor in these potential past liabilities when considering such a purchase.

From a financial institution's perspective, while an e-khata aids in property tax payments, banks typically require a clear title and an Occupancy Certificate (OC) for significant loan amounts. If the e-khata is issued for a property that still lacks a full OC due to unresolved major violations, securing a mortgage could remain challenging. This distinction is crucial: an e-khata regularizes the *revenue record*, not necessarily the *construction legality* in its entirety.

The Way Forward: Urban Planning and Accountability

This GBA decision underscores the complex challenges of urban development in rapidly expanding cities like Bengaluru. While it offers a pragmatic solution to a historical problem, it also risks setting a precedent that might inadvertently encourage future non-compliance if enforcement mechanisms are not simultaneously strengthened. The long-term success of this policy hinges on transparent implementation, clear communication of eligible criteria, and robust oversight.

For the real estate sector, this could lead to a temporary surge in transactions for regularized properties, but it also places a greater onus on builders to adhere to approved plans from the outset. TIVRA News advises both current and prospective property owners to seek independent legal counsel and verify all documents thoroughly before making financial commitments related to these newly regularized properties.
