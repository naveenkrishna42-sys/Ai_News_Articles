Gold Below $3,750: Hawkish Fed Minutes Sink Metals, MCX Gold Down ₹3,016

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-19 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Spot gold broke below $3,750/oz, a 14-month low, after losing the $3,800 floor flagged by FXEmpire as the dollar climbed
  • MCX gold futures dropped ₹3,016 to ₹1.31 lakh per 10 gm, pushing Indian prices to nearly a one-year low
  • Hawkish Fed minutes raise the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding bullion, so real yields and the dollar index remain the two variables to track before buying

Gold Breaks Below $3,750 as Hawkish Fed Minutes Reprice Rate Expectations

Gold fell below $3,750 an ounce on Wednesday, touching a 14-month low and extending a third consecutive session of losses after the Federal Reserve's latest meeting minutes carried a hawkish tone. Silver tumbled in sympathy, underperforming the yellow metal as the dollar firmed and Treasury yields moved higher.

The sequence matches FXEmpire's read that the dollar pushed higher once gold surrendered the $3,800 handle. Kitco's PM report flagged the same technical break, with selling accelerating into the US close rather than fading.

Why Hawkish Minutes Move Bullion So Quickly

The rate-expectation channel

Gold carries no coupon. When minutes indicate policymakers prefer holding rates higher for longer, the opportunity cost of owning bullion rises against yield-bearing assets. Futures traders reprice the timing of cuts, the dollar index gains, and metal contracts sell off inside the same session.

Dollar and yield double pressure

A stronger dollar makes dollar-denominated gold costlier for buyers paying in rupees, euros or yen, softening physical demand exactly when investment flows retreat. Rising yields compound the effect by paying investors to wait. Both forces hit silver harder, since the metal's thinner liquidity amplifies moves in either direction.

India Impact: MCX Gold Falls ₹3,016 to ₹1.31 Lakh

Domestic futures mirrored the global slide. MCX gold contracts dropped ₹3,016 to ₹1.31 lakh per 10 gm, according to The Hindu and BusinessLine, leaving Indian prices near a one-year low. For a household buying 10 gm of jewellery-grade metal, that is a real change in outlay, though making charges, 3% GST and dealer premiums still sit on top of the headline rate.

Buyer's Guide: Jewellery, Coins and ETFs

Falling prices tempt retail buyers, but a 14-month low is a trend, not a discount signal. Jewellery purchases made for weddings or gifting should be sized to need rather than to a price call, and bought in tranches over two to three weeks to average out volatility. Coins and bars carry buy-back spreads that widen in falling markets, so confirm the dealer's repurchase rate before paying.

ETF and sovereign gold bond holders face mark-to-market pain but no credit risk. Investors holding for five years or more should treat this drawdown as noise unless the dollar index and real yields keep climbing together, which historically signals a deeper correction.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Lower entry cost for genuine jewellery demand, with GST and making charges unchanged
  • Third-day selling suggests momentum, so lump-sum buying carries timing risk
  • Silver's fall is sharper, offering better value but far higher volatility
  • Dollar strength erodes returns for Indian buyers even when local prices dip
  • Dealer buy-back spreads widen in falling markets, hurting quick resale

What to Watch Before the Next Purchase

Two variables decide the next leg: the dollar index and the 10-year Treasury yield. If both keep rising after this minutes release, gold's slide likely extends toward the next support zone and buyers should stay in tranches. If yields stall while the dollar softens, the $3,750 break becomes a buying window rather than a warning. Watch Friday's US close for confirmation.
