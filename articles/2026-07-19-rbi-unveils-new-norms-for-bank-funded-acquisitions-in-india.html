RBI Unveils New Norms for Bank-Funded Acquisitions in India

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI releases new master directions for acquisition finance by banks and NBFCs.
  • Banks can now finance up to 80% of the acquisition cost for non-banking entities.
  • New norms require detailed risk assessment and board-approved policies.

The Reserve Bank of India has released a fresh set of guidelines governing how banks and non-banking financial companies can fund acquisitions. The move, part of a broader effort to modernise corporate lending, replaces earlier fragmented instructions with a unified framework.

The new master directions, titled 'Acquisition Finance by Banks and NBFCs', come into effect from October 1, 2026. They aim to bring clarity to a segment that has seen growing demand from companies seeking leveraged buyouts or controlling stakes in other firms.

Key Features of the New Framework

Under the revised norms, banks can finance up to 80% of the acquisition cost for non-banking entities. This includes both equity purchases and asset acquisitions. The remaining 20% must be brought in by the borrower as promoter's contribution.

Loans under this framework can have a maximum tenure of seven years, including a moratorium period of up to two years. Repayment schedules must be linked to the cash flows of the acquired entity or the combined business.

Risk Management and Due Diligence

Lenders are now required to conduct thorough due diligence on the target company's financial health, management quality, and sector outlook. The RBI has mandated that banks obtain independent valuations and legal opinions before sanctioning such loans.

Banks must also put in place board-approved policies that clearly define exposure limits, sectoral caps, and monitoring mechanisms. The guidelines stress that acquisition finance should not be used for speculative purposes or to fund hostile takeovers without proper safeguards.

Scope and Applicability

The framework applies to all scheduled commercial banks, including regional rural banks, and NBFCs classified as systemically important. It covers both domestic and cross-border acquisitions, though overseas deals will require additional approval from the central bank.

For NBFCs, the norms are slightly relaxed. They can finance up to 85% of the acquisition cost, provided their capital adequacy remains above the regulatory minimum. The RBI has also allowed NBFCs to participate in consortium lending for large-ticket acquisitions.

Transition and Impact

Existing acquisition finance facilities will be grandfathered until their maturity, but any restructuring or enhancement will have to comply with the new rules. Banks have been given until September 30, 2026, to align their internal policies.

Industry experts say the move is likely to boost M&A activity in India by making bank funding more predictable. However, they caution that the stricter due diligence requirements could slow down deal closures initially. The central bank has indicated it will review the framework after two years based on market feedback.

The RBI's latest move signals a calibrated approach to risk in the corporate lending space. Market participants will now watch how banks price these loans and whether the new norms lead to a surge in leveraged buyouts.
