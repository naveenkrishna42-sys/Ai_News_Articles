World ORS Day 2026: 5 Early Dehydration Signs You Must Not Ignore

Key points
  • Dehydration signs include dry mouth, dark urine, fatigue, dizziness, and reduced urine output.
  • ORS is scientifically formulated to replace fluids and electrolytes faster than plain water.
  • Cipla Health's new campaign emphasises using the correct WHO-recommended ORS formula.
  • ORS outperforms coconut water and energy drinks for clinical rehydration.

On World ORS Day 2026, health experts are urging people to recognise the earliest signs of dehydration — a condition that can escalate quickly, especially during the monsoon and summer months. While many reach for water or sports drinks, the evidence is clear: Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) remains the fastest, most effective way to restore the body's fluid and electrolyte balance.

Cipla Health has launched a new campaign with the tagline "W.H.O. hai toh woh ORS hai", emphasising that not all rehydration drinks are created equal. The initiative aims to educate the public on using the World Health Organization's recommended low-osmolarity ORS formula — a precise mix of glucose, sodium, and potassium that accelerates absorption in the gut.

5 Early Signs of Dehydration You Should Never Ignore

Dehydration often begins with subtle signals. Ignoring them can lead to serious complications, including kidney strain and heatstroke. Here are five red flags:

  • Dry mouth and sticky saliva: Your body's first cry for water. If your lips feel parched even after sipping, you are already behind on fluids.
  • Dark yellow urine: A simple but reliable indicator. Pale straw colour means you are hydrated; deep amber means drink up — preferably with ORS.
  • Fatigue and sluggishness: Even mild dehydration can drop energy levels significantly because blood volume decreases, making the heart work harder.
  • Dizziness or lightheadedness: When standing up quickly, a drop in blood pressure from low fluid volume can make the room spin. This is a clear sign you need electrolytes.
  • Reduced urine output: If you are not visiting the washroom every few hours, your body is conserving water at the expense of normal function.

ORS vs Other Drinks: What Science Says

A growing debate on social media pits ORS against coconut water and energy drinks. But health experts are unequivocal: for clinical rehydration — especially after diarrhoea, vomiting, or heavy sweating — ORS wins hands down.

Coconut water is rich in potassium but low in sodium, while energy drinks often contain caffeine and high sugar, which can worsen dehydration. ORS is specifically designed to match the electrolyte concentration of blood plasma, allowing the small intestine to absorb water up to three times faster than plain water.

When to Start ORS

Doctors recommend starting ORS at the very first sign of diarrhoea or vomiting. Waiting too long can lead to electrolyte imbalances that affect heart and nerve function. For children and the elderly, who are most vulnerable, ORS can be lifesaving. The World Health Organization estimates that proper ORS use can prevent up to 93% of diarrhoea-related deaths.

Expanding Awareness Through Partnerships

This year, ORSL and PSI India have expanded their joint initiative "Diarrhoea Se Darr Nahi" to more districts, aiming to reach rural communities where dehydration is a leading cause of child mortality. The campaign distributes ORS sachets and trains local health workers to recognise early signs of fluid loss.

Meanwhile, Cipla Health's digital push is targeting urban millennials who often mistake fatigue for stress, ignoring dehydration as a root cause. The message is simple: before you reach for that energy drink, check if your body actually needs ORS.

As monsoon rains continue across several states, health authorities are bracing for a spike in waterborne diseases. Knowing the early signs of dehydration — and having ORS sachets on hand — could make the difference between a quick recovery and a hospital visit.
