Overview of the Hebbal Short Tunnel Project

The Hebbal short tunnel is a proposed underground corridor intended to link the existing Hebbal flyover with the Outer Ring Road, aiming to bypass a congested junction. Planners argue that the tunnel will reduce travel time for vehicles heading toward the northern suburbs and the airport corridor. The project emerged from the Bengaluru Development Authority’s long‑term traffic management plan, which identified the Hebbal node as a chronic bottleneck.

Officials’ Rationale and Traffic Projections

According to the Detailed Project Report (DPR), officials estimate that the tunnel could divert approximately 15‑20 % of peak‑hour traffic away from the surface intersection. This shift is expected to lower average delay at the junction by several minutes per vehicle during rush hours. Proponents also note that reduced idling could cut localized emissions, though they acknowledge that construction‑phase emissions will offset some of these gains.

Ecological and Social Impact Assessment

The Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) prepared for the project flags the removal of several hundred trees along the current flyover alignment as a primary concern. Loss of this green cover may affect microclimate regulation and increase runoff into the nearby Hebbal lake. Additionally, residents have raised worries about noise and vibration during tunnelling, especially for colonies situated close to the proposed entry and exit portals.

Cost, Timeline and Alternatives

While exact figures remain under review, officials have indicated that the tunnel would require an investment running into several hundred crores, with a tentative construction window of three to four years. Critics argue that investing in a short tunnel may yield limited returns compared with alternatives such as upgrading signal coordination, expanding the existing flyover, or enhancing BMTC bus services along the corridor. They contend that a modal shift toward public transport could address congestion with lower ecological footprint and lower capital outlay.

Public Sentiment and Way Forward

Local citizen groups have organised meetings and online petitions questioning both the logic of the tunnel and its ecological cost. Many residents demand a transparent cost‑benefit analysis that includes long‑term maintenance expenses and potential mitigation measures like afforestation elsewhere. The civic authorities have responded by stating that public consultations will continue before any final approval is granted.

Key Insights

  • Pros: Potential reduction in surface congestion, shorter travel times for certain routes, possible decrease in idling‑related emissions.
  • Cons: Significant tree loss, risk of water‑body disturbance, high capital expenditure, uncertain long‑term traffic benefits.
  • Alternatives: Signal optimisation, flyover augmentation, improved bus rapid transit, and demand‑management measures offer lower‑impact pathways.
