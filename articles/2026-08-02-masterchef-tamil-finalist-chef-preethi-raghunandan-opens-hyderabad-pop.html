MasterChef Tamil Finalist Chef Preethi Raghunandan Opens Hyderabad Pop-Up

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chef Preethi Raghunandan, a MasterChef India Tamil finalist, is hosting a pop-up in Hyderabad.
  • The event showcases authentic Tamil cuisine, with a focus on traditional recipes and local ingredients.
  • The pop-up is part of her mission to promote regional Indian food beyond its home state.

A Homecoming of Flavours

Hyderabad, known for its own rich culinary heritage, is now playing host to a slice of Tamil Nadu's food culture. Chef Preethi Raghunandan, a finalist from MasterChef India Tamil, has brought her celebrated pop-up dining concept to the city. The event, which kicked off this weekend, is drawing food enthusiasts eager to taste her take on traditional Tamil recipes.

Raghunandan's journey from home cook to national television finalist has been closely followed by food lovers across the south. Her appearance on the MasterChef platform gave her a wider audience, but she has remained rooted in the cooking style she grew up with. Now, she is translating that familiarity into a curated menu for Hyderabad's diners.

What's on the Menu

The pop-up menu is built around classic Tamil dishes, prepared with techniques that emphasise patience and layering of flavours. From aromatic sambar variants to lesser-known regional specialities, the spread aims to offer both comfort and discovery. Raghunandan has said that sourcing local produce is central to her cooking, and the Hyderabad edition reflects that commitment.

She is known for using traditional vessels and methods wherever possible, which adds an earthy depth to her food. The chef has also included some dishes that she prepared during the competition, giving fans a chance to taste the plates that impressed the judges. Each course is paired with insights into the dish's origins, making the meal an educational experience as much as a culinary one.

Bridging Regional Food Gaps

Raghunandan's pop-up is part of a larger trend of regional Indian chefs taking their cuisines to new cities. While South Indian food is widely available across the country, it is often limited to standardised versions of idli, dosa, and filter coffee. The chef's effort is to showcase the diversity within Tamil cooking, which varies significantly from the coastal belt to the interior districts.

Hyderabad, with its own strong food identity, offers a curious audience. The response so far has been encouraging, with several sold-out seatings in the opening days. Organisers say they are considering extending the run if demand remains high.

What's Next

The pop-up is expected to continue for a limited period, and the chef is also planning a series of workshops for home cooks. Those interested in attending can check the event's official listings for availability. For now, Hyderabad's food scene is richer for having a taste of authentic Tamil fare, and the crossover between the two southern culinary cultures is proving to be a delicious one.
