US judge sets June 2027 trial for Venezuela's Nicolas Maduro

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trial date set for June 2027 in US federal court.
  • Maduro faces charges of drug trafficking and conspiracy.
  • Pretrial hearings have commenced in the case.

Courtroom Date Fixed

A US federal judge has scheduled June 2027 as the trial date for former Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro. The ousted leader appeared in a New York courtroom on Wednesday for a pretrial hearing in his drug trafficking case.

The date was set during a brief proceeding where prosecutors and defence lawyers discussed logistics. Maduro, who was brought to the United States last year, faces charges that he led a cocaine-trafficking conspiracy using state institutions.

The Charges

US prosecutors accuse Maduro of working with Colombian rebels to smuggle tons of cocaine into America. They allege he used the Venezuelan military and intelligence services to protect drug shipments in exchange for bribes.

The indictment, unsealed in 2020, also names several former Venezuelan officials. Maduro has repeatedly denied the allegations, calling them a political vendetta by Washington.

Pretrial Proceedings

Wednesday's hearing was largely procedural. The judge set deadlines for evidence sharing and motions. Both sides agreed to a June 2027 start, which gives them over two years to prepare.

Legal analysts say the timeline is not unusual for complex international cases. The prosecution is expected to call witnesses from multiple countries, including former Maduro aides who have already pleaded guilty in US courts.

Political Context

Maduro was ousted from power following disputed elections in 2024. He was arrested in Caracas by opposition forces and later handed over to US authorities. His trial is seen as a landmark case in the US war on drugs.

The Venezuelan government, now led by a US-backed interim president, has cooperated with the prosecution. Maduro's lawyers argue he cannot receive a fair trial in the United States due to political hostility.

What Comes Next

The next hearing is expected later this year. Jury selection is tentatively set for May 2027. With both sides digging in, the case is likely to stretch on for years.

For now, Maduro remains in federal custody. His legal team has not indicated whether they will seek a plea deal or fight the charges at trial.
