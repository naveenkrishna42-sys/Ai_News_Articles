Trump links US-Saudi nuclear deal to normalisation with Israel

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump said the US-Saudi nuclear deal depends on Saudi Arabia recognising Israel.
  • Riyadh has not publicly budged from its stance that normalisation requires a Palestinian state.
  • The deal would give Saudi Arabia access to US nuclear technology for civilian energy.

A civilian nuclear cooperation agreement between the United States and Saudi Arabia has hit a political roadblock. Former US President Donald Trump has now linked the deal directly to Saudi recognition of Israel, throwing negotiations into turmoil.

The proposed agreement, known as a 123 Agreement, would allow American companies to sell nuclear technology and materials to Saudi Arabia. Riyadh wants this to build a domestic nuclear energy programme. But the political condition attached by Trump has created a fresh standoff.

The demand that changed the game

Trump made the linkage public during a press interaction. He stated that the nuclear deal would not go through unless Saudi Arabia formally recognises the state of Israel. This condition goes beyond the usual non-proliferation safeguards that the US insists on in such agreements.

For Riyadh, this is a heavy demand. Saudi officials have repeatedly said that any normalisation with Israel is conditional on the creation of a Palestinian state. The kingdom has not changed this position publicly, even as it pursues economic diversification and energy security through nuclear power.

Why Saudi Arabia wants nuclear energy

Saudi Arabia has trillions of dollars in oil wealth. Yet, it wants nuclear power for a simple reason: to free up more crude for export. The kingdom burns large amounts of oil domestically to generate electricity, especially during peak summer months when air conditioning demand surges.

By switching to nuclear energy, Saudi Arabia can reduce its internal oil consumption and export more barrels at global prices. This is part of the broader Vision 2030 plan to modernise the economy and reduce dependence on oil revenue. The country also wants to develop a nuclear industry that could eventually include uranium enrichment, which has raised concerns in Washington about proliferation risks.

Israel and the regional context

Israel has long viewed a nuclear-capable Saudi Arabia with suspicion. However, Israeli leaders have signalled that a normalisation deal with Riyadh would be a historic diplomatic win. For Trump, linking the two issues is a way to force a breakthrough in Middle East peace efforts on his terms.

Critics argue that mixing nuclear cooperation with geopolitics could backfire. Saudi Arabia could walk away from the deal entirely. It could also turn to other suppliers such as China or Russia for nuclear technology, which the US wants to prevent.

What happens next

Negotiations remain in limbo. The Biden administration, which had been working on the deal separately, now faces a complicated situation with Trump's public stance. Saudi officials have not made any fresh commitments on Israel recognition.

All eyes are on whether Riyadh will shift its position or whether the nuclear deal will collapse. The outcome will shape not just energy markets in the Gulf but also the broader diplomatic alignment in the Middle East.
