UPI stays free for users; nominal MDR for select merchants, says govt

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • UPI transactions to remain free for users
  • Nominal MDR proposed for select merchant payments
  • Government aims to balance affordability and sustainability

The government has put an end to speculation over Unified Payments Interface (UPI) charges, confirming that the service will remain free for users. In a statement that brings clarity to millions of daily digital transactions, the Centre said that the vast majority of UPI payments will continue to be free for merchants as well, with only a nominal Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) proposed for select categories.

What the government said

Officials clarified that there is no proposal to levy any charge on UPI users. The focus remains on keeping the payment system affordable and accessible, especially for small and medium enterprises that rely heavily on UPI for their daily business.

The proposed MDR, if implemented, would apply only to a small slice of merchant transactions, and the rate would be nominal. The exact categories and thresholds have not been disclosed yet, but the government emphasised that the move is aimed at ensuring the long-term sustainability of the payment ecosystem without burdening the common user.

Why this matters

UPI has become the backbone of India's digital payment infrastructure, processing over a crore transactions daily. Any talk of charges had triggered anxiety among users and small businesses, many of whom have adopted UPI as their primary mode of payment.

The clarification comes amid broader discussions on how to keep the UPI ecosystem financially viable. While the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) has kept the service free so far, there have been debates about the need for a revenue model that supports the infrastructure without discouraging usage.

What is MDR?

MDR is the fee that a merchant pays to the bank or payment service provider for processing a digital transaction. For UPI, this has been zero since its launch, making it one of the most cost-effective payment methods in the world.

The government's stance suggests that any future fee structure would be minimal and targeted, ensuring that the mass adoption of UPI is not disrupted.

Industry and user reactions

Fintech experts have welcomed the clarity, noting that a predictable fee structure could help payment companies innovate without the fear of sudden regulatory changes. However, they also caution that any MDR, however small, could add up for high-volume merchants.

Consumer groups have urged the government to keep the threshold for MDR high enough so that small shopkeepers and street vendors remain unaffected. The government has not yet specified the exact threshold or categories for the proposed fee.

The road ahead

Discussions on the proposed MDR are still at a preliminary stage, and any final decision will likely involve consultations with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and other stakeholders. The government has indicated that it will take a balanced approach, keeping the interests of both users and the payment industry in mind.

For now, UPI remains free for users, and the vast majority of merchants can continue to transact without worrying about additional costs. The next few weeks will be crucial as the government fine-tunes its proposal and decides how to implement it without upsetting the digital payments applecart.
