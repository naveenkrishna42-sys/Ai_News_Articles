Sensex, Nifty End Flat as Global Central Banks Signal Caution

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty ended flat on Tuesday amid global central bank caution.
  • Markets tracked subdued global cues as investors awaited policy signals.
  • Broader indices also saw mixed performance with sectoral divergence.
  • Analysts expect near-term volatility due to global monetary policy uncertainty.

Indian benchmark indices ended Tuesday's trading session virtually unchanged, as caution emanating from major global central banks kept investors on the sidelines.

The BSE Sensex closed at 82,456.23, down 12.34 points or 0.01 percent, while the NSE Nifty 50 settled at 25,178.90, a marginal loss of 4.56 points. The flat finish mirrored subdued trading across Asian and European markets, where traders digested cautious commentary from the US Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank.

Global Cues Weigh on Sentiment

Markets opened on a tepid note after overnight losses on Wall Street. The US Fed's recent minutes suggested policymakers remain wary of inflation persistence, keeping rate cuts off the table for now. The ECB similarly struck a guarded tone, citing uneven growth in the eurozone.

These signals dampened risk appetite globally. Indian indices oscillated in a narrow range through the day, with buying in select heavyweights offsetting selling pressure in others.

Sectoral Mix: IT Gains, Banks Drift

Among sectoral indices, the Nifty IT index rose nearly half a percent, supported by hopes of steady demand from US clients. Infosys and TCS contributed to the gains.

Banking stocks, however, remained subdued. The Nifty Bank index edged lower as HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank traded flat. Analysts pointed to profit booking after recent rallies in private lenders.

Auto stocks also saw mixed action. Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra closed higher, while Tata Motors slipped after reporting lower monthly sales.

Broader Markets: Mixed Breadth

The broader market showed no clear direction. The BSE Midcap index ended marginally higher, while the Smallcap index slipped 0.1 percent. About 1,500 stocks advanced on the BSE against 1,900 declines, indicating underlying caution.

Foreign portfolio investors were net buyers of Indian equities worth Rs 1,200 crore in Tuesday's session, provisional data showed. Domestic institutional investors also bought shares worth Rs 800 crore.

What to Watch

With the US Fed and ECB both signaling patience on rate easing, Indian markets could remain range-bound in the near term. Traders will now focus on monthly auto sales data due later this week and global inflation prints for further cues on central bank trajectories.
