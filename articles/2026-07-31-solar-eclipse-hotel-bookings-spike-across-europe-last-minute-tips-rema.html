Solar eclipse hotel bookings spike across Europe; last-minute tips remain

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hotel bookings spike across Europe ahead of the August total solar eclipse.
  • The eclipse is the biggest in Europe since 1999, coinciding with the Perseid meteor shower.
  • Last-minute travellers can still find rooms by targeting less obvious locations or nearby towns.

Europe's tourism industry is feeling the celestial pull. With the biggest total solar eclipse since 1999 set to sweep across the continent in August, hotel bookings have surged in the path of totality. Travellers are scrambling to secure rooms, but industry watchers say options still remain for the flexible.

The eclipse, which will also coincide with the annual Perseid meteor shower, has turned the event into a rare double-header for skywatchers. NASA has confirmed coverage of the event, which will be visible across the Northern Hemisphere on August 12.

Why the rush?

The last time Europe witnessed a total solar eclipse of this scale was in 1999, and that event drew millions to prime viewing spots. This year's eclipse is expected to be no different, with hotels in the narrow band of totality reporting near-capacity bookings months in advance.

According to travel operators, the allure is not just the eclipse itself but the chance to witness the Perseid meteor shower immediately after. The combination has created a surge that caught some hotels off guard, with many properties now fully booked for the key dates.

How to still find a room

Despite the rush, experts say last-minute travellers are not entirely out of luck. The key is to look beyond the obvious hotspots. Towns and villages just outside the central path of totality often have vacancies, and a short drive can still put you under the full shadow.

Another strategy: consider staying in larger cities that are near the eclipse path but not directly beneath it. Public transport can bridge the gap, and city hotels tend to have more supply. Some travellers are also turning to home-stay platforms, where listings have been slower to fill up.

Practical tips for eclipse chasers

  • Book refundable rates wherever possible, as weather forecasts may shift your plans.
  • Check smaller guesthouses and B&Bs, which often lag behind big chains in booking speed.
  • Look at neighbouring regions – even a 30-minute drive can make a difference.
  • If you're flexible on dates, consider arriving a day early or leaving a day late to avoid peak rates.

More than just a room

Securing a bed is only half the battle. Eclipse chasers also need to plan for viewing spots, as hotels in the path of totality are expected to be crowded with visitors. Open fields, hilltops, and even ferry decks are being scouted by enthusiasts.

NASA has said it will provide live coverage for those who cannot travel, but the agency's own scientists are set to conduct experiments during the event. For many, though, being there in person is the only option.

Officials have not yet confirmed crowd estimates, but local authorities in several countries are bracing for large influxes. Traffic congestion and limited parking are likely on the day itself, so early arrival is advised.

As the date approaches, more cancellations may free up rooms. Travellers who persist—and think beyond the obvious—may still find a way to witness the sky's grand show.

What to watch: Booking platforms are likely to release last-minute inventory as cancellation deadlines pass. Keep an eye on regional tourism websites, and be ready to move quickly when a room appears.
