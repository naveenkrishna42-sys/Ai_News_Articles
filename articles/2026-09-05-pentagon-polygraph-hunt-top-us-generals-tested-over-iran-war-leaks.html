Pentagon Launches Internal Polygraph Sweep Over Iran War Leaks

The US Department of Defence has initiated mass polygraph examinations targeting senior military officers within the Joint Staff, following a series of unauthorised disclosures concerning Iran war operations. The investigation, first reported by The New York Times and corroborated by CBS News, focuses on sensitive intelligence covering weapons stockpiles, strike planning timelines, and battlefield assessments related to ongoing operations in West Asia.

According to multiple US media outlets, the polygraph programme has been extended to several flag-rank officers whose routine duties involve direct access to classified operational data. The action reflects an intensified internal security posture under President Donald Trump, who has publicly framed the leaks as a deliberate sabotage effort targeting US strategic interests in the region.

Scope and Targets of the Polygraph Drive

Sources familiar with the probe told US media that the polygraph sessions have been conducted by the Pentagon's own counterintelligence teams, not external federal agencies. Officers with overlapping access to Iran-related operational briefings, satellite imagery assessments, and munitions inventory reports are reportedly being prioritised. The process includes standard lie-detection questions on unauthorised contacts with journalists and the handling of classified digital systems.

The unusual step of subjecting active-duty senior generals to formal polygraph testing indicates the scale of concern inside the Pentagon. While the military routinely uses polygraphs for espionage cases and security clearance reviews, mass application across a senior command echelon remains a rare and politically charged measure.

Political Pressure and Operational Context

President Trump has publicly criticised the leaks, stating that exposure of operational details puts service members and allied forces at risk. The administration's frustration intensified after media reports disclosed specifics about munition stockpiles used in operations tied to Iran-linked targets, including stockpile locations and resupply logistics.

Defence analysts observe that leak investigations of this intensity often follow two triggers: direct presidential intervention or operational compromise in an active conflict zone. The current drive fits both patterns, with the Pentagon balancing warfighting priorities against tightening information security inside the Washington headquarters environment.

Verified Reporting and Source Cross-Check

Independent confirmation has emerged from multiple credible outlets. CBS News reported that officers were questioned specifically on weapons stockpile data. The New York Times detailed that the polygraph order came directly from senior Pentagon leadership responding to presidential pressure. NDTV and The Times of India carried parallel wire reports citing identical sourcing, indicating a shared intelligence pool rather than speculation.

Operational and Institutional Implications

For active-duty personnel, the development carries several practical consequences. Officers with access to Iran war operational data can expect tighter communications discipline, reduced informal contact with media, and elevated scrutiny on digital handling of classified material. Routine operational briefings may shift further into secured facilities with restricted attendee lists.

For US allies and adversaries monitoring the situation, the polygraph drive signals a hardening of US information discipline during an active military campaign. Analysts note that Washington is signalling that unauthorised disclosures will trigger institutional consequences, regardless of rank or seniority.
