Russia Charges Telegram Founder Pavel Durov With Aiding Terrorism

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Russia formally charged Pavel Durov with aiding terrorism.
  • Durov has been placed on a federal wanted list.
  • The charges mark an escalation in Russia's online crackdown.

Moscow Files Terrorism Charges Against Telegram Chief

Russia has formally charged Pavel Durov, the founder of the encrypted messaging app Telegram, with aiding terrorism. The charge was announced on Wednesday, and authorities have placed him on a federal wanted list.

The development marks a significant escalation in Moscow's long-running battle with the tech entrepreneur. Durov, who left Russia in 2014 after refusing to comply with government demands to block opposition groups on his VKontakte social network, has been a persistent target of the Kremlin.

FSB Issues Arrest Warrant

Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) issued the arrest warrant, accusing Durov of facilitating terrorism through his platform. Officials allege that Telegram has been used by extremist groups to coordinate attacks and spread propaganda.

The specific details of the charges have not been fully disclosed. However, Russian state media have for years accused Telegram of being a haven for terrorists, citing the app's end-to-end encryption as a barrier to surveillance.

Telegram's Response

Telegram has not yet issued a formal statement regarding the charges. The company, which is currently headquartered in Dubai, has previously defended its encryption policies as essential for user privacy and free speech.

Durov has consistently argued that weakening encryption would endanger users worldwide. He has also resisted pressure from Russian regulators to hand over user data, leading to a temporary ban on Telegram in Russia in 2018, which was later lifted.

Broader Crackdown on Online Platforms

The charges against Durov are part of a broader pattern of Russian authorities tightening control over the internet. In recent years, Moscow has fined and blocked several tech platforms for hosting content deemed illegal, including criticism of the war in Ukraine.

Russian lawmakers have also passed legislation requiring messaging apps to provide authorities with decryption keys. Telegram has so far resisted such demands, making it a persistent thorn in the side of the Kremlin.

What the Charges Mean

Being placed on a wanted list means Durov could be arrested if he travels to any country with an extradition treaty with Russia. Currently, Durov resides in the United Arab Emirates, which does not have a formal extradition agreement with Moscow.

Legal experts say the charges are largely symbolic unless Durov sets foot in Russian territory or a jurisdiction that cooperates with Moscow. However, the move could complicate Telegram's operations and deter other tech executives from challenging Russian state policies.

Observers note that the charges also serve as a warning to other platforms. By targeting Durov personally, the Russian government is signaling its willingness to go after individual executives, not just corporate entities.

For now, Durov remains free, but the arrest warrant places him in a precarious position. The next step will likely depend on whether Russia pushes for an international red notice through Interpol or escalates its campaign against Telegram in other ways.
