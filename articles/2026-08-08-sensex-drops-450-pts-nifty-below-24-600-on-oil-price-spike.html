Sensex drops 450 pts, Nifty below 24,600 on oil price spike

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex settled 450 points lower, Nifty ended below 24,600
  • Rising oil prices among key factors dragging markets
  • Global cues add to selling pressure; analysts cite inflation worries

Indian equity benchmarks closed sharply lower on Friday, with the Sensex settling 450 points down and the Nifty ending below the 24,600 mark. The decline was driven largely by a spike in global crude oil prices, which reignited concerns about inflation and its impact on corporate margins.

Traders said selling pressure was broad-based, with banking, auto, and energy stocks among the worst hit. The market's mood turned cautious as investors weighed the possibility of prolonged high input costs and their effect on demand.

Oil prices weigh on sentiment

Brent crude prices have climbed in recent sessions, and Friday's move added to worries that India, a major oil importer, could face higher import bills. This, in turn, puts pressure on the rupee and could prompt the Reserve Bank of India to hold interest rates higher for longer.

According to analysts, the rise in oil prices is a key headwind for equities, as it feeds into inflation expectations and may force central banks to keep monetary policy tight. The market's reaction on Friday reflected these concerns, with investors trimming positions across sectors.

Global cues add to selling

Weak global cues also contributed to the downturn. Asian markets traded mixed, while European indices opened lower, tracking a cautious mood among international investors. The US Federal Reserve's recent commentary on rate cuts has offered little clarity, leaving markets in a wait-and-watch mode.

Domestic institutional investors were net sellers, while foreign institutional investors remained on the sidelines, according to provisional data. The volatility index, India VIX, rose marginally, indicating heightened anxiety among traders.

Sectoral performance

Among sectoral indices, the Nifty Bank fell over 1 per cent, while auto and metal stocks also witnessed significant selling. Energy stocks, including refiners, were mixed, as higher crude prices benefit upstream companies but hurt downstream players.

Midcap and smallcap indices underperformed the benchmarks, reflecting a risk-off sentiment across the broader market. Declining advances outnumbered gaining ones on the BSE, with more than 2,000 stocks ending in the red.

What investors should watch

Market participants are now closely tracking the trajectory of crude oil prices and any fresh cues from global central banks. A sustained rise in oil could further dent sentiment, while any easing in geopolitical tensions might provide relief.

Analysts suggest that until the oil price situation stabilises, equity markets are likely to remain volatile. Earnings season, which is drawing to a close, has offered little comfort, with mixed results from major companies.

Going ahead, investors will also keep an eye on the rupee's movement against the dollar and any policy signals from the RBI. The coming sessions are expected to be data-heavy, with US inflation numbers and domestic industrial production data likely to influence market direction.
