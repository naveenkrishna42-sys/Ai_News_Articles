Iran's IRGC claims drone attack on US base in Kuwait

📅 2026-07-31 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • IRGC claims drone strike on US base in Kuwait
  • Attack described as retaliatory following US strikes on Iran
  • Conflict spreads as new fronts open across the region

Tehran, July 31: Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claimed it targeted a US military base in Kuwait with drones, describing the action as a 'retaliatory attack'. The announcement, carried by Iranian state media, marks a significant escalation in the ongoing West Asia conflict, opening a new front in the Gulf region.

Claim and context

The IRGC statement, released early Friday, said the drones struck a US base in Kuwait. It did not specify the base's exact location or the extent of damage. US Central Command has not yet officially confirmed the attack or responded to the Iranian claim.

The development comes a day after the United States launched what it called 'powerful' strikes on Iranian targets. President Donald Trump, in a statement, said, 'Our time to respond,' signalling a deliberate military escalation. The IRGC's latest move appears to be a direct answer to those strikes.

Kuwait hosts thousands of US troops and is home to Camp Arifjan, a major logistics hub, and Ali Al Salem Air Base. The Pentagon has not commented on whether any of these facilities were hit.

Regional fallout

The conflict, which began with tit-for-tat strikes, is now spreading. Reports from The Times of India and Al Jazeera indicate that new fronts are opening across the region, with Iran seeking to widen the battlefield beyond its borders. Kuwait's involvement, if confirmed, would draw a key Gulf ally of the US directly into the fray.

Iran has previously threatened to target US assets across the Middle East if attacked. The IRGC's claim suggests it is following through on that warning, using drones as a cost-effective and deniable means of striking at American forces.

Regional reactions and uncertainty

Kuwaiti authorities have not issued an official statement yet. The country's foreign ministry often urges restraint in regional conflicts, but a direct attack on its soil would force a response. Other Gulf states, including Saudi Arabia and the UAE, are watching closely, as they host US forces and could be drawn into the crossfire.

Analysts note that Iran's use of drones, rather than ballistic missiles, may be a calculated choice. Drones are harder to intercept and can be launched from mobile platforms, making retaliation more complex. However, they also carry a lower payload, suggesting Iran may be probing US defences rather than seeking maximum destruction.

What to watch

The immediate focus is on the US response. Washington has promised 'powerful' action, and any confirmation of a successful strike on Kuwait could trigger a broader campaign. The coming hours are critical, as both sides weigh their next moves in a conflict that is rapidly spiralling beyond the initial exchange.

Watch for official confirmations from the Pentagon and Kuwaiti authorities, and for any signs of diplomatic intervention from regional powers or the UN. The situation remains fluid, with the potential for further escalation or, conversely, a de-escalation if international pressure mounts.
