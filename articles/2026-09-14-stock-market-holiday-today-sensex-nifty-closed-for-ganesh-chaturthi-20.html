Dalal Street Shuts for Ganesh Chaturthi 2026

The Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) and the National Stock Exchange (NSE) are closed on Monday, 14 September 2026 for Ganesh Chaturthi, the festival marking the birth of Lord Ganesha. There will be no trading in the equity cash, equity derivatives, currency derivatives, commodity derivatives, and Securities Lending and Borrowing (SLB) segments.

The shutdown applies across all exchange platforms, including the BSE's STAR and BSE SME segments and the NSE's main board and SME Emerge platform. Broking back-office operations, mutual fund NAV computation and settlement cycles are also suspended for the day.

Why This Holiday Matters for Traders

Ganesh Chaturthi is a Maharashtra-heavy festival, and Mumbai is India's financial capital. Historically, the exchanges align their annual holiday list with the state government's public holiday calendar, which is why this date appears in the official 2026 NSE and BSE holiday circulars.

The bigger market implication is timing. Because the festival falls on a Monday, Dalal Street gets a three-day break, and positions carried into the weekend remain exposed to global cues without any domestic price discovery. Traders holding leveraged derivative positions should review margin requirements before Tuesday's open.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • No intraday or delivery trades execute on 14 September 2026; all pending orders lapse with the session.
  • GIFT Nifty on NSE IX continues to trade during Indian market hours, offering the only live domestic-linked price signal.
  • Indian ADRs listed in the US, including Infosys, Wipro, HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank, trade normally on the NYSE and Nasdaq.
  • Settlement obligations shift by one working day, so fund pay-in and pay-out timelines adjust accordingly.
  • Brokerage customer care and help desks largely operate on skeletal staffing, so trade-related queries may face delays.

How Ganesh Chaturthi Fits the 2026 Holiday Calendar

Indian exchanges declare roughly 14 to 16 trading holidays each year, split between fixed national holidays such as Republic Day and Independence Day, and moving festival dates such as Holi, Diwali, Eid and Ganesh Chaturthi. Diwali remains the only festival with a special one-hour Muhurat trading session, and that too only in the evening of Laxmi Pujan.

Ganesh Chaturthi is a full-day closure with no Muhurat window. For salaried investors running systematic investment plans (SIPs), the practical effect is minimal. SIP instalments dated 14 September typically get processed on the next working day, and the units are allotted at the NAV of 15 September 2026.

What Investors Should Do Before Tuesday's Open

Review overnight global cues, especially US Federal Reserve commentary, crude oil prices and the dollar index, since Indian markets reopen with a gap-adjustment. F&O traders carrying short-dated options should model a wider implied volatility at the Tuesday open, as a full trading day of global price action gets compressed into a single gap.

Cash-segment investors have nothing to action. Delivery holdings, demat balances and corporate action entitlements remain unaffected by a trading holiday. The next scheduled market holiday after Ganesh Chaturthi will be declared in the exchanges' official 2026 circular, which investors can verify directly on the BSE and NSE websites rather than relying on social media forwards.
