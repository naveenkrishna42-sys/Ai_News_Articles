BJP MP Janardan Mishra defends ethanol blending with sharp retort

📅 2026-08-10 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP MP Janardan Mishra defended the ethanol blending policy with a sharp rhetorical question.
  • He challenged critics, asking if pure petrol would come from their father's houses.
  • The remarks come amid debate over the E20 fuel initiative and its impact on consumers.
  • Mishra stressed the policy's benefits for farmers and the environment.
  • Opposition leaders have questioned the feasibility and affordability of higher ethanol blends.

BJP MP Janardan Mishra has stirred a fresh political row with a blunt defence of the government's ethanol blending policy, asking critics whether they expect pure petrol to be supplied from their own homes. The remarks, made during a parliamentary discussion, have reignited the debate over the country's push towards alternative fuels.

A sharp retort to critics

Speaking in the Lok Sabha, Mishra dismissed concerns raised by opposition members over the rising ethanol content in petrol. He questioned the logic behind demanding unblended fuel, asserting that the policy is both environmentally sound and beneficial for farmers.

"Will pure petrol come from your father's house?" the MP asked, challenging those who have criticised the move. His statement drew immediate reactions from across the political spectrum, with some calling it dismissive of genuine consumer concerns.

The E20 push and its rationale

The government has been steadily advancing its target of blending 20 per cent ethanol in petrol, a plan aimed at reducing crude oil imports and cutting vehicular emissions. The policy, part of the broader energy transition roadmap, has been framed as a win-win for the sugar sector and the environment.

Mishra highlighted these benefits, arguing that ethanol blending supports sugarcane farmers and reduces the nation's dependence on imported fossil fuels. He insisted that the transition is essential for India's long-term energy security.

Opposition's concerns

Critics, however, have pointed to potential downsides, including the impact on food security and the strain on water resources for sugarcane cultivation. Some have also questioned whether the blending targets are realistic, given the current production capacity of ethanol in the country.

Opposition MPs have demanded more transparency on how the policy will affect fuel prices and vehicle performance, particularly for older vehicles not designed for higher ethanol blends. The debate reflects a broader unease about the pace and implementation of the programme.

  • The government aims for 20 per cent ethanol blending by 2025-26.
  • Current blending levels are around 10 per cent, with gradual increases planned.
  • Automakers have been asked to produce E20-compliant engines.

Political fallout

Mishra's comments have added a sharp edge to the ongoing debate, with opposition leaders accusing him of trivialising legitimate questions. Social media has been abuzz with reactions, some supportive of the MP's bluntness, others critical of what they see as arrogance.

The exchange underscores the political sensitivity of the ethanol policy, which touches on agriculture, energy, and consumer economics. As the government pushes ahead, the discourse is likely to remain heated, with both sides digging in.

What happens next will depend on how the government addresses the practical challenges raised by critics, including fuel pricing and vehicle compatibility. The coming months may see further clarifications as the blending programme moves closer to its deadline.
