Boomerang Nebula: The Coldest Known Place in the Universe

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Boomerang Nebula is the coldest known natural object in the universe at -272°C.
  • Its temperature is three times colder than the coldest recorded temperature on Earth in Antarctica.
  • The nebula is colder than the cosmic microwave background radiation, which fills the universe.

What Makes the Boomerang Nebula So Cold?

Deep in the constellation Centaurus, about 5,000 light-years from Earth, lies the Boomerang Nebula. It is the coldest known natural object in the universe. At a staggering -272 degrees Celsius, it is three times colder than Antarctica's lowest recorded temperature.

The nebula's temperature is even lower than the cosmic microwave background radiation — the faint afterglow of the Big Bang that fills all of space. That background sits at about -270.4 degrees Celsius. The Boomerang Nebula is colder than the vacuum of space itself.

How Does It Get That Cold?

Astronomers have observed that a star at the centre of the Boomerang Nebula is dying. It is shedding mass at an extraordinary rate, ejecting gas and dust outward at nearly ten times the speed of sound. This rapid expansion is the key to the extreme cold.

When gas expands quickly, it loses energy and cools down. This is the same principle that makes a can of deodorant feel cold when you spray it. In the Boomerang Nebula, the expansion is so violent that the escaping material drops to a temperature far below anything else found naturally.

Colder Than Antarctica and Space

For comparison, the coldest temperature ever recorded on Earth was at the Vostok Station in Antarctica — about -89.2 degrees Celsius. That is a brutal cold, but it is nothing compared to the Boomerang. The nebula is more than 180 degrees colder than that Antarctic record.

The cosmic microwave background radiation is often called the "temperature of space." Even that benchmark is warmer than the Boomerang Nebula. The nebula is the only natural place known where temperatures drop below the microwave background.

A Cosmic Mystery

The Boomerang Nebula was first discovered in 1980 by astronomers using a telescope in Chile. It was initially thought to be a galaxy, but later observations revealed its true nature. The name comes from its distinctive shape, which resembles a boomerang when seen through early telescopes.

More recent images from the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) have shown the nebula in much greater detail. The outflowing gas is not a simple cloud but a complex structure of filaments and clumps. Scientists are still studying why the expansion is so extreme.

What Happens Next

Astronomers will continue to monitor the Boomerang Nebula with telescopes like ALMA and the James Webb Space Telescope. Understanding how such extreme cooling occurs could help explain the life cycles of stars and the behaviour of matter in the universe's most hostile environments. The Boomerang Nebula remains a record-holder, but future discoveries may find even colder places in the cosmos.
