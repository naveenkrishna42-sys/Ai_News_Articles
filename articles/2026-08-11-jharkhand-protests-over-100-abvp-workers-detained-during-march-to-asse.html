Jharkhand protests: Over 100 ABVP workers detained during march to Assembly

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 100 ABVP workers detained during a march to the Jharkhand Assembly.
  • The protest follows police action against student job aspirants; BJP observed a bandh.
  • Ranchi saw heavy security with six layers of barricades for a 5-km march.
  • Political leaders, including Dipke, condemned the police action against a fasting student leader.

Ranchi: Tensions ran high in Jharkhand on Tuesday as police detained over 100 Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP) workers during a solidarity march towards the state Assembly. The protesters were rallying against the recent police action on student job aspirants, an incident that has sparked widespread outrage and political backlash.

The detentions came amid a state-wide bandh called by the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), which disrupted normal life in several parts of the state. Shops and businesses remained shut in many areas, while vehicular movement was sparse on key routes as a precautionary measure.

Heavy security, multiple barricades

Security was tightened significantly in the state capital, with officials deploying six layers of barricades along the five-kilometre stretch from the protest site to the Assembly. The elaborate arrangement was aimed at preventing the marchers from reaching the legislature, where they intended to submit a memorandum.

Police personnel were seen in large numbers at every checkpoint, and detentions were carried out peacefully, according to officials. The identity of the detained workers and the exact charges, if any, have not been officially confirmed yet, but sources indicate they were taken to temporary holding centres on the outskirts of the city.

Political reactions and condemnation

Political leaders from opposition parties have come out strongly against the police action. Dipke, a prominent leader, condemned the use of force against a fasting student leader in Jharkhand, calling it an attack on democratic dissent. The fasting student leader, whose identity has not been disclosed, has been at the centre of the agitation for the past several days.

The BJP, which has been backing the students' cause, accused the state government of suppressing peaceful protests. Party workers claimed that the detentions were arbitrary and violated the right to protest, while the ruling administration defended the police action, citing the need to maintain law and order.

Background of the protests

The unrest traces back to a series of police actions against job aspirants who were demonstrating over alleged irregularities in recruitment exams. The students have been demanding a re-examination and a transparent process, but authorities have so far not agreed to their demands.

On the day of the march, the BJP's bandh call saw mixed response across districts. While some urban centres reported complete shutdowns, rural areas were relatively unaffected. Train and bus services were disrupted in a few locations, but no major incidents of violence have been reported so far, according to local authorities.

What lies ahead

The detained ABVP workers are expected to be released later in the day after formalities, but the core issue remains unresolved. The student leaders have vowed to continue their agitation until their demands are met, and the BJP has indicated it will escalate its protests if the government does not respond.

All eyes are now on the state administration's next move, particularly whether it will initiate a dialogue with the student representatives or take further preventive measures. The coming days will determine whether this standoff eases or spirals into a larger political confrontation.
