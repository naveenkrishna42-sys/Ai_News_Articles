Motorola Edge 70 Max launch renews concerns over Android update commitments

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Motorola Edge 70 Max launches with Android 14, raising questions about future OS updates
  • Company has a track record of delayed and limited software support for its devices
  • Industry rivals like Samsung and Google now offer longer, more transparent update cycles

Another flagship, familiar questions

Motorola unveiled the Edge 70 Max this week, packing a large battery and competitive specs at an aggressive price point. But the launch has once again highlighted a persistent issue: the company's muddled approach to Android updates.

While the Edge 70 Max ships with Android 14, Motorola has not clearly stated how many major OS upgrades the device will receive. This vagueness is a recurring pattern, leaving buyers to guess whether their expensive new phone will feel obsolete within a couple of years.

A history of mixed signals

Motorola has made conflicting promises over the years. In 2023, it committed to three OS upgrades and four years of security patches for its premium Edge series. However, many users report that updates arrive months after Google releases them, and some mid-range models never see promised upgrades.

The company's update tracker page is often outdated, and support timelines vary wildly between regions. For a brand that sells phones in India at every price point, this inconsistency frustrates buyers who expect a reliable software experience.

What rivals are doing

Competitors have raised the bar. Samsung now guarantees four OS upgrades and five years of security patches for its flagship and mid-range Galaxy devices. Google's Pixel phones get three years of OS updates and five years of security patches, delivered promptly.

Even OnePlus, which had a rocky update history, now promises four OS upgrades and five years of security patches for its newer flagships. Motorola's policy looks increasingly outdated in comparison.

The Edge 70 Max: a case in point

The Edge 70 Max itself is a capable device. It features a massive battery, a smooth 144Hz display, and a capable chipset. But it lacks a telephoto lens, and its software update commitment remains unclear.

PhoneArena calls it 'exquisitely affordable and impressively feature-packed,' but GSMArena notes in an editorial that the device 'exposes a bigger problem with Motorola's software update policy.' The subtext is clear: great hardware is undercut by uncertain software support.

In India, where Motorola enjoys a loyal following for its near-stock Android experience, users are increasingly vocal about update delays. Social media threads and tech forums are filled with complaints about phones stuck on older Android versions.

The bottom line for buyers

For a consumer spending Rs 30,000 or more on a phone, knowing how long it will stay current matters. Motorola's vague messaging makes it hard to justify the investment when rivals offer clear, long-term commitments.

Until Motorola adopts a transparent and consistent update policy, its flagships will carry an asterisk. The Edge 70 Max may be a great phone today, but its long-term value depends on software support that remains uncertain.

Motorola has not responded to queries about the Edge 70 Max's update roadmap. Industry watchers expect the company to clarify its policy before the phone's wider release, but no official announcement has been made.
