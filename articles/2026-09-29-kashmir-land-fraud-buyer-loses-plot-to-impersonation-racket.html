Kashmir Land Fraud: Buyer Loses Plot to Impersonation Racket

Key points

  • Original title deeds are not proof of ownership — only the current revenue record (Jamabandi/Fard) and Custodian clearance for migrant property are.

A Plot Bought, Then Built Over

The Indian Express reported a case that has become routine in Jammu and Kashmir: a man bought land in Kashmir, completed payment and mutation, and six years later found another party constructing a house on the same plot. The construction was not illegal on paper — the seller named on that second deed simply was not the real owner.

Within days of that report, police in Srinagar and Jammu announced a cluster of related arrests: a Srinagar resident held in a land fraud case, a Crime Branch Kashmir (CBK) absconder wanted in five separate cases, a Kashmiri Pandit accused in a migrant property fraud case picked up in Jammu, and an Economic Offences Wing (EOW) arrest of a man accused of impersonating the owner of migrant properties to dupe multiple buyers.

Why Migrant Property Is the Soft Target

Migrant-owned land in the Valley is protected under the J&K Migrant Immovable Property (Preservation, Protection and Restraint on Distress Sales) Act, 1997, with a Custodian overseeing transfers. That protection presumes the owner is absent and the records are clean. Fraudsters exploit both assumptions.

Impersonation, not forgery alone

The EOW case points to a specific method: a man presenting himself as the recorded owner of migrant properties and selling to willing buyers. Impersonation defeats most buyer checks because the fraudster appears in person, signs in person, and often produces genuine-looking identity documents linked to the real owner's name.

The revenue record blind spot

Possession, construction and even resale can proceed on a forged chain of title. A half-built house is a strong signal of perceived ownership — and a nightmare to unwind once concrete is poured, because the second buyer may qualify as an innocent purchaser.

What a Buyer Can Actually Verify

Cases of this type typically attract cheating and forgery charges under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, which replaced the IPC's Section 420 and 468 framework in J&K. Civil recovery, however, is slower than construction. Verification before payment is the only reliable defence.

  • Current revenue record over old deeds: The Jamabandi and Fard from the tehsil, not the seller's photocopy, establish who is recorded today.
  • Mutation trail: An unbroken Intiqal chain across at least 30 years exposes gaps that forgers rely on.
  • Custodian clearance: For any migrant-owned plot, a no-objection from the Custodian of Migrant Property is non-negotiable.
  • Physical possession check: Visit the plot in person, multiple times, and photograph whatever stands on it.
  • Encumbrance and litigation search: Confirm no pending suit, bank charge or court attachment on the survey number.
  • Payment through banking channels: Cheque or RTGS creates a traceable trail that cash payments destroy.
