Minor Girl Booked for Remarks Against PM Modi Apologises, Says 'Influenced'

Key points
  • A 15-year-old girl has been booked for allegedly abusing PM Modi during a protest at Jantar Mantar.
  • The minor has issued a public apology, saying she was 'influenced' and asking for forgiveness.
  • The FIR has been transferred to Delhi, with civil rights activists questioning the legal basis for the case.
  • CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke has criticised the action, asking 'Since when is abusing a crime?'

A 15-year-old girl, booked for allegedly using abusive language against Prime Minister Narendra Modi during a protest at Jantar Mantar, has issued a public apology, saying she was "influenced" and pleading for forgiveness. The development comes amid a growing row over the police action, with civil rights activists questioning whether a minor's remark warrants a criminal case.

Apology and Claim of Influence

In a video statement that has since gone viral, the girl, whose identity has not been officially confirmed, said, "I am just 15, forgive me." She claimed she was influenced by others at the protest and did not fully understand the implications of her words. The apology was shared widely on social media, drawing mixed reactions—some expressing sympathy for her age, others demanding strict action.

The minor was booked after a video of her allegedly making derogatory remarks against the Prime Minister surfaced online, sparking outrage among ruling party supporters. The police registered an FIR, but the case has now been transferred to Delhi, a move that has added to the controversy.

Legal Questions and Criticism

Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the Civil Justice Party (CJP), has slammed the FIR, asking, "Since when is abusing a crime?" He recalled a video involving Uttar Pradesh Chief Minister Yogi Adityanath, suggesting a double standard in how such cases are handled. Dipke's remarks have amplified the debate over free speech and the criminalisation of political dissent.

Legal experts point out that while offensive language can attract charges under various sections of the Indian Penal Code, the application of such laws to a minor is fraught with complications. The juvenile justice system prioritises rehabilitation over punishment, and the girl's age is likely to be a key factor in any proceedings.

Protest Context and Police Action

The protest at Jantar Mantar, where the alleged incident took place, was part of a larger demonstration on a separate issue. The girl's remarks, caught on camera, were not the primary focus of the gathering but quickly became the centre of a political storm. Police have not yet confirmed the exact sections under which the FIR was filed, nor have they detailed the evidence against her.

Officials have also not clarified how the case was transferred to Delhi, though such transfers often occur for procedural reasons. The girl's family has not issued a separate statement, and her legal representation remains unknown.

Broader Debate on Free Speech

The incident has reignited a familiar argument in India: where does the line between legitimate criticism and criminal abuse lie? While the Prime Minister is a public figure and subject to scrutiny, the language used by the girl has been widely condemned. Yet, many argue that a 15-year-old's outburst, even if offensive, should not lead to a police case.

The CJP's reference to the UP CM's video adds another layer, suggesting that political figures are not always held to the same standard. This inconsistency, critics say, undermines the credibility of such actions and fuels perceptions of selective justice.

What Happens Next

The case now rests with Delhi authorities, who will decide whether to proceed against the minor. Given her age, the likelihood of a harsh penalty is low, but the legal process could still drag on, keeping the issue in the spotlight. For now, the girl's apology has done little to quell the controversy.

As the investigation moves forward, all eyes will be on how the courts interpret the law in this unusual case. The outcome could set a precedent for how similar incidents involving minors are treated, making this more than just a one-off apology.
