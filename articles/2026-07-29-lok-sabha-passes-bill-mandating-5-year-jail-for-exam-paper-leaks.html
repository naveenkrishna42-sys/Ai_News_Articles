Lok Sabha Passes Bill Mandating 5-Year Jail for Exam Paper Leaks

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Minimum five-year imprisonment for organised exam malpractices and paper leaks.
  • Bill passed amid political row over Rahul Gandhi's remarks on exam irregularities.
  • Education Minister Pralhad Joshi said the law aims to 'dismantle the paper leak ecosystem'.
  • Union Minister Jitendra Singh stated no students were fired upon during related incidents.

New Law Targets Organised Cheating Networks

The Lok Sabha on Wednesday passed the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026, introducing a minimum five-year prison term for those involved in paper leaks and large-scale exam malpractices. The legislation is designed to crack down on organised networks that profit from undermining the integrity of public recruitment tests.

Education Minister Pralhad Joshi told the House that the Bill aims to 'dismantle the paper leak ecosystem'. He emphasised that the law would act as a strong deterrent against cartels that have repeatedly compromised competitive examinations across the country.

Political Exchange Over Student Safety

The debate turned heated after Congress leader Rahul Gandhi made remarks linking the government's handling of exam irregularities to alleged violence against students. Responding to the charge in the House, Union Minister Jitendra Singh categorically stated that 'no shots were fired at students' during any incident related to exam malpractices.

The Minister's clarification came amid opposition criticism that the government was using a heavy hand rather than addressing the root causes of exam fraud. The exchange highlighted the political sensitivity surrounding the issue, which has affected lakhs of candidates appearing for central and state recruitment exams.

Key Provisions of the Bill

Under the new law, individuals found guilty of leaking question papers or operating cheating syndicates face a minimum of five years in prison, which can extend up to ten years. The Bill also imposes heavy fines on those involved in such activities.

Service providers contracted to conduct examinations will also be held accountable. If a company or its officers are found complicit in malpractices, they can be penalised with fines and debarred from future contracts. The legislation covers all central government recruitment exams and tests conducted by bodies like the Union Public Service Commission, Staff Selection Commission, and Railway Recruitment Boards.

Government's Rationale

The government argued that existing laws were insufficient to deal with the scale and sophistication of modern exam fraud. In recent years, multiple high-profile paper leak cases in states like Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Uttar Pradesh had forced the cancellation of exams, affecting the careers of hundreds of thousands of young job seekers.

Minister Joshi said the Bill was drafted after consultations with state governments and security agencies. He stressed that the focus is on the organisers and facilitators of cheating, not on individual candidates who may be victims of the system.

What happens next? The Bill will now move to the Rajya Sabha for consideration. If passed in the Upper House and signed by the President, it will become law, marking one of the strictest anti-cheating regimes for public examinations in India.
