Hyderabad Monsoon: Nearly 1,000 Waterlogging Hotspots Hit Commuters

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nearly 1,000 waterlogging hotspots have been identified across Hyderabad amid heavy monsoon rains.
  • Commuters face severe traffic disruptions and delays in several parts of the city.
  • Civic authorities are yet to announce a comprehensive mitigation plan.

Hyderabad's monsoon has turned into a test of patience for commuters, with nearly 1,000 waterlogging hotspots reported across the city. The relentless downpour over the past few days has thrown traffic out of gear, leaving motorists stranded and pedestrians wading through knee-deep water.

The sheer scale of the problem has overwhelmed the civic machinery. From busy junctions to residential colonies, water accumulation has become a recurring nightmare. This year, the number of vulnerable points has surged, reflecting the strain on the city's drainage infrastructure.

Where the Water Collects

Areas such as Begumpet, Khairatabad, Ameerpet, and LB Nagar have emerged as chronic trouble spots. Low-lying neighbourhoods like Kukatpally and Mehdipatnam are also witnessing severe inundation after every heavy spell.

Commuters report hours of delays on key corridors, with vehicles crawling through submerged stretches. Two-wheeler riders are particularly vulnerable, often forced to dismount and push their bikes through flooded roads.

Impact on Daily Life

The waterlogging has disrupted not just office-goers but also students and emergency services. Autos and cabs are refusing trips to affected areas, leaving many stranded at bus stops and metro stations.

In some zones, water has entered ground-floor residences and shops, damaging goods and valuables. Residents complain that complaints to the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) have yielded little immediate relief.

Why the Problem Persists

Experts point to rapid urbanisation and encroachments on stormwater drains as key factors. The city's drainage network, built decades ago, is struggling to cope with the current intensity of rainfall.

Blocked culverts and silted channels worsen the situation, turning even moderate rain into a crisis. While the GHMC has deployed pumps and teams, the sheer number of hotspots makes timely response difficult.

Commuters Take Precautions

Many residents are now checking weather apps and social media groups before stepping out. Some offices have allowed work-from-home, but essential workers have no such option.

"I left home an hour early but still reached office late," said a software engineer stuck on the ORR. Such stories are common, as the city grapples with the monsoon's fury.

What Authorities Say

Officials claim they are monitoring the situation and have identified the hotspots for focused action. However, a comprehensive long-term solution is yet to be announced.

The state government has promised to desilt drains and upgrade pumping stations before the next spell. But residents remain sceptical, given the recurring nature of the problem every year.

With the monsoon expected to continue for several more weeks, Hyderabad's battle with waterlogging is far from over. The coming days will test both the city's infrastructure and the resilience of its people.
