Dynaudio soundbar with wooden fins and 24 drivers tested

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Dynaudio's new soundbar features mechanized wooden fins and 24 drivers for Dolby Atmos.
  • An executive discusses designing for increasingly large TVs and hiding tech in furniture.
  • The soundbar aims to blend high-end audio with aesthetic, minimalist design.

A soundbar that moves like a living thing

When I first laid eyes on Dynaudio's latest soundbar, I had to double-check it wasn't a piece of modernist furniture. The unit's front is dominated by sleek wooden fins that shift mechanically as the system powers up, revealing the driver array beneath. It's a theatrical touch, but one that serves a purpose: the fins help direct sound precisely, creating a more convincing Dolby Atmos bubble without cluttering the room with visible speakers.

Inside, the soundbar packs 24 drivers, each individually amplified and tuned. The result, in my testing, was a soundstage that felt impossibly wide — dialogue anchored to the screen, effects swirling overhead, and bass that didn't need a separate subwoofer to make its presence felt. For a single bar, it's an awe-inspiring performance.

Designing for giant TVs and tiny spaces

I spoke with a Dynaudio executive about the challenges of building audio for today's televisions. Screens have grown to 85, 97, even 110 inches, but the space beneath them hasn't. The bar has to be low enough to sit under a massive panel without blocking the picture, yet tall enough to house drivers that can fill a large room. That constraint drove the unique fin mechanism — it allows the bar to stay slim when idle, then expand its acoustic surface when it's time to perform.

The exec also emphasized that modern buyers are increasingly design-conscious. People don't want black plastic boxes dominating their living rooms. They want speakers that disappear into the decor, or better yet, become part of it. This is where the idea of 'beautiful objects' comes in — the soundbar is designed to be seen, not hidden, with real wood veneers and a build quality that rivals high-end cabinetry.

Hiding tech in plain sight

But not everyone wants their audio gear on display. The exec acknowledged that for many customers, the ideal setup is one where the tech is invisible. Dynaudio's approach, he said, is to offer flexibility: the soundbar can be placed on a media console, mounted on a wall, or even integrated into a custom cabinet with the fins acting as a subtle grille. The goal is to deliver elite performance without forcing a visual compromise.

That philosophy extends beyond the soundbar itself. The company is exploring ways to conceal subwoofers and surround speakers into furniture — think coffee tables that double as bass modules, or bookshelf speakers that look like decorative objects. It's a response to a growing demand for home theater that doesn't announce itself.

The sound of things to come

My time with the Dynaudio soundbar left me impressed, not just by the audio quality but by the thoughtfulness of the design. It's a product that respects both the content you're watching and the room you're watching it in. The mechanized fins aren't a gimmick — they're a solution to a real problem, and they look stunning in motion.

As TVs continue to grow and living spaces shrink, expect more brands to follow Dynaudio's lead. The next wave of home audio will likely be defined by how well it integrates with the home itself. Dynaudio has set a high bar, and the industry will be watching.

For now, the soundbar remains a premium offering, and pricing has not been officially announced for all markets. But if this is a glimpse of where home theater is headed, the future sounds — and looks — very good indeed.
