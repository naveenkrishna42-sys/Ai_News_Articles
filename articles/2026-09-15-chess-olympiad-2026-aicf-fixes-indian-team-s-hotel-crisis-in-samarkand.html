Accommodation Chaos Hits Indian Camp in Samarkand

India's chess contingent arrived in Samarkand for the 46th FIDE Chess Olympiad only to spend hours without confirmed hotel rooms, a logistical failure that forced the All India Chess Federation (AICF) to intervene directly with organisers. Captain Srinath Narayanan described the experience as "having a terrible experience," flagging that players were left waiting while other federations were checked in.

The AICF's intervention secured rooms for both the men's and women's squads. The episode is a reminder that for a 180-plus team event, host-city logistics are as decisive as opening preparation.

What Actually Happened, and Why It Matters

According to reports from the ground, the Indian delegation reached the designated hotel without rooms ready, despite confirmed bookings. Players — including some of the world's highest-rated — waited for hours in lobbies rather than resting before the opening rounds. Narayanan's public criticism was notable because team captains rarely escalate issues mid-tournament; doing so signals the problem had crossed a threshold.

For a professional squad, lost rest hours are not cosmetic. Chess at Olympiad level is a seven-to-nine-round grind of three-to-five-hour games. A single night of disrupted sleep can cost half a point, and half a point often separates a medal from fourth place.

Buyer's Guide View: What Federations Should Demand

For national federations budgeting lakhs per campaign, accommodation is not a fringe expense. When selecting events, administrators should insist on written room guarantees, on-site liaison officers, and penalty clauses for booking failures. The AICF's rapid escalation worked — but prevention beats cure.

Practical Benchmarks for Team Logistics

  • Rooms confirmed and inspected at least 24 hours before player arrival.
  • A federation liaison present at check-in with a printed rooming list.
  • Backup hotel identified within 15 minutes of the venue.
  • Direct escalation channel to FIDE's organising committee.

The Bigger Picture

FIDE has expanded the Olympiad into a marquee commercial property, with sponsorship and broadcast revenue climbing each cycle. That growth must be matched by host-city readiness. When elite players — some earning crores in prize money and endorsements — are left waiting in lobbies, the credibility of the event itself is tested.

India's teams eventually settled in, and the women's squad remains in contention to defend its title. Yet the Samarkand episode should push federations to treat logistics with the same seriousness as match preparation.
