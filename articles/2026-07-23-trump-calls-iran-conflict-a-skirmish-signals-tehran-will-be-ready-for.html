Trump calls Iran conflict a ‘skirmish’, signals Tehran will be ready for a deal ‘very soon’

📅 2026-07-23 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump describes Iran operations as a ‘skirmish’, not a full-scale war
  • CENTCOM confirms redirecting 9 ships and disabling 1 vessel in blockade
  • Trump predicts Iran will soon be ready to negotiate a deal

Trump downplays conflict amid escalating blockade

Former US President Donald Trump has described the ongoing military engagement with Iran as a ‘skirmish’, pushing back against characterisations of a full-blown war. He signalled that Tehran would be ready for a diplomatic resolution ‘very soon’.

The remarks came as the US Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed it had redirected nine ships and disabled one vessel as part of a naval blockade near Iranian waters. The operation has drawn sharp reactions from regional powers and raised questions about the scope of American military objectives.

Official statements contrast with field reports

Trump’s characterisation of the situation as a minor engagement stands in contrast to reports from the ground. The New Indian Express noted that Iran has widened its attacks, while Euronews reported that the cost of the conflict is increasing and that the US has shown no interest in talks.

Despite this, Trump insisted that Iran is ‘not ready to make a deal’ now, but predicted that they will be prepared ‘very soon’. The timeline remains unclear, and officials have not yet confirmed any concrete diplomatic channels.

Naval operations and strategic implications

CENTCOM’s blockade has been a key component of the US strategy. According to ABC News, the command ‘disabled’ one ship and redirected nine others, though details on the disabled vessel’s crew and cargo remain sparse. The move is aimed at restricting Iranian maritime trade and military supplies.

Analysts suggest that such operations could escalate into a broader confrontation if Tehran retaliates aggressively. So far, Iran has widened its attacks but has not targeted US naval assets directly.

Mixed signals from Washington and Tehran

Trump’s statements appear to send mixed signals: on one hand, he portrays the conflict as limited; on the other, he claims the US is ‘not finished’. This ambiguity has left allies and adversaries guessing about the next steps.

The Times of Israel quoted Trump as saying Iran is ‘not ready to make a deal,’ but he added that they ‘will be very soon.’ No specific framework or mediator has been mentioned. Indian strategic interests remain watchful, given the region’s impact on energy prices and the large Indian diaspora in the Gulf.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on whether the CENTCOM blockade escalates or de-escalates in the coming days. The key question is whether Tehran will indeed signal readiness for negotiations, or if the ‘skirmish’ widens into a sustained conflict.
