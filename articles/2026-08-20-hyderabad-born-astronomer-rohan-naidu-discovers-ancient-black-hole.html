Hyderabad-born Astronomer Rohan Naidu Discovers Ancient Black Hole

📅 2026-08-20 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rohan Naidu, an MIT-based astronomer originally from Hyderabad, led the discovery of a gas-enshrouded black hole.
  • The celestial body originates from the cosmic dawn, the period shortly after the Big Bang.
  • The discovery challenges current understanding of how early black holes formed and grew in dusty environments.

A Trailblazing Discovery in Deep Space

Rohan Naidu, a Hyderabad-born researcher currently based at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), has captured the global scientific community’s attention. His recent work involves the identification of a rare, gas-enshrouded black hole dating back to the cosmic dawn. This era, occurring shortly after the Big Bang, remains one of the most mysterious periods in the evolution of the universe.

The discovery suggests that black holes in the early universe may have been far more obscured by dust and gas than previous models indicated. This finding provides a new perspective on how massive objects evolved during the infancy of space-time. The research, which has been featured in the scientific journal Nature, highlights the complex nature of celestial bodies during the universe's formative years.

From Engineering Dropout to Astrophysics

Naidu’s path to the frontiers of space science was unconventional. At 18, he made the decision to drop out of an engineering programme, a move that shifted his trajectory toward the stars. His academic pivot eventually led him to the prestigious halls of MIT, where he has since established himself as a key figure in observational astronomy.

Despite his demanding schedule conducting high-level research, Naidu maintains a strong connection to his personal interests. Colleagues often note his passion for Test cricket, a sport he continues to follow closely alongside his work on cosmic phenomena. His journey from Hyderabad to Hawaii—where much of the telescope data for his research is processed—serves as an inspiration for students pursuing non-traditional routes into science.

Solving the Cosmic Mystery

The "black hole star" he identified is characterised by its heavy gas-reddened appearance. Astronomers have long debated how such massive black holes could form so quickly after the Big Bang. The presence of significant dust surrounding the object indicates that the early universe was a much more cluttered and active environment than early theories proposed.

  • The object is located at the cosmic dawn.
  • Heavy gas shrouding makes the black hole difficult to detect with standard equipment.
  • The discovery provides a target for future study using advanced space observatories.

This discovery forces researchers to re-evaluate the timelines of galaxy formation. By observing the way this black hole interacts with its dusty environment, the team can better understand the feedback mechanisms that govern the growth of supermassive black holes. The data gathered from Hawaii’s observatories remains central to validating these new models.

The scientific community now expects further observations of this region to confirm if similar obscured black holes exist elsewhere in the early universe. Follow-up studies are likely to focus on the chemical composition of the dust surrounding the object.
