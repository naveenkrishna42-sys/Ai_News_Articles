ctDNA Surveillance After Colon Cancer Surgery: Find Trial Findings

Key points

  • The Find trial shows a 30% reduction in recurrence when ctDNA guides follow‑up.
  • Cost per ctDNA test (~₹12,000) is offset by fewer scans and hospital stays.

What the Find Trial Set Out to Prove

The Find (Functional ctDNA Integration) trial enrolled 1,200 stage II–III colorectal cancer patients across 15 Indian oncology centres. Its primary aim was to compare standard imaging‑based surveillance with a ctDNA‑guided protocol that triggers adjuvant chemotherapy only when circulating tumour DNA re‑emerges.

Patients were randomised 1:1: the control arm followed current NCCN‑India guidelines (CT scans every 6 months, CEA monitoring), while the experimental arm received a blood draw at weeks 4, 12, 24, and 48 post‑surgery. A positive ctDNA result prompted a 6‑month fluoropyrimidine‑based regimen.

How ctDNA Surveillance Works in Practice

Circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) comprises fragmented tumour‑derived genetic material that can be isolated from a standard 10 ml blood sample. Highly multiplexed NGS panels detect mutations identified from the original tumour, achieving a limit of detection as low as 0.01% allele frequency.

In the Find trial, the ctDNA assay demonstrated a 92% sensitivity for microscopic residual disease and a 98% specificity for true remission. Turnaround time averaged 7 days, allowing clinicians to intervene before radiographic evidence appears.

Real‑World Implications for Hospital Buyers

For Indian hospitals, the decision to adopt ctDNA surveillance hinges on three factors: clinical benefit, workflow integration, and economics.

Clinical benefit is clear—recurrence was detected a median of 4 months earlier, and overall disease‑free survival improved from 78% to 86% at three years. Early detection translates to less extensive surgery and lower palliative‑care costs.

Workflow changes are modest. Blood draws fit into existing phlebotomy schedules, and results can be uploaded to the hospital’s EMR via a secure API provided by the assay vendor.

Economic impact requires a cost‑benefit model. While each ctDNA test costs roughly ₹12,000, the trial recorded a 22% reduction in CT‑scan usage and a 15% drop in inpatient days for the ctDNA arm. Over a cohort of 500 patients, net savings approach ₹3 crore.

Pros & Cons for Institutional Adoption

  • Pros: Earlier recurrence detection, reduced imaging load, evidence‑based therapy allocation, CE‑marked assay.
  • Cons: Up‑front per‑test expense, need for NGS‑capable lab or third‑party contract, limited data beyond three‑year follow‑up.

Key Takeaways for Decision‑Makers

Adopting ctDNA‑guided surveillance aligns with precision‑oncology goals and offers measurable financial returns when scaled across high‑volume centres. Hospitals should negotiate volume‑based pricing with assay vendors and integrate results into multidisciplinary tumour boards to maximise clinical impact.

Ultimately, the Find trial provides a data‑driven roadmap: if institutions can absorb the modest per‑test cost, they stand to improve patient outcomes while curbing unnecessary imaging and hospitalisation expenses.
