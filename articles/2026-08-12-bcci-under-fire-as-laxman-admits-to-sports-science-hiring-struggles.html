BCCI under fire as Laxman admits to Sports Science hiring struggles

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • VVS Laxman admitted BCCI struggled to hire a head of sports science, surprising former cricketers.
  • Multiple candidates withdrew from the recruitment process, raising questions about BCCI's approach.
  • Critics accuse BCCI of taking injuries lightly, while Laxman defends the recovery process at the Centre of Excellence.

Laxman's frank admission triggers backlash

VVS Laxman, the National Cricket Academy (NCA) head, has revealed that the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) faced a 'nightmare' in its search for a head of sports science, a candid admission that has drawn sharp criticism from former cricketers and fans alike. The revelation comes amid a growing injury crisis plaguing Indian cricket, with several key players sidelined in recent months.

Laxman, speaking to reporters, expressed surprise at the difficulty in attracting top medical professionals to the role. "We were surprised by the lack of interest," he said, adding that the BCCI had asked the right questions but may not have liked the answers. His comments have been interpreted by many as an indictment of the board's handling of player fitness and recovery.

Kaif leads the criticism

Former India cricketer Mohammed Kaif was among the first to publicly call out the BCCI, accusing the board of 'taking injuries lightly'. In a series of posts on social media, Kaif questioned how the world's richest cricket board could struggle to hire qualified sports scientists. "If the BCCI can't attract the best, something is fundamentally wrong," he wrote, echoing sentiments shared by many in the cricketing fraternity.

The criticism has intensified following reports that multiple candidates withdrew from the recruitment process for the Sports Science and Medicine (SSM) head position. While the reasons for these withdrawals remain undisclosed, speculation is rife that the board's working culture and inadequate compensation may be deterring potential hires.

Players at COE 'are no machines', says Laxman

In his defence, Laxman insisted that the recovery process at the Centre of Excellence (COE) in Bengaluru was robust, but emphasised that players were not machines. "We cannot rush recovery. Each athlete is different, and we must prioritise long-term health over short-term gains," he said, attempting to assuage concerns about the board's approach to injury management.

However, his remarks have done little to quell the criticism. Cricket analysts have pointed out that the BCCI's reliance on a handful of physios and trainers, rather than a dedicated sports science department, has led to inconsistent injury management. The board's failure to fill the SSM head role, a position critical to overseeing player fitness, has been highlighted as a glaring oversight.

BCCI's search stalls

According to reports, the BCCI's search for an SSM head has stalled after several candidates turned down the offer. The role, which involves coordinating medical staff, designing recovery protocols, and liaising with team management, has been vacant for months. Insiders suggest that the board's rigid hierarchy and lack of autonomy for medical professionals have been significant deterrents.

The timing of this controversy could not be worse, with India's schedule packed with international fixtures and the next World Cup looming. The injury list includes several first-choice players, raising questions about the team's preparedness for major tournaments. Former players have urged the BCCI to act swiftly, warning that continued neglect could cost India dearly on the field.

What lies ahead

The BCCI is expected to address the issue at its next board meeting, with interim measures likely to be announced. However, the board must first acknowledge the gravity of the problem and overhaul its recruitment strategy to attract the best talent. For now, the cricketing world watches closely, hoping that lessons are learned before more careers are derailed by preventable injuries.
