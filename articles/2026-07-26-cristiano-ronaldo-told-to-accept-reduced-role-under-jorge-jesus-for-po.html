Cristiano Ronaldo told to accept reduced role under Jorge Jesus for Portugal future

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Jorge Jesus has communicated to Cristiano Ronaldo that he will not be an automatic starter for Portugal.
  • Ronaldo's 233-cap career with Portugal may depend on accepting a squad role with variable playing time.
  • The decision comes as Portugal looks to integrate younger talent ahead of future tournaments.

A new reality for Portugal's captain

Cristiano Ronaldo has been told in no uncertain terms that his days as an automatic starter for Portugal are over. New manager Jorge Jesus has made it clear that the 233-cap veteran will have to accept a rotational role if he wishes to continue representing his country.

According to sources close to the national team setup, Jesus held a direct conversation with Ronaldo before the latest international break. The message was blunt: 'Sometimes he'll play, other times he won't.'

Jesus lays down the law

Jorge Jesus, who took over the Portugal job after the 2024 European Championship, is known for his tactical flexibility and willingness to make bold decisions. He has reportedly told Ronaldo that the team's needs will come before any individual's legacy.

The manager is keen to build a side that can compete at the highest level without relying solely on its ageing superstar. With younger attackers like Rafael Leão, João Félix, and Gonçalo Ramos knocking on the door, Jesus believes Portugal must evolve.

A career at a crossroads

Ronaldo, 41, remains Portugal's all-time leading scorer and most capped player. His influence in the dressing room is undeniable. But his recent club form — at Al Nassr in Saudi Arabia — has raised questions about his ability to perform at elite international levels consistently.

The forward has faced similar transitions before. At Manchester United, he accepted a reduced role under Erik ten Hag before eventually leaving. For Portugal, however, the stakes are higher. This is his national team, where he has been the undisputed leader for nearly two decades.

What this means for Portugal's future

Jesus's approach signals a clear shift in Portugal's strategy. The team is no longer willing to build its system around one player, even if that player is Cristiano Ronaldo. The focus is on collective performance and long-term planning.

Portugal has a rich pool of talent. The 2026 World Cup qualifiers are approaching, and Jesus wants a squad that can adapt to different opponents and situations. Ronaldo's experience off the bench could be a valuable weapon, but only if he accepts the new dynamic.

Ronaldo has not publicly commented on the development. His camp is believed to be weighing the options. Retirement from international football remains a possibility, though the player has shown no desire to walk away just yet.

The coming weeks will be telling. Portugal's next set of fixtures will reveal whether Ronaldo is willing to sit on the bench for the good of the team — or whether his remarkable international career will reach an unexpected end.
