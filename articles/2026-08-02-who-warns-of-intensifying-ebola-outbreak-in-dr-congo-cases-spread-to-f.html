WHO warns of intensifying Ebola outbreak in DR Congo, cases spread to five provinces

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ebola outbreak in DR Congo intensifies, now spread across five provinces.
  • WHO identifies the Bundibugyo strain as the cause, the worst-ever for this virus in the country.
  • The outbreak is now the world's second-largest Ebola epidemic on record.

The World Health Organization has issued a stark warning about the worsening Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, confirming that the virus has now spread to five provinces. The agency described the situation as intensifying, with new cases continuing to emerge despite ongoing response efforts.

The outbreak, caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the Ebola virus, has become the most severe of its kind ever recorded in the country. According to the WHO, this marks the second-largest Ebola epidemic globally, underscoring the scale of the public health challenge facing Congolese authorities and international partners.

Bundibugyo strain poses distinct challenges

The Bundibugyo virus, one of the six known species of Ebola, has historically caused smaller outbreaks compared to the more infamous Zaire strain. However, this current epidemic has defied expectations, with transmission persisting across a wide geographic area. Health officials note that the strain's lower fatality rate, while less lethal, has not diminished its ability to spread effectively among communities.

Experts point out that the Bundibugyo strain's behaviour in this outbreak differs from previous episodes. The virus has shown resilience in remote and conflict-affected regions, complicating containment measures. Local health workers, already stretched thin, are grappling with logistical hurdles and community mistrust in some areas.

Geographic spread and response efforts

The confirmation of cases in five provinces marks a significant escalation from earlier weeks when the outbreak appeared confined to a single region. The WHO has deployed additional teams to support surveillance, contact tracing, and vaccination campaigns. However, the agency has stressed that resources are finite and that international support is critical to curbing further spread.

Neighbouring countries have heightened border screenings as a precautionary measure. The WHO is coordinating with regional health ministries to ensure a unified response, although the porous nature of borders in Central Africa remains a concern for cross-border transmission.

Historical context and global implications

This outbreak now ranks as the world's second-largest Ebola epidemic ever documented, trailing only the devastating West Africa outbreak of 2014-2016, which claimed over 11,000 lives. That comparison has reignited concerns among global health officials about the potential for the current epidemic to spiral out of control if containment efforts falter.

The DR Congo has experienced numerous Ebola outbreaks in the past, but the frequency and scale of recent events have tested the country's health system. The WHO's warning serves as a reminder that the threat is not merely regional but has implications for global health security, particularly in an era of increased international travel.

What to watch

In the coming weeks, the effectiveness of vaccination drives and community engagement will be key indicators of whether the outbreak can be contained. The WHO is expected to release updated case counts and strategic assessments, which will shape the next phase of the response. For now, the focus remains on halting the virus's march across the Congo's provinces.
