‘Be realistic, we’re in India’: Bombay HC quashes hotel licence suspension over hygiene

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bombay High Court quashed FDA's suspension of a hotel's licence over hygiene issues.
  • The court remarked 'be realistic, we're in India' while setting aside the order.
  • The ruling raises questions about the enforcement of hygiene standards in the hospitality sector.

Court's blunt observation

The Bombay High Court has set aside an order by the Food and Drugs Administration (FDA) suspending a hotel's licence over alleged hygiene violations. In its ruling, the court delivered a sharp reminder to regulators about ground realities in the country.

“Be realistic, we’re in India,” the bench reportedly observed while quashing the suspension. The statement has since been widely noted, highlighting the tension between regulatory expectations and practical enforcement in the hospitality industry.

The FDA's action

The FDA had suspended the hotel's licence citing poor hygiene standards. Officials claimed that inspections revealed unsanitary conditions in the kitchen and storage areas, warranting immediate action.

Hotel management challenged the order in court, arguing that the suspension was disproportionate and that the alleged violations did not pose an imminent health risk. They also contended that the FDA had not followed proper procedure before issuing the suspension.

What the court said

While the exact reasoning in the judgment is yet to be made public in full, sources present in the courtroom said the bench questioned the FDA's approach. The court reportedly pointed out that expecting five-star standards from every establishment in a country like India was unrealistic.

The judges emphasised that while hygiene regulations must be enforced, they should be applied with a sense of proportion. The court also noted that the hotel had been operating without any major complaints from customers for years.

Implications for regulators

The ruling is likely to have a ripple effect on how food and safety authorities handle licence suspensions. Legal experts say it could force the FDA to be more measured in its enforcement actions, especially in smaller cities and towns.

Consumer rights groups, however, have expressed concern that the court's remarks might be misinterpreted as a license to cut corners on hygiene. They argue that food safety standards should not be diluted regardless of location.

What happens next

The hotel's licence has been restored, at least for now. The FDA may review its enforcement guidelines in light of the court's observations. Whether the regulator appeals the decision remains to be seen. For the hospitality sector, the message is clear: rules exist, but they must be applied with a dose of reality.
