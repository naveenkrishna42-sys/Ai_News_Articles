Chennai's top 100 property tax defaulters owe ₹31.39 crore

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Top 100 defaulters owe ₹31.39 crore in property tax to Chennai Corporation
  • Chennai Corporation planning action to recover outstanding dues
  • List of defaulters includes commercial and residential properties

Recovery drive targets big defaulters

Chennai Corporation has identified its top 100 property tax defaulters, who together owe a staggering ₹31.39 crore. The list includes owners of commercial complexes, residential apartments, and industrial units spread across the city.

Officials said the default amount has accumulated over several years, with some property owners failing to pay taxes since 2018. The corporation now plans to intensify its recovery drive, focusing on these high-value defaulters first.

How the dues piled up

Property tax is a major revenue source for the corporation, funding civic amenities like roads, streetlights, and garbage collection. Yet, a significant chunk remains unrealised due to persistent non-payment.

According to sources, many defaulters are repeat offenders who have ignored multiple notices. Others have disputed their assessments, leading to prolonged legal standoffs. The corporation has now decided to move beyond gentle reminders.

What action is being taken

The civic body is preparing to attach properties, seize bank accounts, and even cut water and sewerage connections to compel payment. Officials have warned that no further concessions will be given to those who have avoided taxes for years.

In recent months, the corporation has already recovered a portion of arrears through targeted drives. However, the top 100 list remains a tough nut to crack, with many defaulters being influential business owners.

Public response and transparency

Residents' welfare associations have welcomed the move, urging the corporation to publish the names of defaulters publicly. They argue that transparency will pressure defaulters and ensure fairness for those who pay on time.

Some defaulters, however, claim they have paid their dues but were not given proper receipts or the corporation's records are outdated. Officials have asked such parties to approach the zonal offices with proof of payment.

What happens next

The corporation is expected to release a detailed action plan in the coming weeks, including a timeline for recovery. It has also hinted at stricter penalties, including interest on overdue amounts.

With the city's finances under strain, recovering this ₹31.39 crore could provide a much-needed boost. Whether the defaulters pay up or face attachment remains to be seen.
