SBI eyes 5-6 M&A financing deals under new RBI norms

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • SBI has 5-6 M&A financing deals in the pipeline under the new RBI rules.
  • The deals are expected to close in the coming quarters, subject to regulatory approvals.
  • This signals a robust pipeline for the bank's corporate banking segment.
  • The RBI's new rules have opened up opportunities for banks to finance acquisitions.

M&A financing push

State Bank of India (SBI), the country's largest lender, has 5-6 merger and acquisition (M&A) financing deals in its pipeline, according to a report by BusinessLine. These deals are being structured under the new Reserve Bank of India (RBI) rules that allow banks greater flexibility in financing acquisitions.

The development marks a significant shift in the bank's approach to corporate lending, with SBI now actively courting the M&A space. The deals are understood to be at various stages of negotiation, with some expected to close in the coming quarters.

What the new RBI rules mean

The RBI's revised guidelines on M&A financing have opened up new avenues for banks. Previously, regulatory constraints limited the extent to which banks could fund acquisitions, often forcing companies to rely on alternative funding sources. The new framework, introduced recently, relaxes some of those restrictions, allowing banks like SBI to offer more competitive financing packages.

For SBI, this is a strategic opportunity to deepen its corporate relationships and tap into a growing demand for acquisition financing. The bank has been expanding its corporate banking portfolio, and these deals are expected to contribute meaningfully to its loan growth.

Pipeline details and outlook

While SBI has not disclosed the names of the companies involved or the deal values, the pipeline is said to span across sectors. Sources suggest that the bank is evaluating opportunities in manufacturing, technology, and infrastructure, among others. The deals are likely to involve both domestic and cross-border transactions, though the latter would be subject to additional regulatory scrutiny.

The bank's management is believed to be confident about the deal flow, given the improving economic environment and the uptick in corporate activity. SBI's strong balance sheet and low cost of funds give it an edge in pricing these loans competitively.

Impact on the banking sector

SBI's foray into M&A financing is likely to have a ripple effect on the broader banking sector. Other lenders may follow suit, leading to increased competition in this segment. For corporates, this means more options and better terms when structuring deals.

However, the bank will need to manage risks carefully, especially in a volatile global environment. M&A financing comes with inherent risks, including deal failures and integration challenges. SBI's underwriting standards and due diligence processes will be key to ensuring the portfolio remains healthy.

The coming months will be crucial as the bank works to close these deals. Market watchers will be keenly watching the pace of closures and the quality of the assets added.

As the pipeline materialises, SBI is expected to report a noticeable uptick in its corporate loan book. The bank's ability to execute these transactions efficiently will determine its success in this new arena.

What to watch: The next few quarters will reveal how many of these deals actually close, and whether SBI can maintain its momentum in M&A financing amid a competitive landscape.
