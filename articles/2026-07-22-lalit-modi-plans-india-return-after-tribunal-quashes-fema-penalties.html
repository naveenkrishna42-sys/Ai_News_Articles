Lalit Modi Plans India Return After Tribunal Quashes FEMA Penalties

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • A tribunal has set aside most FEMA violation charges against Lalit Modi and the BCCI.
  • Modi, currently in London, says he will return to India by early next year.
  • The case relates to the 2009 IPL in South Africa; the ED had imposed penalties.
  • Modi called the verdict a vindication and said a chapter is behind him.

Lalit Modi, the former Indian Premier League (IPL) chairman, has announced plans to return to India by early next year. The statement comes after a tribunal set aside most of the Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA) violation charges against him and the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI).

Verdict Brings Relief

The tribunal's ruling quashed the penalty imposed by the Enforcement Directorate (ED) in connection with the 2009 IPL season held in South Africa. The ED had alleged FEMA violations in the transfer of funds related to the tournament.

Modi, who has been living in London since 2010, described the decision as a major relief. "The chapter is behind me," he said in a statement, adding that he looks forward to returning home.

Timeline for Return

"I will return to India by early next year," Modi told reporters. He did not specify an exact date but indicated that the legal clearance has paved the way for his homecoming after nearly 16 years in self-imposed exile.

The ED had originally slapped a penalty running into several crore rupees on Modi and the BCCI. The tribunal's order has significantly reduced the financial liability, though sources say some minor technical issues remain unresolved.

Background of the Case

The case dates back to the 2009 IPL, which was held in South Africa due to the general elections in India. The ED investigated the movement of foreign currency and alleged that Modi and the BCCI had violated FEMA norms during the tournament's organisation.

Modi has consistently denied the charges, arguing that all transactions were legitimate and approved by the BCCI. The tribunal's verdict appears to have vindicated his position on most counts.

Reactions and Next Steps

The BCCI has not yet issued an official statement on the tribunal's order. Legal experts say the ruling could set a precedent for other cases involving sports bodies and foreign exchange regulations.

Modi's return is expected to be closely watched. He remains a polarising figure in Indian cricket, credited with creating the IPL but also facing allegations of financial irregularities that led to his ouster from the BCCI and the league.

For now, Modi is focusing on the legal formalities required for his travel. "I have waited a long time for this day," he said. "I am eager to see my family and friends in India."

The government has not commented on the tribunal's decision. Officials said they are reviewing the order before deciding on any further action.

All eyes will now be on early next year, when Modi is expected to land in India for the first time since 2010. The cricketing fraternity and legal observers will be watching closely.
