NEET Paper Leak: How Experts Used Chits to Smuggle Questions

📅 2026-08-07 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • CBI charges 13 people in NEET-UG 2026 paper leak case
  • Experts memorised questions and used chits to smuggle them out of NTA office
  • Leak syndicate operated across multiple states, exploiting NTA's shortcomings

The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) has filed a chargesheet in the NEET-UG 2026 paper leak case, exposing a brazen operation where testing agency insiders turned conspirators. According to the chargesheet, experts hired by the National Testing Agency (NTA) memorised questions from the exam paper and used small chits to smuggle them out of the office.

The revelations have sent shockwaves through the education sector, raising serious questions about the integrity of one of India's most competitive medical entrance exams. The CBI has charged 13 individuals in connection with a multi-state leak syndicate, according to The New Indian Express.

The Mechanics of the Leak

Investigators have detailed how the experts, who were supposed to be vetting the question paper, instead turned into conspirators. The process was surprisingly low-tech: they would memorise specific questions, write them down on small pieces of paper, and carry these chits out of the NTA's office.

Once outside, the questions were passed on to intermediaries who then circulated them to paying candidates across several states. The CBI's chargesheet, as reported by The Indian Express, notes that NTA's own 'shortcomings' created the conditions that made such a leak possible.

NTA's Security Gaps

The chargesheet highlights multiple lapses in the testing agency's protocols. These included inadequate supervision of experts during the paper-setting process and a lack of stringent checks on personal belongings. The agency's failure to implement basic security measures, such as frisking or restricting the use of paper and pens, allowed the experts to operate with relative ease.

ThePrint's coverage of the CBI chargesheet points to a systemic failure within the NTA, where trust in internal experts was placed above the need for rigorous oversight. The leak was not a one-off act but part of a coordinated effort involving multiple players.

Multi-State Syndicate

The CBI's investigation has uncovered a network that spanned several states, with each node responsible for different parts of the operation. Some individuals were tasked with receiving the leaked questions, while others managed the logistics of distributing them to candidates. The syndicate's reach has alarmed authorities, who are now probing how far the leak extended.

Court proceedings have been fast-tracked, with a special NEET court urging counsel to be punctual, noting that 'every minute is important' in this case, as reported by The Times of India. The urgency reflects the gravity of the situation and the need for a swift resolution to restore public confidence in the examination system.

Legal and Administrative Fallout

The chargesheet names 13 individuals, including NTA experts, who have been accused of criminal conspiracy, cheating, and other offences under the Indian Penal Code. The CBI has also recommended sweeping reforms in the NTA's paper-setting process to prevent future breaches.

Authorities have not yet confirmed whether more arrests are expected, but the investigation is ongoing. The NTA, for its part, has faced intense scrutiny over its handling of the exam, with critics calling for a complete overhaul of the agency's security protocols.

As the case moves through the courts, the focus will be on ensuring that those responsible are held accountable and that such a breach never happens again. The NEET-UG exam is a gateway to medical education for lakhs of students, and its integrity is paramount.

Looking ahead, the CBI's findings are likely to prompt stricter regulations for all national-level exams, with the government expected to introduce new guidelines for the handling of question papers. The coming weeks will reveal whether these measures can restore faith in the system.
