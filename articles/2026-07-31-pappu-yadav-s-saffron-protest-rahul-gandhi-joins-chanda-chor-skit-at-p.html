Pappu Yadav's saffron protest: Rahul Gandhi joins 'chanda chor' skit at Parliament

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pappu Yadav dressed in saffron and enacted a 'chanda chor' (donation thief) skit near Parliament premises
  • Rahul Gandhi made a cameo appearance during the theatrical protest
  • Opposition MPs are protesting alleged theft of Ram Temple donations; VHP and seers have condemned the protest
  • SP pushes for action on Ayodhya donation scam while Congress focuses on police crackdown

New Delhi: Parliament premises turned into a stage on Friday as Rashtriya Janata Dal (RJD) MP Pappu Yadav, draped in saffron robes, enacted a dramatic skit alleging theft of Ram Temple donations. The performance, which drew a crowd of Opposition MPs, took an unexpected turn when Congress leader Rahul Gandhi walked in for a cameo, prompting chants and laughter.

The protest, titled 'Chanda chor, gaddi chhod' — a play on words meaning 'donation thief, leave the vehicle' — was aimed at the government over unverified reports of misappropriation of funds collected for the Ayodhya Ram Temple. Yadav's theatrics included mimicking the act of collecting donations and then surreptitiously pocketing them, a visual that was quickly shared on social media.

Rahul's surprise entry

While Yadav was in the middle of his act, Rahul Gandhi walked up and joined him, adding to the impromptu performance. The two leaders exchanged a few words, and Gandhi appeared to gesture towards the Parliament building, before stepping back. The video of the exchange has since gone viral, with BJP supporters and critics alike sharing clips.

Opposition MPs from several parties, including the Samajwadi Party (SP), Trinamool Congress (TMC), and Left parties, were present at the protest site. They demanded a probe into the alleged diversion of funds meant for the temple construction, which has been a flagship project of the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).

Opposition's dual strategy

The protest exposes a rift in the Opposition's approach. The Samajwadi Party has been pressing for the Ayodhya donation scam to be taken up as a major issue, while the Congress has been focusing on the police crackdown on protesters. The skit appears to be an attempt to bridge that gap with a mix of humour and seriousness.

Sources said SP chief Akhilesh Yadav had urged Opposition leaders to keep the spotlight on the donation issue, which he believes resonates with the public. Congress, on the other hand, has been highlighting what it calls the government's heavy-handed response to protests, including detentions of MPs.

Hindu groups cry foul

The saffron-robed protest did not sit well with right-wing organisations. The Vishva Hindu Parishad (VHP) and a body of seers condemned the demonstration, calling it an insult to the holy attire and the temple cause. Some have demanded the expulsion of the MPs involved, saying the protest was a deliberate attempt to demean the Ram Temple movement.

VHP leaders said the allegations of theft were baseless and accused the Opposition of creating a controversy without evidence. Seers from Ayodhya also issued statements, urging the government to take strict action against those who disrespected the saffron robe.

Parliament security under scrutiny

The fact that the skit was performed inside Parliament premises has raised questions about security and decorum. Officials have not yet confirmed whether any action will be taken against the MPs for violating parliamentary norms. The matter is likely to be raised in the House when it reconvenes.

Meanwhile, the BJP has dismissed the protest as a stunt, with party spokespersons questioning the Opposition's intent. They have pointed out that the temple trust has denied any wrongdoing and that the government has no record of a formal complaint about donation theft.

As the political slugfest continues, the video of Pappu Yadav's 'chanda chor' act remains the talking point. Whether it will translate into political mileage for the Opposition or become a rallying point for the BJP is yet to be seen. Parliamentary proceedings are expected to be turbulent in the coming days, with both sides digging in.
