Prime Minister Narendra Modi hosted a gala dinner for BRICS leaders in Delhi, showcasing India's culinary heritage with an all-vegetarian menu. The spread, designed to impress dignitaries including Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping, featured millet pulao, paneer lababdar, and shahtoot phirni. The event underscored food diplomacy, but also ignited a political row after Congress leader Rahul Gandhi pointed out that 80% of Indians are non-vegetarian. This article breaks down the menu, the political reactions, and the strategic messaging behind the feast.

Inside the BRICS Gala Dinner Menu

The dinner, held at a high-security venue in Delhi, was a carefully curated showcase of Indian cuisine. The menu leaned heavily on vegetarian dishes, reflecting both the host's dietary preferences and India's rich plant-based culinary traditions. Key items included millet pulao—a nod to the International Year of Millets—and paneer lababdar, a rich cottage cheese curry. The dessert, shahtoot phirni, used mulberries, a fruit with historical significance in Indian royal kitchens.

Here is a detailed breakdown of the menu and its significance:

The menu was entirely vegetarian, a decision that aligns with PM Modi's personal diet and the preferences of several other leaders. However, it excluded meat, seafood, and eggs, which are staples for many international guests.

Political Reactions and the '80% Non-Veg' Debate

Congress leader Rahul Gandhi took to social media to remind that 80% of Indians are non-vegetarian, questioning the representativeness of the menu. The BJP quickly retaliated, accusing Congress of doing politics over food and undermining national hospitality. This exchange highlights the ongoing cultural debate in India about dietary choices and their association with identity and politics.

The BJP's IT cell chief Amit Malviya tweeted, "Congress's obsession with non-veg food at a diplomatic event shows their petty mindset." Meanwhile, Rahul Gandhi's post read, "80% of Indians are non-vegetarian. Yet, the official dinner for world leaders is entirely veg. This is not about food, it's about representation." The controversy overshadowed the diplomatic messaging of the event.

Xi Jinping's Absence and Diplomatic Undercurrents

Chinese President Xi Jinping skipped the BRICS dinner, a notable absence that fueled speculation about bilateral tensions. While no official reason was given, analysts suggest it may relate to ongoing border disputes or Xi's scheduling. His absence meant the dinner table lacked a key player, but the menu remained a talking point.

Russian President Vladimir Putin attended and was seen enjoying the vegetarian fare, according to sources. The dinner provided an informal setting for leaders to discuss key issues like trade, security, and climate change, despite the political noise back home.

Millet Diplomacy and India's Soft Power Push

The inclusion of millet pulao was a strategic move to promote India's millet diplomacy. India has been championing millets as a climate-resilient crop, and this dinner served as a platform to showcase it to global leaders. The United Nations declared 2023 as the International Year of Millets, and India continues to push for its adoption worldwide.

By serving millet-based dishes, India subtly advocated for sustainable agriculture and food security, aligning with the BRICS agenda. The menu also included other indigenous ingredients like mulberries, highlighting India's biodiversity. This culinary diplomacy aims to position India as a leader in traditional and sustainable food systems.

Pros and Cons of the Vegetarian Menu

  • Pros:
    • Showcases India's rich vegetarian heritage and culinary diversity.
    • Aligns with PM Modi's personal diet and promotes millets globally.
    • Avoids potential religious or cultural sensitivities around meat.
    • Promotes sustainable and climate-friendly food choices.
  • Cons:
    • May not cater to the dietary preferences of all international guests.
    • Ignited political controversy over representation of non-vegetarian majority.
    • Could be seen as imposing a specific dietary choice on a diverse group.
    • Missed opportunity to showcase India's non-vegetarian cuisine to global leaders.

Conclusion: Food as a Diplomatic Tool

The BRICS dinner in Delhi demonstrated how food can be a powerful tool of diplomacy, conveying messages of culture, sustainability, and hospitality. While the all-vegetarian menu sparked a domestic political debate, it successfully highlighted India's millet mission and culinary traditions. As global leaders dined on paneer lababdar and shahtoot phirni, the event underscored India's soft power and its ability to blend tradition with modern diplomacy.
