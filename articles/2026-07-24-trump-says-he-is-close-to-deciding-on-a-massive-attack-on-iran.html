Trump says he is 'close to deciding' on a 'massive attack' on Iran

📅 2026-07-24 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says he is 'close to deciding' on a 'massive attack' on Iran.
  • Oil prices have surged past $100 a barrel amid the escalating tensions.
  • The US is surging forces toward the Middle East, giving Trump options to expand the conflict.
  • The rapid use of interceptors and precision-guided weapons is worrying the Trump administration.

US President Donald Trump has stated he is 'close to deciding' on launching a 'massive attack' on Iran, according to reports from multiple international news outlets. The remark, made public on Thursday, has sent shockwaves through global markets and diplomatic circles.

The statement comes as the United States surges military forces toward the Middle East, a move that the Wall Street Journal reports is giving the Trump administration options to expand the ongoing conflict with Iran. The rapid pace of operations has raised concerns within the administration about the consumption of critical munitions.

Oil markets react sharply

The Financial Times reports that the price of crude oil has hit $100 a barrel as traders price in the risk of a major escalation. The spike reflects fears that a large-scale US attack on Iran could disrupt oil supplies from the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy shipments.

Analysts say the $100 mark is a psychological threshold that could have knock-on effects on fuel prices and inflation worldwide. India, which imports a significant portion of its crude oil from the Middle East, is closely watching the situation.

US military buildup and concerns

The Wall Street Journal's reporting highlights that the US is not only surging forces but also rapidly expending precision-guided weapons and interceptor missiles in the current campaign. The administration is reportedly worried about the sustainability of such a high-tempo operation.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact size of the force buildup or the timeline for any potential 'massive attack.' The Pentagon has declined to comment on operational details, citing security concerns.

What a 'massive attack' could mean

While the specifics of Trump's plan remain unclear, the term 'massive attack' suggests a scale of bombing significantly larger than the current pattern of airstrikes. Such an operation could target Iran's nuclear facilities, military infrastructure, or leadership.

Al Jazeera's coverage notes that the President's remark comes amid a live war context, where the US is already engaged in military action against Iran. The new threat signals a potential escalation from a limited campaign to a full-scale offensive.

CBS News adds that the rapid use of interceptors and precision-guided weapons is a key worry. This suggests that US stockpiles of these expensive munitions are being depleted faster than anticipated, which could constrain future operations.

Diplomatic fallout

European allies have expressed alarm at the prospect of a massive attack. Diplomatic channels are reportedly active, with several nations urging restraint. Iran has not yet issued a formal response to Trump's latest statement.

The United Nations has called for de-escalation, but no emergency session has been scheduled yet. The situation remains fluid, with global attention fixed on Washington and Tehran.

For now, the world waits to see if Trump will follow through on his threat. The coming days are likely to determine whether the Middle East slides into a wider war or steps back from the brink.
