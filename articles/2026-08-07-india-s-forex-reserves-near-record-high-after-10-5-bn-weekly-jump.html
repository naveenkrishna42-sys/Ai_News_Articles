India's forex reserves near record high after $10.5 bn weekly jump

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Forex reserves rose $10.5 billion to $692.87 billion in the week ended July 31
  • This is the biggest weekly gain in six months, pushing reserves to a three-month high
  • Analysts attribute the surge to strong foreign inflows and RBI's market operations

India's foreign exchange reserves are climbing toward a record high, with the latest weekly data showing the biggest jump in six months. The kitty swelled by $10.5 billion to $692.87 billion for the week ended July 31, according to official data released on Friday.

The surge, which pushed reserves to a three-month peak, has caught the attention of analysts who see further upside in the coming weeks. The previous record high of $704.89 billion was touched in September 2024, and market watchers believe the current momentum could take reserves close to that level again.

What's driving the surge?

Analysts point to a combination of factors behind the sharp weekly rise. Foreign portfolio inflows into Indian equities and debt have been robust, aided by expectations of a US rate cut and a stable domestic macroeconomic outlook. Additionally, the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) intervention in the currency market—buying dollars to prevent excessive rupee appreciation—has added to the reserve pile.

Dealers said the central bank likely absorbed a significant portion of the dollar inflows, which also helped cushion the rupee against volatility. The RBI's market operations have been a key tool in managing the exchange rate while building a buffer against external shocks.

Reserves composition and comfort

The bulk of India's forex reserves is held in foreign currency assets, which rose by $9.1 billion during the week to $607.18 billion. Gold reserves, another major component, also increased by $1.2 billion to $68.4 billion, reflecting both price gains and possible central bank purchases.

Special Drawing Rights (SDRs) with the International Monetary Fund and the reserve tranche position saw marginal changes. The overall reserve level now covers about 11 months of projected imports, offering a comfortable cushion for the economy.

This buffer is critical for a country that relies heavily on imported energy and capital goods. A strong reserve position also reassures foreign investors and credit rating agencies, making it a key metric for macroeconomic stability.

Analyst outlook

Market experts expect the upward trend to continue in the near term. "The combination of strong portfolio flows, a favourable interest rate differential, and likely RBI intervention suggests reserves could test the $700 billion mark again," a senior analyst at a Mumbai-based brokerage said, though he declined to be named.

Some caution remains, however. A rebound in US Treasury yields or a sudden spike in crude oil prices could reverse capital flows, pressuring the rupee and forcing the RBI to sell dollars. But for now, the momentum appears positive.

The rupee itself has remained relatively stable, trading in a tight band against the dollar. The central bank's proactive management has helped limit volatility, even as global markets grapple with uncertainty over monetary policy trajectories.

What to watch next

Investors will now focus on the weekly reserve data due next Friday, which will indicate whether the build-up is sustained. Also on the radar are US inflation numbers and the Federal Reserve's policy signals, which could influence capital flows into emerging markets like India.

If inflows persist, India's reserves could soon notch a new all-time high, providing an additional layer of economic security. For now, the trend is firmly upward, and the central bank's strategy appears to be paying off.
