Google Play to Allow Third-Party App Stores From July 22

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google Play will allow third-party app stores from July 22 following a court order.
  • The move comes after Epic Games withdrew a settlement, prompting judicial intervention.
  • Developers can offer alternative stores within the Google Play ecosystem.
  • The change aims to address antitrust concerns in India's digital market.

Google Play to Host Rival App Stores Starting July 22

Google will allow third-party app stores on its Google Play platform starting July 22, a shift driven by a court order rather than voluntary policy change. The development follows the withdrawal of a settlement with Epic Games, which had challenged Google's app store practices in court.

The move means users in India will soon see alternative app marketplaces available for download directly from Google Play. This is a significant departure from Google's long-standing policy of keeping its storefront exclusive to apps distributed through its own billing and review systems.

Court Order, Not a Corporate Gift

Industry observers have been quick to note that this is not a goodwill gesture from Google. The company is complying with a judicial directive that emerged after Epic Games backed out of a proposed settlement. The original settlement had been seen as a way to avoid further litigation, but its collapse left the court to impose a remedy.

Legal experts point out that the order addresses concerns about Google's dominance in Android app distribution. The Competition Commission of India has also been investigating similar antitrust issues, making this a closely watched case in the tech world.

What Changes on July 22

From next week, developers who operate third-party app stores can apply to have their stores listed on Google Play. These stores will be able to offer their own app catalogues, potentially with different payment systems and review processes.

Users will still need to enable installation from unknown sources for these stores, but the listing on Google Play provides a discoverability channel that was previously unavailable. Google has stated that it will apply the same security and content policies to these third-party stores as it does to regular apps.

Impact on Developers and Users

For Indian developers, this could mean more options for distributing apps without being forced into Google's billing system, which charges a commission of 15-30%. Smaller developers have long complained that these fees eat into their margins, especially for in-app purchases.

Users may see new app stores offering regional or niche content that was previously hard to find. However, security experts caution that users should still exercise caution when downloading apps from unfamiliar storefronts, as Google's vetting process for third-party stores may differ from its own.

The Bigger Picture

The court order is part of a global trend where regulators are forcing tech giants to open up their ecosystems. The European Union's Digital Markets Act has already pushed Apple to allow alternative app stores on iPhones, and similar pressure is building in India.

Google has not indicated whether it will appeal the order. For now, the company is focused on implementing the changes by the July 22 deadline. The tech giant has said it will share more details about the technical requirements for third-party stores in the coming days.

What remains to be seen is how many alternative stores will actually appear on Google Play and whether they can attract enough users to challenge Google's own Play Store dominance. The next few weeks will provide the first real-world test of this court-mandated experiment in app store competition.
