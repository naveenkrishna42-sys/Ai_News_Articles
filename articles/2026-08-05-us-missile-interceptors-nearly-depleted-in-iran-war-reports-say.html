US Missile Interceptors Nearly Depleted in Iran War, Reports Say

📅 2026-08-05 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US has used 'virtually all' long-range precision missiles in Iran war, sources say
  • Nearly 80% of interceptors for a key missile defense system depleted
  • Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth rejects reports of dangerously low stockpiles

Multiple media reports suggest the United States has exhausted a significant portion of its missile interceptor stockpiles during the ongoing conflict with Iran, raising questions about the country's defensive readiness in the region.

Sources familiar with the matter told NDTV that Washington has used 'virtually all' of its long-range precision missiles since the war began. Reuters separately reported that nearly 80 percent of interceptors for a key missile defense system have been depleted, though the specific system was not named in the initial disclosures.

What the Reports Say

The reports point to the heavy reliance on terminal-phase interceptors such as THAAD (Terminal High Altitude Area Defense) and Patriot systems during sustained exchanges with Iranian ballistic missiles. These systems have been central to protecting US assets and allied territories in the Middle East since hostilities escalated.

Defense analysts note that interceptor production lines operate at limited capacity, and replenishing stocks could take months or even years. The exact number of interceptors expended remains classified, but the scale of depletion suggests a prolonged engagement with high-volume missile salvos.

Pentagon Pushback

US War Secretary Pete Hegseth has publicly rejected the media narrative, calling the reports 'not true.' In a statement, he insisted that American missile defense stockpiles remain at adequate levels for current operations and deterrence requirements.

Hegseth's denial, however, has not fully assuaged concerns among allies in the Gulf and in the Indo-Pacific, who rely on US security guarantees. Several partner nations have reportedly sought reassurances about the continuity of missile defense coverage in the region.

Strategic Implications

If the depletion figures are accurate, the US may face difficult choices in the coming months. Priorities could shift to defending key bases and population centers, while accepting higher risk for lower-value targets. The Pentagon may also accelerate purchases from manufacturers like Lockheed Martin and Raytheon, though surge production cannot happen overnight.

The situation underscores the intensity of the Iran war, which has seen some of the largest ballistic missile barrages since the 1991 Gulf War. US forces and their allies have relied heavily on interceptors to neutralize incoming threats, with varying degrees of success.

Officials have not yet confirmed the specific systems affected or the timeline for replenishment. The Pentagon has not released detailed expenditure data, citing operational security.

What to Watch

In the coming weeks, watch for any announcements regarding emergency defense procurement or adjustments to US force posture in the Middle East. Congress may also demand classified briefings on interceptor inventories, especially as defense budgets face renewed scrutiny.

The broader question remains whether the US industrial base can keep pace with the demands of modern missile warfare. For now, the gap between official assurances and media reports is likely to fuel further debate in Washington and allied capitals.
