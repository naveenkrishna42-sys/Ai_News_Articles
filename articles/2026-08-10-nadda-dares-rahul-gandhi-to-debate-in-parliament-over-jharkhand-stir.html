Nadda dares Rahul Gandhi to debate in Parliament over Jharkhand stir

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • JP Nadda dared Rahul Gandhi to participate in a Parliament discussion on the Jharkhand students' protest.
  • Nadda accused Rahul Gandhi of misleading people and deliberately disrupting Parliament.
  • BJP demanded withdrawal of support to Hemant Soren government if students' demands are not met.

NEW DELHI: BJP national president JP Nadda on Monday threw down the gauntlet at Congress leader Rahul Gandhi, daring him to come to Parliament for a discussion on the ongoing students' protest in Jharkhand, asserting that the opposition leader would be given a fitting reply.

Nadda's challenge comes amid escalating political tensions over the agitation by students in the state, with the BJP and Congress trading barbs both inside and outside the legislature.

Accusations of Misleading and Disruption

Addressing reporters, Nadda accused Rahul Gandhi of behaving irresponsibly and deliberately disrupting Parliament proceedings. He alleged that the Congress leader was misleading the public on the Jharkhand issue, particularly regarding events at Jantar Mantar in Delhi.

"No firing took place at Jantar Mantar," Nadda asserted, directly countering claims made by Gandhi and other opposition leaders. The BJP chief's remarks were aimed at what he termed the Congress's "double standards" on the students' protest.

BJP's Demand on Soren Government

The BJP has also turned its fire on the Hemant Soren-led government in Jharkhand. Party leaders demanded that the Congress withdraw its support to the Soren government if the students' demands were not met. This move is seen as an attempt to corner both the Congress and the ruling coalition in the state.

Nadda's dare to Gandhi is the latest salvo in a bitter war of words that has played out over the past week. The BJP has consistently targeted the opposition, accusing it of politicising the students' issue for electoral gains.

Parliamentary Proceedings Impacted

The ongoing standoff has also taken a toll on parliamentary work, with both houses facing disruptions. The BJP has blamed the opposition for the logjam, while Congress and other parties have countered by demanding a structured discussion on the Jharkhand situation.

Sources indicate that the government is ready for a debate but wants the opposition to adhere to parliamentary norms. The onus, they say, is on Gandhi and his allies to allow the House to function.

Observers note that the Jharkhand protest has become a flashpoint in national politics, with both major parties using it to mobilise support ahead of upcoming elections in the state.

As the impasse continues, all eyes are on Parliament's next session. Will Rahul Gandhi take up Nadda's challenge and engage in a full-fledged debate? The answer could determine whether the logjam breaks or deepens further.

For now, the BJP has made its position clear: it is ready for a fight, both on the streets and in the House.
