US Senate passes Russia sanctions bill with 100% tariff threat for India

📅 2026-08-08 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US Senate passes Russia sanctions bill targeting buyers of Russian oil, gas
  • India among five countries that could face 100% tariffs if they continue purchases
  • Bill now moves to House; India's energy imports under scrutiny
  • Potential impact on India-US trade and energy security

Sanctions Bill Clears Senate

The United States Senate has passed a sweeping Russia sanctions bill that includes a provision to impose 100% tariffs on India and four other countries if they continue purchasing Russian oil and gas. The move, reported by The Hindu and other outlets, signals a hardening of Washington's stance against nations that maintain energy ties with Moscow.

The bill, which passed on Friday, is designed to penalise countries that buy Russian energy, a key revenue source for the Kremlin. India, a major buyer of Russian crude, is now in the crosshairs of US legislators who argue that such purchases undermine the effectiveness of Western sanctions.

Who Are the Five Countries?

While the exact list has not been officially confirmed, reports indicate that India is among the five nations targeted. The others are believed to include China, Turkey, and possibly the UAE and Pakistan, though officials have not yet disclosed the full roster.

The proposed 100% tariff would apply to goods exported from these countries to the US, a punitive measure that could severely disrupt trade flows. For India, this poses a significant risk given that the US is one of its largest trading partners.

India's Russian Energy Lifeline

India has ramped up its purchases of Russian crude since the war in Ukraine began, taking advantage of discounted prices to manage its own energy costs. This has drawn repeated criticism from Washington, though New Delhi has defended its position, citing national energy security.

The bill's passage in the Senate is a legislative victory for hawkish lawmakers who have long pushed for stricter enforcement against Russian oil buyers. However, it is not yet law—the bill must still pass the House of Representatives and be signed by the President, and the tariff provision could face amendments or opposition.

What This Means for India-US Trade

If enacted, the 100% tariff would hit Indian exports across sectors, from textiles to pharmaceuticals, potentially costing billions of dollars. Indian officials are likely to engage in intense diplomatic efforts to carve out exemptions or negotiate a compromise before the bill reaches the President's desk.

Analysts note that the bill's language may allow waivers for countries that demonstrate a significant reduction in Russian energy imports. India has already begun diversifying its energy sources, but its reliance on Russian crude remains substantial, and meeting the bill's requirements could be challenging in the short term.

Next Steps

The bill now heads to the House, where its fate is uncertain. Some Republicans and Democrats have expressed concerns about alienating key allies like India, while others view the tariff threat as leverage to force a shift in energy policy.

For New Delhi, the coming weeks will be critical as it navigates this legislative pressure. Indian diplomats are expected to ramp up outreach in Washington, highlighting the country's strategic partnership with the US while seeking to protect its energy interests.

The outcome will be a test of whether the US can balance its sanctions goals with the need to maintain strong ties with India, a key counterweight to China in the Indo-Pacific. As the bill moves through the legislative process, all eyes will be on the White House's response and India's next move.
