Palliative Care Use Low Among Young Adults With Advanced Cancer

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Only 20% of young adults with advanced cancer receive concurrent palliative care.
  • Experts call for palliative care to be a standard part of oncology treatment.
  • Barriers include lack of awareness, limited access, and misconceptions about palliative care.

Alarming Gap in Care for Young Cancer Patients

Palliative care remains critically underused among young adults battling advanced cancer. A recent study reveals that only one in five patients in this age group receives this form of care as part of their treatment plan.

The findings, reported by Medscape and Oncology Nursing News, highlight a significant gap in how cancer care is delivered to patients aged 18 to 39. Despite guidelines recommending early integration of palliative care, most young adults miss out on its benefits.

What Palliative Care Offers

Palliative care focuses on relieving symptoms, managing pain, and improving quality of life. It is not the same as hospice care, which is for end-of-life situations.

Experts stress that concurrent palliative care—given alongside curative treatments—can help patients cope better with the physical and emotional toll of cancer. Dr. Arif Kamal, a leading voice in the field, has stated that palliative care must become a standard part of cancer care for all patients.

Why Young Adults Miss Out

The study points to several reasons behind the low uptake. Many young patients and their families associate palliative care with giving up hope, a misconception that doctors are trying to correct.

Limited access to specialised palliative care teams in community hospitals and cancer centres also plays a role. Additionally, busy oncologists may not always refer patients early, focusing instead on aggressive treatment protocols.

Call for Systemic Change

Oncology Nursing News and other outlets have amplified the call for integrating palliative care into standard oncology practice. The data suggests that without systemic changes, young adults will continue to suffer unnecessarily.

Dr. Kamal and other experts argue that insurance policies and hospital protocols must be updated to prioritise symptom management. Education campaigns aimed at both patients and healthcare providers are also needed to dispel myths around palliative care.

What Comes Next

The study adds weight to ongoing efforts by medical bodies to revise cancer care guidelines. Researchers are now pushing for larger trials to test models of early palliative care integration in younger populations.

For now, the message is clear: young adults with advanced cancer need more than just chemotherapy and radiation. They need a care team that addresses their full range of needs from the moment of diagnosis.
