Multiple Chronic Conditions: Why Diagnosis Often Takes Years

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Patients with multiple chronic conditions often wait years for a correct diagnosis due to fragmented healthcare.
  • Lack of coordination between specialists leads to overlapping or contradictory treatments.
  • Experts call for more integrated care models to speed up diagnosis and improve patient outcomes.

The Long Road to a Diagnosis

For millions of Indians living with multiple chronic conditions, getting the right diagnosis is not a matter of weeks or months. It can take years. Patients often bounce from one specialist to another, accumulating prescriptions but no clear answer.

Doctors say the problem is systemic. A patient with diabetes, arthritis, and hypertension, for instance, may see an endocrinologist, a rheumatologist, and a cardiologist separately. None of them talk to each other. The result is fragmented care that misses the bigger picture.

Why Specialists Don't Connect the Dots

Medical training in India largely remains siloed. Specialists are trained to look at one organ system at a time. When a patient presents with symptoms that cross multiple specialties, the connections are often missed.

Take the example of a patient with fatigue, joint pain, and digestive issues. A gastroenterologist might treat the gut. A rheumatologist might treat the joints. But no one asks if the root cause could be a single autoimmune disorder affecting multiple systems. It can take years before someone puts the pieces together.

The Burden on Patients

The delay in diagnosis is not just frustrating. It is expensive. Patients spend lakhs of rupees on repeated tests, hospital visits, and medications that treat symptoms rather than the underlying condition.

Mental health also takes a hit. Many patients report being told that their symptoms are "all in their head." The lack of a clear diagnosis can lead to anxiety, depression, and a loss of trust in the medical system.

What Needs to Change

Experts argue that India needs more integrated care models. Instead of sending a patient to five different specialists, a single team of doctors should work together. Electronic medical records that all specialists can access would help.

Some hospitals are already experimenting with multidisciplinary clinics. Patients with complex conditions see a team of doctors in one visit. Early results show faster diagnosis and better outcomes. But such clinics are still rare, especially outside major cities.

What Patients Can Do

Doctors advise patients to keep a detailed symptom diary. Note when symptoms appear, what makes them worse, and what tests have already been done. Bring all previous reports to every appointment.

It also helps to have a primary care doctor who coordinates care. A general physician who knows the full history can act as a central point, ensuring that specialists are not working in isolation.

For now, the system remains fragmented. But awareness is growing. Medical colleges are beginning to teach more about multi-morbidity. And patient advocacy groups are pushing for better coordination.

What to watch: Whether the government's Ayushman Bharat digital health mission can create a unified health ID system that lets doctors across India share patient records seamlessly. If it works, the years-long wait for a diagnosis could finally shorten.
