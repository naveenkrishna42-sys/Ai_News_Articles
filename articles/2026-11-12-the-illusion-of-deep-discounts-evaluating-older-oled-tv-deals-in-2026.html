The Illusion of Deep Discounts: Evaluating Older OLED TV Deals in 2026

Headlines touting significant discounts on high-end electronics are always compelling. The prospect of an LG OLED B6 65-inch TV receiving a 50% price cut immediately grabs attention. However, when a deal involves a model from 2016, like the LG OLED B6, being offered in 2026, a critical examination is not just warranted but essential. TIVRA News delves beyond the percentage to unmask the true value proposition for discerning buyers.

The Allure of Legacy OLED: Why Old Deals Can Deceive

The B6's Historical Context

Launched in 2016, the LG OLED B6 series represented a pinnacle of display technology at the time. It offered revolutionary perfect blacks, infinite contrast, and vibrant colours that set a new benchmark for home entertainment. As an early adopter of Dolby Vision HDR, the B6 was a premium television, commanding a high price point upon release. Its impact on the market was significant, cementing OLED's position as a superior display technology for cinematic experiences.

The March of Technology: What's Changed Since 2016

A decade in consumer electronics is an eternity. While the B6 was groundbreaking, subsequent generations of OLED technology have brought substantial improvements. Peak brightness, a common criticism of early OLEDs, has seen significant gains, leading to more impactful HDR presentations. Advanced image processors now offer superior upscaling, motion handling, and object-based tone mapping, enhancing overall picture quality. Burn-in mitigation technologies have also evolved considerably, offering greater peace of mind for users. Furthermore, modern TVs feature HDMI 2.1 ports, enabling crucial gaming features like Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) and Auto Low Latency Mode (ALLM) for consoles like the PS5 and Xbox Series X, alongside support for 4K at 120Hz – capabilities entirely absent in the B6.

Evaluating Real-World Value: Beyond the Discount Percentage

Critical Considerations for Older Panels

A 50% discount on a 2016 model's original retail price is largely irrelevant in 2026. The real market value of such a device, even new in box, would be drastically lower. Buyers must consider potential panel degradation, especially if the unit has been in storage or, worse, on display. Early OLEDs had a higher, albeit still low, propensity for burn-in compared to current models. The smart TV operating system (WebOS 3.0 on the B6) will be severely outdated, likely lacking support for newer apps or receiving critical security updates. Connectivity options are also limited, with no HDMI 2.1 or eARC support, impacting integration with modern sound systems and gaming consoles.

Benchmarking Against Current Standards

Comparing a B6 to a 2026 entry-level OLED (like LG's current A-series or even mid-range LED options) reveals significant disparities. A modern budget OLED will likely offer superior brightness, a more robust and updated smart platform, better processing for standard definition content, and essential gaming features. The perceived 'deal' on a B6 quickly diminishes when weighed against the performance and feature set of contemporary televisions available at similar, or even lower, actual price points.

TIVRA's Buying Advice: When to Consider a Past-Gen OLED

Smart Shopping in 2026: Identifying Genuine Value

For most consumers, purchasing a 2016 model in 2026 is generally not advisable. The technological gap is too wide, and the practical benefits are too few. However, for a very specific niche – perhaps a collector, or someone needing a secondary display for non-critical viewing in a dark room with absolutely no need for modern smart features, gaming, or advanced connectivity – such a deal *might* hold a sliver of appeal, provided the price is commensurately low (far below even a budget 2026 model). Crucially, any such purchase demands a full warranty, verifiable panel hours, and a clear understanding of its limitations.
