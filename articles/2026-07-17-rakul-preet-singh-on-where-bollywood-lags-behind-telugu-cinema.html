Rakul Preet Singh on where Bollywood lags behind Telugu cinema

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rakul Preet Singh says Bollywood lacks the raw energy and mass appeal of Telugu cinema.
  • She points to how Telugu films focus on larger-than-life heroes and strong emotional core.
  • The actress notes that Bollywood often plays safe, while Telugu industry takes bigger risks.

Rakul Preet Singh, who has worked extensively in both Bollywood and the Telugu film industry, has offered a candid assessment of where Hindi cinema is falling short. The actress, known for films like De De Pyaar De and Doctor G, did not hold back while comparing the two industries.

Bollywood lacks the raw energy of Telugu cinema

According to Singh, one of the biggest differences is the energy that Telugu films bring to the audience. She said that Bollywood often plays it safe, while the Telugu industry takes bigger risks with storytelling and presentation.

“Everyone is so energetic and larger than life in Telugu films. That connection with the audience is missing in Bollywood,” she remarked. She added that Telugu filmmakers understand the pulse of the masses better.

Emotional core and hero worship

Singh also pointed to the emotional core that drives Telugu cinema. She said Bollywood often gets caught up in urban narratives and forgets the raw, emotional connect that works in the heartland.

“In Telugu films, the hero is not just a character. He is an emotion. People celebrate him. Bollywood, in trying to be realistic, loses that drama,” she explained.

The actress, who debuted in Bollywood with Yaariyan in 2014, has seen both industries evolve. She believes Telugu cinema has mastered the art of balancing spectacle with sentiment.

Risk-taking vs playing safe

She noted that Bollywood’s reluctance to experiment has hurt its growth. Telugu filmmakers, on the other hand, are willing to back unconventional stories and big-budget spectacles.

“Bollywood is still trying to figure out what works. The Telugu industry knows its audience and gives them exactly what they want — and sometimes more,” Singh said.

Her comments come at a time when several Bollywood productions have underperformed at the box office, while Telugu films like RRR and Baahubali have found pan-India success.

What Bollywood can learn

Singh did not just criticise. She also offered a way forward. She said Bollywood needs to stop imitating Hollywood and instead focus on what Indian audiences truly enjoy — drama, music, and strong emotional arcs.

“There is no shame in being commercial. Telugu films celebrate that. Bollywood needs to stop being apologetic about entertainment,” she added.

The actress, who is currently shooting for multiple projects, said she hopes both industries learn from each other. She believes healthy competition will only make Indian cinema stronger.

Rakul Preet Singh’s comments have sparked a debate online, with many agreeing that Bollywood could take a page out of the Telugu industry’s book. As cross-industry collaborations grow, her perspective highlights a shift in how Indian cinema is being re-evaluated.

What remains to be seen is whether Bollywood producers and directors will heed this advice and adapt their storytelling to match the energy of their southern counterparts.
