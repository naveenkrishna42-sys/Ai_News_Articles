West Asia Crisis: Sensex, Nifty Fall 2% as Brent Crude Tops $100

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty fell 2% during the week ending Friday.
  • Brent crude crossed the $100/barrel mark amid escalating West Asia tensions.
  • Investors turned risk-averse, leading to broad-based selling in equities.

Markets Bleed Red as Geopolitical Fears Intensify

Indian equity benchmarks Sensex and Nifty ended the week with sharp losses, each falling 2% as the West Asia crisis deepened. The selloff was triggered by a surge in global crude prices, with Brent hitting the psychologically significant $100/barrel mark.

Traders said the mood turned cautious after reports of fresh hostilities in the region. The rise in oil prices stoked fears of higher import costs and inflation, which could force central banks to keep interest rates elevated.

Brent at $100: A Pain Point for India

Brent crude crossing $100 is a major worry for India, which imports over 80% of its oil needs. Every dollar rise in crude adds to the country's import bill and pressures the rupee.

Auto, aviation, and FMCG stocks were among the worst hit this week as analysts cut earnings estimates for these sectors. Oil marketing companies also saw volatility, though some gains were logged on expectations of higher refining margins.

Broader Market Under Pressure

The selloff was not limited to blue chips. Midcap and smallcap indices also lost ground, reflecting a broad risk-off sentiment. Foreign portfolio investors were net sellers for most of the week, adding to the pressure.

Domestic institutional buyers stepped in on some days, but their buying was not enough to reverse the trend. Market participants are now watching for any diplomatic moves that could de-escalate tensions.

The volatility index, India VIX, climbed, signaling heightened anxiety among traders. Options data suggested that traders are hedging against further downside in the near term.

What Lies Ahead

All eyes will be on crude prices and the West Asia situation next week. If Brent stays above $100, the market could test lower support levels. The upcoming monthly auto sales data and global cues will also dictate near-term direction.
