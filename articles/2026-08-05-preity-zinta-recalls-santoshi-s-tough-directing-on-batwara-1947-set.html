Preity Zinta Recalls Santoshi's Tough Directing on Batwara 1947 Set

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Preity Zinta recalled being 'tortured' by director Rajkumar Santoshi on the set of Batwara 1947.
  • She said she was physically shaking while filming certain intense scenes.
  • Zinta also spoke about her children being culturally Indian and her return to films after eight years.

Santoshi's Intense Directing Style

Bollywood actress Preity Zinta has opened up about her experience working with director Rajkumar Santoshi on the set of the upcoming film Batwara 1947. In a recent interview, Zinta revealed that Santoshi's demanding approach left her physically shaken during the shoot. 'I was shaking,' she said, describing the intensity of the scenes.

The actress, known for her bubbly on-screen persona, admitted that the director pushed her to her emotional limits. Santoshi, who has a reputation for extracting powerful performances from actors, apparently employed a method that Zinta described as 'torture'—though she clarified it was in a professional sense, aimed at getting the best out of her.

A Harrowing Portrayal of Partition

Batwara 1947, which deals with the traumatic events surrounding India's Partition, required Zinta to tap into deep reservoirs of pain and anguish. The actress said that reading Partition stories for her role was heartbreaking. The film's subject matter, combined with Santoshi's intense direction, created a challenging working environment.

Zinta's candid revelation gives fans a rare glimpse into the actor-director dynamic on set. Despite the difficulty, she appeared to hold no grudges, suggesting that the end result justified the means. The film, which has been in production for some time, is expected to be a significant release given its historical subject.

Motherhood and Cultural Identity

Away from the film set, Zinta also spoke warmly about her personal life. The actress, who is mother to twins via surrogacy, said her children are 'half-Indian and half-American, but culturally one hundred percent Indian.' She expressed gratitude for having a 'cool husband' who supports her professional commitments, including being away for two months at a time for work.

Zinta described motherhood as her 'greatest milestone,' a statement that reflects her current priorities. The actress has been balancing her family life in the United States with her return to Indian cinema after a gap of eight years. Her comeback has been much anticipated by her fan base.

Return to the Big Screen

Batwara 1947 marks Zinta's return to films after a long hiatus. The project brings together a seasoned actress and a director known for his powerful storytelling. Zinta's revelations about the tough shoot have only heightened curiosity about the final product.

As she steps back into the limelight, Zinta seems to be approaching her career with a new perspective, shaped by her experiences as a mother and her time away from the industry. Her comments suggest that while the work was demanding, she remains committed to her craft.

The coming months will show how audiences receive Batwara 1947 and whether Zinta's challenging experience translates into a memorable performance on screen.
