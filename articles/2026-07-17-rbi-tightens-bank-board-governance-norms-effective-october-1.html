RBI tightens bank board governance norms, effective October 1

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI amends norms for bank board composition and independence
  • New rules on director tenure, qualifications, and risk committee structure
  • Regulations effective from October 1, 2026
  • Move aimed at enhancing governance and risk management in banks

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has amended its guidelines for bank board governance, introducing stricter norms on director tenure, independence, and committee composition. The revised rules will come into effect from October 1, 2026, as per a notification issued by the central bank on Tuesday.

Stricter tenure caps and independence rules

The new framework limits the maximum tenure for non-executive directors on bank boards to eight consecutive years. After this period, a cooling-off of at least three years will be mandatory before reappointment.

The RBI has also tightened the definition of 'independent director'. Banks must now ensure that at least one-third of the board comprises independent directors. The central bank has clarified that directors who have served for more than eight years will not be considered independent.

Enhanced risk management committees

All banks are required to constitute a separate risk management committee of the board. This committee must have a minimum of three members, with a majority being independent directors. The chairperson of this committee must be an independent director.

The committee's mandate includes overseeing the bank's risk appetite framework, reviewing risk management policies, and monitoring compliance with regulatory norms. Quarterly meetings are now mandatory for the committee.

Qualifications and fit-and-proper criteria

The amended norms introduce more detailed fit-and-proper criteria for directors. Banks must now verify the educational qualifications, professional experience, and integrity of proposed directors before their appointment. The RBI has listed disqualifying factors such as conviction for offences involving moral turpitude, insolvency, or being declared of unsound mind.

Banks must also ensure that at least 50 percent of the board members have experience in banking, finance, or related fields. The central bank has warned that non-compliance could lead to penalties or restrictions on business activities.

Transition period and grandfathering

Existing directors who have already crossed the eight-year tenure limit will not be automatically removed. The RBI has allowed a transition period until March 31, 2027, for banks to align their boards with the new norms. During this period, banks must submit a board-approved plan to the RBI detailing how they intend to comply.

The central bank has also clarified that the new rules will apply to all commercial banks, including small finance banks and local area banks. However, foreign banks operating through branches in India are not covered under the revised framework.

Industry reaction and implications

The move is seen as a response to governance lapses observed in some banks over recent years. The RBI has been pushing for stronger oversight, especially after instances of fraud and mismanagement surfaced in the sector.

Bankers say the new rules could lead to a churn on boards, particularly for lenders that have long-serving directors. Some smaller banks may find it challenging to source qualified independent directors in time. The industry will watch how the RBI enforces compliance, especially for public sector banks, which have historically had less board independence.

The new norms take effect from October 1. Banks have until then to review their board composition and make necessary appointments.
