Neeraj Chopra, PT Usha Receive CWG Flag as Ahmedabad Prepares for 2030

Key points
  • Neeraj Chopra and PT Usha received the Commonwealth Games flag, signalling the official handover to Ahmedabad for 2030.
  • The event marks the start of India's decade-long preparation, with the Games seen as a potential Olympic audition.
  • Indian cultural grandeur was showcased as the baton's journey concluded, with boxing star Jasimine named flag-bearer for the closing ceremony.

Flag Handover Marks New Chapter

The Commonwealth Games flag has officially changed hands, with Neeraj Chopra and PT Usha receiving it in a ceremony that sets the clock ticking for Ahmedabad 2030. The moment signals the start of India's decade-long journey to host the multi-sport event, a task that officials and athletes alike are approaching with a mix of pride and pragmatism.

The handover took place as the current edition of the Games wrapped up, bringing the sporting focus squarely onto Indian soil. For Chopra, the Olympic gold medallist in javelin, and Usha, the legendary sprinter who now leads Indian athletics administration, the flag is more than a ceremonial object — it is a promise of scale and expectation.

More Than a Games: An Olympic Audition

For many observers, Ahmedabad 2030 is not merely about the Commonwealth Games. The event is being viewed as a critical audition for India's broader ambitions, including a potential Olympic bid. The way India organises the Games, from logistics to athlete welfare, will likely be scrutinised by international sporting bodies far beyond the Commonwealth federation.

Ahmedabad, with its modern stadium infrastructure and growing reputation as a host city, is already being positioned as a showcase. The Narendra Modi Stadium, one of the world's largest cricket arenas, provides a template for the kind of scale the city can manage. But the Commonwealth Games will demand more than one venue — it will test transport, accommodation, and the ability to host thousands of athletes and officials from over 70 nations.

Cultural Grandeur Takes Centre Stage

The handover ceremony was not just about sport. Indian cultural grandeur was on full display as the Commonwealth baton concluded its journey, with performances that highlighted the country's regional diversity and artistic heritage. The presentation leaned heavily on the theme of 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam' — the Sanskrit phrase meaning 'the world is one family' — which has been adopted as a guiding philosophy for Ahmedabad's invitation to the Commonwealth community.

This cultural framing is deliberate. India is seeking to position the Games not just as a sporting event, but as a diplomatic and soft-power exercise. The choice of Ahmedabad, a city with deep historical roots and a rapidly modernising face, reflects that dual ambition.

Athletes in the Spotlight

Amid the ceremony and the political signalling, the athletes remained the emotional core. Boxing star Jasimine has been named as one of India's flag-bearers for the closing ceremony, a recognition of her performances and the rising profile of women's boxing in the country. Her selection underscores the broader narrative of Indian sport — a mix of established stars and emerging talent ready to carry the nation's hopes.

For Chopra and Usha, the flag handover also carries a personal weight. Both have been central figures in Indian athletics — one as a current champion, the other as a pioneer who has transitioned into administration. Their presence at the ceremony links the country's sporting past with its future aspirations.

What to Watch

With the flag now in Indian hands, the focus shifts to planning and execution. Over the coming years, expect announcements on venue development, athlete preparation programmes, and the logistical framework for hosting thousands of visitors. The real test will be whether India can translate its ambition into an event that matches global standards — and whether the Commonwealth Games can serve as a springboard for an even bigger Olympic dream.
