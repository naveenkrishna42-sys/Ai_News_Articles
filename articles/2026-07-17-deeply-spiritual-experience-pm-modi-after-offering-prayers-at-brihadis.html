Deeply spiritual experience: PM Modi after offering prayers at Brihadisvara Temple in Tamil Nadu

PM Modi Visits Historic Brihadisvara Temple in Thanjavur

Prime Minister Narendra Modi offered prayers at the Brihadisvara Temple in Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, on Sunday, describing the visit as a deeply spiritual experience. The temple, built by the Chola emperor Rajaraja I in the 11th century, is a UNESCO World Heritage site and a masterpiece of Dravidian architecture.

Modi spent time at the temple complex, which is dedicated to Lord Shiva. He was seen performing rituals and interacting with priests. The visit comes amid the government's focus on promoting cultural and religious tourism.

A Monument of Chola Grandeur

The Brihadisvara Temple, also known as Peruvudaiyar Kovil, is one of the largest temples in India. Its towering vimana (temple tower) rises to 66 metres, making it a marvel of engineering for its time. The temple's walls are adorned with inscriptions and intricate sculptures that narrate stories from Hindu mythology.

Officials said the Prime Minister's visit was part of his ongoing efforts to highlight India's rich heritage. The temple remains a major pilgrimage site, drawing thousands of devotees daily.

Modi's Spiritual Reflections

In a post on social media, Modi called the experience deeply moving. He noted the temple's historical significance and its role in preserving India's spiritual traditions. The visit also included a brief tour of the temple's inner sanctum and the massive Nandi (bull) statue that guards the entrance.

Local residents gathered outside the temple to catch a glimpse of the Prime Minister. Security was tight, with additional forces deployed in the area. No public address was made during the visit.

Focus on Cultural Heritage

The Brihadisvara Temple is part of the Great Living Chola Temples, a UNESCO World Heritage site that includes the Gangaikonda Cholapuram and Airavatesvara temples. The government has been investing in the conservation of these monuments under various heritage schemes.

Modi's visit is seen as a push to boost religious tourism in Tamil Nadu, a state known for its temple architecture. The temple's annual festivals, including the Mahashivaratri celebrations, attract pilgrims from across the country.

What comes next: The Prime Minister's schedule for the remainder of the day was not immediately disclosed, but his visit is expected to reinforce the cultural ties between the central government and Tamil Nadu's heritage sites.
