No Plan to Extend Monsoon Session, Says Kiren Rijiju Amid Deadlock

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kiren Rijiju says there is no proposal to extend the monsoon session
  • Parliament remains deadlocked over delimitation and FCRA bills
  • Rijiju had met Rahul Gandhi to break the logjam, but no breakthrough yet

Union Minister Kiren Rijiju on Wednesday ruled out any extension of the ongoing monsoon session of Parliament, quashing speculation about a possible special sitting in August. His statement comes as the house remains gripped by a bitter deadlock over the delimitation exercise and the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, with little sign of a thaw.

Speaking to reporters, Rijiju said the government has no proposal before it to stretch the session beyond its scheduled wrap-up. The minister's remarks effectively close the door on hopes that more time could be allotted to break the impasse, which has stalled legislative business for days.

Delimitation Bill at the Heart of the Logjam

The standoff centres on the proposed delimitation bill, which seeks to redraw parliamentary constituencies based on population data. The Opposition, led by the Congress, has demanded that the bill be sent to a select committee for scrutiny, arguing that it could disadvantage southern states whose population growth has been slower.

Sources said Rahul Gandhi, the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, was firm in his refusal to allow the bill's smooth passage unless the government agrees to broader consultations. Rijiju's meeting with Gandhi earlier in the day was part of a last-ditch effort to find common ground, but sources indicate no breakthrough was achieved.

Amit Shah May Speak on FCRA Bill

Alongside delimitation, the FCRA amendment bill has emerged as another flashpoint. The proposed changes aim to tighten oversight of foreign funding for non-governmental organisations, a move the government says is necessary for transparency. Critics, however, view it as a curb on civil society.

Reports suggest Union Home Minister Amit Shah may address the house on the FCRA bill to clarify the government's position. Whether that will be enough to pacify the Opposition remains uncertain, as parliamentary proceedings have been repeatedly adjourned amid noisy protests.

Monsoon Session Sinks Deeper Into Deadlock

The current session, which began last month, has seen little productive work. Several key bills remain pending, and the frequent disruptions have drawn criticism from various quarters. Political observers note that the logjam reflects a broader pattern of strained ties between the ruling dispensation and the Opposition, particularly in the run-up to elections in several states.

Rijiju's outright rejection of an extension means the government must either find a way to pass its agenda in the remaining days or defer contentious items to the winter session. The latter option, while pragmatic, would be seen as a setback for the government's legislative push.

What Happens Next

With the session clock ticking, all eyes are now on the next round of talks between the government and Opposition leaders. If the deadlock persists, the delimitation and FCRA bills could be shelved for now, leaving both sides to regroup. The coming days will reveal whether a compromise is possible or if the monsoon session ends as one of the least productive in recent memory.
