World Breastfeeding Week concludes at Stanley Medical College Hospital

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • World Breastfeeding Week concluded at Stanley Medical College Hospital in Chennai
  • Events focused on benefits of early breastfeeding initiation and exclusive breastfeeding for six months
  • Hospital staff and mothers participated in awareness sessions and counselling

World Breastfeeding Week drew to a close at Stanley Medical College Hospital in Chennai on Friday, capping a week of activities aimed at reinforcing the importance of breastfeeding for infant health. The hospital, one of the city's oldest and largest public medical institutions, organised a series of programmes that saw active participation from expectant mothers, new parents, and healthcare staff.

A week of awareness and support

The celebrations, held from August 1 to August 7, were centred on spreading awareness about early initiation of breastfeeding within the first hour of birth and exclusive breastfeeding for the first six months of a child's life. Medical officers, paediatricians, and nursing staff conducted interactive sessions, addressing common concerns and dispelling myths that often deter mothers from breastfeeding.

According to hospital sources, the programmes included practical demonstrations on correct positioning and latching techniques. Counsellors were on hand to guide mothers, particularly those facing difficulties such as low milk supply or nipple pain. The initiative also highlighted the role of family support in encouraging sustained breastfeeding practices.

Engaging the community

Beyond the hospital wards, the week's events reached out to the surrounding community. Awareness rallies and poster exhibitions were held within the campus, drawing attention to the nutritional and immunological benefits of breast milk. The hospital's staff emphasised that breast milk provides essential antibodies that protect infants from common childhood illnesses such as diarrhoea and pneumonia.

Mothers who participated shared their experiences, with several noting that the sessions empowered them with the confidence to breastfeed successfully. One new mother, who attended a counselling workshop, said she had initially struggled but found the step-by-step guidance invaluable. The hospital also distributed informational pamphlets in Tamil and English to ensure wider reach.

Commitment beyond the week

Doctors at Stanley Medical College Hospital reiterated that breastfeeding support should not be confined to a single week. They pointed to the hospital's ongoing lactation counselling services, available round the clock, and its commitment to the Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative, which promotes skin-to-skin contact and rooming-in. These practices, they said, are integral to establishing a strong breastfeeding routine.

The concluding ceremony on Friday brought together staff and participants to reflect on the week's achievements. Officials acknowledged that while awareness has grown, challenges remain, particularly in urban areas where formula feeding is often perceived as more convenient. Sustained community engagement and policy support, they noted, are essential to normalising breastfeeding as a public health priority.

What next

As the week's activities wind down, the hospital plans to continue its year-round breastfeeding education programmes, with quarterly follow-up sessions for new mothers. Officials also indicated that they would evaluate the response to this year's events to shape an even more robust outreach plan for the next World Breastfeeding Week.
