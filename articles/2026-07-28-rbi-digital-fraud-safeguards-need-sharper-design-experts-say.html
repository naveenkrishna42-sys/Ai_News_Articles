RBI digital fraud safeguards need sharper design, experts say

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has introduced new measures to combat digital payment fraud.
  • Experts say some safeguards are too broad and could inconvenience genuine users.
  • Calls for sharper design and better calibration of the rules.

RBI's anti-fraud push: welcome but not perfect

The Reserve Bank of India's latest measures to curb digital payment fraud have drawn praise from the banking sector and consumer groups. The central bank's intent is clear: tighten the screws on fraudsters who exploit digital channels. But some of the new safeguards, as currently designed, risk creating friction for legitimate users.

Industry experts point out that while the RBI's direction is sound, the implementation details need more thought. Rules that are too rigid can end up slowing down genuine transactions or triggering false alarms. The challenge is to hit the sweet spot between security and ease of use.

What the new rules target

The RBI has mandated stricter authentication norms for high-value transactions and introduced additional checks for merchant payments. Banks are now required to implement real-time fraud monitoring systems and report suspicious activities faster. The idea is to catch fraud before money leaves the victim's account.

However, sources in the banking industry say that some of these checks are being applied in a blanket manner. For example, the requirement for additional factor authentication on all transactions above a certain threshold may not be practical for recurring payments or trusted merchant relationships. This could lead to higher drop-off rates and customer frustration.

Where the design falls short

Experts argue that the RBI's safeguards need to be more nuanced. A one-size-fits-all approach does not work in a country as diverse as India, where digital payment habits range from UPI-based street vendors to high-value corporate transfers. The rules should differentiate between risk profiles.

Another point of concern is the timeline for implementing these measures. Banks say they need more time to upgrade their systems and train staff. Rushing the rollout could lead to technical glitches and compliance gaps. Several industry bodies have already submitted representations to the RBI seeking clarifications and phased implementation.

  • Stricter authentication rules may disrupt recurring payments.
  • Blanket thresholds ignore varied user risk profiles.
  • Banks seek more time to upgrade systems.

What needs to change

The RBI should consider a risk-based framework. Low-risk transactions, such as small-value UPI payments or payments to verified merchants, could be exempt from some of the new checks. High-risk transactions, such as cross-border payments or first-time transfers, could face more scrutiny.

Transparency is another area that needs work. Customers should be clearly informed when a transaction is flagged or delayed. Currently, many users are left in the dark when a payment fails due to a security check, leading to confusion and repeated attempts. A simple SMS or app notification explaining the reason would go a long way.

The road ahead

The RBI has indicated it is open to feedback and may issue revised guidelines soon. Banks and fintech firms are expected to present data on how the current rules impact user experience. The central bank's priority is safety, but it cannot ignore usability. The next few months will show whether the regulator can strike the right balance.
