Trump's new student visa rules: fixed stays and shorter grace periods

📅 2026-07-18 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Foreign students will be subject to fixed four-year stays instead of the previous duration-of-status policy.
  • The grace period for students after program completion will be shortened, reducing time to seek employment or transfer.
  • The Foreign Press Association has slammed the tighter visa rules for journalists as a threat to press freedom.

New visa rules target students, journalists

The Trump administration has unveiled a fresh set of visa regulations that will significantly alter the landscape for foreign students, journalists, and exchange visitors in the United States. The changes, which mark a sharp departure from earlier policies, impose fixed four-year stays for student visa holders and reduce the post-completion grace period. The rules have drawn sharp reactions from press freedom groups and are expected to affect lakhs of Indian students and media professionals.

Under the new framework, student visa holders will no longer enjoy the earlier flexibility of staying for the duration of their academic program. Instead, they will be limited to a fixed four-year term, regardless of whether their course requires longer. The grace period after graduation — time typically used to find employment or switch to another visa category — has also been shortened, though the exact new duration has not been confirmed by officials.

Press freedom groups cry foul

The Foreign Press Association (FPA) has strongly criticised the move to tighten visa rules for journalists, calling it a direct threat to press freedom. In a statement, the FPA said the new rules could make it harder for international journalists to report independently from the United States. The association warned that the changes may deter foreign media professionals from covering American affairs or lead to self-censorship.

The new regulations require journalists to obtain specific endorsements and may limit their ability to travel and report across the country. The FPA noted that such restrictions are unprecedented among democratic nations and could set a worrying precedent. The organisation has urged the administration to reconsider the provisions that target media workers.

India to engage with US on visa issues

The Indian government has indicated that it will take up the matter with US authorities. Officials from the Ministry of External Affairs said that New Delhi plans to engage with Washington over immigration and visa policies that affect Indian nationals. India is one of the largest sources of international students in the US, with over 2.5 lakh students enrolled in American universities in recent years.

The tighter rules could have a substantial impact on this demographic, many of whom rely on the grace period to secure Optional Practical Training (OPT) or H-1B visas. The reduction in the grace period may leave many with limited time to transition into the workforce or return home.

What the changes mean for students

For students, the shift from duration-of-status to a fixed four-year stay means that those enrolled in longer programs — such as certain PhD or research courses — will need to apply for extensions. This adds bureaucratic hurdles and uncertainty. The shortened grace period also puts pressure on graduates to quickly find jobs or leave the country, reducing their flexibility.

Experts say the changes could make the US a less attractive destination for higher education. Countries like Canada and Australia have been rolling out more welcoming policies for international students, and the new US rules may accelerate a shift in student mobility patterns. Indian students, in particular, may now weigh these factors more heavily when choosing a study destination.

The administration has defended the rules as necessary to prevent visa overstays and ensure compliance with immigration laws. Critics, however, argue that the measures are overly restrictive and fail to account for the contributions of foreign students and journalists to the American economy and public discourse.

As the new rules take effect, affected groups are expected to challenge them in court or through diplomatic channels. The coming weeks will show whether the administration is open to modifications or if the tightened regime will remain in place.
