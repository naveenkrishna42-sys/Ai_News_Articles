NC, PDP protests mark 7 years of Article 370 abrogation in J&K

📅 2026-08-05 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • NC and PDP held widespread street protests in J&K marking 7 years since Article 370 was scrapped.
  • PM Modi and Rajnath Singh highlighted development and integration in the region.
  • Opposition leaders reiterated demand for restoration of J&K's statehood.
  • A senior opposition figure warned that statehood could be delayed indefinitely if tied to BJP's electoral fortunes.

Protests mark the day

On Wednesday, the seventh anniversary of the abrogation of Article 370, streets across Jammu and Kashmir witnessed widespread protests organised by the National Conference (NC) and the People's Democratic Party (PDP). The demonstrations were part of a coordinated effort by the two mainstream political outfits to keep the issue alive and press their demand for restoration of the region's special status and statehood.

Party workers and supporters gathered at several locations, holding placards and raising slogans. The protests were largely peaceful, though security forces were deployed in strength in sensitive areas as a precautionary measure. No untoward incident has been reported so far.

The anniversary has become an annual flashpoint in the region, with political parties using the date to reiterate their stance. This year, the focus was as much on the past as on the future — specifically, the question of when and how statehood will be restored.

Government counters with development narrative

While opposition parties marked the day with protests, the central government used the occasion to underscore what it calls a transformation of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh. Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Union Home Minister Rajnath Singh both issued statements hailing the changes since August 5, 2019, when the special status was scrapped and the state was bifurcated into two Union Territories.

Their messages highlighted infrastructure projects, industrial investment, and improved law-and-order situation. The government has consistently argued that the abrogation paved the way for development that was previously held back by special provisions.

Official data and announcements in recent years have pointed to increased tourist footfall, new medical colleges, and better connectivity. However, opposition leaders have countered that such development cannot substitute for political rights and democratic representation.

Opposition's persistent demand

The demand for restoration of statehood has been a recurring theme in J&K politics since 2019. The Supreme Court, in its December 2023 verdict, upheld the abrogation but also directed that statehood be restored at the earliest. That directive has not yet been implemented, and the issue has become a rallying point for the opposition.

Leaders from the NC and PDP have repeatedly said that Union Territory status is temporary and that full statehood is non-negotiable. They argue that without statehood, the region's legislative powers remain curtailed, undermining democratic governance.

Political observers note that the central government has not given a clear timeline for restoration, despite the court's observation. This ambiguity has fueled frustration among opposition ranks.

'Could wait hundreds of years'

A particularly sharp remark came from a senior opposition figure during the day's events. Responding to suggestions that statehood might be restored if the BJP returns to power, the leader said that if such restoration depends on the BJP coming to power, the wait could extend to "hundreds of years."

The comment, widely quoted on news channels, underscores the deep distrust between the opposition and the central government on this matter. It also reflects the political chasm that remains seven years after the historic decision.

The opposition has been pushing for a resolution in Parliament, but repeated attempts have not yielded any concrete assurance from the government.

What lies ahead

The coming months are likely to see continued pressure from the opposition, especially with assembly elections in J&K expected in the near future. The restoration of statehood is expected to be a central campaign issue.

For now, the anniversary has served as a reminder that while the legal framework has changed, the political debate over J&K's status remains far from settled. All eyes will now be on the Election Commission and the central government to see if any concrete steps follow the Supreme Court's directive.
