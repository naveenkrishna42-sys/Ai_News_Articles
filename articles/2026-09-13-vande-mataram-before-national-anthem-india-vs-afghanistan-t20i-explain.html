Vande Mataram Before National Anthem: A Historic First in Indian Cricket

The first T20I between India and Afghanistan in Delhi on September 13, 2026, became a milestone in Indian sports history. For the first time at an international cricket match, the national song 'Vande Mataram' was played before the national anthem, 'Jana Gana Mana'. The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) implemented this change following a directive from the central government, aiming to standardise pre-match ceremonies across all international fixtures.

The decision has drawn widespread attention, with many questioning the protocol and its implications for future matches. While the national anthem is enshrined in the Constitution and protected by law, 'Vande Mataram' holds the status of national song, historically significant in India's freedom struggle. The BCCI's move is seen as an attempt to honour both, but the sequencing has raised eyebrows.

Why the Change? Government Directive and BCCI's Role

The shift did not happen overnight. According to sources within the BCCI, the central government issued a recommendation to include 'Vande Mataram' in pre-match ceremonies to promote national unity and cultural heritage. The BCCI, which organises all major domestic and international cricket in India, decided to implement it starting with the Afghanistan series. The board has not yet issued a formal statement, but officials indicate that the practice will continue for all future home matches.

Critics argue that the national anthem should remain the sole pre-match ritual, as it is a symbol of the nation's sovereignty. Supporters, however, view the inclusion of 'Vande Mataram' as a tribute to India's diverse cultural fabric and a nod to its revolutionary history. The debate reflects broader conversations about patriotism and symbolism in public life.

What This Means for Fans and Future Matches

For fans attending matches at venues like the Arun Jaitley Stadium in Delhi, the experience now includes two patriotic songs back-to-back. The BCCI has not clarified whether 'Vande Mataram' will be played at all international matches or only select ones. However, given the government's push, it is likely to become a regular feature. This could also extend to other sports, as the directive may apply to all national events.

From a commercial standpoint, the change adds a few minutes to the pre-match ceremony, but broadcasters have adapted seamlessly. The move could also influence merchandise and fan engagement, with 'Vande Mataram' gaining renewed prominence.

Expert Analysis: Protocol, Patriotism, and Practicality

Constitutional experts point out that while 'Vande Mataram' is the national song, it does not have the same legal protection as the national anthem. The anthem must be played before any event, but the song's inclusion is discretionary. The BCCI's decision is thus a policy choice, not a legal requirement. From a practical standpoint, the sequencing may need standardisation to avoid confusion at future events.

For cricket fans, the change is a talking point, but the focus remains on the game. India won the match convincingly, with standout performances from both sides. The pre-match ceremony, however, will be remembered as a historic moment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will Vande Mataram be played before every India match?

The BCCI has not confirmed, but indications suggest it will become a regular feature for home matches.

Is it mandatory to play Vande Mataram before the national anthem?

No, it is not mandatory by law. The BCCI implemented it based on a government recommendation.

What is the difference between the national anthem and national song?

The national anthem, 'Jana Gana Mana', is the official anthem of India, while 'Vande Mataram' is the national song, both adopted in 1950.

As the series progresses, all eyes will be on whether this ritual continues and how it shapes the pre-match experience for players and fans alike.
