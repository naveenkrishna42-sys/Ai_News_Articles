Gift Nifty, US-Iran Tensions, Oil Prices: 8 Factors That Shifted Indian Markets Over Weekend

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gift Nifty indicated a negative start for Indian indices on Monday.
  • US-Iran tensions escalated over the weekend, driving crude oil prices higher.
  • Q1 earnings season continues, with several major companies reporting results.
  • Global cues remain mixed, with European and Asian markets showing weakness.
  • Rising oil prices could stoke inflation fears and weigh on market sentiment.

Weekend Triggers That Set the Tone

The Indian stock market heads into a new week with a host of domestic and global factors that shifted over the weekend. Gift Nifty, which tracks the Nifty 50 futures traded on the Singapore Exchange, pointed to a negative opening for the benchmark indices on Monday.

Analysts say the market is bracing for volatility as a mix of geopolitical, macroeconomic, and corporate events converge.

Geopolitical Flashpoint: US-Iran Tensions

The biggest trigger over the weekend was the escalation in US-Iran tensions. Reports of fresh military posturing in the Middle East rattled global markets. Crude oil prices surged on fears of supply disruptions from the region.

Brent crude climbed past $85 a barrel, a level that typically raises alarm bells for oil-importing nations like India. Higher oil prices can widen the trade deficit, stoke imported inflation, and pressure the rupee.

Gift Nifty and Global Cues

Gift Nifty was trading around the 24,800 mark, down nearly 100 points from Friday's close. This suggests Indian indices could open in the red, tracking weakness in Asian peers.

Asian markets were mostly lower on Monday. Japan's Nikkei fell over 1%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng also declined. US stock futures were flat to slightly negative after a mixed close on Wall Street on Friday.

Q1 Earnings Season in Full Swing

Corporate earnings remain a key domestic driver. Several companies are set to report their April-June quarter results this week. The market will parse through management commentary on demand, margins, and future outlook.

Investors are particularly focused on sectors like IT, banking, and consumer goods, where earnings have been a mixed bag so far.

Oil Prices and Inflation Worries

The spike in crude oil prices is a major concern for the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). If sustained, it could delay any potential rate cuts. Bond yields have already inched up, reflecting inflation expectations.

The rupee, which has been relatively stable, could come under renewed pressure if oil stays elevated. A weaker rupee makes imports costlier and adds to corporate input costs.

FII and DII Activity

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have been net sellers in recent sessions, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) have provided support. The trend over the next few days will be crucial for market direction.

If FII selling intensifies due to global risk aversion, the market could see a sharper correction.

What to Watch This Week

Investors will closely track crude oil prices, the US dollar index, and any fresh geopolitical developments. On the domestic front, Q1 results from key companies and macroeconomic data will be in focus.

The market is likely to remain range-bound with a negative bias in the short term. Any escalation in the US-Iran situation could trigger further selling.
