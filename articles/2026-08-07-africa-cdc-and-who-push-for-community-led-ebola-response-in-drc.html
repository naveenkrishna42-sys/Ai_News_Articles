Africa CDC and WHO push for community-led Ebola response in DRC

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Africa CDC and WHO call for urgent, community-led measures to contain Ebola in the DRC
  • Officials warn the virus may be mutating as the outbreak escalates
  • Health workers threaten to abandon response over unpaid wages, disrupting efforts as deaths top 1,800

Joint call for action

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) and the World Health Organization (WHO) have issued a joint appeal for urgent, community-led action to contain the ongoing Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC). The call comes as the outbreak shows no signs of slowing, with officials now warning that the virus may be mutating.

In a statement released on Thursday, the two bodies stressed that top-down interventions alone will not suffice. They emphasised the need to work closely with local communities, who remain the first line of defence in detecting cases and curbing transmission.

Alarming developments

According to the WHO, the Ebola virus behind the massive outbreak in the DRC could be undergoing changes, raising fresh concerns among scientists and public health experts. While details remain scarce, any genetic shift could affect how the virus spreads and how existing treatments perform.

The outbreak has already claimed more than 1,800 lives, according to reports. The true scale of the crisis, however, may be understated, as access to affected areas remains limited and surveillance systems are stretched thin.

Health workers on the brink

Compounding the crisis, health workers on the frontlines have threatened to abandon the response. Unpaid wages and the deadly risk of infection have pushed many to the edge. In recent days, protests have erupted among Ebola responders, disrupting critical activities such as contact tracing and safe burials.

The situation threatens to unravel months of progress. Without a stable and motivated workforce, the response risks losing momentum exactly when it is needed most.

Community trust is key

Both Africa CDC and WHO have long acknowledged that community engagement is not a secondary component but a central pillar of outbreak control. Mistrust of responders and government institutions has hampered earlier efforts in the DRC, and rebuilding that trust requires more than just technical expertise.

The current appeal reflects a broader shift in global health strategy — one that places communities at the heart of emergency response. This includes involving local leaders, addressing rumours and misinformation, and ensuring that affected populations have a say in how aid is delivered.

What happens next

The coming weeks will be critical. Authorities must resolve the grievances of health workers quickly, while also ramping up genomic surveillance to monitor any mutations. International partners, including India and other nations with deep public health expertise, will be watching closely.

The focus now shifts to whether the DRC government and its global allies can translate this urgent call into concrete, on-the-ground action before the outbreak spirals further out of control.
