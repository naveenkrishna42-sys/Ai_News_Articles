Technology reshapes Bengaluru construction sector, efficiency gains reported

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Technology is increasingly central to construction in Bengaluru, from planning to execution.
  • Digital tools improve project efficiency, reduce delays, and enhance worker safety.
  • Industry experts highlight growing adoption of BIM, drones, and AI in local projects.

BENGALURU: The construction sector in Bengaluru is undergoing a quiet revolution, driven by the rapid adoption of digital tools and technologies. Industry experts say this shift is no longer optional but essential for keeping pace with the city's growing infrastructure demands.

Digital tools changing project planning

From Building Information Modelling (BIM) to drone-based site surveys, technology is reshaping how projects are conceived and executed. These tools allow architects and engineers to create detailed 3D models, identify potential issues before ground is broken, and track progress in real time.

“The days of relying solely on paper blueprints are fading,” a senior project manager at a leading Bengaluru realty firm said. “BIM alone has cut design conflicts by nearly 30 per cent on our recent projects.”

The shift is particularly visible in high-rise residential and commercial complexes coming up along Outer Ring Road and Whitefield. Developers are investing in cloud-based platforms that connect everyone from the site supervisor to the client.

Efficiency gains and cost savings

Technology is driving measurable improvements in efficiency. Automated concrete batching plants, GPS-enabled equipment tracking, and AI-powered scheduling tools are helping contractors complete projects faster and with fewer cost overruns.

“We have seen a 15-20 per cent reduction in material waste on sites where we use digital inventory management,” a construction technology consultant noted. “That translates to crores in savings for large projects.”

Safety has also improved. Wearable devices that monitor worker fatigue and environmental sensors that detect hazardous gas levels are becoming more common on Bengaluru construction sites. This has led to a decline in reportable accidents, according to industry data shared with TIVRA News.

Challenges remain in adoption

Despite the clear benefits, the transition is not seamless. Many small and medium contractors still rely on traditional methods, citing high upfront costs and a lack of skilled personnel as barriers.

“The cost of software licenses and training can be prohibitive for smaller firms,” a representative from a local builders' association said. “But without adoption, they risk being left behind as clients demand greater transparency and faster delivery.”

The state government has introduced some incentives for tech adoption, including subsidies for BIM software and safety equipment. However, industry bodies say more needs to be done, particularly in skilling the workforce.

What to watch next

As Bengaluru pushes ahead with mega-infrastructure projects like the suburban rail network and elevated corridors, the role of technology in construction will only deepen. The coming months will show whether smaller players can bridge the digital divide — or if the sector will see a consolidation driven by tech-savvy firms.
