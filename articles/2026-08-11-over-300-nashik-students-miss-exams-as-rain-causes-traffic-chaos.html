Over 300 Nashik students miss exams as rain causes traffic chaos

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Heavy rainfall in Nashik caused severe traffic congestion and waterlogging.
  • Over 300 students missed their university examinations due to the disruption.
  • University officials are yet to announce a reschedule for affected candidates.

Nashik, July 8: Heavy rainfall lashed parts of Nashik on Wednesday, throwing traffic out of gear and leaving over 300 university students stranded and unable to reach their examination centres on time. The city witnessed waterlogging in several low-lying areas, with commuters reporting long delays on key routes.

The downpour, which began early in the morning, intensified during peak hours, turning roads into slow-moving rivers. Students travelling from distant suburbs bore the brunt, as buses and auto-rickshaws struggled to navigate the flooded streets.

Traffic snarls and waterlogging disrupt morning commute

Several arterial roads, including College Road and the Nashik-Pune highway stretch, reported heavy congestion. Vehicles crawled at a snail's pace, and some two-wheeler riders were seen wading through knee-deep water.

Local authorities deployed personnel to manage traffic at key junctions, but the sheer volume of water overwhelmed the drainage system. Commuters took to social media to share photos of submerged crossings and stalled vehicles, with many urging others to stay indoors unless absolutely necessary.

The India Meteorological Department had earlier issued a warning for heavy rainfall in the region, but the intensity caught many by surprise. A student stranded near Gangapur Road said, "I left home an hour early, but the bus got stuck for nearly 45 minutes. By the time I reached the centre, the exam had already begun."

Examination centres see empty seats

University examination officials confirmed that at least 300 candidates across various colleges could not appear for their scheduled papers. Attendance sheets at several centres showed numerous absentees, a rare sight for a mid-session examination.

Proctors at one centre in the Cidco area noted that some students arrived late but were allowed to sit, as per university norms. However, those who arrived after the grace period were turned away, leaving many in tears.

The affected papers included undergraduate and postgraduate courses, though the university has not yet provided a subject-wise breakdown. Officials said they are compiling the list of absentees from all centres.

University response awaited

As of now, the university has not issued an official statement on whether a special examination will be conducted for the affected students. A senior administrative officer said, "We are assessing the situation and will take a decision after reviewing the reports from all examination centres."

Students, meanwhile, are anxious. Many have submitted representations to their college principals, requesting that the university consider the weather disruption as a valid reason for absence. One final-year student said, "This was my last attempt. I don't know what I'll do if they don't arrange a retest."

Parents have also voiced concerns, urging the administration to show leniency given the extraordinary circumstances. The district collector's office has been in touch with university officials, though no formal intervention has been announced yet.

What to watch next

With more rain predicted over the next 48 hours, the university is expected to make an announcement soon. Students are advised to monitor official channels for updates on rescheduled exams and to plan travel with extra time.
