Why Karnataka’s Hanuman Shrines Matter

Karnataka blends rugged hills, lush valleys, and centuries‑old devotion, making it a hotspot for Hanuman worship. The nine temples highlighted here draw pilgrims from across South India and offer a mix of historic architecture, scenic surroundings, and vibrant festivals.

For travelers based in Bengaluru, the sites are clustered within 150 km, allowing a single‑day itinerary or a relaxed weekend circuit. Understanding each temple’s unique draw helps you prioritize stops that match your spiritual or photographic interests.

Temple‑by‑Temple Guide

1. Shri Hanuman Temple, Hampi

Set against the UNESCO‑listed ruins of Vijayanagara, this shrine sits near the iconic Virupaksha Temple. The idol is carved from a single granite block, exuding a powerful aura.

2. Shri Hanuman Temple, Kolar

Located on a hilltop overlooking Kolar Gold Fields, the temple offers panoramic views and a tranquil ambience. Early morning visits are rewarded with cool breezes and fewer crowds.

3. Shri Hanuman Temple, Chikmagalur

Nestled amid coffee plantations, this temple is famed for its annual “Hanuman Jayanti” procession that winds through mist‑covered hills.

4. Shri Hanuman Temple, Mysore

Just outside the city’s famous palace complex, the temple features a towering 30‑foot bronze statue of Hanuman, illuminated at night.

5. Shri Hanuman Temple, Shivamogga

Perched on the banks of the Sharavathi River, the shrine is a popular stop for pilgrims heading to Jog Falls, offering a serene riverside setting.

6. Shri Hanuman Temple, Tumkur

Known for its “Bajrang Bali” idol that is said to grant wishes, the temple’s annual “Hanuman Yatra” draws thousands from neighboring districts.

7. Shri Hanuman Temple, Hassan

Built in the Hoysala style, the temple showcases intricate stone carvings and a rare depiction of Hanuman holding a mountain of herbs.

8. Shri Hanuman Temple, Belgaum

Located near the historic Belgaum Fort, this shrine blends Maratha and Deccan architectural motifs, creating a unique visual experience.

9. Shri Hanuman Temple, Bidar

Set within the ancient Bidar Fort complex, the temple’s sanctum houses a silver‑crowned Hanuman idol, attracting devotees during the “Makara Sankranti” festival.

Pros & Cons of Visiting These Shrines

  • Pros: Free entry, rich cultural heritage, diverse landscapes, easy road connectivity from Bengaluru.
  • Cons: Some sites lack dedicated parking, peak festivals can cause crowding, limited vegetarian food stalls near remote temples.

Practical Planning Tips

Start early to beat traffic, especially for the farther Hampi and Bidar locations. Carry bottled water and a light snack, as many rural temples have limited vending options.

Most temples accept digital payments for donations, but keep a small cash reserve for local vendors. Dress modestly—shoulders and knees covered—to respect sanctity and avoid entry restrictions.

Consider a two‑day loop: Day 1 covering Kolar, Tumkur, and Mysore; Day 2 for Hampi, Chikmagalur, Shivamogga, Hassan, Belgaum, and Bidar. This spreads travel time and lets you linger for sunrise or evening aarti at select sites.

Final Thoughts

Karnataka’s Hanuman temples offer more than ritual worship; they provide a window into the state’s layered history and natural beauty. By leveraging the distance, timing, and unique highlights outlined above, pilgrims and tourists alike can craft a rewarding itinerary that balances devotion with discovery.
