Avalanche on Broad Peak kills famed climber Nirmal Purja, nine others

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nirmal Purja and nine other climbers died in an avalanche on Broad Peak in Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan.
  • Purja's body was recovered three days after the avalanche, with all ten climbers confirmed dead.
  • The incident has sent shockwaves through the global mountaineering community, with tributes pouring in.

Deadly avalanche on Broad Peak

Renowned mountaineer Nirmal Purja and nine other climbers have been killed in an avalanche on Broad Peak in Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan, officials confirmed on Sunday. The disaster struck the 8,051-metre peak, the world's 12th highest, during a climbing expedition.

Rescuers recovered Purja's body three days after the avalanche, with all ten climbers now confirmed dead. The recovery operation was hampered by treacherous weather and difficult terrain, officials said.

Purja's body found after three-day search

Search teams finally located Purja's remains on the mountain, bringing closure to a grim wait for families and the climbing community. The confirmation came after days of intense efforts by Pakistani authorities and international rescue teams.

Details of the other nine climbers have not yet been fully released, though officials have indicated they included both foreign and local mountaineers. The exact circumstances of the avalanche, including its trigger and timing, remain under investigation.

A legend of high-altitude climbing

Purja, a former Gurkha and British Special Boat Service soldier, was one of the most celebrated climbers of his generation. He shot to global fame in 2019 when he scaled all 14 of the world's 8,000-metre peaks in just six months and six days, shattering the previous record.

His feats, documented in the acclaimed film '14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible', inspired millions and redefined what was thought achievable in high-altitude mountaineering. Purja was also known for his daring rescue missions, including saving a Malaysian climber from Everest's 'death zone' in 2018.

Global tributes and questions

The mountaineering world has reacted with shock and grief. Tributes have poured in from climbers, adventurers, and fans, hailing Purja as a pioneer who pushed human limits with grit and humility.

This tragedy underscores the inherent risks of climbing in the Karakoram range, where unpredictable weather and avalanches remain constant threats. Safety protocols and expedition practices on Broad Peak are now under scrutiny, though no official statement has been issued yet.

As the bodies are brought down, families and authorities are preparing for repatriation and memorials. The broader climbing community is left to reflect on the loss of a titan, even as the mountain reclaims its silence.

What happens next will depend on the ongoing investigation and the climbing season's remaining expeditions, with many calling for enhanced safety measures in the region.
