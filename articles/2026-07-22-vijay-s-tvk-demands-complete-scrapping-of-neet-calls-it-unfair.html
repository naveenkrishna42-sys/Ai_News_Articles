Vijay's TVK Demands Complete Scrapping of NEET, Calls It 'Unfair'

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • TVK chief Vijay announced the party's 'uncompromising' stand against NEET.
  • The demand comes amid renewed protests by medical aspirants in Tamil Nadu.
  • Vijay's party has called for the exam to be scrapped entirely, not just reformed.

Actor-turned-politician Vijay, the chief of Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK), has demanded the complete scrapping of the National Eligibility-cum-Entrance Test (NEET). The party described its position as an 'uncompromising and firm stand' against the medical entrance exam.

The demand comes at a time when protests by medical aspirants in Tamil Nadu have gained momentum again. TVK has argued that NEET is structurally biased against students from rural backgrounds and those who study in Tamil-medium schools.

Why TVK is Opposing NEET

Vijay has repeatedly stated that NEET creates an uneven playing field. The party believes the exam favours students from affluent urban families who can afford expensive coaching. "This exam is a social injustice," Vijay has said at public rallies.

TVK's position is that neither tweaks nor exemptions will solve the problem. The party insists that only a full repeal of NEET will ensure equal access to medical education for all students in Tamil Nadu.

Political Context in Tamil Nadu

Tamil Nadu has a long history of opposition to NEET. The state government, led by the DMK, has passed multiple resolutions in the assembly against the exam. These have been pending with the Union government for years.

Vijay's TVK is now pushing the issue into the spotlight again. The party is trying to position itself as the true voice of student rights in the state. Critics, however, have pointed out that Vijay was relatively quiet on the issue during earlier phases of protests.

Recent events have lent fresh urgency to the demand. The brother of a NEET aspirant from Tamil Nadu who died by suicide recently joined a protest organised by the Centre for Juridical and Policy Research (CJP). This has amplified calls for action.

Can NEET Actually Be Scrapped?

Legal experts say that scrapping NEET is not straightforward. The exam is mandated by a Supreme Court judgment and subsequent parliamentary legislation. Any change would require either a new law or a successful challenge in the highest court.

The Union government has so far defended NEET as a measure to standardise medical admissions across India. It has dismissed state-level opposition as politically motivated. TVK's demand, while politically potent, faces significant legal and procedural hurdles.

What Happens Next

TVK is expected to raise the issue in the upcoming assembly session. The party may also organise public agitations to keep pressure on the state and central governments. For now, the debate over NEET in Tamil Nadu is far from settled.
