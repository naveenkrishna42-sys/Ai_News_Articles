WHO chief visits DR Congo as Ebola workers strike over unpaid wages

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • WHO director-general visits eastern DR Congo to show support for Ebola response
  • Health workers strike over unpaid salaries, hampering containment efforts
  • Outbreak has killed 1,700, making it the world's second-largest Ebola epidemic

Visit amid crisis

The head of the World Health Organization (WHO) has arrived in the Democratic Republic of Congo to bolster the fight against the country's deadliest Ebola outbreak. The visit comes at a critical moment, as the epidemic continues to surge in the eastern provinces and some frontline workers have downed tools over unpaid wages.

The WHO chief's presence underscores the international community's concern over an outbreak that has already claimed 1,700 lives. This makes it the second-largest Ebola epidemic ever recorded, trailing only the devastating West Africa outbreak of 2014-2016.

Strike adds to challenges

The strike by health workers has added a fresh layer of complexity to an already difficult response. Reports indicate that many local nurses, hygienists, and burial team members have not received their salaries for months, a situation that has forced some to stop working entirely.

This has created gaps in surveillance and patient care, particularly in remote communities where mistrust of responders is already high. Health officials fear that any interruption in the response could allow the virus to spread further into densely populated areas.

Bundibugyo virus strain

The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, a less common strain of Ebola. While it has a lower fatality rate than the Zaire strain, it still poses a serious threat, especially in a region plagued by conflict and population displacement.

Local health authorities, with support from the WHO and other partners, have been conducting ring vaccination campaigns and community outreach. However, insecurity and the strike have slowed these efforts, prompting the need for high-level intervention.

International support and next steps

The WHO chief's itinerary includes meetings with government officials, health workers, and community leaders. The goal is to reassure responders of continued support and to find a resolution to the pay dispute that has crippled parts of the operation.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of workers affected by the strike or the total amount of unpaid wages. Negotiations are reportedly underway to address the workers' grievances and resume full operations.

The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether the outbreak can be brought under control. With the world's second-largest Ebola epidemic on record, the stakes could not be higher for the region and for global health security.
