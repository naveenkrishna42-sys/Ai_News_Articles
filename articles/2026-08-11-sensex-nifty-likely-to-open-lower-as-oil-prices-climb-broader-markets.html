Sensex, Nifty likely to open lower as oil prices climb; broader markets in focus

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rising crude oil prices may pressure Indian markets, especially oil importers.
  • Broader markets, including midcap and smallcap indices, will be in focus.
  • Analysts expect volatility due to global cues and domestic earnings.

Market Opens on Cautious Note

Indian equity benchmarks are likely to open on a tepid note on Tuesday, as investors weigh the impact of higher crude oil prices on the domestic economy. The Nifty 50 is expected to face resistance at higher levels, with selling pressure visible in oil-dependent sectors such as aviation, paints, and FMCG.

At the time of writing, SGX Nifty futures traded marginally lower, indicating a soft start for the indices. The broader markets — including midcap and smallcap stocks — will be closely watched, as they have shown resilience in recent sessions despite the benchmark's sideways movement.

Crude Oil Prices Cast a Shadow

Brent crude futures have climbed to multi-week highs, driven by supply concerns and geopolitical tensions. For India, which imports over 80% of its crude oil requirements, a sustained rise in oil prices could widen the current account deficit and stoke inflationary pressures.

This has prompted traders to trim positions in rate-sensitive sectors, while defensive bets on IT and pharma are being preferred. "The market is in a consolidation phase, and oil is the key variable to watch," said a derivatives analyst at a domestic brokerage.

Broader Markets in Spotlight

Midcap and smallcap indices have outperformed the frontline indices over the past month, and this trend is expected to continue. However, valuations in these segments remain stretched, and any sharp correction in global equities could trigger profit-booking.

Market participants are also keeping an eye on the rupee, which has weakened slightly against the dollar due to the oil price surge. A weaker rupee makes imports costlier and can dent sentiment for foreign portfolio investors.

Key Levels and Stock Cues

For the Nifty, immediate support is seen at the 24,200-24,300 zone, while resistance is placed around 24,600. Bank Nifty, which has been under pressure due to concerns over deposit growth, will be a key driver for the market's direction.

In corporate news, some large-cap companies are scheduled to announce their quarterly results later this week, which could add to volatility. Traders are advised to keep positions light and use any dips to accumulate quality stocks.

Global Cues and Data Watch

Asian markets were mixed in early trade, with Japan's Nikkei and China's Shanghai Composite showing divergent trends. Wall Street closed flat overnight, as investors awaited key US inflation data due later this week.

Back home, foreign institutional investors were net sellers in the previous session, while domestic institutional investors provided support. The market breadth is expected to remain mixed, with selective buying in metal and energy stocks.

As the session progresses, the focus will shift to the movement of the rupee and crude oil prices. Any unexpected spike in oil could trigger a sharp sell-off, while a pullback may provide some relief to the bulls.

Going ahead, the market will take cues from the upcoming US inflation numbers and domestic macroeconomic data. For now, traders are likely to stay nimble and avoid building aggressive positions until the direction becomes clearer.
