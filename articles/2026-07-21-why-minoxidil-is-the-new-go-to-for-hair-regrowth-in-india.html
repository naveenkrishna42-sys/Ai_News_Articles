Why minoxidil is the new go-to for hair regrowth in India

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Minoxidil is available over the counter in India and costs under ₹500 per month.
  • Dermatologists say it works best for androgenetic alopecia when used consistently for 3-6 months.
  • Common side effects include scalp irritation and initial shedding; serious issues are rare but need medical attention.

Hair loss has long been a source of anxiety for millions of Indians. But in recent months, one treatment has quietly moved from dermatologist clinics to everyday conversations: minoxidil.

Originally developed as a blood pressure medication, minoxidil is now the most widely used topical treatment for pattern baldness. Available over the counter in India, it costs under ₹500 for a month's supply. That affordability, combined with growing awareness, is driving a surge in demand.

Why people are switching to minoxidil

The treatment works by stimulating hair follicles and prolonging the growth phase of hair. It does not require a prescription, which makes it an easy first step for many.

Dermatologists report that more patients in their 20s and 30s are coming in asking specifically for minoxidil, often after trying shampoos, oils, and supplements that showed little result. Social media and word-of-mouth recommendations have played a large role.

Doctors caution that minoxidil must be used consistently. Missing applications for more than a few days can reverse gains within weeks. Results typically take three to six months to become visible.

What dermatologists want you to know

Minoxidil is not a cure for all types of hair loss. It works best for androgenetic alopecia — the common hereditary pattern baldness seen in both men and women. It does not help with sudden shedding caused by stress, illness, or nutritional deficiencies.

Dr. Nandini Agarwal, a leading dermatologist based in Mumbai, says patients often expect overnight transformation. “Minoxidil is a long-term commitment. If you stop, the hair you gained will fall out within a few months,” she told Vogue India.

She also emphasises that starting early — when thinning is just noticeable — yields better results than waiting for large bald patches to form.

Side effects: what to expect and when to worry

Common side effects include scalp itching, redness, and flaking. These are usually mild and can be managed by switching to a foam formulation or reducing frequency.

A more surprising effect is the “dread shed” — a temporary increase in hair fall during the first few weeks. This happens as minoxidil pushes resting hairs out to make way for new growth. Dermatologists say this is normal and should not be a reason to stop.

Serious side effects are rare but include dizziness, rapid heartbeat, and swelling of the face or hands. These require immediate medical attention. A consultation with a doctor before starting is advised, especially for those with heart conditions.

The growing market and what comes next

Indian pharmaceutical companies are now producing minoxidil in multiple strengths — from 2% to 10% — and in varied formats: topical solutions, foams, and even oral tablets. The oral version, however, requires a prescription and carries higher risk of systemic side effects.

Dermatologists warn against self-prescribing higher strengths in the hope of faster results. “More is not better. A 10% solution can cause intense irritation without improving outcomes,” Dr. Agarwal noted.

The rise of online consultations and pharmacy apps has made access even easier. Many users buy minoxidil without ever seeing a doctor. While this expands reach, experts advise at least one professional evaluation before beginning treatment.

As the conversation around hair loss becomes less stigmatised, minoxidil is likely to remain a first-line option. The coming months will likely see more research on combination therapies and long-term safety in the Indian population.
