Sensex Plunges 700 Points Intraday as Nifty Breaches 23,250 Support Amid Broad Sell-Off

Key points
  • Broad-based selling across sectors pushed the Sensex down over 700 points intraday, erasing early gains
  • Nifty 50 broke below the critical 23,250 support level, signalling bearish momentum
  • Global cues, institutional profit-taking, and rising crude prices amplified the downward spiral

What Triggered the Market Crash

The Indian equity market witnessed a severe intraday sell-off on Friday, with the Sensex plunging over 700 points from its opening levels. The Nifty 50 index slipped below the psychologically significant 23,250 mark, alarming retail and institutional investors alike. The sell-off was not confined to a single sector; instead, it was a broad-based correction that dragged most blue-chip stocks into negative territory within the first hour of trading.

Early weakness was compounded by persistent foreign portfolio outflows and a sharp rise in crude oil prices, which heightened concerns over inflation and corporate earnings growth. Domestic institutional investors were observed trimming positions in banking and metals stocks, two sectors that had carried the market higher over the previous two quarters.

1. Global Cues and Foreign Selling

Weak Asian markets overnight, particularly in Tokyo and Hong Kong, set a cautious tone for Indian equities at the open. European futures also indicated a risk-off sentiment. Foreign portfolio investors have sold over ₹18,000 crore in Indian debt and equity markets in the preceding two weeks, and Friday's session saw that pace accelerate. The outflows put additional downward pressure on liquidity, especially in large-cap financials and IT shares.

2. Banking and Metals Sector Pressure

Private sector banks, which had been the backbone of the Nifty's rally, turned into the biggest laggards. Shares of major lenders fell between 2% and 4% intraday as concerns over rising non-performing assets and margin pressure from higher borrowing costs weighed on sentiment. Metals stocks, especially those exposed to export demand, also saw heavy selling as global commodity prices softened.

3. IT and Auto Stocks Under Pressure

The IT sector continued to struggle despite reporting decent quarterly results for several firms. Investors appeared to discount earnings beats, preferring to book profits after a strong run-up. Auto stocks, particularly those tied to rural demand, weakened on expectations of a muted monsoon impact in the coming quarter.

Key Real-World Highlights for Investors

  • Pro: The sharp correction has brought several quality large-cap stocks closer to their fair valuation bands after months of overvaluation. Long-term investors may find accumulation opportunities in fundamentally strong names that were trading at premium multiples.
  • Con: Retail investors who entered the market at recent highs face significant paper losses. The breach of 23,250 on the Nifty suggests further downside risk until a decisive support level near 22,800 is tested and held.
  • Pro: Debt mutual funds may benefit from the flight-to-safety move, as government securities and corporate bonds see renewed demand during equity sell-offs.
  • Con: Small-cap and mid-cap funds are likely to experience deeper drawdowns compared to large-caps, given thinner liquidity and higher beta during correction phases.
  • Key Insight: The India VIX spike above 18 confirms that market participants expect continued volatility. Options data suggests that traders are pricing in a test of the 22,800 level on the Nifty before any meaningful recovery.

What Investors Should Watch Next

Traders and portfolio managers should closely monitor the 22,800 level on the Nifty as the next major support. A breach below that threshold could open the door to a further 2-3% correction. On the upside, reclaiming 23,250 with strong volume will be essential to restore short-term bullish confidence.

The Reserve Bank of India's upcoming monetary policy review and the government's interim fiscal measures will be critical catalysts. Investors should avoid panic selling in fundamentally sound holdings but must reassess overexposed portfolios, particularly in the banking and metals segments where earnings risk remains elevated.

Market operators are advised to maintain higher cash allocations until the volatility index recedes below 15, signalling a return to calmer trading conditions.
