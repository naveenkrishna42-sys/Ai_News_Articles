Jensen Huang Resigns: Colette Kress Takes Interim Charge at Nvidia

Key points
  • Nvidia's board has installed CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO after Jensen Huang's resignation, with a formal search underway for a permanent chief.
  • Unverified allegations of workplace relationships, first reported by Reuters and The New York Times, now sit alongside the corporate succession story and remain unproven.
  • For enterprise GPU buyers, the practical risks are roadmap continuity, allocation priority and contract clauses rather than any immediate change in silicon.

The Announcement and the Facts on Record

Nvidia confirmed on 12 November 2026 that Jensen Huang has resigned as president and chief executive officer, closing a tenure that began when he co-founded the company in 1993. The board named chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive while a search committee evaluates internal and external candidates, according to the company statement first carried by Business Standard.

Kress is not a caretaker appointment. She has run Nvidia's finance function since 2013, steered the balance sheet through the data-centre buildout, and sits close to the supply commitments that define the company's revenue visibility. Her elevation keeps institutional memory intact at a moment when investors are most sensitive to execution risk.

What is confirmed, and what is not

Reuters and The New York Times reported that Huang had a relationship with a second Nvidia employee, with the Times specifying that the executive reported to him. Those accounts rest on unnamed sources. Nvidia has not published investigation findings, no regulatory action has been announced, and the allegations remain unproven. The Times of India reported that the US President commented on the matter and confirmed speaking with Huang, adding a political dimension that carries no evidentiary weight.

Readers should separate three things: the resignation is confirmed; the relationship allegations are reported but unverified; and the national-security framing circulating in some outlets is speculation.

Who Is Colette Kress?

Kress joined Nvidia in 2013 from Cisco's business technology finance organisation and previously spent nearly a decade at Microsoft. Her record is one of capital discipline during hypergrowth — managing gross margin, buybacks, and the multi-billion-dollar prepayments that lock in advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory capacity.

Her interim mandate will be tested on three fronts: holding foundry and memory allocations through the next two quarters, reassuring hyperscalers that the accelerated computing roadmap is unchanged, and managing disclosure around the internal inquiry.

What It Means for Enterprise GPU Buyers

Procurement teams should not expect silicon changes. Architecture decisions, CUDA compatibility and software support cycles are set by engineering leadership and multi-year roadmaps, not by a single executive. The real exposure sits in commercial terms: allocation priority, delivery windows on rack-scale systems, and service-level commitments embedded in multi-year agreements.

Buyers holding pending quotes should push for written confirmation that pricing, allocation and support terms survive a leadership change. Channel partners should verify that existing distributor agreements remain valid and that warranty obligations are unaffected.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Continuity signal: Elevating the sitting CFO signals the board wants financial and supply-side stability, not strategic reinvention.
  • Roadmap risk is low, disclosure risk is higher: Product cadence is unlikely to shift; governance reporting may become more conservative in the interim period.
  • Contract hygiene matters now: Buyers should formalise allocation, pricing and support terms in writing during the transition window.
  • Allegations remain allegations: No verified finding exists on the workplace relationship claims, and treating them as settled fact would be inaccurate.
  • Search timeline is the swing factor: A quick internal appointment steadies investor confidence; a prolonged external search invites speculation about strategic direction.

Watch the next earnings call for two tells: whether Kress retains the CFO title alongside the interim CEO role, and whether the board commits to a search deadline. Both will indicate how long the interim arrangement is expected to last.
