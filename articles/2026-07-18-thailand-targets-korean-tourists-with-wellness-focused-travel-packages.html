Thailand targets Korean tourists with wellness-focused travel packages

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • Thailand's tourism authorities are shifting focus from nightlife to wellness for Korean visitors
  • New packages include spa treatments, yoga retreats, and traditional Thai medicine
  • Korean travelers spent over 1.5 billion baht in Thailand last year, but numbers dipped recently

Wellness over nightlife: Thailand's new pitch to Koreans

Thailand is rethinking its tourism playbook. The country, long known for its beaches, street food and nightlife, is now marketing itself as a sanctuary for the soul. The target: Korean tourists, who have been travelling less to the kingdom in recent months.

The Tourism Authority of Thailand (TAT) has launched a campaign called 'Healing is the new luxury'. It aims to draw Korean visitors with curated wellness itineraries — spa retreats, meditation sessions, and traditional Thai healing practices.

Korean travellers spent more than 1.5 billion baht in Thailand last year. But the numbers have dipped. Officials say changing preferences and competition from other destinations are factors.

What the new packages offer

The wellness packages include stays at luxury resorts with on-site Ayurvedic centres, forest bathing programmes in Chiang Mai, and detox retreats in Koh Samui. Some packages also include visits to Thai temples for guided meditation.

Thai tourism officials are working with Korean travel agencies and influencers to promote these packages. Social media campaigns highlight the 'healing' angle, using hashtags like #HealingInThailand and #WellnessThai.

TAT governor Thapanee Kiatphaibool said in a statement that Korean tourists are increasingly seeking 'meaningful experiences' rather than just sightseeing. 'They want to rest, reset, and reconnect,' she said.

Why Korean travellers matter

South Korea is one of Thailand's top tourism markets. In 2019, before the pandemic, over 1.8 million Koreans visited Thailand. The number recovered to 1.6 million last year. But early 2026 numbers show a slight decline.

Industry insiders point to rising airfares and the strength of the Thai baht as reasons. Some Korean travellers are also choosing Japan and Vietnam instead. Thailand needs a fresh hook.

The wellness pivot is not just about marketing. Thailand has invested heavily in health tourism infrastructure. The country now has more than 1,200 hospitals and clinics certified for medical tourism. Many spas and resorts now offer programmes designed specifically for East Asian visitors.

Competition from other destinations

Thailand is not alone in chasing the wellness traveller. Bali, Sri Lanka, and even South Korea itself have been promoting similar packages. But Thailand's advantage is its established tourism infrastructure and relatively lower costs.

Korean tourists in particular are known for their high spending on beauty and wellness services back home. Thailand hopes to capture some of that spending by offering premium experiences at a fraction of the price.

Some Thai spa operators report a 20 percent increase in Korean bookings since the campaign launched. But others say it is too early to tell. 'The concept is good, but execution matters,' said one Bangkok-based travel consultant who asked not to be named.

What happens next depends on how quickly Thai authorities can train staff, certify wellness programmes, and ensure quality control. If the pilot phase succeeds, Thailand plans to expand the campaign to other Asian markets, including China and Japan.
