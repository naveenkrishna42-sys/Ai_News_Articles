Sensex, Nifty post second weekly gain on RBI policy, FII inflows

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Markets extended gains for a second consecutive week
  • RBI policy and FII support drove the rally
  • Sensex climbed 374 points; Nifty ended flat

Indian equities capped a second consecutive week of gains, with the Sensex climbing 374 points and the Nifty ending largely flat, as investors took heart from the Reserve Bank of India's policy stance and sustained foreign institutional investor (FII) inflows. The positive close on Friday capped a week that saw the benchmark indices navigate a mix of global cues and domestic factors.

RBI policy and FII flows underpin sentiment

The central bank's policy announcement during the week provided a clear directional cue, reassuring markets about liquidity and growth support. Dealers said the commentary reinforced expectations of a stable rate trajectory, which in turn encouraged buying in rate-sensitive sectors.

Foreign investors remained net buyers through the week, adding to the positive undertone. Their participation helped offset some of the volatility stemming from global events, including fluctuating crude oil prices and geopolitical developments.

Reliance, ICICI Bank lead the charge

Buying was concentrated in index heavyweights, with Reliance Industries and ICICI Bank emerging as the primary drivers of the Sensex's gains. Both stocks saw sustained demand, lifting the benchmark despite a broadly flat showing from the Nifty.

Market participants noted that the divergence between the two indices reflected a selective approach, with investors favouring large-caps over mid- and small-caps. The Nifty's flat close suggested that profit-taking in some pockets capped broader upside.

Crude ease and geopolitical hopes add tailwinds

Easing crude oil prices provided additional support, as lower input costs are seen as positive for corporate margins and the country's import bill. Reports of a potential US-Iran peace deal also improved risk appetite, with investors hoping for reduced geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

However, the overhang of the CAS (Customs Automated System) issue continued to weigh on certain sections of the market. Analysts said the glitch had caused some operational disruptions, but the overall impact on market sentiment was limited.

What lies ahead

Looking forward, traders will keep a close watch on global crude prices, FII flow data, and any further clarity on the US-Iran situation. Domestic macros, including inflation prints and corporate earnings, will also guide market direction in the coming sessions.

With the RBI policy behind them, investors are likely to shift focus to quarterly results and global cues. The market's ability to sustain the upward momentum will depend on whether foreign inflows continue and geopolitical risks remain contained.
