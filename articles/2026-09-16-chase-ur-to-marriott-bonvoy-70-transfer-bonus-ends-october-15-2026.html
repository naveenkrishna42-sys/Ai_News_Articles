Chase's 70% Marriott Bonvoy Transfer Bonus: The Numbers That Matter

Chase has activated a 70% conversion bonus on Ultimate Rewards (UR) transfers to Marriott Bonvoy, running through October 15, 2026. The standard transfer ratio of 1:1 is temporarily lifted to 1:1.7, which means every 1,000 UR points you move converts into 1,700 Bonvoy points. For Indian cardholders holding Chase-issued cards through global programmes or NRI accounts, this is a meaningful uplift, but only if the redemption maths works in your favour.

This is not a flat cashback offer. It is a loyalty currency arbitrage play, and the window is roughly four weeks from the date of this report.

What 70% Actually Buys You

Marriott Bonvoy points are among the weaker major hotel currencies on a per-point basis. A Category 5 property in India typically prices at 30,000–40,000 points per night, while Category 7–8 properties in Europe or the Maldives can demand 60,000–100,000 points. At the standard 1:1 ratio, transferring UR to Bonvoy usually destroys value because UR points redeem at 1.5–1.8 paise each through Chase Travel or airline partners.

The 70% bonus narrows that gap. Take 60,000 UR points. Transferred at the bonus rate, they become 102,000 Bonvoy points. If those points secure two nights at a Category 6 property that would otherwise cost ₹45,000 per night, you are extracting roughly ₹90,000 of hotel value from points you could have redeemed for about ₹90,000–₹1,08,000 in cash-equivalent travel. The bonus makes the transfer neutral rather than loss-making, and clearly profitable if you target peak-season stays where cash rates spike.

Who Should Transfer and Who Should Not

Transfer if you fit these profiles

  • You have a confirmed, high-cash-rate Bonvoy redemption already available for booking.
  • You are topping up an existing Bonvoy balance to reach an award threshold.
  • You are targeting luxury properties where cash rates exceed ₹40,000 per night.
  • You hold a large UR balance you cannot deploy on better airline partners.

Skip the transfer if

  • You have no specific award stay planned before your points expire.
  • You value UR for Hyatt, United, or Air Canada Aeroplan transfers, which typically yield 1.5–2.0 paise per point.
  • You need flexibility to convert to cash back or statement credit.
  • You are chasing Bonvoy status without a redemption plan.

Practical Benchmarks and Buyer Context

Marriott Bonvoy runs frequent transfer bonuses, but 70% sits at the higher end of recent promotions. Historically, bonuses have ranged from 30% to 50%, with occasional 100% offers tied to specific co-branded cards. A 70% bonus with a clean deadline and no card-tier restriction is above average.

The critical risk is irreversibility. Once UR points leave Chase and land in Bonvoy, they cannot return. Bonvoy points also carry dynamic pricing, so the award chart you see today may shift before you book. Confirm availability, lock the award, then transfer. Do not transfer first and search later.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Effective 1:1.7 ratio is the headline number; anything less than a 50% bonus is usually not worth the transfer.
  • Best value comes from peak-season luxury redemptions, not off-peak Category 3–4 stays.
  • Transfers post within 48 hours, so a last-minute award booking on October 14 carries real risk.
  • Chase has not indicated whether this bonus will extend beyond October 15, 2026.

Treat this as a targeted redemption tool, not a default move. If you have a confirmed Bonvoy award in mind where the 70% uplift beats your best UR alternative, act before the deadline. If not, hold your Ultimate Rewards points and wait for a stronger airline transfer bonus.
