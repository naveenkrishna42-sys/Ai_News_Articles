Over 3.6 Lakh Evacuated as Wildfires Rage Across France and Spain

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • More than 360,000 people evacuated from France and Spain due to wildfires.
  • French firefighters encountered a 'pyrocumulonimbus' cloud for the first time.
  • Critical firefighting window opens as hotter weather is forecast to return.
  • Tens of thousands displaced near Bordeaux and in other affected regions.

Unprecedented wildfires sweeping through parts of France and Spain have forced more than 360,000 people from their homes, authorities said Sunday. The mass evacuation, one of the largest in recent European history, underscores the severity of a fire season that has already scorched thousands of hectares.

In France, the worst-hit area remains the Gironde region near Bordeaux, where tens of thousands have fled. Fire crews are battling multiple fronts, with flames threatening residential areas and vineyards alike. Spanish authorities report similar scenes in several provinces, where heat and dry winds have turned forests into tinderboxes.

First 'Pyrocumulonimbus' Cloud Recorded in France

French firefighters encountered a rare and dangerous phenomenon this week: a pyrocumulonimbus cloud. This thunderstorm-like cloud forms directly above intense fires, generating its own weather system. It can produce lightning, erratic winds, and even fire tornadoes.

Experts say the pyrocumulonimbus event near Bordeaux is the first ever recorded in France. The cloud's formation signals extreme fire behavior, making suppression efforts significantly more complex and hazardous for ground crews.

Critical Window Before Heat Returns

Officials have described the current period as a critical window for bringing the blazes under control. Cooler temperatures and higher humidity have given firefighters a temporary advantage. However, forecasts predict hotter, drier weather will return within days.

“Every hour counts,” a French emergency services spokesperson said. Teams from across the country, along with reinforcements from other European nations, are working to establish containment lines. Aerial tankers have been dropping water and retardant continuously, but the scale of the fires has stretched resources thin.

Evacuation Centres Under Pressure

Evacuation centres in both countries are struggling to accommodate the displaced. In France, stadiums and school gymnasiums have been converted into temporary shelters. Officials report that many evacuees left with little more than the clothes they were wearing.

In Spain, local authorities have urged residents in several provinces to remain vigilant. Police are patrolling evacuated neighbourhoods to prevent looting. The Spanish government has activated emergency protocols and requested assistance from the European Union's civil protection mechanism.

The economic toll is already mounting. Vineyards in Bordeaux, some centuries old, have been damaged or destroyed. Farmers in both nations are counting losses as livestock and crops are consumed by the flames. Insurers have warned that claims will run into hundreds of millions of euros.

Environmental groups are pointing to the fires as a stark example of climate change. Prolonged droughts and record-breaking heatwaves have made southern Europe increasingly prone to such disasters. Scientists say fire seasons are starting earlier and ending later than they did a generation ago.

The coming days will test whether the current lull in weather conditions can hold. Firefighters remain on high alert, bracing for the return of extreme heat that could fan the flames once again.
