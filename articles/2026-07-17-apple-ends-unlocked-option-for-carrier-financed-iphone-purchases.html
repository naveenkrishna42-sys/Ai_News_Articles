Apple Ends Unlocked Option for Carrier-Financed iPhone Purchases

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • iPhones bought via carrier financing are now locked to that carrier at purchase.
  • Previously, buyers could select an unlocked model even when using a carrier payment plan.
  • The change affects T-Mobile, Verizon, and other US carriers using installment plans.

Apple has stopped allowing customers to purchase unlocked iPhones when they use carrier financing. The change closes a loophole that let buyers sidestep network restrictions while still paying in installments.

Previously, shoppers could select the 'unlocked' model when checking out on Apple's website, even if they chose a carrier payment plan. That option is now gone. Carrier-financed iPhones are locked to the carrier from the moment of purchase.

What the Policy Shift Means

For customers who buy iPhones through T-Mobile, Verizon, AT&T, or other carriers using monthly installment plans, the device will remain tied to that network until the loan is paid off. Apple's storefront now reflects this restriction.

The policy applies to all carrier-financed purchases made through Apple. Buyers who want an unlocked phone must now pay the full upfront cost or choose a carrier that offers early unlock terms.

Why Apple Made the Change

Carriers have long required that financed devices stay locked to prevent customers from switching networks before completing payments. The old loophole allowed users to bypass this by selecting the unlocked option, effectively getting a carrier-subsidized phone without the lock.

Apple's move aligns its store policy with carrier requirements. The company has not released an official statement explaining the decision, but the change removes a workaround that carriers had flagged as problematic.

Impact on T-Mobile and Verizon Customers

T-Mobile and Verizon buyers were among those who frequently used the loophole. Both carriers offer device financing plans, and customers could previously request an unlocked model at checkout. Now, that workaround is closed.

AT&T already locked its financed devices, so the policy shift primarily affects the other two major US carriers. Smaller carriers that use Apple's financing system will also see their devices locked.

Customers who already purchased unlocked iPhones through financing before the change are not affected. The new policy applies to purchases made after the update went live.

What About International Buyers?

The policy change is currently limited to the United States. International markets where Apple sells carrier-financed iPhones may see similar updates in the future, but the company has not announced any plans.

In countries where carrier locking is regulated differently, the rules may vary. Buyers outside the US should check local policies before purchasing.

What to Watch

Apple's move brings its online store in line with physical retail practices, where carrier-financed phones are already locked. The shift may push more customers toward upfront payments or third-party retailers that offer unlocked devices.

Industry observers expect carriers to maintain their current lock policies. For buyers who want flexibility, the only remaining option is to pay full price or wait until the installment plan is completed.
