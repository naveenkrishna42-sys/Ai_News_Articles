Faintest planet ever spotted from Earth discovered after decade-long search

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • The planet is the faintest ever directly imaged from Earth, discovered using NASA's Webb Telescope.
  • It orbits a young star and had been hiding in plain sight for over a decade.
  • The discovery was reported by Nature, The Hindu, and other outlets on July 21, 2026.

Astronomers have detected the faintest planet ever directly spotted from Earth, a discovery that ended a decade-long search around a young star. The planet, previously hidden in the star's glare, was finally revealed using NASA's James Webb Space Telescope.

Researchers reported the finding on Tuesday, July 21, 2026, in a study published in Nature. The planet orbits a relatively young star, though the star's exact age and distance remain under scientific discussion.

Hidden in plain sight for ten years

The planet had been elusive for over ten years. Earlier telescopes, including ground-based observatories, could not separate the planet's faint light from the bright glow of its host star. It was effectively hiding in plain sight.

The Webb Telescope's advanced infrared capabilities allowed scientists to finally spot the object. Its faintness sets a new record for direct imaging of exoplanets from Earth.

How Webb made the breakthrough

The discovery relied on Webb's coronagraph, an instrument that blocks out starlight to reveal dimmer nearby objects. This technique let the telescope capture the planet's thermal glow.

NASA's press release stated that the planet was found in a well-known star system. The system had been studied for years, but no instrument before Webb had the sensitivity to confirm the planet's presence.

What this means for exoplanet science

Direct imaging of exoplanets is rare. Most planets are detected indirectly through transits or gravitational wobbles. This find proves that Webb can spot planets that are far fainter than any seen before.

Scientists say the discovery opens a new window for studying young, cool planets. The planet's atmosphere and composition can now be analyzed, offering clues about how planetary systems evolve.

The Hindu reported that the planet is a "new world" revealed after a decade of hiding. The Times of India called it a "hidden planet" discovered by the Webb Telescope.

Officials have not yet confirmed the planet's size or mass. Early estimates suggest it is several times the mass of Jupiter, but more observations are needed.

Next steps for researchers

The team plans to conduct follow-up spectroscopy to study the planet's chemical makeup. This could reveal water, methane, or carbon dioxide in its atmosphere.

Astronomers also hope to search for more planets in the same system. The star's debris disk may hide additional worlds.

For now, the faintest planet ever seen from Earth has finally come to light.
