Samsung unveils AI smart glasses powered by Android XR

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Samsung's new smart glasses run on Google's Android XR operating system.
  • The glasses feature a 9-hour battery life and weigh under 50 grams.
  • They integrate with Samsung's Galaxy ecosystem, including phones and watches.
  • The launch positions Samsung to compete directly with Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses.
  • Partnerships with Gentle Monster and Warby Parker are confirmed for frame designs.

Samsung has officially entered the wearable AI space with the launch of its first smart glasses, powered by Google's Android XR platform. The announcement, made on Wednesday, confirms months of speculation about the company's plans to bring a full-fledged AR and AI experience to everyday eyewear.

The device is designed to work seamlessly within the Galaxy ecosystem, pairing with smartphones, watches, and other Samsung devices. Google's Android XR operating system serves as the backbone, enabling hands-free AI assistance, navigation, and real-time information overlays.

Battery Life and Design

One of the standout figures is the 9-hour battery life, a significant claim for a device packing sensors, cameras, and a constant connection to a phone. Samsung has not yet revealed the exact battery capacity, but the company states the glasses can last a full day of typical use.

The frames themselves are lightweight, reportedly under 50 grams. Samsung has partnered with Gentle Monster and Warby Parker to offer a variety of frame styles, moving away from the bulky, tech-heavy look of earlier smart glasses. The goal is to make them look like regular prescription or fashion eyewear.

Features and Integration

The glasses include built-in speakers, a microphone, and cameras for capturing photos and video. Users can issue voice commands through Google Assistant, get turn-by-turn directions projected into their field of view, and receive message notifications without pulling out a phone.

Deep integration with the Galaxy ecosystem means the glasses can pull data from a connected Galaxy Watch — showing heart rate or step count — and can trigger actions on a Galaxy phone, like starting a music playlist or sending a preset text reply. Samsung is also pushing third-party developer support through Android XR.

Meta has dominated the smart glasses market with its Ray-Ban Stories and newer AI-powered frames. Samsung's entry, with the backing of Google's software and its own hardware ecosystem, signals a direct challenge. The company has not yet revealed pricing or a specific launch date for markets outside of initial availability.

Analysts will be watching how Samsung handles privacy concerns. The presence of cameras and always-on microphones has previously drawn scrutiny. Samsung has stated that a physical shutter covers the camera lens and that a LED indicator lights up when recording, but full privacy policy details have not been released.

What happens next depends on Samsung's rollout speed and pricing. The company is expected to begin a limited release in South Korea and the United States before the end of the year, with a wider global launch in early 2027. Developer kits for Android XR are already being distributed, aiming to build an app ecosystem before the consumer push begins.
