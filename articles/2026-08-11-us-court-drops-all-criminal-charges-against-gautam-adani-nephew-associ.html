US court drops all criminal charges against Gautam Adani, nephew, associate

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • US District Court dismisses all criminal charges against Gautam Adani, Sagar Adani and Vneet Jaain
  • The case had alleged bribery and fraud related to solar power contracts
  • Gautam Adani said his faith in truth and law remains unwavering after the ruling

A US District Court has dismissed all criminal charges against industrialist Gautam Adani, his nephew Sagar Adani and associate Vneet Jaain, bringing a definitive end to a case that had cast a shadow over the Adani Group for years. The ruling, delivered on Monday, marks a significant legal victory for the trio, who were accused of involvement in an alleged bribery scheme tied to solar energy contracts in India.

The charges, originally filed in 2024, alleged that the Adani executives had paid bribes to Indian officials to secure favourable terms for power supply agreements. The case had triggered political controversy in India and rattled investor confidence in the conglomerate. Monday's dismissal, however, closes that chapter in the US legal system, with the judge's order effectively clearing the defendants of all counts.

A multi-year legal battle ends

The criminal case had been closely watched on both sides of the Atlantic. It stemmed from a US Securities and Exchange Commission investigation into alleged irregularities in a solar power project. The Adani Group consistently denied the allegations, calling them baseless. In a statement following the ruling, Gautam Adani said his “faith in truth and law remains unwavering”, echoing the group's long-standing position that the charges were without merit.

The dismissal comes after months of legal wrangling, during which the defence argued that the US court lacked jurisdiction over actions allegedly taken in India. The prosecution's case had also faced repeated setbacks, including evidentiary challenges and questions over witness credibility.

What the ruling means for the Adani Group

For the Adani Group, the verdict removes a major overhang that had weighed on its stock prices and international fundraising efforts. Shares of Adani Group companies are expected to rally in early trade, analysts said. The group has continued to expand its portfolio across ports, energy, and digital infrastructure despite the legal turbulence.

Legal experts note that while the criminal case is over, the group may still face civil proceedings or regulatory scrutiny in other jurisdictions. However, Monday's court order is a clean dismissal, not a settlement, which is a crucial distinction. It means the defendants were not required to pay fines or admit wrongdoing.

Political and corporate reactions

The ruling has drawn reactions from political leaders and business chambers in India, with several calling it a vindication of the Adani Group's position. Opposition parties, which had used the case to attack the government over its ties with the conglomerate, remained muted. The government itself made no official comment.

Internationally, the case had become a flashpoint in discussions about foreign investment in India's renewable energy sector. The dismissal may help restore confidence among global investors, who had become cautious after the allegations surfaced.

What happens next

While the criminal charges are gone, the Adani Group still faces separate civil lawsuits in the US filed by investor groups. Those cases, which allege securities fraud, are unlikely to be affected by Monday's criminal dismissal. The group has said it will defend those cases vigorously.

For Gautam Adani, the ruling clears the way for him to focus on the group's ambitious expansion plans, including a $100-billion investment in green energy over the next decade. The coming weeks will show whether the legal relief translates into renewed momentum for the conglomerate's projects and market performance.
