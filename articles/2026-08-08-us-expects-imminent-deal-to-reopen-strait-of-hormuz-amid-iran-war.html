US expects imminent deal to reopen Strait of Hormuz amid Iran war

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • US expects a deal soon on the Strait of Hormuz, a critical oil shipping route.
  • Trump says the war with Iran will end 'pretty soon'.
  • Israeli strikes in Lebanon have killed over 4,000 since March, per Health Ministry.
  • Analysts suggest Trump may need to compromise with Iran to reopen the strait.

Diplomatic push for Hormuz

The United States has indicated that an agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz could be reached soon, even as the conflict in West Asia continues to exact a heavy toll. A senior US official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said talks have advanced significantly in recent days, though no formal deal has been signed. The strait, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes, has been a flashpoint since the outbreak of hostilities.

President Donald Trump, in a statement to reporters, said he believes the war with Iran will end 'pretty soon'. His remarks come amid mounting international pressure to de-escalate, with global energy markets still reeling from supply disruptions. The White House has not detailed the contours of a potential agreement, but sources suggest maritime security guarantees are central to the discussions.

Regional toll mounts

Meanwhile, the human cost of the wider conflict shows no sign of abating. Lebanon's Health Ministry reported that Israeli strikes have killed more than 4,000 people in the country since March. The figure, which includes civilians, underscores the expanding scope of a war that began with focused exchanges but has since drawn in multiple fronts.

In Tehran, official media have struck a defiant tone, insisting that any deal must respect Iran's sovereign rights over its territorial waters. Analysts note that Iran's primary leverage remains its ability to disrupt shipping, a tactic it has employed effectively to force negotiations. The Iranian foreign ministry has yet to comment publicly on the reported US expectations.

A compromise in the making

Observers in New Delhi and Washington point to a possible trade-off: Iran may agree to free passage for oil tankers in exchange for relief from certain sanctions. India Today reported that Trump may need to offer compromises to Tehran to secure a reopening of the strait, a move that could face resistance from hawks in his administration and from Gulf allies wary of Iranian concessions.

For India, the stakes are particularly high. A prolonged closure of the strait would disrupt crude imports, a significant share of which transit this route. The government has been in touch with both US and Iranian officials, urging restraint and a speedy resolution. However, official channels have remained tight-lipped about any Indian role in the mediation efforts.

What lies ahead

As diplomatic channels buzz with activity, the immediate focus is on whether a formal announcement will materialise in the coming days. The US has not set a public deadline, but the phrase 'pretty soon' from the President suggests urgency. Meanwhile, the humanitarian situation in Lebanon and parts of Iran continues to deteriorate, with aid agencies reporting critical shortages of medical supplies.

The coming week could prove decisive. If a deal is struck, it would mark a significant de-escalation, though challenges remain in implementing any agreement on the ground. If talks falter, the risk of further escalation—and more casualties—looms large. For now, the world watches the strait, and the negotiators, with cautious hope.
