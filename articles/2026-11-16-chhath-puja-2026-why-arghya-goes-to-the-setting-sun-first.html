Chhath Puja 2026: Dates and the Four-Day Sequence

Chhath Puja is observed on Kartik Shukla Shashthi and is the only major Hindu festival where the setting sun receives worship before the rising one. In 2026, the four-day vrat runs from November 12 to November 15, with Sandhya Arghya on Saturday, November 14 and Usha Arghya the following dawn.

The festival is centred on Surya and Chhathi Maiya, identified with Shashthi Devi, the guardian of progeny

Significance of the Ancient Sun Worship

Chhath Puja is a festival deeply rooted in ancient Vedic traditions, primarily dedicated to Surya Dev (the Sun God) and Chhathi Maiya (Goddess Shashthi). It is unique among Hindu festivals for its rigorous abstinence and the worship of both the setting and rising sun. Devotees, known as Vratis, undertake a 36-hour fast without water, observing strict purity and discipline. The central belief is that worshipping the Sun God bestows health, wealth, and prosperity, while Chhathi Maiya blesses families with progeny and protects children. The festival is a profound expression of gratitude to the sun for sustaining life on Earth and is celebrated with immense devotion, particularly in the Indian states of Bihar, Jharkhand, Uttar Pradesh, and parts of Nepal, with its observance now spreading globally among the diaspora.

The Four-Day Observance: A Detailed Analysis

Each day of Chhath Puja holds specific rituals and spiritual significance, guiding devotees through a journey of purification, devotion, and gratitude.

Day 1: Nahay Khay (November 12, 2026, Thursday)

The festival commences with 'Nahay Khay', meaning 'bath and eat'. On this day, Vratis take a holy dip, traditionally in a river or pond, to purify themselves. The entire house is cleaned thoroughly. Only satvik (pure and vegetarian) food is prepared and consumed once after the bath. The most common meal is boiled rice (bhat) with bottle gourd (lauki) sabzi and chana dal. This marks the beginning of the strict regimen of purity and abstinence that will continue for the next three days.

Day 2: Lohanda and Kharna (November 13, 2026, Friday)

The second day is known as 'Lohanda' or 'Kharna'. Vratis observe a full-day fast without water. In the evening, after sunset, they prepare and consume 'kheer' (rice pudding) made with jaggery and 'roti' (chapatis). This meal is offered to Chhathi Maiya before the Vrati partakes in it. Following this meal, the Vrati begins the rigorous 36-hour nirjala (without water) fast, which continues until the following morning's offerings.

Day 3: Sandhya Arghya (November 14, 2026, Saturday)

This is the most significant day of Chhath Puja, dedicated to offering prayers to the setting sun. Vratis, still observing the waterless fast, prepare a variety of offerings known as 'prasad'. These include 'thekua' (a traditional sweet made from wheat flour and jaggery), seasonal fruits, sugarcane, sweet lime, and other homemade delicacies. These items are meticulously arranged in a 'soop' (winnowing basket) and a 'daura' (bamboo basket). In the evening, devotees gather at riverbanks, ponds, or makeshift water bodies, where they stand in the water and offer 'arghya' (oblation) to the setting sun, praying for health, prosperity, and the well-being of their families. The atmosphere is charged with devotion, hymns, and folk songs dedicated to Chhathi Maiya and Surya Dev.

Day 4: Usha Arghya and Parana (November 15, 2026, Sunday)

The final day begins before sunrise. Vratis, along with their families, return to the same bathing ghats. They stand in the water once again, eagerly awaiting the first rays of the rising sun. As the sun appears on the horizon, 'arghya' is offered to the rising sun, marking the conclusion of the main rituals. After the 'Usha Arghya', devotees break their 36-hour fast by consuming the 'prasad' and sharing it with family and friends. This act of breaking the fast is known as 'Parana'. The festival concludes with blessings sought from elders and the distribution of prasad, symbolizing sharing and community bonding.
