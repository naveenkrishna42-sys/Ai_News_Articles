Trump rejects Iran's compensation demand, insists Tehran pay for war deaths

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump rejected Iran's compensation demand in the ongoing US-Iran conflict.
  • He insisted Tehran must pay for 'all the people they killed' and those injured.
  • The issue is expected to be raised in future talks, though Hormuz deal hopes are fading.

US President Donald Trump has dismissed Iran's demand for compensation over the recent war, turning the tables on Tehran by insisting that it is Iran that must pay for the lives lost and people injured during the conflict. His remarks, made public on Monday, came as diplomatic efforts to salvage a deal around the Strait of Hormuz appeared to be slipping away.

Trump's blunt response to Tehran's demand

Speaking to reporters, Trump rejected the Iranian government's claim for war damages outright. Instead, he asserted that Iran should compensate for the casualties its actions have caused. "They want compensation? They should pay us for all the people they killed," he said, according to remarks carried by international news agencies.

The President's statement marks a sharp escalation in rhetoric, as both sides continue to trade blame over the human cost of the conflict. While the exact figures of casualties remain unconfirmed, the toll has been a central point of contention in the ongoing standoff.

Hormuz deal hopes fade

The demand comes at a time when hopes for a negotiated settlement over the Strait of Hormuz—a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments—are dwindling. Earlier reports had suggested that both Washington and Tehran were exploring avenues for dialogue, but the latest exchange has cast a shadow over those prospects.

According to sources familiar with the matter, the compensation issue is now expected to be a key sticking point in any future talks. Trump has indicated that he plans to raise the matter in upcoming discussions, though no formal schedule for negotiations has been announced.

A war of numbers and blame

The dispute over compensation is not merely financial—it has become a proxy for assigning responsibility for the war itself. Tehran has framed its demand as reparations for damage inflicted on Iranian soil, while Washington argues that Iran's own aggression triggered the conflict.

Legal experts note that international law on war reparations is murky, especially when the conflict's origins are disputed. Neither side has yet provided a detailed breakdown of the costs they claim, and independent verification has been difficult amid the ongoing hostilities.

What this means for the region

The hardening of positions on both sides raises the risk of further instability in the Gulf region. Oil markets have been volatile in recent weeks, with traders closely watching every development around the Strait of Hormuz. A prolonged impasse could have ripple effects on global energy prices.

Meanwhile, humanitarian concerns continue to mount. International organisations have repeatedly called for a ceasefire to allow aid to reach affected civilians, but so far, diplomatic efforts have yielded little progress.

As the war of words intensifies, the focus now shifts to whether the two sides can find any common ground. With Trump insisting on compensation from Iran and Tehran unwilling to back down, the path to de-escalation appears narrow. Observers will be watching for any signs of a shift in tone in the coming days, especially as the issue is expected to be raised in future diplomatic engagements.
