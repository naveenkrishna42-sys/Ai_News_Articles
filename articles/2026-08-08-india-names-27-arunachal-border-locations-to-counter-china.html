India names 27 Arunachal border locations to counter China

📅 2026-08-08 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • India names 27 locations in Arunachal Pradesh, including 21 land areas, four passes, and a lake.
  • The move counters China's attempts to assign Mandarin names to Indian territory.
  • Official mapping aims to assert Indian sovereignty over the region.

New Delhi: India pushes back with official names

The Indian government has officially mapped and standardised names for 27 locations along the Arunachal Pradesh border, a direct counter to China's repeated attempts to assign Mandarin names to the same areas. The announcement was made on Friday, marking a significant step in the ongoing border nomenclature tussle.

Of the 27 sites, 21 are land areas, four are passes, and one is a lake. The move is seen as a firm assertion of India's sovereignty over the region, which China claims as part of its own territory.

Naming the frontier

The newly standardised names cover a mix of strategic and geographic features. The four passes and the lake are among the most significant, as they are often used as reference points in border discussions. Officials said the naming exercise was aimed at ensuring uniformity in official records, maps, and communications.

This is not the first time India has taken such a step. In previous years, the government has renamed or standardised names of border features to reinforce its position. The latest move comes amid heightened sensitivity over China's use of Mandarin names for locations inside Indian territory.

A counter to Chinese renaming

China has been actively assigning Mandarin names to places in Arunachal Pradesh, which it refers to as Zangnan. These names have appeared on Chinese maps and in official statements, drawing sharp reactions from New Delhi. By formally mapping these 27 locations with standard Indian names, the government aims to create an unambiguous record that can be used in diplomatic and administrative contexts.

The Ministry of External Affairs and the Survey of India are believed to have coordinated the exercise. However, officials have not yet disclosed the full list of names or the criteria used for selection.

Strategic and symbolic significance

The naming of border locations is both a strategic and symbolic move. It sends a clear message that India will not cede ground—literally or figuratively—on its territorial claims. The four passes, in particular, hold tactical importance as they are potential routes for movement along the frontier.

The lake, though unnamed in the official release, is likely to be a well-known water body that has featured in earlier boundary disputes. Standardising its name helps in avoiding confusion in future negotiations.

What this means for border diplomacy

India and China have been locked in a border standoff in several sectors of the Line of Actual Control (LAC) since 2020. While military-level talks have been ongoing, the nomenclature issue adds another layer to the dispute. By formalising these names, India is essentially creating a counter-narrative to China's claims.

Observers note that such naming exercises are part of a broader strategy to assert sovereignty without escalating military tensions. It is a quiet but firm response, one that is likely to be noticed in Beijing.

Reactions and next steps

There has been no official reaction from China so far. In India, the move has been welcomed by strategic analysts and political leaders who see it as a necessary step. The government has not indicated whether more locations will be named in the future, but it is expected that the exercise will continue as part of regular mapping updates.

As the border dispute remains unresolved, such measures are likely to persist. The naming of these 27 locations is a reminder that the contest over the region is not just about territory, but also about the very names on the map.

Going forward, the focus will be on how China responds and whether this leads to further exchanges in the ongoing border dialogue. For now, India has made its position clear—one name at a time.
