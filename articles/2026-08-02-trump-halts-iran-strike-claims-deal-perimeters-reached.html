Trump halts Iran strike, claims 'deal perimeters' reached

📅 2026-08-02 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump cancelled an Iran strike, citing 'deal perimeters' reached
  • Netanyahu holds off new strikes as diplomatic efforts intensify
  • Iranian media denies Trump's claim that Tehran asked US not to strike

Strike called off at the eleventh hour

US President Donald Trump has called off a planned military strike on Iran, saying that the 'perimeters' of a deal to end the conflict have been reached. The announcement came late on Saturday, sending ripples through West Asia and Washington alike.

According to reports, the strike was poised to target Iranian nuclear facilities in response to recent escalations. However, in a sudden reversal, Trump indicated that diplomatic channels had opened up 'rapidly', making the use of force unnecessary for now.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who had earlier pushed for a tougher US response, has also agreed to hold off on new strikes, aligning with Washington's shift towards negotiations.

What 'deal perimeters' mean

The phrase 'deal perimeters' is deliberately vague, leaving analysts guessing about the scope of any potential agreement. Trump's statement suggests that both sides have agreed on broad frameworks, but specifics—such as uranium enrichment levels, sanctions relief, and regional security guarantees—remain unclear.

Officials in Washington have not yet confirmed the details of any proposed deal, nor have they offered a timeline for formal talks. The White House has only said that negotiations are 'moving faster than expected'.

For Tehran, the prospect of a deal would bring much-needed economic relief, though hardliners within Iran's establishment remain sceptical of US intentions.

Diverging narratives from Tehran

Iranian state media has pushed back against Trump's claim that Tehran asked Washington not to strike. In a sharp rebuttal, Iranian outlets have denied any such request, framing the US climbdown as a victory for Iran's deterrence posture.

This divergence in narratives underscores the fragile trust between the two countries. While Trump projects an image of a deal within reach, Iran's leadership appears keen to avoid any perception of weakness at home.

The Islamic Republic has long insisted that it will not negotiate under the threat of military action. Whether this latest pause in hostilities leads to genuine talks or merely a tactical breather remains an open question.

Regional and global implications

The cancellation of the strike has been met with cautious relief across the Gulf, where an escalation would have risked drawing in multiple players. Oil markets, which had been bracing for supply disruptions, are likely to respond positively to the news.

European powers, including France and Germany, have been quietly pushing for de-escalation and are expected to back any renewed diplomatic push. The UN's nuclear watchdog, the IAEA, has also signalled its readiness to resume verification activities if a framework is agreed upon.

However, sceptics point out that past attempts at engagement have collapsed over verification and enforcement issues. The coming weeks will test whether both sides can translate 'perimeters' into a concrete, verifiable agreement.

What to watch next

All eyes are now on the White House and Tehran for any official confirmation of talks. A formal announcement of a negotiation schedule would mark a significant shift from the brinkmanship of recent days.

For now, the world waits to see if this unexpected pause becomes a permanent ceasefire or just a prelude to another round of confrontation. The stakes, as always in West Asia, could not be higher.
