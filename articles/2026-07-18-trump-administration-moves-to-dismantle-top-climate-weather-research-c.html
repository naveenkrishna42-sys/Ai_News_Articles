Trump administration moves to dismantle top climate, weather research centre

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Environment Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Trump administration has begun dismantling a major climate and weather research centre.
  • The move has raised alarms among scientists and environmental advocates.
  • Details on the full scope of the dismantling remain unclear.

Administration Targets Key Research Hub

The Trump administration has initiated steps to dismantle a prominent climate and weather research centre, according to a report by the Los Angeles Times. The move, confirmed by sources familiar with the matter, is part of a broader push to scale back federal involvement in climate science.

The centre, which has been a cornerstone for atmospheric studies, provides critical data used by meteorologists, farmers, and disaster management agencies across the country. Its work also supports global climate models that inform policy decisions.

Impact on Research and Operations

Staff at the centre have been notified of impending cuts, with some programs facing immediate suspension. Scientists involved in long-term climate monitoring projects have expressed deep concern over the loss of continuity in data collection.

Without this data, experts warn, the ability to predict extreme weather events like hurricanes, heatwaves, and floods could be severely compromised. The dismantling could also affect international collaborations, as the centre shares its findings with agencies worldwide.

Broader Context of Environmental Policy

This decision aligns with the administration's previous actions to withdraw from international climate agreements and roll back environmental regulations. Critics argue that dismantling the centre undermines scientific progress and public safety.

Supporters of the move, however, contend that it reduces government overreach and redirects resources to other priorities. The administration has not yet detailed alternative plans for weather monitoring or climate research.

Reactions from the Scientific Community

Leading scientists and environmental groups have condemned the move, calling it a short-sighted decision that will have long-term consequences. The American Meteorological Society issued a statement urging the government to reconsider.

“This is a direct attack on our ability to understand and respond to climate change,” said a senior researcher at the centre, speaking on condition of anonymity. The researcher added that many employees are now considering leaving the field altogether.

Lawmakers from both parties have also weighed in, with some demanding a full explanation from the administration. A hearing on the matter is expected to be scheduled in the coming weeks.

What Comes Next

As the dismantling process begins, the scientific community is mobilising to push back. Petitions and legal challenges are being drafted, though their success remains uncertain. The world will be watching to see if any of the centre’s core functions can be preserved or if this marks the end of an era in climate research.
