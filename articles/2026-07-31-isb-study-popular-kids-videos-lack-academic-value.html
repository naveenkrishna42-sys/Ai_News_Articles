ISB study: popular kids' videos lack academic value

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • ISB research analyzed popular children's videos and found limited academic value.
  • Study highlights a gap between entertainment and educational content for kids.
  • Experts suggest parents and platforms need to focus on learning outcomes.

Hyderabad: A new study by the Indian School of Business (ISB) has found that many videos popular among children fail to provide meaningful academic value. The research, which analyzed a broad sample of content watched by young audiences, suggests that while these videos are engaging, they do little to support learning objectives.

The findings have sparked a conversation among educators and parents about the role of digital media in early childhood development. With screen time rising across Indian households, the study raises questions about what children are actually absorbing from the content they consume.

What the study found

The ISB research team examined videos that rank among the most-viewed by children on popular streaming platforms. Their analysis focused on elements such as vocabulary richness, problem-solving scenarios, and educational messaging. The results indicate that the majority of these videos prioritize entertainment over instruction, with few incorporating structured learning elements.

Researchers noted that while some videos include basic counting or alphabet recitation, the depth and consistency of educational content remain shallow. This, they argue, limits the potential for these videos to contribute to cognitive development in a measurable way.

Implications for parents

For parents, the study serves as a reminder to look beyond view counts and popularity metrics. Many assume that a video's widespread appeal translates to learning value, but the ISB findings suggest otherwise. The researchers advise caregivers to actively curate content that aligns with specific educational goals.

Pediatric experts and child development specialists have echoed this concern, noting that passive viewing of high-stimulation videos may reduce opportunities for interactive learning. The study's authors recommend that parents supplement screen time with activities that encourage questioning and creativity.

What platforms can do

The study also turns the spotlight on streaming services and content creators. With millions of children engaging with these platforms daily, there is a clear opportunity to integrate educational design without compromising entertainment value. The ISB team suggests that platforms could adopt content rating systems that highlight educational merit.

Creators, too, could collaborate with educators to develop material that is both fun and instructive. Some industry observers believe this could become a differentiator in a crowded market, as parents become more discerning about what their children watch.

Broader context

This is not the first time concerns have been raised about children's digital content. Earlier studies have pointed to issues like shortened attention spans and reduced physical activity linked to excessive screen time. However, the ISB study adds a new dimension by focusing specifically on the academic utility of popular videos.

The research arrives at a time when digital learning tools are being integrated into classrooms across India. As schools adopt hybrid models, the quality of at-home screen content becomes increasingly relevant to a child's educational journey.

Looking ahead

The ISB team plans to expand its research to include regional language content and different age groups. Future findings could offer more nuanced guidance for parents and policymakers. For now, the message is clear: popularity does not equal pedagogy.

As the debate continues, parents are encouraged to review their children's watchlists with a critical eye. The next wave of children's programming may need to find a better balance between delight and learning.
