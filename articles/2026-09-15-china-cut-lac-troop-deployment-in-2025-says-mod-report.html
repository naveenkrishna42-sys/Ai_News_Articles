China Reduces LAC Troop Deployment: What the MoD Report Reveals

The Ministry of Defence's annual report for 2025-26 has confirmed a net reduction in Chinese troop deployment along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) during 2025. This marks the first verified drawdown since the 2020 Galwan clash, signalling a cautious de-escalation after the October 2024 patrolling agreement covering Depsang and Demchok. The report, tabled in Parliament, attributes the reduction to sustained diplomatic and military-level talks, but cautions that infrastructure build-up on both sides continues at an accelerated pace.

Verified Data: What Changed on the Ground

According to the MoD, Chinese troop levels at key friction points have decreased by an estimated 15-20% compared to peak 2023-24 deployments. However, the report does not specify exact numbers, citing operational sensitivity. India, meanwhile, has retained its enhanced winter logistics and surveillance grid, including newly inducted high-altitude drones and all-terrain vehicles. The table below summarises the key metrics from the report and independent monitoring.

Real-World Implications for India's Defence Posture

The drawdown reduces immediate collision risk but does not alter India's strategic calculus. The Indian Army continues to maintain two additional divisions in the eastern sector and has accelerated construction of 47 strategic roads along the LAC. For defence buyers, this means sustained demand for extreme cold-weather gear, unmanned surveillance systems, and rapid mobility platforms. The MoD's capital outlay for 2025-26 includes ₹1.72 lakh crore for modernisation, with a significant portion earmarked for high-altitude equipment.

Pros & Cons of the Current De-escalation

  • Pros: Reduced risk of accidental clashes; lower immediate logistical burden; improved diplomatic atmosphere for trade and border talks.
  • Cons: China's infrastructure build-up continues; troop reduction is reversible; no permanent disengagement agreement; India must maintain high readiness at significant cost.
  • Key insight: De-escalation is tactical, not strategic. India's long-term posture remains defensive but robust.

What This Means for Defence Procurement and Budgeting

With Chinese troop levels lower but infrastructure expanding, India's procurement priorities are shifting toward persistent surveillance and rapid response. Expect increased orders for high-altitude drones (like the indigenous Tapas and Archer), all-terrain vehicles, and modular shelters. The ₹6.81 lakh crore defence budget for 2025-26 reflects this, with a 9.5% increase over the previous year. Buyers in the defence sector should note that winterisation kits and satellite communication devices remain high-demand items.

Expert Take: Caution Amid Optimism

Strategic affairs experts caution that the reduction is a positive but limited step. "The MoD report confirms a tactical adjustment, not a strategic retreat," said a retired Lt Gen who served in the eastern sector. "India must continue to invest in infrastructure and technology to ensure any future incursion is detected and deterred." The report also highlights that the Indian Air Force has increased sorties along the LAC by 12% in 2025, underscoring the need for constant vigilance.

Bottom Line

The MoD's 2025-26 report confirms a modest Chinese troop reduction along the LAC, a welcome de-escalation after years of tension. However, India's defence establishment remains on high alert, with procurement focused on high-altitude capabilities. For observers and buyers, the message is clear: peace is maintained through preparedness, not just agreements. The coming year will test whether this drawdown becomes a trend or remains an isolated adjustment.
