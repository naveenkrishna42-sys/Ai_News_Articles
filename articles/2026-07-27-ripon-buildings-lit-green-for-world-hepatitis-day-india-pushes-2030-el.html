Ripon Buildings Lit Green for World Hepatitis Day, India Pushes 2030 Elimination Goal

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ripon Buildings in Kolkata illuminated green to mark World Hepatitis Day.
  • PIB India highlights efforts to eliminate hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030.
  • PILBS, Mohali to hold a liver health camp coinciding with the awareness drive.
  • Experts stress early therapy as many Indians lack symptoms until the disease is advanced.

Kolkata's iconic Ripon Building, the seat of the city's municipal corporation, glowed in green light on Monday evening to mark World Hepatitis Day. The illumination is part of a broader awareness push by health authorities across the country.

A Landmark Turns Advocate

The British-era building, usually seen in its original white facade, was bathed in green to symbolise hope and the global fight against hepatitis. The gesture aligns with similar light-up campaigns at monuments worldwide, urging people to get tested and vaccinated.

Local officials said the visual spectacle was meant to grab attention and start conversations. "When a landmark changes colour, people stop and ask why. That is the first step towards awareness," a Kolkata Municipal Corporation spokesperson noted.

India's Push for a Hepatitis-Free 2030

The illumination coincides with the Press Information Bureau's renewed call to accelerate India's fight against hepatitis. The government has set a target to eliminate the disease as a public health threat by 2030, aligning with World Health Organization goals.

PIB India's statement highlighted stepped-up screening drives, free treatment programmes under the National Viral Hepatitis Control Program, and a focus on high-burden states. Health ministry data shows an estimated 4 crore Indians carry the hepatitis B or C virus, with many unaware of their status.

Liver Health Camp in Mohali

Adding to the day's initiatives, the Postgraduate Institute of Liver and Biliary Sciences in Mohali announced a liver health camp. The camp will offer free consultations, rapid testing for hepatitis B and C, and vaccinations. Officials expect high turnout as word spreads through local community health workers.

The camp is part of a series of such events planned across Punjab and neighbouring states over the next month. "Early detection saves lives. A simple blood test can make the difference between a manageable condition and liver failure," a PILBS doctor said.

The Silent Threat: Why Early Therapy Matters

Medical experts point to a worrying trend: many Indian patients show no symptoms until the liver is severely damaged. A recent case reported by The Indian Express illustrated this starkly — a man with no signs of hepatitis B was diagnosed only after routine screening, by which point the infection had already caused significant damage.

Doctors stress that hepatitis B and C can remain dormant for years while silently scarring the liver. "By the time jaundice or fatigue appear, the disease is often advanced. That is why we need routine screening, especially for high-risk groups," said a hepatologist based in New Delhi. The call for early therapy is central to India's elimination roadmap. Antiviral drugs can suppress hepatitis B and cure hepatitis C in most cases, but only if caught in time.

Officials have not yet confirmed the number of people screened or vaccinated during Monday's events. Detailed reports from states are expected in the coming weeks.

As the green lights on Ripon Buildings fade for the night, health workers gear up for the next phase: sustained outreach in villages and towns where awareness remains low. The next milestone on the calendar is the liver health camp in Mohali, where hundreds may learn their status for the first time.
