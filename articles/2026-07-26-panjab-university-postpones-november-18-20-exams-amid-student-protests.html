Panjab University Postpones November 18-20 Exams Amid Student Protests

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Panjab University postponed exams from November 18-20 due to student protests.
  • Students are protesting against the exam schedule and recent fee increases.
  • University officials said new dates will be announced soon.
  • Protests have been ongoing for several days on the campus.

Exams Postponed After Student Agitation

Panjab University has postponed all examinations scheduled for November 18, 19, and 20 following days of student protests on its Chandigarh campus. The decision was announced on Sunday evening by the university's examination department.

Students have been agitating against the current exam schedule, which they say was released without adequate notice. Many also oppose a recent hike in examination and registration fees.

What Led to the Protests

The protests began late last week when student groups demanded a revision of the exam timetable. They claimed the schedule clashes with other academic commitments and leaves little time for preparation.

On Saturday, a large group of students gathered near the administrative block, raising slogans and blocking entry for several hours. University security personnel were deployed, but no violence was reported.

Some student leaders also submitted a memorandum to the vice-chancellor, listing their demands. These include a rollback of fee increases and a more transparent process for setting exam dates.

University's Response

In a brief statement, the university said the postponement was made "in the interest of students' welfare and to maintain peace on campus." It added that revised dates for the postponed exams will be announced within a week.

Officials have not said whether any further changes to the academic calendar are being considered. The university is expected to hold a meeting with student representatives on Monday to discuss the demands.

Broader Context

Panjab University, one of India's oldest and most prestigious institutions, has seen periodic student unrest over fee structures and administrative decisions in recent years. The current protests are part of a wider pattern of student activism on fee hikes across several Indian universities.

The university has around 20,000 students across its main campus and affiliated colleges. Many of them have been vocal on social media, using hashtags like #PUFeeHike and #ExamScheduleChange to amplify their demands.

Local political parties have also weighed in, with some supporting the students' cause. However, the university administration has stressed that it is committed to resolving the issue through dialogue.

What happens next will depend on Monday's meeting. If an agreement is reached, the university may announce new exam dates and a revised fee structure. If not, the protests could escalate in the coming days.
