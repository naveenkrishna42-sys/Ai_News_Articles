Man beaten to death in drunken brawl over missing phone; four held in Chennai

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Victim, 35, died after being assaulted with wooden logs and iron rods during a brawl over a missing phone
  • Four men arrested; all were reportedly intoxicated during the incident in Chennai's Vyasarpadi area
  • Police registered a case of murder and are investigating the sequence of events leading to the fatal assault

A 35-year-old man was beaten to death during a drunken brawl that erupted over a missing mobile phone in Chennai late Saturday night. Police have arrested four men in connection with the murder.

The incident took place in the Vyasarpadi area, a densely populated neighbourhood in north Chennai. According to officers, the victim and the accused were part of a group that had been drinking together when the argument began.

How the fight turned fatal

The victim, identified as S. Kumar, a daily-wage labourer, had been drinking with several others at a local spot. Around 11 pm, one of the men realised his phone was missing and accused Kumar of taking it.

What started as a heated exchange quickly escalated. The accused—reportedly in an inebriated state—picked up wooden logs and iron rods lying nearby and attacked Kumar. Neighbours who heard the commotion rushed to the scene but could not stop the assault.

By the time they pulled the men apart, Kumar had sustained severe injuries to his head and torso. He was rushed to the Government Stanley Medical College Hospital, where doctors declared him brought dead.

Arrests and investigation

Police from the Vyasarpadi station reached the spot after receiving a call from the hospital. Based on witness statements, they arrested four men in their 20s and 30s. All four were still under the influence of alcohol when taken into custody.

A case under Section 302 (murder) of the Indian Penal Code has been registered. The accused have been remanded to judicial custody. Investigators are verifying if the missing phone was ever recovered from the victim or if the accusation was baseless.

Police said they are also looking into whether any prior enmity existed between the victim and the accused. Preliminary inquiries suggest the group often drank together and that the argument was entirely spontaneous.

Rising concern over liquor-related violence

This is the third such fatal brawl reported in the city this month. Local residents in Vyasarpadi said open consumption of liquor in the area has been a persistent problem, with altercations turning violent frequently.

Community leaders have urged the police to increase patrolling and crack down on illegal liquor vends operating in the locality. Authorities have promised stricter enforcement, though no concrete measures have been announced yet.

The body of the victim has been sent for post-mortem. His family, which includes a wife and two young children, has demanded strict punishment for the accused.

The case is under investigation, and police are recording statements from more eyewitnesses. Further arrests have not been ruled out.
