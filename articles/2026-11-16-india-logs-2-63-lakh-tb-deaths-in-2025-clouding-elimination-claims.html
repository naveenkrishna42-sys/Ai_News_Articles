India's TB Death Toll Hits 2.63 Lakh as WHO Data Complicates Elimination Story

India recorded an estimated 2.63 lakh tuberculosis deaths in 2025, according to the latest World Health Organization assessment, a figure that sits awkwardly against official declarations of rapid progress toward the country's goal of eliminating the disease by 2025. The data forces a harder look at what "elimination" actually means and whether the aggressive timeline was ever clinically realistic.

The Gap Between Ambition and Outcome

India committed to eliminating TB by 2025, five years ahead of the global Sustainable Development Goal deadline of 2030. Elimination here is a technical benchmark, reducing incidence to 44 cases per lakh population and mortality to 3 per lakh, not the complete eradication of the bacterium.

Official communications highlight a 38% decline in TB mortality from the 2015 baseline and a treatment success rate of 94%. These are genuine gains. Yet 2.63 lakh annual deaths shows the absolute burden stays severe in a country of roughly 1.4 billion people.

What the WHO Report Actually Signals

The WHO's framing centers on financing sustainability. As external donor funding for TB programs contracts globally, domestic spending now accounts for 86% of India's TB response, per the report. This shift cuts both ways: it reflects growing national ownership but exposes programs to domestic fiscal pressure.

For health analysts tracking India's public health commitments, the funding mix matters. Heavy reliance on national budgets makes TB programs vulnerable to competing priorities during economic slowdowns or fiscal tightening.
