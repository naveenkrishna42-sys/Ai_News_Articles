Taapsee Pannu’s Gandhari: Plagiarism Claims and the Cost of Originality

📅 2026-08-26 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Writer Kalyan Bandi alleges Gandhari mirrors his 2019 script.
  • Kanika Dhillon’s Katha Pictures maintains the project is an original creative work.
  • Copyright disputes in Bollywood often lead to costly pre-release injunctions.

The Anatomy of a Legal Cloud

The Hindi film industry has long struggled with the murky boundaries of intellectual property. As Taapsee Pannu’s latest action-thriller Gandhari gears up for its streaming debut, the project has hit a familiar snag. Writer Kalyan Bandi has alleged that the film’s narrative structure bears a suspicious resemblance to his own work registered in 2019, triggering a copyright controversy that threatens to overshadow the film’s marketing campaign.

Kanika Dhillon’s Katha Pictures has firmly denied these claims, asserting the originality of the script. However, in the high-stakes world of digital-first releases, even a whiff of plagiarism can create significant friction with platform partners like Netflix. When a writer alleges theft, the legal burden shifts to proving independent creation, a process that is often time-consuming and expensive.

The Stakes of Script Ownership

At the core of this dispute is the vulnerability of independent writers versus established production houses. While the industry relies heavily on 'script doctors' and collaborative writing rooms, the documentation of story ideas remains a weak point. If a court finds merit in Bandi’s claims, the production house could face an injunction, causing a massive disruption to the release schedule and substantial financial loss.

Why Copyright Rows Are Trending

The surge in demand for gritty, high-concept thrillers has led to a saturation of similar tropes in Indian cinema. Writers often find that their 'unique' ideas are essentially thematic echoes of existing works, leading to inevitable clashes. This case is a stark reminder that registration alone does not prevent litigation; the burden of proof relies on the 'substantial similarity' test, which is notoriously difficult to arbitrate.

  • The Similarity Trap: Common themes in action-thrillers often blur the line between inspiration and infringement.
  • Documentation Gap: Many writers fail to secure ironclad contracts before pitching, leaving them vulnerable if a project is shelved and later resurrected by another team.
  • Reputational Risk: For a star like Taapsee Pannu, who often champions 'content-driven' cinema, being linked to a legal controversy can dilute the brand value of the performance.

Ultimately, the legal system will decide if the similarities are coincidental tropes or a direct derivation. For now, the production house must balance a aggressive legal defense with a PR strategy that keeps the audience focused on the trailer rather than the courtroom filings. The industry would benefit from a more transparent registry system that allows for easier verification of creative assets, reducing the frequency of these eleventh-hour standoffs.
