Overview of Market Movements on Oct 9 2026

On the morning of October 9, 2026, Indian equity markets opened with caution as the Gift Nifty futures indicated a modestly negative bias. The prior session had seen a pronounced selloff in US technology shares, which spilled over into Asian markets and weighed on sentiment for domestic IT stocks. Traders were also awaiting the forthcoming TCS Q2 FY27 results, expected later in the day, to gauge the health of the sector’s bellwether.

Gift Nifty Trends

The Gift Nifty, a proxy for the Nifty 50’s overseas trading, slipped around 0.3% in the pre‑open session, reflecting lingering risk‑off sentiment from Wall Street. This early movement often translates into a similar opening gap for the Nifty 50 on the NSE, setting the initial tone for intraday trading.

US Tech Selloff Influence

US technology indices fell sharply after disappointing earnings guidance from several mega‑cap firms, triggering a risk‑averse shift across global equity markets. In India, the Nifty IT index, which includes TCS, Infosys, and Wipro, opened lower, as investors reassessed growth expectations for the sector amid higher interest rates and moderating demand outlook.

TCS Q2 Results Outlook

Analysts anticipated TCS to report modest revenue growth driven by digital transformation deals, while margin pressure could persist due to wage hikes and rupee volatility. The outcome of this release was seen as a potential catalyst for either a rebound in IT stocks or further downside if guidance fell short of expectations.

Five Key Factors Driving Sensex and Nifty 50 on Oct 9

Beyond the immediate cues from Gift Nifty, US tech, and TCS, broader fundamentals are shaping the trajectory of India’s benchmark indices. The following five factors are considered the most influential for the session.

1. Global Interest Rate Outlook

The Fed’s stance remains the primary driver of global risk appetite. Any hint of prolonged higher rates tends to strengthen the dollar, press emerging market currencies, and lead to portfolio reallocation away from equities.

2. Domestic Inflation Trends

While India’s inflation has moderated from peak levels, sticky food and fuel prices keep the RBI cautious. A surprise uptick could prompt a policy pause, affecting market liquidity.

3. Corporate Earnings Season

Strong earnings beats, particularly from large‑cap exporters, can offset macro headwinds. Conversely, widespread misses may exacerbate selling pressure, especially in sectors with high valuations.

4. FII and DII Flows

FIIs have been net sellers in recent sessions due to global risk aversion, while DIIs have shown selective buying in value pockets. The net flow determines intraday momentum.

5. Geopolitical Tensions

Although currently a lower‑impact factor, any escalation in conflict zones can spike oil prices and trigger a flight‑to‑safety, indirectly affecting Indian markets via import costs and investor sentiment.

Pros and Cons of Current Market Setup

  • Pros: Potential for IT sector rebound if TCS delivers in‑line results; domestic consumption resilience supports non‑IT sectors.
  • Cons: Persistent global rate uncertainty may keep FII inflows muted; rupee volatility could erode earnings for export‑oriented firms.
