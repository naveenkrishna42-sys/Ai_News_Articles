Karnataka CM invites Tamil Nadu CM Vijay to inspect Cauvery dams by helicopter

📅 2026-07-30 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Karnataka CM invites Tamil Nadu CM Vijay for helicopter inspection of Cauvery dams.
  • Cauvery Water Management Authority upheld order for Karnataka to release water to Tamil Nadu.
  • Kannada activists in Mandya halted screening of Vijay's film 'Jana Nayagan' amid ongoing stir.

In a surprise move aimed at easing the long-running Cauvery water dispute, Karnataka Chief Minister has invited his Tamil Nadu counterpart Vijay for a joint helicopter inspection of key dams along the river. The offer comes days after the Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA) upheld an order directing Karnataka to release water to Tamil Nadu.

Helicopter tour offer

The Karnataka CM proposed that both leaders fly together over the Krishnaraja Sagar and Kabini dams to see the ground reality of water storage. Officials said the invitation is meant to build trust and show Tamil Nadu that Karnataka is not holding back water unnecessarily.

Vijay's office has not yet responded to the offer. The Tamil Nadu CM has been vocal about Karnataka failing to comply with the CWMA's directive to release 3,500 cusecs of water per day to Tamil Nadu's delta farmers.

Water sharing order upheld

The CWMA recently upheld the Cauvery Water Regulation Committee's order for Karnataka to release water to Tamil Nadu. This has put the Karnataka government under pressure from both the court and its own farming community.

Karnataka has argued that its reservoirs are at critically low levels and that releasing more water could jeopardise drinking water supplies in its own cities and towns. The CWMA, however, ruled that the distress-sharing formula must be followed as per the final award of the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal.

Mekedatu project setback

Adding to Karnataka's challenges, the Central Water Commission returned the state's revised detailed project report for the Mekedatu balancing reservoir. The CWC flagged significant changes in the revised plan, demanding fresh clarifications.

The Mekedatu project, proposed across the Cauvery in Ramanagara district, has been a flashpoint between the two states. Tamil Nadu opposes it, saying it would store water meant for its farmers. Karnataka insists the project is only for drinking water and power generation.

Protests in Mandya

On the ground, emotions remain high. Kannada activists in Mandya district intensified their stir against the water release. They halted the screening of Tamil Nadu CM Vijay's latest film 'Jana Nayagan' at a local theatre, forcing the management to cancel shows.

Mandya, in the heart of the Cauvery delta region in Karnataka, has been a traditional hub of pro-Kannada activism on the water issue. Protests have seen farmers block highways and hold rallies demanding the state government not bow to pressure from Tamil Nadu.

The Cauvery dispute, one of India's oldest inter-state river conflicts, has seen periodic flare-ups for decades. The Supreme Court and various tribunals have delivered multiple verdicts, but implementation remains contentious every summer when water levels drop.

What happens next depends on Vijay's response to the helicopter invitation. If accepted, it could mark a rare moment of direct dialogue between the two chief ministers on the issue. If declined, the matter will likely head back to the courts for another round of legal wrangling.
