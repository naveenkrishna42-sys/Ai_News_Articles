Student protests in India give opposition a rare opening against Modi

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Thousands of students are protesting across several Indian states over alleged exam paper leaks and lack of jobs.
  • The protests have given the opposition a rare opportunity to attack the Modi government on youth issues.
  • The government has denied the allegations and called the protests politically motivated.

Protests spread across multiple states

Thousands of students have taken to the streets in several Indian states over the past week, alleging irregularities in competitive examinations and demanding more government jobs. The protests, which began in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh, have now spread to Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and parts of the capital, Delhi.

Students are blocking highways and holding demonstrations outside government offices. They are demanding a probe into alleged paper leaks that they say have compromised the fairness of recruitment exams for teaching and other government posts.

Opposition sees an opening

The unrest has given India's opposition parties a rare political opening against Prime Minister Narendra Modi, whose government has dominated national politics for over a decade. Leaders from the Congress party and regional outfits have been quick to join the protests, accusing the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party of failing the youth.

Rahul Gandhi, a senior Congress leader, addressed a gathering of students in Patna on Monday, calling the situation a “national crisis.” Other opposition figures have used social media to amplify the students' demands, framing the issue as a failure of the Modi government's promise of job creation.

Government dismisses allegations

The central government has denied any systemic failure. In a statement, the Union Education Ministry said the examination process is robust and that isolated incidents of malpractice are dealt with strictly. Officials have also suggested that the protests are being orchestrated by political rivals to destabilise the government ahead of upcoming state elections.

“There is no proof of a large-scale leak. The opposition is trying to politicise a few isolated cases,” a ministry spokesperson said.

Unemployment remains a key concern

The protests come at a time when India's youth unemployment rate remains high. According to government data, the unemployment rate for those aged 15-29 was around 18% in early 2026, though independent surveys put it higher. The lack of formal jobs has been a persistent challenge for the Modi government, which has prioritised infrastructure and manufacturing growth.

Students say the exam paper leaks are just one symptom of a deeper problem. “We study hard, but there are no jobs. And even when we clear exams, we hear that papers were sold,” said a protester in Lucknow, who did not want to be named.

Political implications for Modi

The protests are an unusual challenge for Modi, who has maintained a strong grip on the political narrative since coming to power in 2014. His government has often defused opposition attacks by focusing on welfare schemes and nationalist rhetoric. But the student unrest directly touches on the economic anxieties of a large, young voting bloc.

Political analysts say the opposition has struggled for years to find a unifying issue. The student protests may offer that chance, but only if the opposition can sustain the momentum and avoid internal divisions.

What happens next will depend on how the government responds. If it agrees to a judicial inquiry or announces new job schemes, the protests may lose steam. But if it continues to dismiss the demands, the demonstrations could grow, providing the opposition with a longer political window.
