Gold prices climb as Iran conflict and rate hike jitters battle for market focus

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gold prices climbed as investors bought the dip amid escalating conflict in the Middle East.
  • Rising expectations of a Federal Reserve rate hike are curbing gold's safe-haven appeal.
  • Market participants are watching the $4,000 level to see if it holds as a floor in the coming week.

Gold prices edged higher on Friday as escalating conflict in the Middle East drew dip buyers back into the market, but a fresh wave of Federal Reserve rate hike expectations capped gains. The yellow metal is once again caught between its traditional safe-haven appeal and the headwinds of tighter monetary policy.

Iran war premium reignites

Renewed hostilities involving Iran have injected a fresh risk premium into gold. Investors who had stepped back from the rally in recent weeks saw the dip as an opportunity to re-enter, pushing prices upward. The geopolitical uncertainty continues to underpin demand, even as other forces pull in the opposite direction.

Analysts note that the market is pricing in a higher probability of a Fed rate increase to combat inflationary pressures tied to the conflict. A stronger dollar, driven by those expectations, typically weighs on gold. This tug-of-war has left the metal volatile.

Testing the $4,000 floor

The key question for traders now is whether the $4,000 per ounce mark can hold as a support level. Market participants are watching closely to see if that psychological barrier acts as a floor or if a deeper correction is in store. The coming week will be critical in determining the metal's near-term direction.

Some analysts believe that if the conflict intensifies, safe-haven buying could push gold higher. Others warn that if the Fed signals a more aggressive tightening cycle, even geopolitical premium may not be enough to sustain the rally.

Safe-haven demand vs. monetary policy

Gold's traditional role as a hedge against uncertainty is being tested by the reality of rising interest rates. When the Fed hikes rates, the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold increases. This dynamic has been a persistent theme throughout the year.

Data from the options market suggests that while bullish bets remain, there is also significant hedging against a potential pullback. This indicates that many investors are not fully convinced that the rally can continue unchecked. The balance between risk appetite and monetary tightening remains delicate.

What to watch

All eyes will be on next week's economic data and any Fed commentary that could shift rate expectations. A weaker-than-expected jobs report or inflation reading could ease rate hike fears and give gold room to run. Conversely, any signal of a firmer policy stance could test the $4,000 floor again.
