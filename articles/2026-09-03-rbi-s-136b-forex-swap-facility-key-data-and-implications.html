Record Forex Inflows Boost India's International Reserves

In 2026, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) witnessed a historic surge in foreign exchange inflows, with the forex swap facility drawing a staggering $136.38 billion. This marks a significant increase from previous years, reflecting India's growing appeal as an investment destination and the robust performance of its financial sector.

FCNR(B) Deposits: The Backbone of Forex Mobilisation

More than $127 billion was mobilised through Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) Deposits (FCNR(B)), a specialized deposit scheme designed to facilitate foreign exchange transactions. This scheme has been instrumental in enhancing India's international reserves and providing a stable platform for foreign investors.

Non-Resident Investments

Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) played a pivotal role in this surge, contributing $100 billion to the FCNR(B) scheme. This substantial investment not only boosted India's forex reserves but also provided a significant boost to the Indian economy.

Banking Units in IFSC

Banking units in the International Financial Services Centres (IFSC) in India disbursed $52 billion against FCNR(B) deposits. This indicates a robust and efficient financial ecosystem that supports foreign exchange transactions and investments.

ICICI Bank's Contribution

ICICI Bank, one of India's leading financial institutions, mobilised around ₹1.70 lakh crore via FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI swap facility. This underscores the bank's significant role in facilitating foreign exchange transactions and its commitment to supporting India's financial sector.

Economic Implications and Future Outlook

The record inflows and mobilisation of funds via FCNR(B) deposits have several economic implications. Firstly, it indicates a strong demand for Indian financial instruments, which can attract more foreign investments. Secondly, it provides a stable platform for foreign exchange transactions, which is crucial for India's economic stability and growth.

Looking ahead, these developments suggest that India's financial sector is well-positioned to continue attracting foreign investments and supporting economic growth. The robust performance of the FCNR(B) scheme and the forex swap facility indicates a strong and resilient financial ecosystem that can weather economic storms and support long-term growth.
