Sensex drops 400 pts, Nifty slips below 24,600 on global cues

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 400 points in early trade on Friday
  • Nifty slipped below 24,600 level
  • Selling pressure attributed to weak global cues and financial stocks

Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply lower on Friday, extending the previous session's losses, as weak global cues and selling in financial heavyweights dragged the market. The BSE Sensex slid over 400 points in early trade, while the Nifty 50 breached the 24,600 mark, reflecting cautious investor sentiment.

Market opens in red

The Sensex was trading at around 80,900 levels, down more than 400 points from its previous close. The Nifty, on the other hand, was hovering near 24,550, after opening below the psychological 24,600 level. The decline was broad-based, with banking, IT, and auto stocks leading the losses.

Market analysts attributed the fall to persistent selling by foreign institutional investors (FIIs) and weak global cues. Asian peers were trading mixed, with Japan's Nikkei and China's Shanghai Composite showing marginal declines, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng was flat.

Financial stocks weigh heavy

Financial stocks were the biggest drag on the benchmark indices, with major private sector lenders and public sector banks trading in the red. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India were among the top losers on the Sensex. The Nifty Bank index was down over 1 per cent in early trade.

IT stocks also witnessed selling pressure, with Infosys, TCS, and Wipro trading lower. The Nifty IT index fell nearly 0.8 per cent, as technology stocks globally remained under pressure amid concerns over slowing demand and rising interest rates.

Broader market under pressure

The broader market also witnessed selling, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices declining in tandem with the benchmarks. However, some defensive sectors such as FMCG and pharma showed relative resilience, offering limited support to the market.

Analysts said the market is likely to remain volatile in the near term, given the uncertainty over global interest rates and the upcoming quarterly earnings season. The rupee was also trading lower against the US dollar, adding to the negative sentiment.

What's next for investors

Investors will be closely watching the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy stance, as well as global cues, including US inflation data due later this month. Market participants are also keeping an eye on the movement of crude oil prices, which have remained elevated in recent weeks.

While the near-term outlook appears cautious, some analysts believe the correction offers a buying opportunity for long-term investors in quality stocks. The market's direction in the coming sessions will depend on how global factors evolve and whether domestic institutional buying can offset FII selling.
