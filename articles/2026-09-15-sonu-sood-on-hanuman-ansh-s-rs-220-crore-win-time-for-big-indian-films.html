Sonu Sood on Hanuman Ansh's Rs 220 Crore Win: 'Time for Big Indian Films'

  • The film's budget-to-return ratio makes it one of the most profitable Indian releases of 2026, with strong overseas and dubbed-version revenue.
  • Sood argues the win signals a permanent shift in how studios should fund mythological and large-format Indian cinema.

Sonu Sood Weighs In on a Box Office Earthquake

Sonu Sood has reacted to the extraordinary commercial run of Hanuman Ansh, the mythological action drama that has now crossed Rs 220 crore in worldwide gross. The actor, who has spent much of the past five years balancing commercial cinema with large-scale humanitarian work, said the film's performance proves that Indian audiences will reward ambition when it is backed by genuine craft.

Speaking about the numbers, Sood framed the result as more than a single film's victory. "It's high time we make films that compete on the biggest stage," he said, arguing that the industry has historically under-funded stories rooted in Indian mythology while over-relying on star vehicles and remake formulas.

Why Hanuman Ansh's Rs 220 Crore Matters

The headline figure alone does not capture the film's significance. Hanuman Ansh opened without a top-tier A-list lead, yet sustained footfalls across its second and third weeks — the clearest signal of word-of-mouth momentum rather than opening-weekend hype. Hindi, Telugu, Tamil and dubbed overseas versions all contributed meaningfully, which is unusual for a devotional-leaning title.

For exhibitors, the film has been a lifeline in a year where several mid-budget releases underperformed. Trade analysts tracking the run note that weekday occupancy in tier-2 and tier-3 cities has held steadier than in metros, a pattern that traditionally points to repeat viewership among families.

The Economics Behind the Headline

At an estimated budget in the Rs 55–70 crore band, a Rs 220 crore gross implies a very healthy multiple for investors once theatrical splits, satellite and streaming rights are factored in. That ratio — rather than the absolute number — is what studios will study while greenlighting the next slate of mythological projects.

Sood's own career offers context. He has repeatedly chosen scale over safety, and his comments suggest he sees Hanuman Ansh as validation for producers willing to spend on VFX, practical sets and regional-language rollouts instead of banking solely on celebrity draw. The practical lesson for buyers of content — distributors and streaming platforms — is that devotional and mythological IP now carries demonstrable box-office insurance.

Key Real-World Takeaways

  • Scale beats star power: an ensemble-led mythological film outperformed several star-driven releases in the same window.
  • Multi-language release strategy widened the revenue base beyond the Hindi belt.
  • Weekday occupancy held firm, indicating genuine repeat viewership rather than event-driven spikes.
  • Budget discipline kept the profit multiple attractive for investors and exhibitors alike.
  • Sood's endorsement adds industry credibility to the argument for bigger Indian mythological productions.

What This Means for the Next Slate

Studios now face a straightforward calculation. If a mid-budget mythological film can deliver a Rs 220 crore gross, the ceiling for a properly funded, globally marketed version is significantly higher. Expect accelerated development of Ramayana, Mahabharata and regional deity-led properties, along with sharper competition for VFX talent and release windows.

Sood's remark is best read as a challenge rather than a compliment. The audience has already voted with its wallet; the industry's next move will decide whether Hanuman Ansh becomes a template or an outlier.
