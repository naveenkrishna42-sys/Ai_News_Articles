Pak Anthem for Saudi-Turkey Defence Pact Sparks Online Ridicule

📅 2026-08-11 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Pakistani anthem for the Saudi-Turkey defence pact has gone viral, drawing ridicule for its promotional tone.
  • The pact, signed in Mecca, is seen as a setback for India and a warning for China, while Iran views it as a shift in regional perceptions.
  • Pakistani authorities ordered restaurants in Islamabad to decorate in red and green to celebrate the agreement.

Anthem Draws Mockery

A newly released Pakistani anthem celebrating the defence pact between Saudi Arabia and Turkey has sparked a wave of online ridicule. Titled '3 Flags, 1 Heart', the song was meant to rally support for the tripartite agreement signed in Mecca. Instead, it became fodder for memes and sarcastic comments across social media platforms.

Critics pointed out the anthem's overtly promotional tone, with some likening it to a corporate jingle rather than a serious diplomatic tribute. The video features flags of the three nations, but netizens were quick to question the timing and necessity of such a production.

Regional Reactions

For India, the Mecca pact is being viewed as a strategic setback. Analysts suggest the alignment of Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan could alter the balance of power in South Asia and the Gulf, potentially complicating New Delhi's diplomatic and security calculations.

China, too, has reason to take note. The pact is seen as a warning that regional players are forging deeper military and economic ties independent of Beijing's influence. While China has maintained strong relations with both Saudi Arabia and Turkey, the new trilateral framework could introduce friction in its own strategic outreach.

Iran's Perspective

Iran, a historical rival of Saudi Arabia, has offered a surprisingly measured response. Officials in Tehran have described the pact as evidence of a 'change in perception' towards the United States. This suggests that even traditional adversaries are reassessing their positions in a rapidly shifting geopolitical environment.

The Iranian read hints at a broader realignment, where regional powers are increasingly looking to hedge against reliance on Washington. Whether this leads to de-escalation or new rivalries remains an open question.

Domestic Celebrations and Criticism

Back home, Pakistani authorities have ordered restaurants in Islamabad to decorate their premises in red and green to celebrate the pact. The directive, reported by local media, is part of a broader campaign to project unity and enthusiasm for the agreement.

However, the move has drawn mixed reactions. Some citizens see it as a harmless display of national pride, while others question the focus on symbolism over substance. With economic challenges looming, many wonder if such gestures translate into tangible benefits for the country.

What Next?

The coming months will reveal whether the Mecca pact moves beyond rhetoric into concrete defence collaborations. Observers will watch for joint military exercises, intelligence-sharing mechanisms, and arms deals that could reshape regional security dynamics.
