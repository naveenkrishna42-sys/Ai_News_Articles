IDBI Bank sale: Ex-official calls for halt on governance, sovereignty concerns

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Former finance secretary E A S Sarma has called for a halt to IDBI Bank's privatisation, citing governance and sovereignty concerns.
  • Sarma argues the sale violates legal provisions and breaches a parliamentary assurance given during the bank's 2018 bailout.
  • The IDBI Bank Officers' Association has announced a nationwide hunger strike on July 27 to protest the sale.

Former top bureaucrat raises red flags

E A S Sarma, a former central government finance secretary, has urged Prime Minister Narendra Modi to immediately halt the privatisation of IDBI Bank. In a detailed letter, Sarma argues the sale process raises serious questions about governance and national sovereignty.

Sarma contends that the proposed transfer of majority stake to a private entity violates specific legal provisions under which the bank was restructured. He also claims the move breaches a parliamentary assurance given during the bank's 2018 bailout that its public sector character would be preserved.

Legal and parliamentary objections

The former bureaucrat points to the IDBI Bank (Transfer of Undertaking and Repeal) Act, 2003, and subsequent amendments. According to Sarma, privatisation requires fresh parliamentary approval, which has not been sought. He calls the current process a violation of the assurance given to Parliament when the Centre infused over Rs 9,000 crore into the bank to keep it afloat.

Sarma's letter, addressed to the Prime Minister and copied to the finance minister and the Reserve Bank of India governor, demands a comprehensive review. He warns that selling a bank with the government's implicit guarantee to a foreign private entity could compromise India's financial sovereignty.

Union announces protest

The IDBI Bank Officers' Association has announced a nationwide hunger strike on July 27 to protest the privatisation drive. The union claims the sale will lead to job losses and reduced customer service standards.

Union leaders argue that IDBI Bank, despite its past troubles, has shown signs of recovery. They question the need to sell a profitable public sector entity at this juncture.

New bids revive stalled process

The privatisation process, which had stalled for months, has reportedly received new interest. Sources indicate that Fairfax Group and Emirates have submitted fresh bids, reviving the sale. Reports also suggest the government is close to accepting a sweetened offer from Fairfax.

The government had earlier aimed to offload its entire 45.48% stake along with management control. LIC, which holds 49.24% in IDBI Bank, is also expected to sell its stake as part of the transaction.

What lies ahead

The government has not officially responded to Sarma's letter or the union's protest call. The next few weeks will be crucial as the Centre weighs legal objections against the need to close the sale and meet its disinvestment targets. All eyes will be on the RBI's final nod on the bidders' fit-and-proper status.
