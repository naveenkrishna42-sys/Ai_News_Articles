Sara Grey: The comic book history of Jean Grey's psychic sister in Spider-Man: Brand New Day

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sadie Sink is playing Sara Grey, the lesser-known psychic sister of Jean Grey, in the upcoming film.
  • Sara Grey first appeared in Marvel Comics in 2005's 'X-Men: Phoenix – Endsong' as a psionic construct created by the Phoenix Force.
  • The character has a complex history involving time travel and alternate realities, distinct from Jean Grey's more famous persona.
  • Marvel Studios is bringing a deep-cut character to the big screen, signalling a focus on secondary X-Men lore.
  • The role was kept secret by Sink throughout the press tour, with the actress saying she was 'fine with keeping a secret'.

The mystery around Sadie Sink's role in 'Spider-Man: Brand New Day' has finally been solved. The 'Stranger Things' star is playing Sara Grey — the psychic sister of Jean Grey. This is not a name most casual Marvel fans will recognise. To understand who Sara Grey is, you need to dig into some of the more obscure corners of X-Men lore.

Who is Sara Grey in the comics?

Sara Grey first appeared in 2005's 'X-Men: Phoenix – Endsong'. She is not a biological sibling. Instead, she was created as a psionic construct by the Phoenix Force. The entity needed a vessel to communicate with the Grey family after Jean's death. So it built Sara from fragments of psychic energy and the family's collective memories.

This makes Sara a walking paradox. She exists as a ghost-like being with telepathic abilities. She has always been aware she is not a real person. That existential crisis defines her character. She craves connection but knows she is a fabrication.

A character shaped by trauma and time travel

Sara Grey's story took a strange turn during the 'X-Men: Secret Invasion' tie-ins. The Skrulls manipulated her, exploiting her loneliness. Later, she was killed and resurrected multiple times. The character eventually merged with the cosmic entity known as the Phoenix itself, gaining immense power.

In more recent comics, Sara was revealed to be the mother of a future version of Rachel Summers — Jean Grey's daughter from an alternate timeline. This makes her a grandmother in a convoluted, time-twisted sense. The character has never been a mainstream fixture, but she has a loyal fanbase among X-Men completists.

Why Sara Grey for 'Spider-Man: Brand New Day'?

Marvel Studios has a history of pulling from deep-cut comic arcs. Bringing Sara Grey into a Spider-Man film seems odd on the surface. But the movie is reportedly dealing with multiversal chaos and the fallout from 'Spider-Man: No Way Home'. Sara Grey's connection to the Phoenix Force fits neatly into a story about fractured realities and psychic phenomena.

Sadie Sink kept her role a closely guarded secret throughout the press tour. She told The Times of India: 'I was fine with keeping a secret.' The studio likely wanted to avoid spoiling the film's central twist. Sara Grey appearing in a Spider-Man movie suggests the X-Men universe is being woven into the larger Marvel Cinematic Universe in subtle, unexpected ways.

What this means for the MCU

This casting confirms that Marvel is not just introducing the X-Men through their most famous characters like Wolverine or Professor X. They are planting seeds with secondary figures. Sara Grey could serve as a bridge to the full introduction of Jean Grey and the Phoenix saga later down the line.

It also adds a layer of psychic drama to a Spider-Man story. Peter Parker has dealt with tech villains and interdimensional threats, but a telepathic sibling from another franchise is new territory. The film might explore themes of identity and memory, which Sara Grey's origin story is built around.

For now, fans are left to speculate how much of Sara's comic history will make it to the screen. The character's psionic nature and existential loneliness could be powerful dramatic material. Or she could be a brief cameo designed to tease bigger things. Either way, Sadie Sink has stepped into one of the most intriguing roles of the year.

Marvel has not confirmed whether 'Spider-Man: Brand New Day' will directly set up a Jean Grey or Phoenix Force storyline. But with the X-Men slowly entering the MCU, Sara Grey's presence suggests the studio is thinking long-term. Audiences should watch for how her psychic abilities connect to the larger multiverse plot in the film's final act.
