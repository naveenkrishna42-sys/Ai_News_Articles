CBDT issues guidance note on crypto-asset reporting under I-T Act

Key points
  • CBDT issues guidance note on crypto-asset reporting under I-T Act, 2025
  • Guidance covers reporting obligations for virtual digital asset transactions
  • Aims to enhance tax compliance and transparency in crypto dealings

CBDT releases guidance note on crypto reporting

The Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) has issued a guidance note on reporting crypto-assets under the Income-Tax Act, 2025. The move is part of the government's ongoing efforts to bring virtual digital assets under the tax net.

The note provides clarity on how crypto-assets should be reported for tax purposes. It details the obligations for individuals and entities dealing in such assets.

Key compliance requirements

The guidance specifies reporting formats and timelines for transactions involving virtual digital assets. Exchanges and investors must now adhere to these updated norms.

Tax experts say the note fills several gaps in the existing framework. It addresses areas such as valuation of crypto-assets and treatment of losses.

The CBDT has also clarified the scope of reporting for foreign crypto exchanges operating in India. Non-compliance could attract penalties under the I-T Act.

Impact on investors and exchanges

The note mandates detailed disclosures for every crypto transaction. Investors must report holdings and trades in their annual tax returns.

Crypto exchanges will have to submit periodic statements to the tax department. This is expected to help the government track high-value transactions more effectively.

The guidance comes amid a broader push to regulate the crypto sector in India. The government has been tightening rules on virtual digital assets since last year.

Industry response and next steps

Industry bodies have welcomed the clarity but flagged concerns over compliance costs. Smaller exchanges may struggle with the new reporting requirements.

The CBDT has allowed a transition period for entities to update their systems. Officials say the focus is on voluntary compliance rather than punitive action.

Taxpayers are advised to consult professionals to ensure accurate reporting. The department is expected to release further FAQs in the coming weeks.

The government's next move could include a dedicated crypto tax law. For now, the guidance note sets the stage for tighter oversight of the asset class.
