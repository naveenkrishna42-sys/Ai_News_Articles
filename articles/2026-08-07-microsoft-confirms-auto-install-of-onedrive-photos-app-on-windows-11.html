Microsoft confirms auto-install of OneDrive Photos app on Windows 11

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Microsoft admits to force-installing the OneDrive Photos app on Windows 11 and enterprise PCs.
  • The company says the app uses AI for photo organization, but not for facial recognition training.
  • Users express frustration over the lack of consent and the difficulty of removing the app.

Microsoft has confirmed that it is automatically installing its AI-powered OneDrive Photos app on Windows 11 devices, including enterprise PCs. The admission follows widespread user complaints about the unexpected appearance of the app on their systems.

The company insists the app is not being used to train facial recognition models on users' photos. However, the move has sparked privacy concerns and frustration among Windows 11 users who say they were not given a choice in the matter.

What the OneDrive Photos app does

The app is designed to help users organise and search their photos using artificial intelligence. It can group similar images, identify objects and scenes, and offer smart search features across both local and cloud-stored pictures.

Microsoft claims the AI-driven features are meant to enhance user experience, not to harvest personal data. Yet the company's silence on how the app was rolled out has done little to reassure users.

Enterprise PCs included

Reports indicate that the app has also been silently installed on enterprise machines, which run under corporate IT policies. This has raised eyebrows among administrators who expect tighter control over software deployments.

Microsoft has not clarified whether IT departments were notified in advance or given the option to block the installation. The lack of transparency has added to the growing criticism.

User backlash and removal options

Windows 11 users have taken to forums and social media to express their displeasure. Some have called the move an intrusion, while others have said it is another reason to abandon the platform altogether.

For those who want to remove the app, it is possible to uninstall it through the Settings menu or via PowerShell commands. However, the process is not straightforward for the average user, and there are fears the app may return with future updates.

Microsoft's response and privacy assurances

In its statement, Microsoft reiterated that the OneDrive Photos app does not use facial recognition for training purposes. The company says the AI is limited to on-device processing and cloud-based features that users can control.

Still, privacy advocates argue that automatic installation of any AI-powered software without explicit consent is a worrying trend. They point out that even if photos are not used for training, the app's access to image libraries is a significant concern.

Microsoft has not yet said whether it will change its rollout approach or offer clearer opt-out mechanisms in future updates.

As Windows 11 users continue to voice their concerns, the company may need to address the backlash more directly. The coming weeks will show whether Microsoft adjusts its strategy or stands firm on the automatic installation.
