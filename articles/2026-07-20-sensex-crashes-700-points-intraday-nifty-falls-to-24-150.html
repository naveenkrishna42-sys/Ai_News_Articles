Sensex crashes 700 points intraday, Nifty falls to 24,150

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 700 points intraday on Monday.
  • Nifty dropped below the 24,150 mark during the session.
  • Banking and IT stocks led the decline, dragging the broader market.
  • Analysts cited global cues and profit booking as key reasons.
  • Market volatility remains elevated amid uncertainty over foreign fund flows.

Markets in the red: Sensex tanks 700 points

Indian equity markets witnessed a brutal sell-off on Monday, with the BSE Sensex crashing over 700 points during intraday trading. The broader Nifty index slipped below the 24,150 level, sending shockwaves through Dalal Street.

The sharp decline was broad-based, with banking and IT stocks bearing the brunt of the selling pressure. Heavyweights like HDFC Bank, Reliance Industries, and Infosys saw significant losses, pulling the indices lower.

What is driving the slump?

Market participants pointed to a combination of global and domestic factors behind the rout. Weak cues from Asian markets, rising US bond yields, and concerns over foreign portfolio investor (FPI) outflows weighed heavily on sentiment.

“The market is reacting to global headwinds and profit booking at higher levels. Investors are cautious ahead of key US economic data,” said a senior analyst at a domestic brokerage. The selling was aggravated by expiry-related volatility in the derivatives segment.

Banking and IT stocks under pressure

The Nifty Bank index fell sharply, dragged by losses in HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Kotak Mahindra Bank. The IT pack also remained weak, with TCS and Wipro among the top losers. The broader market indices — midcap and smallcap — also declined in line with the benchmarks.

“The sell-off in banking is on concerns over margin pressure. IT stocks are down due to fears of a slowdown in US demand,” another analyst noted. The rupee also weakened against the dollar, adding to the anxiety for foreign investors.

What lies ahead for investors?

Analysts expect volatility to persist in the near term. The market is likely to remain driven by global cues, FPI flows, and domestic macroeconomic data. Traders are advised to stay cautious and avoid leveraged positions.

Support for the Nifty is seen at the 24,000 mark, while resistance is placed around 24,500. A decisive close below 24,000 could trigger further selling pressure in the coming sessions.
