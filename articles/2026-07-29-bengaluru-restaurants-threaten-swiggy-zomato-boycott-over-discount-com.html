Bengaluru restaurants threaten Swiggy-Zomato boycott over discount, commission war

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru restaurateurs accuse Swiggy and Zomato of forcing unsustainable discounts.
  • Owners say high platform commissions leave them with razor-thin margins.
  • A collective boycott is being threatened if demands for fair terms are not met.

Bengaluru's restaurant owners have drawn a line in the sand. They are threatening to delist from food delivery giants Swiggy and Zomato if the platforms do not roll back deep discounts and reduce commission rates. The simmering tension has now reached a flashpoint.

The discount trap

Restaurateurs say the platforms routinely push promotions that slash menu prices by 40 to 50 percent. These discounts, they argue, are often funded entirely by the restaurants, not the aggregators.

"A customer orders a dish worth Rs 300 but pays only Rs 150 after the platform's coupon. The platform still takes its 25 percent commission on the original price," explained a restaurant owner on condition of anonymity. "We end up losing money on every order."

Commission conundrum

Swiggy and Zomato typically charge restaurants between 18 and 25 percent per order as commission. For small and medium eateries, these charges eat into already thin profit margins. Many say the model is unsustainable.

"We are not against technology. We are against being forced into a loss-making arrangement," said a representative of a city restaurant association. The group claims over 200 outlets in Bengaluru are ready to walk away if terms do not change.

Past protests, new urgency

This is not the first time Indian restaurants have clashed with delivery platforms. In 2020, the National Restaurant Association of India filed a complaint with the Competition Commission over alleged predatory pricing. Similar boycotts were threatened in cities like Delhi and Mumbai.

This time, the anger feels more acute. Post-pandemic recovery has been uneven, and rising ingredient costs have squeezed margins further. Restaurants say the platforms' policies are pushing them to the edge.

Swiggy and Zomato have not yet issued detailed responses to the latest boycott threat. Both companies have repeatedly argued that their commissions cover logistics, technology, and customer acquisition.

What the aggregators say

In previous statements, the platforms have said that discounts are optional and that restaurants can choose not to participate. They also point to the visibility and order volume that come with being listed on their apps.

But restaurateurs counter that delisting is not a real option. "If you leave Swiggy and Zomato, you lose half your business overnight. That is the power they have," one owner said. The threat of a coordinated boycott is meant to level that power imbalance.

What happens next

Talks between restaurant bodies and the aggregators are expected in the coming weeks. If no deal is reached, Bengaluru could see a significant number of restaurants go dark on the apps.

The outcome will likely set a precedent for other cities. For now, both sides are holding their ground, and the city's diners may soon find fewer options on their favourite food apps.
