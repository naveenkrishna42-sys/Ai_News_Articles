CII's 15 Financial Reforms to Power India's $26.5 Trillion Goal

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-22 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • CII wants public sector banks given operational autonomy and professional boards before India scales credit to fund a $26.5 trillion economy by 2047.
  • Deepening the corporate bond market, insurance and pension penetration are core to the package, not side notes.
  • MSME credit delivery, digital lending rules and a unified fraud registry round out the 15-point submission to the High-Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat.

Why the 15-Point Submission Lands Now

The Confederation of Indian Industry has placed 15 priority reform inputs before the High-Level Committee on Banking for Viksit Bharat, the panel tasked with redrawing India's financial architecture for 2047. The timing is deliberate. India's gross domestic product sits at roughly $4 trillion, and the stated destination is $26.5 trillion in a little over two decades.

That gap cannot be closed by government spending alone. It requires bank credit, bond issuance, insurance premiums and pension savings to compound at a pace India has never sustained. CII's submission is essentially a checklist of the bottlenecks that would otherwise cap that growth.

The 15 Reforms on the Table

1. Operational autonomy for public sector banks

PSBs hold roughly 55-60% of banking assets. CII argues they cannot compete for large corporate and infrastructure mandates while answering to multiple masters on pricing and appointments.

2. Professional board governance

Independent directors with domain expertise, fixed tenures and clear CEO accountability, benchmarked against private-sector boards rather than administrative norms.

3. Scale through consolidation

Fewer, larger banks with balance sheets deep enough to fund ₹10,000-crore-plus project loans without consortium fragmentation.

4. A functioning stressed-asset market

Faster resolution pathways and wider participation by asset reconstruction companies so that bad loans do not sit on books for years.

5. Deepening the corporate bond market

India's bond market is roughly 18% of GDP against well over 50% in mature markets. CII wants easier issuance, better secondary-market liquidity and repo access for corporate paper.

6. Simpler securitisation and credit derivatives

Banks need to recycle capital. Standardised securitisation structures let them offload seasoned loan pools and lend again.

7. MSME credit delivery

Guarantee-backed lending and cash-flow-based underwriting to close a credit gap that industry bodies estimate at ₹20-25 lakh crore.

8. Digital lending guardrails

Proportionate rules for fintech lenders, with clear accountability for the regulated entity behind every digital loan.

9. Unified KYC and a fraud registry

One reusable verification and a shared fraud database to cut onboarding costs and blunt mule-account fraud.

10. Higher insurance penetration

India's insurance penetration remains near 4% of GDP. CII seeks a wider product suite and capital inflows into the sector.

11. Pension and retirement savings

Expanding voluntary pension coverage to build the long-term institutional capital that infrastructure debt needs.

12. Stronger development finance institutions

DFIs with low-cost, long-tenor funding to underwrite greenfield infrastructure that commercial banks avoid.

13. Blended finance and infrastructure debt funds

Structures that draw pension and insurance money into roads, transmission and renewables through credit-enhanced vehicles.

14. A fintech sandbox with proportionate regulation

Testing frameworks that let innovation scale without regulatory arbitrage between banks and non-banks.

15. Capital markets access

Easier listing norms, deeper retail participation and simpler routes for mid-sized firms to raise equity.

What It Changes for Borrowers and Savers

Bank autonomy and governance reform decide whether a mid-sized manufacturer gets a sanction in weeks or quarters. Bond market depth decides whether a large firm borrows from a bank or from investors, freeing bank capital for smaller borrowers. Insurance and pension reform decide whether household savings stay in gold and real estate or move into long-tenor financial instruments.

The committee's mandate is advisory. Implementation will be phased, and any near-term change in lending costs or deposit rates is unlikely. Read the submission as a direction of travel rather than an immediate policy trigger.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pro: A single coherent reform list covering banking, insurance, pensions and capital markets.
  • Pro: Governance and autonomy recommendations target the actual decision bottleneck in PSBs.
  • Pro: Bond market and securitisation asks directly address capital recycling.
  • Con: Advisory in nature; no binding timeline or legislative commitment attached.
  • Con: Consolidation carries integration and workforce risk if executed without sequencing.
  • Con: MSME credit gap estimates remain contested, complicating targets.
