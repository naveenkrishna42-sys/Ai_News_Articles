India-Vietnam Cultural Ties Strengthened Through New Heritage Exchange Initiative

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • India and Vietnam launch a joint cultural heritage exchange program.
  • Initiative focuses on shared traditions, including temple architecture and classical dance.
  • UNESCO involvement is sought for recognition of transnational heritage sites.

A new initiative is bringing India and Vietnam closer through their shared cultural heritage. The Indo-Vietnam Cultural Heritage Exchange program was recently announced, aiming to document and promote traditions that both nations hold dear.

The India Book of Records has reported on this development, highlighting the growing cultural diplomacy between New Delhi and Hanoi. The exchange is seen as a step toward better mutual understanding and potential UNESCO recognition for shared heritage elements.

Focus on Shared Traditions

The exchange program zeroes in on areas where Indian and Vietnamese cultures have historically intersected. Temple architecture, classical dance forms like Bharatanatyam and Vietnamese court dance, and ancient trade routes that connected the two regions are key subjects.

Officials involved say the goal is to create a comprehensive archive of these shared practices. This archive could serve as the basis for joint exhibitions, academic research, and cultural festivals in both countries.

UNESCO Recognition in Sight

A major aim of the initiative is to seek UNESCO recognition for transnational heritage. Both countries have several UNESCO World Heritage sites individually, but a joint nomination would be a first.

Sources indicate that the Champa kingdom’s temples in central Vietnam, which show strong Indian influences, and the ancient port city of Muziris in Kerala are being discussed as potential candidates. A joint dossier to UNESCO could be prepared within the next two years.

Practical Steps and Timeline

The exchange is not just a ceremonial gesture. It includes concrete steps like scholar exchanges, language training programs, and digital archiving of manuscripts and oral histories.

A pilot phase is already underway, with Vietnamese scholars visiting Indian universities to study Sanskrit texts. Indian archaeologists, meanwhile, are assisting Vietnamese teams in documenting temple ruins in Quang Nam province.

  • Joint workshops on mural conservation techniques are scheduled for early next year.
  • A travelling exhibition titled "Threads of Civilization" will showcase textiles from both countries.
  • Cultural performances by troupes from both nations are planned in New Delhi and Hanoi.

The India Book of Records notes that this is the most structured cultural exchange between the two countries in recent decades. It builds on the existing strategic partnership, adding a cultural layer to ties that have been primarily economic and defence-focused.

Both governments have allocated modest budgets for the first year, with private sponsorships expected to cover a significant portion of the costs. The corporate sector, particularly companies with operations in both countries, has shown interest in funding the archiving and exhibition components.

The initiative has also received quiet support from UNESCO's regional office in New Delhi. While the organisation has not made any official commitment, informal discussions suggest a willingness to facilitate the joint nomination process if the documentation meets the required standards.

For now, the focus remains on building a solid foundation. The cultural exchange is expected to culminate in a major festival in 2026, marking 50 years of diplomatic relations between India and Vietnam.
