US-Iran escalation enters 6th night: Bridge strike kills 7, Tehran fires missiles

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US airstrikes hit five bridges in southern Iran, killing at least 7 people.
  • Iranian state media reports damage to Iranshahr Airport in the southeast.
  • Tehran launched retaliatory missiles, raising fears of a wider regional conflict.
  • Sirens sounded in Qatar; White House claims Tehran still wants a diplomatic deal.

Bridge strikes and casualties

The military escalation between the United States and Iran entered its sixth consecutive night on Thursday, with no signs of de-escalation. US forces struck five bridges in southern Iran, causing widespread damage to civilian infrastructure.

At least seven people were killed in the strikes on the bridges, according to Iranian officials. The targeted bridges were key transit points in the southern region, and their destruction has disrupted movement for thousands of civilians. Rescue teams are still searching for survivors in the debris.

Iranian state media reported that the US attack also damaged Iranshahr Airport in southeastern Iran. Details on the extent of the damage remain unclear, though officials confirmed no flights were operating at the time of the strike.

Tehran retaliates with missiles

Hours after the US strikes, Tehran launched a salvo of missiles targeting positions it claims are linked to US forces in the region. The exact locations and impact of the Iranian missile barrage have not been independently verified.

Air raid sirens went off in Qatar, a key US ally in the Gulf, as a precautionary measure. Qatari authorities urged residents to remain indoors but reported no direct hits or casualties.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard Corps said in a statement that the missile attacks were "a response to the bombing of civilian infrastructure." The statement warned of further retaliation if US operations continue.

White House: Tehran still wants a deal

Despite the escalating violence, the White House struck a cautious tone. A spokesperson said intelligence assessments indicate that Tehran still wants a diplomatic resolution to the crisis.

“Iran’s leadership is sending mixed signals — launching missiles while signaling a willingness to negotiate,” the spokesperson said. “The door for diplomacy remains open, but our military operations will continue to protect American interests and personnel.”

Regional analysts remain sceptical. The six-day offensive marks the longest sustained US military action against Iran since the 2020 assassination of General Qasem Soleimani. The targeting of bridges and an airport suggests a shift in strategy — hitting civilian infrastructure rather than purely military assets.

Humanitarian toll and regional fears

Human rights groups have condemned the US strikes on civilian infrastructure. The United Nations called for an immediate de-escalation, warning that the conflict could spiral into a broader regional war.

Iran’s southern provinces, already grappling with economic hardship, now face severe transport disruptions. The destruction of the five bridges has cut off supply routes for food, medicine, and fuel.

In the Gulf, neighbouring states are bracing for further fallout. Saudi Arabia and the UAE have urged restraint, while Iraq and Syria — where Iranian-backed militias are active — remain on high alert.

What happens next hinges on whether Washington and Tehran can find an off-ramp. Neither side has publicly backed down, and the sixth night of strikes suggests both are prepared for a prolonged confrontation.
