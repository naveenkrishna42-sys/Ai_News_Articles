Govt mulls legal action against illegal screenings of Diljit Dosanjh's 'Satluj'

📅 2026-07-16 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government evaluating legal steps against unauthorised screenings of 'Satluj'.
  • Producer and distributors have flagged piracy concerns to authorities.
  • Action may include raids, fines, and criminal complaints under copyright law.

Piracy concerns prompt government review

The government is considering legal action against unauthorised screenings of Punjabi superstar Diljit Dosanjh's latest film, 'Satluj'. Sources in the Ministry of Information and Broadcasting confirmed that the matter is under active review.

Officials are examining reports that the film was shown in certain venues without proper licensing or permission from the producers. The unauthorised screenings have raised serious concerns about copyright infringement and revenue loss.

Producers flag violations to authorities

Earlier this week, the film's production team approached the ministry and law enforcement agencies with evidence of illegal public exhibitions. They argued that such screenings undermine the theatrical release and hurt the film's commercial prospects.

In a statement, a representative for the producers said the team is cooperating with the government to identify and penalise those responsible. The exact number of illegal screenings or locations involved has not been disclosed.

Copyright law provides strong remedies

Under the Copyright Act, 1957, unauthorised public performance of a film is a criminal offence. Penalties can include imprisonment of up to three years and fines. The government can also order seizures of equipment used in such screenings.

Legal experts say the government has the power to act swiftly. 'If they choose to pursue this, it could serve as a strong deterrent against piracy,' said a Delhi-based lawyer familiar with entertainment law.

Industry watchers call for stricter enforcement

The film industry has long demanded stricter action against piracy and illegal screenings. The case of 'Satluj' has reignited the debate over inadequate enforcement of intellectual property rights in India.

Trade analysts say unauthorised screenings, especially in small towns and rural areas, cost the industry crores of rupees every year. The government's move is being seen as a potential turning point in the fight against film piracy.

Officials have not yet confirmed the timeline for any legal action. They are expected to issue guidelines for local authorities to crack down on such violations in the coming weeks.
