Infantino warns FIFA members: back World Cup plan or lose $40 million funding

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • FIFA President Gianni Infantino links member funding to support for a new World Cup plan
  • The plan involves a $20 billion commercial vehicle to attract external investors
  • UEFA has called an emergency meeting as opposition to the proposal hardens
  • European football bodies are considering a boycott of the World Cup over the plan

Infantino issues ultimatum to FIFA members

FIFA President Gianni Infantino has told member associations they must back a controversial World Cup expansion plan or lose access to $40 million in development funding. The warning came during a closed-door meeting with national football federation heads, according to sources familiar with the discussions.

The Swiss-Italian administrator is pushing for a radical overhaul of the World Cup format that would create a new commercial vehicle worth $20 billion. The plan aims to lure external investors into FIFA's flagship tournament, but has sparked fierce resistance from European football's governing body.

UEFA pushes back against $20 billion proposal

European football is considering a World Cup boycott over what critics call a sellout plan that prioritises commercial interests over the sport's integrity. UEFA has called an emergency meeting of its 55 member associations to coordinate a response, with several major European federations expressing deep unease.

The proposed $20 billion commercial vehicle would give private investors significant influence over the World Cup's future direction. FIFA argues the funds are needed to develop the sport globally, but opponents say it risks turning football's premier event into a corporate showcase.

Financial leverage and political fallout

Infantino's linking of development funding to political support has raised eyebrows among FIFA's 211 member associations. The $40 million per association represents a substantial sum for many smaller federations, effectively forcing them to choose between financial survival and sporting principle.

Sources within FIFA indicate the president is determined to push the plan through before the 2026 World Cup, co-hosted by the United States, Canada and Mexico. The timing has added urgency to what is shaping up to be one of the most contentious governance battles in the organisation's history.

European clubs also watch closely

European club associations have also expressed concern about the plan's implications for the global football calendar. The expanded World Cup would require significant scheduling changes, potentially clashing with domestic leagues and club competitions that generate billions in revenue.

FIFA has not yet released detailed financial projections for the commercial vehicle, leading to speculation about which investors might be involved. Industry analysts suggest sovereign wealth funds and private equity firms could be among the bidders if the plan goes ahead.

The coming weeks will see intense lobbying on both sides, with UEFA's emergency meeting expected to produce a formal statement of opposition. Infantino's ability to maintain unity among FIFA's diverse membership will be tested as never before.

What happens next depends on whether UEFA can rally enough support to force a compromise, or whether Infantino's financial ultimatum will carry the day. FIFA's next council meeting is expected to be the decisive battleground.
