Bengaluru Woman Loses Rs 40 Lakh in Jewellery to Fake Astrologer

A 52-year-old woman from Bengaluru has been cheated of gold and diamond jewellery worth Rs 40 lakh by a man posing as an astrologer. According to the New Indian Express, the accused promised to perform special rituals that would double the value of her ornaments. Instead, he disappeared with the valuables, leaving the family with a hefty financial and emotional loss.

The case was registered at a local police station after the woman filed a complaint. Investigators say the suspect had been building trust with the victim over several weeks, using a mix of astrological predictions and community references. This is not an isolated incident; Bengaluru has seen a spate of similar frauds where self-styled godmen and astrologers target vulnerable individuals.

How the Fraud Unfolded: Modus Operandi

The accused reportedly approached the woman at her residence in a south Bengaluru neighbourhood. He claimed to have divine powers and said her jewellery was “cursed” and needed purification. He offered to perform a ritual that would multiply the ornaments. The woman handed over the jewellery, and the man left with a promise to return after the ritual. He never came back.

Police sources indicate the suspect used a fake identity and may have targeted other women in the area. The investigation is ongoing, and officers are checking CCTV footage and call records. The case highlights the need for public awareness about such con artists who exploit religious beliefs.

Rising Trend of Astrology-Related Frauds in Bengaluru

Bengaluru, with its mix of tradition and modernity, has become a hotspot for such frauds. Data from the city police shows a steady increase in cheating cases involving fake astrologers and godmen. In many instances, victims are middle-aged or elderly women who are emotionally vulnerable. The amounts range from a few lakhs to crores.

Experts point out that these fraudsters often operate in gangs and use multiple identities. They may also be involved in other crimes like house-breaking. The police advise citizens to verify credentials before handing over valuables or money.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • High-value target: The victim lost jewellery worth Rs 40 lakh, which is a significant amount for a middle-class family.
  • Trust-based fraud: The accused spent weeks building rapport, making the victim lower her guard.
  • Repeat offenders: Many fake astrologers have prior cheating cases and operate across multiple cities.
  • Low conviction rate: Victims often hesitate to report due to social stigma, allowing fraudsters to escape.

What Buyers and Families Should Watch For

While this case involves jewellery, the underlying scam tactics apply to any high-value transaction. Whether you are buying gold, investing in property, or seeking spiritual services, due diligence is critical. Never hand over valuables to unverified individuals. Always insist on receipts and involve family members in major decisions.

For those in Bengaluru, the police have a dedicated cybercrime helpline (1930) for reporting frauds. Reporting quickly can help trace the accused and recover assets. The city also has consumer courts where victims can seek compensation.

Safety Checklist for Avoiding Astrology Frauds

  • Verify identity: Ask for government-issued ID and check for any criminal records.
  • Never hand over valuables: No genuine astrologer will ask for jewellery or large sums for rituals.
  • Consult family: Discuss any such proposal with relatives before acting.
  • Report immediately: If cheated, file an FIR without delay and inform the cybercrime cell.
  • Spread awareness: Share warnings with elderly neighbours who are often targeted.

The Bottom Line

The Rs 40 lakh jewellery fraud in Bengaluru is a stark reminder that superstition can be costly. As police investigate, the case underscores the importance of scepticism and vigilance. For a city known for its tech-savvy population, such crimes reveal a persistent gap in public awareness. Buyers and families must treat unsolicited spiritual offers with suspicion and prioritise legal verification over blind faith.
