IAF Wing Commander Arrested in Honey-Trap Op for Leaking Defence Secrets

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • IAF wing commander arrested in Delhi for allegedly leaking defence secrets
  • Honey-trapped by a woman online who connected him to a Pakistani handler
  • Four-month-long Delhi Police operation culminated in the arrest
  • Booked under the Official Secrets Act

New Delhi: A serving Indian Air Force (IAF) wing commander has been arrested by Delhi Police for allegedly leaking sensitive defence information to a Pakistani intelligence operative. The arrest came after a four-month-long operation that began with a tip-off about suspicious online activity.

The Honey-Trap Trap

According to police sources, the officer was honey-trapped by a woman on a social media platform. The woman, believed to be a front for Pakistani intelligence, reportedly engaged the officer in chats that gradually turned from casual conversation to requests for classified military details.

Investigators say the officer allegedly shared information on troop movements, technical specifications, and other operational details. The exchanges, spanning several weeks, were allegedly facilitated by a Pakistani handler who coordinated the data transfer.

Four-Month Surveillance

The Delhi Police's Special Cell launched the operation after receiving intelligence inputs about potential leaks. Over four months, the team monitored the officer's communications, tracked his movements, and built a detailed case before moving in for the arrest.

Sources indicate that the police had to coordinate with multiple agencies to ensure the evidence was watertight. The arrest was made without incident, and the officer has since been booked under the Official Secrets Act.

IAF and Police Response

The IAF has not yet issued a public statement regarding the arrest. However, officials confirmed that the officer has been suspended and is being held for questioning. The Delhi Police are now probing the extent of the damage caused by the alleged leaks.

The case has once again highlighted the growing threat of honey-trapping, where foreign intelligence agencies use romantic or sexual lures to compromise individuals with access to sensitive information. Security agencies have repeatedly warned personnel about such tactics.

Wider Investigation Underway

Investigators are now examining the officer's digital footprint to determine if any other personnel were approached or compromised. The woman who allegedly honey-trapped the officer has not been identified or located, and officials have not confirmed whether she operated from within India or abroad.

The arrest is a significant catch for the Delhi Police, which has been under pressure to crack down on espionage networks. The case also raises questions about the effectiveness of existing security protocols within the armed forces.

As the investigation continues, the focus will be on tracing the full network of handlers and operatives linked to this operation. The officer remains in police custody, and further disclosures are expected as the probe deepens.
