Jantar Mantar protest: Over 50 detained as Reservation Hatao Andolan demands quota overhaul

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 50 protesters detained at Jantar Mantar during Reservation Hatao Andolan demonstration
  • Police deployed Rapid Action Force as crowd swelled near Parliament Street
  • Protesters demand reservation be based on economic status rather than caste
  • Organisers claim the current system perpetuates inequality among the poor

Police detain protesters at Jantar Mantar

Delhi Police detained over 50 people on Friday as a large crowd gathered at Jantar Mantar for the Reservation Hatao Andolan, demanding a complete overhaul of India's affirmative action system. Officers used barricades to control the gathering near Parliament Street, with Rapid Action Force personnel stationed at key points as the protest swelled through the afternoon.

The demonstration brought together groups advocating for a shift from caste-based reservation to economically-determined quotas. Police moved in after crowds attempted to breach security barriers, officials said.

Demand for economic criteria over caste

The protesters argue that reservation benefits should target the genuinely poor regardless of caste. Their central demand is that economic backwardness replace caste identity as the primary criterion for allocating seats in education and government jobs.

Organisers of the movement contend that the current system disproportionately benefits privileged sections within reserved categories while leaving the most needy without support. They have called for means-tested quotas that would redirect benefits to families below a certain income threshold.

Police presence and crowd control

Authorities deployed significant security infrastructure around the protest site. Rapid Action Force units were positioned at multiple entry points to Jantar Mantar, a venue traditionally used for demonstrations in the capital.

Delhi Police confirmed the detentions but did not immediately release details about potential charges. Officials said arrangements were in place to handle the expected crowd, which grew larger than anticipated.

Rapid Action Force deployment

The presence of RAF personnel signalled concerns about potential unrest. The force, trained for crowd management and civil disturbance response, typically deploys when protests risk escalating beyond routine control.

Protesters who spoke to reporters said they intended to hold a peaceful demonstration. Event organisers had coordinated with police in the lead-up to the gathering, though both sides offered conflicting accounts of prior agreements.

What happens next

Leaders of the Reservation Hatao Andolan have indicated plans to continue their campaign, though no further demonstrations have been formally announced. The protest is likely to reignite debate in Parliament, where similar demands for reservation reform have surfaced during previous sessions. Officials have not yet confirmed whether formal talks with protest leaders will be scheduled.
