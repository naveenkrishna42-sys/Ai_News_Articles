Chennai fishermen seek CM Vijay's help to retrieve 7 boats held in Andhra

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Seven fishing boats from Kasimedu are reportedly held by Andhra Pradesh police.
  • Fishermen have petitioned Tamil Nadu CM Vijay for immediate retrieval.
  • The incident highlights cross-border maritime disputes between the two states.

Boats seized, livelihoods at stake

Fishing crews from Chennai's Kasimedu harbour have appealed to Chief Minister Vijay to secure the release of seven boats currently in the custody of Andhra Pradesh police. The vessels, owned by local fishermen, were reportedly taken during a recent patrol along the state's maritime boundary.

The fishermen claim the boats were operating within Tamil Nadu waters when they were intercepted. Andhra authorities, however, have not yet issued an official statement regarding the seizure, leaving families in limbo.

For the affected households, the boats are more than assets—they are the sole means of income. With each day of impoundment, daily earnings vanish, and crews are unable to plan their next voyage.

Formal plea to the Chief Minister

A delegation of Kasimedu fishermen met with local officials and submitted a memorandum addressed to CM Vijay. The plea urges the Chief Minister to take up the matter with his Andhra Pradesh counterpart and secure the boats' immediate return.

The fishermen have also requested compensation for the days lost and a clear protocol to prevent such incidents in the future. They argue that repeated detentions have become a recurring problem, undermining their confidence in cross-border fishing operations.

Community leaders stress that this is not an isolated case. Similar seizures have occurred in the past, they say, though the frequency has increased in recent months.

Political and bureaucratic channels

The Tamil Nadu government has acknowledged receipt of the representation. Officials in the fisheries department have indicated that the matter is being taken up with the Union government, as maritime jurisdiction involves multiple agencies.

Meanwhile, the fishermen's association has threatened to intensify protests if no progress is made by the end of the week. They have planned a series of demonstrations outside the state secretariat if the boats remain unreleased.

Legal experts note that such disputes often require diplomatic negotiation rather than judicial intervention, given the complexities of state and central maritime laws.

Impact on the community

Kasimedu is one of the largest fishing harbours in Chennai, supporting thousands of families directly and indirectly. The current impoundment has disrupted supply chains, with markets reporting reduced catches.

Local traders say prices for fresh fish have risen by nearly 15% over the past week due to the shortage. Consumers bear the brunt, but the long-term damage falls on the fishing crews, who face mounting debts.

Fishermen have also expressed concern over the condition of their boats, which are exposed to the elements while docked in Andhra. They worry about engine damage and gear loss, which would add to their financial burden.

Previous incidents and unresolved tensions

Officials from the Tamil Nadu fisheries department recall at least three similar incidents in the last two years, though none involved this many boats at once. In each case, the vessels were returned after prolonged negotiations, but the process took weeks.

The fishermen now want a faster, more transparent mechanism. They have proposed a joint patrol system along the disputed boundary, with representatives from both states on board to verify fishing zones in real time.

Such a proposal, however, requires political will from both governments, and there is no indication yet that talks have begun.

What to watch next

All eyes are on CM Vijay's response. A swift, decisive move could ease tensions and set a precedent for handling cross-border maritime disputes. If the boats are not returned soon, the fishermen's protests are likely to escalate, drawing wider attention to a simmering issue that affects coastal communities on both sides.
