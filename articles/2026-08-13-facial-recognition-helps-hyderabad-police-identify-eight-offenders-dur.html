Facial recognition helps Hyderabad Police identify eight offenders during Bonalu

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Eight offenders identified during Bonalu using facial recognition technology
  • Technology helped in identifying suspects linked to theft and chain-snatching
  • Hyderabad Police deployed the system to enhance security at the festival

Tech-assisted policing at the festival

Hyderabad Police have identified eight offenders during the ongoing Bonalu festivities using facial recognition technology. The identification was part of a broader effort to tighten security at one of the city's most attended religious events.

The suspects are believed to be linked to a series of thefts and chain-snatching incidents reported in the crowded areas around temples and procession routes. Police have not yet disclosed the names of the accused or the exact locations where they were flagged.

How the system works

Facial recognition software was deployed at strategic points across the city, feeding live footage into a control room where officers cross-checked faces against a database of known offenders. The system flagged matches in real time, allowing ground teams to move in swiftly.

Officials said the technology supplemented traditional surveillance methods, which include plainclothes officers and CCTV monitoring. This is not the first time Hyderabad Police have used such tools during major festivals, but the scale of deployment this year is said to be larger.

Crime pattern during Bonalu

Bonalu attracts lakhs of devotees, creating dense crowds that are often exploited by pickpockets and snatchers. Chain-snatching incidents typically spike during the festival, with women being the most vulnerable targets.

Police had earlier issued advisories urging devotees to avoid wearing heavy jewellery and to stay alert in crowded zones. The latest arrests are expected to send a signal to repeat offenders that technology is being used to track them.

Privacy concerns and safeguards

The use of facial recognition has drawn criticism from civil liberties groups in the past, who have questioned its accuracy and the potential for misuse. Police maintain that the technology is used strictly for law enforcement and that data is handled according to protocol.

No official statement has been made regarding how long the captured footage will be retained or whether it will be used for future investigations. The Hyderabad Police have not yet responded to queries on these aspects.

Meanwhile, the festival continues, with the final procession scheduled for later this week. Police have said that surveillance will remain in place until the event concludes.

The identification of the eight offenders is likely to be followed by formal arrests and chargesheets. More details are expected once the police issue a public statement on the matter.
