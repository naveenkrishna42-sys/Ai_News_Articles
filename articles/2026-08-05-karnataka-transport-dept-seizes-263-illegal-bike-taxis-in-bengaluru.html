Karnataka Transport Dept seizes 263 illegal bike taxis in Bengaluru

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • 263 illegal bike taxis seized across Bengaluru
  • Operation by Karnataka Transport Department
  • Enforcement drive targets unlicensed operators

The Karnataka Transport Department has seized 263 bike taxis across Bengaluru in a coordinated enforcement drive against illegal operations. The action, which took place over recent days, targeted two-wheelers being used for commercial passenger transport without valid permits.

Officials said the crackdown was part of an ongoing effort to rein in unauthorized bike taxi services that have proliferated across the city. Many of these vehicles were found operating through app-based platforms, bypassing state regulations.

Enforcement Details

The seizures were carried out by teams from the transport department's enforcement wing, which conducted checks at multiple locations across Bengaluru. Sources said the drive covered busy corridors, tech parks, and transit hubs where bike taxis are most active.

Each seized vehicle was impounded and moved to designated depots. The department has not yet disclosed the total fines levied, but officials indicated that penalties would be imposed on both riders and platform operators found violating norms.

Why the Crackdown?

Bike taxis in Karnataka require a transport license and must comply with safety and insurance rules. Many of the seized vehicles lacked these documents, raising concerns about passenger safety and accountability.

The transport department has repeatedly warned that operating without a permit is illegal. Despite this, the demand for quick, affordable rides has fueled a grey market of unregistered bike taxis, particularly in congested areas where cars struggle to move.

Reactions and Compliance

Riders caught during the drive face hefty fines and potential legal action. The department has also said it will notify the aggregator platforms whose drivers were found in violation, asking them to delist such operators.

Some ride-hailing companies have previously sought clarity on the rules, arguing that state policies are ambiguous. However, transport authorities maintain that the law is clear, and enforcement will continue until compliance improves.

Officials have not confirmed whether the drive will expand to other cities in the state, but they hinted that similar operations are being planned in Mysuru and Hubballi.

What Happens Next

The transport department is expected to release a detailed report on the seizures, including the number of repeat offenders and platform penalties. Commuters are advised to check whether their bike taxi has a valid permit before boarding.

In the coming weeks, authorities may also hold meetings with aggregator representatives to discuss a compliance framework. Until then, riders and platforms should brace for more such checks across Bengaluru.
