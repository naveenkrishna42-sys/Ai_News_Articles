Why Assam's Kamakhya Temple Shuts Down for Three Days Every Year

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Kamakhya Temple in Assam closes for three days during the Ambubachi Mela.
  • The closure is linked to the belief that Goddess Kamakhya menstruates during this time.
  • The temple reopens on the fourth day with celebrations marking fertility and renewal.

The Annual Closure of Kamakhya Temple

Every year, the Kamakhya Temple in Assam closes its doors for three days. This happens during the Ambubachi Mela, a major festival held in June. The shutdown is not due to maintenance or a lack of priests. It follows a centuries-old tradition rooted in local belief.

The temple, situated on the Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, is one of the most revered Shakti Peethas in India. Thousands of devotees visit the shrine daily. But for these three days, the inner sanctum stays locked.

The Belief Behind the Shutdown

According to temple tradition, the three-day period marks the annual menstruation of Goddess Kamakhya. The temple trust maintains that the goddess is in a state of rest during this time. No rituals are performed. The main doors remain shut, and devotees are not allowed to offer prayers inside.

This belief is linked to the temple's mythology. The Kamakhya Temple is dedicated to the goddess of fertility and desire. The closure is seen as a time of purification and renewal. The goddess is considered to be in her menstrual phase, after which she returns to her full power.

What Happens During the Closure

During the three days, no regular worship takes place. The priests and temple staff follow strict codes of conduct. No cooked food is offered. Only fruits, flowers, and raw items are allowed. Devotees are permitted to pray from outside the main temple building.

The Ambubachi Mela attracts a large number of sadhus, ascetics, and pilgrims. Many camp near the temple. The atmosphere is one of austerity and devotion. The temple complex remains active, but the main shrine stays closed.

Reopening and Celebrations

On the fourth day, the temple reopens with elaborate ceremonies. The goddess is bathed, clothed, and offered fresh food. This marks the end of her menstrual period. The reopening is celebrated with great enthusiasm.

Devotees then enter the sanctum to receive prasad. The temple trust distributes cloth pieces soaked in the goddess's “blood,” which is considered highly auspicious. This practice draws many visitors from across the country.

What to Watch Next

The temple management will announce the exact dates for next year's Ambubachi Mela closer to the event. Visitors planning to attend should check for updates on the closure schedule. The tradition remains unchanged, drawing thousands who seek the goddess's blessings during this unique period.
