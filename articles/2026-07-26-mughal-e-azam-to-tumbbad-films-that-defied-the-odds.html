Mughal-e-Azam to Tumbbad: Films That Defied the Odds

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mughal-e-Azam took 16 years and nearly bankrupted its producer.
  • Sohum Shah spent over a decade bringing Tumbbad to the screen.
  • Gangs of Wasseypur faced financial hurdles and a delayed release.

The Epic That Took a Lifetime

K Asif's Mughal-e-Azam is often called the crown jewel of Indian cinema. But few remember that the film was nearly never made. Conceived in the 1940s, the project ran into one crisis after another. The original lead, Chandramohan, died during production. Then Dilip Kumar came on board, but the film's budget ballooned.

Producer Shapoorji Pallonji had to sell his wife's jewellery to keep the sets standing. The famous 'Sheesh Mahal' set alone cost a fortune. It took 16 years and a small fortune to finally release the film in 1960. Today, it is a classic, but back then, it was a gamble that nearly broke everyone involved.

Horror and Hurdles: The Tumbbad Story

Decades later, another film faced a similar battle. Tumbbad, the 2018 horror-fantasy, was a labour of love for actor-producer Sohum Shah. The idea was born in 2012, but the film went through multiple rewrites and funding troubles. Shah spent years pitching the project to studios that turned it down.

The team shot the film in a remote village in Maharashtra, often in harsh conditions. Post-production took over a year because of the complex visual effects. When it finally released, Tumbbad won critical acclaim but had a modest box office run. It later found a massive audience on streaming platforms.

Gangs of Wasseypur: A Saga of Patience

Anurag Kashyap's Gangs of Wasseypur was another film that defied expectations. The project was initially conceived as a single film but grew into a two-part epic. The budget was tight, and the production faced delays because of casting issues and location problems.

Manoj Bajpayee and Nawazuddin Siddiqui were on board, but the film's unconventional structure worried distributors. It took years to find a buyer. When it released in 2012, the film was a critical hit and became a cult classic. It proved that a film's journey doesn't have to be smooth to be successful.

What These Films Teach Us

These stories are not just about money or time. They are about the stubbornness of filmmakers who refused to give up. Mughal-e-Azam survived a change of lead actor and a war. Tumbbad survived a decade of rejection. Gangs of Wasseypur survived a sceptical industry.

Each film eventually found its audience, but the road was brutal. The lesson is simple: great art often comes from the hardest battles. The next time a film takes years to release, remember that it might just be another Mughal-e-Azam in the making.

What remains to be seen is whether today's streaming-driven industry will allow such long-gestation projects to survive. The patience of a theatrical audience is shrinking, but the hunger for unique stories is not.
