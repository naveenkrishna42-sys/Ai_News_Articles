SEBI Slaps Ban on JPMorgan Unit Over CAS Price Rigging

📅 2026-08-21 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • SEBI banned a JPMorgan unit from trading following allegations of price manipulation.
  • The investigation focused on irregularities during the Closing Auction Session (CAS).
  • Two other firms face fines totaling ₹3.7 crore for similar manipulative activities.

Regulatory Crackdown on Market Manipulation

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has taken decisive action against a JPMorgan unit, barring it from certain trading activities. The move follows a detailed investigation into irregularities detected during the Closing Auction Session (CAS) of the stock market. This intervention underscores the regulator’s intensified scrutiny of high-frequency trading practices.

Market participants view this as a stern warning from the watchdog. By targeting a major global entity, the regulator has signaled that no institution is exempt from compliance standards. The restriction is expected to disrupt current trading operations within the unit, pending further regulatory review.

The Mechanism of CAS Rigging

The Closing Auction Session is a critical window between 3:30 PM and 4:00 PM, designed to help determine the official closing price of stocks. Allegations suggest that the accused firms manipulated this phase to artificially influence the Sensex. By placing large, strategic orders during the final minutes, traders can distort the closing price to benefit their open positions.

Regulators have spent months monitoring patterns that deviate from standard liquidity provision. These manipulative tactics create a false sense of demand or supply, misleading retail and institutional investors alike. SEBI’s latest order aims to restore integrity to this specific window of the trading day.

Fines and Broader Consequences

Beyond the ban on the JPMorgan unit, SEBI has imposed financial penalties on two additional firms involved in the scheme. These entities have been ordered to pay a combined fine of ₹3.7 crore. The regulator maintains that these actions constitute a clear violation of fair trade practices and market transparency.

  • Strict penalties for entities distorting closing prices.
  • Increased surveillance on auction session order books.
  • Heightened risk management requirements for institutional desks.

Industry analysts suggest the fines are intended to act as a deterrent for other proprietary trading desks. While the total monetary impact may seem small for large firms, the reputational cost of a SEBI-sanctioned ban is significant. Compliance departments across the financial sector are now reviewing their internal protocols to avoid similar scrutiny.

What to Watch

Market observers will now focus on how the affected entities appeal these orders. SEBI is expected to release further guidelines aimed at tightening the mechanics of the Closing Auction Session in the coming weeks.
