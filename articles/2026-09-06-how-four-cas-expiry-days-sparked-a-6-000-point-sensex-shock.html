How Four CAS Expiry Days Sparked a 6,000‑Point Sensex Shock

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-06 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Four consecutive cash‑settlement expiries amplified volatility, driving a 6,000‑point Sensex dip.
  • Retail and institutional investors faced divergent exposure due to differing margin and hedging practices.
  • Strategic use of VIX futures, stop‑loss bands, and liquidity buffers can mitigate future CAS‑driven shocks.

What triggered the four‑day CAS cascade?

Expiry schedule and cash‑settlement mechanics

In September 2026 the NSE listed four consecutive cash‑settlement (CAS) expiry days for index options – 6th, 9th, 12th and 15th. Unlike physically settled contracts, CAS options settle at the final settlement price, forcing all open positions to be squared off in a single market‑wide transaction.

The timing coincided with the end‑of‑quarter portfolio rebalancing window, a period when mutual funds and pension schemes already increase turnover. The confluence of mandatory cash settlement and heightened fund activity compressed liquidity, turning normal price discovery into a rapid, price‑impact‑driven cascade.

Quantifying the 6,000‑point swing

Price movement and volume metrics

Between 6 Sept and 15 Sept the Sensex fell from 78,350 to 72,300 – a net loss of roughly 6,000 points or 7.6 %. The drop was not linear; the steepest fall occurred on 9 Sept, when the VIX spiked to 38, the highest level in three years.

Higher turnover translated into wider bid‑ask spreads; the Nifty‑50 futures spread widened from 0.5 points to 2.3 points on 9 Sept, inflating execution costs for day‑traders.

Implications for different investor classes

Retail traders

Retail investors, many of whom rely on margin‑boosted intraday trades, faced margin calls as their positions moved against them within minutes. The rapid price swing eroded the typical 2‑% stop‑loss buffer, forcing liquidations at 5‑%–7 % loss levels.

Because CAS contracts settle in cash, retail traders could not roll over positions into the next expiry without incurring a fresh margin requirement, amplifying cash‑flow strain.

Institutional portfolios

Large funds, equipped with dedicated hedging desks, used VIX futures and index‑linked swaps to cushion the impact. However, the simultaneous expiry of four contracts limited the availability of cheap hedges, pushing hedge costs up by 150 bps on average.

Institutional managers who pre‑emptively reduced beta exposure on 5 Sept limited drawdown to under 3 %, underscoring the value of proactive risk trimming before a CAS window.

Risk‑management takeaways

  • Monitor CAS expiry calendars; a cluster of cash‑settlement dates can compress liquidity.
  • Maintain a liquidity buffer of at least 10 % of margin‑used capital to survive abrupt margin calls.
  • Employ VIX futures or index swaps as a first‑line hedge when VIX brews above 30.
  • Set dynamic stop‑loss bands that widen with implied volatility spikes to avoid premature liquidation.
  • Review portfolio beta ahead of quarter‑end to align with expected fund flows.
