Feeble Trough May Bring Rain to Coastal Tamil Nadu, Interior Stays Hot

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A feeble trough over the Bay of Bengal may bring light rain to coastal Tamil Nadu.
  • Interior districts will continue to experience sweltering heat.
  • The break from dry conditions is expected to be brief and uneven.

The Regional Meteorological Centre in Chennai has indicated that a feeble trough over the Bay of Bengal could bring some relief from the ongoing dry spell in parts of Tamil Nadu. Coastal districts, particularly those along the northern coast, may see light to moderate rain over the next two days. However, forecasters caution that this is not a widespread weather event.

Limited Relief Along the Coast

The trough, currently positioned off the Andhra Pradesh coast, is expected to move southwards. This could trigger isolated showers in districts such as Chennai, Kanchipuram, and Cuddalore. Officials said the rain, if it materialises, would be brief and patchy.

Residents in these areas may experience a temporary dip in daytime temperatures. The maximum temperature in Chennai, which has hovered around 37 degrees Celsius, could drop by a couple of degrees. But the respite is unlikely to last beyond 48 hours.

Interior Districts to Remain Sweltering

The story is different for interior Tamil Nadu. Districts like Salem, Erode, Madurai, and Tiruchirappalli will continue to face hot and humid conditions. The trough's influence is not expected to reach these regions.

Daytime temperatures in these areas are likely to stay above 39 degrees Celsius. The lack of cloud cover and dry northwesterly winds will keep the heat intense. Officials have advised residents to stay hydrated and avoid prolonged exposure to the sun.

What Is a Feeble Trough?

A trough is an elongated area of relatively low pressure. When it is described as 'feeble', it means the associated weather activity is weak and disorganised. Such systems often produce scattered, light rain rather than heavy downpours.

This particular trough is not expected to intensify. Weather models show it dissipating by the weekend. The limited moisture available in the atmosphere also restricts its potential to generate significant rainfall.

Mixed Outlook for the Region

The dry spell has been a concern for farmers in rain-fed areas. The brief break may offer some moisture for standing crops, but it is not enough to alleviate the overall deficit. The northeast monsoon, which is the main rain-bearing season for Tamil Nadu, is still months away.

Meanwhile, the state's water reservoirs continue to report below-average storage levels. Officials are monitoring the situation and have not ruled out the possibility of water restrictions in some districts if the dry weather persists.

Forecasters will be watching the Bay of Bengal for any development next week. For now, the feeble trough offers only a faint glimmer of hope for a parched region.
