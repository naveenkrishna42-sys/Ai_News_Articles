AIIMS Doctor Shares Tips to Manage High Cholesterol for a Healthy Heart

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • High cholesterol is a major risk factor for heart disease, which kills 32% of people globally.
  • An AIIMS doctor recommends simple dietary swaps like reducing saturated fats and increasing fibre.
  • Regular exercise and weight management are crucial for maintaining healthy cholesterol levels.

Heart disease remains the world's biggest killer. The World Health Organization calls it the number one cause of death, responsible for 32% of all global fatalities. High cholesterol is a key contributor to this statistic.

In India, the numbers are equally worrying. A sedentary lifestyle and changing dietary habits have pushed cholesterol levels up across age groups. Doctors now see patients in their 30s with dangerously high lipid profiles.

What is High Cholesterol?

Cholesterol is a waxy substance your body needs to build cells. Problems arise when levels of low-density lipoprotein (LDL) — the 'bad' cholesterol — become too high. This can clog arteries, leading to heart attacks or strokes.

An AIIMS doctor recently shared practical tips for keeping cholesterol in check. The advice is straightforward and does not require expensive supplements or extreme diets.

Dietary Changes That Work

The first step is reducing saturated fats. These are found in red meat, butter, cheese, and fried foods. Swapping them for unsaturated fats — olive oil, nuts, avocados — can make a measurable difference.

Increase soluble fibre. Oats, barley, beans, apples, and carrots help absorb cholesterol and remove it from your body before it enters the bloodstream. Adding just one serving of oats to your breakfast can help.

Eat more omega-3 fatty acids. Fatty fish like salmon, mackerel, and sardines are excellent sources. For vegetarians, flaxseeds and walnuts are good alternatives.

Lifestyle Habits That Protect Your Heart

Exercise is non-negotiable. The AIIMS expert recommends at least 30 minutes of moderate activity five days a week. Brisk walking, cycling, or swimming all count. Regular movement helps raise HDL — the 'good' cholesterol — which protects your heart.

Weight management goes hand in hand with exercise. Even a 5-10% reduction in body weight can improve cholesterol numbers. This is especially relevant for those with a family history of heart disease.

Avoid smoking and limit alcohol. Smoking damages blood vessels and lowers good cholesterol. Alcohol, especially in excess, raises triglyceride levels.

When to See a Doctor

Not everyone needs medication. For many, lifestyle changes are enough. But if your LDL remains high despite diet and exercise, your doctor may prescribe statins. These drugs are safe and effective when taken as directed.

The AIIMS doctor stresses that routine check-ups are vital. A simple blood test called a lipid profile can tell you your numbers. Adults should get this done once a year, especially after age 30.

Heart disease does not announce itself. By the time symptoms appear, damage is often already done. Managing cholesterol is one of the most effective ways to prevent it.

What comes next? Public health experts are pushing for more awareness campaigns in local languages. The goal is to make heart-healthy habits accessible to everyone, not just those who can afford gym memberships or organic food.
