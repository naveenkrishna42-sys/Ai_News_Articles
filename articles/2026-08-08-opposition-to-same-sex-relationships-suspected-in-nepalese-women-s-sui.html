Opposition to same-sex relationships suspected in Nepalese women's suicide in Bengaluru

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two Nepalese women found dead in their Bengaluru residence; suicide suspected.
  • Police suspect opposition to their same-sex relationship from family members as a possible trigger.
  • Authorities probing circumstances; no official confirmation yet on the motive.

Bengaluru: Two Nepalese women were found dead in their rented apartment in the city on Friday, in what police suspect is a case of suicide linked to familial opposition to their same-sex relationship. The incident has sent shockwaves through the local community and raised questions about the pressures faced by LGBTQ+ individuals, particularly those from conservative backgrounds.

Discovery and initial probe

The bodies were discovered in the early hours of the morning at a residence in the southern part of Bengaluru. Police were alerted by neighbours who reported a foul smell emanating from the flat. Upon breaking in, they found the two women, both in their late twenties, hanging from a ceiling fan.

No suicide note was recovered from the spot, a senior police officer told The New Indian Express. However, preliminary inquiries suggest the women had been in a relationship for several years and were facing intense pressure from their families back in Nepal to end it. "We are investigating all angles," the officer said, requesting anonymity.

Family opposition suspected

Sources close to the investigation said the women had recently returned from a trip to Nepal, where they reportedly faced confrontation from relatives who disapproved of their relationship. The couple had been living in Bengaluru for about two years, working in the private sector, and had kept their personal life largely private.

"It appears they were distressed after their families learned about their relationship and expressed strong opposition," a police official said. "The women may have felt isolated and saw no way out." The police have contacted the families in Nepal and are awaiting their arrival for further questioning.

LGBTQ+ pressures in focus

The case has reignited discussions about the mental health challenges faced by LGBTQ+ individuals in India and neighbouring countries, where societal and familial acceptance remains limited despite legal victories. In 2018, the Supreme Court of India decriminalised homosexuality, but social stigma persists, often driving individuals to conceal their identities or face rejection.

Activists in Bengaluru expressed grief over the tragedy, calling for greater mental health support and safe spaces for queer individuals. "This is a heartbreaking reminder that legal progress does not always translate into social acceptance," said a local queer rights advocate. "Many still face immense pressure from family, especially in immigrant communities where cultural ties are strong."

Investigation ongoing

The police have registered a case of unnatural death and are conducting a thorough investigation. They are also examining the women's phones and laptops to understand their state of mind in the days leading up to the incident. Officials have not yet confirmed whether any external coercion was involved, and post-mortem reports are awaited.

The Nepalese consulate in Bengaluru has been informed and is extending consular assistance to the families. Meanwhile, the bodies have been sent to a government hospital for autopsy, after which they will be handed over to relatives.

As the investigation unfolds, the focus remains on unravelling the circumstances that led to the tragic end of two young lives. The case also underscores the urgent need for culturally sensitive mental health resources and dialogue around LGBTQ+ acceptance, particularly within diaspora communities. Authorities are urging anyone with information to come forward, and counselling services have been offered to friends of the deceased.

What happens next will depend on the findings of the autopsy and the statements of the families. The police have indicated that if evidence of abetment emerges, charges could be filed. For now, the community mourns a loss that might have been prevented with greater understanding and support.
