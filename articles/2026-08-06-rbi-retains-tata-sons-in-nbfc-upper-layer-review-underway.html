RBI Retains Tata Sons in NBFC Upper Layer; Review Underway

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has retained Tata Sons in the Upper Layer NBFC list under a new principle-based framework.
  • RBI Governor Malhotra confirmed the decision, citing the company's continued qualification.
  • Tata Sons' deregistration plea remains under review, with a fresh NBFC-UL list expected soon.

RBI's Decision Under New Framework

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has retained Tata Sons in the Upper Layer of its Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) list, a decision that comes under a newly adopted principle-based framework. RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra confirmed the move, stating that Tata Sons continues to meet the criteria for this category under the revised assessment method.

The Upper Layer classification, introduced in 2021, applies to NBFCs that pose heightened systemic risk due to their size, interconnectedness, and complexity. Being in this tier means stricter regulatory oversight, including enhanced governance and capital requirements.

The decision follows months of deliberation as the central bank transitioned from a purely quantitative threshold-based approach to a more nuanced, principle-driven evaluation.

Governor's Stance and Rationale

Governor Malhotra, addressing reporters, explained that the new framework evaluates a broader set of parameters beyond just asset size. These include the nature of activities, shadow banking risks, and the entity's role in the financial system.

"Tata Sons, given its significant presence and interconnected operations, continues to qualify under this framework," Malhotra said. He did not elaborate on specific metrics but emphasised that the assessment is now more holistic.

The RBI's stance signals that large conglomerates with diverse financial interests cannot easily escape this regulatory bracket, even if they argue their primary business is non-financial.

Tata Sons' Deregistration Plea Hangs in Balance

Separately, Tata Sons' application for deregistration as an NBFC remains under review. The company has long contended that its core identity is that of a holding company for the Tata Group's various businesses, not a lender or financial intermediary.

Sources indicate that the RBI has not yet taken a final call on this plea, which could potentially remove Tata Sons from the NBFC fold entirely. If accepted, it would mark a significant shift in how the central bank treats investment holding companies within conglomerates.

Industry observers note that a decision either way will set a precedent for other corporate houses with similar structures. The outcome could reshape the regulatory perimeter for several large Indian business groups.

What Lies Ahead

The RBI is expected to release a fresh list of NBFCs in the Upper Layer soon. This will clarify which entities fall under the stricter regime and could include additions or removals based on the new principle-based criteria.

For Tata Sons, the immediate future hinges on the deregistration review. A rejection would mean continued compliance with the Upper Layer norms, while approval could trigger a broader reassessment of holding companies across India Inc.

Market participants will watch closely for the final list and any accompanying commentary from the central bank, as it will provide clarity on how the regulator intends to balance systemic risk oversight with corporate structure flexibility.
