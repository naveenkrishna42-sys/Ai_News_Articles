RBI bans banks from disabling borrowers' mobile devices

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has barred banks and lenders from disabling mobile devices of defaulting borrowers.
  • The directive aims to curb coercive recovery practices and protect consumer rights.
  • Lenders must now adopt fair and transparent recovery mechanisms.
  • The move follows complaints of unethical practices in loan recovery.

RBI's new directive on loan recovery

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has issued a clear directive to all banks and lending institutions: no more disabling of mobile devices belonging to defaulting borrowers. The central bank's order, reported by The Economic Times, puts an end to this coercive recovery tactic that had drawn widespread criticism.

Under the new rule, lenders cannot remotely lock or disable the smartphones of borrowers who have fallen behind on repayments. This practice, often embedded in the fine print of digital lending apps, had become a flashpoint for consumer grievances.

Why the RBI stepped in

Over recent years, the use of device-level controls as a recovery tool has raised serious ethical and legal concerns. Borrowers, many of whom were already under financial stress, found their primary communication tool rendered useless without warning.

The RBI's move signals a firm stance against predatory behaviour in the lending ecosystem. By prohibiting such actions, the central bank seeks to restore balance in the lender-borrower relationship, ensuring that recovery processes remain within legal and humane boundaries.

This is not the first time the RBI has tightened norms around digital lending. The regulator has consistently pushed for transparency, fair practices, and accountability among banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs).

Impact on lenders and borrowers

For lenders, the directive means a significant shift in how they handle delinquencies. Banks and fintech firms will now have to rely on conventional recovery channels—calls, notices, and legal recourse—rather than technological strong-arming.

Borrowers, on the other hand, get a layer of protection that was long overdue. They can now engage with lenders without the fear of losing access to their devices, which are often essential for work, communication, and daily life.

The RBI's order is expected to curb the misuse of device permissions that many digital lending apps demand during the onboarding process. It also serves as a warning to lenders that non-compliance will attract regulatory action.

What this means for the digital lending space

The Indian digital lending market has grown rapidly, with apps offering instant loans to millions. But this growth has also brought complaints of harassment and unethical recovery methods. The RBI's latest directive is a step toward cleaning up that space.

Industry experts believe the move will push lenders to adopt more robust risk assessment and collection strategies. Instead of relying on device lockdowns, they will need to improve their underwriting processes and engage with borrowers more constructively.

Consumer rights advocates have welcomed the development, calling it a positive step for financial inclusion. They argue that access to mobile devices is now a basic necessity, and denying it amounts to an unfair restriction on individual freedom.

Looking ahead

The RBI has not specified the penalties for violations, but the message is unambiguous. Lenders must recalibrate their recovery frameworks to be compliant and ethical.

As the digital lending ecosystem evolves, borrowers can expect greater scrutiny of app-level permissions and more stringent oversight of recovery practices. The focus now shifts to implementation—how quickly and effectively banks and fintechs adapt to the new rules will determine the next chapter in India's credit market.
