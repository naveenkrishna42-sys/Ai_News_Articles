Bengaluru's Mavalli Tiffin Rooms to open first Australia outlet in Sydney

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • MTR to open its first Australian outlet in Sydney
  • The 100-year-old Bengaluru brand is expanding internationally
  • No opening date or location details have been announced yet

A century-old legacy heads Down Under

Bengaluru's beloved Mavalli Tiffin Rooms (MTR), the iconic eatery that has served filter coffee and crispy dosas for over a century, is set to cross oceans. The restaurant will open its first Australian outlet in Sydney, marking a significant step in the brand's global journey.

The announcement, made this week, has sparked excitement among the city's food lovers and the Indian diaspora in Australia. For generations of Bengalureans, MTR is more than a restaurant—it is a culinary landmark rooted in tradition.

From Lalbagh to the world

Founded in 1924 near the Lalbagh Botanical Gardens, MTR began as a small vegetarian restaurant. Over the decades, it grew into a household name, famous for its authentic Karnataka-style meals, rava idli, and signature masala dosa.

The brand also became a pioneer in packaged foods, selling spice powders, mixes, and ready-to-eat snacks across India and abroad. Yet, its restaurant arm has remained largely domestic, with branches mainly in Bengaluru and a few other Indian cities.

Sydney now becomes the latest destination, and the first in Australia, for this storied brand. The move signals MTR's intent to tap into a growing appetite for regional Indian cuisine among international audiences.

Why Sydney?

Australia has seen a steady rise in Indian migration and tourism over the years. Sydney, as the country's largest city, offers a dense, diverse food market with a strong South Asian presence.

Industry observers note that MTR's entry is likely to cater not just to expatriates craving home-style food, but also to Australians curious about authentic South Indian flavours. The brand's vegetarian-only menu could also appeal to a wider health-conscious demographic.

However, MTR faces stiff competition in Sydney, where several Indian restaurants already operate successfully. Its century-old reputation and loyal customer base may give it an edge, but adapting to local tastes and sourcing ingredients will be key.

Expansion plans and challenges

MTR's management has not yet confirmed the exact location within Sydney or a timeline for the launch. Company representatives have indicated that the outlet will be a full-service restaurant, likely mirroring the traditional Bengaluru dining experience.

Logistical details, such as supply chains for authentic spices and ingredients, remain unclear. Officials have also not disclosed whether the Australian venture will be a franchise or a company-owned operation.

This is not MTR's first international foray. The brand has previously explored markets in the Middle East and Southeast Asia, though with limited restaurant presence. Packaged foods, however, are already exported to several countries.

A growing trend of Indian food exports

MTR's move reflects a broader trend of Indian food brands expanding overseas. From street-food chains to fine-dining concepts, Indian cuisine is gaining global recognition, and established names are leveraging their heritage to stand out.

For Bengaluru, the news is a point of pride. The city has watched MTR evolve from a small tiffin room to a cultural icon, and now, an international ambassador of its culinary identity.

What remains to be seen is how MTR adapts its century-old recipes to an Australian palate. The brand has historically been cautious about change, but international expansion demands flexibility. If successful, Sydney could become a launchpad for further outlets across Australia and beyond.

Food enthusiasts and the Indian community in Sydney will be watching closely as MTR takes this leap. The coming months are expected to bring more details on the menu, location, and opening date.
