37 Linked to India Crime Rings Charged in US, California Ties

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • 37 individuals charged for links to Indian crime rings operating in the US.
  • Offenses include racketeering and fraud with ties to Southern California.
  • Investigation highlights international scope of organized crime networks.

Federal Charges Unsealed

A federal grand jury has indicted 37 individuals accused of being part of criminal networks based in India. The charges, unsealed this week, detail a sweeping racketeering and fraud conspiracy that prosecutors say extended into Southern California.

The defendants face a range of counts, including conspiracy to commit wire fraud and money laundering. Authorities allege the groups operated call centers and other schemes that defrauded American victims of millions of dollars.

Southern California Connection

Investigators traced some of the fraudulent activities to locations within Southern California. The region served as a hub for laundering money and coordinating logistics for the overseas operations, according to court documents.

Details on specific cities or businesses involved have not been released. Officials say the investigation remains active, with potential for additional charges.

How the Schemes Worked

The accused are believed to have impersonated government officials and tech support agents. They would contact victims, often elderly, and demand payments for fake debts or services.

Proceeds were funneled through a network of bank accounts and shell companies, some set up in the US. The indictment alleges the groups used sophisticated methods to avoid detection.

International Cooperation

The case is the result of a joint operation between US federal agencies and Indian law enforcement. Extradition proceedings for some defendants are expected to begin soon.

Prosecutors describe the network as highly organized, with clear hierarchies and roles. The charges aim to dismantle what they call a significant threat to financial systems on both sides of the Pacific.

What Comes Next

Arraignments for the defendants in US custody are scheduled in the coming weeks. Those still at large, believed to be in India, are subjects of international arrest warrants.

Authorities are urging potential victims to come forward as the investigation broadens. The case underscores the growing challenge of cross-border financial crime.
