India urges citizens to leave Iran, postpone travel amid instability

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Indian embassy in Iran advises citizens to postpone travel and leave the country
  • Advisory cites 'rise in instability' without specifying details
  • Move follows similar advisories from other nations amid regional tensions

The Indian embassy in Iran has issued a stark advisory, urging all Indian nationals to leave the country and postpone non-essential travel. The warning, published on Sunday, cites a "rise in instability" as the reason for the heightened alert.

The embassy's statement advises Indian citizens currently in Iran to depart via available commercial flights. It also asks those planning to visit to defer their travel until further notice.

Advisory details and context

The advisory stops short of detailing the specific nature of the instability. Officials have not confirmed whether this is linked to internal protests, regional military tensions, or a broader security deterioration.

This move aligns with similar steps taken by other countries in recent weeks. Several Western nations have also updated their travel advisories for Iran, reflecting growing unease over the situation in the region.

Previous warnings and current stance

India has a significant diaspora in Iran, including professionals, students, and businesspeople. The embassy has not disclosed the exact number of Indians currently in the country.

In its advisory, the embassy has asked citizens to register with the mission if they choose to remain. It has also shared emergency contact numbers for assistance.

Broader regional picture

Iran has witnessed multiple flashpoints over the past year, including domestic unrest and heightened rhetoric with Israel and the United States. The Indian advisory does not link the warning to any single event.

Diplomatic sources indicate the embassy is monitoring the situation hour by hour. The Indian Ministry of External Affairs has not issued a separate statement, but the embassy's advisory carries the government's full backing.

Commercial airlines continue to operate flights out of Tehran and other major cities. The embassy has not reported any disruption to air travel as of now.

What this means for travellers

For Indians with booked travel to Iran, the advisory effectively cancels plans unless they are deemed essential. Travel insurance may not cover trips made against government advice.

The embassy has urged citizens to stay in touch via its social media channels and website for updates. It has not specified a timeline for a review of the advisory.

Observers will watch for statements from other Indian missions in the Middle East, as the region remains volatile. The coming days may see further clarifications from New Delhi on the duration and scope of this warning.
