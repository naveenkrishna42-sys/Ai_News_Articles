AI investor Leopold Aschenbrenner unwinds all public stock positions after losses

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Leopold Aschenbrenner forced to sell all public stock holdings after significant losses.
  • The liquidation affects a portfolio that once bet heavily on AI-related equities.
  • Sources say the unwind was not voluntary but driven by margin calls and investor pressure.

A forced retreat from public markets

Leopold Aschenbrenner, the high-profile AI investor known for bold bets on artificial intelligence stocks, has liquidated his entire public equity portfolio, sources told Forex Factory. The unwind comes after steep losses that made his positions untenable.

The move marks a dramatic reversal for Aschenbrenner, who had built a reputation as a vocal bull on AI-related companies. His fund had concentrated holdings in several high-growth tech stocks that have tumbled in recent months.

What triggered the sell-off

Sources familiar with the matter said the liquidation was not voluntary. Margin calls from brokers and pressure from limited partners forced Aschenbrenner to exit all public stock positions. The exact size of the losses could not be confirmed, but they are understood to be substantial.

The fund's strategy had relied heavily on leveraged positions in AI stocks, a bet that soured as the sector corrected sharply. Several of the companies in his portfolio reported disappointing earnings, triggering a cascade of selling.

Background of the investor

Aschenbrenner first gained attention for his bullish calls on artificial intelligence, predicting the technology would reshape global markets. He raised capital from institutional investors and wealthy individuals, promising outsized returns from the AI boom.

His fund had been one of the more aggressive players in the space, often taking concentrated positions in a handful of stocks. That strategy worked well during the AI rally but left little room for error when sentiment shifted.

Market impact and reaction

The forced liquidation adds to selling pressure on already weak AI stocks, though the broader market impact remains unclear. Other investors in the sector are closely watching for further unwinds.

Industry peers expressed caution. One fund manager, speaking on condition of anonymity, said: "When a big player gets margin-called, it can create a ripple effect. Everyone is checking their own exposure."

Aschenbrenner's office did not respond to requests for comment. The fund has not issued any public statement about the move.

What comes next

Aschenbrenner's ability to raise fresh capital is now in doubt. With his public equity positions gone, the investor may shift focus to private markets or shut down the fund entirely. For now, the AI investment community is waiting to see if other leveraged players face similar pressure.
