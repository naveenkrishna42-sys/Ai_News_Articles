Bright meteoroid streaks across Punjab sky, videos go viral

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • A bright light was seen streaking across the Punjab sky on Thursday night.
  • Viral videos captured the event, leaving residents awestruck and speculating about a meteoroid.
  • Experts suggest it was likely a meteoroid burning up in Earth's atmosphere.

Residents across Punjab were left stunned on Thursday night as a bright light streaked across the sky, lighting up the darkness for a few seconds. Videos of the celestial event quickly went viral on social media, with many speculating whether it was a meteoroid.

The flash was reported from multiple districts, including Amritsar, Ludhiana, and Jalandhar. Witnesses described it as a brilliant white-blue streak that appeared to break apart before vanishing.

Viral videos capture the moment

Several videos posted online show the sky suddenly illuminated by a fast-moving object. In one clip, the light appears to grow brighter before fading out, leaving a trail of smoke.

"I was on my rooftop when I saw this huge ball of fire rushing down. It was unlike anything I've ever seen," said a resident from Amritsar, who filmed the event. The videos have been shared thousands of times, with users debating its origin.

Meteoroid or space debris?

Experts suggest the phenomenon was likely a meteoroid entering Earth's atmosphere. When these rocky fragments from space hit the atmosphere at high speeds, they burn up, producing bright streaks known as meteors.

"What we saw was typical of a meteoroid. The brightness and the trail indicate it was a decent-sized piece, maybe a few metres across, that disintegrated," said a scientist from the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, speaking to The Tribune.

Others have not ruled out the possibility of space debris—man-made objects like defunct satellites—re-entering the atmosphere. But officials have not yet confirmed any specific debris event over Punjab.

No reports of damage or impact

So far, there have been no reports of any debris reaching the ground or causing damage. Meteoroids of this size usually vaporise completely before hitting the surface.

Local authorities said they have not received any distress calls related to the event. The Punjab State Disaster Management Authority said it was monitoring the situation but had no immediate cause for concern.

Astronomy enthusiasts in the region have been advised to report any unusual sightings to the nearest planetarium or science centre. The Nehru Planetarium in New Delhi said it had received several queries about the light.

For now, the sighting remains a talking point across Punjab. Social media is flooded with reactions ranging from awe to scientific curiosity. Many are hoping for more details from space agencies about the object's trajectory and origin.

What to watch: The Indian Space Research Organisation and other agencies may release further analysis in the coming days if more data emerges from satellite tracking or ground observations.
