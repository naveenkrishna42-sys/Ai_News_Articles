Hyderabad footpaths turn hazardous after encroachment drives

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Recent drives to clear encroachments have left footpaths damaged and uneven.
  • Pedestrians are forced onto carriageways, increasing accident risks.
  • Civic authorities yet to initiate repair work on affected stretches.

Footpaths left in shambles

Months after authorities removed encroachments from footpaths across Hyderabad, several stretches remain in a state of disrepair. The very paths meant to offer safe passage now pose serious risks to pedestrians, with broken tiles, exposed utility ducts, and uneven surfaces dotting the city's thoroughfares.

Residents and commuters say the situation has worsened post-monsoon, as waterlogging has further eroded the already fragile pavements. Many are forced to walk on the main carriageway, dodging speeding vehicles and two-wheelers that frequently mount the kerb.

Pedestrians bear the brunt

Daily walkers, particularly senior citizens and schoolchildren, are the worst affected. "We have no choice but to step onto the road," said a local shopkeeper near Ameerpet, who requested anonymity. "The footpath is so broken that it's safer to walk on the asphalt, even with traffic whizzing past."

Commuters using public transport also face difficulties, as bus stops and metro stations are often surrounded by damaged walkways. In several areas, stormwater drains remain uncovered, creating open pits that turn deadly after dark.

Encroachment removal, but no repair

Civic agencies have confirmed that the footpath damage stems from recent anti-encroachment drives, during which illegal structures and vendor stalls were dismantled. However, the subsequent restoration of the pavement surface has not been carried out systematically.

Officials from the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) have not yet provided a timeline for repairs. A senior official, speaking on condition of anonymity, acknowledged the issue and said complaints have been received from multiple wards. "We are compiling a list of affected stretches," he said, without elaborating on when work would begin.

Residents demand accountability

Local resident welfare associations have written to the GHMC, urging immediate action. They point out that the situation violates the city's pedestrian-friendly policies and the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, which mandates accessible walkways.

Activists argue that removing encroachments is only half the job. "The authorities must ensure that footpaths are rebuilt to standard width and quality," said a city-based urban planner. "Otherwise, the drives end up harming the very people they were meant to help."

What lies ahead

With the civic body yet to announce a repair schedule, pedestrians in Hyderabad may have to navigate these hazardous paths for weeks to come. The GHMC's next council meeting could see councillors raise the issue, but until then, the onus remains on residents to report damaged stretches through the city's grievance portal.

All eyes are now on the municipal commissioner's office to see if a concrete plan emerges before the next monsoon season.
