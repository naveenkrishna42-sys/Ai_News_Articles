Samsung's foldable lead under threat as Apple enters the market

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Samsung's dominance in foldable phones faces its biggest challenge yet as Apple prepares to launch its first foldable device.
  • Analysts predict Samsung's market share could fall to 32% as competition intensifies.
  • Samsung is reportedly working on a passport-style foldable form factor to differentiate itself.
  • The company has lost recent foldable sales battles to rivals, raising pressure to innovate.

The challenger becomes the challenged

Samsung built the foldable smartphone market. It launched the Galaxy Fold in 2019, when competitors were still perfecting candy-bar slabs. For years, the category was Samsung's to lose.

Now it is losing it. Apple is expected to release its first foldable device within the next year. Analysts predict Samsung's foldable market share could drop to 32 percent once the iPhone maker enters the segment. That would be a steep fall from the near-total dominance Samsung once enjoyed.

PhoneArena reported that Samsung already lost the latest quarterly foldable sales battle, and it was not even close. The company that defined the form factor is now scrambling to defend it.

Why the passport form factor matters

Samsung is reportedly working on a passport-style foldable phone. The design folds vertically like a book, similar to a passport, rather than the current clamshell or horizontal book-style folds. The Verge notes that this form factor matters because it could offer a larger internal screen while keeping the external display practical.

The Indian Express reported that Samsung's passport-style phone is seen as a strategic pivot. The company needs to offer something Apple cannot easily copy. A unique aspect ratio and fold mechanism could give Samsung a differentiating feature.

But the clock is ticking. Apple's supply chain is already locked in for millions of foldable display units. The company has a history of entering mature markets and reshaping them overnight.

Market share erosion

Notebookcheck's analysis shows Samsung's foldable market share is under pressure from multiple directions. Chinese brands like Huawei, Oppo and Honor have launched aggressively priced foldables with impressive hardware. Samsung's latest Galaxy Z Fold and Z Flip models have not generated the same excitement as earlier generations.

The Times of India reported that Samsung now must defend the market it created. Analysts say the company cannot afford to play it safe with incremental updates. The next generation of foldables needs to be a leap, not a step.

Price is another factor. Apple's first foldable is expected to be priced at a premium, but the brand's ecosystem loyalty could pull users away from Samsung even at a higher cost. Samsung's mid-range foldable experiments have not gained enough traction to offset the loss of flagship buyers.

What Samsung must do now

Samsung's R&D pipeline is reportedly focused on durability and display innovation. The passport-style phone could feature a crease-free screen and improved hinge mechanism. These are areas where early foldables faced criticism.

The company also needs to refine its software experience. Foldable phones require apps that adapt seamlessly to different screen sizes. Samsung has worked with Google to improve Android's foldable support, but Apple's ecosystem advantage could be significant.

Marketing will play a role too. Samsung's foldable ads have focused on productivity and multitasking. Apple is likely to position its foldable as a lifestyle device, emphasizing design and simplicity.

The stakes are high. If Samsung loses its lead in foldables, it loses a category it invented. The company that once disrupted the smartphone market now finds itself defending against the biggest disruptor of all.

Apple's foldable launch is still months away. Samsung has a narrow window to release a device that redefines the category. The passport-style phone could be its best bet — or its last chance.
