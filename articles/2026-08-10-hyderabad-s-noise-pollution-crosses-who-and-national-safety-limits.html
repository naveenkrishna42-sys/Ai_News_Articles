Hyderabad's noise pollution crosses WHO and national safety limits

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Noise levels in Hyderabad exceed WHO guidelines and India's ambient noise standards.
  • High traffic and construction are major contributors to the rising decibel levels.
  • Experts call for better enforcement of noise rules and public awareness.
  • Authorities have not yet announced specific new measures to tackle the issue.

Hyderabad's streets are getting louder, and not in a good way. Recent measurements show that noise levels in several parts of the city are exceeding both World Health Organization (WHO) guidelines and India's own ambient noise standards. The findings have raised concerns among residents and health experts about the long-term impact on public well-being.

What the data says

Monitoring conducted across different zones of Hyderabad recorded average noise levels that are several decibels above the permissible limits. According to the WHO, sustained exposure to noise above 55 decibels during the day can lead to health issues, while India's Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) sets the limit for residential areas at 55 dB during daytime and 45 dB at night. However, readings in many parts of the city have consistently crossed these thresholds.

The problem is not new, but the latest data underscores how entrenched the issue has become. Busy intersections, commercial hubs, and even some residential neighbourhoods are bearing the brunt of the noise, which often continues well past midnight.

Why is Hyderabad so loud?

Traffic remains the single biggest contributor. The city's growing vehicle population, frequent honking, and the rumble of heavy vehicles create a constant din, especially during peak hours. Construction activity, which has boomed across Hyderabad in recent years, adds to the cacophony with drilling, piling, and other machinery operating at high volumes.

Unlike air pollution, which gets significant attention, noise pollution often goes unnoticed. There are no visible plumes of smoke or dust, but the effects are real. Prolonged exposure to high noise levels can lead to stress, sleep disturbances, hearing loss, and even cardiovascular problems, say doctors.

Rules exist, but enforcement is weak

India has established noise pollution rules under the Environment (Protection) Act, but implementation in Hyderabad has been patchy at best. The city's police and pollution control board are tasked with monitoring and controlling noise, but the sources say that violations are rarely penalised. The lack of adequate monitoring equipment and personnel further hampers enforcement.

Residents in affected areas have complained about loudspeakers during festivals, construction sites operating at night, and the constant blare of horns. While some awareness campaigns have been run, they have had limited impact. Many experts argue that what is needed is a more systematic approach, including regular monitoring and stricter penalties for repeat offenders.

Health and quality of life at stake

The consequences of chronic noise exposure are not just about annoyance. Public health specialists point out that children studying in noisy environments may face concentration issues, and the elderly are particularly vulnerable to sleep disruption and elevated stress levels. The cumulative effect on a city's productivity and quality of life is significant, even if it is not immediately visible.

Hyderabad is not alone in this. Many Indian cities struggle with noise pollution, but the city's rapid growth makes it a particularly stark example. Without intervention, the situation is likely to worsen as population and vehicular density increase.

What can be done

Experts suggest a multi-pronged strategy: installing real-time noise monitors in key areas, enforcing restrictions on honking and construction hours, and promoting the use of quieter technologies in infrastructure projects. Public awareness is also crucial, as many residents may not even realise that the sounds they are exposed to are harmful.

The city's authorities have yet to announce any concrete steps in response to the latest data. But with growing evidence of the health risks, there is increasing pressure on officials to act. For now, the noise continues, but the conversation around it is getting louder.

As Hyderabad expands, the question is whether its leaders will treat noise pollution with the same urgency as other environmental issues. The next few months will show if any policy changes follow the recent findings, or if the city remains trapped in a cycle of rising decibels.
