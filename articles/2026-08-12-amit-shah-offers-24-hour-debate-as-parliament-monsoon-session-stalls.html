Amit Shah offers 24-hour debate as Parliament monsoon session stalls

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Amit Shah offers a 24-hour debate window to break the Parliament logjam over Delhi protests.
  • Rahul Gandhi insists he is not interested in a lecture and wants a structured discussion.
  • Lok Sabha adjourned till 2 p.m.; Rajya Sabha sees continued protests by opposition MPs.
  • BJP's J.P. Nadda claims no shots were fired; Rahul Gandhi questions if Shah ordered shooting.

Deadlock persists on Day 18 of Monsoon Session

The monsoon session of Parliament remained deadlocked for the 18th consecutive day on Wednesday, with the government and the Opposition trading barbs over the Delhi protests. Union Home Minister Amit Shah said he was 'ready for discussion' on the issue, but Congress leader Rahul Gandhi shot back that he was 'not interested in a lecture'.

The Lok Sabha was adjourned till 2 p.m. shortly after it assembled, while protests continued in the Rajya Sabha. The Opposition has been demanding a detailed statement from the Home Minister on the police action during recent protests in the national capital.

Shah's offer of a 24-hour window

Amit Shah, in a bid to break the logjam, said he was ready to answer all questions and offered a 24-hour window for a structured debate. “I am ready for all questions,” Shah said, according to sources present in the House. The Home Minister's offer is seen as an attempt to counter the Opposition's charge that the government is avoiding a discussion.

However, the Congress and other opposition parties have remained sceptical. They argue that a mere statement without a structured debate would not suffice. The demand for a joint parliamentary committee or a detailed statement from the Prime Minister has also been raised by some members.

Rahul Gandhi's sharp retort

Rahul Gandhi, speaking to reporters outside Parliament, said he was not looking for a lecture. “The country wants to know if Shah ordered the shooting,” Gandhi said, referring to allegations that police fired at protesters. The Congress leader's remarks came after BJP president J.P. Nadda claimed that “no shots were fired” during the protests.

The exchange has further escalated tensions between the two sides. Gandhi also questioned the government's reluctance to have a full-fledged debate, saying the Opposition was being “muzzled”. The Congress has been pressing for a discussion under a rule that allows for a votable motion, which the government has so far resisted.

What the government says

BJP leaders have maintained that the government is open to a discussion but not under the terms dictated by the Opposition. Nadda's assertion that no shots were fired is part of the government's broader defence of its handling of the protests. “The law and order situation in Delhi is under control, and the police acted within the framework of the law,” a senior BJP leader said on condition of anonymity.

The government has also accused the Opposition of stalling the legislative agenda. Several key bills, including the contentious land acquisition amendment, are pending passage in this session. Officials said the prolonged disruption has affected the government's legislative timetable.

What happens next

Both Houses are scheduled to reconvene later in the day. The government has indicated it is willing to extend the session if necessary to pass pending legislation. The Opposition, meanwhile, has vowed to continue its protest until its demands are met.

Political observers say the ball is now in the government's court. If Shah's offer of a 24-hour window is accepted, it could pave the way for a resolution. If not, the deadlock is likely to persist, further stalling parliamentary business.

The coming hours will be crucial as both sides test each other's resolve. Whether the monsoon session ends with a breakthrough or a breakdown remains to be seen.
