US House Advances Bill That Could Impose 100% Tariffs on Indian Imports

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-16 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • The US House has advanced a sanctions bill that could let President Trump impose tariffs of up to 100% on countries buying Russian oil, including India.
  • Indian exports worth nearly $90 billion annually to the US — textiles, gems, pharma, shrimp — face the sharpest risk if the tariff clause is invoked.
  • The bill still needs a final House vote, Senate passage and presidential signature, so no tariff is in force today, but the threat is now legislative, not rhetorical.

What Just Happened in Washington

The US House has cleared a procedural hurdle on a sweeping Russia sanctions bill that would hand President Donald Trump the authority to impose tariffs of up to 100% on countries that continue buying Russian crude. India is squarely in the crosshairs, alongside China, because both remain large purchasers of discounted Russian oil. The bill now heads toward a final House vote before it can move to the Senate and eventually the President's desk.

This is no longer a campaign talking point. It is a legislative mechanism — a cudgel written into law that a future White House can wield without fresh Congressional approval each time.

Why India Is Named, Not Just Implicated

India's crude import basket has leaned heavily on Russian grades since 2022, when Western sanctions pushed Moscow to offer deep discounts. Indian refiners, particularly Reliance Industries and state-run IOC, BPCL and HPCL, have been among the largest beneficiaries. The bill's sponsors argue that this buying spree funds Moscow's war machine.

New Delhi counters that it is simply chasing the cheapest barrel for its 1.4 billion citizens, and that Western capitals themselves continued trading with Russia in other sectors. That argument may win diplomatic sympathy, but it will not stop a tariff clause once enacted.

What 100% Tariffs Would Actually Do to Prices

Consider a $10 shrimp consignment from Andhra Pradesh. A 100% tariff doubles the landed cost to $20 before retail mark-up. American importers would either absorb the hit, renegotiate with Indian suppliers, or switch to Ecuador and Vietnam. In pharma, where Indian generics save US insurers billions, a full tariff would raise drug costs for American patients — a politically awkward outcome that may itself restrain the White House.

For Indian exporters, the practical benchmark is simple: any tariff above 25–30% usually makes a product unviable against Chinese, Vietnamese or Mexican alternatives. A 100% rate is effectively a market ban.

Pros, Cons and Real-World Signals

  • Pro (for the US): Creates maximum leverage to choke Russian oil revenues without deploying troops or sanctions on US firms.
  • Con (for India): Nearly $90 billion in annual exports and millions of jobs across Surat, Tiruppur, Vizag and Ahmedabad sit exposed.
  • Pro (for India): The tariff is discretionary, not automatic — leaving room for diplomacy, oil-import diversification and a negotiated carve-out.
  • Con (for US buyers): Higher prices on generics, diamonds and seafood, with limited short-term substitutes at scale.
  • Watch signal: Any acceleration in Indian refiners cutting Russian crude volumes would signal New Delhi is pre-empting the clause.

What Indian Buyers and Exporters Should Do Now

Nothing changes overnight. The bill needs a final House vote, Senate clearance and a presidential signature. But the smart move is preparation, not panic. Exporters with heavy US exposure should model a 25%, 50% and 100% tariff scenario, review force majeure and price-escalation clauses, and explore Gulf, African and Southeast Asian buyers.

Importers of American goods in India should also watch for reciprocal action from New Delhi, which has historically matched US tariffs tit-for-tat on items like almonds, apples and Harley-Davidson motorcycles. The next 60 to 90 days of legislative movement will decide whether this remains a threat or becomes the new cost of doing business with America.
