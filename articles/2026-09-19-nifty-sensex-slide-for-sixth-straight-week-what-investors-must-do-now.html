Six Weeks of Pain: Nifty and Sensex Post Longest Losing Run

Indian equity benchmarks extended their losing run to a sixth consecutive week, with the Nifty 50 and BSE Sensex closing the week ended 19 September 2026 in the red. Persistent global tensions, elevated crude oil prices and continued foreign institutional investor (FII) selling kept buyers on the back foot, even as domestic mutual fund inflows provided a partial cushion. The sixth straight weekly decline is a rare occurrence and has pushed both indices below their short-term moving averages, a technical signal that traders are watching closely.

For retail investors, the important question is not whether the fall looks scary on the screen but whether the underlying earnings and valuation case for Indian equities has changed. So far, the correction appears sentiment-driven rather than fundamentals-driven, with most largecap earnings estimates for FY27 broadly intact.

What Is Driving the Selloff

Global Tensions and Risk-Off Sentiment

Escalating geopolitical friction across key trade and energy corridors has pushed global investors toward safe-haven assets such as US Treasuries, gold and the dollar. When global risk appetite falls, emerging markets like India are typically among the first to see outflows. Crude oil staying above comfortable levels adds to India's import bill and current account worries, pressuring the rupee and, in turn, rate-sensitive sectors.

FII Outflows and Valuation Reset

Foreign portfolio investors have been net sellers through much of this stretch, trimming exposure to high-valuation pockets. Domestic institutional investors, including SIP-driven mutual fund flows, have absorbed a large part of that supply, which is why the fall has been orderly rather than panic-driven. Still, the tug-of-war between FII selling and DII buying has kept the market range-bound with a downward bias.

Pros and Cons for Investors Right Now

  • Pro: Corrections of this length historically improve entry valuations for long-term SIP investors.
  • Pro: Domestic flows remain resilient, reducing the risk of a disorderly crash.
  • Pro: Defensive sectors offer relative stability for risk-averse portfolios.
  • Con: Six-week losing streaks can extend if global tensions worsen or crude spikes further.
  • Con: Midcap and smallcap valuations, though corrected, are still above long-term averages.
  • Con: FII selling can pressure the rupee and import-heavy sectors.

How Buyers Should Position

Long-term investors with a five-year-plus horizon should treat this as a staggered accumulation window rather than a moment to exit. Allocating fresh money in tranches across largecap index funds and quality flexicap funds reduces timing risk. Traders, however, should respect stop-losses, as momentum remains weak and a breakdown below key support levels could trigger another leg down.

Watch three signals: a sustained fall in crude oil, softening US bond yields, and FII flows turning net positive. Until at least two of these align, expect volatility to persist. Keep 10–15% cash in portfolios to deploy on deeper dips, and avoid leveraged positions in high-beta pockets.
