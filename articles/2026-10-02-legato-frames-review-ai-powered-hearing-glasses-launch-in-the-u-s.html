Overview of Legato Frames

Legato, a hearing‑technology startup, has released its AI hearing glasses in the United States. Marketed as Legato Frames, the product merges prescription eyewear with an open‑ear hearing assistance system that uses artificial intelligence to isolate and amplify speech in noisy environments. The frames are designed for people with mild to moderate hearing loss who prefer a discreet alternative to traditional hearing aids.

How the AI Hearing Technology Works

The core of the system is a four‑microphone beamforming array embedded in the temples. Captured sound is processed by an on‑device AI engine that suppresses background noise while enhancing frequencies associated with human speech. Legato states that the AI model runs on a Qualcomm QCS603 system‑on‑chip, a platform already used in various wearable audio devices. The processed audio is delivered through tiny speakers positioned near the ear canal, preserving an open‑ear feel that avoids occlusion.

Practical Implications for Buyers

For prospective buyers, the most immediate considerations are comfort, battery endurance, and cost. The frames weigh approximately 30 grams without lenses, making them comparable to standard eyewear. Battery life is rated at up to eight hours of continuous use, which covers a typical workday but may require recharging for extended outdoor activities. Connectivity relies on Bluetooth 5.2, allowing simultaneous pairing with a smartphone and a secondary device such as a tablet for media streaming.

Prescription compatibility is a key selling point. Legato Frames are Rx‑ready and can accommodate lenses up to +4.00 diopters sphere, covering most common vision corrections. Users must purchase lenses separately through an optician, adding to the overall cost. The frames themselves are priced at $349 in the U.S. market, positioning them above basic over‑the‑counter hearing aids but below many premium prescription hearing aid bundles.

Value Justification and Market Context

When evaluated against traditional hearing aids, Legato Frames offer a distinct value proposition: discretion and multi‑functionality. Users gain vision correction and hearing assistance in a single wearable, potentially reducing the stigma associated with visible hearing devices. However, the technology does not provide the same level of amplification or customizable audiograms that a clinician‑fit hearing aid can deliver. For individuals whose primary difficulty is speech intelligibility in moderate background noise, the AI‑driven enhancement may be sufficient; those with more severe loss may still need a dedicated medical device.

From a buyer’s perspective, the total cost of ownership includes the frame price, lens expenses (typically $100‑$250 depending on material and coatings), and occasional app‑based software updates (free). The eight‑hour battery life suggests a nightly charging routine, similar to smart glasses or wireless earbuds. Users should also consider the reliance on a companion smartphone app for adjusting AI parameters, which adds a layer of complexity for less tech‑savvy consumers.

Pros and Cons

  • Discreet open‑ear design that avoids the plugged‑ear feeling of traditional aids.
  • Integrated prescription lens support combines vision and hearing correction.
  • Real‑time AI speech enhancement works well in cafés, offices, and street environments.
  • Bluetooth 5.2 enables multipoint streaming for calls and media.
  • Eight‑hour battery may require midday recharge for heavy users.
  • Lenses are sold separately, increasing the total out‑of‑pocket cost.
  • Advanced audiometric customization is limited compared to clinic‑fit hearing aids.
