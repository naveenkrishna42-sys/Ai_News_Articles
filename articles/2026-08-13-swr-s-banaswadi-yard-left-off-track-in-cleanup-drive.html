SWR's Banaswadi yard left off-track in cleanup drive

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Banaswadi yard in Bengaluru has been overlooked in SWR's cleanliness campaigns.
  • The yard is strewn with garbage, plastic waste, and overgrown vegetation.
  • Railway officials have not yet commented on when the yard will be cleaned.

Bengaluru's Banaswadi railway yard, operated by South Western Railway (SWR), has been conspicuously absent from the zone's much-publicised cleanliness drives. While stations and tracks across the city receive regular attention, this facility remains buried under garbage and unchecked vegetation.

The yard, which handles freight and stabling operations, has turned into an eyesore. Locals and railway staff alike have pointed out heaps of plastic waste, discarded packaging, and dry leaves accumulating over weeks, if not months.

A yard forgotten in plain sight

SWR has rolled out several "Swachh Rail" initiatives in recent months, targeting major stations like KSR Bengaluru City and Yeshwanthpur. Banaswadi, however, has not seen a similar push. The contrast is stark for a facility that sits in a densely populated neighbourhood.

Residents near the yard say the stench and mosquito menace worsen during the monsoon. "The tracks are barely visible under the bushes," said a local shopkeeper, who did not wish to be named. "It's not just dirt; it's become a health hazard."

What the yard looks like now

A quick look at the site reveals overflowing dustbins, scattered food wrappers, and broken wooden pallets lying between the rails. Overgrown shrubs have crept onto the ballast, and stagnant water puddles have formed in low-lying areas.

Railway sources, speaking on condition of anonymity, admitted that the yard's cleaning schedule is "irregular at best." They added that staff shortages and a lack of dedicated sweeping equipment have made matters worse. The yard's isolation from passenger areas means it rarely attracts supervisory scrutiny.

SWR's silence on the matter

When contacted, SWR officials did not offer a clear timeline for a cleanup. A spokesperson said only that "all yards under the zone will be covered in future drives," without specifying when Banaswadi would be taken up.

The zonal railway has not issued any formal statement about the yard's condition. This silence has frustrated local activists, who have been demanding action for months through social media and ward-level complaints.

Why this matters

Dirty yards are not just an aesthetic problem. They can affect track drainage, attract rodents, and even pose fire risks during dry spells. For a railway network that prides itself on modernisation, such neglect undermines the larger narrative of efficiency and care.

Banaswadi's yard is also used by maintenance crews daily. Working amid filth and overgrowth slows down inspections and increases the risk of accidents. The issue, therefore, is operational, not merely cosmetic.

What happens next

The onus now rests on SWR's divisional office in Bengaluru to schedule a special cleanliness camp at Banaswadi. Local residents plan to escalate the matter to the Divisional Railway Manager if no action is visible within a fortnight.

For now, the yard remains a glaring exception in a city that is otherwise tightening its rail hygiene standards. Whether SWR will bring it up to speed or let it slide further is a question that will likely be answered in the coming weeks.
