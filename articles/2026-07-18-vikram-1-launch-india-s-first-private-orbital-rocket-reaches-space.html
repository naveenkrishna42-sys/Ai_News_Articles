Vikram-1 Launch: India's First Private Orbital Rocket Reaches Space

📅 2026-07-18 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vikram-1 is India's first private orbital-class rocket to successfully reach orbit.
  • The rocket carried technology payloads and postcards into space.
  • Skyroot Aerospace, founded by ex-ISRO scientists, achieved the historic milestone.
  • Prime Minister Modi praised the launch as a landmark for India's private space sector.

India's private space sector entered a new orbit on Saturday. Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 rocket lifted off and successfully placed multiple payloads into orbit. The mission marks the first time an Indian private company has achieved an orbital-class launch.

The rocket carried technology demonstrators and a batch of postcards into space. Skyroot confirmed the payloads were deployed successfully. The company had earlier described the mission as a test of its end-to-end launch capabilities.

Breaking into an elite club

With this launch, Skyroot Aerospace has joined a very small group of private companies worldwide that have placed a payload in orbit. Only a handful of firms, mostly in the United States, have accomplished this feat.

The Economic Times described the event as Skyroot 'crashing an elite party'. Until now, orbital-class launches from Indian soil were the exclusive domain of the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Skyroot's success signals a shift in the country's space ecosystem.

Built by ex-ISRO scientists

Skyroot Aerospace was founded by former ISRO scientists Pawan Kumar Chandana and Naga Bharath Daka. The team brought decades of experience from India's national space programme to the private sector.

NDTV reported that the duo's journey from government labs to a private launch pad symbolises the new era of space entrepreneurship in India. The company has been developing the Vikram series of rockets since 2018, with support from state and private investors.

Government and industry reaction

Prime Minister Narendra Modi hailed the launch. In a statement, he called it a historic moment for India's space sector and a testament to the talent of Indian scientists and entrepreneurs.

The Times of India noted that the achievement comes as India pushes to open its space industry to private players. The Indian Space Policy 2023 had paved the way for companies like Skyroot to build and launch their own rockets. Saturday's success is the first major milestone under that framework.

What was on board

The payloads included technology demonstrators designed to test new systems in orbit. Skyroot also flew a batch of postcards, part of a public outreach campaign. The company had invited people to send messages to space, a gesture aimed at generating public interest in the mission.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact altitude or orbital parameters achieved. Skyroot is expected to release more technical details in the coming days.

The launch was originally scheduled for earlier in the week but faced a brief delay due to weather conditions. The team resolved the issue and proceeded with the launch on Saturday morning.

Skyroot now plans to scale up its operations. The company has already announced development of Vikram-2, a larger rocket capable of carrying heavier payloads. The next version is expected to target commercial satellite launches for both domestic and international clients.

For India's private space sector, Saturday's launch is not the finish line. It is the starting point. The real prize — a sustainable commercial launch business — now lies ahead.
