Sensex drops 715 pts, Nifty below 24,000; media, realty lead losses

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 715 points, Nifty slipped below 24,000 on July 22.
  • Media and realty sectors dragged the market, while FMCG and auto showed resilience.
  • Investors await global cues and earnings for further direction.

Market opens lower, extends losses through the day

Indian equity benchmarks ended deep in the red on July 22, with the Sensex tumbling 715 points and the Nifty sliding below the psychologically important 24,000 mark. The sell-off was broad-based, but media and realty stocks bore the brunt, while FMCG and auto counters offered some cushion.

Trading began on a cautious note and selling pressure intensified as the session progressed. Weak global cues, coupled with profit booking in recently outperforming sectors, weighed on investor sentiment.

Media and realty lead the decline

The media index was the worst performer, falling sharply as investors booked profits after a strong run. Realty stocks also came under heavy selling, with developers and related firms losing ground amid concerns over valuation and rising input costs.

Analysts pointed to a lack of fresh triggers as a key reason for the pullback. "The market is taking a breather after the recent rally," said a market observer. "Sectors that had run up sharply are seeing profit taking."

FMCG and auto show resilience

Defensive plays, particularly FMCG, held up better than the broader market. Investors moved to staples amid the volatility, seeking shelter in companies with steady demand and pricing power.

Auto stocks also displayed relative strength, supported by expectations of healthy quarterly numbers and improving rural demand. However, gains were limited and could not offset the overall negative bias.

Broader market and volatility

The broader indices mirrored the benchmark losses, with midcap and smallcap stocks also facing selling pressure. Market breadth was negative, with more declines than advances on the exchanges.

Volatility index rose during the session, indicating heightened nervousness among traders. Foreign institutional investors have been net sellers in recent sessions, adding to the pressure on domestic equities.

Experts suggest that the market may remain rangebound in the near term, with investors closely tracking global developments, crude oil prices, and the progress of the monsoon for further direction.

What to watch next

Investors will now look ahead to upcoming earnings releases from major companies and global central bank meetings. Any positive surprise on the earnings front or easing of global tensions could help the market regain its footing.

For now, traders are advised to stay cautious and avoid aggressive bets until clearer signals emerge.
