WHO warns 1 in 5 people will get cancer; cases may hit 35 million by 2050

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • WHO says 1 in 5 people globally will develop cancer during their lifetime.
  • New cancer cases projected to reach 35 million per year by 2050, up from about 20 million in 2022.
  • Lifestyle factors and environmental pollution are major drivers behind the predicted rise.

Alarming global cancer projection

The World Health Organization has issued a stark warning: one in every five people on the planet will develop cancer during their lifetime. The projection comes as part of a new analysis that forecasts a dramatic surge in cancer cases worldwide over the next three decades.

According to WHO data, new cancer cases could reach a staggering 35 million per year by 2050. That marks a roughly 70% increase from the estimated 20 million cases diagnosed in 2022.

What is driving the rise?

The agency points to a combination of lifestyle changes and environmental risks as primary accelerants. Tobacco use, alcohol consumption, unhealthy diets, and physical inactivity are major contributors. But the WHO also highlights a growing threat: pollution.

Air pollution, contaminated water, and exposure to industrial chemicals are increasingly linked to cancers of the lung, breast, and digestive tract. The burden is expected to hit low- and middle-income countries hardest, where healthcare systems are less equipped to handle the surge.

Lifestyle and pollution: a deadly mix

Experts say the rise is not inevitable. Many cancers are preventable through screening, vaccination, and changes in behaviour. But the WHO warns that without aggressive public health interventions, the numbers will keep climbing.

„The data is a wake-up call,“ a WHO official said. „We cannot treat our way out of this crisis. Prevention must be the cornerstone of the global response.“

India in the crosshairs

India is among the countries facing a significant increase. With a large population, rising pollution levels, and a growing prevalence of lifestyle diseases, the country is expected to see a sharp rise in cancer incidence. The WHO has urged governments to invest in early detection and affordable treatment.

The financial burden is also a concern. Cancer care can push families into poverty, especially in regions without universal health coverage. The agency is calling for stronger health systems and greater access to palliative care.

Global goals and action

Despite the grim forecast, some experts remain cautiously optimistic. Initiatives like the WHO's Global Cancer Targets aim to reduce premature deaths from non-communicable diseases by one-third by 2030. But progress has been uneven.

Isabel Mestres, a global health advocate, said she is „excited to see global cancer goals turning into action.“ She pointed to successful tobacco control measures and HPV vaccination campaigns as examples of what is possible when political will aligns with public health priorities.

The WHO emphasises that the 35 million figure is not a fixed destiny. With sustained investment in prevention, early diagnosis, and treatment, millions of lives could be saved.

What happens next depends on how seriously governments take the warning — and how quickly they act.
