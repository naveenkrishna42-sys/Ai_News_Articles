Jefferies turns bullish on India, reshuffles portfolio; drops HDFC Bank

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Jefferies sees brighter India outlook on strong credit growth and capital inflows.
  • Rupee outlook improves, supporting Indian markets.
  • Chris Wood sells HDFC Bank and PB Fintech, buys two new stocks including MCX.
  • Portfolio revamp reflects confidence in financial and consumption sectors.

Global brokerage Jefferies has turned more bullish on India, citing a ramp-up in credit growth and capital inflows that are expected to support the domestic market. In a recent portfolio revamp, the firm’s chief equity strategist Chris Wood removed HDFC Bank and PB Fintech from his India model portfolio, replacing them with two new additions, including Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX).

The move comes amid improving macroeconomic signals, with the rupee showing a more stable outlook and foreign investors returning to Indian equities. Jefferies’ revised stance underscores a growing conviction that India’s growth story remains intact, even as global uncertainties persist.

Credit growth and capital flows brighten outlook

Jefferies’ optimism is anchored in two key pillars: robust credit expansion and sustained capital inflows. Domestic credit growth has accelerated in recent months, driven by demand from retail and small businesses, while foreign portfolio investors have steadily increased their exposure to Indian stocks and bonds.

The report notes that these factors are likely to provide a solid foundation for corporate earnings and market valuations. A stronger rupee, supported by improved macros, adds to the favourable backdrop, making Indian assets more attractive to overseas investors.

Portfolio overhaul: HDFC Bank out, MCX in

Chris Wood, known for his long-running Greed & Fear investment strategy, has reduced exposure to HDFC Bank and PB Fintech, the parent of Policybazaar. In their place, he has added MCX and another stock, reflecting a shift toward sectors poised to benefit from increased market activity and financialisation of savings.

HDFC Bank’s removal is notable, given its heavyweight status in Indian indices and its recent underperformance relative to peers. Analysts suggest that the bank’s valuation and near-term growth prospects may have prompted the reallocation, though Jefferies has not publicly detailed its reasoning.

Market implications and investor takeaway

Jefferies’ revised outlook adds to a growing chorus of bullish calls on India from global brokerages. The combination of strong credit growth, policy continuity, and improving external balances is seen as a powerful mix for sustained market gains.

For investors, the portfolio changes signal a preference for companies leveraged to financial market volumes, such as MCX, over traditional banking plays. The move also highlights a tactical shift toward mid-cap and emerging themes, even as large-cap banks face margin pressures.

While the near-term outlook is positive, risks remain, including global rate movements and domestic inflation. However, Jefferies’ decision to increase risk appetite in India suggests confidence in the country’s ability to weather these challenges.

As the second half of the fiscal year unfolds, market watchers will track whether credit growth sustains its momentum and if capital inflows remain sticky. The coming months will also reveal whether other global funds follow Jefferies’ lead in recalibrating their India portfolios.
