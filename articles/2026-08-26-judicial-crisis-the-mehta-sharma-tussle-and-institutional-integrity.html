Judicial Crisis: The Mehta-Sharma Tussle and Institutional Integrity

📅 2026-08-26 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Justice Sandeep Mehta has formally requested the removal of Rajasthan HC Acting CJ SP Sharma.
  • Allegations centre on administrative overreach and questions regarding judicial integrity.
  • The standoff highlights systemic vulnerabilities in the appointment of Acting Chief Justices.

The Anatomy of a Judicial Standoff

The recent correspondence from Supreme Court Justice Sandeep Mehta to Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, seeking the removal of Rajasthan High Court Acting Chief Justice SP Sharma, is more than a mere personnel dispute. It marks a rare and significant public airing of internal friction within the higher judiciary. The accusations, which touch upon "gross maladministration" and integrity concerns, strike at the bedrock of public trust in the state’s highest judicial office.

When a sitting Supreme Court judge takes the extraordinary step of writing to the CJI to challenge the fitness of a High Court leader, it suggests that internal checks have either failed or been bypassed. This is not a matter of a singular administrative disagreement. It is a fundamental questioning of whether the current leadership at the Rajasthan High Court possesses the moral and functional authority to preside over the bench.

The Weight of Administrative Oversight

The role of an Acting Chief Justice is meant to be a transient, stabilizing force. However, the current situation reveals the inherent risks when an 'acting' head exercises full-scale administrative powers without the long-term accountability or the rigorous vetting process associated with a permanent appointment. Justice Mehta’s intervention suggests that the power to manage rosters and judicial resources has been exercised in a manner that compromises the independence of the court.

Critics of the current system point out that the lack of a permanent appointment for extended periods leaves high courts vulnerable to internal lobbying and power struggles. If an Acting Chief Justice is perceived to be acting with partiality, the ripple effect on case assignment and the morale of the subordinate judiciary is immediate and damaging. The urgency in Justice Mehta’s appeal reflects a fear that further inaction will cause irreparable harm to the Rajasthan High Court’s institutional reputation.

Institutional Integrity and Future Risks

The implications of this request reach far beyond Jaipur. The Supreme Court Collegium must now weigh the damage to the image of the judiciary against the potential disruption caused by the removal of a high-ranking official. If the allegations of "questionable integrity" are substantiated, the fallout could lead to a broader review of how Acting Chief Justices are selected and monitored.

  • Pro: Immediate intervention prevents further administrative decay.
  • Pro: Upholds the principle of judicial accountability.
  • Con: Risk of deepening internal factions within the High Court.
  • Con: Public perception of judicial infighting may undermine public confidence.

What remains to be seen is how CJI Surya Kant navigates this request. A swift resolution is necessary to prevent the Rajasthan High Court from becoming a theater for internal litigation. The judiciary's strength relies on the perception that it is governed by merit and strict adherence to protocol. Any perception that administrative power is being misused—whether for personal gain or to influence the outcome of matters—must be met with decisive, transparent action.

Observers should monitor the next Collegium meeting for any shifts in judicial appointments in Rajasthan. The speed at which this complaint is addressed will serve as a bellwether for the current administration's stance on internal dissent and the maintenance of judicial standards. If the judiciary cannot police its own leaders effectively, the call for external reform will only grow louder.
