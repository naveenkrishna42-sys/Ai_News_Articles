Perseid meteor shower 2026: Best time and tips to watch shooting stars

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Perseid meteor shower active from July to August, peaks mid-August.
  • Best viewing after midnight, away from city lights.
  • No special equipment needed; just find a dark spot and look up.

What is the Perseid meteor shower?

The Perseid meteor shower is one of the most anticipated celestial events of the year. It occurs every summer when Earth passes through the debris trail left by Comet Swift-Tuttle. As these tiny particles burn up in our atmosphere, they create bright streaks of light across the sky.

This year, the shower is already active and will continue through late August. The peak, when the most meteors are visible, is expected around mid-August 2026. During that time, skywatchers could see up to 60 to 100 meteors per hour under ideal conditions.

When and where to look

The best time to watch is after midnight, when the Earth's rotation points your location directly into the meteor stream. However, some meteors can be seen as early as 10 p.m. local time.

Find a spot far from city lights. Light pollution from towns and cities will wash out fainter meteors. A dark rural area, a park, or even a beach with an unobstructed view of the sky works well. No telescope or binoculars are needed — your naked eyes are perfect for this.

Tips for the best experience

Bring a blanket or a reclining chair so you can look straight up without straining your neck. Give your eyes at least 20 minutes to adjust to the darkness. Avoid looking at your phone, as its bright screen will ruin your night vision.

Dress warmly, even if it is summer. Nighttime temperatures can drop, especially if you are out for an hour or more. Pack snacks and water, and be patient. Meteors often come in bursts followed by lulls.

Weather and moon conditions

This year, the moon will be in a waning crescent phase during the peak, meaning it will not interfere much with meteor viewing. A bright moon can wash out fainter streaks, so this is a good year for the Perseids.

Check the local weather forecast before heading out. Clear skies are essential. If clouds are expected, plan for another night close to the peak, as the shower will still be active a few days before and after.

What to expect

Perseid meteors are known for their speed and brightness. They travel at about 37 miles per second and often leave persistent trains — glowing trails that linger for a second or two. Some may even produce fireballs, which are exceptionally bright meteors.

Do not expect a constant stream of shooting stars. There will be moments of quiet, then sudden flurries. The key is to relax and enjoy the show.

For those who miss the peak, the shower will still produce a decent number of meteors for a few nights afterward. Mark your calendar for mid-August and prepare for a night under the stars.
