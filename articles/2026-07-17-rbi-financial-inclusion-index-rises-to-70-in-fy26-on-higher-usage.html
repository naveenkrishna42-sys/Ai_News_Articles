RBI Financial Inclusion Index rises to 70 in FY26 on higher usage

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's Financial Inclusion Index reached 70 in FY26, up from 64.2
  • The improvement is attributed to higher usage of banking services and digital transactions
  • The index measures access, usage, and quality of financial services

Index climbs on usage metrics

The Reserve Bank of India's Financial Inclusion Index rose to 70 in the financial year 2025-26, up from 64.2 a year ago. The central bank attributed the jump primarily to higher usage of banking services and digital payments across the country.

The index, which tracks three broad dimensions — access, usage, and quality — captures how deeply financial services have penetrated among Indians. Usage saw the strongest improvement, officials indicated, as more people transacted through formal channels.

What the index measures

The RBI first introduced the composite Financial Inclusion Index in 2021. It combines 97 indicators drawn from banking, insurance, pension, and securities markets. Each dimension carries a specific weight: access (35%), usage (45%), and quality (20%).

For FY26, the access sub-index stood at 85.6, quality at 79.4, and usage at 58.6. The usage component, though still the lowest, recorded the sharpest year-on-year gain, reflecting a surge in transaction volumes through UPI, digital wallets, and branch banking.

Digital payments drive growth

Industry analysts pointed to the rapid expansion of UPI and internet banking as key factors behind the index's rise. The number of unique digital payment users crossed the 50-crore mark in FY26, according to NPCI data cited in the report.

Rural and semi-urban regions contributed significantly to the uptick. Banks have expanded their branch networks and deployed more banking correspondents, bringing services closer to underserved populations.

Quality of services improves

The quality dimension, which measures customer satisfaction, grievance redressal, and financial literacy, also improved. The RBI has been pushing banks to strengthen their internal ombudsman mechanisms and reduce complaint turnaround times.

However, the gap between access and usage remains a concern. While nearly 96% of adults now have a bank account, the central bank's data suggests that a significant portion of these accounts see low transaction activity.

What lies ahead

The RBI has set an aspirational target of taking the index past 80 within the next three years. Achieving that will require deeper engagement with infrequent users and further expansion of digital infrastructure in remote areas.

The central bank is expected to release a detailed breakdown of the index components in its next Financial Stability Report. Policymakers will watch for whether the usage metric can sustain its momentum as the economy moves deeper into a cash-lite phase.
