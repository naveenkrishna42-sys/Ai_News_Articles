Tunnel Road Project Faces Halt Over Hebbal Lake Impact Concerns

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Authorities may stop tunnel road work unless Hebbal Lake impact is assessed
  • Environmental concerns raised over potential damage to the lake ecosystem
  • Decision pending on environmental clearance and mitigation measures

Project on Hold Pending Environmental Review

Bengaluru's ambitious tunnel road project could face an abrupt halt unless authorities clarify its impact on Hebbal Lake, one of the city's largest and most ecologically sensitive water bodies. The warning comes from senior officials overseeing the infrastructure initiative, who have made it clear that environmental concerns cannot be brushed aside.

The project, designed to ease traffic congestion on some of Bengaluru's busiest corridors, has been under scrutiny for months. Now, with the lake's health hanging in the balance, the state government is treading cautiously.

Why Hebbal Lake Matters

Hebbal Lake, spread over 150 acres, is not just a recreational spot but a crucial recharge zone for the city's groundwater. Environmentalists have long flagged the risks of tunneling near such water bodies, warning that vibrations and groundwater disruption could cause irreversible damage.

A senior official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said: "We cannot proceed without a clear picture of how the tunnel will affect the lake's water levels and surrounding ecology. If the impact is not properly assessed and mitigated, we will have to pause the work."

What the Authorities Are Saying

The Bangalore Development Authority (BDA) and the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) are jointly reviewing the project's environmental clearance. Sources indicate that a detailed study on the lake's hydrology and soil stability is being commissioned.

If the study reveals significant risk, the project could face delays of several months, or even a complete rerouting of the tunnel alignment. This would be a major setback for a city that desperately needs better road infrastructure.

Environmentalists Applaud Caution

Conservation groups have welcomed the cautious approach, though they remain skeptical about the authorities' commitment. "We've heard promises before, but the lake's condition has only worsened," said a local activist. "Any tunnel work near Hebbal Lake without robust safeguards would be a disaster."

The lake, which was once a thriving wetland, has already shrunk due to encroachments and pollution. Environmentalists argue that the tunnel road project could be the final blow.

What Happens Next

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for the environmental study, but a decision is expected within the next few weeks. The government is under pressure from both commuters and environmentalists, making this a delicate balancing act.

If the project is halted, it would be a significant victory for activists, but a headache for city planners. For now, all eyes are on the study's findings and the government's next move.
