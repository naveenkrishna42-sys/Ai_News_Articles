Arrest in Hyderabad Over Alleged Infant Trafficking

Details of the Incident

Hyderabad police on Wednesday detained a woman accused of attempting to sell an eight-day-old baby, officials confirmed. The infant was recovered safely from the suspect’s possession in a residential area of the old city, following a complaint that triggered a swift investigation.

According to the complaint, the woman had been attempting to hand over the newborn to an unidentified buyer for a negotiated sum. Police intervened after local residents flagged suspicious movements near a housing colony late on Tuesday night.

Legal Framework and Charges

The accused has been booked under Section 376(1)(2)—human trafficking—along with Sections 363 and 364A of the Indian Penal Code, and provisions of the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956. Additional charges under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015, may also be applied given the involvement of a minor.

Child trafficking cases in Telangana have shown a marginal rise over the past year, with the state anti-trafficking unit reporting over 120 cases annually. Authorities emphasize that illegal adoption rackets often exploit vulnerable families and use digital platforms to coordinate transactions.

Broader Context and Enforcement Response

This arrest comes weeks after a similar case in Secunderabad where two individuals were apprehended for running a fake adoption network. Both cases highlight the vulnerability of newborns and the urgent need for stronger monitoring of informal childcare arrangements.

Social welfare departments have urged citizens to report any suspicious activity involving minors directly to the state child protection commission. The infant from Wednesday’s case has been shifted to a government observation home for medical examination and rehabilitation.

Authorities Reinforce Anti-Trafficking Measures

Telangana’s Director of Women and Child Protection Services stated that special task forces are being deployed to monitor online and offline networks involved in illegal adoptions. The department is also coordinating with NGOs to strengthen shelter infrastructure for rescued children.

Community awareness drives are being planned across Hyderabad and Ranga Reddy districts to educate parents, especially in economically disadvantaged neighborhoods, about legal adoption procedures and the risks posed by intermediaries.
