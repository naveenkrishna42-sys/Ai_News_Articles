Ranbir Kapoor defends Ramayana VFX, hits back at CGI critics

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ranbir Kapoor defends the VFX work in Ramayana against online criticism
  • He says artists have worked for years on the film's visual effects
  • Director Nitesh Tiwari and producer Namit Malhotra also responded to the criticism
  • The film's modern aesthetic has sparked debate among audiences and experts

Mumbai, August 12: Actor Ranbir Kapoor has broken his silence on the barrage of criticism directed at the visual effects in the upcoming epic Ramayana. Responding to online detractors who have dismissed the film's CGI as subpar or AI-generated, Kapoor defended the painstaking work of the VFX teams behind the project.

"There's countless hours of work where artists are working for years," Kapoor said, pushing back against the assumption that the film's effects were rushed or machine-made. His comments come as the film's first look and teaser continue to divide opinion across social media platforms.

Artists, not algorithms

Kapoor's remarks were echoed by director Nitesh Tiwari and producer Namit Malhotra, who jointly addressed the criticism during a recent promotional event. The trio took umbrage at the suggestion that the film's visual grandeur was simply the product of a few clicks on an AI tool.

"People think someone's just used AI," Kapoor said, quoting the general sentiment online. He insisted that the reality is far more complex, with hundreds of artists contributing to each frame over several years.

The film, which has been in production for an extended period, reportedly involved extensive pre-visualisation, motion capture and digital set extensions. The team's defence suggests they are keen to correct the narrative that the epic was assembled with shortcuts.

Online CGI critics under fire

The backlash against Kapoor's comments has been mixed. While some fans have rallied behind the actor, others remain unconvinced, pointing to specific frames they claim look artificial. The debate has also drawn in fashion and film experts in Bengaluru, who have weighed in on the film's "modern aesthetic" and its departure from traditional depictions of the Ramayana.

Kapoor, however, appeared unbothered by the scepticism. "People online have become CGI critics," he said, suggesting that armchair analysis often ignores the technical and artistic challenges of mounting a production of this scale.

The actor's defence is notable, as it marks one of the first times the film's lead has publicly addressed the technical scrutiny. Tiwari, known for his meticulous storytelling, and Malhotra, a seasoned VFX producer, have both backed the work, insisting that the final product will speak for itself.

A modern Ramayana

The film's visual style, which blends realism with grandeur, has been a talking point since its announcement. Some critics argue that the sleek, polished look undermines the raw, mythological essence of the epic. Others, however, see it as a bold reinterpretation suited for contemporary audiences.

Kapoor's comments suggest the team is firmly behind their creative choices. "There's countless hours of work where artists are working for years," he reiterated, underscoring the dedication involved.

As the release date approaches, the debate over the film's VFX is unlikely to fade. But for now, the cast and crew are standing their ground, confident that the craftsmanship will win over even the harshest detractors.

What lies ahead

With the film's theatrical release still months away, more promotional material is expected to drop in the coming weeks. The makers will be hoping that a fuller reveal of the VFX-heavy sequences will shift the conversation from scepticism to appreciation.

For Kapoor, the focus remains on the art. "People think someone's just used AI," he said, "but the truth is, it's years of human effort." Whether that effort translates into box-office success and critical acclaim remains to be seen, but the team's conviction is unmistakable.
