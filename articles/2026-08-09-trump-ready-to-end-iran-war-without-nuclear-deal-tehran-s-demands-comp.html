Trump ready to end Iran war without nuclear deal; Tehran's demands complicate exit: Report

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump signals readiness to end Iran war without a nuclear agreement
  • Focus shifts to reopening Strait of Hormuz amid Tehran's demands
  • US official expects deal between Iran and Oman on Hormuz soon

Shift in US Strategy

Washington is signalling a major shift in its approach to the conflict with Iran. According to a new report, President Donald Trump is now ready to end the war without securing a formal nuclear deal with Tehran. The focus, instead, has moved to reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil supplies.

The report, citing sources familiar with the discussions, suggests that the US administration is willing to drop the nuclear issue entirely if Iran agrees to reopen the strait. This marks a significant departure from earlier demands that any ceasefire be tied to a comprehensive nuclear agreement.

Tehran's Complicating Demands

However, Tehran's demands are complicating any potential exit from the conflict. Iranian officials have reportedly attached conditions that go beyond the nuclear programme, seeking broader guarantees on sanctions relief and regional security. These demands have slowed down negotiations, even as both sides express a desire to end the fighting.

The report indicates that while Trump is eager to declare the war over "pretty soon", the path to that declaration is not straightforward. The Iranian leadership appears unwilling to make concessions without clear, verifiable commitments from the US side.

Hormuz Deal Nears

Amid these complexities, there are signs of progress on a narrower front. A US official has told Reuters that a deal between Iran and Oman on the Strait of Hormuz is expected soon. Such an agreement could pave the way for the reopening of the strait, which has been a flashpoint in the conflict.

The strait's closure has had a ripple effect on global energy markets, with oil prices spiking and shipping insurance costs soaring. A deal on Hormuz would be a significant step toward de-escalation, even if the broader nuclear question remains unresolved.

What It Means for the Region

For India and other major importers of Gulf oil, the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz would be a welcome relief. The disruption has already impacted supply chains and fuel prices in the subcontinent. Diplomatic observers note that a Hormuz-first approach could be a pragmatic way to reduce tensions without getting bogged down in the nuclear impasse.

Yet, the report cautions that Tehran's demands could still derail the process. The Iranian side is said to be pushing for a more comprehensive settlement, which may not be achievable in the short term. This leaves the situation fluid, with both sides manoeuvring for leverage as they seek a way out of the war.

Looking Ahead

As negotiations continue, all eyes will be on the Oman-mediated talks. If a Hormuz deal is finalised, it could serve as a confidence-building measure, potentially leading to broader discussions. For now, the question remains whether Trump can deliver on his promise of ending the war "pretty soon" without a nuclear deal, and whether Tehran will accept a partial resolution.

The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether the conflict winds down or escalates further.
