Bypoll results Monday: Bankipur, Datia, Manjalpur verdicts to test key faces

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bypoll results for Bankipur, Datia and Manjalpur assembly seats to be declared on Monday.
  • All eyes on candidates PK and Ashutosh Tiwari in the three-state electoral test.
  • Congress suspends former Datia MLA Rajendra Bharti a day before counting.
  • Outcome in Datia could have wider political implications for Madhya Pradesh.

Counting day arrives after fierce campaigns

Vote counting for by-elections to three assembly seats across Bihar, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh will take place on Monday. The results will decide the fate of several key candidates, including PK and Ashutosh Tiwari, who have been at the centre of the political buzz in their respective constituencies.

The bypolls were held last week in Bankipur (Bihar), Manjalpur (Gujarat) and Datia (Madhya Pradesh). Each seat has its own political context, and the verdicts are being watched closely by regional and national parties.

Bankipur and Manjalpur: A test of local equations

In Patna's Bankipur assembly seat, the contest has drawn attention due to the presence of PK, whose campaign focused on local issues and development. The seat has traditionally been a stronghold of the Janata Dal (United), but this bypoll has seen a multi-cornered contest, making the outcome unpredictable.

Manjalpur in Vadodara, Gujarat, is another constituency where the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party is facing a test. Ashutosh Tiwari, the candidate in focus, has been campaigning on a plank of infrastructure and civic amenities. The bypoll was necessitated after the sitting MLA's seat fell vacant, and both the BJP and the Congress have invested heavily in the campaign.

Datia: Congress suspends ex-MLA Rajendra Bharti

In Madhya Pradesh's Datia seat, the Congress has taken an aggressive step just a day before counting. The party suspended former MLA Rajendra Bharti, who had been disqualified, triggering the bypoll. The suspension comes hours before the counting of votes, and it signals internal friction within the state unit.

Political observers believe the Datia outcome could have a wider impact on Madhya Pradesh politics. The seat was held by Bharti, who had won as a Congress candidate but was later disqualified. His suspension now adds a layer of uncertainty to the already tense contest.

What the suspension means

The Congress's decision to suspend Bharti is being seen as a move to distance the party from a legislator whose disqualification became a liability. However, it could also alienate his supporters in the constituency, who may have voted for the party out of loyalty to him.

In the run-up to the bypoll, Bharti had been vocal about his grievances, and his suspension may influence how votes are counted in his former bastion. The BJP, on its part, has been highlighting the Congress's internal troubles as evidence of administrative failure.

Counting procedure and what to watch

Counting will begin at 8 am on Monday across designated centres in all three states. Electronic voting machines will be opened in the presence of candidates' representatives, and strict security arrangements have been made to ensure a smooth process.

Officials have not yet indicated when final results are expected, but trends usually emerge within a few hours. The Election Commission has set up control rooms in each district to monitor the counting and address any complaints.

The bypoll results will be a litmus test for the ruling parties in Bihar, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh. For the Congress, the Datia suspension and the performance of its candidates in the other two states will be closely scrutinised. For the BJP, retaining Manjalpur and making inroads in Datia would be a morale booster ahead of future electoral battles.

As the day of reckoning arrives, all eyes are on the counting centres. The verdicts will not just decide three MLAs but will also shape the political narrative in the three states in the coming months.
