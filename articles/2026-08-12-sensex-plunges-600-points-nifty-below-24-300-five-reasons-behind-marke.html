Sensex Plunges 600 Points, Nifty Below 24,300: Five Reasons Behind Market Crash

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 600 points, Nifty below 24,300
  • Five key reasons identified for the crash
  • Market volatility expected to continue

Mumbai: Indian equity markets witnessed a sharp selloff on Wednesday, with the BSE Sensex plunging over 600 points and the Nifty 50 slipping below the crucial 24,300 mark. The benchmark indices opened on a weak note and extended losses through the morning session, tracking a mix of global and domestic headwinds.

Investors lost significant wealth in early trade as selling pressure intensified across sectors. The broad-based decline was led by banking, IT, and auto stocks, with only a few defensive counters showing resilience.

Global Cues Weigh on Sentiment

Asian markets traded lower, mirroring overnight weakness on Wall Street. US equities ended in the red as concerns over interest rates and geopolitical tensions dampened risk appetite. The selloff in global markets spilled over to Indian bourses, with foreign institutional investors (FIIs) reportedly pressing sales.

Rising crude oil prices added to the worries. Brent crude hovered near elevated levels, stoking fears of imported inflation and its impact on India's fiscal metrics. A stronger dollar against the rupee further compounded the pressure on domestic equities.

Five Key Reasons Behind the Crash

Market analysts pointed to five primary factors driving Wednesday's decline:

  • Global risk-off mood: Weak US and Asian cues triggered a broad-based selloff.
  • FII outflows: Continuous selling by foreign investors put pressure on large-cap stocks.
  • Rising crude prices: Higher oil prices threatened to widen the current account deficit and stoke inflation.
  • Rupee depreciation: The currency's slide against the dollar made Indian assets less attractive.
  • Sectoral weakness: Banking and IT stocks, which have heavy index weightage, led the decline.

Sectoral Performance

All major sectoral indices on the National Stock Exchange traded in the red. The Nifty Bank index fell sharply, dragged by losses in private lenders. IT stocks also tumbled on concerns over global demand and currency fluctuations.

Midcap and smallcap stocks were not spared either, with the broader market witnessing significant erosion. Analysts said the correction was overdue after recent gains, and profit-booking at higher levels added to the downward momentum.

What Analysts Are Saying

Market experts noted that the immediate trigger for the crash was the combination of global and domestic factors. Some suggested that the selloff could deepen if crude prices continue to rise or if FIIs accelerate their exit.

However, others pointed out that domestic institutional investors (DIIs) have been supporting the market, which could cushion the downside. The volatility index, India VIX, spiked, indicating heightened uncertainty among traders.

Outlook for the Session

Traders are now watching for any recovery in the afternoon session, but the mood remains cautious. Key support for the Nifty is seen around the 24,200 level, while the Sensex has a psychological support at 79,500.

No official statement has been issued by market regulators or the finance ministry so far. The coming sessions will depend on global cues, particularly the movement of US yields and crude oil prices. Investors are advised to stay alert as volatility is likely to persist in the near term.
