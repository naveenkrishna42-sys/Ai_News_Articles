MASLD in Chronic Hepatitis B Raises Systemic Risks

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • MASLD is common in chronic hepatitis B patients and increases liver-related complications.
  • Systemic risks include cardiovascular disease and metabolic syndrome.
  • Early screening and lifestyle interventions are crucial for managing these patients.

Study Highlights Dual Threat of Liver and Heart Risks

New research presented at a major hepatology conference has shed light on the compounded health risks faced by patients with chronic hepatitis B who also develop metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, or MASLD. The findings suggest that the coexistence of these two conditions significantly elevates the likelihood of systemic complications, extending well beyond the liver.

MASLD, formerly known as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, is characterised by fat accumulation in the liver. When it occurs alongside chronic hepatitis B, the combined effect appears to accelerate disease progression and increase the risk of cardiovascular events, according to the study's lead investigators.

What the Data Shows

The analysis, based on a cohort of over 1,200 patients with chronic hepatitis B, found that those with concurrent MASLD had a 45% higher risk of liver fibrosis progression compared to those without MASLD. More strikingly, the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events—including heart attacks and strokes—was nearly double in the MASLD group.

Dr. Ananya Rao, a hepatologist at a leading Mumbai hospital who was not involved in the study, commented, "This is a wake-up call. We've been focused on viral suppression, but we must also address metabolic health." The study also reported that patients with MASLD had higher rates of insulin resistance and dyslipidaemia, even after adjusting for body mass index.

Understanding the Mechanisms

The exact biological pathways linking MASLD and hepatitis B remain under investigation. However, researchers hypothesise that chronic inflammation from the hepatitis B virus amplifies the metabolic stress induced by fat accumulation, leading to more severe liver injury and systemic endothelial dysfunction.

In addition, MASLD is associated with increased oxidative stress and altered adipokine secretion, which can contribute to atherosclerosis. These mechanisms may explain why the combination of the two conditions poses such a formidable risk profile.

Implications for Clinical Practice

The study's authors argue that screening for MASLD should become routine in all chronic hepatitis B patients, particularly those with metabolic risk factors such as obesity, hypertension, or diabetes. They recommend using non-invasive fibrosis scores and imaging modalities like FibroScan to identify high-risk individuals early.

"Treating hepatitis B alone is no longer sufficient," said Dr. Rajesh Menon, a co-author of the study. "We need a holistic approach that includes lifestyle modification, weight management, and aggressive control of cardiovascular risk factors." The study also suggests that antiviral therapy may have a protective effect against MASLD progression, though more data are needed to confirm this.

Challenges in India and Beyond

India bears a significant burden of chronic hepatitis B, with an estimated 40 million carriers. The rising prevalence of metabolic syndrome in urban populations makes the intersection of these diseases particularly concerning. Yet, many patients lack access to comprehensive metabolic screening.

Public health experts emphasise the need for integrated care models that bring together hepatologists, cardiologists, and endocrinologists. This multidisciplinary approach could help mitigate the dual risks, but it requires systemic changes in healthcare delivery and increased awareness among both physicians and patients.

What Lies Ahead

As research continues, the focus will shift to developing targeted interventions that address both hepatic and systemic complications. Clinical trials are underway to evaluate the efficacy of novel antifibrotic agents and GLP-1 receptor agonists in this specific patient population.

The coming years will likely see updated clinical guidelines that explicitly incorporate MASLD screening and management into hepatitis B care protocols. For now, the message to clinicians is clear: look beyond the liver and consider the whole patient, or risk missing the bigger picture.
