RAF Chief Admits Excessive Force

Key points

  • Pellet guns and electric shock weapons banned at Jantar Mantar

Excessive Force Admission

The chief of the Rapid Action Force (RAF) has admitted to the use of excessive force during recent protests in Delhi. This admission comes after reports of pellet guns and electric shock weapons being used against protesters at Jantar Mantar.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact circumstances surrounding the use of these weapons, but the RAF chief has taken a step towards addressing the issue by banning the use of pellet guns and electric shock weapons at Jantar Mantar.

Protests in Delhi

Protesters in Delhi have reported being shot at with pellet guns and beaten with shock batons. The police have denied these allegations, but the admission by the RAF chief suggests that excessive force may have been used. The use of pellet guns and electric shock weapons has been a subject of controversy, with many questioning their legality for crowd control in India.

Reactions to the Admission

Politicians have begun to weigh in on the issue, with some calling for action to be taken against those responsible for authorizing the use of pellet guns. Rahul Gandhi has stated that he will raise the issue in Parliament, alleging that Amit Shah authorized the use of pellet guns against students.

Investigations and Next Steps

As the situation continues to unfold, officials have not yet confirmed whether an investigation will be launched into the use of excessive force. The banning of pellet guns and electric shock weapons at Jantar Mantar is a step towards addressing the issue, but more needs to be done to ensure that protesters' rights are protected.

The incident has sparked a larger debate about the use of force during protests in India, and many are waiting to see how the government will respond to the RAF chief's admission.

What's Next

The government's response to the RAF chief's admission will be closely watched in the coming days. With Parliament set to discuss the issue, it remains to be seen what actions will be taken to address the use of excessive force during protests.
