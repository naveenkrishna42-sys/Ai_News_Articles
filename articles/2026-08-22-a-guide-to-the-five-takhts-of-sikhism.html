A Guide to the Five Takhts of Sikhism

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Sacred Places Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Five Takhts represent the highest temporal authority in Sikhism.
  • Each site is deeply linked to the lives and teachings of the Sikh Gurus.
  • Planning a pilgrimage requires understanding the geographical spread across India.

The Significance of the Takhts

In Sikhism, a 'Takht'—literally meaning a throne—serves as a seat of religious and temporal authority. There are five such sacred sites, each holding immense historical weight. They are not merely places of worship but centers where the community addresses critical issues, guided by the principles laid down by the ten Sikh Gurus. Pilgrims visit these sites to seek spiritual connection and to pay homage to the sacrifices made for the faith.

The Five Seats of Authority

The Akal Takht, located within the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar, is the oldest. It was established by Guru Hargobind in 1606 as a symbol of political sovereignty. The second is Takht Sri Damdama Sahib in Talwandi Sabo, known as the place where Guru Gobind Singh finalized the compilation of the Guru Granth Sahib. Takht Sri Keshgarh Sahib in Anandpur Sahib is the birthplace of the Khalsa, established in 1699.

Takht Sri Patna Sahib, situated in Bihar, marks the birthplace of Guru Gobind Singh. Finally, Takht Sri Hazur Sahib in Nanded, Maharashtra, is the site where the tenth Guru breathed his last. Each of these locations features distinct architecture, yet all share a profound atmosphere of devotion, constant recitation of Gurbani, and the practice of Langar, the community kitchen that serves free meals to all visitors regardless of background.

Spiritual Etiquette

Visitors are expected to observe strict decorum when entering any of the Takhts. This includes covering one’s head with a scarf or cloth, removing shoes before entry, and maintaining silence or soft tones. Photography inside the sanctum sanctorum is generally prohibited to preserve the sanctity of the space. Participation in the community kitchen, or Seva, is a deeply encouraged act of humility and service.

Practical Visiting Information

The best time to visit these sites is during the cooler months between October and March. For the Akal Takht and Keshgarh Sahib, the nearest major transit hub is Amritsar and Chandigarh, respectively. Patna Sahib is easily accessible via the Patna Junction railway station, while Nanded serves as the gateway for Hazur Sahib. If you are planning a comprehensive pilgrimage, consider combining a visit to the Punjab-based Takhts with a trip to the nearby historic sites of Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar or the serene gardens of Anandpur Sahib.
