Xi's Five-Point BRICS Plan: What It Proposes

At the 2026 BRICS Summit in New Delhi, Chinese President Xi Jinping unveiled a five-point action plan focused on artificial intelligence, smart manufacturing, and digital connectivity. The plan, presented during the plenary session, calls for a BRICS AI cooperation framework, a smart manufacturing alliance, and a joint fund for industrial upgrading. While Beijing frames these as inclusive development tools, Indian officials see potential red flags for India's tech sovereignty and strategic autonomy.

Xi also used the platform to denounce "might-makes-right" logic, a phrase widely interpreted as a veiled critique of US-led tech restrictions. However, the absence of concrete implementation details has left many BRICS members, including India, cautious.

Why India Is Wary

India's concerns stem from three core issues. First, the proposed AI framework could entrench Chinese technical standards, reducing interoperability with India's own digital public infrastructure like UPI and Aadhaar. Second, the smart manufacturing alliance may prioritise Chinese firms for 5G, robotics, and industrial IoT deployments, squeezing Indian players. Third, Beijing's continued reference to Tibet and Taiwan in bilateral statements complicates trust.

A senior Indian official, speaking on condition of anonymity, told TIVRA News that New Delhi has not formally endorsed any of the five initiatives. The Ministry of External Affairs has not issued a public statement. India's stance aligns with its broader push for multi-alignment, but the tech dimension adds a new layer of complexity.

Technical and Commercial Implications

For Indian buyers and enterprises, the plan's technical specifications could influence procurement decisions. If BRICS adopts Chinese AI chips or smart manufacturing protocols, Indian companies may face compatibility issues with Western systems. The table below summarises the key elements of Xi's plan and their potential impact.

BRICS AI Cooperation FrameworkJoint research, data sharing, ethics guidelinesData localisation conflicts; potential reliance on Chinese cloud infrastructure
Smart Manufacturing AllianceIndustrial IoT, robotics, 5G-enabled factoriesCompetition for Indian MSMEs; possible Chinese dominance in standards

Pros and Cons for India

  • Pro: Access to affordable AI and smart manufacturing tech for Indian MSMEs.
  • Pro: Potential collaboration on climate-friendly industrial processes.
  • Con: Risk of Chinese technical standards undermining India's digital sovereignty.
  • Con: Data-sharing provisions may conflict with India's data protection laws.
  • Con: Geopolitical tensions over Tibet and Taiwan could derail cooperation.

Indian industry bodies have taken note. The Confederation of Indian Industry (CII) declined to comment on record, but a senior executive said off-record that any BRICS tech framework must respect India's data localisation norms and intellectual property rights.

What Happens Next

Xi's plan is expected to be discussed at the upcoming BRICS Sherpa meeting, where India will have a chance to shape the agenda. New Delhi may push for amendments to ensure technology transfer is voluntary and that data flows are regulated under national laws. For now, Indian buyers should watch for any pilot projects that could set precedents. The cost of participation, both financial and strategic, remains unclear. Given India's push for self-reliance in critical technologies, any BRICS initiative that compromises that goal will face stiff resistance.
