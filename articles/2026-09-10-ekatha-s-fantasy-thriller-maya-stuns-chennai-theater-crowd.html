Ekatha's 'Maya': A Bold New Voice in Chennai Theater

In a city where Tamil theater has long been dominated by mythologicals and family dramas, Ekatha's latest production, Maya, arrives as a refreshing departure. This original fantasy thriller weaves together ancient lore with contemporary tension, marking a deliberate shift toward genre experimentation in Chennai's live performance scene.

Storytelling Meets Spectacle

Written and directed by Anjali Mehta, a playwright known for her work in Bangalore's alternative theater circuit, Maya draws heavily from Sanskrit epics while introducing a psychological twist — the protagonist grapples with illusion versus reality after discovering a mystical artifact. The narrative is structured in three acts, each escalating the stakes through a mix of monologue-driven scenes and choreographed ensemble sequences.

Performance and Production Design

The cast of twelve delivers performances that balance dramatic intensity with subtle nuance. Lead actor Karthik Ramesh carries much of the emotional weight, portraying the tormented scholar Arjun with commendable restraint. Behind the scenes, lighting designer Priya Natarajan uses shadow play and colored gels to evoke a dreamlike atmosphere, particularly during the climactic illusion sequences. Minimalist set pieces — a few carved pillars and a central sculpture — suggest grandeur without overwhelming the intimate theater space.

Real-World Impact for Theater-Goers

For Chennai audiences, Maya represents both accessibility and ambition. At ₹450 per ticket, it sits comfortably within the mid-tier pricing bracket — more affordable than most English-language films but pricier than community-run amateur productions. The choice of Alliance Française as venue ensures good acoustics and seating for about 120 patrons per show, making it an ideal fit for experimental works seeking engaged viewership rather than mass appeal.

  • Pros: Original storyline rooted in Indian mythology; strong lead performance; innovative use of light and sound.
  • Cons: No subtitles offered for non-Tamil speakers; limited weekend shows may restrict attendance.
  • Key Insight: This is one of the few recent plays attempting to merge genre fiction with classical themes in Tamil Nadu’s theater ecosystem.

Looking Ahead

Efforts are already underway to adapt Maya into an audio podcast format, with recordings scheduled for October. Mehta has also hinted at expanding the story into a full-length film, should audience response remain positive through the current run. For now, Chennai theater lovers have until September 28 to experience a rare blend of intellect and spectacle under one roof.
