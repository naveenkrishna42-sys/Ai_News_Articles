6 Food Trends Shaping Fashion in 2025, Says Vogue

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • Butter yellow emerges as a dominant colour across fashion and food
  • Cherry prints and accessories gain popularity, inspired by culinary aesthetics
  • Dairy-inspired textures and pastries influence fabric and design choices

When Food Meets Fashion

In 2025, the boundary between the kitchen and the catwalk has blurred further. Vogue's latest report highlights six food trends that designers and stylists are watching closely. From colour palettes to textures, what we eat is increasingly shaping what we wear.

This crossover is not entirely new, but its intensity has grown. Social media platforms, especially Instagram and TikTok, have accelerated the visual dialogue between food styling and fashion styling. A single viral dish can now inspire a fabric print within weeks.

Butter Yellow Takes Over

One of the most prominent trends is butter yellow. The soft, creamy shade, reminiscent of melted butter, has appeared on runways in Milan and Paris. Fashion houses are pairing it with neutral tones like beige and off-white to create a warm, comforting aesthetic.

Food brands have also embraced the colour in packaging and product design. The association with dairy and baked goods evokes nostalgia, making it a safe yet striking choice for both industries.

Cherry Motifs and Prints

Cherries are the fruit of the moment. From cherry-printed dresses to cherry-shaped handbags, the motif is popping up across collections. Vogue notes that the fruit's bold red hue and glossy texture translate well into accessories and statement pieces.

The trend extends beyond apparel. Jewellery designers are crafting cherry-inspired earrings and necklaces, while footwear brands are adding cherry embellishments to sandals and sneakers. The playful, almost kitschy appeal is resonating with younger consumers.

Dairy-Inspired Textures

Texture is another area where food trends are influencing fashion. The smooth, pillowy feel of fresh cheese or whipped cream is being mimicked in fabrics like quilted nylon and plush velvet. Designers are experimenting with puffy silhouettes that mimic the look of a soufflé or a marshmallow.

This tactile approach is also visible in knitwear. Chunky, airy knits that resemble a frothy cappuccino foam are appearing in winter collections. The goal is to create garments that feel as comforting as a warm meal.

Pastry-Inspired Accessories

Accessories are getting a bakery makeover. Croissant-shaped bags and baguette-inspired clutches have been spotted on several red carpets. While the baguette bag is a 90s classic, the new iterations are more literal, with flaky textures and golden-brown hues.

Even jewellery is joining the pastry party. Pendants shaped like macarons and earrings resembling cinnamon rolls are being sold by independent designers and high-street brands alike. The trend taps into a desire for whimsy and escapism.

Spice-Coloured Palettes

Spices are also dictating colour schemes. Turmeric yellow, paprika red, and cardamom green are replacing the muted pastels of previous seasons. These vibrant, earthy tones are being used in both casual and formal wear, adding warmth and energy.

Vogue suggests that this shift reflects a broader cultural move towards bold, expressive choices. Consumers are increasingly drawn to colours that evoke flavour and exoticism, moving away from minimalist greys and blacks.

What This Means for Consumers

For the average shopper, these trends offer fresh ways to experiment with personal style. Incorporating a butter-yellow scarf or a cherry-printed top can instantly update a wardrobe without a complete overhaul. As with all trends, the key is to adapt them to individual taste rather than follow them blindly.

Fashion retailers are already stocking these items, and early sales data suggests strong consumer interest. Whether the trends sustain beyond 2025 remains to be seen, but their initial impact is undeniable.

As the year progresses, watch for these food-inspired elements to appear in wedding season collections and festive wear. The crossover between culinary and fashion worlds is set to deepen, with more collaborations likely on the horizon.
