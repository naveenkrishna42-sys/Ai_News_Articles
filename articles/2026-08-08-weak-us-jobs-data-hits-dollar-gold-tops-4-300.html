Weak US Jobs Data Hits Dollar; Gold Tops $4,300

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • US non-farm payrolls came in below expectations, dimming Fed hike prospects.
  • Dollar slid to its lowest level since May; gold surged past $4,300.
  • Geopolitical risks remain elevated, keeping investors on edge.

The dollar took a sharp hit on Friday after a disappointing US jobs report, while gold surged past the $4,300 mark for the first time. The weaker-than-expected non-farm payrolls data has thrown cold water on expectations of a September rate hike by the Federal Reserve, leaving currency markets in a tailspin.

Dollar Slips to Lowest Since May

The greenback fell to its lowest level since May, as traders digested the implications of a cooling labour market. According to Action Forex, the weak jobs data has pushed out expectations of a Fed hike, with many now betting the central bank will hold rates steady for longer.

Bloomberg reported that the dollar's decline has been steep, with the currency index dropping sharply against a basket of major peers. The move reflects growing conviction among investors that the Fed's tightening cycle may be nearing its end, even as inflation remains above target.

Gold Shines Brightly Above $4,300

Gold, often seen as a safe-haven asset, has been a major beneficiary of the dollar's weakness. Spot gold prices climbed above $4,300 an ounce, a level not seen in recent memory, as investors sought refuge from currency volatility and economic uncertainty.

The rally in bullion underscores a broader shift in market sentiment. With the dollar under pressure and rate hike odds fading, precious metals have become an attractive store of value. Analysts note that gold's breakout could signal further upside, especially if geopolitical risks continue to simmer.
