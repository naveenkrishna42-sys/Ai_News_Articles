8 CINTAA Members Resign, Allege Lack of Transparency in Leadership

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Eight members of CINTAA have resigned citing lack of transparency in the organisation's functioning.
  • Allegations of misuse of position have been levelled against Poonam Dhillon and Padmini Kolhapure.
  • Upasana Singh has termed the recent EGM 'illegal' and demanded fresh elections amid a 'very bad atmosphere'.

The Cine and TV Artistes' Association (CINTAA) is in the throes of a fresh crisis after eight of its members tendered their resignations, alleging a lack of transparency in the organisation's functioning. The departures come amid escalating accusations against two of its most prominent office bearers — actors Poonam Dhillon and Padmini Kolhapure — who have been accused of misusing their positions.

The resignations have triggered a war of words within the industry body, with veteran actor Upasana Singh calling the recent Extraordinary General Meeting (EGM) 'illegal' and demanding fresh elections. The internal turmoil, which had been simmering for weeks, has now burst into the open, casting a shadow over the organisation's credibility.

Allegations of Misuse and 'Vulgar' Claims

The departing members have not held back in their criticism. According to complaints circulating among CINTAA members, Dhillon and Kolhapure have been accused of leveraging their leadership roles for personal gain. The specifics of these allegations remain unclear, but the phrase 'lack of transparency' has become the rallying cry for those stepping away from the organisation.

Dhillon, who has served as a prominent face of CINTAA, broke her silence on the matter, dismissing the accusations as 'vulgar allegations'. She has denied any wrongdoing, but her response has done little to quell the discontent among the rank and file. Kolhapure, on the other hand, has yet to issue a detailed public statement, though sources suggest she is consulting with legal advisors.

Upasana Singh's 'Illegal' EGM and Demand for Fresh Polls

Upasana Singh, known for her comic roles in television and film, has emerged as a vocal critic of the current leadership. She has described the atmosphere within CINTAA as 'very bad', and has gone a step further by questioning the legitimacy of the recent EGM.

'They are not there to fire them, cancel their work or suspend them,' Singh said, referring to the leadership's actions. Her demand for fresh elections has found resonance among a section of members who believe the current executive committee has lost the moral authority to govern. The resignations of 11 executive committee members, reported earlier, have only added to the chaos.

Leadership Under Siege

For Dhillon and Kolhapure, this is a delicate moment. Both are respected figures in the Hindi film and television industry, with careers spanning decades. Yet, the allegations have put them on the defensive. The Hindustan Times has reported that members have formally complained about the duo's conduct, though the exact nature of the complaints has not been made public.

The resignations have also raised questions about the governance of CINTAA, a body that represents thousands of artistes across India. In an industry where survival often depends on collective bargaining, a fractured leadership could weaken the organisation's ability to negotiate on behalf of its members.

What's Next for CINTAA?

The coming weeks will be crucial. With calls for fresh elections growing louder, the onus is on the leadership to either address the concerns or step aside. A faction of members is reportedly preparing to move a formal resolution for a no-confidence vote.

For now, the industry is watching closely. If the crisis deepens, it could set a precedent for how artistes' bodies handle internal dissent. The ball is in CINTAA's court.
