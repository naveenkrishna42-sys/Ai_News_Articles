‘Possessed, ritualistic dance and high-energy’; ‘Red woman’ steals the show at Kodungallur Bharani

The ‘Red Woman’ Takes Centre Stage

At the famous Kodungallur Bharani festival in Kerala, one performer became the talk of the crowd. A woman, dressed entirely in red, performed a ritualistic dance that witnesses described as both ‘possessed’ and ‘high-energy’. Her movements were intense, her eyes wild, and her presence electrified the thousands who had gathered at the temple grounds.

Devotees and tourists alike stopped to watch as she spun, jumped, and swayed in a trance-like state. Many shouted praises, others recorded the moment on their phones. It was a spectacle that captured the raw, devotional spirit of the festival.

What is Kodungallur Bharani?

Kodungallur Bharani is an annual festival at the Kodungallur Bhagavathy Temple, dedicated to the goddess Bhadrakali. The event is known for its ecstatic, trance-inducing dances performed by devotees. Participants often believe they are possessed by the goddess, and their movements are not rehearsed but spontaneous and frenetic.

The festival attracts huge crowds from across the state. It is one of the most vibrant and intense temple festivals in Kerala. The ‘red woman’ became the highlight of this year’s edition.

Why Red?

Red is a colour strongly associated with the goddess Kali. It symbolises power, ferocity, and divine energy. The woman’s choice of attire was not lost on the crowd. Many saw it as a sign that she was fully possessed by the goddess.

Photographers and videographers jostled for a clear shot. Social media platforms were flooded with clips of her performance within hours. The festival authorities said no formal complaints were registered, and the event passed off peacefully.

A Festival of Devotion and Dance

The Kodungallur Bharani is not just a religious event; it is a cultural spectacle. Drum beats, loud chants, and the smell of incense fill the air. The dances are often intense, with participants entering a state of possession. It is common to see people beating their chests, whirling swords, or dancing on hot coals.

This year, the ‘red woman’ became the symbol of that fervour. Her performance lasted several minutes, and she was eventually escorted away by temple volunteers. Officials have not yet confirmed her identity. Locals say she is a regular devotee from a nearby village.

Some observers have raised concerns about the intensity of such performances. But for most, it is a revered tradition. The temple trust has not issued any statement regarding the woman or her dance.

What Next?

As videos of the ‘red woman’ continue to circulate online, the Kodungallur Bharani festival will proceed with its remaining rituals. The temple is expected to release an official update on the event later this week. For now, the image of a possessed woman in red, dancing with abandon, remains the enduring memory of this year's celebrations.
