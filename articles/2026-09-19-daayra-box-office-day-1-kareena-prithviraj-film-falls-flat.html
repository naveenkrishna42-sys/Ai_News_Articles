Daayra Day 1 Box Office: A Staggering Underperformance

Meghna Gulzar's Daayra, headlined by Kareena Kapoor Khan and Prithviraj Sukumaran, has delivered a disappointing opening day at the Indian box office. Early trade estimates place the film's day one nett collection at roughly ₹4-5 crore across all languages, a figure that falls well short of expectations for a project of this scale. The film arrived on a Friday that also saw the release of Vibe, but both new titles were comprehensively overshadowed by the continuing juggernaut of Hanuman Ansh, which is now in its 43rd day of release.

For a film carrying a reported production and marketing budget in the ₹60-70 crore range, a ₹4-5 crore opening signals a steep recovery challenge. Even with a strong weekend multiplier, the math is unforgiving: the film would need a near-4x weekend jump just to approach a respectable first-week total.

Screen Count and Occupancy: Where the Numbers Went Wrong

Daayra secured a wide release of approximately 2,000-2,200 screens across India, including a dubbed footprint in the South. Despite the screen count, occupancy in premium multiplexes during evening shows hovered in the 20-30% band in major metros, with single screens faring worse. The film's niche, dialogue-driven appeal — a Meghna Gulzar signature — did not translate into the mass opening that a Kareena Kapoor Khan starrer typically commands.

Why Hanuman Ansh Is Still the Story

The animated mythological epic Hanuman Ansh has redefined the Hindi box office cycle. On its 43rd day, it continues to post collections that rival or exceed fresh releases, a phenomenon rarely seen outside of films like Baahubali 2 or Pushpa 2. Its hold on family audiences and repeat viewership has effectively compressed the window for new films to find their footing.

For buyers and exhibitors, this creates a clear programming dilemma: allocating prime screens to a new, unproven title versus retaining a proven crowd-puller. Daayra and Vibe both lost this battle on day one, with several multiplex chains reducing their shows by Saturday morning.

Audience and Critical Reception: Praise for Performances, Doubts on Screenplay

Early audience reviews on social media and platforms like iDiva praised the lead performances — Kareena Kapoor Khan's restrained turn and Prithviraj Sukumaran's intensity drew consistent applause. However, a recurring criticism centred on the screenplay's pacing and narrative ambiguity. The Hindu's review noted that Gulzar challenges the "circle of public conscience," a thematic ambition that may not resonate with the mass audience seeking weekend entertainment.

Gulzar herself, in an Indian Express interview, spoke about AI versus "Human Instinct, Human Intelligence" — a philosophical framing that aligns with the film's introspective tone but does little to drive footfalls.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pros: Strong lead performances; a director with a credible track record (Raazi, Talvar); positive critical notices for craft and intent.
  • Cons: Poor day one occupancy; stiff competition from Hanuman Ansh; mixed word-of-mouth on screenplay; high budget relative to opening.
  • Exhibitor impact: Show reductions likely from day two; recovery dependent on urban multiplex holdover.
  • Buyer takeaway: Streaming rights may become the primary recovery route if theatrical legs do not improve by day five.

Verdict: A Tough Road Ahead

Daayra needed a double-digit opening to justify its scale. At ₹4-5 crore, it now depends on a rare weekend surge and strong weekday stability — a combination that eluded most 2026 releases facing the Hanuman Ansh wave. For trade analysts, the film reinforces a hard truth: star power alone cannot override a crowded calendar and a competitor with unprecedented staying power.
