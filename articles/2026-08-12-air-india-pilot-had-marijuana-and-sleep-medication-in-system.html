Air India Pilot Had Marijuana And Sleep Medication In System

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Air India pilot's confirmatory test positive for marijuana
  • Pilot was also on sleep medication, had sleep difficulty
  • Flight had autopilot disconnect after three hydraulic alerts
  • Air India has not yet commented on the findings

Drug Test Confirms Marijuana Use

An Air India pilot who operated a Phuket-Delhi flight has tested positive for marijuana in a confirmatory drug test, according to TV channels. The pilot, who was at the helm of the aircraft, is now under scrutiny after the initial screening flagged the substance.

The confirmatory test, which followed a preliminary positive result, has raised serious questions about cockpit safety protocols. Aviation regulators have strict rules prohibiting any substance use by flight crew, and this incident has put the spotlight on Air India's internal monitoring systems.

Sleep Medication Complicates Picture

Sources indicate the pilot had been struggling with sleep difficulty and was also on sleep medication at the time of the flight. The combination of marijuana and sleep aids in a pilot's bloodstream is a major red flag for aviation safety experts.

The presence of both substances could impair cognitive function and reaction times, which are critical during flight operations. The pilot's medical history and prescription details are now part of the investigation, though officials have not yet confirmed the exact medications involved.

Flight Experienced Hydraulic Alerts

The Phuket-Delhi flight reportedly faced a harrowing situation when the autopilot disconnected after three hydraulic alerts. Sources told India Today that the aircraft encountered these alerts mid-flight, forcing the crew to handle the situation manually.

Air India had earlier downplayed the incident, attributing the issues to 'turbulence' according to documents reviewed by NDTV. However, the hydraulic alerts suggest a more serious technical problem that may have been exacerbated by the pilot's condition.

Documents Contradict Official Narrative

Documents obtained by NDTV indicate that Air India's initial communication to regulators and passengers downplayed the severity of the flight's technical failures. The airline reportedly described the events as turbulence-related, but the internal records point to multiple system warnings.

This discrepancy has led to questions about transparency in reporting safety incidents. Aviation watchdogs are expected to review whether the airline followed proper reporting procedures under DGCA rules.

Investigation Underway

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has been alerted to the findings, and the pilot's license is likely to be suspended pending a full inquiry. The pilot has not yet made a public statement, and Air India has not commented on the drug test results.

This incident adds to a growing list of safety concerns for Air India, which has been under pressure to improve its operational standards. The airline's management is reportedly reviewing its crew health monitoring policies.

What remains unclear is how the pilot passed earlier medical checks and whether the sleep medication was prescribed. The investigation will also look into whether the flight should have been grounded given the pilot's condition.

As the probe continues, the aviation industry will be watching closely to see if stricter drug testing and mental health support measures are introduced for cockpit crews.
