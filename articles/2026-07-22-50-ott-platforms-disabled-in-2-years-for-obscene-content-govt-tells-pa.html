50 OTT Platforms Disabled in 2 Years for Obscene Content, Govt Tells Parliament

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • 50 OTT platforms disabled in two years for obscene content and other violations under the IT Act.
  • Government ramps up digital safety measures, including 6,650 cyber awareness workshops reaching 11.37 lakh citizens.
  • Action taken under Section 69A of the IT Act, targeting content that threatens public order or decency.

The government has disabled 50 over-the-top (OTT) platforms in the last two years for violating Indian laws by displaying obscene content and other infractions. The disclosure was made in Parliament, highlighting a sustained crackdown on digital content that authorities deem harmful.

Action Under IT Act Provisions

Officials confirmed the platforms were blocked under Section 69A of the Information Technology Act, 2000. This provision allows the government to block public access to any content that threatens India's sovereignty, security, public order, or decency.

The Ministry of Information and Broadcasting, which monitors OTT content, flagged multiple violations. These included explicit sexual material, vulgar language, and content that could incite communal disharmony.

Wider Digital Safety Push

Beyond the takedowns, the government has expanded its digital literacy campaign. Over 11.37 lakh citizens have been reached through 6,650 cyber awareness workshops conducted nationwide.

These workshops focus on safe internet practices, identifying harmful content, and reporting mechanisms. The initiative targets schools, colleges, and community centres, particularly in rural areas where digital awareness remains low.

Regulatory Framework Tightens

The crackdown follows the 2021 IT Rules, which mandated stricter self-regulation for OTT platforms. Platforms must now appoint a grievance officer and a nodal officer to address user complaints within 48 hours.

Industry insiders say the government has also increased informal monitoring. Multiple OTT services have received advisory notices asking them to ensure age-gating and content warnings are prominently displayed.

Impact on the OTT Industry

The disabled platforms primarily consisted of smaller, regional players. Many operated without proper registration or ignored repeated warnings from the ministry.

Major streaming services like Netflix, Amazon Prime, and Disney+ Hotstar have largely complied with the new rules. They now routinely add content descriptors and age ratings, a practice that was inconsistent before 2021.

What Critics Say

Free speech advocates argue the broad language of Section 69A allows for excessive censorship. They point out that 'obscene' and 'harmful' content definitions vary across cultural contexts.

The government maintains that the actions target only content that violates existing Indian laws. Officials say the goal is to protect children and vulnerable adults from explicit material, not to curb creative expression.

The number of disabled platforms is expected to rise as more complaints are processed. The government has also announced plans to introduce a new Digital India Act, which will replace the two-decade-old IT Act and include stricter provisions for OTT regulation.

Watch for further parliamentary debates on the Digital India Bill, expected to be tabled in the winter session. The bill's final shape will determine how OTT platforms operate in India for years to come.
