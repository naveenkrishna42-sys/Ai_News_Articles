India summons Russian diplomat over death of four Indian seafarers in Black Sea attack

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • India summoned Russian Deputy Chief of Mission to lodge a strong protest.
  • Four Indian seafarers were killed and one critically injured in a missile attack on a cargo vessel near Odesa port.
  • The Indian government described the incident as 'unacceptable' and demanded a thorough investigation.
  • Families of the victims in Kerala are in shock, with one victim having recently returned from his wedding plans.

New Delhi summoned Russia's Deputy Chief of Mission on Tuesday to lodge a strong protest over the killing of four Indian seafarers in a missile strike on a cargo vessel off the coast of Ukraine's Odesa port. The Indian government termed the incident 'unacceptable' and called for a full investigation.

The civilian ship was hit while operating in the Black Sea, an area that has seen repeated attacks since the escalation of the Russia-Ukraine conflict. One Indian national was also critically injured in the strike and remains hospitalised.

Strong diplomatic protest

Foreign Secretary Vinay Kwatra conveyed India's 'deep anguish' to the Russian diplomat, who was summoned to South Block. The Ministry of External Affairs issued a statement saying the attack on a merchant vessel was 'unacceptable' and that such acts must stop.

India also demanded safe passage for the injured seafarer and immediate repatriation of the mortal remains of the four deceased. The Russian side was reminded of its obligation under international law to ensure the safety of civilian shipping.

Dream voyage ends in tragedy

One of the victims was a 28-year-old merchant navy officer from Kerala who had finalised his wedding plans just weeks ago. Relatives told local media that he had called his mother hours before the attack, saying the ship was preparing to leave the conflict zone.

Another victim was a father of two from Tamil Nadu who had taken up the assignment to secure his children's education. The four sailors were part of a crew of about 20 on board a cargo vessel flagged to a foreign nation, officials said.

Kerala in mourning

In the coastal district of Alappuzha, neighbours gathered at the home of one victim as news of his death spread. Local priests and community leaders offered prayers. The district administration said it has been in touch with the Ministry of External Affairs to facilitate the return of the bodies.

The injured sailor is reported to be in a critical but stable condition at a hospital in Odesa. The Indian consulate in Istanbul is coordinating with local authorities for his medical evacuation, though officials have not yet confirmed a timeline.

Background of the attack

Ukrainian officials reported that a Russian missile struck the merchant vessel as it was anchored in a designated humanitarian corridor. The attack came amid a renewed Russian campaign against port infrastructure in southern Ukraine.

Russia has not officially commented on the specific incident. However, Moscow has previously stated that it does not target civilian vessels, blaming the Ukrainian military for using the corridor for military purposes.

New Delhi has consistently called for a return to dialogue and a peaceful resolution of the conflict, but Tuesday's summoning marks one of the strongest diplomatic actions India has taken since the war began.

India is now pushing for an independent inquiry into the attack. The government is also expected to review the safety advisories for Indian seafarers operating in conflict zones near the Black Sea.
