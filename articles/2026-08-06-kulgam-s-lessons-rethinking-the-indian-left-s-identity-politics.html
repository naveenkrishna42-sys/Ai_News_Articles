Kulgam's Lessons: Rethinking the Indian Left's Identity Politics

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kulgam's events challenge the Indian Left's focus on identity over material issues.
  • The article calls for a return to class-based politics and local engagement.
  • It highlights the disconnect between urban Left intellectuals and rural realities.

A Political Reckoning in the Valley

The dust has settled in Kulgam, but the political questions it raised are far from resolved. For the Indian Left, the events in this south Kashmir district are not just a regional concern—they are a mirror held up to a movement struggling to find its footing.

Frontline Magazine's recent analysis, titled 'Beyond Identity: What Kulgam Teaches the Indian Left,' argues that the episode exposes a deeper malaise. The Left, it suggests, has become overly preoccupied with identity-based mobilisation, often at the expense of the material struggles that once defined its core.

The Limits of Identity Frameworks

In Kulgam, the ground reality is one of economic distress, unemployment, and a fragile peace process. Yet, the political discourse, particularly from urban intellectual circles, tends to frame the situation in narrow identity terms—religious, regional, or cultural.

This framing, the analysis contends, misses the point. For the ordinary citizen in Kulgam, the daily concerns are not abstract debates about representation. They are about jobs, livelihoods, and the ability to live without fear. The Left's insistence on identity-first politics, the piece argues, risks alienating it from these very constituencies.

A Disconnect Between Theory and Practice

The critique extends to the organisational culture of the Indian Left. There is a visible gap between the theoretical pronouncements of party intellectuals and the on-ground work of local cadres. In Kulgam, this gap has been stark.

While national leaders issue statements on constitutional propriety and minority rights, local activists struggle to articulate a coherent economic alternative. The result is a political vacuum, one that other forces are all too ready to fill. The analysis urges a reconnection with grassroots realities—listening to what people actually need, rather than imposing pre-packaged ideological solutions.

Economic Questions Take Centre Stage

The article does not dismiss identity entirely, but it argues that the Left must subordinate it to class and economic questions. In Kulgam, as elsewhere in India, the pandemic-era economic shock and subsequent inflation have hit the working poor hardest.

These are the voters the Left claims to represent. Yet, the messaging often gets lost in a fog of cultural symbolism. The piece calls for a shift back to basics: land rights, minimum wages, public health, and education. These, it asserts, are the issues that can rebuild trust and relevance.

A Challenge for Leadership

The analysis also implicitly challenges Left leadership at all levels. It is no longer enough to be a voice of protest; the Left must offer a credible path to governance.

In Kulgam, that means engaging with local administrative structures, not just agitating against them. It means building alliances with farmers' unions, labour collectives, and small traders. The intellectual arrogance of the urban Left, the piece suggests, is a luxury the movement can no longer afford.

What Kulgam teaches, ultimately, is that political relevance is earned through tangible outcomes. The Left must prove it can improve lives, not just interpret them.

The Road Ahead

As the political calendar moves forward, the Indian Left faces a critical choice. It can cling to the comfort of identity-based slogans, or it can re-embrace the messy, difficult work of material politics.

The coming months will show whether these lessons from Kulgam translate into a changed strategy—or remain just another academic post-mortem.
