BBMP to issue notices to departments over waste clearance

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BBMP to issue notices to government departments over uncleared waste
  • Move follows complaints about garbage accumulation on departmental premises
  • Departments given deadline to comply or face further action

Bengaluru's civic body is set to crack down on government departments that have been lax in clearing waste from their premises. The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) will issue formal notices to departments that have failed to comply with existing waste management rules, officials said on Friday.

The decision comes after a series of complaints about garbage piling up on land owned by various state and central government offices. Residents and local groups have repeatedly flagged these spots as breeding grounds for mosquitoes and stray animals, adding to the city's mounting sanitation woes.

Notices to be served

BBMP officials confirmed that the notices will be served under the Karnataka Municipal Corporations Act, which makes it mandatory for all property owners—including government bodies—to ensure cleanliness. The move is part of a broader push to hold every stakeholder accountable, not just households and private businesses.

While the exact number of departments targeted has not been disclosed, sources indicate that the list includes offices with large campuses and vacant plots. These sites have often been neglected, with officials citing a lack of funds or manpower as reasons for the delay.

Deadline for compliance

The notices will give departments a specific timeframe to clear the waste and submit a compliance report. Failure to do so could lead to fines or further legal action, though the BBMP has not yet specified the penalty amount.

This is not the first time the civic body has attempted to enforce cleanliness rules on government properties. Previous drives have seen mixed results, with some departments acting quickly while others have dragged their feet.

Public pressure mounts

Residents in several parts of Bengaluru have taken to social media to name and shame departments that have allowed garbage to accumulate. Some have also filed formal complaints with the BBMP's grievance redressal cell, which has pushed the issue to the top of the civic body's agenda.

Environmental activists have welcomed the move but argue that sustained monitoring is key. "Notices are a start, but we need regular inspections and public reporting mechanisms to ensure these sites stay clean," said one activist, speaking on condition of anonymity.

What's next

The BBMP is expected to release the full list of departments served notices within a week. Officials have also hinted at deploying mobile squads to conduct surprise checks at these locations in the coming months.

For now, the focus remains on getting departments to act before the monsoon season worsens the situation. Whether the notices translate into visible change will depend on how seriously the departments take the directive.
