Experts Reclassify Obesity as Chronic Disease in New Medical Consensus

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • OSSI and ESI release first joint consensus on obesity management.
  • Medical experts emphasize that obesity is a chronic disease requiring clinical care.
  • Focus shifts toward sustainable, personalized lifestyle changes over extreme dieting.

A Shift in Clinical Perspective

India’s medical fraternity has formally redefined obesity, moving away from the outdated notion that weight gain is purely a consequence of personal failure. In a landmark move, the Obesity Surgery Society of India (OSSI) and the Endocrine Society of India (ESI) have released their first joint consensus on obesity management. This document signals a fundamental change in how the medical community approaches patients struggling with excess weight.

For decades, individuals living with obesity have faced social stigma, often being told that their health status is a direct result of weak willpower or poor discipline. The new consensus rejects this narrative. Instead, it positions obesity as a complex, chronic disease that requires professional medical intervention and long-term care, much like diabetes or hypertension.

Moving Beyond Extreme Diets

The joint report highlights that traditional, rapid-fix solutions often fail because they ignore the biological complexities of weight regulation. Many patients report a cycle of losing and regaining weight, which is often dismissed as a lack of effort. Experts argue that this "yo-yo" effect is frequently a clinical response to hormonal and metabolic imbalances rather than a lack of character.

The consensus encourages a move toward sustainable, personalized lifestyle changes. Rather than pushing for extreme caloric restriction or unsustainable fitness regimens, doctors are advised to tailor management plans to individual metabolic profiles. This personalized approach aims to improve long-term outcomes while reducing the psychological burden often associated with weight-loss programs.

Combating the Stigma of Weight

Stigma remains a significant barrier to effective healthcare in India. When patients feel judged by their clinicians, they are less likely to seek help until their health issues become critical. By framing obesity as a chronic condition, the medical bodies hope to foster a more empathetic environment in clinics across the country.

Healthcare providers are now being encouraged to integrate stigma-free care into their practice. This includes updated diagnostic protocols that prioritize a patient's overall metabolic health rather than focusing solely on the number on a scale. Training programs for medical staff are expected to reflect these findings, ensuring that the clinical experience is supportive and data-driven.

  • Focus on long-term metabolic health over short-term weight loss.
  • Remove the psychological bias currently present in patient-doctor interactions.
  • Implement evidence-based, personalized treatment strategies.

The medical community will now monitor the implementation of these guidelines across secondary and tertiary care hospitals. Observers are waiting to see how these recommendations influence insurance coverage and public health policies in the coming months.
