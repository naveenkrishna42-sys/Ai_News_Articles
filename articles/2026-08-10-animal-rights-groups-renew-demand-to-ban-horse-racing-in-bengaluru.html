Animal rights groups renew demand to ban horse racing in Bengaluru

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Animal rights groups have renewed their demand for a ban on horse racing in Bengaluru
  • Activists cite animal cruelty concerns in their campaign against the sport
  • The demand has sparked fresh debate over the future of horse racing in the city

Animal rights groups in Bengaluru have renewed their demand for a complete ban on horse racing, arguing that the sport inflicts prolonged suffering on animals. The call comes amid growing scrutiny of the racing industry's practices and its treatment of horses.

Fresh push against an old sport

Activists have long contended that horse racing is inherently cruel, subjecting animals to physical strain, injuries and stressful conditions. They argue that horses are often pushed beyond their limits for the sake of entertainment and betting revenue.

The latest demand is part of a sustained campaign by animal welfare organisations to shut down racing operations in the city. Their stance has found support among sections of the public who question the ethics of the sport.

The industry's counter-argument

Racing bodies and breeders, however, maintain that the sport is regulated and that horses receive proper veterinary care, nutrition and rest. They point to existing oversight mechanisms and argue that a ban would hurt livelihoods dependent on the industry.

Supporters of racing also claim that the animals are bred specifically for the sport and are well looked after. They say that ending racing could lead to neglect or abandonment of horses with no other purpose.

Regulatory grey areas

While racing in India operates under state-level rules, enforcement of animal welfare standards has often been questioned. Activists say inspections are inadequate and that injuries are common, though official data on such incidents is not consistently published.

Authorities have not yet indicated any move to act on the demand. The issue remains a contentious one, with both sides citing their own interpretations of animal welfare and economic impact.

A wider debate on animal sports

The demand in Bengaluru is part of a larger conversation in India about the acceptability of sports involving animals. Similar calls have been made in other states, though racing continues to operate in several cities.

Public opinion appears divided, with some viewing racing as a tradition and a legitimate sport, while others see it as an outdated practice that must end. The debate has gained traction on social media, where animal rights campaigns often find vocal support.

What lies ahead

For now, no formal proposal to ban racing has been tabled before the state government. Activists say they will continue to press their case, organising awareness drives and seeking legal intervention if needed.

The coming months could see the demand gain political traction, especially if more welfare groups join the campaign. Whether the authorities respond with stricter oversight or an outright ban remains to be seen.
