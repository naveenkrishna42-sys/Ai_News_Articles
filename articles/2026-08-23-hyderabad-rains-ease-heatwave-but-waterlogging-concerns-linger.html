Heavy Rains Bring Relief but Waterlogging Woes

Hyderabad experienced heavy rains on Sunday, providing much-needed relief from the ongoing heatwave. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) reported that several parts of the city received over 50 mm of rainfall, with the Manikonda area recording the highest at 75 mm. The sudden downpour brought down temperatures significantly, with the mercury dropping by up to 10 degrees Celsius in some areas.

Waterlogging and Infrastructure Strain

The heavy rains, however, have also caused waterlogging in several key areas, including Banjara Hills, Jubilee Hills, and Khairatabad. The Hyderabad Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board (HMWS&SB) has issued warnings about potential infrastructure damage, particularly to the city's drainage systems. The sudden shift from extreme heat to heavy rains has overwhelmed the city's preparedness, leading to temporary disruptions in traffic and public services.

Residents React to Unpredictable Weather

Residents of Hyderabad have expressed mixed reactions to the sudden weather shift. While many welcomed the relief from the heat, others were caught off guard by the sudden downpour, leading to minor inconveniences. The sudden change in weather patterns has also raised concerns among meteorologists about the increasing unpredictability of weather in the region.

Expert Analysis

Dr. Arun Kumar, a climate scientist at the Centre for Climate Change Research, India, attributes the sudden weather shift to the city's unique microclimate. Hyderabad's rapid urbanization and lack of green cover have made it more susceptible to extreme weather events. Dr. Kumar warns that such events could become more frequent in the future, necessitating better urban planning and infrastructure development.
