Mumbai Rains: Schools Shut, University Exams Delayed as Downpour Hits City

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • All schools in Mumbai closed for the day due to heavy rainfall.
  • Mumbai University postpones scheduled exams until further notice.
  • Local train and road services disrupted, with waterlogging reported in low-lying areas.

Mumbai woke up to a relentless downpour on Monday, prompting authorities to shut schools and postpone university exams as the city's infrastructure struggled under the deluge.

The India Meteorological Department has issued a heavy rain alert for the city and its suburbs, with several areas reporting more than 200 mm of rainfall in the past 24 hours. The civic body has declared a holiday for all municipal and private schools as a precautionary measure.

Schools Shut, Exams Postponed

The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation announced that all schools in the city will remain closed today. Parents have been advised to keep children indoors as waterlogging and traffic jams make commuting hazardous.

Mumbai University has followed suit, postponing all scheduled examinations until further notice. A university spokesperson said a revised timetable will be released once the weather improves. Students are urged to check the official website for updates.

Transport Snarls and Waterlogging

Local train services, the city's lifeline, are running behind schedule on all four lines. Several tracks were submerged in the early hours, forcing trains to crawl at reduced speeds. Commuters reported waiting for over an hour at major stations like Dadar and Kurla.

Road traffic is equally chaotic. The Western and Eastern Express Highways have witnessed long tailbacks, with waterlogging reported at low-lying points such as Hindmata, Sion, and King's Circle. The BMC has deployed pumps to clear water, but progress is slow.

Flight and Bus Operations Affected

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport is operational, but several flights have been delayed due to poor visibility. Passengers are advised to check with their airlines before heading to the airport.

BEST bus services are also disrupted, with many routes diverted away from flooded stretches. The civic transport body has urged commuters to use alternate modes where possible.

Weather Warning and Precautionary Steps

The IMD has warned that heavy rainfall is likely to continue over the next 24 to 48 hours. It has advised residents in low-lying areas to move to safer locations and avoid unnecessary travel.

The administration has set up control rooms and deployed disaster response teams in vulnerable areas. Fishermen have been advised not to venture into the sea as conditions remain rough.

Officials have not yet confirmed any casualties or major damage, but reports of trees uprooted and localised flooding are emerging from several neighbourhoods. Citizens are urged to stay indoors and dial 1916 for emergency assistance.

As the monsoon intensifies over the west coast, Mumbai is likely to brace for more rain in the coming days. The next 48 hours will be critical as authorities work to restore normalcy and prevent further disruption.
