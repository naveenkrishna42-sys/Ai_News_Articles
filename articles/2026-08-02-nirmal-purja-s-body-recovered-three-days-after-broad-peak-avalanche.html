Nirmal Purja's body recovered three days after Broad Peak avalanche

📅 2026-08-02 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nirmal Purja's body recovered three days after avalanche on Broad Peak
  • Three other climbers' bodies also found after the deadly incident
  • Purja had earlier climbed all 14 peaks above 8,000m in record time

Rescue teams have recovered the body of legendary mountaineer Nirmal 'Nimsdai' Purja three days after an avalanche swept through Broad Peak in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK). Officials confirmed that the bodies of three other climbers were also found at the site, bringing the confirmed death toll from the incident to four.

Purja, a former elite UK commando and one of the most celebrated high-altitude climbers of his generation, had been attempting what his team described as a final, personal dream on the 8,051-metre peak. The avalanche struck without warning, catching the climbers in a vulnerable section of the mountain.

A career that rewrote mountaineering

Purja shot to global fame in 2019 when he climbed all 14 of the world's mountains above 8,000 metres in just six months and six days — a record that shattered the previous mark by over seven years. His 'Project Possible' feat was widely hailed as one of the greatest achievements in the history of high-altitude climbing.

Born in Nepal's Myagdi district, Purja served with the British Army's Gurkha regiment before joining the Special Boat Service, where he spent nearly a decade. His military background, he often said, gave him the mental toughness and discipline that defined his climbing style.

Beyond the records, Purja was known for rescue missions at altitude. In 2018, he abandoned his own summit push on K2 to help a stranded climber, an act that earned him widespread respect in the climbing community.

The Broad Peak tragedy

Broad Peak, the 12th highest mountain in the world, sits in the Karakoram range near the Pakistan-China border. It is considered technically less demanding than K2 but still carries significant avalanche risk, particularly in the upper sections where snow accumulation can be unpredictable.

The avalanche hit on the mountain's standard route, a corridor that climbers have used for decades. Details of exactly how the four climbers were caught remain unclear. Officials have not yet confirmed the identities of the other three victims, though family members have been informed in at least two cases, according to local sources.

Rescue operations were hampered by poor weather and the logistical difficulty of working at such altitude. Teams from Pakistan's tourism department and international climbers already on the mountain joined the search effort.

Final dream

Purja had spoken in recent interviews about wanting to climb Broad Peak in winter, a challenge that has only been completed once. His team said he viewed the 2026 expedition as a return to his roots — a quieter, more personal project after years of record-chasing and filmmaking.

In 2021, a documentary about his 2019 feat, '14 Peaks: Nothing Is Impossible', was released on Netflix, introducing him to a global audience far beyond the climbing world. He had also launched a climbing gear brand and mentored young Nepali climbers.

The mountaineering community has responded with an outpouring of tributes. Fellow climbers described him as a once-in-a-generation talent whose impact on the sport would be felt for decades.

What lies ahead

Authorities are working to bring the bodies down from the mountain, a process that could take several days given the terrain and weather conditions. An official investigation into the avalanche has been announced, though such inquiries often yield limited conclusions given the unpredictable nature of mountain hazards.

For the climbing world, the loss marks a sombre end to a remarkable life. Purja is survived by his wife, Suchi, and their young son. His final expedition, like so much of his career, was one that pushed the boundaries of what was thought possible — and it ended the way the mountains so often do, on their own terms.
