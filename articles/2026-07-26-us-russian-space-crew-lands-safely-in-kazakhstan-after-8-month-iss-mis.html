US-Russian space crew lands safely in Kazakhstan after 8-month ISS mission

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Expedition 74 crew returned to Earth aboard Soyuz MS-28 spacecraft
  • NASA astronaut and two Roscosmos cosmonauts landed safely in Kazakhstan
  • Eight-month mission included scientific experiments and station maintenance
  • Landing marks routine crew rotation for International Space Station

Crew returns after extended stay in orbit

A NASA astronaut and two Russian cosmonauts touched down safely in the steppes of Kazakhstan on Sunday, bringing an eight-month mission aboard the International Space Station to a close. The Soyuz MS-28 spacecraft carrying the Expedition 74 crew landed as planned, according to Roscosmos, Russia’s space agency.

The crew undocked from the ISS earlier in the day and fired braking rockets to begin their descent through the atmosphere. Parachutes deployed over the Kazakh desert, slowing the capsule before a final touchdown on the dry plains.

Routine rotation, critical science

The three crew members — one from NASA and two from Roscosmos — had been living and working on the orbiting laboratory since late 2023. During their stint, they conducted experiments in biology, physics, and materials science, alongside routine maintenance of station systems.

Expedition 74 was part of the ongoing partnership between the United States and Russia on the ISS, a collaboration that has continued despite geopolitical tensions on Earth. The mission was a standard crew rotation, replacing earlier teams and ensuring the station remained staffed around the clock.

Smooth landing in Kazakh steppe

Search and recovery teams reached the capsule within minutes of touchdown. The crew was assisted out of the spacecraft and placed in reclining chairs for initial medical checks, a standard procedure after months in microgravity.

Officials confirmed all three were in good health. They will be flown to their respective space agency facilities for further evaluation and rehabilitation to readjust to Earth’s gravity.

What comes next

The ISS remains operational, with the next crew — Expedition 75 — already preparing for launch. The safe return of this crew clears the way for continued rotations and experiments aboard the station, which has been continuously inhabited for over two decades.

Both NASA and Roscosmos have not yet announced specific dates for the next launch, but standard scheduling typically places the next crew handover within a few months.
