Netanyahu calls PM Modi, vows to bolster Israel-India ties

📅 2026-08-08 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Netanyahu dialled PM Modi and discussed recent developments in West Asia
  • Both leaders reaffirmed commitment to deepening India-Israel bilateral ties
  • Phone call comes amid ongoing regional tensions and diplomatic outreach

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Friday telephoned Prime Minister Narendra Modi, in a call that officials described as warm and substantive. The conversation, which took place against the backdrop of volatile West Asian geopolitics, saw Netanyahu thank Modi and pledge to further cement ties between the two nations.

Focus on West Asia developments

The two leaders discussed the latest situation in West Asia, a region that has remained fraught with conflict and diplomatic flux. While the specifics of the discussion were not publicly detailed, the Prime Minister's Office in New Delhi confirmed that the call covered regional developments and bilateral matters of mutual interest.

Netanyahu's outreach comes at a time when Israel has been actively engaging with friendly nations to shore up support and communicate its positions. The phone call underscores the growing strategic convergence between India and Israel, which has expanded well beyond defence and agriculture into technology, water management, and cybersecurity.

Bilateral ties on an upward trajectory

India and Israel elevated their relationship to a strategic partnership during Modi's historic visit to Israel in 2017, the first by an Indian prime minister. Since then, high-level exchanges have become more frequent, with trade and investment flows growing steadily. The two countries have also collaborated on several infrastructure and innovation projects, reflecting a relationship that is no longer limited to arms deals.

Netanyahu's message of gratitude and commitment to strengthening ties is seen as a signal of continuity, even as both leaders navigate complex regional and domestic challenges. The conversation also touched on shared concerns, including counter-terrorism and maritime security in the Indian Ocean region, though official readouts remained guarded.

Diplomatic signalling amid regional flux

The timing of the call is significant. West Asia is witnessing shifting alliances, with several nations reassessing their positions amid ongoing hostilities and peace efforts. For Israel, India remains a crucial partner in Asia, both as a counterweight in multilateral forums and as a reliable technological and economic ally.

For India, engagement with Israel is part of a broader West Asian strategy that balances ties with Iran, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE. New Delhi has consistently maintained that its relationships in the region are independent and issue-based, and Friday's call reinforces that approach without alienating other partners.

Observers note that such direct leader-to-leader communication helps manage differences and build trust, especially when regional crises have the potential to spill over into global supply chains and energy markets. India has also been vocal about the need for de-escalation and humanitarian access in conflict zones, a stance that New Delhi has reiterated in international forums.

What to watch next

With both leaders signalling a desire to deepen cooperation, the coming months could see new agreements in trade, technology, and defence manufacturing. The frequency of high-level visits is also likely to increase, with potential exchanges at multilateral summits later this year.

As the West Asian situation evolves, the India-Israel partnership will be tested on how it navigates sensitivities with other regional players. For now, the phone call serves as a reaffirmation of a bond that both capitals clearly value.
