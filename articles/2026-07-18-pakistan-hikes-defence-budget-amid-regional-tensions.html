Pakistan hikes defence budget amid regional tensions

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Wars & Conflicts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pakistan has raised its defence budget for the upcoming fiscal year.
  • The increase is attributed to regional conflicts and security concerns.
  • Details of the exact allocation and breakdown are still awaited.

Pakistan has announced a significant increase in its defence spending for the upcoming fiscal year, a move officials attribute to ongoing regional conflicts and evolving security threats. The budget hike, confirmed by the finance ministry, underscores Islamabad's focus on military modernisation amid a volatile neighbourhood.

Budget details

The new defence allocation marks a notable rise from the previous year's expenditure. While the exact percentage increase has not been officially detailed, sources indicate that the funds will be directed towards upgrading equipment, enhancing border security, and supporting counter-terrorism operations.

Pakistan's defence budget has historically consumed a substantial portion of the national exchequer, and this year's hike continues that trend. The government has not yet disclosed the full breakdown of the spending, but analysts expect a focus on air and naval capabilities.

Regional context

The decision comes against the backdrop of heightened tensions in South Asia. Neighbouring Afghanistan remains unstable, and ongoing hostilities between India and Pakistan have kept military readiness a top priority. Officials in Islamabad have repeatedly cited the need to maintain a credible deterrent posture.

Pakistan's military has been engaged in multiple fronts, including counter-insurgency operations along the western border. The increased funding is seen as a response to what the government describes as a complex and unpredictable security environment.

Economic implications

Defence spending increases often raise questions about fiscal discipline in a country facing economic challenges. Pakistan is currently navigating a tight fiscal space, with high inflation and a need to secure external financing. The government, however, has argued that national security cannot be compromised.

Critics have pointed out that larger defence outlays could crowd out spending on social sectors like health and education. The finance ministry has not commented on how the budget will be balanced, but it is expected to present a full fiscal plan in the coming weeks.

The hike is likely to be debated in parliament, where opposition parties have already signalled they will seek greater transparency on military expenditures. Past defence budgets have often been passed with minimal scrutiny, but this year's increase may invite more intense discussion.

What to watch

All eyes will be on the budget session in Islamabad, where the government is expected to provide further details on the defence allocation. The international community, particularly neighbouring countries and global financial institutions, will be watching closely for how this spending aligns with Pakistan's economic commitments.
