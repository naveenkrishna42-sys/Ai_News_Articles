Meta Apologises For Blocking PM Modi's Facebook Video, Cites Technical Glitch

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Meta has apologised for briefly removing a Facebook video of Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
  • The company cited a 'technical glitch' and said the removal was an error.
  • The incident has sparked criticism and calls for a ban on Meta's AI tools in India.

Meta has apologised after a Facebook video of Prime Minister Narendra Modi was briefly removed from the platform. The company cited a 'technical glitch' for the action, sources told NDTV.

The video, which featured PM Modi taking a selfie, was taken down for a short period before being restored. Meta later said the removal was an error and has since apologised for the incident.

Meta's Response

Meta said the video was removed due to a technical issue, not a policy violation. The company did not provide further details on what caused the glitch.

“The video was removed in error and has been restored. We apologise for the inconvenience,” a Meta spokesperson said, according to reports from The Times of India and Hindustan Times.

Criticism and Calls for Action

The incident has drawn sharp criticism from various quarters. Some have accused Meta of interfering in India's democratic processes and economy.

WION reported that the incident has led to calls for banning Meta's AI tools in India, with critics alleging that such algorithms are being used to suppress political speech. The removal has been linked to concerns over deepfake detection algorithms mistakenly flagging legitimate content.

India Today highlighted that the removal of the PM's video has raised questions about the reliability of Meta's automated moderation systems. Critics argue that the company's AI tools are not mature enough to handle nuanced political content without human oversight.

What Happened

The video was taken down on Tuesday, July 28, 2026. It showed PM Modi taking a selfie at an event. The exact duration of the removal has not been confirmed by officials.

Sources said the video was flagged by Meta's automated systems before being manually reviewed and restored. The company has not disclosed whether any human reviewer was involved in the initial takedown.

Broader Implications

The incident comes amid growing scrutiny of social media platforms and their content moderation practices in India. The government has previously clashed with tech giants over issues of censorship and algorithmic bias.

This is not the first time Meta has faced criticism in India. The company has been accused of bias in handling content from political figures and parties. The latest incident is likely to intensify calls for greater transparency in how these platforms moderate political speech.

The Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology has not yet commented on the matter. Officials are expected to seek a detailed explanation from Meta in the coming days.

As Meta works to contain the fallout, the incident serves as a reminder of the fragility of trust between tech platforms and governments. The coming weeks will show whether this apology is enough to quell the growing demand for accountability. Watch for any formal government notice or parliamentary discussion on the issue.
