Awarapan 2 Leads Advance Booking, But Batwara 1947 Wins Screen Count

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Awarapan 2 sold 5,500 tickets on day one of advance booking
  • Batwara 1947 demands all single-screen shows over Awarapan 2
  • Writer of Awarapan 2 denies using nostalgia bait in the sequel

The box office clash between Awarapan 2 and Batwara 1947 is shaping up to be one of the most closely watched battles of the year. Early trends show a split verdict: Emraan Hashmi's film is leading in advance bookings, but Sunny Deol's period drama is dominating the single-screen circuit.

Trade analysts note that while Awarapan 2 has taken a clear lead in prepaid tickets, exhibitors in several circuits are leaning toward Batwara 1947 for prime shows. The gap between the two films at the box office window may not reflect the actual screen allocation on release day.

Advance booking numbers tell one story

Day one of advance booking for Awarapan 2 kicked off with roughly 5,500 tickets sold across major chains. The film, starring Emraan Hashmi and Disha Patani, has generated strong buzz among urban audiences, particularly in multiplexes.

That figure, while promising, is not a runaway hit by pre-sale standards. It suggests solid but not explosive demand. The real test will come over the next 48 hours as bookings open for the weekend, especially in mass-market centres.

Single-screen exhibitors lean toward Batwara 1947

Reports from trade circles indicate that Batwara 1947 has been demanded by single-screen owners across north and central India. The film, headlined by Sunny Deol, is being positioned as a hard-hitting partition-era drama, a genre that historically resonates with single-screen audiences.

Several exhibitors have reportedly asked for all their single-screen shows to be allocated to Batwara 1947, leaving Awarapan 2 with limited presence in those venues. This is a significant factor, as single screens still contribute heavily to opening-weekend numbers in many territories.

Why the split matters

The divergent trend highlights a familiar pattern in Hindi cinema. Multiplexes favour urban, relationship-driven stories; single screens respond to action, emotion and star power. Awarapan 2, a sequel to the 2007 cult hit, leans on its lead pair's chemistry and a modern soundtrack. Batwara 1947, by contrast, banks on historical gravitas and Sunny Deol's mass appeal.

Both films are eyeing the same Independence Day weekend window, which makes the clash even more intense. The final screen count will likely be split unevenly, with multiplexes tilting toward Awarapan 2 and single screens favouring Batwara 1947.

No nostalgia bait, says writer

Amid the box office chatter, the writer of Awarapan 2 has clarified that the sequel is not relying on nostalgia to pull in audiences. In a recent interview, the writer stated, "Didn't want to patch any story," emphasising that the film is a fresh narrative rather than a rehash of the original.

That stance may help the film with younger viewers who did not grow up with the first instalment. But it also means the makers cannot depend solely on the original's fan base to drive footfalls.

What to watch next

With both films set to hit theatres this week, the next few days of advance bookings will be crucial. If Awarapan 2 continues to lead in prepaid sales while Batwara 1947 holds the single-screen advantage, the opening-day numbers could be remarkably close.

Industry observers will be tracking not just collections but also occupancy rates, especially in the evening shows where the real battle will be fought.
