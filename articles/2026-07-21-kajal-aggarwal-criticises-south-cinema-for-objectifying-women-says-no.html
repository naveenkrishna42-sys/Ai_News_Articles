Kajal Aggarwal criticises South cinema for objectifying women, says no to fruit-throwing scenes

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kajal Aggarwal criticised the portrayal of women in South Indian cinema as objectifying
  • She refused to shoot scenes where fruits are thrown on her navel
  • The actress called for better, more respectful roles for women in the industry

Actress Kajal Aggarwal has taken a strong stand against the way women are often depicted in South Indian cinema. In a recent interview, she made it clear that she will no longer be part of scenes that reduce her to an object.

“You are not throwing fruits on my navel,” she said, referring to a particular trope in many films where cameras linger on a woman’s body during song sequences or comedic moments. Her blunt remark has sparked fresh debate about sexism in the film industry.

A long-standing complaint

For decades, critics have pointed out that many South Indian movies treat female characters as decorative items rather than fully realised people. Kajal’s comments echo that frustration.

She stated that while the industry has evolved in many ways, the camera’s gaze on women has remained uncomfortably fixed. “We need better writing and direction that respects women,” she added.

The actress did not name specific films or directors but made it clear that she will be selective about her projects going forward.

Industry reaction and context

Several other actresses have spoken about similar experiences, though most have done so cautiously. Kajal’s directness is notable given the power dynamics in the industry.

Her comments come at a time when conversations about workplace dignity and representation are gaining ground across Indian cinema. The MeToo movement and changing audience sensibilities have pushed some filmmakers to reconsider old formulas.

Still, many female actors say they continue to face pressure to conform to stereotypical roles that prioritise their appearance over their talent.

What Kajal wants to see

Kajal Aggarwal called for more nuanced roles for women—characters with agency, intelligence, and depth. She believes the audience is ready for such change.

“We are not just props,” she said. “We have stories to tell, and we want to be seen as people, not body parts.”

Her stance has been widely shared on social media, with many fans and fellow artists applauding her courage.

It remains to be seen whether filmmakers will take note. But Kajal’s words have added to a growing chorus demanding a shift in how women are portrayed on screen.
