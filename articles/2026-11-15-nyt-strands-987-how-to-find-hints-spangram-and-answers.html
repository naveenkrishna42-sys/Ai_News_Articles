What Strands Asks of Solvers on November 15, 2026

Strands is The New York Times word-search variant, and puzzle #987 is dated Sunday, November 15, 2026. Unlike a classic word search with a supplied word list, Strands hides one connected idea across the board, and the spangram tells you what that idea is.

The board resets at midnight in the player's local time zone, which means #987 unlocks at different hours across New York, London and Mumbai. That rolling release is why hint and answer articles appear in staggered waves through the day rather than all at once.

We are not printing the #987 theme words or spangram here. Any page that claims to confirm the board before the New York Times publishes it is guessing, and reading a full answer list before you play removes the only reason to open the puzzle.

How the Grid and the Spangram Work

A Strands board runs six columns by eight rows for 48 letters. Answer paths are continuous and can bend, double back and travel diagonally, so a word rarely sits in the straight line a traditional word search trains you to expect.

The spangram touches two opposite sides of the grid, running either left-to-right or top-to-bottom. It names the category holding the theme together and is not counted as one of the theme words, a distinction that trips up newer solvers who keep hunting for it inside the answer set.

A Repeatable Method for Cracking #987

1. Hunt the spangram first

Scan the top and bottom rows, then the left and right columns, for a phrase long enough to cross the full width or height of the board. Finding it early converts a vague search into a targeted one, because the category narrows every candidate word that follows.

2. Bank bonus words to buy hints

Every valid dictionary word that is not part of the theme counts as a bonus word, and three of them unlock a hint that exposes the path of one theme word. Obvious plurals and simple verb forms are often rejected, so test unusual combinations before assuming the board is resisting you.

3. Work the theme, not the dictionary

Once the spangram names the category, generate candidates from the theme and trace them on the board rather than reading the grid for anything recognisable. Themes in Strands frequently rely on wordplay, so a plural, a compound or a loose synonym is usually the intended answer.

4. Check the puzzle number before you read any answer

Hint aggregators keep older boards live, and #986 covers November 14, 2026. Confirm both the number and the date on any page you open; mismatched pages are the most common cause of solvers walking away with yesterday's answers.

Where Verified Hints Actually Come From

Outlets including CNET, TechRadar and Yahoo Tech publish tiered hint pages that reveal a nudge first and the full list further down. Read only as far as you need, and stop at the first spoiler rather than scrolling to the bottom out of habit.

The most reliable source remains the game itself. The in-game hint system, fed by bonus words, is generated from the same board you are playing and cannot be out of date.
