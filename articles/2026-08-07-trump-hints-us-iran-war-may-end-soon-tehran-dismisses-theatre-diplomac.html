Trump hints US-Iran war may end soon; Tehran dismisses 'theatre diplomacy'

📅 2026-08-07 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says the war with Iran cannot 'go much longer' and hints at a near end.
  • Iran mocks Trump's peace signals as 'theatre diplomacy'.
  • Iran and Oman agree to reopen the Strait of Hormuz for 60 days, with coordinates under discussion.
  • Oil prices rise over concerns about the Strait of Hormuz reopening plans.

Trump signals endgame in US-Iran conflict

United States President Donald Trump has indicated that the conflict with Iran is nearing its conclusion, stating that the war cannot 'go much longer'. His comments, made on Thursday, come amid escalating tensions in the Persian Gulf and growing international pressure for a ceasefire.

'It will end pretty soon,' Trump said, without offering a specific timeline or conditions for peace. The remarks are among his most explicit yet on the trajectory of the hostilities, which have rattled global energy markets and raised fears of a wider regional conflagration.

Tehran's dismissive response

Iranian officials have wasted no time in ridiculing Trump's overtures, dismissing them as 'theatre diplomacy'. Tehran's mocking tone suggests deep scepticism about Washington's intentions, even as diplomatic channels appear to be quietly opening.

The Iranian foreign ministry has not issued an official statement on Trump's latest remarks, but the dismissive characterisation from Tehran underscores the fragility of any potential peace process. Analysts note that years of mutual distrust could undermine even the most well-intentioned mediation efforts.

Strait of Hormuz: A tentative breakthrough

In a parallel development, Iran and Oman have reportedly reached an understanding on the coordinates for a route through the Strait of Hormuz, with plans to reopen the critical waterway for 60 days. The strait, through which roughly a fifth of global oil passes, has been a flashpoint throughout the conflict.

Iran's Ministry of Foreign Affairs confirmed the agreement, though details remain sparse. The reopening, if implemented, could ease supply fears and stabilise oil prices, which have been volatile since the war began. However, the 60-day window suggests a provisional arrangement, subject to further negotiations.

Oil markets on edge

Oil prices have ticked upward on concerns over the Strait of Hormuz reopening plans, with traders wary of the logistical and security challenges involved. Even as diplomatic efforts advance, the risk of disruption persists, keeping markets on a knife's edge.

The 60-day reopening is being viewed as a test of both sides' commitment to de-escalation. Whether it leads to a durable peace or merely a temporary lull remains an open question, with regional powers watching closely.

What to watch next

The coming weeks will be critical, as the 60-day window for the Hormuz reopening unfolds and Trump's promised endgame takes shape. Whether Tehran's 'theatre diplomacy' jibe translates into concrete negotiating positions, or remains a rhetorical shield, will determine if this conflict truly winds down.
