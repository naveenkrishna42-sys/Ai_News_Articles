Mumbai Police book organisers of Shivaji Park protest attended by Uddhav Thackeray

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mumbai Police registered a case against organisers of a protest at Shivaji Park attended by Uddhav Thackeray.
  • The protest allegedly violated Supreme Court orders and noise pollution rules.
  • The case was filed under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code and the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules.

Mumbai Police have registered a case against the organisers of a protest held at Shivaji Park last week that was attended by Shiv Sena (UBT) chief Uddhav Thackeray. The police action comes after complaints that the gathering violated multiple legal restrictions.

Violations alleged at the protest site

According to officials, the organisers did not obtain the required permissions for the event. The protest also allegedly breached Supreme Court orders that restrict large gatherings and the use of loudspeakers in residential areas.

Residents near the park had filed complaints about excessive noise and disruption. The police have invoked sections of the Indian Penal Code related to public nuisance and disobedience to orders. They have also cited the Noise Pollution (Regulation and Control) Rules, 2000.

Shivaji Park's sensitivity as a venue

Shivaji Park in Dadar has been a politically significant ground for decades. It is also a designated silence zone, which makes it subject to strict noise regulations. The Supreme Court had earlier imposed restrictions on the use of loudspeakers and amplified sound systems in the area.

Police sources said the protest organisers failed to comply with these conditions. The event saw a large turnout of Shiv Sena (UBT) supporters, and Thackeray addressed the crowd from a temporary stage.

No arrests have been made so far. The investigation is ongoing, and more individuals could be named in the case.

Political reactions and next steps

Shiv Sena (UBT) leaders have criticised the police action, calling it a vendetta by the ruling Mahayuti government. They have argued that similar protests by other parties have gone unchecked.

The police have not commented on the political allegations. They have said the case is based on specific complaints and evidence of violations.

The next hearing in the matter is expected in a local court. The organisers have been asked to appear before the investigating officer later this week.

What happens now depends on whether the organisers seek anticipatory bail or challenge the FIR in court. The police are also likely to examine whether Thackeray himself could be called for questioning, though no such notice has been issued yet.
