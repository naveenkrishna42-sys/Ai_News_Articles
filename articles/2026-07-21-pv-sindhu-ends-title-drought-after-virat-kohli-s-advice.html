PV Sindhu ends title drought after Virat Kohli's advice

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • PV Sindhu won the Japan Open BWF Super 750 title, ending a prolonged title drought.
  • She revealed a phone call with cricketer Virat Kohli provided key advice on handling pressure.
  • The win marks a significant comeback, with Sindhu adopting a data-driven training approach.

Phone call that changed everything

PV Sindhu's return to the winner's circle at the Japan Open began with a phone call. Not to a coach or a sports psychologist, but to cricketer Virat Kohli.

Speaking after the final, Sindhu revealed that Kohli's advice helped her break free from a cycle of near-misses. The conversation focused on finding joy in competition rather than obsessing over results.

Kohli's advice: find joy, not pressure

Kohli told Sindhu that champions are defined not by their trophies, but by their ability to rise after falls. The cricketer emphasised the importance of enjoying the process and not letting the weight of expectations crush a player's spirit.

Sindhu admitted she had been overthinking every match. The chat with Kohli reminded her that she had already proven her mettle. What remained was to play freely.

A data-driven comeback

The Japan Open triumph was not just a mental reset. Sindhu's camp had reengineered her game using data analytics. Shot selection, rally patterns, opponent weaknesses — everything was broken down into numbers.

This radical approach helped Sindhu identify specific areas of improvement. She worked on her net play and defensive retrievals, both of which paid off during the tournament. The result was a more complete player on court.

Former teammates and rivals took note. Saina Nehwal posted on social media, 'You can never write a champion off.' The message captured the sentiment of Indian badminton fans who had waited patiently for Sindhu's return to form.

Ending the longest drought

The Japan Open title was Sindhu's first BWF Super 750 win in years. The drought had stretched longer than any in her career. Each final loss added to the narrative that she had lost her killer instinct.

But Sindhu silenced those doubts with a commanding performance in the final. She moved better, struck harder, and made fewer unforced errors. The victory was comprehensive.

Kohli's advice had clearly worked. Sindhu looked relaxed during the trophy ceremony, smiling broadly. The relief was visible.

What this means for Paris 2028

The Japan Open win comes at a crucial time. With the Paris 2028 Olympics approaching, Sindhu's return to form gives Indian badminton a genuine medal contender.

She has already won silver and bronze at previous Games. A gold in Paris would complete her collection. For now, she is taking it one tournament at a time.

Sindhu's team will continue with the data-driven approach. They believe the numbers will keep her ahead of younger challengers. The phone call with Kohli might have fixed the mind, but the data is fixing the game.
