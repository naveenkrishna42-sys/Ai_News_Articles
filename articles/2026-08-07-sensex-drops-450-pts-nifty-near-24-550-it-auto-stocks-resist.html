Sensex drops 450 pts, Nifty near 24,550; IT, auto stocks resist

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell nearly 450 points in early trade on Friday.
  • Nifty traded near 24,550, with IT and auto stocks bucking the trend.
  • Broader market witnessed selling pressure amid global cues.

Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply lower on Friday, with the Sensex shedding nearly 450 points and the Nifty slipping towards the 24,550 level. The decline was broad-based, although select IT and auto stocks managed to stay afloat, offering some respite to investors.

Market opens in red

The BSE Sensex dropped as much as 448 points in early deals, while the NSE Nifty traded around 24,550, down about 0.6 percent from the previous close. Selling pressure was visible across most sectors, with financials, metals, and energy stocks leading the slide.

Traders pointed to weak global cues and profit-booking after recent gains as key reasons for the downturn. The volatility index ticked up, signalling cautious sentiment among market participants.

IT and auto stocks resist the fall

Despite the negative breadth, IT stocks such as Infosys, TCS, and HCL Technologies traded in positive territory, supported by a weaker rupee and expectations of steady deal wins. Auto majors including Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra also held their ground, helped by robust monthly sales data.

Analysts said the resilience in these pockets reflected sector-specific fundamentals rather than a broader market mood. However, they cautioned that sustained buying would be needed to reverse the downtrend.

Broader market under pressure

The midcap and smallcap indices also traded lower, with declines outpacing the benchmarks. Selling was particularly intense in public sector banks and realty stocks, which had rallied sharply in the previous session.

Foreign institutional investors remained net sellers in the cash market, while domestic institutions provided some support. The rupee weakened against the US dollar, adding to the pressure on import-heavy sectors.

What's driving the decline

Global markets turned risk-off overnight after US inflation data came in hotter than expected, raising concerns about prolonged high interest rates. Asian peers, including Japan's Nikkei and China's Shanghai Composite, also traded lower.

Domestically, traders were cautious ahead of key economic data releases due later in the day. The market's focus will also be on quarterly earnings from a few large-cap companies scheduled to report over the weekend.

Outlook for the session

Analysts expect the market to remain volatile in the near term, with support for the Nifty seen around the 24,400-24,450 zone. A recovery above 24,700 could attract fresh buying, they said.

Investors are advised to monitor global cues and stock-specific developments closely. The coming sessions will likely hinge on inflation data and central bank commentary from major economies.

For now, the market awaits clarity on whether the current dip is a healthy correction or the start of a deeper slide. Trading volumes are expected to pick up as the day progresses.
