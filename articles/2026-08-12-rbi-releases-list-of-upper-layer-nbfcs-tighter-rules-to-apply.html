RBI releases list of upper-layer NBFCs; tighter rules to apply

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has identified NBFCs in the upper layer based on a set of criteria.
  • These entities will be subject to enhanced regulatory requirements.
  • The list is part of the central bank's scale-based regulation framework.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has released the much-anticipated list of non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) categorised under the upper layer. This is part of the central bank's scale-based regulation framework, designed to tighten oversight of larger shadow banks.

The list, published on Thursday, identifies NBFCs that meet the threshold for systemic importance. These entities will now face stricter norms on capital adequacy, governance, and risk management.

Who is on the list?

The RBI has not disclosed the exact number of NBFCs in the upper layer. However, the list is expected to include major players in the lending and infrastructure finance space. The central bank has said that the identification is based on factors such as asset size, public interface, and interconnectedness with the financial system.

Under the scale-based regulation, NBFCs are divided into four layers: base, middle, upper, and top. The upper layer is reserved for those that pose significant risk to the system but do not yet warrant the top-tier classification.

What changes for these firms?

Once classified as upper layer, NBFCs will have to comply with additional requirements. These include a higher capital buffer, better disclosure standards, and more rigorous board oversight. The RBI has also mandated that these firms adopt a more conservative approach to risk-taking.

The central bank has given these NBFCs time to adjust to the new rules. The transition is expected to be phased, with full compliance required by a specified date.

Why this matters

The move is part of the RBI's broader effort to prevent the kind of stress that hit the NBFC sector in the past. By identifying systemically important firms, the regulator aims to catch vulnerabilities early and ensure better resilience.

Industry observers note that the list is not static. The RBI will review the classification periodically, meaning companies can move between layers based on their evolving risk profile. This keeps the framework dynamic and responsive to changing conditions.

For now, the immediate focus is on the listed NBFCs, which must now prepare for a more demanding regulatory environment. Their next steps will be closely watched by investors and market participants.

The RBI has not yet announced the exact timeline for the next review or the specific penalties for non-compliance. Market watchers will be tracking how the upper-layer NBFCs adapt to the stricter norms in the coming quarters.
