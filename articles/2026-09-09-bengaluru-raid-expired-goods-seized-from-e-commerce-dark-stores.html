Bengaluru Raid: Expired Goods Seized from E-Commerce Dark Stores

  • The crackdown highlights gaps in inventory management and quality control at quick-commerce and online grocery platforms.

What the Bengaluru Raid Uncovered

In a coordinated operation on September 9, 2026, enforcement officials in Bengaluru raided several e-commerce fulfilment centres and dark stores across the city, seizing a significant quantity of expired, nearly expired, and mislabelled products. The drive, which targeted major online grocery and quick-commerce platforms, was initiated after multiple consumer complaints regarding the delivery of substandard goods.

Officials from the Department of Legal Metrology and the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) conducted simultaneous inspections at storage facilities in Whitefield, HSR Layout, and Rajajinagar. Seized items included packaged food, dairy products, baby care items, and over-the-counter medicines, all found to have either crossed their expiry dates or bearing incorrect manufacturing and best-before labels.

According to an official statement, at least 12 dark stores and three central warehouses were found violating norms. The seized goods, valued at an estimated ₹14 lakh, were destroyed on-site to prevent any chance of re-entry into the supply chain. The platforms involved have been issued show-cause notices, and further investigation is underway.

Why This Crackdown Matters for Online Shoppers

This raid exposes a worrying trend: as quick-commerce platforms rush to fulfil orders in 10-20 minutes, inventory rotation and quality checks are being compromised. Dark stores—small, neighbourhood-based fulfilment centres—often operate with minimal staff and automated systems, making it easier for expiring stock to slip through undetected.

For consumers, the implications are serious. Consuming expired food or medicine can lead to food poisoning, allergic reactions, or reduced efficacy of drugs. Mislabelled products, particularly those with incorrect allergen information, pose an even greater risk for individuals with specific dietary restrictions or health conditions.

The enforcement action also signals a regulatory shift. State authorities are now actively scrutinising e-commerce supply chains, not just physical retail stores. This means platforms can no longer rely on third-party sellers to ensure compliance; they will be held accountable for the entire chain of custody.

Key Insights for Consumers and Platforms

  • Consumer vigilance is essential: Always check the expiry date and packaging integrity before accepting delivery. If the product is already near expiry (less than 30 days), consider rejecting it, especially for perishables.
  • Platforms must invest in automated expiry tracking: Manual checks are unreliable. RFID tags and AI-based inventory systems can flag expiring batches in real time, preventing such lapses.
  • Legal recourse is available: Under the Legal Metrology Act and FSSAI regulations, consumers can file complaints against platforms selling expired goods. The raid is a reminder that regulators are willing to act.

How to Protect Yourself When Ordering Online

First, always purchase from reputable platforms that have a transparent return and refund policy. Second, upon delivery, inspect the product's MRP and manufacturing/expiry date—do not rely solely on the app's product page, which may show outdated information.

Third, if you receive an expired or damaged product, document it with photos and immediately report it to the platform's customer support. If the issue is not resolved, escalate to the legal metrology department or FSSAI's helpline. The Bengaluru raid proves that regulators are listening—so don't hesitate to raise your voice.
