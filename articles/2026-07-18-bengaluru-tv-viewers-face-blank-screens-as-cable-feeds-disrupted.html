Bengaluru TV Viewers Face Blank Screens as Cable Feeds Disrupted

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cable operators in Bengaluru cut TV feeds due to a payment dispute with broadcasters.
  • Viewers across the city experienced blank screens for several hours on Friday evening.
  • The dispute revolves around carriage fees and new pricing regulations.

Thousands of television sets across Bengaluru went blank on Friday evening, leaving viewers frustrated as cable operators pulled the plug on feeds over a payment dispute with broadcasters. The disruption, which began around 7 pm, affected several major cable networks in the city, cutting off access to popular channels.

Blank Screens Across the City

Residents in areas like Koramangala, Indiranagar, Whitefield, and Jayanagar reported losing signal mid-programme. Many took to social media to vent their anger, with some saying they were left staring at a message that read 'Will be right back...after a short break'.

For families settling in for the evening, the blackout was sudden and unexplained. One viewer in Basavanagudi said the screen went dark during a prime-time news bulletin, with no prior warning from the cable operator.

Payment Dispute at the Heart of the Blackout

Industry sources told Bangalore Mirror that the disruption stems from a disagreement between cable operators and broadcasters over carriage fees and the implementation of the new tariff order. Cable operators claim broadcasters have not adhered to the revised pricing framework, while broadcasters insist on payment for channel bundles.

The dispute has been brewing for weeks. On Friday, operators decided to switch off feeds as a pressure tactic. A cable operator in Malleswaram, speaking on condition of anonymity, said: "We are not getting a fair deal. The broadcasters are charging us more than what the new rules allow. We had no choice but to stop the signal."

Viewers Left in the Dark

For many Bengalureans, the blackout hit at an inconvenient time. Office-goers returning home found their TV sets useless. In some households, children missed their favourite cartoons, while others could not watch the evening news or cricket highlights.

Consumer rights groups have criticised both sides for dragging viewers into the fight. "This is a classic case of corporate disputes hurting the common man," said a local activist. "People pay their cable bills regularly. They deserve uninterrupted service."

Authorities have not yet intervened. The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) has the power to mediate such disputes, but no official statement has been issued so far. The cable operators' association is expected to meet broadcasters over the weekend to resolve the deadlock.

Meanwhile, some residents have switched to streaming services or Doordarshan's free-to-air channels. But for many, especially the elderly and those without internet access, the blank screen remains a source of inconvenience and confusion.

The blackout is the latest in a series of similar disruptions across Indian cities. In 2023, a similar dispute in Mumbai left thousands without TV for two days. Industry watchers say such standoffs are becoming more frequent as the broadcasting business model faces strain from digital competition and regulatory changes.

What happens next depends on the weekend talks. If no agreement is reached, Bengaluru could see a prolonged blackout. Viewers, for now, can only wait — and hope the message on their screens is not a permanent goodbye.
