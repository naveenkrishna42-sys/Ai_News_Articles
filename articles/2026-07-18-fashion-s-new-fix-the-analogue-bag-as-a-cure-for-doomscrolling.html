Fashion’s new fix: The analogue bag as a cure for doomscrolling

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • Designers are creating bags with tactile features like zippers, pockets, and hardware to engage users physically.
  • The trend reflects a broader cultural shift away from constant screen time and toward mindful, hands-on activities.
  • Analogue bags are becoming status symbols for those seeking authenticity and a break from digital saturation.

In an era defined by infinite scrolling and notification overload, a quiet rebellion is taking shape—not in boardrooms or on protest marches, but in handbags. Designers are increasingly crafting what industry insiders are calling ‘analogue bags’: accessories designed to be touched, fidgeted with, and physically engaged with, offering a tactile antidote to the digital deluge.

The rise of the fidget-friendly bag

These aren’t your standard totes. Analogue bags come loaded with zippers, buckles, compartments, and hardware that invite the wearer to interact. Think of them as adult fidget spinners, but far more stylish. The idea is simple: instead of reaching for your phone during a idle moment, you reach for your bag.

For a generation accustomed to doomscrolling through news feeds, this shift is deliberate. Brands are tapping into a growing desire for mindfulness and sensory satisfaction. The bag becomes a tool for grounding, a physical object that demands attention without demanding data.

Why now? A cultural backlash against screens

The timing is no accident. Mental health experts have long warned about the effects of constant screen exposure, and consumers are beginning to listen. The analogue bag trend dovetails with a broader push for ‘slow living’—from vinyl records to printed books to board games.

Fashion, always a mirror of cultural anxieties, is now offering a solution. These bags aren’t just accessories; they’re statements. Carrying one signals a choice: to be present, to touch something real, to disconnect from the digital hive mind. It’s a status symbol for the screen-weary.

Craftsmanship over connectivity

Luxury labels are leaning into the trend, producing bags that emphasize intricate detailing and hand-finishing. The appeal lies in the contrast to the mass-produced, algorithm-driven goods that dominate fast fashion. Every zipper pull, every leather tab is an invitation to pause.

Emerging designers are also jumping in, often using repurposed materials and artisanal techniques. The result is a market where the value isn’t just in the brand name, but in the experience of using the bag. It’s a return to craft—and a rejection of the virtual.

From doomscrolling to do-it-yourself

Social media, ironically, has fueled the trend. Videos of people unzipping compartments, clicking buckles, and arranging items in their analogue bags have gone viral on platforms like Instagram and TikTok. The hashtag #analoguebag has amassed millions of views.

But there’s a deeper psychology at play. Doomscrolling is compulsive, passive consumption. The analogue bag offers agency. It requires active engagement—opening, closing, sorting, touching. For many, it’s a small but meaningful reclamation of control in a world that often feels out of control.

What the future holds

As the trend gains momentum, analysts predict it will evolve beyond handbags into other fashion categories—jackets with elaborate zipper systems, shoes with adjustable straps, jewellery that doubles as tactile objects. The question is whether this is a passing fad or a permanent shift in how we relate to what we wear.

For now, the analogue bag is carving out a niche: part accessory, part therapy tool. It’s a reminder that sometimes the best way to disconnect is to reach out and touch something real. The next few seasons will reveal whether this quiet rebellion has staying power—or if the digital world will find a way to reel us back in.
