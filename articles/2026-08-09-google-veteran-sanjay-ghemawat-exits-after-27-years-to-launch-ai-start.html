Google veteran Sanjay Ghemawat exits after 27 years to launch AI startup

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sanjay Ghemawat, a 27-year Google veteran, has resigned to start an AI-focused company.
  • His exit follows a wave of top AI researchers leaving Google to launch their own ventures.
  • CEO Sundar Pichai praised Ghemawat as 'incomparable' in an internal memo.
  • The new startup will focus on AI-driven discovery, though details remain scarce.

A 27-year run comes to an end

Sanjay Ghemawat, one of Google's most distinguished engineers, has stepped down after a 27-year stint at the company. His next move? Building an AI-driven discovery startup, a venture that signals yet another high-profile departure from the tech giant's corridors.

Ghemawat's name may not ring bells outside engineering circles, but within Google, he is a legend. He was part of the early team that shaped the company's infrastructure, working alongside CEO Sundar Pichai and other luminaries. His departure, confirmed this week, has sparked a mix of nostalgia and curiosity across the industry.

What we know about the new venture

Details about the startup remain under wraps. What is clear is that it will focus on AI-driven discovery, a broad term that could span anything from personalised content recommendations to novel search mechanisms. Ghemawat has not yet commented publicly on the roadmap, and the company has not announced funding or a launch timeline.

The move places him in a growing cohort of Google alumni who have left to chase AI opportunities. In recent months, at least four other top researchers from Google's AI ranks have reportedly formed their own startup, according to The New York Times and WIRED. This exodus has raised questions about how Google plans to retain its brightest minds as the AI race intensifies.

Pichai's emotional farewell

In an internal memo, Pichai reportedly called Ghemawat 'incomparable', a rare tribute from a CEO known for measured language. The note also revived a classic insider joke about Jeff Dean, another Google legend and long-time collaborator of Ghemawat. The jest, which has circulated among Googlers for years, pokes fun at Dean's outsized reputation. Its reappearance in the wake of Ghemawat's exit suggests the moment carries emotional weight within the company.

Colleagues describe Ghemawat as a quiet force behind many of Google's core systems. His work on infrastructure and distributed computing helped the company scale from a scrappy search engine to a global tech behemoth. For many, his exit feels like the closing of an era.

Why this matters for Google

Ghemawat's departure is not just a sentimental loss. It highlights a broader trend: the AI talent market is fiercely competitive, and Google is no longer the default destination for top researchers. The launch of new startups by its alumni could also mean more competition in the AI space, both for talent and for cutting-edge innovation.

Google, for its part, has been pushing hard on its AI momentum, as its own blog post titled 'The next chapter of our AI momentum' suggests. The company continues to invest heavily in AI research and products, but it faces an uphill battle to keep its best people from leaving.

The road ahead

For Ghemawat, the new venture is a chance to shape AI discovery on his own terms. For Google, it is a reminder that even the most loyal engineers can be lured by the promise of building something new.

As the startup takes shape, observers will watch closely for details on its product and team. What is certain is that Ghemawat's next act will draw attention, both for his pedigree and for the growing wave of AI entrepreneurship among Google's alumni.

In the coming months, expect more announcements from the startup, and perhaps more exits from Google's ranks. The AI talent war shows no signs of cooling, and the industry is all the more dynamic for it.
