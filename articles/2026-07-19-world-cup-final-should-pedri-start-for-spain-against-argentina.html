World Cup Final: Should Pedri Start for Spain Against Argentina?

  • Pedri, Gavi, and Nico Williams are reportedly benched for the 2026 World Cup final.
  • Questions arise over Spain's tactical approach against Lionel Messi's Argentina.

The Selection Dilemma

As Spain prepares to face Argentina in the FIFA World Cup 2026 final in New York, a major talking point has emerged. Head coach Luis de la Fuente appears to have made a bold call on his starting eleven. Reports suggest that Barcelona midfielder Pedri, along with fellow youngsters Gavi and Nico Williams, will begin the match on the bench.

The decision has sparked intense debate among fans and pundits. Pedri, 23, has been a creative heartbeat for Spain throughout the tournament. His ability to control tempo and unlock defences seemed tailor-made for a final against Argentina.

Tactical Gamble or Prudent Move?

Why would de la Fuente leave out such a pivotal player? The answer likely lies in the opposition. Argentina, led by Lionel Messi, thrives on quick transitions and exploiting space. Spain may opt for a more physically robust midfield to counter that threat.

Sources suggest the coach is prioritising defensive solidity and directness. This could mean starting players like Fabián Ruiz or Mikel Merino, who offer more ball-winning ability. The risk, however, is losing the creative spark that Pedri provides against a disciplined Argentine backline.

Fan Reaction and Stakes

The news has not sat well with many supporters. Social media is flooded with questions: “Why isn’t Pedri playing?” and “Why are Gavi and Nico Williams benched?” The sentiment is that Spain’s best football in the tournament has come when these young stars dictate play.

This is a World Cup final. The stakes could not be higher. Dropping a player of Pedri’s calibre is a gamble that could define de la Fuente’s legacy. If Spain struggles to create chances, the decision will face heavy scrutiny.

What the Coach Might Be Thinking

De la Fuente may be looking at Argentina’s midfield pressure. Enzo Fernández and Rodrigo De Paul are relentless in their pressing. Pedri, while brilliant, can be targeted physically. The coach might be saving him as a second-half weapon, when legs tire and space opens up.

It is a high-risk, high-reward strategy. Argentina will not make it easy, and Spain cannot afford a slow start. The team that takes the field at kick-off will set the tone for the entire match.

All eyes will be on the official team sheet when it is released. Whether Pedri starts or not, his potential introduction from the bench could be the decisive moment. For now, the debate rages on — does he deserve to start the biggest game of his career?
