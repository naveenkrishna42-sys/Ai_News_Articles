Rahul Gandhi attacks Amit Shah over pellet firing at CJP march as Parliament resumes

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rahul Gandhi attacked Amit Shah in Lok Sabha over pellet firing at CJP protest march
  • Opposition to seek Amit Shah's resignation over police action against NEET protesters
  • Parliament session resumes with government planning a bill, NEET row remains flashpoint

The resumption of the Parliament session on Monday saw an immediate clash between the ruling dispensation and the opposition, with Congress leader Rahul Gandhi launching a sharp attack on Union Home Minister Amit Shah over the alleged use of pellets against protesters at a recent CJP march.

Raising the issue in the Lok Sabha, Gandhi accused the government of turning the security forces into 'pellet guns' to suppress dissent. The reference was to the anti-NEET protests that had escalated in the capital last week, culminating in a march organised by the Combined Jammu and Kashmir Forum (CJP).

Opposition demands Shah's resignation

Leaders from the CPI(M) and other opposition parties have demanded Amit Shah's resignation over the police action. CPI(M) general secretary Sitaram Yechury said the Home Minister's response to the protests was 'unacceptable in a democracy'.

The opposition is expected to bring a no-confidence motion against the government if it does not act on the NEET row and the alleged use of force against students and activists. Sources within the opposition said they would not let the House function until the government agrees to a debate on the issue.

Govt plans a bill move amid chaos

Meanwhile, the government is planning to introduce a new bill in the Lok Sabha this week. The exact contours of the legislation remain unclear, but sources indicate it could be related to higher education reforms or national security. The move is seen as an attempt to seize the narrative away from the NEET controversy.

The ruling BJP has accused the opposition of disrupting Parliament for political gain. Union Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, who was at the centre of the NEET row, has been removed from his post, but the opposition says this is not enough.

From Jantar Mantar to Parliament

The NEET protest, which began at Jantar Mantar with thousands of students demanding a re-examination, has now moved inside Parliament. The Congress and its allies have vowed to corner the government on the issue. Rahul Gandhi said in the House that the first demand of the opposition is the removal of the 'corrupt' Education Minister, even though Pradhan has already stepped down.

The opposition is demanding a full parliamentary committee probe into the NEET paper leak, and compensation for affected students. Government officials have not yet confirmed whether they will agree to these demands.

The House witnessed repeated adjournments as sloganeering from both sides made it impossible to transact business. The Speaker has appealed for calm, but with both sides dug in, a quick resolution appears unlikely.

The Parliament session is scheduled to run for three weeks, but if the current mood persists, legislative business could be severely hampered. The government's bill, if introduced, will test the opposition's unity and resolve.
