India Simplifies Visas: 13 Categories Cut to 9, Forms 70% Leaner

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Ministry of Home Affairs has consolidated 13 visa categories into 9 broader classifications to cut confusion and processing time.
  • Application form fields have been reduced by 65–70%, eliminating repeated data entry across tourism, business and medical visa streams.
  • The reform is aimed at attracting more foreign visitors by making India's entry process faster and simpler to navigate.

India has overhauled its visa framework to draw more foreign visitors, cutting the number of visa categories from 13 to 9 and trimming application form fields by as much as 70%, according to the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA). The reform, reported across Hindustan Times, The Hindu, NDTV, Telegraph India and Bloomberg, targets tourism, business and medical travel — segments long slowed by paperwork.

What Changed in India's Visa Framework

The MHA has consolidated 13 existing visa categories into 9 broader classifications, folding overlapping sub-types into simpler buckets. Alongside the reduction, the online application form has been cut by 65–70%, removing redundant fields that previously forced applicants to re-enter the same personal and travel details multiple times.

Why Fewer Categories Matter for Travellers

For years, applicants struggled to identify the correct visa type, with narrow sub-categories creating confusion at the application stage and at immigration counters. Collapsing 13 categories into 9 reduces misclassification, a common cause of rejected or delayed applications.

Faster, Cleaner Applications

India already operates one of the world's largest e-Visa programmes, and the leaner form builds on that digital backbone. Fewer fields mean shorter completion times, fewer errors, and quicker verification for consular officers — a direct benefit for last-minute business flyers and tourists.

Boost to Tourism and Medical Travel

Medical tourism is a high-value segment for India, drawing patients from Africa, South Asia and the Gulf for treatment at private hospital chains. Simplifying the medical and medical-attendant visa streams lowers friction for patients travelling with caregivers, supporting hospitals and the wider healthcare economy.

Key Highlights: Pros and Trade-Offs

  • Pro — Less paperwork: A 65–70% shorter form cuts data-entry errors and speeds up submissions.
  • Pro — Clearer choices: Nine categories are easier to understand than thirteen overlapping ones.
  • Pro — Economic upside: Smoother entry supports tourism arrivals, business travel and medical value travel.
  • Con — Transition confusion: Applicants may briefly be unsure which new bucket their purpose of travel falls under.
  • Con — Detail pending: Travellers should verify the exact new category names on the official portal before applying.

What Applicants Should Do Next

Travellers planning a trip should confirm their eligibility under the revised categories directly on the official Indian e-Visa portal rather than relying on older guides. Frequent visitors and those applying for medical or business travel stand to gain the most from the shorter forms, while first-time applicants should double-check that their purpose of visit maps correctly to one of the nine consolidated categories.

The reform signals a clear policy direction: prioritise ease of entry to compete for global tourists and business travellers. As the new structure rolls out, the practical test will be whether faster forms translate into measurably quicker approvals at scale.
