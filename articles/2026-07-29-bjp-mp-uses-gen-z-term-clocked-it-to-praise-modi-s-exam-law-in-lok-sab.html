BJP MP Uses Gen-Z Term 'Clocked It' to Praise Modi's Exam Law in Lok Sabha

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP MP Tejasvi Surya said 'Modi clocked it' in Lok Sabha while supporting the anti-paper leak bill
  • The bill was passed by voice vote amid opposition protests over Rahul Gandhi's remarks on Amit Shah
  • Vande Mataram Bill was also passed in Rajya Sabha during the same session

Gen-Z Slang Echoes in Parliament

A new anti-paper leak bill took centre stage in the Lok Sabha on Tuesday, but it was a single phrase from a BJP MP that grabbed attention. Tejasvi Surya, the MP from Bangalore South, stood up and said, 'PM Modi clocked it.'

The phrase, popular among Gen-Z and social media users, means someone has understood a situation perfectly or done something well. Surya used it to praise the government's quick response to repeated examination paper leaks across the country.

Opposition MPs were not amused. They accused the ruling party of trivialising a serious issue. The debate quickly turned heated, with members trading charges over who was responsible for the leaks in the first place.

The Paper Leak Bill and the Backdrop

The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Bill, 2026, aims to crack down on organised gangs that leak question papers. It proposes strict punishment, including up to 10 years in jail and hefty fines for those found guilty.

The bill was passed by voice vote in the Lok Sabha, but not without drama. Congress MP Rahul Gandhi reportedly blamed Home Minister Amit Shah for the firing on students during previous protests over paper leaks. This led to a fierce exchange between the treasury and opposition benches.

Sources said the Opposition demanded a detailed discussion on the failure of existing systems. However, the government pushed for a swift passage, citing the urgent need to protect student interests.

Vande Mataram Bill Also Passed

In the Rajya Sabha, the Vande Mataram Bill was passed during the same session. The bill seeks to make the singing of the national song mandatory in schools and government offices once a week. Details of the bill's provisions are still emerging.

The phrase 'waste-guna-huiya'—another Gen-Z term meaning something is useless—was also heard in the chamber during the debate. MPs across party lines used the informal language, reflecting a changing parliamentary culture.

Opposition leaders later said the use of such terms showed a lack of seriousness. But ruling party MPs argued that the Prime Minister's grasp of contemporary issues, including youth concerns, was commendable.

What Happens Next

The anti-paper leak bill will now go to the Rajya Sabha for consideration. The government hopes to pass it before the end of the current session. Meanwhile, the debate over student safety and exam integrity is unlikely to cool down anytime soon.
