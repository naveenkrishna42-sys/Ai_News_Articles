Chennai job fair for persons with disability sees strong turnout

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Job fair held in Chennai for persons with disability
  • Event connects jobseekers with potential employers
  • Focus on inclusive hiring and accessibility

Inclusive hiring initiative

Chennai witnessed a dedicated job fair for persons with disability on Thursday, organised to bridge the gap between talented jobseekers and employers committed to inclusive hiring. The event, held in the city, drew a significant number of participants eager to explore career opportunities in a supportive environment.

Organisers said the fair was designed to address the specific challenges faced by persons with disability in the job market. By bringing together multiple companies under one roof, the initiative aimed to simplify the application process and foster direct interaction between candidates and recruiters.

Employers and opportunities

Several companies from diverse sectors, including IT, manufacturing, and services, set up stalls at the venue. Representatives from these firms were on hand to discuss job roles, company policies, and the accommodations available for employees with disabilities.

The response from employers was encouraging, with many expressing a willingness to adapt their recruitment processes to be more accessible. This included providing sign language interpreters and ensuring the venue was fully wheelchair-accessible, a detail that did not go unnoticed by attendees.

Jobseekers' perspective

Candidates who attended the fair shared mixed feelings of hope and pragmatism. Many appreciated the effort to create a dedicated space for them, noting that such events reduce the anxiety often associated with traditional job interviews. Others, however, pointed out that the real challenge lies in sustaining these opportunities beyond the fair.

One attendee, who preferred to remain anonymous, said the fair was a positive step but stressed the need for more consistent follow-up from employers. "It is good to see so many companies here, but we hope they are genuinely serious about hiring us," the individual told The Hindu.

Accessibility and support

Volunteers and staff were deployed throughout the venue to assist candidates with navigation and documentation. Resource materials in Braille and large print were also made available, reflecting a thoughtful approach to inclusivity.

Organisers noted that the fair was not just about immediate job placements but also about sensitising employers to the capabilities of persons with disability. Workshops on resume writing and interview skills were conducted on the sidelines, adding value to the experience.

Looking ahead

While the fair concluded successfully, the organisers have not yet announced the total number of job offers made. Follow-up sessions are expected to be scheduled to track the progress of candidates who interacted with employers.

As inclusive hiring gains momentum in Chennai, the focus will be on converting such events into long-term employment pathways. Observers will be watching to see whether more companies adopt accessible recruitment practices throughout the year.
