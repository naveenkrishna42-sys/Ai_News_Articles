UFBU warns of indefinite strike over PLI, banking issues

📅 2026-08-23 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • UFBU signals indefinite strike over PLI and banking issues
  • Punjab & Sind Bank paid PLI to senior executives, sparking UFBU's anger
  • UFBU-IBA meeting discussed PLI for Scale IV and above, five-day banking and other issues

Union of Financial Bank Unions calls for indefinite strike

India's Union of Financial Bank Unions (UFBU) has announced an indefinite strike, signaling a major disruption in the banking sector. The union's decision comes after Punjab & Sind Bank, a prominent public sector bank, paid Performance Linked Incentives (PLI) to its senior executives, a move that UFBU claims is a deliberate provocation.

PLI payments and UFBU's response

Punjab & Sind Bank recently paid PLI to its senior executives, a practice that UFBU has been opposing. The union argues that this payment is a clear indication of the bank's favoritism towards its top management, which undermines the principles of meritocracy and transparency.

In response to this, UFBU has called for agitation and strike action. The union's official statement warned that the revised PLI scheme for senior bank officers is a direct challenge to the bank employees' rights and interests.

UFBU-IBA meeting discusses PLI and banking issues

A bipartite meeting between UFBU and the Indian Banks' Association (IBA) was held to discuss the PLI scheme and other banking issues. The meeting focused on the PLI for Scale IV and above officers, as well as the five-day banking and other pending issues.

The meeting was expected to provide a platform for both parties to express their concerns and find a mutually acceptable solution. However, the outcome of the meeting is yet to be announced, leaving the banking sector in a state of uncertainty.

What's next?

The banking sector is bracing for a potential indefinite strike by UFBU, which could have significant implications for the economy. The union's decision to go on strike is a clear indication of its dissatisfaction with the current PLI scheme and other banking issues. It remains to be seen how the IBA and the government will respond to UFBU's demands and whether they will be able to find a solution that satisfies both parties.
