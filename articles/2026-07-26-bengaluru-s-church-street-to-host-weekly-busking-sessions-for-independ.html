Bengaluru's Church Street to host weekly busking sessions for independent musicians

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Weekly busking sessions to be held on Church Street in Bengaluru
  • Platform aims to support independent musicians and local talent
  • Initiative part of efforts to revive street culture and public spaces

Bengaluru's Church Street, already a hotspot for cafes and nightlife, is set to turn into a stage for independent musicians. The street will host weekly busking sessions, offering a platform for local artists to perform live in public.

The initiative, reported by The Hindu, aims to revive street culture and give independent musicians a space to showcase their talent. Sessions are expected to take place on designated days, with organisers handling logistics like sound systems and permissions.

What the sessions entail

Each busking session will feature performances by independent musicians, ranging from solo acts to small bands. The focus is on genres like indie, folk, and acoustic music.

Organisers have not yet confirmed the exact schedule or the selection process for artists. But the plan is to keep it open and inclusive, encouraging both emerging and established musicians to participate.

Supporting local talent

Bengaluru has a thriving independent music scene, but venues for live performances are limited. Many artists struggle to find affordable spaces to play.

This initiative could change that. By using a public street, it removes entry barriers and lets musicians connect directly with audiences. It also adds a new layer to Church Street's already vibrant character.

Reviving street culture

Busking has a long history in cities worldwide, from London's tube stations to New York's subway platforms. In India, street performances have been rare due to regulatory hurdles and lack of infrastructure.

Bengaluru's move could set a precedent. If successful, it may encourage other cities to adopt similar models, turning public spaces into cultural hubs.

The civic authorities are reportedly on board. They see this as a way to make streets safer and more lively after business hours.

What musicians think

Several independent musicians have expressed interest. Many see it as a chance to build an audience without the pressure of a formal gig.

"It's hard to get booked in clubs if you don't have a following," one musician told The Hindu, speaking on condition of anonymity. "This is a low-stakes way to play and get noticed."

Others caution that sound levels and crowd management will need careful planning. Church Street can get crowded, and balancing noise with nearby businesses will be a challenge.

Looking ahead

The first session is expected to begin soon, though no date has been announced. Organisers are working on finalising the weekly schedule.

If the pilot works, the model could expand to other streets in Bengaluru. For now, independent musicians have a new stage to call their own, right in the heart of the city.
