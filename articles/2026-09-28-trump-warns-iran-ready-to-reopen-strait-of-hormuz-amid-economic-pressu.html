Trump Warns Iran Ready to Reopen Strait of Hormuz Amid Economic Pressure

Overview of Trump’s Hormuz Claim

Former U.S. President Donald Trump asserted that Iran is prepared to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, quoting Iranian officials as saying “They have no money coming in, dying.” The comment followed Trump’s rejection of a recent Iranian peace proposal, positioning the strait’s status as a bargaining chip in broader negotiations over sanctions and regional security.

Historical Context: Hormuz Tensions Since 2019

In mid‑2019, Iran’s seizure of a British‑flagged tanker and subsequent threats to close the strait pushed Brent prices above $70 per barrel and lifted VLCC spot rates by roughly 20%. The episode ended after diplomatic back‑channels secured a de‑escalation, but it demonstrated how quickly risk premiums can spike when the waterway’s safety is questioned.

Economic Ripple Effects on Global Oil Markets

The Strait of Hormuz channels roughly 20‑30 million barrels of oil per day, about a third of seaborne crude exports. Any credible threat of closure or reopening directly influences benchmark prices such as Brent and WTI. Analysts estimate that a temporary blockage could push Brent prices up by $5‑$8 per barrel, while a swift reopening tends to relieve premiums by $2‑$4.

Shipping, Freight and Insurance Metrics

Maritime freight rates for Very Large Crude Carriers (VLCCs) traversing the Gulf typically rise 15‑25 % during heightened tension periods. War risk insurance premiums can jump from 0.05 % of vessel value to 0.20 % or more when the strait is perceived as unsafe. Conversely, a confirmed reopening often sees premiums revert to baseline within 48‑72 hours.
