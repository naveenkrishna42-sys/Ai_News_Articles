Rs 1,000-cr civic works stalled in Bengaluru as costs spike

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Civic works worth Rs 1,000 crore are stalled in Bengaluru due to a sharp rise in construction costs.
  • The spike in prices of steel, cement, and other materials has made project estimates untenable.
  • Officials are yet to confirm revised timelines or additional funding for the affected works.

Bengaluru's ambitious civic improvement drive has hit a roadblock. Works worth Rs 1,000 crore across the city lie stalled as construction costs have climbed steeply, leaving contractors and civic authorities at an impasse. The projects, which include road relaying, stormwater drains, and footpath upgrades, were meant to ease the city's chronic infrastructure woes. Instead, they now stand as half-finished reminders of a budgeting crunch.

Cost escalation beyond estimates

The core issue is the sharp rise in prices of key materials—steel, cement, bitumen, and fuel—which has pushed project costs far beyond what was budgeted. Contractors, who had quoted rates based on earlier price levels, are now refusing to proceed at the old rates. They argue that continuing would mean incurring heavy losses, a stance that has effectively frozen work on multiple sites.

Civic officials acknowledge the standoff but have not yet offered a clear way out. "We are examining the revised estimates," a senior official said, requesting anonymity. "But any decision on additional funds will take time." The delay has already pushed several projects past their original deadlines.

Impact on key corridors

The stalled works are spread across Bengaluru's eight zones, with some of the most affected being major arterial roads and residential stretches. Residents in areas like Whitefield, Yelahanka, and Banashankari have reported open trenches and uneven surfaces that have been left unattended for weeks. For a city already struggling with monsoon flooding, the incomplete stormwater drains add to the risk of waterlogging.

Local traders, too, are feeling the pinch. Shop owners along stretches where footpath work has stopped say the disruption has hurt business, with no clear end in sight. "We were told the work would be done in three months. It's been six," said a shopkeeper on a busy commercial street, echoing a common complaint.

Contractors seek revised rates

Contractors' associations have formally approached the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) with requests to renegotiate rates. They point to official indices that show construction costs rising by double digits over the past year. Without a revision, they warn, many small and mid-sized contractors could be forced to abandon projects altogether.

However, any revision would require approval from the state government, a process that could take months. Meanwhile, the BBMP's own funds are stretched, with revenue collection falling short of targets. The civic body has not yet indicated whether it will seek additional budgetary support or re-tender the stalled works, both of which would further delay completion.

What lies ahead

The standoff is unlikely to resolve quickly. Officials have hinted at a possible interim solution—allowing contractors to claim a provisional cost adjustment—but no formal announcement has been made. In the meantime, the city's infrastructure deficit continues to grow, and citizens are left to navigate unfinished roads and blocked drains.

For now, all eyes are on the BBMP's next council meeting, where the revised estimates are expected to be discussed. Whether the civic body will find the funds to restart the works, or whether the projects will be shelved indefinitely, remains to be seen. One thing is certain: Bengaluru's wait for smoother roads is far from over.
