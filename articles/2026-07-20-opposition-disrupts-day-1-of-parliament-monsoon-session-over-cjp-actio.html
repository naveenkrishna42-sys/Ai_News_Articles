Opposition Disrupts Day 1 of Parliament Monsoon Session Over CJP Action, Paper Leak

📅 2026-07-20 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Opposition MPs disrupted proceedings in both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha on the first day of the Monsoon Session.
  • Protests are against the Centre's crackdown on the CJP protest and a recent examination paper leak.
  • Chairperson and Speaker adjourned the Houses multiple times amid the uproar.

Chaos on Opening Day

The Monsoon Session of Parliament began on a turbulent note on Monday, with Opposition members storming the Well of both Houses. The protests forced the Chair to adjourn proceedings within minutes of the day's start.

Members of the Congress, Trinamool Congress, and other Opposition parties raised slogans against the government. They were demanding a statement from the Prime Minister on two issues: the recent crackdown on the CJP (Communist Party of India [Marxist-Leninist]) protest and a massive state-level examination paper leak.

Two Issues, One Uproar

The CJP crackdown, which took place earlier this month, has drawn sharp criticism from Opposition ranks. They allege the government is using heavy-handed tactics to silence dissent. In Parliament, leaders called the action an assault on democratic rights.

Simultaneously, the paper leak controversy—which has affected lakhs of students in a key state recruitment exam—has erupted into a national issue. The Opposition has accused the state and central governments of failing to secure the examination process, demanding a CBI investigation and the resignation of the responsible minister.

House Adjourned Multiple Times

In the Lok Sabha, Speaker Om Birla repeatedly urged members to return to their seats, but the din persisted. He eventually adjourned the House till 12 noon. The Rajya Sabha saw a similar scene, with Chairman Jagdeep Dhankhar adjourning the Upper House after the Opposition refused to relent.

During the brief moments of order, the government's legislative agenda—which includes key economic bills and the supplementary demands for grants—remained unaddressed. Sources indicate the government is prepared to discuss the issues in a formal debate, but the Opposition has insisted on an immediate, unconditional statement from the top leadership.

Government's Stance

Union Parliamentary Affairs Minister Pralhad Joshi appealed to the Opposition to allow the House to function. He said the government is open to a discussion under the rules, but not under duress.

“The country wants Parliament to run. We are ready to discuss every issue, but there must be order first,” Joshi told reporters outside Parliament. He also dismissed the Opposition's demand for a PM statement on the CJP issue, calling it a law and order matter best left to the courts.

BJP floor leaders accused the Opposition of running away from a debate on the government's development agenda. They pointed to the recent drop in retail inflation and the uptick in GDP growth as achievements that deserve parliamentary attention.

What Next?

Both Houses have been adjourned for the day. The government and the Opposition are expected to hold a meeting of floor leaders on Tuesday morning to break the deadlock. If no consensus is reached, the disruption is likely to continue into the second day, putting the government's legislative calendar under immediate pressure.
