Trump warns Houthis, Iran of 'major military punishment' after Red Sea tanker attacks

📅 2026-07-24 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump warns of 'major military punishment' for Houthis and Iran over Red Sea attacks on Saudi vessels.
  • Brent crude oil prices surge past $100 per barrel, putting pressure on India's energy imports.
  • A Saudi oil tanker bound for India turns back as Houthis threaten key Red Sea shipping lanes.
  • UN envoy expresses alarm and calls for immediate talks to de-escalate the situation.

White House warns of retaliation

US President Donald Trump has threatened the Houthi movement and Iran with 'major military punishment' following a series of attacks on Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea. The warning came late Wednesday as tensions in the vital waterway escalated sharply.

Trump did not specify the timing or nature of the potential response but said the US would not tolerate disruptions to global shipping. The statement marks a significant hardening of the American position after weeks of increasing Houthi maritime operations.

Oil prices breach $100 mark

The attacks have directly impacted global energy markets. Brent crude surged past $100 per barrel on Thursday, a level not seen in months, as traders priced in the risk of a prolonged blockade. For India, which imports roughly 85% of its crude oil needs, the spike poses a direct economic challenge.

A significant portion of India's crude imports from the Middle East transits through the Red Sea and the Bab el-Mandeb strait. Any sustained disruption could force New Delhi to tap into strategic reserves or seek alternative, more expensive supplies from other regions.

Saudi tanker turns back

In a worrying sign for Indian energy security, a Saudi oil tanker carrying crude destined for India has turned back from the Red Sea. The vessel, whose identity has not been officially confirmed, reversed course after the Houthis threatened to choke what they called a 'key supply route' for enemy states.

The incident has sent shivers through the shipping industry. Insurance premiums for vessels transiting the area have skyrocketed, and several major shipping lines are now considering rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, a journey that adds weeks to delivery times.

UN calls for de-escalation

The United Nations special envoy for Yemen has expressed alarm at the escalating situation. In a statement, the envoy urged all parties to exercise maximum restraint and return to the negotiating table.

“The Secretary-General is deeply concerned by the recent attacks on commercial vessels in the Red Sea. He calls for an immediate de-escalation and urges all parties to prioritize dialogue over confrontation,” the statement read. The plea comes as efforts to broker a permanent ceasefire in Yemen remain stalled.

India's crude routes under stress

For India, the crisis could not have come at a worse time. The government is already grappling with inflationary pressures, and a sustained rise in crude prices will likely feed into higher fuel and transportation costs across the economy.

Officials in New Delhi are monitoring the situation closely. While the government has not issued a formal statement, sources indicate that contingency plans are being reviewed. The Indian Navy already maintains a presence in the Gulf of Aden, but a full-scale escort operation would be a major undertaking.

What to watch: All eyes are now on the US response. Any American military action against Houthi positions in Yemen or Iranian assets could further inflame the region, sending oil prices even higher and deepening the crisis for import-dependent nations like India.
