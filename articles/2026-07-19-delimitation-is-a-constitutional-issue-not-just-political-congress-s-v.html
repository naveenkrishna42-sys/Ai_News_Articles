Delimitation is a constitutional issue, not just political: Congress's Venugopal

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • K.C. Venugopal calls delimitation a Constitutional issue, not just an INDIA bloc matter.
  • Derek O'Brien demands all-party meet on delimitation and foreign bills.
  • Opposition lists paper leaks, Ram Temple donation 'theft', ethanol policy, Manipur for Parliament discussion.
  • Monsoon Session begins amid protests over NEET, Ram Temple row, and delimitation debate.

Venugopal warns delimitation is about Constitutional framework

Congress leader K.C. Venugopal has asserted that the upcoming delimitation exercise is not merely a political issue for the opposition INDIA bloc, but a matter that concerns the very foundations of India's Constitutional framework. His remarks come as Parliament prepares for a stormy Monsoon Session beginning Saturday.

Venugopal's statement signals that the Congress is treating the redrawing of Lok Sabha constituencies based on the 2026 Census as a core issue that could alter the political balance of the country. The party has been pushing for a delayed or re-evaluated delimitation process, arguing that southern states which have controlled population growth will lose parliamentary seats to the north.

Opposition demands all-party meet, multiple debates

Trinamool Congress MP Derek O'Brien has written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi, demanding a convening of an all-party meeting to discuss delimitation and two key foreign bills pending in Parliament. O'Brien's letter underscores the opposition's strategy to force a broader consultation on issues that have long-term implications for federalism.

The opposition's agenda for the session is packed. They have asked the government to schedule debates on the alleged theft of donations meant for the Ram temple in Ayodhya, the controversial ethanol procurement policy affecting sugar mills, and the ongoing crisis in Manipur. These demands were raised collectively by opposition parties during a meeting of the Business Advisory Committee.

Paper leaks and NEET protests fuel session tension

The Monsoon Session is also expected to be rocked by protests over the NEET-UG exam paper leak and the subsequent cancellation of results. Opposition parties have made it clear they will not allow the House to function unless the government agrees to a detailed discussion on the issue. Student groups have been protesting across the country, demanding a CBI investigation and accountability from the testing agency.

The Ram Temple donation controversy has added another layer of confrontation. The opposition claims that funds collected in the name of the temple were misappropriated, and they want a full parliamentary debate on the matter. The government has so far dismissed these allegations as baseless.

Manipur violence remains a flashpoint

The situation in Manipur, where ethnic violence has continued for over a year, remains a major flashpoint. Opposition MPs have insisted on a separate discussion on the restoration of peace and the rehabilitation of displaced people in the state. The government has maintained that law and order has improved, but opposition leaders say the ground reality tells a different story.

Parliamentary affairs minister Pralhad Joshi has urged all parties to cooperate for a productive session, but the opposition's list of demands suggests the monsoon session could be one of the most turbulent in recent years.

All eyes will be on how Speaker Om Birla handles the adjournment motions and whether the government is willing to concede floor time for these contentious debates. The session's outcome could set the tone for the winter session and the run-up to the next general election.
