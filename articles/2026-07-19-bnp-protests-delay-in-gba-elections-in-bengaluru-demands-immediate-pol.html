BNP Protests Delay in GBA Elections in Bengaluru, Demands Immediate Polls

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BNP leaders staged a protest in Bengaluru over delayed GBA elections.
  • The party has demanded that elections to the Garden Block Association be held without further delay.
  • Protestors accused the state government of deliberately stalling the electoral process.

Bengaluru, July 19: Leaders and workers of the Bengaluru Nagarika Parishad (BNP) staged a protest here on Sunday, demanding immediate elections to the Garden Block Association (GBA). The party has accused the state government of deliberately delaying the polls.

Protest at City Center

The demonstration took place near the city centre, with party members holding placards and shouting slogans. They demanded that the state election commission schedule the GBA elections without any further postponement.

BNP leaders said the delay was undermining local governance. They argued that residents of the garden blocks are being denied their democratic right to elect representatives.

Allegations of Stalling

Speaking at the protest, a senior BNP leader alleged that the ruling party is afraid of losing the elections. He claimed the government is using technical excuses to push back the polling date.

“The elections were due months ago. There is no valid reason for this delay. The government is deliberately stalling the process to avoid facing the people,” the leader said. He did not provide specific evidence for the claim.

Demand for Immediate Action

The party has submitted a memorandum to the district administration, urging them to intervene. The memorandum states that the delay is a violation of the GBA bye-laws and the principles of democratic representation.

Protestors warned that if the elections are not held within a month, they will intensify their agitation. The BNP has also threatened to approach the Karnataka High Court if the government does not respond.

Background of the Dispute

The GBA elections have been a contentious issue in Bengaluru for several months. The association manages common amenities and maintenance in several residential garden blocks across the city.

Previous attempts to hold elections were stalled due to disputes over voter lists and the boundaries of voting wards. The state government has cited these technical issues as reasons for the postponement. However, the BNP insists that these are solvable problems that should not derail the democratic process.

Officials from the state election commission have not yet commented on the protest or the timeline for the elections.

What happens next depends on the government's response to the BNP's memorandum. If no action is taken, the matter could move to the courts.
