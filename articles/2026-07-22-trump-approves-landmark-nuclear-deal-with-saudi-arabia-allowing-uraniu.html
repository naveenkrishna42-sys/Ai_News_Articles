Trump Approves Landmark Nuclear Deal With Saudi Arabia, Allowing Uranium Enrichment

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • President Trump approved a landmark nuclear deal with Saudi Arabia.
  • The agreement may permit Saudi Arabia to enrich uranium, a first for a non-NPT state.
  • Critics, including former Israeli leaders, warn of a potential nuclear arms race in the Middle East.
  • The deal is seen as a major diplomatic win for the Saudi government.
  • Details of the agreement, including safeguards, have not been fully disclosed.

Landmark Agreement Signed

US President Donald Trump has approved a landmark nuclear cooperation agreement with Saudi Arabia, according to reports from multiple international news outlets. The deal, described as a significant diplomatic victory for the kingdom, could allow Riyadh to enrich uranium for the first time.

The agreement was finalized late Tuesday, with the White House confirming the move. While the full text of the accord has not been made public, reports from The Wall Street Journal and The New York Times indicate it includes provisions for the kingdom to develop its own nuclear fuel cycle.

Enrichment Rights at the Core

The core of the deal centres on Saudi Arabia's right to enrich uranium. This capability is a major sticking point in non-proliferation circles, as enriched uranium can be used both for civilian nuclear power and, at higher levels of purity, for nuclear weapons.

The United States has historically insisted that countries receiving American nuclear technology forgo enrichment and reprocessing. The Saudi agreement appears to break from that precedent. The kingdom has long argued it needs the ability to produce its own fuel for energy security and economic diversification under its Vision 2030 plan.

Regional Concerns Mount

The deal has already drawn sharp criticism, particularly from Israel. Former Israeli prime ministers and security officials have publicly pilloried the pact, with one ex-leader calling it an invitation to an "insane nuclear arms race" in the Middle East.

Israeli officials have long viewed a nuclear-capable Saudi Arabia as a direct threat to their country's qualitative military edge. While Saudi Arabia has not officially sought a weapon, the enrichment capability puts it on a technological path that could be weaponised quickly if the kingdom ever chose to leave the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT).

Strategic Implications

For the Trump administration, the deal is a strategic move to deepen ties with Riyadh and counter Iranian influence. The administration has framed the agreement as a way to help Saudi Arabia meet its growing energy demands while maintaining US oversight and safety standards.

Critics, however, argue that by granting enrichment rights, the US is undermining decades of non-proliferation policy. The move could also pressure other nations in the region, such as the United Arab Emirates and Turkey, to demand similar terms, potentially triggering a cascade of nuclear programmes.

What Comes Next

Congress now has the opportunity to review the deal. Lawmakers from both parties have expressed concerns about the lack of a "gold standard" agreement that would require Saudi Arabia to forgo enrichment entirely.

Officials in Riyadh have not yet commented on the specifics. The coming weeks will be critical as the details of the safeguards and inspection regimes are debated in Washington and capitals across the Middle East.
