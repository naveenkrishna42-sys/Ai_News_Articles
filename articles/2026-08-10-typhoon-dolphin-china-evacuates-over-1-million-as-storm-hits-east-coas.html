Typhoon Dolphin: China evacuates over 1 million as storm hits east coast

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over one million people evacuated in eastern China ahead of Typhoon Dolphin's landfall in Zhejiang.
  • Record rainfall dumped on Shanghai, raising concerns for crops and urban flooding.
  • China issued a red alert, the highest weather warning, as the storm hit the coast.

Massive evacuation as Typhoon Dolphin makes landfall

China has evacuated more than one million people as Typhoon Dolphin slammed into its eastern coast, bringing ferocious winds and record rainfall. The storm made landfall in Zhejiang province on Monday, triggering the country's highest weather alert — a red alert — as authorities scrambled to protect residents and infrastructure.

The scale of the evacuation underscores the severity of the threat. Officials moved swiftly to relocate people from low-lying areas and coastal regions, prioritising safety over disruption. While the full extent of damage is yet to be assessed, early reports indicate significant flooding and infrastructure strain.

Record rainfall drenches Shanghai

Shanghai, China's financial hub, bore the brunt of Dolphin's fury, with record rainfall dumped on the city. Streets turned into rivers, and authorities warned of urban flooding risks. The downpour has raised concerns about the resilience of the city's drainage systems, which are being tested beyond their usual capacity.

The deluge also poses a threat to agriculture, with crops in the surrounding regions vulnerable to waterlogging. Analysts are already flagging potential disruptions to supply chains, though the full impact on harvests remains unclear. Local governments have mobilised emergency teams to pump out water and clear blocked drains.

Red alert: A rare and urgent warning

The red alert issued by China's meteorological authorities is reserved for the most extreme weather events. It signals winds of exceptional strength and rainfall that could cause catastrophic damage. Residents in affected areas have been urged to stay indoors, avoid low-lying zones, and heed evacuation orders.

This is not the first time China has faced a major typhoon this season, but Dolphin's intensity and path have made it particularly dangerous. The storm's slow movement has allowed it to dump prolonged rainfall, heightening the risk of landslides and river overflows. Emergency services remain on high alert, with rescue teams pre-positioned in vulnerable districts.

Crops at risk as storm lingers

Beyond the immediate threat to life and property, Typhoon Dolphin poses a significant risk to crops. The eastern coast is a key agricultural region, and the combination of strong winds and heavy rain can flatten fields, damage orchards, and spoil stored grain. Rice paddies, in particular, are vulnerable to submersion.

Bloomberg reports that the storm's impact on crops is a major concern for markets, as any shortfall could affect domestic food prices. However, officials have noted that advance warnings and evacuation efforts have helped mitigate some risks. The full agricultural toll will only become clear once floodwaters recede.

What to watch next

As Typhoon Dolphin moves inland, its strength is expected to weaken, but the threat of flooding will persist for days. Authorities are monitoring river levels and preparing for potential secondary disasters such as landslides. The coming days will reveal the storm's true toll on lives, property, and the region's food supply.
