Why joint pain worsens before rain: The barometric pressure link

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Falling barometric pressure before rain can cause joint tissues to expand, triggering pain.
  • People with arthritis or old injuries are most susceptible to weather-related joint pain.
  • Doctors advise staying active, keeping warm, and consulting a specialist if pain persists.

For millions of Indians, the arrival of monsoon brings more than just relief from heat. It also brings a familiar ache in the knees, hips, and fingers. Many report that their joints start hurting a day or two before the first shower.

Orthopaedic surgeons say this is not a myth. Changes in barometric pressure — the weight of the air around us — can directly affect the human body, especially joints that are already compromised.

What barometric pressure does to your joints

Barometric pressure is the force exerted by the atmosphere at a given point. When a storm or rain approaches, this pressure drops. The body's tissues, including those around joints, respond to this change.

“As barometric pressure falls, the tissues inside the joint can expand slightly,” explains a physician. This expansion puts pressure on nerves and can cause pain, particularly in joints that already have inflammation or reduced cartilage.

The effect is most noticeable in people with osteoarthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, or old fractures. Their joint capsules are less flexible, making them more sensitive to even tiny shifts in pressure.

Why some feel it more than others

Not everyone with joint pain reacts to weather changes. Studies suggest that about two-thirds of people with arthritis report some sensitivity to weather. The exact reason remains unclear, but doctors point to a combination of factors.

Low barometric pressure can also cause tendons, muscles, and scar tissue to expand. For someone with a healed ligament tear or a surgical scar, this expansion can tug on surrounding nerves and trigger discomfort.

Humidity and temperature play a role too. Cold, damp weather can make muscles stiff, reducing blood flow to already tender areas. This is why winter and monsoon are peak seasons for joint complaints across India.

When to see a doctor

Occasional weather-related joint pain is common and usually not a cause for alarm. But if the pain becomes frequent, severe, or interferes with daily activities, it is time to seek medical advice.

“If you notice swelling, redness, or warmth around the joint, or if the pain does not go away after the weather clears, you should see an orthopaedic surgeon,” doctors advise. These could be signs of underlying arthritis or inflammation that needs treatment.

Patients who rely on painkillers more than twice a week should also consult a specialist. Long-term use of over-the-counter pain medication can have side effects on the stomach, kidneys, and liver.

Simple ways to manage weather-related joint pain

Doctors recommend staying active even during rainy days. Low-impact exercises like walking, stretching, or yoga help keep joints flexible. Applying a warm compress can relax stiff muscles and improve blood circulation.

Maintaining a healthy weight reduces stress on weight-bearing joints such as the knees and hips. Wearing comfortable, supportive footwear also helps absorb shock and protect joints.

For those with chronic arthritis, doctors may prescribe anti-inflammatory medications or physiotherapy. Some patients benefit from wearing joint braces during weather changes.

As the monsoon season intensifies across India, those with old injuries or arthritis can expect some discomfort. Keeping the body warm, staying active, and monitoring symptoms closely are the best strategies. If the pain does not ease or worsens, a visit to the orthopaedic surgeon is the next step.
