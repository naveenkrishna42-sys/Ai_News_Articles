Google Play Store Opens to Third-Party App Stores from July 22

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Third-party app stores will be available on Google Play from July 22, 2026.
  • Google and Epic withdrew their joint bid to modify a US court injunction on the Play Store.
  • The move ends a long legal battle and reshapes Android's app distribution model.

Google Play to Host Rival App Stores

Google will allow third-party app stores on its Play Store from July 22, 2026, marking a major shift in Android app distribution. The change comes after Google and Epic Games withdrew their joint bid to modify a US court injunction that had blocked earlier attempts to open up the platform.

The decision effectively ends one of the most high-profile legal battles in the tech industry. Both companies had been fighting over whether Google could force developers to use its own billing system and distribution channel.

Epic Settlement Withdrawn

The original settlement, reached earlier this year, would have required Google to allow third-party app stores. But both sides filed a motion to withdraw it this week. Legal experts say the move clears the path for a new era where Android users can download apps from stores other than Google's own.

Google has not detailed the exact terms of the withdrawal. But the company confirmed that third-party app stores will begin appearing on the Play Store from next week.

What Changes for Users

From July 22, Android users in the US — and potentially globally — will see options to install app stores from companies like Epic Games, Amazon, and others directly from the Play Store. This bypasses the need to sideload apps, a process many users find confusing or risky.

Google has said it will implement safety checks for third-party stores. But the company has not shared the full list of partners or the timeline for a global rollout.

The Legal Backdrop

The original lawsuit, filed by Epic in 2020, alleged Google's Play Store practices were anti-competitive. A US court ruled in Epic's favor in 2024, ordering Google to allow third-party stores. The injunction was later stayed pending appeals.

By withdrawing their joint bid to modify that injunction, Google and Epic have effectively accepted the court's original order. This ends years of litigation that had kept the issue in legal limbo.

Industry Reactions

Analysts see this as a win for Epic and other developers who have long argued that Google's 30% commission on in-app purchases is unfair. Smaller developers may now have more options to reach users without paying high fees.

But some cybersecurity experts have raised concerns. Allowing third-party stores could increase the risk of malware if Google's vetting process is not robust. The company has promised to use its Play Protect system to scan apps from third-party stores.

Google has not commented on how it will enforce its security policies across multiple storefronts. The company is expected to release more details in the coming days.

For now, Android users in the US should expect to see new app store options appear on their devices from July 22. How quickly this expands to other markets — and how Google balances openness with security — will be the key story to watch in the months ahead.
