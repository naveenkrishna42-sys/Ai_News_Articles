UPI to remain free for consumers, Sitharaman assures Rajya Sabha

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sitharaman assures Rajya Sabha that UPI will remain free for consumers.
  • Clarifies that the Taxation Laws Amendment Bill does not enable UPI transaction charges.
  • Government reiterates commitment to affordable digital payments for all.
  • Bill passed amid concerns over possible charges on UPI transactions.

No charges on UPI, says FM in Parliament

Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman on Monday assured the Rajya Sabha that Unified Payments Interface (UPI) will remain free for consumers. Replying to a debate on the Taxation Laws Amendment Bill, she categorically stated that the government has no intention to levy any charge on UPI transactions.

The clarification comes amid growing concerns that the new legislation could pave the way for a transaction fee on UPI payments. Several members across party lines had sought clarity on the issue during the discussion.

What the new Bill actually does

The Taxation Laws Amendment Bill, which Parliament cleared on Monday, primarily seeks to amend certain provisions of the Income-tax Act and the Finance Act. It does not, according to the Finance Minister, contain any clause that would allow banks or payment aggregators to impose charges on UPI transactions.

Her statement was aimed at dispelling fears raised by digital payment users and industry watchers. The Bill had been criticised by some experts as a potential enabler of UPI charges, a concern that the government has now sought to address head-on.

Government's position on digital payments

Sitharaman reiterated that the government views UPI as a public good and a key pillar of its digital infrastructure. She emphasised that the zero-charge framework for consumers would continue, ensuring that the country's most popular retail payment system remains accessible to all.

The Finance Minister's assurance is significant given UPI's scale in India. With billions of transactions processed every month, any move to introduce charges would have a direct impact on millions of users, from street vendors to salaried professionals.

Industry and consumer reactions

The clarification has been welcomed by fintech industry players, who had been bracing for a possible disruption. Many had argued that imposing a fee on UPI could reverse the gains made in financial inclusion and digital adoption over the past several years.

Consumer rights groups have also reacted positively, though some have called for the government to put the assurance in writing through a formal notification. They point out that ambiguity in legislation can often lead to unintended interpretations later.

The Indian Express had earlier noted that the new Bill put free UPI under threat and deserved wider debate. The government's response in Parliament has now narrowed that concern, at least for the moment.

What remains unclear

While the Finance Minister has ruled out consumer charges, the question of interchange fees between banks and payment service providers remains unresolved. Industry sources suggest that discussions on cost-sharing within the UPI ecosystem are still underway, though no final decision has been announced.

The Reserve Bank of India had in 2023 proposed a tiered structure for UPI charges, but the idea was shelved after public backlash. Whether such proposals resurface remains to be seen.

For now, the government's stance is clear: the consumer will not pay. The focus, officials say, is on ensuring that UPI's growth story continues without adding to the financial burden of the common citizen.

Looking ahead

The next few months will be closely watched for any regulatory guidelines that might clarify the cost structure for UPI service providers. For consumers, however, the immediate takeaway from Monday's assurance is that their daily digital transactions will not attract any extra charges.
