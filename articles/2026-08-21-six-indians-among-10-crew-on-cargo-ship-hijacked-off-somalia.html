Six Indians Among 10 Crew on Cargo Ship Hijacked Off Somalia

Key points
  • Cargo ship with Turkish weapons hijacked off Somalia
  • Six Indian nationals among the 10 crew members on board
  • Incident follows two tanker hijackings with 22 Indians in the region
  • Somali piracy showing biggest resurgence in a decade, experts say

Six Indian nationals are among the 10 crew members of a cargo ship hijacked by armed pirates off the coast of Somalia, Indian officials confirmed on Thursday. The vessel was reportedly carrying a consignment of Turkish weapons at the time of the attack.

The hijacking marks the third such incident involving Indian sailors in the region within a span of four days, raising fresh concerns over the safety of international shipping lanes in the Horn of Africa.

Second Hijacking in Days

According to initial reports, the cargo ship was intercepted by pirates in waters near Somalia's semi-autonomous Puntland region. The exact circumstances of the attack, including the vessel's name and route, have not been officially disclosed by authorities.

This incident comes just days after two tankers carrying 22 Indian crew members were hijacked by armed pirates off the coast of Yemen and Somalia. The crew of those vessels are believed to be in captivity, though no group has formally claimed responsibility for the seizures.

Shipping and maritime security analysts point to a worrying pattern: the recent attacks appear coordinated, with pirates operating with greater sophistication than seen in years.

Resurgence of Somali Piracy

The hijackings signal what experts describe as the biggest resurgence of Somali piracy in a decade. After largely being suppressed by international naval patrols and onboard security measures around 2012, pirates have begun to re-emerge, emboldened by shifting geopolitical dynamics.

Analysts attribute the renewed activity to the ongoing conflict in West Asia, which has stretched naval resources and created a security vacuum in critical waterways. Some reports suggest pirates are receiving support from Houthi militants, who have been engaged in a separate campaign against shipping in the Red Sea and Gulf of Aden.

The combination of regional instability and reduced maritime patrols has created a fertile ground for piracy to thrive once again, particularly in the waters between Somalia, Yemen, and the wider Indian Ocean.

Indian Crews in the Crosshairs

For India, the recent spate of hijackings is a stark reminder of the risks faced by its seafarers, who constitute a significant portion of the global merchant navy workforce. The 22 Indians on the two tankers, along with the six on the latest vessel, are now the focus of intense diplomatic and naval efforts.

The Indian Navy has historically deployed assets in the region for anti-piracy operations, and officials have indicated that all available measures are being taken to ensure the safe return of the crew. However, the exact location of the hijacked ships remains unclear, complicating any potential rescue mission.

Families of the crew members have appealed to the government for urgent intervention, though officials have not confirmed any direct contact with the hijackers.

Regional Naval Response

Maritime security agencies in the region, including those from Somalia's Puntland administration, have been alerted. International naval forces, including those from the European Union and other nations operating under counter-piracy mandates, are said to be monitoring the situation.

The hijacking of a vessel carrying military cargo adds a layer of complexity, raising questions about the potential for escalation and the motives behind the attack. Whether the weapons were the target or the ship was seized for ransom remains unconfirmed.

Shipping companies are now being advised to review their security protocols and consider rerouting vessels away from high-risk zones, though such measures come with significant economic costs.

As the situation develops, the focus will be on securing the safe release of the crew and determining whether this marks a new phase of piracy in the region. The coming days will be critical in assessing the response of both Indian and international naval forces to this growing threat.
