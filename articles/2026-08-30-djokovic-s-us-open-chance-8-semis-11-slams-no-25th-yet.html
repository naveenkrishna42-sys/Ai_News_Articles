Djokovic's US Open chance: 8 semis, 11 Slams, no 25th yet

📅 2026-08-30 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Djokovic has reached 8 US Open semifinals but never won the title
  • He is 4 Slams short of Roger Federer's all-time record
  • The 2026 US Open features a strong field of contenders

Djokovic's Grand Slam drought

Novak Djokovic, the all-time Grand Slam leader with 24 titles, is set to make his 11th appearance at the US Open in 2026. Despite reaching the semifinals 8 times, he has never won the title, a drought that stretches back to 2021.

US Open 2026 contenders

The 2026 US Open field is stacked with contenders. Carlos Alcaraz, the 2022 US Open champion, is a top seed. Jannik Sinner, a rising star, and Daniil Medvedev, a former US Open finalist, are also in the mix. Djokovic will need to overcome these and other top players to claim his 25th Slam.

Djokovic's preparation

Djokovic has been preparing rigorously for the US Open. He took some time off after his early exit at the Cincinnati Masters, which he believes gave him the rest he needed. He is confident in his preparation and feels 'as best as I can be' for the tournament.

Key matchups

Djokovic's path to the title will likely involve tough matchups. He could face Alcaraz in the semifinals, a rematch of their 2023 Australian Open final. Other potential opponents include Sinner and Medvedev. Winning any of these matchups will be crucial for Djokovic.

Broader impact

Djokovic's performance at the US Open will have significant implications. A win would extend his all-time Grand Slam record and solidify his legacy as the greatest tennis player of all time. It would also provide a much-needed boost to his confidence and form, which have been inconsistent in recent years.

Tournament dynamics

The US Open is known for its unpredictable nature. The surface, a fast hard court, can produce upsets. Djokovic will need to be at his best to navigate the tournament's challenges. His ability to handle pressure and adapt to different conditions will be key.
