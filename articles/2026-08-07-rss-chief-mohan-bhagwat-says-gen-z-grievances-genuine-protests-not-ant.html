RSS Chief Mohan Bhagwat Says Gen Z Grievances Genuine, Protests Not Anti-National

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mohan Bhagwat says Gen Z grievances are genuine and protesters are not anti-national
  • Remark draws praise from Omar Abdullah; Abhijeet Dipke responds to critics
  • Bhagwat backs democratic right to dissent, calling protesters 'our own people'

Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) chief Mohan Bhagwat on Friday said the grievances of Gen Z are genuine and asserted that protesting does not make the younger generation anti-national. His remarks, which underline the democratic right to dissent, have sparked reactions across the political spectrum.

Genuine Grievances, Not Anti-National

Speaking at an event, Bhagwat addressed the concerns of the younger generation, saying their frustrations stem from real issues. He stressed that the youth's willingness to protest reflects their engagement with society, not a lack of patriotism.

“Protesting doesn't make Gen Z anti-national,” Bhagwat said. He added that protesters are “our own people,” reinforcing the idea that dissent is a legitimate part of democracy. The RSS chief's comments come amid growing debates over youth activism and national identity.

Political Reactions

The statement drew immediate praise from former Jammu and Kashmir chief minister Omar Abdullah. Abdullah lauded Bhagwat's remark, noting that it acknowledges the legitimacy of peaceful protest. His endorsement signals a rare moment of agreement across political lines.

Meanwhile, Shiv Sena leader Abhijeet Dipke took a swipe at critics of Bhagwat's comments. In a post that appeared to address naysayers, Dipke said, “To whom it may concern,” implying that the RSS chief's stance should silence those who label protesters as anti-national. The exchange highlights the shifting discourse around dissent in India.

A Broader Message on Democracy

Bhagwat's remarks go beyond Gen Z, offering a broader endorsement of democratic values. By describing protesters as “our own people,” he sought to bridge divides between generations and ideologies. The RSS chief's words carry weight given the organisation's influence in the country's socio-political fabric.

His backing of the democratic right to dissent aligns with constitutional principles, yet it also marks a notable departure from hardline rhetoric often seen in political discourse. Observers see this as an attempt to engage with younger Indians who are increasingly vocal on issues like employment, climate change, and social justice.

What This Means for Gen Z

For many young Indians, Bhagwat's acknowledgment is a validation of their concerns. The statement may encourage more open dialogue between the establishment and the youth. It also challenges the narrative that protest is synonymous with disloyalty.

While reactions vary, the consensus is that this is a significant moment. The RSS chief's words could influence how other leaders frame their responses to youth-led movements in the coming months.

As the debate continues, all eyes will be on whether this rhetoric translates into policy or remains a symbolic gesture. For now, Bhagwat has set a tone that prioritises listening over condemnation.
