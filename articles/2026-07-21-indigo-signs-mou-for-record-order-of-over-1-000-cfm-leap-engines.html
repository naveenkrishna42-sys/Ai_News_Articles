IndiGo signs MoU for record order of over 1,000 CFM LEAP engines

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • IndiGo signed a MoU for over 1,000 CFM LEAP engines to power its Airbus A320 family fleet.
  • The deal includes a repair and maintenance shop, likely to be set up in India.
  • This is the largest single engine order in IndiGo's history, underscoring its expansion plans.
  • CFM International, a joint venture between GE and Safran, will supply the engines.

Record engine order for IndiGo

IndiGo has signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) with CFM International for over 1,000 LEAP-1A engines to power its Airbus A320 family aircraft. The deal, announced on Monday, marks the largest single engine order in the airline's history.

The order covers engines for the carrier's existing and future fleet of A320neo and A321XLR aircraft. IndiGo currently operates over 300 Airbus planes and has hundreds more on order.

CFM International, a 50-50 joint venture between GE Aerospace and Safran Aircraft Engines, will supply the LEAP engines. The engine model is already used on IndiGo's A320neo fleet.

Repair shop plans in India

Alongside the engine order, IndiGo has also signed a separate agreement to set up a repair and overhaul shop in India. The facility will handle maintenance of the LEAP engines, reducing downtime for the airline.

Sources indicate the repair shop could be located near one of IndiGo's major hubs, though officials have not confirmed the exact site. The move aligns with the government's push for domestic aviation maintenance capabilities.

IndiGo CEO Pieter Elbers said the deal supports the airline's growth strategy. "This order ensures we have the most reliable and efficient engines for our expanding fleet, while the repair shop will strengthen our operational self-reliance," he said in a statement.

Expansion amid fierce competition

The order comes as IndiGo aggressively expands its domestic and international network. The airline now flies to over 80 domestic and 30 international destinations.

India's aviation market is one of the fastest-growing globally, and IndiGo holds a market share of over 55%. The new engines will power planes for both short-haul and medium-haul routes, including to Southeast Asia and the Middle East.

Industry analysts say the deal underscores IndiGo's confidence in its long-term growth. "This is a bet on the Indian market's sustained demand for air travel," said an aviation analyst who did not wish to be named.

The LEAP engine is known for its fuel efficiency, offering 15% better fuel consumption than older models. This will help IndiGo control operating costs, a key factor in its low-cost model.

Financial terms of the deal were not disclosed. Engine list prices range from $10 million to $15 million each, but bulk orders typically get significant discounts.

IndiGo's move also puts pressure on competitors like Air India and SpiceJet, which are also expanding fleets. Air India recently placed a large order for Airbus and Boeing planes, but engine choices are still being finalised.

CFM International has a strong presence in India, already powering IndiGo, Go First, and Vistara aircraft. The LEAP engine has faced some technical issues globally, but CFM says reliability has improved in recent years.

What to watch next: The final contract will be signed in the coming months. IndiGo will also need to secure slots for engine deliveries, which can be tight due to global supply chain constraints. The repair shop's location and timeline are yet to be announced.
