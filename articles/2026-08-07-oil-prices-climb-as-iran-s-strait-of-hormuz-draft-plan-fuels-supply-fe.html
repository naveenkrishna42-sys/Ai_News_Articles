Oil prices climb as Iran's Strait of Hormuz draft plan fuels supply fears

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran's draft plan for Strait of Hormuz raises supply disruption fears
  • Oil prices climb as markets weigh restrictive measures
  • Iran-Oman talks continue amid hopes for reopening and peace deal

Oil prices climbed on Friday as markets absorbed news of Iran's restrictive draft plan for the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of global petroleum flows. The proposal, which has not been made public in full, has reignited concerns over potential supply disruptions in one of the world's most critical shipping lanes.

Brent crude and West Texas Intermediate both edged higher in early trading, extending gains from the previous session. The move comes as traders grapple with a widening gap between diplomatic overtures and hardline policy signals from Tehran.

Draft plan stokes uncertainty

Details of the draft plan remain sketchy, but sources familiar with the matter suggest it includes stricter navigation protocols and expanded inspection rights for Iranian authorities. Such measures, if implemented, could slow tanker traffic and raise insurance costs for shippers transiting the strait.

Iran has defended the plan as a sovereign right to manage its territorial waters. But analysts note that the timing is sensitive, arriving just as Iran and Oman are reportedly working on a framework to ease tensions and potentially reopen the strait to normal traffic.

"The market is caught between hope and fear," said one Singapore-based crude trader. "Every headline about talks pulls prices down, and every hint of restrictions pushes them back up."

Talks continue, but no deal yet

Iran-Oman negotiations have been underway for weeks, with both sides signalling a willingness to reach a compromise. Oman, a longtime mediator in the region, has hosted several rounds of discussions aimed at de-escalating the situation.

Yet no formal agreement has been announced. Iranian officials have stressed that any deal must address its security concerns, while international buyers are pressing for guarantees of unhindered passage. The gap between these positions keeps the market on edge.

Reports of Iran striking 'hostile targets' in the strait earlier this week added to the volatility, though no casualties or vessel damage have been confirmed. The incident underscored how quickly rhetoric can escalate into action.

Global impact and market reaction

Asian refiners and European importers are watching the strait closely, given their heavy reliance on Middle Eastern crude. Any prolonged disruption could force buyers to seek alternative supplies from the Atlantic Basin, pushing freight rates higher and tightening global inventories.

Chip stocks weakened and oil held steady in broader markets as investors awaited clearer signs of progress. The wait-and-see mood reflects a broader uncertainty about whether diplomacy will succeed before tensions boil over.

Some analysts argue that the price reaction may be overdone, noting that Iran has both economic and political incentives to avoid a full closure. But others point out that even a partial restriction could have outsized effects, given the lack of spare capacity in the market.

What to watch

All eyes will be on the next round of Iran-Oman talks, expected to resume in the coming days. A breakthrough could send prices sliding; a breakdown could push them sharply higher. Traders are also monitoring US naval deployments in the region, which have increased in recent weeks.

For now, the market remains hostage to headlines from Muscat and Tehran. The strait stays open, but the margin for error is razor-thin.
