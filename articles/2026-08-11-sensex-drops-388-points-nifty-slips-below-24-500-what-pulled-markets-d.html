Sensex drops 388 points, Nifty slips below 24,500: What pulled markets down

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 388 points, Nifty slipped below 24,500
  • Sell-off attributed to global cues and profit booking
  • Investors await key economic data for direction

Indian equity markets opened on a weak note on Tuesday, with the Sensex plunging 388 points and the Nifty slipping below the crucial 24,500 mark. The decline, which erased early gains, left investors scrambling for answers as volatility gripped Dalal Street.

The sell-off was broad-based, with losses across banking, IT, and auto stocks. Market participants pointed to a mix of global headwinds and domestic profit booking as the primary triggers for the sharp drop.

Global Cues Weigh Heavy

Weak global cues set the tone for the day. Asian peers traded lower amid concerns over slowing global growth and uncertainty around US interest rates. The overnight weakness on Wall Street, driven by tech stock sell-offs, added to the pessimism.

Oil prices also remained elevated, stoking fears of persistent inflation. Rising crude typically hurts India's fiscal math and corporate margins, prompting investors to trim positions in riskier assets.

Profit Booking After Recent Rally

After a strong run over the past few weeks, many investors chose to book profits at higher levels. The Nifty had been hovering near record highs, and analysts said the market was ripe for a correction.

Domestic institutional investors were net sellers, while retail participation also dipped. The lack of fresh triggers, with earnings season largely behind, left the market without a clear direction.

Sectoral Impact

Banking stocks were among the biggest drags, with heavyweight lenders like HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank contributing significantly to the Sensex's fall. IT stocks also came under pressure, tracking a decline in global tech peers.

Auto and FMCG counters saw moderate losses, though defensive sectors like pharma managed to stay afloat. The broader market indices, including the BSE Midcap and Smallcap, also traded lower, indicating that the sell-off was not just limited to large caps.

What Investors Should Watch

Market experts said that near-term movement will be dictated by global cues, particularly the upcoming US inflation data. A higher-than-expected reading could strengthen the case for prolonged high interest rates, which would weigh on emerging markets like India.

On the domestic front, foreign institutional investor flows will be closely tracked. Any sustained outflow could add further pressure on the rupee and equities, though a dip in crude prices might provide some relief.

For now, traders are advised to stay cautious and avoid building aggressive positions until the market finds a stable footing. The coming sessions are likely to remain volatile, with 24,500 acting as a key support level for the Nifty.
