Akhilesh Yadav Questions BJP's Hold on Uttar Pradesh Ahead of 2027

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Akhilesh Yadav predicts BJP may lose Uttar Pradesh in upcoming elections
  • He criticizes Modi's foreign policy as ineffective for the state
  • Yadav emphasizes the need for a strong INDIA alliance to challenge BJP

In a wide-ranging interview with Frontline Magazine, Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav has directly questioned the Bharatiya Janata Party's grip on Uttar Pradesh. The former chief minister's remarks come at a time when the state is gearing up for the 2027 assembly elections, and the national opposition is trying to stitch together a credible challenge to the ruling party.

Yadav did not hold back, stating that the BJP's popularity in the state has been on a downward slide. He pointed to recent by-election results and local body polls as indicators of a shifting mood among voters. "The people of Uttar Pradesh have seen through the empty promises," he said, without offering specific data to back his claim.

INDIA Alliance and the Road Ahead

The Samajwadi Party leader stressed that the INDIA alliance, a coalition of opposition parties, must present a united front to defeat the BJP. He argued that the alliance's success hinges on seat-sharing arrangements and a common agenda focused on jobs, inflation, and farmer distress.

"We cannot afford to fight among ourselves," Yadav said, referring to the internal friction within the alliance. He acknowledged that differences remain but expressed confidence that a consensus would be reached before the next general elections. The interview did not specify a timeline for finalizing the alliance's strategy.

Modi's Foreign Policy Under Fire

Yadav also took aim at Prime Minister Narendra Modi's foreign policy, particularly its impact on Uttar Pradesh. He argued that the government's focus on global diplomacy has not translated into tangible benefits for the state's economy or its migrant workers abroad.

"What has the foreign policy done for the weavers of Varanasi or the youth in Kanpur?" he asked, implying that the benefits of international engagements have not trickled down. He specifically criticized the handling of relations with Gulf countries, which are home to a large number of UP-origin workers.

The SIR Factor

The mention of SIR, likely referring to a political strategy or individual, was brief but pointed. Yadav suggested that the BJP's reliance on a single leader or slogan is no longer working. He urged voters to look beyond personalities and focus on governance issues.

Political analysts say Yadav's comments reflect a broader effort by the opposition to frame the upcoming elections as a referendum on the BJP's performance rather than a popularity contest. However, the BJP has dismissed such criticism, pointing to its victory in the 2024 Lok Sabha polls in the state.

What's at Stake for Uttar Pradesh

Uttar Pradesh sends 80 MPs to the Lok Sabha, the highest among all states. Winning the state is often seen as a prerequisite for forming the central government. The BJP won 62 of those seats in 2024, but by-elections since then have narrowed that margin.

Yadav's optimism may be premature, but it signals that the opposition is no longer willing to cede the state to the BJP without a fight. The Samajwadi Party is banking on a coalition of backward castes, Muslims, and disgruntled farmers to swing the vote.

What happens next will depend on how the INDIA alliance resolves its internal differences and whether the BJP can reverse the perception of complacency. The next few months of political maneuvering in Lucknow will be crucial for both sides.
