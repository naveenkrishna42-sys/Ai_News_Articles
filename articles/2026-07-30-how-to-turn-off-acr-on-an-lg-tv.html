How to turn off ACR on an LG TV

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • ACR (Automatic Content Recognition) tracks what you watch to target ads and sell data.
  • LG TVs run webOS and have ACR enabled by default, often buried in settings.
  • You can disable ACR by going to Settings > All Settings > General > Live Plus and toggling it off.
  • For ads, go to Settings > All Settings > General > Additional Settings and turn off 'Use viewing information for ads'.
  • If you want maximum privacy, disconnect the TV from the internet entirely.

Modern smart TVs watch you back. Automatic Content Recognition, or ACR, is a feature baked into most LG televisions running webOS. It identifies the shows, movies, and even ads on your screen, then sends that data to LG and its partners. The goal: serve you targeted commercials and build a profile of your viewing habits.

For many users, this feels like an invasion. The good news is you can shut it off. It just takes a few steps — and knowing where to look.

What is ACR and why does it matter?

ACR works in the background. It takes a snapshot of what you're watching every few seconds, matches it against a database, and logs the content. LG uses this for its Live Plus service, which offers interactive ads and recommendations.

The privacy concern is straightforward. Your viewing data becomes a commodity. It can be shared with advertisers, data brokers, and analytics firms. While LG says the data is anonymised, critics argue the practice is opaque and hard to opt out of. A 2024 report from RTINGS.com highlighted that many smart TVs — including LG models — make disabling ACR a labyrinthine process.

Steps to disable ACR on an LG TV (webOS)

On any LG TV running webOS 4.0 or later, start by pressing the Settings button on your remote. Navigate to All Settings (the gear icon), then select General.

Look for Live Plus. This is the feature that enables ACR-based interactivity. Select it and toggle the switch to Off. That stops the core content recognition.

Next, go back to General and select Additional Settings. Find the option labelled Use viewing information for ads. Turn it off. This prevents LG from using your viewing data to personalise advertisements.

If you want to be thorough, also disable Interest-based advertising under the Privacy menu. This cuts off ad targeting from LG's partners.

What about ads and voice data?

ACR is not the only data collector. LG's webOS also sends data through its advertising ID and voice recognition features. To disable the advertising ID, go to Settings > All Settings > General > Privacy and turn off Ad Personalisation.

For voice data, if you use the remote's voice control, LG processes those commands. You can disable voice data collection by going to Settings > All Settings > General > Voice and turning off Voice Recognition Service. This will stop the TV from sending your voice clips to LG's servers.

The nuclear option: disconnect from the internet

If you are deeply concerned about privacy, the most effective step is to take your LG TV offline. A smart TV that cannot reach the internet cannot phone home. You lose streaming apps and firmware updates, but you gain complete control over your data.

You can still use external devices like a Fire Stick, Apple TV, or gaming console for streaming. Those devices have their own privacy settings, but they keep the TV itself isolated. Some users prefer this trade-off.

To disconnect, simply go to Settings > All Settings > Network > Wi-Fi and forget your network. Or unplug the Ethernet cable.

What to watch next

Privacy regulations in India and abroad are tightening. The upcoming Digital Personal Data Protection Act may force TV manufacturers to be more transparent about ACR. For now, the onus remains on the user to dig through menus and turn off tracking. If you own an LG TV, spending five minutes on these settings is the only way to stop it from watching you.
