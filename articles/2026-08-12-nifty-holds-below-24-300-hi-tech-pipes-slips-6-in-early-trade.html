Nifty Holds Below 24,300; Hi-Tech Pipes Slips 6% in Early Trade

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nifty trades below 24,300, Sensex flat in early session
  • Hi-Tech Pipes shares drop 6%
  • Investors watch global cues for direction

Indian equity benchmarks opened on a cautious note on Wednesday, with the Nifty struggling to hold above the 24,300 mark. The Sensex was largely flat in early trade, reflecting a wait-and-watch approach among investors amid mixed global signals.

The Nifty hovered around the 24,280-24,300 zone, while the Sensex traded with marginal gains. Market breadth was slightly negative, with advances roughly matching declines on the BSE.

Hi-Tech Pipes in Focus

Shares of Hi-Tech Pipes fell as much as 6% in morning trade, making it one of the prominent losers on the bourses. The steel pipes manufacturer saw heightened selling pressure, though no immediate company-specific announcement was available at the time of writing.

Analysts suggested the drop could be profit-taking after recent gains, but they cautioned that volumes would determine whether the selling sustains. The stock has been volatile in recent sessions, tracking broader metal sector trends.

Broader Market Cautious

Most sectoral indices were mixed. IT and banking stocks provided some support, while metals and realty faced mild selling pressure. The midcap and smallcap indices were trading with small losses, indicating a lack of conviction among risk-takers.

  • Nifty IT was up 0.3% in early deals
  • Nifty Metal index slipped 0.5%
  • Volatility index remained elevated

Global cues were subdued. Asian markets traded mixed, with investors awaiting key US inflation data due later this week. The rupee was steady against the dollar, offering little directional impetus.

What to Watch

Traders are likely to track foreign fund flows, crude oil prices, and the movement of the rupee for near-term direction. A break above 24,300 on the Nifty could trigger short covering, while a sustained fall below 24,200 may invite further selling.

With no major domestic triggers scheduled, the market could remain range-bound. The focus now shifts to the US inflation print and its implications for global interest rates, which could set the tone for the next leg of the move.
