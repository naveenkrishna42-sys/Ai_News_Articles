Assam Floods: Village Erased in an Hour, Death Toll Climbs to 78

📅 2026-07-30 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • A village in Assam was completely submerged in an hour due to flash floods.
  • The state-wide death toll has risen to 78, with over 3 lakh people affected.
  • Floodwaters are receding in some areas, but residents face severe shortages of food and clean water.
  • Experts call for long-term solutions to the state's recurring flood crisis.

Village Swallowed in Minutes

In a stark illustration of Assam's flood fury, a small village along the Brahmaputra basin was completely submerged within an hour on Wednesday. Residents had little time to salvage belongings, fleeing with only the clothes on their backs as muddy waters engulfed homes and livestock.

Local officials confirmed the village, whose name is being withheld pending family notifications, is no longer habitable. The incident has become the focal point of the state's worsening flood crisis, which has now claimed at least 78 lives.

Over 3 lakh people across 28 districts have been affected, with rescue teams struggling to reach isolated communities. The Assam State Disaster Management Authority has deployed additional boats and relief materials to the worst-hit areas.

Waters Recede, Misery Remains

While floodwaters have begun to recede in some northern districts, the struggle for survivors is far from over. In temporary relief camps, families report acute shortages of drinking water, dry food, and medical supplies.

“We lost everything — our house, our crops, our cattle. Now we wait for help that arrives too little, too late,” said a resident of Dhemaji district, one of the hardest-hit regions. Authorities have set up over 200 relief camps, but many remain inaccessible due to damaged roads and submerged bridges.

The Indian Army and National Disaster Response Force have been conducting aerial surveys and airdropping supplies. However, with more than 1,000 villages under water, the scale of the disaster is overwhelming existing resources.

Annual Crisis Needs Permanent Fix

The recurring floods have sparked renewed debate on long-term solutions. Singer and activist Papon, a native of Assam, has called for a comprehensive plan to tackle the state's annual deluge. “We can't keep calling this unprecedented every year,” he said in a statement. “We need sustainable measures — better embankments, early warning systems, and climate-resilient infrastructure.”

Experts point to deforestation, encroachment on floodplains, and climate change as key factors worsening the crisis. The Brahmaputra River, which swells during the monsoon, often changes course unpredictably, erasing entire villages overnight.

State officials have assured that compensation will be provided to affected families, and a detailed assessment of damages is underway. The flood toll is expected to rise as rescue teams reach more remote areas.

As Assam stares at yet another devastating monsoon season, the question remains: will the response shift from emergency relief to lasting preparedness?
