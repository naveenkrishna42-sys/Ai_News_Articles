NAB ALL Accor 30% Conversion Bonus: The Basics

NAB Rewards has launched a limited-time 30% bonus on point transfers to ALL Accor, valid for conversions made between 1 and 30 September 2026. The offer applies to NAB Rewards, NAB Rewards Platinum, and NAB Rewards Signature cards. Cardholders must transfer a minimum of 2,000 NAB Rewards points in a single transaction to trigger the bonus.

ALL Accor is the loyalty programme of Accor, covering brands such as Sofitel, Pullman, Novotel, ibis, and Raffles. Points are redeemable for free nights, room upgrades, and on-property experiences. The bonus effectively increases the standard conversion rate, making this one of the more generous transfer promotions available to Australian cardholders this quarter.

How the Bonus Works

Standard NAB Rewards conversions to ALL Accor are processed at a 2:1 ratio—2 NAB points yield 1 Accor point. With the 30% bonus, that ratio improves to approximately 1.54:1, meaning 1,000 NAB points convert to about 650 Accor points instead of 500. The bonus is applied automatically at the time of transfer; no promo code is required.

Transfers are typically instant for ALL Accor, though NAB states it can take up to 5 business days. Once credited, Accor points remain valid for 12 months, but any earning or redemption activity extends the expiry by 12 months, making them effectively perpetual for active members.

Real-World Value: Crunching the Numbers

Accor points carry a fixed redemption value of 2,000 points for €40 (about A$65) off a stay, which works out to roughly 3.25 Australian cents per point when used for hotel bookings. However, redemption availability and dynamic pricing can lower that to 2.5 cents. For this analysis, we use a conservative 2.5 cents.

Without the bonus, 10,000 NAB Rewards points convert to 5,000 Accor points, worth about A$125. With the 30% bonus, you receive 6,500 Accor points, worth approximately A$162.50—a 30% uplift. For a cardholder who spends A$5,000 per month on a NAB Rewards Signature card (earning 1.25 points per A$1), that’s 6,250 points monthly, or 75,000 points annually. Converting all of it during the bonus window yields 48,750 Accor points, worth roughly A$1,219 in hotel stays.

Pros & Cons

  • Pros:
    • 30% bonus is at the higher end of transfer promotions in the Australian market.
    • Accor points are easy to redeem across a wide range of hotel tiers, from economy ibis to luxury Sofitel.
    • No cap on the number of points you can transfer, so high spenders can maximise the bonus.
    • Transfers are generally fast, reducing the risk of missing the deadline.
  • Cons:
    • Minimum transfer of 2,000 points may deter low-balance cardholders.
    • Accor points have a fixed value, so you won’t get outsized redemption value like with airline awards.
    • NAB Rewards points can also be transferred to other partners (e.g., Velocity), so you forgo those options.
    • Bonus is capped by the September window; points transferred in October earn no bonus.

Should You Transfer?

If you have a near-term Accor stay planned or can book within 12 months, this bonus delivers solid value. The 30% uplift effectively turns 2 NAB points into 1.3 Accor points, improving your return on spend by nearly a third. However, if you are chasing premium cabin flights, transferring to an airline partner may still be the better play. For everyone else, this is a straightforward way to boost your hotel balance without changing your spending habits.

To participate, log in to your NAB Rewards account, select ALL Accor as the transfer partner, and initiate a transfer of at least 2,000 points before 30 September 2026. The bonus points will appear alongside the base points in your Accor account.
