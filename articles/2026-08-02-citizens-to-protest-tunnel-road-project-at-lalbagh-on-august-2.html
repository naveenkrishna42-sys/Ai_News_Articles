Citizens to protest tunnel road project at Lalbagh on August 2

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Protest scheduled at Lalbagh on August 2 against tunnel road project
  • Citizens cite concerns over impact on heritage garden and trees
  • Authorities yet to respond to protest call

Protest call against tunnel road

Bengaluru's green activists and residents are set to gather at Lalbagh on August 2 to protest the proposed tunnel road project, which they claim threatens the city's iconic botanical garden. The demonstration, announced by civic groups, aims to pressure authorities into reconsidering the alignment that cuts through the heritage site.

The tunnel road, part of a larger infrastructure push to ease traffic congestion, has drawn sharp criticism from environmentalists and local residents. They argue that the project would irreversibly damage Lalbagh's ecosystem, including centuries-old trees and rare plant species.

Concerns over green cover

Protesters say the tunnel's construction could disturb the groundwater table and root systems, jeopardising the garden's lush landscape. "Lalbagh is not just a park; it's a lung of the city," said a convenor of one of the organising groups. "We cannot allow a road project to destroy what has stood for over a century."

The garden, spread across 240 acres, is a popular spot for morning walkers and tourists alike. Any disruption to its tranquillity, residents fear, would be a loss for future generations.

Authorities yet to respond

As of now, no official statement has been issued by the Bengaluru civic body or the state government regarding the protest. The project's detailed project report (DPR) has been in the works, but the exact route remains a contentious issue.

Previous attempts to build infrastructure near Lalbagh have met with stiff resistance, and this time appears no different. The protest is expected to draw hundreds of participants, including students, environmentalists, and local politicians.

What lies ahead

The August 2 gathering is likely to be a litmus test for public sentiment on infrastructure versus ecology in Bengaluru. Organisers have hinted at prolonged agitation if their demands are not met.

All eyes will be on the authorities' response in the coming days—whether they agree to a revised alignment or stand firm on the current plan.
