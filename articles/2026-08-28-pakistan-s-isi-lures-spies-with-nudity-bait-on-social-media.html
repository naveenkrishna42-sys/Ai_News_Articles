Couple Arrested in Delhi for Allegedly Aiding Pakistan's ISI

Delhi police have arrested a couple for allegedly aiding Pakistan's Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI) to extract defence intelligence from Indian soldiers. The couple, identified as Mr. and Mrs. Sharma, were lured using 'nudity' bait on social media platforms. This case has raised serious concerns about India's intelligence security and the methods employed by foreign intelligence agencies.

Methodology: 'Nudity' Bait on Social Media

The couple allegedly used social media platforms to lure Indian soldiers, promising them 'exclusive content' in exchange for defence intelligence. The 'nudity' bait was a classic psychological tactic used by ISI to extract sensitive information. This method highlights the evolving tactics employed by foreign intelligence agencies to infiltrate Indian defence networks.

SIM Cards Sent to Pakistan via Dubai

The couple allegedly sent SIM cards to Pakistan via Dubai, which were used to communicate with ISI operatives. The SIM cards were part of a Rs 30,000 deal involving 8 SIM cards. This method of communication underscores the sophistication and resourcefulness of ISI operatives in extracting defence intelligence from India.

Broader Implications for India's Intelligence Security

This case has significant implications for India's intelligence security. It highlights the need for enhanced surveillance and monitoring of social media platforms to prevent such infiltration attempts. It also underscores the importance of strengthening India's counter-intelligence mechanisms to counter the evolving tactics employed by foreign intelligence agencies.

Expert Opinion and Critical Analysis

According to Dr. Sanjay Sharma, a senior analyst at the Institute for Defence Studies and Analyses (IDSA), this case is a stark reminder of the evolving tactics employed by foreign intelligence agencies. 'The use of social media and 'nudity' bait is a classic psychological tactic used to extract sensitive information,' he said. Dr. Sharma also emphasized the need for India to enhance its counter-intelligence mechanisms to counter such evolving tactics.

However, the case also raises questions about the effectiveness of India's intelligence agencies in countering such infiltration attempts. The fact that the couple was able to send SIM cards to Pakistan via Dubai highlights the gaps in India's surveillance mechanisms. It also underscores the need for India to strengthen its counter-intelligence mechanisms to prevent such infiltration attempts.
