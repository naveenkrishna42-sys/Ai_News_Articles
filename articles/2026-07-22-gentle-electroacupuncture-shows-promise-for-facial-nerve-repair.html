Gentle electroacupuncture shows promise for facial nerve repair

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Study compared electroacupuncture to standard acupuncture and medication for facial paralysis.
  • Gentle electroacupuncture showed better improvement in facial nerve function scores.
  • Research suggests electroacupuncture may promote nerve regeneration and recovery.

Study examines electroacupuncture for facial paralysis

A new study suggests that gentle electroacupuncture may help repair damaged facial nerves in patients with peripheral facial paralysis. The research, which compared three treatment groups, found that those receiving electroacupuncture showed better recovery in facial movement and symmetry.

Peripheral facial paralysis, often caused by conditions like Bell's palsy, can lead to drooping of one side of the face, difficulty closing the eye, and problems with smiling or frowning. While many patients recover on their own, some are left with lingering weakness.

How the study was conducted

Researchers divided patients into three groups: one received standard acupuncture, another got gentle electroacupuncture, and the third was given conventional medication. The teams used two scoring systems — the Facial Disability Index (FDIP) and the Facial Disability Index (FDIS) — to measure changes before and after treatment.

The FDIP score assesses physical function, like the ability to eat, drink, and speak. The FDIS score looks at social and emotional well-being, including how the condition affects daily life.

Electroacupuncture showed better results

According to the study, the electroacupuncture group showed greater improvement in both FDIP and FDIS scores compared to the other groups. Gentle electrical stimulation applied to acupuncture points appeared to boost nerve repair processes.

Researchers note that electroacupuncture may work by increasing blood flow to the affected area and promoting the release of growth factors that help nerves regrow. The therapy is considered gentler than standard electroacupuncture, using lower electrical currents to avoid discomfort.

Implications for treatment

While the study is promising, doctors caution that more research is needed before electroacupuncture becomes a standard treatment. Current options for facial paralysis include steroids, antiviral drugs, physical therapy, and in some cases, surgery.

Electroacupuncture is already used in some parts of the world for pain management and nerve conditions. If further trials confirm these findings, it could offer a non-invasive option for patients who do not respond well to medication.

Experts say patients should always consult a qualified practitioner before trying any new therapy. Facial paralysis can have multiple causes, including stroke, infection, or injury, and treatment must be tailored to the underlying condition.

What happens next

The research team plans larger, longer-term studies to confirm the results and determine the optimal treatment protocol. For now, electroacupuncture remains an experimental but hopeful avenue for those seeking to regain facial function.
