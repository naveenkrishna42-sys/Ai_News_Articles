Candida auris: Drug-resistant fungus spreads across 27 US states

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 3,400 cases of Candida auris have been reported across 27 US states, according to CDC data.
  • The drug-resistant fungus can hide in hair and enter the bloodstream, posing life-threatening risks.
  • Texas leads the US in reported cases of this dangerous fungal infection.
  • The fungus contaminates healthcare spaces, with experts warning about its rapid spread.

Rapid Spread Across US Healthcare Facilities

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) has recorded over 3,400 cases of Candida auris across 27 US states. The drug-resistant fungus, which can contaminate healthcare spaces, has become a growing concern for hospitals and nursing homes nationwide.

Texas leads the country in reported cases, according to recent data. The infection, which can be life-threatening, has been detected in patients across multiple states, with numbers climbing steadily over the past year.

A Fungus That Hides and Strikes

Candida auris is particularly dangerous because it can hide in hair and on skin, making it difficult to eradicate from healthcare environments. Once it enters the bloodstream, the fungus can cause severe infections that resist standard antifungal treatments.

Health officials have noted that the pathogen spreads easily in hospitals, long-term care facilities, and other medical settings. Contaminated surfaces, medical equipment, and even healthcare workers' hands have been identified as potential transmission routes.

Why This Outbreak Is Different

Unlike many other fungal infections, Candida auris is resistant to multiple antifungal drugs. This makes treatment options limited and increases the risk of complications, particularly for patients with weakened immune systems.

The CDC has been tracking the fungus since it first emerged in the United States several years ago. Recent data suggests a sharp uptick in cases, prompting renewed attention from public health authorities.

What Healthcare Facilities Are Doing

Hospitals in affected states have ramped up screening protocols for high-risk patients. Infection control teams are using specialized cleaning agents to eliminate the fungus from surfaces, and patients diagnosed with Candida auris are being isolated to prevent further spread.

Healthcare workers are also being trained to recognise symptoms early. The fungus can cause fever, chills, and other signs of systemic infection, but these symptoms are often mistaken for other conditions, delaying diagnosis.

Public Health Response

The CDC has issued guidelines for healthcare providers on how to detect and manage Candida auris cases. These include recommendations for laboratory testing, patient isolation, and environmental cleaning.

State health departments are collaborating with federal agencies to monitor the spread. However, officials have not yet confirmed the full extent of the outbreak, as some cases may go undetected due to limited testing capacity.

What You Should Know

For most healthy individuals, Candida auris poses little threat. The fungus primarily affects people who are already hospitalised, have invasive medical devices, or have compromised immune systems.

Family members visiting patients in healthcare facilities are advised to practice good hand hygiene and follow facility protocols. The CDC also recommends that patients who have been hospitalised in affected areas be tested if they develop unexplained symptoms.

As the number of cases continues to rise, public health experts are urging hospitals to strengthen their infection control measures. The coming months will be critical in determining whether the spread can be contained.
