High Court Pulls Up SBI for Unauthorised Fixed Deposit Deduction

The Punjab & Haryana High Court has ordered the State Bank of India (SBI) to refund ₹19.90 lakh to an NRI customer after the bank illegally deducted the amount from his NRE fixed deposit. The court not only directed the refund but also imposed interest on the deducted sum, slamming the bank for violating fundamental banking norms. The judgment, delivered in September 2026, underscores the vulnerability of depositors even at India's largest public sector bank.

According to court records, the customer held an NRE fixed deposit with SBI. Without obtaining his consent or producing any court order, the bank adjusted the deposit against a third-party liability—a clear breach of the depositor's trust and RBI guidelines. The court observed that SBI's action was arbitrary and contrary to the basic principles of banking law.

What Really Happened: The Case in Brief

The petitioner, an NRI, had parked funds in an NRE fixed deposit with SBI. The bank, citing an alleged debt owed by a third party, unilaterally debited ₹19.90 lakh from the deposit. The customer challenged this in the High Court, arguing that he had no liability and that the bank had no authority to touch his funds without due process. The court agreed, noting that SBI failed to produce any document showing the customer's consent or a valid legal order.

The judgment emphasised that banks are custodians of public money and cannot act as judge and jury in recovery matters. The court directed SBI to refund the amount with interest, though the exact interest rate was not specified in the order.

Key Legal Takeaways for Depositors

This ruling reinforces a fundamental principle: banks cannot unilaterally adjust fixed deposits to recover third-party debts. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has repeatedly clarified that deposits are sacrosanct and can only be adjusted with the depositor's explicit consent or under a court order. SBI's action violated both the RBI's directives and the Indian Contract Act.

  • Consent is non-negotiable: No bank can debit your FD without your written authorisation.
  • Court order required for third-party claims: If a third party claims your deposit, the bank must obtain a court order, not act unilaterally.
  • Interest on wrongful deduction: Courts can and do award interest on illegally withheld amounts, as seen here.
  • Regulatory oversight: The RBI's Banking Ombudsman scheme provides a quick redressal channel for such grievances.

Pros and Cons: What This Means for Bank Customers

  • Pros:
    • Strengthens depositor rights against arbitrary bank actions.
    • Sets a precedent for other courts to follow.
    • Encourages banks to follow due process, reducing harassment.
    • Highlights the importance of RBI's consumer protection framework.
  • Cons:
    • Legal recourse is time-consuming and expensive for the average customer.
    • Banks may still attempt such deductions, hoping customers won't challenge them.
    • Interest awarded may not fully compensate for the loss of liquidity and mental agony.

What Should Depositors Do?

If you find an unauthorised debit from your fixed deposit, act immediately. First, file a written complaint with the bank's branch manager and demand a copy of the transaction details. If the bank fails to resolve it within 30 days, escalate to the RBI Banking Ombudsman under the Integrated Ombudsman Scheme. Maintain all correspondence and avoid verbal assurances. For large amounts, consult a lawyer specialising in banking law—the SBI case shows that courts are willing to hold banks accountable.

This ruling is a wake-up call for banks to tighten their internal controls. For depositors, it is a reminder that vigilance and legal awareness are your best protection.
