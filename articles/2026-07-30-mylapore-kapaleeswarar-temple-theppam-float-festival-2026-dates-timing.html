Festival Overview and Significance

The annual Theppam Float Festival at Mylapore Kapaleeswarar Temple in Chennai is one of the most anticipated spiritual events for devotees of Lord Shiva. Scheduled for 2026, the festival transforms the temple tank into a stage for a grand procession where the deity is taken on a beautifully decorated float.

Known locally as Mylai Kapali Temple, this historic shrine draws thousands of devotees during the Teppam festival. The event is not just a visual spectacle but a deep-rooted tradition that has been celebrated for centuries.

History and Spiritual Meaning

The Theppam festival traces its origins to ancient Tamil traditions where water bodies were considered sacred conduits for divine blessings. The float, or theppam, carries the idol of Lord Shiva (as Kapaleeswarar) and his consort Goddess Parvathi (as Karpagambal) around the temple tank.

Devotees believe that witnessing the float procession washes away sins and brings prosperity. The festival typically falls in the Tamil month of Thai (January-February), aligning with the star of Thiruvathirai, which is considered auspicious for Lord Shiva.

Key Rituals and Celebrations

The festival spans several days, with the main float procession occurring on the final evening. The temple tank is illuminated with lamps, and the air fills with the sound of devotional music and chants.

Priests perform special pujas before the float is set afloat, and devotees offer prayers, flowers, and coconuts. The sight of the decorated float gliding over the water under a canopy of lights is a deeply moving experience for participants.

Dates and Timings for 2026

While temple authorities have not yet confirmed exact dates for the 2026 Theppam festival, it is traditionally observed in late January or early February. Devotees are advised to check the official temple website or local announcements closer to the event.

Typically, the float procession begins in the evening, around 6 PM, and continues for several hours. The temple remains open from early morning to late night during the festival days, with special darshan arrangements for devotees.

How to Participate

The Mylapore Kapaleeswarar Temple is located in the heart of Chennai, easily accessible by road and public transport. Devotees can attend the festival free of charge, though special seating areas may be available for a fee.

It is advisable to arrive early to secure a good view of the tank. The temple authorities often arrange for additional parking and crowd management measures during the festival.

As the 2026 dates approach, devotees can look forward to a spiritually uplifting experience that blends faith, tradition, and community celebration at one of Chennai's oldest temples.
