Renukaswamy murder: Two co-accused seek approver status

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two co-accused in the Renukaswamy murder case have filed petitions to become approvers.
  • The move could provide crucial evidence for the prosecution.
  • The court is yet to rule on the petitions.

Fresh Turn in Murder Case

In a significant development in the Renukaswamy murder case, two co-accused individuals have approached the court seeking to turn approvers. The petitions were filed before the designated court in Bengaluru on Friday, marking a potential shift in the trial's trajectory.

The accused, whose names have not been officially disclosed, have expressed willingness to testify against the primary suspects. If accepted, they would become state witnesses, a move that could bolster the prosecution's case with first-hand accounts of the alleged crime.

Legal Implications of Approver Status

Under Indian criminal law, an approver is a co-accused who is granted pardon by the court in exchange for a full and truthful disclosure of all facts within their knowledge. This mechanism is often used to unravel complex conspiracies where direct evidence is scarce.

Legal experts suggest that the court will scrutinise the credibility and materiality of the proposed testimony before granting approval. The judge will also assess whether the accused have made a clean breast of their involvement, as any concealment could lead to the revocation of the pardon.

Case Background

The Renukaswamy murder case has been under investigation since the body was discovered under circumstances that pointed to foul play. The police have already arrested several individuals, including the prime accused, and charge sheets have been filed in the matter.

The case has drawn attention due to the nature of the alleged crime, though specific details of the incident remain under judicial seal. The court has been hearing arguments from both the defence and prosecution, with the trial expected to proceed once all preliminary motions are settled.

Next Steps in Court

The bench has not yet announced a date for the hearing on the approver petitions. Meanwhile, the prosecution is expected to file its response, and the defence counsels for the main accused are likely to oppose the move, arguing that such testimony could be coerced or unreliable.

Observers note that if the court allows the two co-accused to turn approvers, it could significantly shorten the trial timeline and expose the alleged conspiracy's inner workings. However, the decision will hinge on the judiciary's assessment of the evidence's integrity.

As the legal process unfolds, all eyes will be on the court's ruling, which could set a precedent for how similar applications are treated in high-profile murder cases in Karnataka.
