Section 66A: Why India's Obsolete Cyber Law Lingers

📅 2026-08-31 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Section 66A has not been used since 2019, raising questions about its necessity.
  • The Supreme Court has not yet ruled on its validity, leaving the law in a legal limbo.
  • Experts argue that the law is outdated and needs to be revised or repealed.

The Ghost of a Law

Section 66A of the Information Technology Act, 2000, has been a specter in India's cyber laws since its inception. Dubbed the 'dead law' by critics, it has not been invoked in any case since 2019, leaving many to question its relevance in today's digital landscape.

The law, which criminalizes sending offensive messages through communication services, was a response to the rise of online communication and social media. However, as digital platforms evolved, the law struggled to keep pace, becoming increasingly outdated.

The Supreme Court's Silent Treatment

The Supreme Court has not yet ruled on the validity of Section 66A, leaving the law in a legal limbo. This inaction has led to a situation where the law continues to exist on paper, but is not being enforced or applied in real-world scenarios.

Critics argue that the Supreme Court's silence is a missed opportunity to address the law's issues. They believe that the Court should have either struck down the law or made necessary amendments to align it with modern digital realities.

Expert Opinions and Critical Analysis

Legal experts and cyber law specialists have varying opinions on the future of Section 66A. Some argue that the law should be repealed, as it is no longer effective in curbing online harassment. Others believe that the law should be revised to better reflect the current digital landscape.

One expert, who requested anonymity, stated that the law is a 'relic' of the past. They argued that it is time for India to move on from this outdated law and focus on more effective measures to combat online harassment.

The Broader Impact

The continued existence of Section 66A has broader implications for India's digital ecosystem. It sends a message that the government and the judiciary are not keeping pace with the rapid changes in technology. This can erode public trust in the legal system and discourage innovation in the digital space.

Moreover, the law's inaction can have a chilling effect on free speech and expression. If a law is not enforced, it can be seen as ineffective, and people may be less likely to take it seriously. This can lead to a culture of impunity, where online harassment and abuse go unchecked.

Way Forward

There are several ways forward for Section 66A. The government could consider repealing the law, as it is no longer effective. Alternatively, the law could be revised to better reflect the current digital landscape. The Supreme Court could also play a role in this process, by either striking down the law or making necessary amendments.

In the meantime, it is crucial for the government and the judiciary to stay ahead of the curve and adapt to the changing digital landscape. This will not only ensure the effectiveness of cyber laws but also foster a culture of innovation and trust in the digital space.
