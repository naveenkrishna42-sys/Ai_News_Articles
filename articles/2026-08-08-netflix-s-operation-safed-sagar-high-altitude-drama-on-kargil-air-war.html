Netflix's 'Operation Safed Sagar': High-Altitude Drama on Kargil Air War

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Netflix series 'Operation Safed Sagar' focuses on the Indian Air Force's role in the 1999 Kargil War.
  • The production employed 4,000 people daily and cost Rs 215 crore, making it one of India's most expensive web series.
  • Reviews note strong performances by Siddharth and Jimmy, though some critics say it falls back on familiar jingoistic tropes.
  • The series has sparked renewed interest in the IAF's Operation Safed Sagar, a lesser-known chapter of the conflict.

A Rs 215 Crore Bet on the Skies

Netflix has reportedly spent Rs 215 crore on its upcoming series 'Operation Safed Sagar', a high-octane retelling of the Indian Air Force's role in the 1999 Kargil War. The production, which employed 4,000 people daily at its peak, is one of the most expensive web series ever made in India. It aims to bring to the screen a chapter of the conflict that is often overshadowed by the Army's ground operations.

The series takes its name from the IAF's classified air campaign, which was instrumental in dislodging Pakistani intruders from mountain peaks in the Dras, Batalik, and Turtuk sectors. While the Kargil War has been portrayed in films like 'LOC: Kargil' and 'Gunjan Saxena', this is the first major streaming project dedicated entirely to the air force's perspective.

Human Stories Amid the Missiles

Early reviews have praised the performances of Siddharth and Jimmy Sheirgill, who reportedly anchor the drama with grounded portrayals of pilots and commanders. The Hindu's review notes that the two actors 'lend heart to a racy wartime drama', suggesting the show balances technical spectacle with emotional heft.

The Telegraph India reports that Darjeeling veteran T.K. Singha was roped in as a consultant to ensure authenticity in depicting the IAF's operations. Singha, who served in the region, helped the creators understand the logistical nightmares of flying sorties at altitudes exceeding 20,000 feet, where thin air and unpredictable weather made every mission a gamble.

Critics Question the Tone

Not all responses have been glowing. The Wire's review, titled 'Operation Safed Sagar Hints at Fatigue Around Lazy Jingoism, but Eventually Succumbs', argues that the series starts with nuance but slips into familiar jingoistic territory. The critic points out that while the show attempts to humanise Pakistani soldiers, it ultimately prioritises spectacle over substance.

This tension between commercial appeal and historical accuracy is a recurring challenge for war dramas. The production's scale—4,000 crew members daily, extensive VFX work, and real fighter jet sequences—suggests Netflix is betting on spectacle to draw viewers, but the critical response indicates that audiences may expect more than just aerial action.

Why This Story Matters Now

The Kargil War remains a defining moment in India's post-independence military history. Operation Safed Sagar, in particular, marked the first time the IAF used precision-guided munitions in combat, a tactical shift that had long-term implications for the force's capabilities. Yet, for many young Indians, this history is a footnote in school textbooks.

By bringing this chapter to a global streaming platform, the series could educate a new generation about the air force's sacrifices. It also arrives at a time when streaming giants are increasingly looking to India for stories that resonate both domestically and internationally, with war dramas proving to be a reliable genre.

The series is expected to premiere later this year, with Netflix yet to confirm an exact date. As the streamer ramps up its Indian originals slate, all eyes will be on whether this high-altitude gamble pays off critically and commercially.

For now, the conversation shifts to post-release reception and whether the show can sustain interest beyond its opening weekend. The next few weeks will reveal if 'Operation Safed Sagar' soars or stalls in the crowded skies of Indian streaming.
