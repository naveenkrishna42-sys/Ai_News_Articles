Vikram Sahney's Punjab role: BJP's lone AAP MP at Santosh's events

📅 2026-08-26 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vikram Sahney, ex-AAP MP, alone from his party at BJP leader BL Santosh's Punjab events
  • Santosh's Punjab visit includes zonal meetings and anti-drug yatra launches
  • BJP's strategy to boost Sikh votes amid Punjab's political turmoil

Vikram Sahney's Presence at Santosh's Events

Vikram Sahney, a former Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) MP, has become the sole representative of his party at the events organized by BJP leader B.L. Santosh in Punjab. This development has sparked discussions about the BJP's strategy to engage with Sikh voters, who have been a significant part of the AAP's support base.

Santosh's Punjab Visit

B.L. Santosh, a prominent leader from the BJP, has embarked on a two-day visit to Punjab. During his visit, he is scheduled to hold zonal meetings and launch anti-drug yatras in all 117 segments of Punjab. These events are part of the BJP's broader strategy to counter the influence of the AAP and the Congress in Punjab.

BJP's Sikh Outreach Strategy

The BJP's decision to engage with Sikh voters, particularly through the presence of ex-AAP MP Vikram Sahney, is seen as a strategic move. The party aims to tap into the Sikh community's discontent with the AAP and the Congress, which have been accused of neglecting the community's interests.

Punjab's Political Turmoil

Punjab's political landscape is currently in a state of flux. The recent debacles faced by the Congress and the AAP have left a vacuum that the BJP is looking to fill. The party's efforts to boost Sikh votes are part of a larger strategy to regain influence in the state.

Expert Analysis

Political analyst Amritpal Singh believes that the BJP's strategy to engage with Sikh voters is a calculated move. He notes that the party is keeping the alliance option open, indicating that it is not ruling out the possibility of forming a government with other parties. Singh also points out that the BJP's focus on Sikh issues is a reflection of the community's growing influence in Punjab's politics.
