Allahabad HC Seeks Reply on Plea to Bar Entry, 'Kar Seva' at Krishna Janmabhoomi Site

📅 2026-07-18 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Allahabad High Court seeks reply on plea to prohibit entry and 'Kar Seva' at the disputed Krishna Janmabhoomi site.
  • The plea is part of the ongoing legal battle between Hindu and Muslim litigants over the Mathura complex.
  • The hearing has been adjourned, with the Supreme Court also deferring its proceedings on a related lead lawsuit.

The Allahabad High Court on Friday sought a response from relevant parties on a petition that demands a complete ban on public entry and any 'Kar Seva' (voluntary service) at the disputed Krishna Janmabhoomi-Shahi Idgah Mosque complex in Mathura.

The court has now adjourned the hearing on the matter, pushing the next date of proceedings to a later period. The petition, filed by Hindu litigants, argues that allowing unrestricted access or religious service at the site could alter the status quo and potentially lead to law and order issues.

The Core of the Dispute

The Krishna Janmabhoomi-Shahi Idgah Mosque dispute is one of India's most sensitive property battles. Hindu petitioners claim the 17th-century Shahi Idgah Mosque was built on the birthplace of Lord Krishna, demanding the land be handed over to them. Muslim parties, represented by the mosque committee, maintain that the land was legally donated by a king and that the mosque has stood there for centuries.

The latest plea specifically seeks to prevent any 'Kar Seva'—a term that evokes the controversial demolition of the Babri Masjid in 1992—at the Mathura site. The petitioners argue that such activities would violate existing court orders and disrupt communal harmony.

Legal Proceedings Gain Momentum

The Allahabad High Court's decision to seek replies comes amid multiple legal fronts in the case. Separately, the Supreme Court of India has also deferred the hearing on the Krishna Janmabhoomi dispute. The apex court was scheduled to hear a petition from Hindu litigants who are seeking an agreement on which lawsuit should be treated as the lead case among the several filed over the Mathura property.

This procedural step is crucial because it will determine the pace and direction of the entire litigation. The Supreme Court's deferment indicates that both sides are still negotiating the framework for the main trial. The next hearing in the Supreme Court is expected to take place after the summer break.

What is 'Kar Seva' and Why It Matters

The demand for a ban on 'Kar Seva' is a red flag for the courts. In legal terms, 'Kar Seva' refers to voluntary physical labour, often undertaken by religious groups for construction or renovation. In the context of the Ram Janmabhoomi-Babri Masjid dispute, it became a political flashpoint that led to widespread violence.

By seeking a specific injunction against it, the petitioners are trying to ensure that no unilateral action is taken on the ground while the case is sub judice. The mosque committee has supported this stance, arguing that any such activity would be illegal and provocative.

What Happens Next

The Allahabad High Court has given the respondents time to file their counter-affidavits. The next hearing in the High Court is scheduled for after the Supreme Court clarifies the lead lawsuit issue. All eyes are now on the apex court's decision in August, which will set the stage for the main trial in Mathura.
