World Sjögren's Day 2026: Know the Early Symptoms and Why Awareness Matters

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • World Sjögren's Day is observed annually on July 23 to raise awareness about the autoimmune disease.
  • The 2026 theme focuses on reducing misdiagnosis, as symptoms are often mistaken for other conditions.
  • Early symptoms include dry eyes, dry mouth, and joint pain, which can affect multiple organ systems.

A Day for Awareness

World Sjögren's Day is observed every year on July 23, marking the birthday of Swedish ophthalmologist Henrik Sjögren, who first identified the condition. This year, the day carries a sharper focus: cutting down the years of misdiagnosis that patients often endure.

Sjögren's disease is a chronic autoimmune disorder. It primarily attacks the body's moisture-producing glands, but its reach can extend far beyond dry eyes and a dry mouth. For many, the journey to a correct diagnosis is long and frustrating.

The 2026 Theme: More Than Dry Eyes

This year's global campaign, spearheaded by organizations including the Sjögren's Foundation and supported by pharmaceutical companies like Amgen, carries the message “More Than Dry Eyes.” The aim is to broaden public and medical understanding of the disease's many faces.

Fatigue, joint pain, and organ involvement are common but often dismissed. The campaign pushes for earlier recognition of these systemic symptoms, which can mimic other conditions like lupus or rheumatoid arthritis. Reducing misdiagnosis is the core goal for 2026.

Early Symptoms You Should Not Ignore

The hallmark symptoms are persistent dryness. Patients often report a gritty sensation in the eyes, as if sand is lodged under the lids, and a constant need to sip water to swallow or speak comfortably.

But the disease doesn't stop there. Many experience profound fatigue, swelling in the joints, and a dry cough. Some develop rashes or nerve pain. Because these symptoms overlap with other illnesses, Sjögren's is frequently missed in its early stages.

Why Diagnosis Takes So Long

Studies show the average time from symptom onset to diagnosis can exceed three years. A comprehensive review published in Cureus highlights unmet needs, diagnostic challenges, and emerging therapeutic strategies. The lack of a single definitive test is a major hurdle.

Doctors often rely on a combination of blood tests for specific antibodies, eye exams, and biopsies of the lip. Even then, symptoms can be dismissed as allergies or stress. The 2026 campaign pushes for better clinician education to shorten this diagnostic odyssey.

Effective Ways to Manage the Condition

There is no cure, but symptoms can be managed. Artificial tears and saliva substitutes offer relief for dryness. Prescription drugs like pilocarpine can stimulate saliva and tear production.

For systemic symptoms, doctors may prescribe hydroxychloroquine or immunosuppressants. Lifestyle changes also help: staying hydrated, using humidifiers, and practicing good oral hygiene to prevent dental decay. Patients are advised to work closely with a rheumatologist.

What Comes Next

Research into better treatments is accelerating. New biologic drugs targeting specific immune pathways are in clinical trials. The hope is that future World Sjögren's Days will mark not just awareness, but clearer diagnostic paths and more effective therapies for the millions living with this condition in India and worldwide.
