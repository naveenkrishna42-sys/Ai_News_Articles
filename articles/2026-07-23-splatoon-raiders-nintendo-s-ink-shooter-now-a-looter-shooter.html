Splatoon Raiders: Nintendo’s Ink-Shooter Now a Looter Shooter

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Splatoon Raiders is a single-player looter shooter, a first for the series.
  • Nintendo Switch 2 exclusive shifts focus from multiplayer to solo treasure hunts.
  • Early reviews praise the new gameplay loop and deeper lore integration.

Nintendo has taken a bold step with the latest entry in its colourful ink-shooting franchise. Splatoon Raiders, a title built from the ground up for the Nintendo Switch 2, abandons the series’ traditional turf-war multiplayer in favour of a single-player looter shooter experience. Early impressions suggest the gamble may pay off.

A new formula for an old favourite

Instead of four-on-four ink battles, Raiders drops players into sprawling, loot-filled levels. The objective is simple: hunt for treasure, blast enemies, and collect ever-better gear. The core movement and ink mechanics remain, but the loop now revolves around upgrading weapons and abilities rather than covering the map in paint.

“The series has always had the bones of a great action game,” one early review notes. “Now those bones have meat on them.” The Switch 2’s improved hardware allows for denser environments and smoother performance, giving the single-player campaign room to breathe.

Why the shift matters

Nintendo has long kept Splatoon focused on competitive online play. Raiders breaks that pattern entirely. The new structure means players can progress at their own pace, grinding for rare drops and customising their loadout without the pressure of a live match.

Loot rarity tiers — common, rare, and legendary — add a layer of strategy. A well-timed special weapon can clear a room, but only if you’ve invested the time to find its upgrade path. The result is a game that rewards patience and exploration as much as reflexes.

Lore meets gameplay

Fans who followed the series’ cryptic sunken scrolls will find plenty to sink their teeth into. Raiders weaves its narrative directly into missions, with collectible lore entries explaining the world’s post-apocalyptic history. Polygon has noted that the game offers “all the Splatoon lore you need to know” for newcomers and veterans alike.

Crunchyroll’s overview trailer highlighted the game’s treasure-hunting theme, showing Inklings diving into ruins and vaults. The single-player focus lets the story take centre stage without the distraction of competitive matchmaking.

Early reception

Mashable called the title “single-player fun,” while Nintendo’s own “Ask the Developer” series has detailed the design philosophy behind the shift. The consensus is clear: Raiders isn’t just a spin-off, but a legitimate evolution of the franchise.

Critics have pointed out that the looter shooter genre is crowded. But Nintendo’s signature polish and the unique ink-based combat system give Raiders a distinct identity. The Switch 2 exclusive status also means it’s a system seller for Nintendo’s new console.

What comes next

Nintendo has not announced post-launch content plans for Splatoon Raiders. Given the looter shooter model, expansions and seasonal events seem likely. For now, the game stands as a confident experiment — one that proves the series can thrive outside the arena.

All eyes are now on how the community reacts to a single-player Splatoon. If early buzz is any indication, the ink may have found a new canvas.
