Citizen drive maps Bengaluru stormwater drains to aid flood planning

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Citizens, researchers and data analysts are collaborating to map Bengaluru's stormwater drain network.
  • The initiative aims to create a comprehensive database to help predict and mitigate urban flooding.
  • The campaign highlights the role of community participation in urban environmental management.

Mapping the invisible network

Bengaluru's stormwater drains, a sprawling network that most residents rarely notice, are now the focus of a novel citizen-driven mapping campaign. The effort seeks to bring together residents, data scientists and academic researchers to document the city's drainage infrastructure in unprecedented detail.

Organisers say the project is not just about creating a map. It is about building a shared, open resource that can inform urban planning and flood response. The initiative comes as the city grapples with recurrent waterlogging during the monsoon, a problem often worsened by encroachments and poor maintenance of the drain network.

Citizens as data collectors

Volunteers have fanned out across Bengaluru's neighbourhoods, documenting the condition, width and flow of drains. They photograph culverts, note blockages and record the points where sewage appears to mix with stormwater. Each observation is tagged with location data, which is then fed into a central platform.

The campaign's organisers stress that local knowledge is vital. Long-time residents often know which stretches overflow after heavy rain and where drains have been illegally built over. By collecting this ground-level information, the project aims to create a more accurate picture than official records alone can provide.

Bridging research and ground reality

Researchers from city universities are involved in validating the data and analysing patterns. The goal is to identify critical trouble spots and understand how the drain network behaves under different rainfall intensities. This analysis could help civic agencies prioritise desilting and repair work.

For the researchers, the campaign is also a test of how participatory science can work in a large Indian city. The hope is that the methodology developed here can be replicated elsewhere, offering a template for other cities facing similar infrastructure challenges.

A database for the future

The collected data is being compiled into a publicly accessible database. Once complete, it will allow planners to simulate flood scenarios and assess the impact of proposed constructions near drain lines. It will also serve as a baseline for monitoring changes over time.

Officials from the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) have been briefed about the campaign, though their formal response is still awaited. The organisers say they are open to sharing the data with the civic body, provided it is used for public benefit.

Challenges and next steps

Mapping the entire network is a massive undertaking. Bengaluru's stormwater drains run to hundreds of kilometres, and not all are easily accessible. Volunteers have had to navigate narrow lanes, construction sites and, in some cases, hostile terrain. Still, the campaign has managed to cover a significant portion of the city in a few months.

There are also questions about data accuracy and how to keep the database updated. The organisers plan to train local community groups to continue the monitoring after the initial mapping phase ends. They are also exploring partnerships with tech companies to use satellite imagery and machine learning for cross-verification.

As the campaign gains momentum, its organisers hope it will nudge the city towards more transparent and inclusive infrastructure planning. The next phase involves releasing a preliminary report and holding public consultations with residents and ward-level officials.

The monsoon will be the first real test of the mapped data. If the predictions hold up, the campaign could prove that citizen science is not just an academic exercise but a practical tool for urban resilience.
