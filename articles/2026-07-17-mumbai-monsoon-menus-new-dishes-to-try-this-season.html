Mumbai monsoon menus: New dishes to try this season

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Restaurants across Mumbai are introducing monsoon specials featuring seasonal produce like corn, ginger, and local greens.
  • Popular additions include crispy corn fritters, bhutta chaat, and ginger-infused beverages.
  • Chefs are focusing on comfort food that pairs well with the rainy weather.

Mumbai's food scene is getting a seasonal makeover. As the monsoon clouds roll in, restaurants across the city are rolling out limited-edition menus that celebrate the flavours of the rains. From crispy corn fritters to spicy bhutta chaat and warm ginger teas, the new offerings are designed to complement the grey skies and steady drizzle.

Corn takes centre stage

Corn is the undisputed star of monsoon menus this year. Several outlets are serving bhutta — grilled corn on the cob — with a twist. Instead of the usual lime and chilli powder, chefs are experimenting with flavoured butters and spice blends.

At a popular bandra eatery, the bhutta is now available with a smoked paprika and garlic butter glaze. Another restaurant in colaba is offering a deconstructed version: kernels tossed in chaat masala and served in a cup with diced onions, tomatoes, and a squeeze of lemon.

Crispy corn fritters are also making a comeback. These golden, deep-fried bites are being paired with mint chutney and a tangy tamarind dip. Some places are even adding a sprinkling of cheese for extra indulgence.

Warm beverages and comfort food

The monsoon calls for warm, soothing drinks. Ginger tea is appearing on menus across the city, often with variations like honey-ginger or lemon-ginger. A cafe in powai has introduced a "monsoon special" ginger-tulsi infusion, which it describes as both calming and immune-boosting.

Comfort food is another big theme. Restaurants are bringing back old favourites like onion pakoras, masala chai, and piping hot soups. A marine drive restaurant is serving a creamy tomato soup with croutons, while a juhu outlet offers a spicy pumpkin and coconut milk soup.

Some places are also adding heartier options. A bandra-based italian restaurant has introduced a monsoon-exclusive pasta: penne in a light cream sauce with mushrooms, peas, and a hint of truffle oil. The dish is meant to be "rainy-day comfort on a plate," according to the chef.

Seasonal ingredients and local produce

Chefs are leaning into seasonal, locally sourced ingredients. The monsoon brings an abundance of leafy greens like amaranth and fenugreek, which are finding their way into salads and stir-fries. A restaurant in worli is serving a sauteed fenugreek and corn dish with a tempering of mustard seeds and curry leaves.

Mangoes are also still in season, and some eateries are using them in salads and desserts. A dessert menu at a colaba cafe features a mango-cardamom panna cotta, a light and creamy way to end a meal.

"The monsoon is a time to experiment with textures and flavours," says a chef at a leading mumbai restaurant. "We want our guests to feel warm and satisfied, not weighed down."

What to expect next

Most monsoon specials will be available until the end of august. Some restaurants are planning to rotate their menus weekly, introducing new dishes as the season progresses. Food lovers can expect more seasonal ingredients like jackfruit and raw banana to appear in coming weeks. The trend suggests that mumbai's culinary scene will continue to adapt to the weather, offering fresh reasons to dine out even when it rains.
