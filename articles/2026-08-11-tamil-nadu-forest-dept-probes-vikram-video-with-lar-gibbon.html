Tamil Nadu Forest Dept Probes Vikram Video With Lar Gibbon

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Video of actor Vikram with a Lar Gibbon has gone viral, prompting a probe by the Tamil Nadu Forest Department.
  • The endangered primate was traced to a farm in Perundurai, Erode, where two Lar gibbons are now to be confiscated.
  • One of the gibbons has reportedly died unreported, deepening the case and sparking public outrage over wildlife laws.

Viral Video Triggers Investigation

A video featuring Tamil actor Chiyaan Vikram with an endangered Lar Gibbon has triggered a formal investigation by the Tamil Nadu Forest Department. The clip, widely shared on social media, shows the actor in what appears to be a casual setting with the primate. The sight of the exotic animal in a private space has raised serious questions about the legality of its possession and the treatment of protected wildlife.

The Forest Department has not yet issued an official statement on the specifics of the probe. However, sources indicate that the department has initiated inquiries to trace the origin of the animal and verify whether the actor had any role in its custody. Officials have not confirmed whether Vikram has been summoned for questioning.

Lar Gibbons Traced to Erode Farm

Investigations have reportedly led authorities to a private farm in Perundurai, a town in Erode district. According to The Times of India, two Lar gibbons have been traced to this location and are to be confiscated by the Forest Department. The animals are believed to have been kept in captivity, which would be a direct violation of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972.

The Lar Gibbon, a species native to the northeastern parts of India and parts of Southeast Asia, is listed as endangered under the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) Red List. Keeping such species without proper authorization is illegal under Indian law, and the department's move to seize the animals signals a stricter enforcement stance.

One Animal Dies Unreported

The case has taken a grim turn with reports that one of the Lar gibbons has died, and the death was not reported to the authorities. The New Indian Express has reported that the surviving pair has been moved back to Erode as part of the ongoing investigation. The unreported death has intensified public scrutiny, with many demanding accountability for the alleged negligence.

Wildlife experts point out that exotic animals often suffer in captivity due to inadequate care and unsuitable environments. The lack of transparency around the death has prompted outrage, with social media users questioning whether the rich are above the law.

Public Outrage and Legal Questions

The controversy has sparked a broader debate about the enforcement of wildlife laws in India. NDTV quoted social media reactions under the headline, "Laws Don't Apply To The Rich," reflecting a growing sentiment that influential individuals often escape accountability. The actor, known for his roles in films like 'Vikram' and 'Cobra', has not yet responded to the allegations.

Legal experts note that if the investigation finds evidence of unlawful possession, the actor could face charges under the Wildlife Protection Act, which prescribes penalties ranging from fines to imprisonment. However, officials have not yet confirmed any formal charges against Vikram or the farm's owners.

The Forest Department's probe is still in its early stages, and the confiscation of the gibbons is expected to be completed in the coming days. The animals' relocation to a proper rehabilitation facility will likely be a key next step. Whether the department will summon Vikram for questioning remains unclear, but the case has already put the spotlight on the illegal trade of exotic animals in India.

As the investigation unfolds, activists are calling for stricter monitoring of private farms and residences for such wildlife. The incident may also prompt a review of how wildlife crimes are reported and prosecuted in the state.

The coming weeks will be crucial as the Forest Department decides on further legal action. All eyes are on whether this case will set a precedent for handling similar violations involving high-profile individuals.
