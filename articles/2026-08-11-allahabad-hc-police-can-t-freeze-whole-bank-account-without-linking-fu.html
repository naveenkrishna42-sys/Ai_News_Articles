Allahabad HC: Police Can't Freeze Whole Bank Account Without Linking Funds to Crime

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Court says freezing entire account violates fundamental rights under Article 300A.
  • Police must show specific link between funds and crime under BNSS Section 173.
  • Interim relief granted; full hearing scheduled in six weeks.

The Allahabad High Court has ruled that police cannot freeze an entire bank account unless they can establish a clear connection between the funds in the account and the alleged crime. The judgment, delivered under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), sets an important precedent for protecting citizens' property rights during criminal investigations.

Justice Rohit Ranjan Agarwal observed that freezing an entire account without demonstrating how the money is linked to the offence would amount to a violation of Article 300A of the Constitution, which guarantees the right to property. The Court emphasised that the power to freeze accounts under BNSS is not arbitrary and must be exercised only to the extent necessary for the investigation.

What the Court Said

The Court was hearing a petition challenging an order by a lower court that permitted the freezing of a bank account in connection with a criminal case. The petitioner argued that the entire account had been frozen even though the police had not shown that all the funds in it were related to the alleged offence.

Referring to the relevant provisions of the BNSS, the Court held that freezing is an exceptional measure and should be limited to the amount or portion of funds that are reasonably suspected to be tainted. The judge noted that a blanket freeze would cause undue hardship to the account holder and could disrupt legitimate financial transactions.

Implications for Investigation Procedures

The ruling clarifies that investigating agencies must now provide concrete evidence linking specific transactions or balances to the crime before seeking a freeze order. This is expected to reduce instances where entire accounts are immobilised, often leaving individuals and businesses unable to access their own money for months.

Legal experts point out that the judgment aligns with the broader scheme of the BNSS, which aims to balance investigative powers with individual rights. The Court also observed that if the police fail to establish a nexus between the funds and the crime, the freeze order would be legally unsustainable.

Interim Relief and Next Steps

In the present case, the High Court granted interim relief to the petitioner, allowing partial access to the frozen account while the matter is finally heard. The Court has scheduled the next hearing in six weeks, during which the police will have to justify the freezing of the entire account.

Until then, the account holder can operate the account to the extent permitted by the interim order, subject to any conditions imposed by the Court. The order also serves as a guideline for lower courts and police authorities across the state.

Broader Impact on Banking and Investigations

This judgment may prompt banks and law enforcement to adopt more nuanced procedures when dealing with suspected accounts. Instead of a blanket freeze, agencies might now be required to seek freezing of specific amounts that are directly linked to the alleged crime.

For ordinary citizens, the ruling offers reassurance that their bank accounts cannot be frozen arbitrarily. It reinforces the principle that the power to investigate cannot override fundamental rights without adequate justification.

As the case progresses, the final interpretation of BNSS provisions on freezing will be keenly watched by legal practitioners and law enforcement agencies alike. The High Court's stance is likely to influence similar matters across the country.
