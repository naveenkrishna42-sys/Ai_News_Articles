Ashish Yadav ends India's 10-year wait for U20 javelin medal with silver

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ashish Yadav clinched silver in men's javelin at the World Athletics U20 Championships.
  • This is India's first U20 javelin medal since Neeraj Chopra's gold in 2016.
  • The silver opens India's medal account at the ongoing championships.

Ashish Yadav has ended India's decade-long wait for a javelin medal at the World Athletics U20 Championships, clinching silver in the men's event on Saturday. The achievement marks the first time an Indian has stood on the podium in this discipline since Neeraj Chopra's historic gold in 2016.

The silver also opened India's medal account at the ongoing championships, giving the contingent a much-needed boost early in the competition. While details of the winning throw and the margin of defeat are yet to be officially confirmed, the result has already sparked celebrations back home.

A decade in the making

Neeraj Chopra's gold in 2016 had set a benchmark that remained unmatched for ten years. His rise from a junior champion to Olympic and world gold medallist had placed Indian javelin on the global map, but the junior circuit had not produced a similar breakthrough since.

Ashish's silver now bridges that gap. For a country that has grown accustomed to Chopra's consistency at the senior level, this result signals that the pipeline of young throwers is finally delivering.

What the silver means for Indian athletics

The U20 World Championships serve as a launching pad for future stars. Chopra's gold in the same event preceded his senior success, and Ashish's podium finish will inevitably draw comparisons. But the young thrower will be keen to carve his own path.

For the Indian athletics federation, the medal is a validation of its junior development programmes. With a growing pool of talent in throwing events, the hope is that Ashish's performance is not an isolated flash but part of a broader trend.

Next steps for Ashish

Ashish is expected to return to India in the coming days, where he will likely receive a hero's welcome. The Athletics Federation of India is yet to announce any formal felicitation, but the achievement is unlikely to go unnoticed.

His immediate focus will be on transitioning to the senior circuit, where the competition is fiercer and the distances required are significantly higher. The technical work, coaches say, will now shift to building consistency and endurance.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether Ashish will feature in upcoming senior meets, but his silver provides a strong case for early exposure. With the next Olympic cycle already underway, the timing could not be better.

As Indian athletics celebrates this long-awaited medal, all eyes will be on how Ashish Yadav builds on this momentum. The junior podium is a statement; the senior one will be the real test.
