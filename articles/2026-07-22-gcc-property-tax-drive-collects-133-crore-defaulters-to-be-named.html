GCC property tax drive collects ₹133 crore, defaulters to be named

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • GCC collected ₹133 crore through a special property tax recovery drive.
  • Defaulters' names will be published to increase compliance and transparency.
  • The drive targeted both residential and commercial property owners.

GCC's tax recovery drive yields ₹133 crore

The Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC) has collected ₹133 crore through a focused property tax recovery drive. Officials confirmed the figure on Wednesday, highlighting the success of recent enforcement measures.

The drive, which began earlier this month, targeted defaulters across all 15 zones of the city. Teams conducted door-to-door visits and issued final notices to property owners with outstanding dues.

Defaulters list to be made public

GCC commissioner said the civic body will soon publish the names of property tax defaulters. This step aims to create public pressure and encourage voluntary compliance.

“We have identified a large number of defaulters who have not paid taxes for years. Publishing their names will ensure accountability,” a senior official said.

The list will include details such as property address, zone, and amount due. Officials expect this move to prompt many defaulters to clear their dues before the list is released.

Focus on high-value properties

The recovery drive prioritised high-value residential and commercial properties. Teams used data from previous assessments to identify owners with significant arrears.

In many cases, officials issued notices under the Chennai City Municipal Corporation Act, which allows for attachment of property if dues remain unpaid. However, no such action has been taken yet.

Commercial properties contributed a major share of the collected amount. Many business owners paid up after receiving final notices, sources said.

Tax compliance remains a challenge

Property tax compliance has been a persistent issue for GCC. Despite multiple reminders and online payment options, many residents and businesses delay payments.

The corporation estimates that over ₹300 crore in property tax remains unpaid. The current drive has recovered nearly half of this amount, but officials acknowledge that more work is needed.

“We are using a combination of persuasion and enforcement. The aim is to ensure that everyone pays their fair share,” the official added.

What happens next

GCC plans to continue the drive for another month. Officials will also cross-check data with other government departments to identify properties that have evaded tax assessment.

The publication of the defaulters list is expected within the next few weeks. Property owners with pending dues are advised to clear them before the list is released to avoid public embarrassment and further legal action.
