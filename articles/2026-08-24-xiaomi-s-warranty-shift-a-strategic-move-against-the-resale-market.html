Xiaomi’s Warranty Shift: A Strategic Move Against the Resale Market

📅 2026-08-24 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Xiaomi formally ends warranty support for second-hand device buyers.
  • The policy change impacts battery protection plans and standard hardware coverage.
  • The move aims to consolidate brand control and curb unofficial service channels.

The End of Warranty Transfers

Xiaomi has officially tightened its service terms, explicitly excluding second-hand devices from its warranty and battery protection coverage. This policy shift marks a departure from the more flexible, albeit informal, service culture that often characterized the secondary market for budget-friendly smartphones in India.

For the average consumer, this means that a device’s warranty is now tethered strictly to the original purchaser. If you buy a Xiaomi phone from an online marketplace or a local refurbisher, you are effectively operating without a safety net from the manufacturer.

Why Xiaomi is Drawing a Line

The company is likely attempting to mitigate the operational costs associated with supporting hardware that has passed through multiple hands. Often, second-hand devices are subjected to unauthorized repairs or non-standard battery replacements, which can lead to safety risks and inflated service center liability.

By restricting warranties, Xiaomi is effectively forcing a clear distinction between "new" and "refurbished." This allows the brand to protect its margins while ensuring that only devices verified through its own supply chain receive priority support. It is a calculated move to discourage the grey market, which often undercuts official sales.

The Broader Market Impact

This policy change will likely ripple through the Indian resale market. Buyers who previously relied on the "remainder of warranty" as a selling point will find their devices losing value faster. Refurbished phone retailers will now have to factor in their own insurance costs, potentially driving up the price of used handsets.

  • Increased risk for individual buyers on peer-to-peer platforms.
  • Refurbished retailers will need to provide their own internal warranties.
  • Xiaomi gains better control over service center footfall and resource allocation.
  • The move may push users toward newer, entry-level models rather than high-end used ones.

Ultimately, this is a signal that Xiaomi is maturing its service model to prioritize brand integrity over market saturation. While it may frustrate bargain hunters, it aligns with a strategy aimed at standardizing the user experience. Whether this forces competitors to follow suit remains the key question for the mobile sector.
