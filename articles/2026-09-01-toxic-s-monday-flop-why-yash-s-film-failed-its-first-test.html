Toxic's Monday collections fall short of expectations

Yash's latest film Toxic faced a crucial test on its first Monday, and it failed to meet expectations. The film, which starred Kiara Advani and marked Yash's return to the big screen, earned just Rs 9.5 crore on its sixth day, falling short of the Rs 10 crore benchmark that many analysts had predicted.

This underperformance is significant because the Monday collections are often seen as a barometer for a film's commercial viability. If a film cannot cross the Rs 10 crore mark on its first Monday, it is often considered a sign of trouble ahead.

Kantara composer defends Yash's film

Kantara composer Ajaneesh Loknath has come to Yash's defense, stating that the audience of today would call even Lagaan ‘boring’ if it were released today. He argued that Toxic's underperformance is not a reflection of Yash's acting skills, but rather a result of the changing tastes of the audience.

Loknath's comments are interesting because they highlight the evolving nature of the Indian audience. What was considered a hit film a decade ago might not be as appealing today. This is a trend that Yash and other actors need to keep in mind as they plan their future projects.

Kichcha Sudeep stands by Yash amid negative reviews

Kichcha Sudeep, who played a key role in Toxic, has stood by Yash amid the film's negative reviews. He stated that he believed in Yash's acting and that the reviews were not a reflection of his performance.

Sudeep's comments are significant because they show that even in the face of criticism, Yash has support from his co-stars. This support can be crucial in helping Yash navigate the challenges that come with making a film.

Sumalatha urges Yash to focus on his career

Actor Sumalatha has urged Yash to focus on his career, not just Toxic's box office. She stated that Yash should not let the film's underperformance affect his confidence and that he should continue to take on challenging roles.

Sumalatha's comments are important because they highlight the need for actors to have a long-term perspective. While box office success is a key indicator of a film's commercial viability, it should not be the only factor that actors consider when choosing their projects.

The industry is dealing with a slowdown in ticket sales, and this is having a ripple effect on the release schedules of films. Toxic's underperformance could be a sign of things to come for the industry.
