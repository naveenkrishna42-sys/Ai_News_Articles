India to keep buying Russian crude despite US Senate sanctions bill

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • India will continue Russian crude purchases despite US Senate sanctions bill
  • Kpler analyst warns of supply risks for Indian refiners
  • Indian refiners expect costlier crude from September as sanctions loom

India is unlikely to scale down its purchases of Russian crude in the near term, even as the US Senate has passed a bill that could tighten sanctions on Moscow, according to sources familiar with the matter. The development comes at a time when global oil markets are already skittish, and Indian refiners are bracing for costlier imports from September.

The US Senate's nod to the sanctions bill has raised fresh concerns about the stability of crude supplies to India, which has emerged as one of the biggest buyers of Russian oil since the Ukraine conflict began. A Kpler analyst flagged the risk, noting that the new legislation could complicate payments, shipping, and insurance for Indian refiners reliant on Russian barrels.

Why India is sticking with Russian crude

Indian refiners have consistently defended their purchases of Russian crude, arguing that the discounts make commercial sense. State-run and private refiners have snapped up discounted barrels over the past two years, helping India manage its import bill while keeping domestic fuel prices in check.

Government sources indicate that New Delhi sees no immediate reason to alter its procurement strategy. The country's dependence on imports to meet over 85 per cent of its crude demand leaves little room for ideological posturing, they argue. Instead, the focus remains on securing the most cost-effective supplies available.

Diplomatic pressure from Washington has not translated into a policy shift so far. India has maintained a fine balance, condemning the conflict in principle while avoiding direct sanctions on Russia. That balancing act is likely to continue, even as the US legislative push intensifies.

The sanctions bill and its ripple effects

The US Senate's move is aimed at tightening the screws on Moscow, but its impact could spill over to buyers like India. Analysts warn that secondary sanctions—penalties on entities that facilitate Russian oil trade—could make banks and insurers wary of handling Indian crude transactions.

Kpler's analysis suggests that the risk to Indian crude supplies is real, though not immediate. The bill still needs to clear procedural hurdles and could face amendments before becoming law. Even then, waivers or carve-outs for key allies are possible, but nothing is guaranteed.

Indian refiners are already preparing for a tighter market. Reports indicate they are accelerating term deals and seeking alternative suppliers to hedge against potential disruptions. The September timeline is being watched closely, as that is when the new sanctions could start biting.

Refiners brace for pricier crude

The immediate pain for Indian refiners may come in the form of higher prices. If Russian crude becomes harder to source or insure, the discount that made it attractive could shrink. That would force refiners to turn to Middle Eastern or African grades, which typically command a premium.

Industry executives say that a shift away from Russian barrels would not happen overnight. Existing contracts and shipping arrangements provide some buffer. But the longer the sanctions threat persists, the more expensive it becomes to sustain the current supply mix.

Fuel prices at the pump in India have remained stable so far, but that could change if crude costs climb. The government has shown little appetite for cutting taxes on petrol and diesel, leaving refiners to absorb the hit. That could squeeze margins and, eventually, trickle down to consumers.

What to watch

The coming weeks will reveal whether the US bill moves closer to enactment and whether India secures any exemptions. Indian refiners are likely to keep buying Russian crude until the legal and logistical costs outweigh the benefits. The global oil market, meanwhile, will stay on edge, watching for any sign that Indian demand for Russian barrels is wavering.
