Christopher Nolan's 'The Odyssey' crosses Rs 100 crore in India within 6 days

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • The film crossed Rs 100 crore in India on Day 6 of its release.
  • Starring Matt Damon, the epic is based on Homer's ancient Greek poem.
  • The film has sparked renewed interest in the original text among audiences.
  • Scholars have raised questions about Nolan's interpretation of the source material.

Christopher Nolan's 'The Odyssey' has stormed past the Rs 100 crore mark at the Indian box office within six days of its release. The epic, starring Matt Damon, has become Nolan's fastest film to reach the milestone in the country.

The film's strong performance comes despite mixed reactions from academic circles. Several classicists have publicly questioned Nolan's adaptation of Homer's ancient Greek poem, pointing to deviations from the original text.

A darker hero emerges

Nolan's version presents a less triumphant, more introspective Odysseus. The director has chosen to focus on the psychological toll of the hero's decade-long journey home from the Trojan War.

This approach has divided audiences. Some praise the fresh perspective, while others argue the film strays too far from the epic's original spirit. The Conversation noted that Nolan's Odyssey 'reveals a darker, less triumphant heroism'.

Box office momentum

The film's Indian box office run has been consistent. Strong word-of-mouth and repeat viewings have helped maintain momentum through the first week.

Industry analysts expect the film to continue its strong run into the second weekend. The holiday season in several parts of the country has also contributed to higher footfall in theatres.

Global impact and cultural resurgence

Beyond the numbers, 'The Odyssey' has triggered a cultural phenomenon. The BBC reports that fans are being inspired to go back to the source material, with sales of Homer's epic seeing a noticeable uptick.

Euronews has described the film as making 'history at the global box office for Christopher Nolan', though specific global figures have not been confirmed by the studio.

Scholarly skepticism

Not everyone is celebrating. The AP reports that scholars are questioning Nolan's choices. Some argue the film takes liberties with the narrative structure and character motivations that fundamentally alter the meaning of the original work.

Nolan has not publicly responded to these criticisms. The director is known for keeping a low profile during a film's theatrical run.

What happens next will depend on how the film holds in the second week. With no major Hollywood releases scheduled for the next fortnight, 'The Odyssey' has a clear runway to add substantially to its Indian total.
