NALSAR students oppose CJI Surya Kant as convocation chief guest

📅 2026-08-10 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Students at NALSAR University of Law, Hyderabad, have objected to CJI Surya Kant being invited as the chief guest for the upcoming convocation.
  • The outgoing batch has asked the university administration to 'strongly reconsider' the invitation.
  • The students' concerns appear tied to the CJI's role in recent judicial decisions, though specific reasons have not been officially detailed.
  • The university has not yet responded publicly to the students' request.

Students raise objections

Outgoing students at NALSAR University of Law in Hyderabad have formally objected to the invitation extended to Chief Justice of India Surya Kant for their convocation ceremony. The students have urged the university administration to 'strongly reconsider' the decision, according to reports.

The objection has surfaced days before the scheduled event, triggering a debate within the campus over the appropriateness of the choice. While the exact reasons for the students' stand have not been publicly detailed, the move signals growing unease among the student body regarding the CJI's conduct in certain matters.

Context of the dissent

NALSAR, one of India's premier national law universities, has a history of students taking vocal positions on issues of judicial accountability and institutional integrity. The current objection appears to be rooted in the students' perception of recent judicial decisions or actions involving Chief Justice Surya Kant.

Neither the university nor the Chief Justice's office has issued an official response to the students' request. The lack of an immediate reaction suggests the administration may be weighing the implications of altering the convocation plan at this stage.

University's position unclear

The NALSAR administration has not yet confirmed whether it will accede to the students' demand. Past instances of student protests at the university have sometimes led to changes in guest lists, but the final call typically rests with the executive council.

Legal education circles are watching the development closely, as convocation chief guests are often chosen to inspire graduating batches. A public disagreement of this nature is rare and could set a precedent for how institutions engage with student dissent.

What students are demanding

The students have not called for a boycott or disruption of the ceremony, but have expressed their discomfort through a formal representation. They have asked the administration to reconsider the invitation, indicating a preference for an alternative chief guest who aligns more closely with their expectations of judicial conduct.

The demand has found resonance among alumni and faculty members, though opinions remain divided. Some argue that the CJI's presence would be an honour for the institution, while others support the students' right to voice their concerns.

Broader implications

This episode comes at a time when the judiciary's interactions with the legal community are under greater scrutiny. The students' move is being interpreted as a generational push for greater transparency and accountability from the highest judicial office.

It remains to be seen whether the university will engage in a dialogue with the students or stand by its original decision. The convocation is likely to proceed as scheduled, but the controversy has already cast a shadow over the proceedings.

As the date approaches, all eyes will be on NALSAR's response to the students' appeal, and whether the CJI's visit will ultimately go ahead as planned.
