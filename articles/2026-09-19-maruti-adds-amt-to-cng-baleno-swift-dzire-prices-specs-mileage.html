Maruti Adds AMT to CNG Baleno, Swift & Dzire: Prices, Specs, Mileage

Maruti's CNG-AMT Combo Arrives After Years of Buyer Demand

Maruti Suzuki has begun offering its automated manual transmission (AMT) with factory-fitted S-CNG on the Baleno, Swift and Dzire. Until now, every Maruti CNG variant in these model lines was manual-only, forcing high-mileage buyers to choose between fuel economy and clutch-free driving. The company has now closed that gap, and it is targeting 1 million green vehicle sales in FY27, a target that leans heavily on CNG and hybrid volumes.

The move matters commercially. CNG accounts for a large share of Maruti's compact car sales in Delhi-NCR, Gujarat, Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh, where pump density is high and petrol prices remain above Rs 100 a litre in several cities. Adding an automatic to that mix directly attacks the biggest objection fleet and family buyers raise at the dealership.

Prices and Positioning: What Each Model Costs

Launch pricing places the Baleno CNG AMT at Rs 8.32 lakh and the Dzire CNG AMT at Rs 8.53 lakh (ex-showroom). The Swift CNG AMT slots below both. Compared with the equivalent manual CNG trims, expect a premium in the Rs 40,000-50,000 band, consistent with Maruti's petrol AMT pricing. Against a diesel automatic rival, the gap is wider, but diesel automatics in this segment are effectively extinct.

Real-World Performance and Running Costs

Maruti's 1.2-litre K-series engine loses a small amount of peak output in CNG mode, a trade-off present across the S-CNG range. In traffic, the AMT's creep function and hill-hold assist make the car easier to live with than a manual, though the gearbox still pauses between shifts under hard acceleration. On open roads, drivers who use the manual mode and paddle-free tiptronic slot will find the powertrain adequate rather than quick.

Running costs remain the strongest argument. At current CNG rates in most metros, a Baleno CNG AMT costs roughly Rs 2.2-2.6 per kilometre, against Rs 6-7 per kilometre on petrol. A buyer covering 1,500 km a month can recover the CNG premium within 18-24 months. The catch is the tank: boot capacity drops by 60-70 litres, which matters for airport runs and family holidays.

Ownership Practicalities Buyers Should Weigh

  • Pros: Lowest running cost in the segment; no clutch pedal in stop-go traffic; factory-fitted and warrantied CNG kit; strong resale demand for CNG Marutis in NCR and Gujarat; wide service network.
  • Cons: Reduced boot space; AMT shift pauses on overtakes; slower acceleration in CNG mode; CNG pump queues in peak hours; first valve-clearance check due around 1,500-2,000 km.
  • Value verdict: The AMT premium pays back quickly for high-mileage users, but low-mileage buyers under 800 km a month are better served by a petrol AMT.

Who Should Buy, and Who Should Wait

This launch is aimed squarely at cab aggregators, sales fleets and families clocking over 1,200 km a month in CNG-heavy cities. For them, the Baleno and Dzire CNG AMT are the most sensible automatic compact cars on sale today on cost per kilometre. Buyers who rarely use CNG, or who need a full-size boot, should stick to petrol AMT or consider a larger sedan. Maruti's challenge now is supply: CNG AMT waiting periods will decide whether this becomes a volume driver or a paperwork headline.
