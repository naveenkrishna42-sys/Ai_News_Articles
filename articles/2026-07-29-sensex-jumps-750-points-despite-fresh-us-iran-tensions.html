Sensex Jumps 750 Points Despite Fresh US-Iran Tensions

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex surged over 750 points, Nifty crossed 24,220 in early trade.
  • IT and consumer stocks led the rally, offsetting concerns from US-Iran tensions.
  • Markets opened strong, with Sensex rising over 820 points at one point.
  • Analysts cite buying in beaten-down sectors and positive global cues.
  • Fed policy decisions later this week remain a key trigger for further direction.

Indian equity benchmarks staged a sharp recovery on Wednesday, with the Sensex jumping over 750 points in early trade, despite fresh geopolitical tensions between the United States and Iran. The rally was broad-based, led by IT and consumer stocks, as investors shrugged off overnight concerns.

The BSE Sensex opened over 650 points higher and surged past the 750-point mark within minutes, while the NSE Nifty 50 index topped the 24,220 level, recovering from recent losses. At the day's peak, the Sensex was up more than 820 points.

IT and Consumer Stocks Lead the Charge

Information technology shares were among the top gainers, with the Nifty IT index rising over 2 percent. Heavyweights like Infosys, TCS, and HCL Technologies saw strong buying interest. Consumer goods stocks also rallied, with Hindustan Unilever and ITC contributing significantly to the index gains.

Banking stocks, which had been under pressure in recent sessions, also bounced back. The Nifty Bank index moved higher, supported by gains in HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Kotak Mahindra Bank. Analysts said the buying was driven by value hunting after a recent correction.

Geopolitical Tensions Take a Back Seat

The rally came even as fresh tensions between the US and Iran threatened to roil global markets. Reports of increased military posturing in the Gulf region had weighed on Asian peers early in the day, but Indian markets appeared to look past the development.

Traders said domestic factors, including expectations of strong corporate earnings and a rebound in foreign fund inflows, were providing support. The rupee also strengthened against the US dollar, adding to the positive sentiment.

Global Cues and Fed Watch

Global markets were mixed, with US futures trading flat to slightly positive. Investors are now focused on the US Federal Reserve's policy decision, due later this week. The outcome is expected to influence capital flows into emerging markets like India.

Crude oil prices, a key concern for India given its import dependence, remained elevated but did not spike further, providing some relief. The benchmark Brent crude was trading around $82 per barrel.

Market breadth was positive, with over 1,800 stocks advancing on the BSE against 1,100 declining. Broader indices also outperformed, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices gaining over 1 percent each.

Analysts caution that the rally could be short-lived if geopolitical tensions escalate or if the Fed signals a prolonged tight monetary policy. However, for now, the bulls seem to have regained control.

All eyes will now be on the Fed's rate decision and commentary later this week, which could set the tone for markets in the coming days.
