RTI reveals 10 injured by pellets during Jantar Mantar CJP protest

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • RTI response reveals at least 10 people were injured by pellets during the CJP protest at Jantar Mantar on July 20.
  • Two Delhi hospitals treated protesters for pellet, shot, or projectile injuries.
  • Delhi Police has sought details from CRPF regarding the use of pellet guns at the protest.

At least 10 people were injured by pellets during the protests at Jantar Mantar on July 20, according to a Right to Information (RTI) response. The RTI was filed by a former Trinamool Congress MP, and its findings have now brought the issue of pellet gun use during the protest back into focus.

The response, obtained from two Delhi hospitals, confirms that protesters were treated for what were described as 'pellet, shots or projectile injuries'. The exact nature of the weapons used has not been officially confirmed by authorities yet.

RTI findings and hospital records

The RTI response lists at least 10 individuals who sought treatment at two Delhi hospitals on the day of the protest. The hospitals reported injuries consistent with pellet guns, which are often used for crowd control in sensitive areas.

The former TMC MP who filed the RTI has cited these records as evidence that pellet guns were used against protesters. The response does not specify the severity of the injuries or the current condition of those treated.

Delhi Police seeks CRPF details

Meanwhile, Delhi Police has reportedly sought details from the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) regarding the use of pellet guns at the student protest. This request suggests an internal review of the circumstances surrounding the protest is underway.

The CRPF is often deployed for crowd control in the national capital, and its role in the July 20 protest is now under scrutiny. Police officials have not yet commented publicly on the RTI findings or the nature of the inquiry.

Context of the protest

The Jantar Mantar protest on July 20 was part of a larger movement by the Campaign for Justice and Peace (CJP), which has been demanding action on a range of issues. The protest drew students and activists, and the day saw heightened tensions between demonstrators and security forces.

The use of pellet guns has been a contentious issue in India, with critics pointing to the risk of severe injuries, including loss of eyesight. However, security agencies maintain that pellets are a less-lethal option compared to firearms.

What happens next

The RTI response and the police's request for CRPF details could lead to a deeper review of crowd-control protocols. It remains to be seen whether the findings will prompt any official statement or a change in the use of pellet guns at future protests.

Watch for further developments from Delhi Police and the CRPF, as well as any response from the CJP organisers. The issue is likely to be raised in Parliament when it is in session.
