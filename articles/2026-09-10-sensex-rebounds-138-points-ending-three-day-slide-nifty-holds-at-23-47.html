Sensex Rebounds 138 Points, Ending Three-Day Slide; Nifty Holds at 23,478

Market Recap: A Turnaround After Three Days of Decline

The BSE Sensex closed at 71,862, up 138 points, ending a three‑day losing streak that saw the index slide over 300 points in total. Meanwhile, the NSE Nifty 50 held steady at 23,478, barely moving from its previous close. The modest rally was anchored by a softer global risk‑off sentiment and a surprise dip in crude oil prices.

Volume on both exchanges was moderate, with the Sensex recording a turnover of roughly ₹1.2 lakh crore, indicating cautious participation rather than a full‑blown buying frenzy. The market’s resilience suggests that investors are waiting for clearer direction from upcoming macro data.

What Drove the Reversal?

Macro Backdrop: Global Cues and Domestic Data

U.S. Treasury yields eased after the Federal Reserve signaled a possible pause in rate hikes, easing the pressure on emerging‑market equities. In India, the latest RBI policy meeting left the repo rate unchanged, reinforcing the view that monetary conditions remain accommodative.

Additionally, the RBI’s latest inflation report showed a marginal dip to 4.6% YoY, well within the 4‑6% target band. Lower inflation expectations have buoyed sentiment, especially in interest‑sensitive sectors such as real estate and consumer durables.

Sector Performance: Winners and Laggers

Financials led the rally, with HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank gaining 1.2% and 1.0% respectively, driven by better‑than‑expected loan growth. Information technology stocks also performed well; TCS and Infosys each added around 0.8% as foreign institutional investors (FIIs) increased their exposure.

Conversely, energy stocks lagged due to the dip in crude prices, with Reliance Industries slipping 0.5%. Metals remained flat, reflecting uncertainty over global demand.

Implications for Investors

Short‑Term Strategies

Traders may focus on the momentum in financials and IT, using intra‑day strategies to capture the next wave of buying. The modest turnover suggests that large‑cap stocks will continue to dominate, while mid‑caps could see higher volatility.

Risk‑averse investors might consider hedging with index futures, especially if the market tests the 71,500 support level again. A breach could reopen the short‑term downtrend.

Long‑Term Outlook

Fundamentally, India’s growth trajectory remains strong, with GDP projected at 7.2% for FY27. The equity market’s valuation is still attractive compared to global peers, offering a compelling entry point for long‑term portfolios.

However, investors should monitor fiscal deficits and global trade dynamics, as any adverse shift could reignite volatility.
