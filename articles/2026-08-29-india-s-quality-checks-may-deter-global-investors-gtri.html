India's quality checks may deter global investors: GTRI

📅 2026-08-29 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's stringent quality checks may deter global investors
  • Government is working to ease these checks to attract more foreign investment
  • GTRI's report highlights the potential impact of these checks on India's manufacturing sector

India's Drive for Global Factories Faces Quality Check Hurdles

India's ambitious plan to attract global factories is facing a potential roadblock: its stringent quality checks. According to a report by the Global Trade Research Initiative (GTRI), these checks may be putting off foreign investors, despite the government's efforts to woo them.

Government Efforts to Attract Investors

The Indian government is actively pursuing various strategies to attract foreign investment. The Economic Times Centre has been going all-out to woo Japanese companies, while Piyush Goyal, the Minister for Commerce and Industry, has been in talks with Japan about increasing basmati rice exports from India.

The Impact of Quality Checks

The GTRI report suggests that India's quality checks, particularly the Quality Control Orders (QCOs), may be a significant deterrent. These orders, which are issued by the government to ensure that imported goods meet certain quality standards, can be time-consuming and costly. For foreign investors, this could mean higher costs and longer lead times, making India less attractive as a manufacturing hub.

Pros and Cons of Stringent Quality Checks

  • Pros: Ensure that imported goods meet certain quality standards, protect domestic industries, and promote fair trade.
  • Cons: Can be time-consuming and costly, deter foreign investors, and potentially stifle economic growth.

Government's Response

In response to these concerns, the government is working to ease the QCOs, particularly for the hi-tech sector. This move is expected to make India more attractive to foreign investors, who are often looking for a balance between quality control and ease of doing business.

Conclusion

While India's drive for global factories is commendable, the stringent quality checks may be a significant hurdle. The government's efforts to ease these checks, particularly for the hi-tech sector, could be a game-changer. However, it remains to be seen whether these measures will be enough to attract the desired level of foreign investment.
