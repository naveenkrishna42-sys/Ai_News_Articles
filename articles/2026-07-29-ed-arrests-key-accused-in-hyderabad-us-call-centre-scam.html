ED arrests key accused in Hyderabad US call centre scam

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • ED arrests key accused in US call centre scam in Hyderabad.
  • The scam targeted US citizens, defrauding them by posing as tax officials.
  • Investigation is ongoing to trace the money trail and other accused.

The Enforcement Directorate (ED) has arrested a key accused in a major call centre scam that targeted US citizens from Hyderabad. The arrest was made on Wednesday under the provisions of the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA).

The accused is believed to be a central figure in the fraudulent operation that posed as US tax officials to extort money from victims across the Atlantic.

The Scam's Modus Operandi

The call centre scam involved operators calling US residents and pretending to be from the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). They would threaten victims with arrest or legal action if they did not pay alleged tax dues.

Victims were coerced into making payments through gift cards or wire transfers. The proceeds were then laundered through a network of accounts in India and abroad.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total amount defrauded, but the scam is believed to have run for several months, affecting hundreds of victims.

ED's Investigation and Arrest

The ED's investigation began after a complaint from the Cyberabad police, who had earlier cracked down on the call centre operation. The agency traced the money trail and identified the key accused as a mastermind behind the scheme.

The accused was taken into custody after a lengthy probe that involved collecting evidence from multiple sources, including digital devices and bank records. The ED is now working to locate other suspects and freeze assets linked to the scam.

This arrest marks a significant step in dismantling the fraud network, which had been operating with impunity for some time.

Broader Implications for Cybercrime

This case highlights the growing threat of cross-border cybercrime originating from India. Call centre scams have become a multi-crore illegal industry, often targeting vulnerable individuals in wealthier nations.

Authorities in Hyderabad have been intensifying efforts to combat such operations, with several raids conducted in recent months. The ED's involvement signals a focus on the financial dimensions of these crimes.

Legal experts say that successful prosecution in such cases requires strong international cooperation, especially to gather evidence from foreign jurisdictions.

The accused will be produced before a special PMLA court in Hyderabad. The ED is expected to seek custody to further unravel the scam's network.

What comes next will depend on how much information the accused can provide about the larger criminal enterprise and the whereabouts of other key players.
