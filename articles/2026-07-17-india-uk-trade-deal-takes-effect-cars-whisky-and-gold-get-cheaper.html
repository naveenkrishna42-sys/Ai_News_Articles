India-UK trade deal takes effect: Cars, whisky, and gold get cheaper

📅 2026-07-17 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • The India-UK CETA came into effect on July 15, 2026, eliminating tariffs on 95% of Indian exports to Britain immediately.
  • British cars imported into India will become cheaper by up to 20% from day one, but Scotch whisky tariffs will be phased out over 10 years.
  • Indian gold jewellery and textiles gain immediate duty-free access to the UK market, while British chocolate and cheese prices are expected to drop in India.

The India-UK Comprehensive Economic Trade Agreement (CETA) came into effect on July 15, 2026, marking the first major bilateral trade pact Britain has signed since leaving the European Union. The deal eliminates tariffs on more than 95% of Indian exports to the UK immediately, while British goods will see phased tariff reductions over the next decade.

What gets cheaper in India?

British car buyers in India are the biggest immediate winners. Import duties on UK-made cars, including premium brands like Jaguar Land Rover, have been slashed from 100% to 80% on day one. The tariff will continue to fall in stages, reaching zero by 2036.

British chocolate and cheese will also see price drops. Tariffs on confectionery and dairy products have been cut by 15-20% immediately, with full elimination scheduled within five years. Scotch whisky, however, remains the most politically sensitive item. Its 150% import duty will be reduced gradually over 10 years, disappointing distillers who wanted faster access.

UK financial services firms, including banks and insurers, have gained greater market access in Indian cities like Mumbai and Delhi. The deal allows British firms to set up branches without requiring local incorporation, a significant regulatory win.

What gets cheaper in Britain?

Indian exports to the UK receive immediate duty-free status on 95% of product lines. The first symbolic shipment — a consignment of gold jewellery from Calcutta — left the very day the pact kicked off, as reported by the Telegraph India.

Indian textiles, leather goods, and pharmaceuticals now enter Britain without tariffs. This is expected to lower prices for British consumers on everything from cotton shirts to generic medicines. Indian steel and aluminium products also benefit, though the UK has retained safeguard measures to prevent a sudden surge in imports.

The deal also covers services. Indian IT professionals and healthcare workers will find it easier to get work visas in the UK under new streamlined procedures. The agreement includes mutual recognition of professional qualifications for architects, accountants, and nurses.

Intellectual property and the missing Scotch

The CETA includes a comprehensive intellectual property chapter, as noted by Bar and Bench. India has agreed to strengthen enforcement against counterfeit goods, particularly in sectors like pharmaceuticals and software. The UK, in turn, has recognized India's geographical indications for Darjeeling tea and Basmati rice.

But Scotch whisky remains a sticking point. The Times of India reports that British distillers will have to wait a full decade before tariffs on their product vanish. Indian officials argue the phased approach protects domestic liquor manufacturers, who had lobbied hard against immediate tariff cuts.

The deal does not cover all contentious areas. Data localization rules and digital trade norms were left for future negotiations. Both sides have agreed to review the pact every five years.

What happens next

Customs officials in both countries are working this week to clear the first wave of duty-free goods. The Indian government will monitor domestic industries for potential harm, particularly in the automobile and dairy sectors. The real test of the CETA will come when the first quarterly trade data is released in October 2026.
