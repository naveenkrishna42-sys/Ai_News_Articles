BSE to replace Wipro in Nifty 50 index: What it means

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • BSE will replace Wipro in the Nifty 50 index effective August 28.
  • The change follows Wipro's decline in market capitalisation and trading activity.
  • NSE said the reconstitution is based on six-month average data and will not affect index levels.

In a significant reshuffle of India's benchmark equity index, Asia's oldest stock exchange, BSE, is set to replace IT services major Wipro in the Nifty 50. The National Stock Exchange (NSE) announced the change on Monday, with the new composition effective from August 28.

The move marks a notable shift in the index's technology-heavy weightage. Wipro, once a heavyweight in India's IT sector, has seen its market capitalisation and trading volumes dwindle in recent quarters, making it ineligible for a spot in the top 50 listed companies by free-float market cap.

Why BSE made the cut

BSE Ltd, which operates the Bombay Stock Exchange, has emerged as a direct beneficiary of the surge in retail participation in Indian markets. Its own stock has rallied sharply over the past year, buoyed by record volumes in the derivatives segment and a booming primary market. The exchange's inclusion in the Nifty 50 reflects its growing heft in the country's financial infrastructure.

According to NSE's index methodology, the reconstitution is based on the average free-float market capitalisation and trading turnover over the past six months. BSE's consistent performance in these metrics pushed it into the top 50, while Wipro's decline pushed it out.

Impact on fund flows

The swap will trigger forced buying and selling by passive funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) that track the Nifty 50. Fund managers typically rebalance their portfolios on the effective date, which could lead to sharp volume spikes in both BSE and Wipro shares around August 28.

Analysts estimate that BSE could see inflows of several hundred crore rupees from index-tracking funds, while Wipro may witness outflows of a similar magnitude. However, the actual numbers will depend on the total assets under management (AUM) of funds tracking the index.

What this means for investors

For retail investors, the change is a reminder of the dynamic nature of India's equity markets. The Nifty 50 is not a static list; it evolves with the economy. The exit of Wipro, a company that has been part of the index for over two decades, underscores the challenges faced by legacy IT firms amid rapid technological shifts and intense global competition.

On the other hand, BSE's inclusion highlights the growing financialisation of Indian savings. The exchange's own shares have become a proxy for the broader market's health, and its presence in the benchmark could attract more institutional interest.

Index changes do not alter the intrinsic value of a company. Wipro's fundamentals remain intact, and its removal from the Nifty 50 could make its valuation more attractive to some investors. But the loss of passive demand is a real headwind.

Next steps

The NSE has also announced changes to other indices, including the Nifty Next 50, as part of its semi-annual review. The exchange will publish the full list of additions and deletions on its website. Market participants will watch for any further surprises in the coming weeks as fund houses adjust their portfolios.

For now, all eyes will be on August 28, when the new index composition takes effect. Until then, expect some volatility in both BSE and Wipro shares as traders position ahead of the rebalancing.
