Cognizant Q2 revenue up 4.5% to $5.48 bn, meets estimates, demand steady

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cognizant's Q2 revenue rose 4.5% year-on-year to $5.48 billion, in line with market estimates.
  • The company raised its FY26 earnings per share outlook, signaling confidence in profitability.
  • Demand remains steady, though AI-led deal momentum begins to cool and client spending stays cautious.

Revenue growth matches forecasts

Cognizant Technology Solutions reported a 4.5% rise in revenue for the second quarter of fiscal 2026, reaching $5.48 billion. The figure matched analyst estimates, providing a measure of stability for the IT services major. The company attributed the growth to steady demand across key verticals, even as macroeconomic uncertainties persist.

The Nasdaq-listed firm, which counts banking, financial services, and healthcare among its largest clients, said operational efficiencies helped it navigate a cautious spending environment. Net income for the quarter came in at $580 million, against $560 million in the same period last year.

FY26 EPS outlook raised

Cognizant raised its full-year earnings per share (EPS) guidance, a move that caught the attention of analysts. The revised outlook suggests management expects better margin performance in the second half of the fiscal year. “We are confident in our ability to deliver value through cost optimization and strategic investments,” the company said in a statement.

The upward revision comes despite a mixed macroeconomic backdrop. Client budgets remain under scrutiny, with enterprises prioritising automation and efficiency over large-scale transformation projects. Cognizant’s own commentary pointed to “measured” spending patterns, particularly in discretionary IT services.

AI deal momentum shows signs of cooling

While artificial intelligence has been a bright spot for the IT industry over the past two years, Cognizant’s latest results indicate the pace of AI-led deal wins may be slowing. Analysts tracking the company noted that AI-related contract signings, while still positive, are not accelerating at the rate seen in previous quarters.

This trend mirrors broader industry observations. Many clients have moved from pilot phases to selective deployment, leading to smaller deal sizes. Cognizant’s management acknowledged that AI integration into existing workflows is happening, but at a more deliberate pace than initially anticipated.

Cautious client spending persists

Client caution remains a recurring theme in Cognizant’s earnings narrative. The company’s revenue guidance for the current quarter fell slightly short of some optimistic projections, reinforcing the view that enterprises are not yet opening their wallets wide. “Spending is there, but it’s tactical,” an industry analyst noted.

Cognizant’s peers, including Infosys and Tata Consultancy Services, have also flagged similar trends in their recent earnings calls. The sector as a whole is contending with a shift towards shorter-duration contracts and a focus on cost takeout rather than growth-led investments.

The company did not provide a specific revenue forecast for the third quarter, but indicated that it expects sequential growth in line with seasonal patterns.

What to watch next

Investors will now focus on Cognizant’s ability to sustain margin expansion while navigating a cautious demand environment. The raised EPS guidance will be tested in the coming quarters as the company balances investments in AI and automation against client budget constraints. Any shift in the pace of large deal closures could determine whether Cognizant can maintain its growth trajectory through the second half of FY26.
