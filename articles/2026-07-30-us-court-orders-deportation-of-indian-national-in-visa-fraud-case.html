US court sentences Indian national for staged robbery visa fraud

A US federal court has sentenced Indian national Mitul Patel for his role in a visa fraud scheme that involved a fake armed robbery. The court has also ordered his deportation following the completion of his sentence.

Patel was convicted for orchestrating a staged robbery at a liquor store in Worcester, Massachusetts. The incident, which took place at Austin Liquors, was fabricated to help individuals obtain U Visas reserved for victims of certain crimes who assist law enforcement.

The scheme and its unraveling

According to court documents, Patel conspired with others to fake a robbery at the store. The goal was to make it appear that certain individuals had been victims of a violent crime, thereby qualifying them for U Visas. These visas are designed to encourage victims to cooperate with authorities in criminal investigations.

The plan unraveled when investigators found inconsistencies in the accounts provided by those involved. The US Department of Homeland Security and local law enforcement eventually uncovered the fraud.

Patel was sentenced to a term in federal prison. The exact length of the sentence has not been disclosed by officials.

Deportation and legal consequences

Along with the prison term, the court ordered that Patel be deported from the United States after serving his time. This is a standard outcome for non-citizens convicted of serious crimes involving fraud.

The case underscores the stringent checks in place for immigration benefits. US authorities have been increasingly vigilant about visa fraud, particularly schemes that exploit victim-based programs.

What this means for visa applicants

Legal experts say the case serves as a warning to those attempting to game the system. U Visas are a lifeline for genuine victims, and fraud can undermine the program's integrity.

The US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) has not commented on whether other individuals involved in the scheme face charges. However, the investigation remains open.

Patel's case is now headed to the immigration courts for the deportation process. He is expected to be transferred to immigration custody after his sentence is served.
