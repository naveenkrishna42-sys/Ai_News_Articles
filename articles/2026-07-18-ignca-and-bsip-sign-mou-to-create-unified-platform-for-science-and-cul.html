IGNCA and BSIP sign MoU to create unified platform for science and culture

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • IGNCA and BSIP signed an MoU to establish a unified platform for science and culture.
  • The platform aims to integrate scientific research with cultural heritage preservation.
  • The collaboration is expected to promote interdisciplinary studies and public engagement.

MoU signed to bridge science and culture

The Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA) and the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP) have signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to create what they are calling India’s Unified Platform for Science and Culture. The agreement was formalised in New Delhi on Wednesday.

Officials said the platform is designed to bring together the two institutions’ expertise. IGNCA’s work in arts, heritage, and humanities will now be linked with BSIP’s research in palaeosciences and ancient environments.

What the platform will do

The unified platform is expected to facilitate joint research projects. Scientists and cultural scholars will collaborate on studies that explore India’s past through both archaeological and artistic lenses.

The MoU also provides for shared access to collections and databases. IGNCA’s archives on traditional knowledge and art forms will be accessible to BSIP researchers, while BSIP’s fossil and sediment records will be available for cultural interpretation.

Workshops, seminars, and public lectures are planned under the agreement. The goal is to create a space where scientific methods help decode cultural artefacts and where cultural insights inform scientific inquiry.

Interdisciplinary approach

This is not the first time such a cross-domain partnership has been attempted, but officials described this as a more structured attempt. The platform aims to move beyond occasional collaboration into a sustained institutional framework.

IGNCA has long worked on documenting intangible heritage. BSIP, one of India’s oldest research institutes, specialises in understanding climate and vegetation history. The overlap, according to the MoU, lies in reconstructing ancient human-environment interactions.

Both institutions believe that separating science from culture has limited the understanding of India’s civilisational history. The new platform is expected to correct that by enabling researchers to work with a unified dataset.

Public and academic engagement

The platform will also have a public outreach component. Exhibitions and digital resources will be developed to make the combined findings accessible to students and the general public.

Academic exchanges are part of the agreement. Researchers from both sides will be able to spend time at each other’s facilities. This, the MoU states, will help build a new generation of scholars comfortable working across disciplines.

Funding details were not disclosed. The institutions are expected to seek support from government schemes that promote interdisciplinary research.

What happens next

A joint steering committee will be set up to implement the platform. The first projects are expected to be announced within the next few months. Both IGNCA and BSIP will need to resolve data-sharing protocols and intellectual property terms before work begins. The success of the platform will depend on how seamlessly two very different institutional cultures can merge.
