Chennai Corporation Councillors Accuse TN Govt of Denying Their Rights

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Councillors claim the state government has curtailed their powers and bypassed their role in local governance.
  • Allegations include delayed fund releases and lack of consultation on key civic projects.
  • The councillors have demanded immediate intervention to restore their statutory rights.

Chennai Corporation councillors have levelled serious allegations against the Tamil Nadu government, accusing it of systematically denying them their rights and undermining their role in local governance. The councillors, from multiple wards across the city, say they have been reduced to mere spectators in decisions that directly affect their constituencies.

Systematic sidelining alleged

Speaking to reporters, several elected representatives claimed that the state government has been bypassing the corporation's elected wing on key civic matters. They alleged that crucial decisions on infrastructure projects, budget allocations, and land use are being taken without their input or consent.

“We are not being consulted on anything. Our suggestions are ignored. How can we serve our voters if we have no say in what happens in our wards?” a senior councillor said, requesting anonymity. The councillors argue that this amounts to a violation of the 74th Constitutional Amendment, which empowers urban local bodies.

Funds and powers under strain

A major point of contention is the alleged delay in releasing funds allocated for ward-level development. Councillors claim that even after the corporation passes resolutions, the state government sits on them for months, stalling work on roads, drains, streetlights, and sanitation.

“The money is sanctioned by the corporation, but the release is held up by the state. Our hands are tied,” another councillor said. They also accused the government of appointing special officers to oversee projects without involving the elected ward representatives, further eroding their authority.

Lack of consultation on key projects

The councillors pointed to recent large-scale projects in Chennai—such as the proposed elevated corridor and new solid waste management plants—as examples where they were not consulted. They said residents in their wards have been left in the dark, and the councillors have no answers to give.

“The government announces things on its own. We get to know about them from the news. Then people come to us, and we have nothing to tell them,” a councillor said. This has led to growing frustration among both elected representatives and the public.

Demand for intervention

The councillors have demanded that the Tamil Nadu government immediately restore their statutory rights and ensure that the Chennai Corporation functions as an autonomous body as per the Constitution. They have threatened to escalate the matter through protests and legal action if no corrective steps are taken.

“We are not asking for anything extraordinary. We just want to do the job we were elected to do. If the government does not listen, we will have no choice but to take to the streets and the courts,” one of them warned.

The state government has not yet responded to the allegations. Officials in the municipal administration department were unavailable for comment. Political observers say the standoff highlights a recurring tension between state governments and elected urban local bodies across India, where devolution of power often remains incomplete.

What comes next will depend on whether the state government chooses to engage with the councillors or continues to sideline them. The coming weeks are likely to see more protests and possibly a legal challenge if the demands are not met.
