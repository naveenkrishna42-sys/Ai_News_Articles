Jithender Reddy urges Centre to notify Hyderabad-Bengaluru defence corridor

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Jithender Reddy has urged the Central government to notify the Hyderabad-Bengaluru defence corridor.
  • He argued the corridor would boost defence manufacturing, create jobs, and attract investment in Telangana.
  • The demand comes amid ongoing efforts to expand defence industrial infrastructure beyond existing nodes.

Hyderabad: In a fresh push for defence manufacturing growth in the region, BJP leader and former MP Jithender Reddy has urged the Central government to formally notify the Hyderabad-Bengaluru defence corridor. The demand, made public on Monday, seeks to give the proposed industrial belt an official stamp that could unlock faster approvals, dedicated funding, and policy incentives.

Reddy argued that a notified corridor would act as a magnet for both domestic and foreign defence firms looking to set up units in Telangana and Karnataka. He pointed to the existing defence corridors in Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu as examples where formal notification had accelerated ground-level progress.

What the corridor promises

The Hyderabad-Bengaluru stretch is already home to several defence PSUs, private suppliers, and research institutions. Yet, without a formal notification, industry players often face delays in land acquisition, environmental clearances, and infrastructure allocation.

Reddy stressed that a notified status would change that. It would allow coordinated development of common facilities, testing labs, and skill development centres along the route. He also noted that the corridor could serve as a bridge between Hyderabad's established aerospace ecosystem and Bengaluru's strong IT and R&D base.

Jobs and investment at stake

The former MP highlighted the employment potential, saying the corridor could generate thousands of direct and indirect jobs in engineering, manufacturing, and support services. He urged the Centre to prioritise the region, especially as Telangana looks to diversify beyond IT and pharma.

Local industry bodies have echoed similar sentiments in the past, calling for a dedicated defence industrial node near Hyderabad. The city already houses facilities of DRDO, HAL, and several private defence startups, making it a natural fit for a larger industrial corridor.

Political and bureaucratic push

Reddy's appeal comes at a time when the Central government has been actively promoting defence self-reliance. The 'Make in India' initiative in defence has led to record export figures and increased private sector participation. A formal notification for the Hyderabad-Bengaluru corridor would align with these national priorities.

However, no official response from the Ministry of Defence or the Department of Defence Production has been reported yet. The proposal will likely require inter-ministerial consultation, particularly involving the Ministries of Defence, Commerce, and Heavy Industries.

What to watch next

Whether the Centre takes up the request in the upcoming parliamentary session remains to be seen. Industry watchers will also track if the Telangana government formally backs the demand, which could add political weight to Reddy's appeal. For now, the ball is in the Centre's court.
