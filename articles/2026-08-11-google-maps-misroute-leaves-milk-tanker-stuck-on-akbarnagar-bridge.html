Google Maps Misroute Leaves Milk Tanker Stuck on Akbarnagar Bridge

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Milk tanker followed Google Maps and got stuck on Akbarnagar bridge
  • Bridge is not designed for heavy vehicles like tankers
  • Traffic disrupted for several hours; crane used to free the tanker
  • Police urge drivers to rely on local signage over navigation apps

Blind Trust in Navigation App Leads to Bridge Blockage

A milk tanker driver's blind faith in Google Maps turned into a logistical nightmare on Tuesday morning when the heavy vehicle got wedged on the Akbarnagar bridge. The driver, who was following the app's instructions, found himself stuck after the tanker's body got caught against the bridge structure.

The incident brought traffic on the busy stretch to a standstill for several hours as local authorities rushed to the spot. Eyewitnesses said the driver looked visibly perplexed, repeatedly checking his phone even as the vehicle remained firmly lodged.

Narrow Bridge, Heavy Vehicle — A Recipe for Trouble

The Akbarnagar bridge, known for its narrow carriageway, is not designed to handle heavy commercial vehicles like milk tankers. Yet, the navigation app directed the driver onto the bridge, ignoring the physical constraints that any local driver would have immediately recognised.

According to officials, the tanker's size and weight made it impossible to reverse or manoeuvre out without risking further damage. Police had to arrange for a crane to lift the vehicle and ease it out of the tight spot, a process that took considerable time and effort.

Traffic Snarls and Commuter Woes

As news of the stuck tanker spread, curious onlookers gathered, adding to the chaos. Commuters heading to work found themselves trapped in long queues, with many forced to take alternate routes. Autorickshaw drivers and local shopkeepers said such incidents were becoming more frequent as drivers increasingly rely on apps over local knowledge.

"The app says 'this way' and the driver just follows, without looking at the road ahead," said a local trader who witnessed the scene. "These bridges are old and narrow. They can't take this kind of load."

Police Advisory: Use Local Sense Alongside GPS

Traffic police at the scene managed the situation, but not before the bridge remained blocked for several hours. A senior officer advised drivers, especially those operating heavy vehicles, to cross-check navigation app routes with local signage and road conditions.

"Navigation apps are useful, but they are not foolproof," the officer said. "Drivers must use their own judgement, especially on unfamiliar routes. Blindly following the app can lead to such mishaps."

The tanker was eventually freed, and the bridge was reopened to traffic. No injuries were reported, but the incident serves as a reminder of the growing dependence on technology and its occasional pitfalls.

What Next?

Local authorities are now considering placing clearer signage on the bridge warning heavy vehicles of the width restriction. They have also urged mapping services to update their data for the area to prevent similar incidents in the future.
