Residents plea to save 'wilting' plants at GCC park in Kalakshetra Colony

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Residents have raised a plea to save 'wilting' plants at the GCC park on MGR Road in Kalakshetra Colony.
  • The park, maintained by the Greater Chennai Corporation, shows signs of neglect with dried and dying vegetation.
  • Locals urge immediate watering and maintenance to revive the green space.
  • The issue highlights broader concerns over park upkeep in the city.

Residents of Kalakshetra Colony in Chennai have raised an urgent plea to save the 'wilting' plants at the Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC) park on MGR Road. The park, once a lush green space for the community, now shows signs of severe neglect. Dried leaves and drooping stems are a common sight.

Locals say the plants have not been watered properly for weeks. Many have turned yellow and appear beyond recovery. The park, a popular spot for morning walkers and children, has lost its charm.

Neglect and decaying greenery

The condition of the park has deteriorated rapidly over the past few weeks. Residents point to broken sprinklers and dry soil as evidence of poor maintenance. Some plants have completely dried up, with others barely surviving.

One resident, who did not wish to be named, said the park was once a 'green oasis' in the neighbourhood. Now, it looks 'abandoned'. The lack of regular watering has hit the plants hard, especially during the ongoing summer months.

The GCC is responsible for the upkeep of this park. But residents say the corporation's staff have been irregular in their duties. The park's irrigation system is not functioning properly, leaving the plants to wilt under the sun.

Community appeal for action

Residents have appealed to the local GCC office to take immediate action. They want the corporation to repair the watering system and ensure daily maintenance. 'We need the park back in its former glory,' said another resident.

Some locals have started watering the plants themselves, but it is not enough. The park covers a large area, and individual efforts cannot sustain it. The community fears that without swift intervention, the greenery will die completely.

The issue also highlights a broader problem in Chennai. Many public parks in the city face similar neglect, with residents often having to step in to fill gaps left by authorities.

Broader implications for city parks

Chennai's parks are vital for the city's environment and residents' well-being. They provide shade, reduce heat, and offer spaces for recreation. When parks like the one on MGR Road wilt, it affects the entire neighbourhood.

Environmental activists note that urban green spaces require consistent care. Without it, they quickly turn into dry patches. The situation in Kalakshetra Colony is a reminder of the need for better municipal oversight.

Residents hope the GCC will respond soon. They have submitted written complaints and are waiting for a reply. Some have even reached out to local councillors for support.

The next few weeks will be critical. If the plants are not revived soon, many may not survive the summer. The community remains hopeful but anxious.
