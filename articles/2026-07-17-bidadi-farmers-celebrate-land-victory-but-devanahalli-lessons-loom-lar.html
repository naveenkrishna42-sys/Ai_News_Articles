Bidadi Farmers Celebrate Land Victory, but Devanahalli Lessons Loom Large

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bidadi farmers claim victory in land acquisition dispute with state government.
  • Devanahalli farmers faced similar success initially but later encountered delays and unmet promises.
  • Experts caution that celebration may be premature without sustained legal and political follow-through.

Farmers in Bidadi are celebrating what they see as a hard-won victory in a long-running land acquisition dispute with the Karnataka government. But as the cheers echo through the villages on the outskirts of Bengaluru, those who remember the Devanahalli story are urging caution.

Land acquisition battles in Karnataka rarely end with a single verdict. The Devanahalli experience, where farmers initially tasted success only to see their gains erode over time, offers a stark warning.

The Bidadi Win: A Moment of Relief

In recent weeks, farmers from several villages in Bidadi received official communication that the government had agreed to revise compensation rates and return certain parcels of land that were earlier notified for acquisition. For a community that has been protesting and petitioning for years, this felt like a decisive win.

“We have been waiting for this day. Our struggle has finally paid off,” said a farmer who did not wish to be named. The mood in the villages is upbeat, with families hoping to rebuild their lives on terms that are fairer than before.

Devanahalli: A Cautionary Tale

But just 30 kilometres away, the story of Devanahalli offers a different ending. In 2006, farmers there successfully resisted the acquisition of their land for the Bengaluru International Airport expansion. They won a court order that stopped the project and secured promises of higher compensation.

Yet, over the next decade, those promises frayed. Delays in payment, legal appeals by the government, and the slow grinding of bureaucratic machinery left many farmers in limbo. Some never received the full compensation. Others saw their land values stagnate as development bypassed their villages.

“We thought we had won too,” recalled a Devanahalli farmer. “But winning in court is not the same as winning on the ground.”

Lessons for Bidadi

Analysts point out that the Bidadi victory, while significant, is still fragile. The government could appeal the decision, or introduce new acquisition notifications that circumvent the current settlement. Legal experts suggest that farmers must now focus on implementation — ensuring that the revised compensation is disbursed without delay and that the land returns are executed smoothly.

“Celebration is natural, but vigilance is necessary,” said a land rights activist familiar with both cases. “The Devanahalli farmers had the law on their side, but they lacked the sustained political pressure to make it stick.”

The Bidadi farmers have formed a coordination committee to monitor the government's next steps. They say they will not disband until every rupee is paid and every acre is returned.

What Comes Next

The coming weeks will be critical. The government is expected to issue a formal order on the compensation revision. If it does, the farmers will have a binding document to enforce. If it delays, the momentum of the victory could dissipate.

For now, the mood in Bidadi is one of cautious optimism. But the shadow of Devanahalli hangs over every handshake and every celebratory feast. The real test is yet to come.
