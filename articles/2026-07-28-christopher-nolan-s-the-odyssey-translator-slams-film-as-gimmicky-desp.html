Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey: Translator slams film as ‘gimmicky’ despite ₹140 crore India haul

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Translator Emily Wilson criticised Nolan's adaptation as 'gimmicky' with 'no sex'.
  • The film has earned $652 million globally and ₹140 crore in India in 10 days.
  • The Odyssey is set to face competition from 'Spider-Man: Brand New Day' at the Indian box office.

Translator takes aim at Nolan’s epic

Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey has crossed the $652 million mark globally, but not everyone is celebrating. Emily Wilson, the renowned translator of Homer’s original epic, has called the film adaptation “abysmal” and “gimmicky”.

In a scathing review, Wilson took particular issue with the film’s sanitised portrayal of the ancient world. She noted that Nolan’s version has “no sex”, stripping the source material of its raw human complexity.

No sex, no depth?

Wilson’s criticism has been echoed by other commentators. Writing for The Guardian, Zoe Williams questioned, “Why has Christopher Nolan got rid of all the sex in The Odyssey?” The translation scholar described the film as an “uncomplicated man” story, lacking the moral and erotic tensions that make the poem endure.

The BBC reported Wilson’s remarks, framing them as a clash between literary fidelity and blockbuster spectacle. Nolan’s film, she argued, sacrifices the story’s messy humanity for a clean, visually impressive but hollow narrative.

Box office triumph continues

Despite the literary backlash, The Odyssey is a commercial juggernaut. In India alone, the film has earned over ₹140 crore in its first 10 days, according to The Times of India. The numbers place it among the highest-grossing Hollywood releases in the country this year.

The film now faces its next test: a clash with Spider-Man: Brand New Day. Industry watchers are keen to see if Nolan’s epic can hold its ground against the Marvel machine.

What the critics say

Wilson’s critique is not the only negative review, but it carries weight given her authority on the source text. She has described the film as “gimmicky”, suggesting Nolan relied on technical bravura instead of emotional resonance.

Other reviewers have been more generous, praising the director’s visual ambition and the performances of the ensemble cast. The divide highlights a familiar tension: can a faithful adaptation also be a crowd-pleaser?

What happens next

Nolan’s The Odyssey will continue its theatrical run, with the Spider-Man showdown looming in India. Whether the translator’s words dent its box office momentum remains to be seen.
