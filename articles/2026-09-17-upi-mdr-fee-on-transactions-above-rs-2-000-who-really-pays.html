UPI MDR Fee on Transactions Above Rs 2,000: Who Really Pays?

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-17 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • MDR applies only to merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, not person-to-person transfers, so ordinary senders remain unaffected.
  • The fee is borne by merchants, typically 0.5% to 1.1%, not by consumers directly, though some may pass it on via pricing.
  • RuPay debit cards and UPI-linked credit card bill payments face different MDR treatment, creating confusion for buyers and sellers.
  • A Supreme Court plea and broker/AMC concerns signal unresolved regulatory friction around the NPCI circular.
  • For small merchants, the real cost is margin erosion; for banks and fintechs, it is a long-awaited revenue stream.

What Exactly Is the UPI MDR Fee?

India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) has been free for merchants since its 2016 launch. The National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) circular now permits banks and payment aggregators to levy a Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on UPI transactions above Rs 2,000. Below that threshold, UPI stays free. Person-to-person (P2P) transfers remain untouched entirely, so sending money to a friend or family member still costs nothing.

The government has publicly rejected claims of US pressure behind the move, citing the NPCI circular and a RuPay advantage instead. That framing matters for buyers because it signals the fee is a domestic settlement decision, not a foreign trade concession.

The Verified Numbers: What Merchants Actually Pay

Specification / MetricVerified Details
MDR thresholdMerchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000
P2P transfersZero MDR, unchanged
Indicative MDR range0.5% to 1.1% depending on merchant category and payment method

RuPay debit card treatmentSeparate MDR structure, cited by government as a competitive advantage

Those percentages look small until you scale them. A merchant processing Rs 10 lakh a month in UPI sales above Rs 2,000 could absorb roughly Rs 5,000 to Rs 11,000 monthly in MDR. For a kirana store running on 8-12% gross margins, that is a direct hit to working capital.

Who Benefits and Who Bleeds

  • Banks and payment aggregators: Finally earn interchange revenue on a rail they have subsidised for years.
  • RuPay and card networks: Gain a relative pricing advantage over zero-MDR UPI on larger tickets.
  • Large merchants: Can absorb MDR and may negotiate lower rates; they also gain card-like dispute mechanisms.
  • Small merchants: Face margin erosion with limited bargaining power and no easy way to split bills below Rs 2,000.
  • Consumers: Not charged directly, but indirect price pass-through and split-payment behaviour are real risks.

The Supreme Court Plea and Regulatory Friction

A plea in the Supreme Court challenges the Centre's decision to levy MDR on merchant UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, arguing it undermines the promise of free digital payments. Separately, SEBI has said it will hear broker and Asset Management Company (AMC) concerns on MDR, which matters because mutual fund and brokerage transactions often exceed the threshold and sit outside typical retail merchant categories.

Moneycontrol has raised the open question of whether MDR applies to credit card bill payments made through UPI. Until NPCI or the RBI clarifies, buyers should assume the answer depends on whether the payment is classified as a merchant transaction or a financial-service transfer.

Practical Buyer Implications

If you are a consumer, your UPI app will not suddenly display a fee on a Rs 500 chai payment. But for purchases above Rs 2,000, expect three behavioural shifts. First, some merchants may request split payments or cash for large tickets. Second, cashback and reward offers on UPI credit cards may be recalibrated as interchange economics change. Third, RuPay debit card usage could rise at large merchants where MDR parity makes cards competitive again.

For merchants, the decision point is simple: accept the fee as a cost of digital scale, or route large transactions through lower-MDR instruments. BharatPe has backed the move, suggesting parts of the merchant fintech ecosystem see long-term infrastructure benefits outweighing short-term costs.

The fee is not a consumer tax. It is a merchant-side cost that will quietly reshape pricing, routing, and reward structures across India's digital payments stack.
