Calcutta High Court Denies TMC Plea To Access ED-Frozen Bank Accounts

📅 2026-07-20 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Calcutta High Court rejected TMC's plea to operate accounts frozen by the Enforcement Directorate.
  • The accounts, worth Rs 440 crore, were frozen as part of an ED probe into alleged financial irregularities.
  • The court observed that no prima facie case existed to grant interim relief to the Mamata Banerjee faction.

The Calcutta High Court on Monday refused to grant interim relief to the Trinamool Congress (TMC), rejecting the party’s plea to operate bank accounts frozen by the Enforcement Directorate (ED). The accounts, collectively holding approximately Rs 440 crore, were attached as part of an ongoing money laundering investigation.

Justice Sabyasachi Bhattacharya, presiding over the single-judge bench, declined to issue any interim order allowing the TMC’s Mamata Banerjee-led faction to access the funds. The court noted that the party had failed to establish a prima facie case for such relief.

Court's Observations on the Plea

The judge specifically pointed out that the TMC’s argument lacked the legal grounds necessary to override the ED’s actions. “No prima facie case is made out for grant of any interim order,” the court stated in its ruling.

The ED had frozen the accounts earlier this year, alleging that the funds were linked to a larger scheme of money diversion. The probe agency has been investigating transactions worth over Rs 133.84 crore that were allegedly diverted by the party.

Background of the ED Probe

The Enforcement Directorate’s investigation stems from a complaint regarding the alleged misappropriation of funds. The agency claims that the TMC, through its various accounts, received and channeled money in a manner that violated the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA).

In its submissions before the court, the ED argued that allowing the party to operate the accounts would hinder the investigation. The agency contended that the frozen amounts were directly linked to the proceeds of crime.

The TMC, on its part, had sought the court’s permission to access the accounts for routine administrative expenses, including paying salaries and maintaining party operations. Party lawyers argued that the freeze was causing severe operational difficulties.

What the Ruling Means

Monday’s order is a significant setback for the TMC, which has been battling multiple ED investigations in recent months. The party had hoped for interim access to the funds while the main case remains pending.

The court’s refusal does not close the matter permanently. The TMC is expected to pursue its main petition challenging the ED’s action. However, the ruling makes clear that the judiciary is not inclined to intervene at this stage without a stronger legal basis.

Legal experts note that the standard for obtaining interim relief in PMLA cases is high. Courts typically require a demonstration of irreparable harm and a strong likelihood of success on the merits — thresholds the TMC apparently failed to meet.

The case is listed for further hearing next month. The court has directed the ED to file its complete reply, after which the main arguments will commence.

The ruling comes amid heightened political tensions in West Bengal, with the TMC frequently accusing the central government of using investigative agencies to target opposition parties. The ED has denied these allegations.
