School Fees Just the Start: Hidden Costs Crush Indian Families

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Indian households face hidden education costs beyond tuition, including coaching fees, transportation, and supplies.
  • A recent report highlights that private coaching centers are booming despite India spending ₹1.2 lakh crore on education.
  • Experts call for greater transparency and regulation of ancillary school expenses to ease the financial burden on parents.

The Real Price of Schooling

For millions of Indian parents, the annual school fee bill is only the first shock. A quiet crisis is unfolding in households across the country as the hidden costs of educating children pile up — from private coaching to transportation, uniforms, and extracurricular activities.

NDTV's recent report, titled 'School Fees Are Just The Beginning', underscores a grim reality: the financial burden of schooling often extends far beyond what is listed in the prospectus. Many families budget for tuition, only to find themselves scrambling to meet unexpected expenses that can double or triple their annual outlay.

Coaching Centres: A ₹1.2 Lakh Crore Question

India spent a staggering ₹1.2 lakh crore on education in the last financial year, yet private tutoring centres continue to thrive. A report by indiaherald.com posed a blunt question: if public spending is so high, why are private tutors still raising the next generation?

The answer lies in a growing trust deficit. Many parents feel that school curricula are not enough to secure competitive exam results or even basic academic success. Coaching centres, particularly in metros and tier-2 cities, have become a non-negotiable expense for families striving to give their children an edge.

GujaratSamachar English noted that skyrocketing private school costs are squeezing household budgets. A middle-class family in Ahmedabad now spends nearly 40% of its monthly income on a single child's education — a figure that includes school fees, coaching, transport, and supplies.

The Coaching Centre Conundrum

The Statesman highlighted what it called the "coaching centre conundrum." While these institutes fill a perceived gap in the formal education system, they also operate with little regulatory oversight. Fees can vary wildly, and quality is inconsistent.

Parents often have no choice but to pay. "If I don't send my child to coaching, he falls behind in class. The school itself assumes everyone is taking extra classes," said a parent in Delhi, speaking on condition of anonymity. This pressure, combined with rising inflation, is forcing families to take loans or cut back on other essentials.

The Indian Express, in its editorial on private investment in education, argued that India needs "idealism without illusions." The piece called for a more honest conversation about what education actually costs, and who bears the burden.

From Fees to Field Trips: The Hidden Add-Ons

The hidden costs are not limited to coaching. Annual fees often exclude field trips, laboratory charges, sports equipment, and even basic stationery. Schools increasingly charge for "extras" like smart-class technology, summer camps, and annual day events.

Transportation is another major drain. With schools often located far from residential areas, bus fees can run into thousands of rupees per month. For families with two or more children, the cumulative cost becomes unsustainable.

Many parents report that they are asked to purchase specific brands of uniforms, shoes, and books from designated vendors, often at inflated prices. Schools defend these practices by citing quality and uniformity, but critics argue they create an unnecessary financial strain.

Officials have not yet confirmed any plans to regulate these ancillary charges, but the issue is gaining attention. Consumer activists have called for schools to provide a complete, itemised cost sheet at the time of admission, so parents know what to expect.

What Comes Next?

The debate over hidden education costs is unlikely to fade. With the next academic session approaching, parents are bracing for another round of fee hikes and surprise charges. Policymakers are under pressure to bring transparency to school finances and to rethink the role of private coaching in a system that already spends over a lakh crore rupees annually.
