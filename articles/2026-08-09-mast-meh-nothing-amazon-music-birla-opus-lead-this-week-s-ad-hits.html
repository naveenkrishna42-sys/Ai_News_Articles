Mast & Meh: Nothing, Amazon Music, Birla Opus lead this week's ad hits

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nothing, Amazon Music and Birla Opus Paints top this week's Mast list
  • Pears, Mahindra Thar Roxx and Crocs fail to impress, landing on the Meh side
  • The weekly roundup by Storyboard18 and Exchange4Media also highlights nostalgia, humour and community-driven campaigns
  • Other notable campaigns include Heineken and Goldfish, as brands lean into emotional and playful storytelling

This week's advertising scorecard is out, and it's a mix of bold bets and missed opportunities. Storyboard18's latest Mast & Meh list has crowned Nothing, Amazon Music and Birla Opus Paints as the standout campaigns, while Pears, Mahindra Thar Roxx and Crocs have been relegated to the Meh corner.

The weekly feature, which tracks the pulse of Indian advertising, has become a reliable barometer for what resonates with consumers. This week, it's clear that brands leaning into nostalgia, sibling banter and community spirit are striking the right chord.

The Mast: What Worked

Nothing, the consumer tech brand known for its transparent design philosophy, has grabbed the top spot. The campaign, which has been making waves across digital and social platforms, seems to have hit the sweet spot with its minimalist yet striking approach. It's a reminder that sometimes less is more, especially when the product speaks for itself.

Amazon Music's entry into the Mast list comes as no surprise. The streaming platform has been steadily building a reputation for campaigns that blend emotion with entertainment. This week's effort appears to have tapped into the collective mood, offering a soundtrack to the country's varied moments of joy and reflection.

Birla Opus Paints, a relative newcomer in the paints segment, has managed to paint a strong impression. The campaign, which reportedly focuses on the emotional weight of choosing the right colours for a home, has resonated with audiences looking for more than just a functional product. It's a bold move in a category dominated by legacy players, and it seems to be paying off.

The Meh: What Missed

On the flip side, Pears, Mahindra Thar Roxx and Crocs have found themselves on the Meh list. While these brands have loyal followings, their latest efforts seem to have fallen flat. Pears, known for its gentle and caring image, may have struggled to stand out in a crowded personal care market. Mahindra Thar Roxx, despite the buzz around the vehicle, appears to have delivered a campaign that didn't quite match the excitement of the product launch. Crocs, the quirky footwear brand, might have missed the mark by leaning too heavily into its usual quirkiness without adding a fresh twist.

It's not all doom and gloom, though. The Meh list is as much a learning opportunity as the Mast list is a celebration. For these brands, the feedback from consumers and industry watchers could be the nudge they need to recalibrate their messaging.

Nostalgia, Humour and Community

Beyond the top picks, this week's ad landscape has been defined by three recurring themes: nostalgia, humour and community. Exchange4Media's roundup highlights how brands are increasingly turning to these emotional triggers to cut through the noise. Nostalgic singalongs, in particular, have emerged as a powerful tool, transporting audiences back to simpler times while subtly reinforcing brand recall.

Sibling banter, another recurring motif, has proven to be a goldmine for relatability. Whether it's the bickering that turns into affection or the shared memories that bind families, these narratives are striking a chord with viewers across age groups. It's a departure from the polished, aspirational advertising that dominated the past decade, and it's refreshing to see.

Meanwhile, ADWEEK's 'Campaigns We Like' has spotlighted international efforts from Heineken and Goldfish, which have also leaned into these themes. The global shift towards authentic, grounded storytelling is evident, and Indian brands are clearly taking notes.

What the Week Tells Us

The Mast & Meh list is more than just a ranking; it's a reflection of consumer sentiment. This week, it suggests that audiences are craving campaigns that feel human. The winners are those that manage to evoke a genuine emotional response, whether it's a laugh, a tear or a shared sense of belonging. The losers, on the other hand, are those that rely on formulaic approaches or fail to evolve with the times.

For marketers, the takeaway is clear: authenticity wins. In a world where consumers are bombarded with content, the brands that stand out are those that speak to real experiences, real emotions and real communities. It's a lesson that even the most established names can't afford to ignore.

As the week rolls on, all eyes will be on how these brands respond. Will the Meh listers bounce back with stronger campaigns? Will the Mast leaders sustain their momentum? The advertising world is nothing if not dynamic, and next week's list is already shaping up to be another interesting read.
