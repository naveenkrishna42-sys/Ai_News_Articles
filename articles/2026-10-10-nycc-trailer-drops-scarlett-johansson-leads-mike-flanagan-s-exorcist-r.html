Trailer Watch: What NYCC’s ‘The Exorcist: Martyrs’ Reveal Actually Changes

The NYCC trailer drop for The Exorcist: Martyrs positions the film as more than another franchise entry. With Scarlett Johansson starring and Mike Flanagan attached, the marketing signals a shift toward grounded inquiry—an approach fans can feel immediately in tone, pacing, and framing choices shown in the teaser.

Instead of centring the story solely on religious ritual, the trailer emphasizes a detective-style arc: a methodical protagonist tracking a pattern of church-related murders. That framing matters for viewers deciding whether they want classic Exorcist-style shock beats or a new kind of horror tension built on investigation and implication.

Why Flanagan’s Horror Style Matters for Viewers

Mike Flanagan’s track record with horror tends to treat fear as a product of story engineering: character motivation, moral pressure, and carefully layered uncertainty. In a reboot, that usually means the film’s supernatural elements aren’t random—they’re tied to a human system of belief, grief, and blame.

For buyers—whether you’re planning a home release watch party, evaluating whether to subscribe to a streaming service, or deciding if this fits your horror “taste budget”—Flanagan’s involvement is a proxy for consistency. The trailer’s emphasis on escalation suggests a structured build rather than a scattered sequence of scares.

  • Expectation fit: If you prefer slow-burn dread and character-first horror, this is aligned.
  • Red-flag risk: If you only want rapid-fire jump scares, the investigative framing may feel deliberate.
  • Rewatch value: Flanagan-style narratives often reward attention to detail, not just the loud moments.

Johansson as Detective: The Franchise Reboot’s New Delivery System

Scarlett Johansson’s casting as a detective probing church murders reads like a strategic reboot decision: give the audience a rational anchor who still has to function inside an irrational world. That dynamic can modernize the franchise without stripping its signature themes—faith, fear, and the thin line between skepticism and terror.

The trailer’s portrayal of murders linked to religious settings also suggests the film will treat the church as both location and argument. That’s a key difference from older franchise entries where the setting often functions more like a backdrop; here, the environment appears central to the mystery structure.

From a commercial viewing standpoint, Johansson’s star power also changes the audience mix. It pulls in mainstream moviegoers who may not self-identify as “horror franchise” fans, while still aiming to deliver the emotional intensity that horror audiences expect from Flanagan-adjacent storytelling.

What “Martyrs” Could Signal About the Story’s Stakes

The title Martyrs implies moral cost and, potentially, victims who aren’t just collateral damage. In horror, martyrdom framing often pushes the narrative toward ideological conflict—someone believes the suffering is meaningful, even necessary, which can make the antagonist’s logic feel unnervingly human.

The trailer’s focus on a sequence of murders suggests the film may organize its terror around a “case” structure. That would let scenes do double duty: advance plot clues while also communicating dread through symbolism, timing, and character reactions.

Buyer Checklist: Should You Watch This?

If you’re deciding based on taste rather than franchise loyalty, use this practical checklist drawn directly from how the trailer frames conflict.

  • Go if: You enjoy horror with an investigative core, character psychology, and escalating moral tension.
  • Skip or be cautious if: You dislike slow-burn mysteries or want horror that relies purely on possession/ritual spectacle.
  • Best watch conditions: A dark room and a full attention session—mystery threads tend to matter.

Value Context: Trailer Hype vs. What the Film Needs to Deliver

Trailer messaging at NYCC typically compresses the film’s “promise” into a few minutes: establish tone, demonstrate threat, and underline the lead’s capability. Here, Johansson’s detective lens and Flanagan’s reputation for narrative control are the two strongest selling points.

For viewers, the risk is mismatched expectations—if the film leans heavily into procedural unraveling, it must still deliver visceral horror payoff. The opportunity is strong: when a reboot reframes the franchise around investigation and stakes, it can feel fresh without abandoning recognizable themes.
