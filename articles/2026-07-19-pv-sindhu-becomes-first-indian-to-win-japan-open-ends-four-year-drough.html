PV Sindhu becomes first Indian to win Japan Open, ends four-year drought against Yamaguchi

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • PV Sindhu won the Japan Open, a first for any Indian shuttler
  • She beat Akane Yamaguchi for the first time in four years
  • Coach Vimal Kumar's pep talk and Sindhu's self-belief were key to the victory

Sindhu ends long wait for Japan Open title

PV Sindhu scripted history on Sunday by becoming the first Indian shuttler to win the Japan Open. The former world champion defeated local favourite Akane Yamaguchi in the final, snapping a four-year losing streak against her Japanese rival.

The victory marks a significant milestone in Sindhu's career, which has seen ups and downs since her gold medal at the 2019 World Championships. The Japan Open, a Super 750 event, had eluded Indian shuttlers until now.

Four-year drought against Yamaguchi ends

Sindhu's win against Yamaguchi was particularly special. The Japanese star had beaten Sindhu in their last five encounters, with the Indian's last victory dating back to 2022. The crowd at the Yoyogi National Gymnasium was firmly behind Yamaguchi, but Sindhu held her nerve.

The match showcased Sindhu's trademark attacking play and improved court coverage. She dominated the rallies, forcing errors from the world number two. The final scoreline reflected Sindhu's control throughout the contest.

Self-belief and coach's role

Speaking after the match, Sindhu opened up about the criticism she faced during her lean patch. "A lot of people asked what's happening? Career done? But I kept believing," she said. The 31-year-old credited her coach Vimal Kumar for keeping her motivated.

"Vimal's constant pep talk bears fruit," noted reports from the event. Kumar had been working on Sindhu's mental toughness and tactical approach in recent months. The result validates the changes made to her training regimen.

Gopichand and peers react

National coach Pullela Gopichand said Sindhu remains a threat for the Olympics. "This Japan Open victory is a big achievement for Sindhu," he stated. "I'm pleased for her. She has shown she can compete at the highest level."

Fellow Indian shuttlers also congratulated Sindhu on social media. The win comes at a crucial time, with the Paris Olympics less than two years away. Sindhu has been working to regain her form after early exits in several tournaments last year.

What this means for Indian badminton

Sindhu's Japan Open triumph adds to India's growing list of achievements in international badminton. It follows Saina Nehwal's historic wins and the rise of men's singles players like Lakshya Sen and HS Prannoy.

The victory also boosts Sindhu's ranking and confidence. She will look to carry this momentum into the upcoming World Championships and the Asian Games. India now has a genuine contender in the women's singles category for major events.

With the win, Sindhu has silenced critics who questioned her ability to beat top players consistently. The Japan Open title proves she still has the hunger and skill to compete with the best in the world.

What to watch

Sindhu will next be seen at the World Championships in August, where she will aim to add another medal to her collection. Her form will be closely monitored as the Olympic qualification period begins later this year.
