BMC doctors call off protest after HC directive

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • BMC MARD withdrew protest following Bombay High Court's intervention
  • High Court directed both sides to resolve differences through dialogue
  • Emergency services in civic hospitals remained functional throughout

MUMBAI: The Maharashtra Association of Resident Doctors (MARD), representing resident doctors in Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) hospitals, has called off its ongoing protest following a directive from the Bombay High Court on Thursday. The decision came after the court intervened, asking both the doctors and the civic body to resolve their differences through dialogue.

Court's intervention leads to truce

A division bench of the Bombay High Court, hearing a petition related to the stir, observed that such protests cannot be allowed to continue at the cost of patient care. The court reportedly suggested that the BMC and the doctors' association sit together to address the grievances.

Following the direction, MARD leaders announced the withdrawal of the protest, stating that they would comply with the court's order. The association has asked its members to resume regular duties with immediate effect.

What triggered the protest

The doctors had been on protest over issues related to working conditions, stipends, and other demands. The stir had caused disruption in outpatient departments (OPDs) and elective surgeries in several civic-run hospitals across Mumbai, though emergency services were maintained.

The BMC, on its part, had maintained that most of the demands were under consideration and urged the doctors to return to work. The standoff had been continuing for a few days before the court stepped in.

Patients caught in the middle

While the protest was on, patients in civic hospitals faced inconvenience, with many routine procedures being postponed. Hospital administrations had put up notices about the reduced services, asking patients to cooperate.

Health activists and patient groups had expressed concern over the impact of the stir on the public healthcare system, which serves a large number of people from lower and middle-income groups in the city.

What happens next

With the protest called off, the BMC and MARD are expected to hold talks under the court's supervision to iron out the remaining issues. The High Court has reportedly scheduled a follow-up hearing to monitor the progress of these discussions.

Both sides have indicated willingness to reach a settlement, with the focus now on ensuring that such disruptions do not recur. The residents' doctors have said they will continue to press their demands through proper channels.

Observers will be watching whether the civic body and the doctors can arrive at a lasting agreement, given the recurring nature of such disputes in public health institutions. The court's involvement is likely to set a precedent for handling similar standoffs in the future.
