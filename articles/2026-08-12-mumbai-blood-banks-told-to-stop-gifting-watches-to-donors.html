Mumbai Blood Banks Told to Stop Gifting Watches to Donors

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mumbai blood banks receive notices over gifting watches to donors.
  • Authorities flag the practice as a potential violation of donor safety norms.
  • Blood banks asked to comply with revised guidelines or face action.

Mumbai's blood banks are in the spotlight after regulatory authorities issued notices against the practice of gifting watches to blood donors. The directive, which has caught several institutions off guard, stems from concerns that such incentives may cross ethical and legal lines.

Gifting Watches: A Growing Concern

Over the past year, several blood banks in the city had introduced the practice of presenting wristwatches to donors as a token of appreciation. The move was aimed at boosting donor turnout. However, officials now argue that such gifts could unduly influence donors, potentially compromising the voluntary nature of blood donation.

The notices, sent to multiple blood banks across Mumbai, explicitly ask them to cease the practice immediately. Institutions that fail to comply may face penalties, including suspension of licenses, under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act.

What the Notices Say

Sources indicate that the notices cite existing guidelines under the National Blood Transfusion Council, which discourage any form of material incentive for blood donation. The guidelines emphasise that donation should remain a purely altruistic act, free from any commercial or material inducement.

Blood bank officials have been given a deadline to respond and confirm compliance. Some have already begun reviewing their donor appreciation programmes to align with the new directive.

Mixed Reactions from Blood Banks

While some blood bank administrators have welcomed the clarification, others expressed surprise. Several institutions had viewed the watch gifts as a harmless gesture of gratitude. One administrator, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the practice had helped attract first-time donors, especially young professionals.

However, health experts point out that the primary concern is not the gift itself but the principle. If donating blood becomes associated with rewards, it could deter repeat donations when no such incentives are offered. "The focus must remain on the life-saving impact of donation, not on what the donor receives in return," a public health official noted.

Regulatory Push for Transparency

The notices are part of a broader regulatory push to ensure transparency and ethical compliance across blood banks in Maharashtra. In recent months, authorities have also tightened scrutiny over donor screening and storage practices. The watch issue, though minor in scale, highlights the need for uniform standards.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of blood banks that received the notices. They have, however, indicated that the directive applies to all licensed facilities in Mumbai, and possibly across the state.

What Donors Should Know

For regular donors, the change means that while they may no longer receive a watch, they can still expect basic refreshments and certificates of appreciation, which are permitted under current rules. The authorities have clarified that these do not constitute undue inducement.

Blood banks are now expected to submit a written undertaking within a stipulated timeframe, confirming that they have discontinued the practice. Any repeat violation could invite stricter action, including a show-cause notice.

As the deadline approaches, the city's blood banks are recalibrating their outreach strategies. The next few weeks will show whether this regulatory nudge leads to a broader review of donor incentives across the state.
