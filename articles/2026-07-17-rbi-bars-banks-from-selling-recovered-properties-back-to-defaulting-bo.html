RBI Bars Banks From Selling Recovered Properties Back To Defaulting Borrowers

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI issues directive barring sale of recovered immovable properties to borrowers
  • Move aims to prevent defaulters from repurchasing assets at discounted rates
  • Banks must now sell such properties to third parties through transparent auction

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has tightened the screws on bad loan recovery by barring banks from selling recovered immovable properties back to the original borrowers. The directive, issued on Thursday, closes a loophole that had allowed defaulting borrowers to reclaim their assets at prices far below market value.

The central bank's new rule applies to all banks, including public sector lenders, private banks, and foreign banks operating in India. It covers properties recovered under the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest (SARFAESI) Act, 2002.

Why The RBI Stepped In

Banks have been recovering properties from defaulters and then selling them back to the same borrowers in some cases. This practice defeated the purpose of recovery and encouraged moral hazard, where borrowers deliberately defaulted expecting to buy back their assets cheaply.

The RBI observed that such transactions did not represent a genuine recovery. Instead, they allowed the borrower to retain possession while the bank marked the loan as settled. This distorted the true picture of asset quality on bank books.

What The New Rules Mean

Under the fresh directive, banks cannot sell recovered immovable properties to the borrower who originally defaulted, their relatives, or any entity controlled by them. The property must go through a transparent auction process open to third-party buyers.

"The sale of immovable property, acquired by a bank through the enforcement of security interest, shall not be made to the borrower who had availed the loan," the RBI said in its circular. Banks have been asked to ensure that auction notices clearly state this restriction.

Auction Process Tightened

The central bank also clarified that banks must conduct auctions in a fair, transparent, and non-discriminatory manner. The sale price should reflect the prevailing market value. Banks cannot negotiate directly with borrowers post-recovery.

If a bank fails to sell the property through auction, it may approach the Asset Reconstruction Company (ARC) or take other legally permissible routes. The property cannot be held indefinitely by the bank.

Impact On Borrowers And Banks

For borrowers, the move removes any chance of keeping their property after a default. It sends a clear signal that defaulting on a loan will result in permanent loss of the asset. This could improve repayment discipline.

For banks, the recovery process becomes more straightforward. They can now focus on realising the best possible value from auctioning properties to genuine third-party buyers. This could improve recovery rates and reduce the stock of non-performing assets (NPAs).

The RBI's action comes amid rising stress in the banking sector. Gross NPAs of scheduled commercial banks stood at 2.8% as of March 2026, with some segments showing higher delinquency rates. The central bank has been tightening norms to ensure faster and more genuine resolution.

What Banks Must Do Now

Banks have been directed to review their existing recovery processes and ensure compliance with the new rule. They must update their internal policies and auction documentation to reflect the restriction. Any ongoing sale processes involving the borrower must be halted immediately.

The RBI has warned that non-compliance will attract supervisory action. Banks found violating the directive could face penalties or restrictions on their recovery operations.

Industry experts say the move will bring more discipline to the recovery ecosystem. "This is a welcome step. It prevents collusive deals between banks and borrowers and ensures that recovered assets go to genuine buyers at fair prices," a senior banking analyst told NDTV Profit.

Banks are expected to issue revised auction guidelines in the coming weeks. The impact of the rule will become clearer once the first few auctions under the new framework are completed.
