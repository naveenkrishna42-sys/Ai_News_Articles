India, U.S. Space Officials Meet at ISRO Bengaluru HQ for Civil Cooperation Talks

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • India-U.S. Civil Space Joint Working Group met at ISRO Headquarters in Bengaluru
  • The meeting focused on expanding bilateral civil space cooperation between the two nations
  • Both sides discussed potential collaboration in satellite navigation, earth observation, and space exploration

Bilateral Space Dialogue in Bengaluru

The India–U.S. Civil Space Joint Working Group convened at the ISRO Headquarters in Bengaluru on Wednesday, bringing together officials from both nations to discuss ongoing and future collaborative efforts in civil space activities. The meeting marks another step in the long-standing partnership between the Indian Space Research Organisation and American space agencies.

Representatives from both sides engaged in detailed discussions covering multiple domains of mutual interest in space science and technology. The working group serves as a key platform for coordinating civil space initiatives between the two countries.

Areas of Discussion

According to the official release from ISRO, the meeting addressed cooperation in satellite navigation systems, earth observation missions, and space exploration activities. Both delegations explored potential joint projects that could leverage the complementary strengths of each nation's space programme.

The discussions also covered policy-level coordination and regulatory frameworks that govern bilateral space collaboration. Such frameworks help ensure smooth execution of joint missions and technology-sharing arrangements.

Long-Standing Partnership

India and the United States have developed significant space cooperation over the past two decades. The relationship spans satellite launches, data sharing, and collaborative research initiatives. American companies have utilized Indian launch services, while Indian scientists have contributed to various international space projects.

The Civil Space Joint Working Group specifically focuses on non-military applications, emphasizing peaceful uses of outer space. This distinction allows for open collaboration in scientific and commercial space activities.

Looking Ahead

Officials indicated that follow-up meetings and technical exchanges may be scheduled in the coming months to advance specific proposals discussed during the Bengaluru session. The outcomes of the working group meeting will inform future bilateral engagements in the civil space sector.
