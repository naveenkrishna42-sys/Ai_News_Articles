16 Delhi Metro Stations Shut Amid CJP Protest; Commuters Hit Hard

📅 2026-07-23 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • 16 metro stations including Rajiv Chowk and ITO were closed for over seven hours
  • The shutdown was prompted by security concerns during a protest by the CJP
  • Opposition criticised the move, citing heavy-handed tactics by plainclothes personnel

Massive Disruption on Delhi Metro Network

Commuters across Delhi faced severe disruption on Wednesday as the Delhi Metro shut down 16 stations for over seven hours. The closures, which began early in the day, were triggered by security concerns linked to a protest by the Confederation of Indian Professionals (CJP).

Key stations including Rajiv Chowk, ITO, and several others on busy corridors were sealed. Thousands of office-goers, students, and daily travellers were left stranded or forced to find alternate routes.

Government Cites Security, Opposition Cries Foul

Officials stated that the decision was taken to prevent any untoward incident during the protest. However, the opposition slammed the government, alleging a heavy-handed crackdown. Reports emerged of plainclothes personnel carrying nail-studded sticks being deployed at certain locations.

Critics accused authorities of overreacting and causing unnecessary hardship. They argued that shutting down a vital public transport system for hours was disproportionate and punitive.

Commuters Bear the Brunt

For daily users, the closures turned routine journeys into ordeals. Many reported waiting for hours at stations that remained open, while others abandoned the metro altogether and took crowded buses or autos.

Social media was flooded with complaints and pictures of long queues outside unaffected stations. The Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) issued periodic updates but did not initially specify a timeline for reopening.

Metro Services Resume After Extended Delay

Services at the affected stations were gradually restored in the evening, nearly seven hours after the first closures were announced. DMRC officials confirmed that normal operations resumed after a thorough security review.

The exact number of personnel deployed and the nature of the security threat have not been officially disclosed. Neither the CJP nor the Delhi Police have issued detailed statements on the events that led to the shutdown.

Observers are now watching for any official clarification on the security protocols that govern such large-scale disruptions, and whether the government will face further political heat over the handling of the protest.
