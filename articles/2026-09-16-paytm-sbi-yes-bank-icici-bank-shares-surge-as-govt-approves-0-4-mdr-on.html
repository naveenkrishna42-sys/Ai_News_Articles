Paytm, SBI, YES Bank, ICICI Bank Shares Surge as Govt Approves 0.4% MDR on UPI Transactions Above ₹2,000

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-16 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • 0.4% MDR applies only to UPI transactions exceeding ₹2,000, aiming to boost revenue sustainability.
  • Paytm led the rally with ~6% intraday gain; banks saw 2‑4% upside on fee‑sharing expectations.
  • Potential risks include merchant pushback, regulatory scrutiny, and limited transaction volume above the threshold.

Overview of the New MDR Policy

The Government of India, backed by the RBI, has introduced a 0.4% merchant discount rate (MDR) on Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions that exceed ₹2,000. The move is positioned as a measure to ensure the long‑term financial sustainability of the UPI ecosystem, which has hitherto operated at zero cost for merchants. The fee will be levied on the merchant side and shared among the acquiring bank, the issuing bank, and the UPI infrastructure provider.

Only transactions above the ₹2,000 threshold attract the charge; smaller retail payments remain free. This tiered approach attempts to balance revenue generation for payment players with the policy goal of keeping low‑value digital transactions cost‑free for consumers and small businesses.

Immediate Market Reaction

Following the announcement, Paytm’s stock jumped approximately 6% intraday, outperforming the Nifty 50’s 0.8% rise. Shares of State Bank of India (SBI), YES Bank and ICICI Bank each gained between 2% and 4% as investors anticipated a new revenue stream from fee sharing on high‑value UPI settlements.

Trading volumes in the affected counters spiked to 1.5‑2 times their average daily levels, indicating heightened investor interest. Analysts noted that the price move reflects a re‑rating of the payment firms’ forward earnings, assuming a modest uptake of the MDR on eligible transactions.

Financial Impact Assessment

To gauge the potential revenue uplift, consider that UPI processed roughly ₹13 lakh crore in FY 2025‑26, with about 12% of transaction value occurring above ₹2,000. Applying a 0.4% MDR on that slice yields an estimated gross fee pool of ₹624 crore annually. After the typical 50‑50 split between acquiring and issuing banks and a small share to the NPCI‑UPI platform, each participating bank could see incremental fee income in the range of ₹150‑200 crore per year, depending on market share.

For Paytm, which acts as both a payment gateway and a wallet provider, the MDR could add roughly ₹80‑100 crore to its FY 2026‑27 revenue base, translating to a 3‑4% boost assuming current merchant acquisition rates.

Key Risks and Outlook

Merchant associations have voiced concern that the fee may discourage high‑value UPI adoption, pushing customers back to card networks or cash for large purchases. If the share of transactions above ₹2,000 falls significantly, the projected revenue upside could be halved.

Regulatory scrutiny remains a factor; any future revision of the MDR threshold or rate would directly affect earnings forecasts. Additionally, competitive pressure from other fintech players offering zero‑fee incentives could limit Paytm’s ability to pass the cost onto merchants.

Overall, the policy creates a modest but tangible revenue opportunity for payment firms and banks, contingent on merchant acceptance and transaction mix stability. Investors should monitor quarterly MDR‑related disclosures and merchant feedback to gauge the sustainability of the early share price gains.
