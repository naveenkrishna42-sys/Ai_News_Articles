Ashish Yadav wins javelin silver at U20 Worlds, 10 years after Neeraj's gold

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ashish Yadav wins silver in javelin at World Athletics U20 Championships, India's first medal in the event since Neeraj Chopra's 2016 gold.
  • Pooja enters high jump final; two Indians qualify for long jump medal round; Amanat Kamboj makes women's discus throw final.
  • The performance signals growing depth in Indian athletics at the junior level, with multiple finalists across events.

A decade later, a new name emerges

Ten years after Neeraj Chopra stunned the world with gold at the World U20 Championships, India has another javelin medallist. Ashish Yadav claimed silver at the 2026 World Athletics U20 Championships, opening India's medal account at the meet.

The achievement ends a long wait for Indian javelin throwers at this level. Since Chopra's historic throw in 2016, no Indian had reached the podium in the event. Yadav's silver now places him in that select company, even as his career is just beginning to take shape.

While details of his winning throw are still awaited, the result has already been confirmed by multiple reports. The silver is India's first medal at these championships, a significant early boost for the contingent.

More Indians in medal contention

Yadav's silver wasn't the only reason for Indian fans to smile. Pooja has entered the high jump final, keeping alive hopes of another podium finish. Two Indians have also qualified for the long jump medal round, though their names are yet to be officially announced.

In the women's discus throw, Amanat Kamboj has made the final, adding to the growing list of Indian finalists. The spread of events—from throws to jumps—suggests a broadening of India's athletics base beyond the traditional sprint and distance events.

This is a marked shift from a decade ago, when Chopra's gold was seen as an outlier. Now, multiple Indians are competing at the business end of global youth championships across disciplines.

Building on Chopra's legacy

Chopra's 2016 gold in Bydgoszcz was a watershed moment for Indian athletics. It announced the arrival of a generational talent who would go on to win Olympic and world titles. For young throwers like Yadav, that path has become a blueprint.

Yadav's silver is unlikely to draw the same headlines as Chopra's gold, but its significance is not lost on those tracking Indian athletics. It proves that the 2016 result was not a one-off, but the beginning of a pipeline.

The challenge now is consistency. Junior medals do not guarantee senior success, as many before have shown. But they do provide the exposure and confidence that can propel an athlete to the next level.

What lies ahead

With the high jump, long jump, and discus finals still to come, India's medal tally at the U20 Worlds could grow. The performances so far indicate a well-prepared contingent, and the athletes will look to capitalise on their form.

For Yadav, the immediate focus will be on translating this silver into a strong senior career. The Asian Games and the Olympics remain the ultimate targets, and this result gives him a solid foundation.

All eyes will now be on the upcoming finals, as Indian fans hope for more podium finishes. The next few days will reveal whether this silver is the start of a larger haul or a lone bright spot.
