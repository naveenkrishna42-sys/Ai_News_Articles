US urges free, fair polls in PoK amid Pakistan crackdown and protests

📅 2026-08-07 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • US urges Pakistan to exercise restraint and act according to law in PoK.
  • UN chief backs a probe into killings during protests in PoK.
  • Pakistan alleges an 'Operation Sindoor 2.0' plot by India behind poll protests.

The United States has called for 'free and fair' elections in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK) as Pakistan faces mounting international scrutiny over a violent crackdown on protesters and unrest tied to the ongoing poll process. The demand came amid reports of deaths and injuries during demonstrations that have rocked the region in recent days.

A US official, speaking on condition of anonymity, urged Islamabad to 'exercise restraint and act according to law' while dealing with the protests. The statement signals growing concern in Washington over the deteriorating situation in PoK, where local groups have been demonstrating against the electoral exercise.

UN backs investigation into protest killings

The United Nations has joined the chorus of concern, with the UN chief backing a probe into the killings of protesters in PoK. The endorsement adds weight to calls for accountability, though no formal investigation mechanism has been announced yet.

The UN's stance reflects unease over the use of force against civilians in a region already marked by decades of political and military tension. Diplomats say the organisation is watching the situation closely, but any concrete action remains uncertain.

Pakistan's counter-allegation against India

In a sharp escalation, Pakistan has blamed India for the unrest in PoK, alleging a plot under the codename 'Operation Sindoor 2.0' to destabilise the region during elections. Islamabad claims New Delhi is orchestrating protests to disrupt the poll process, though it has not provided public evidence to support the charge.

Indian officials have dismissed the allegation as baseless, pointing to Pakistan's internal troubles. The exchange has further inflamed rhetoric between the two nuclear-armed neighbours, even as the ground situation in PoK remains volatile.

What is driving the protests?

The protests in PoK appear to stem from local grievances over the electoral process, including perceptions of a rigged system and limited political representation. Residents have taken to the streets in several towns, leading to clashes with security forces.

Reports from the region describe a heavy security presence, curfews in some areas, and a communications blackout that has made independent verification difficult. Casualty figures have not been officially confirmed, but local sources cite multiple deaths.

Global reactions and diplomatic pressure

The US call for restraint is part of a broader international response, with human rights groups also urging Pakistan to halt the crackdown. The timing is awkward for Islamabad, which has sought to project stability ahead of the election results.

Analysts note that the unrest could undermine the credibility of the poll outcome, even if voting proceeds. The Pakistani government, however, insists the elections are free and fair and has rejected external interference.

As the situation unfolds, the focus now shifts to whether Pakistan will allow an independent inquiry into the protest deaths and how India responds to the 'Operation Sindoor 2.0' claim. Both sides have shown little appetite for de-escalation, leaving the region on edge.
