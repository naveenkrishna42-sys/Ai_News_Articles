Vodafone Idea Q1 loss narrows to ₹3,754 crore, revenue up 6%

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Revenue up 6% YoY to ₹11,689 crore
  • ARPU rises 10.2% to ₹195
  • Loss narrows to ₹3,754 crore from ₹6,579 crore
  • Exceptional gain and customer additions aid performance

Vodafone Idea has reported a narrower quarterly loss for the April-June period, helped by higher per-user revenue and an exceptional gain. The telecom operator's consolidated net loss stood at ₹3,754 crore, down 43% from ₹6,579 crore in the same quarter last year.

Revenue from operations rose 6% year-on-year to ₹11,689 crore, in line with street expectations. The company's average revenue per user (ARPU), a key metric for telecom firms, jumped 10.2% to ₹195, aided by tariff hikes and better subscriber mix.

Operational metrics show mixed trends

Vodafone Idea added customers during the quarter, though the pace of additions remained modest. The company's subscriber base stood at around 210 million at the end of June, according to company disclosures.

Data usage per subscriber continued to climb, with monthly data consumption per user rising to nearly 20 GB. The company also reported an exceptional gain during the quarter, which helped cushion the bottom line, though details of the gain were not immediately specified.

ARPU growth and tariff outlook

The 10.2% sequential jump in ARPU reflects the impact of tariff revisions implemented in the previous year. Vodafone Idea has been focusing on upgrading customers to higher-value plans, a strategy that has started to pay off.

Analysts note that further ARPU improvement will depend on the company's ability to retain premium customers and expand 4G coverage. The company's 4G subscriber base grew marginally during the quarter, indicating steady but slow progress.

Debt and capital expenditure

Vodafone Idea's gross debt remained elevated at around ₹2.1 lakh crore, including deferred spectrum payments and AGR liabilities. The company has been in talks with lenders for additional funding, though no final agreement has been announced.

Capital expenditure for the quarter was modest, as the company continues to prioritise deleveraging over aggressive network expansion. Management has previously indicated that it plans to roll out 5G in select circles once funding is tied up.

What analysts are watching

Investors will be keenly tracking the company's ability to narrow losses further in coming quarters. The telecom sector's competitive dynamics remain intense, with Reliance Jio and Bharti Airtel continuing to invest heavily in network infrastructure.

Vodafone Idea's market share has stabilised in recent months, but the company still faces significant challenges in matching rivals' capital spending. The outcome of its ongoing fundraising efforts will be critical to its medium-term trajectory.

The company's board is expected to meet later this month to consider a fresh equity raise, according to sources familiar with the matter. Any announcement on this front could provide a clearer picture of how Vodafone Idea plans to fund its 5G rollout and service debt.
