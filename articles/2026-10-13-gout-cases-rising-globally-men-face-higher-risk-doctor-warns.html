Why Gout Cases Are Increasing Worldwide

Gout, a form of inflammatory arthritis, has seen a steady rise in cases globally, with men being disproportionately affected. According to recent health reports, the condition is becoming more common due to factors such as aging populations, dietary habits, and rising obesity rates. Dr. R.K. Sharma, a rheumatologist based in Delhi, explains that hyperuricemia—the buildup of uric acid in the blood—is the primary cause of gout and can lead to severe joint damage if left untreated.

Early Warning Signs Every Patient Should Recognize

The classic symptom of gout is a sudden, severe burning pain in one joint, often the big toe. This episode typically occurs at night and may be accompanied by redness, warmth, and extreme tenderness. Other early signs include persistent joint stiffness and unexplained fatigue. Dr. Sharma emphasizes that early diagnosis and intervention are critical to preventing recurrent attacks and long-term joint complications.

Who Is Most at Risk?

Men are more likely to develop gout than women, especially after the age of 40. This gender disparity is partly due to lower uric acid excretion in men and higher purine intake through alcohol and red meat. Postmenopausal women also face an increased risk. Additional risk factors include a family history of gout, chronic conditions such as hypertension and diabetes, and certain medications like diuretics. Obesity further amplifies the risk by increasing uric acid production and reducing its elimination.

Preventive Measures That Actually Work

Managing gout effectively requires a combination of dietary changes and medical treatment. Patients are advised to limit intake of high-purine foods such as shellfish, organ meats, and sugary beverages. Alcohol, particularly beer, should be consumed sparingly. Weight loss, regular exercise, and staying hydrated can significantly reduce the frequency of gout attacks. Medications such as urate-lowering therapies help control blood uric acid levels and prevent flare-ups.

When to See a Specialist

While occasional joint pain may seem minor, repeated gout attacks can lead to permanent joint damage and kidney problems. Dr. Sharma recommends consulting a rheumatologist if symptoms persist or worsen. Blood tests can confirm elevated uric acid levels, and imaging may be necessary to assess joint damage. With proper management, most patients can lead active lives without the fear of sudden, debilitating pain.
