Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 Campaign: The 'Other Tim Cook' Gambit Explained

  • At ₹1,64,999, it remains a luxury purchase, but Samsung's trade-in and financing options lower the entry barrier.

Samsung's Unconventional Marketing Move

Samsung has ignited social media by enlisting a different Tim Cook—a namesake of Apple's CEO—to promote the Galaxy Z Fold 8. The campaign, first reported by Forbes, plays on the tech world's obsession with Apple versus Samsung. It's a cheeky nod to the competition, but does it mask the Fold 8's real improvements? We dissect the phone's specifications, pricing, and practical value for Indian buyers.

Galaxy Z Fold 8: Key Specifications and Upgrades

The Fold 8 refines the foldable formula with meaningful hardware upgrades. Samsung claims a 15% lighter hinge mechanism, reducing the phone's weight to 239 grams—noticeably lighter than the Fold 7's 253 grams. The cover display now measures 6.5 inches with a 120Hz adaptive refresh rate, while the inner 7.9-inch AMOLED 2X panel peaks at 2,600 nits, making it usable under harsh sunlight. The crease is less visible thanks to a new water-drop hinge design.

Performance Benchmarks and Real-World Usage

Early benchmarks show the Snapdragon 8 Gen 4 for Galaxy scoring around 2.2 million on AnTuTu, a 20% jump over the Fold 7. In practical terms, this means smoother multitasking with up to four apps in split-screen and faster AI processing for features like live translation and photo editing. The 4,600mAh battery lasts about 9 hours of continuous video streaming, roughly on par with last year's model. Charging remains at 45W, taking 30 minutes to reach 50%—not class-leading, but adequate.

Pricing and Value in the Indian Market

At ₹1,64,999 for the base variant, the Fold 8 undercuts the hypothetical iPhone Fold but sits above the OnePlus Open 2 (₹1,39,999). Samsung offers trade-in bonuses up to ₹40,000 and no-cost EMI for 24 months, bringing the effective monthly outlay to about ₹6,875. For professionals who rely on a single device for work and play, the Fold 8's desktop-mode DeX and S Pen support (sold separately) justify the premium. However, the lack of a bundled charger and the expensive repairs (inner screen replacement costs ₹35,000) are significant drawbacks.

Pros and Cons: What Buyers Must Know

  • Pros: Lighter and more durable design; brighter, less creased display; top-tier performance; versatile camera system; long software support (4 years OS, 5 years security).
  • Cons: High price; slow charging compared to Chinese rivals; S Pen not included; repair costs remain prohibitive; marketing stunt may distract from genuine innovation.

The Bottom Line

Samsung's decision to hire a different Tim Cook is a clever publicity stunt, but the Galaxy Z Fold 8 stands on its own merits. It's a refined foldable with tangible upgrades, albeit at a luxury price. Buyers seeking the best foldable experience should consider it—just don't let the marketing gimmick overshadow the engineering.
