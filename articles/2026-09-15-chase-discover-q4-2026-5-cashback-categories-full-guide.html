Chase and Discover have confirmed their rotating 5% cashback categories for the fourth quarter of 2026, and the overlap between the two programmes is unusually narrow. That matters for anyone running a two-card cashback stack, because the value of each card depends less on the headline 5% rate and more on how well the categories match actual household spend between October and December.

Q4 2026 Categories At A Glance

Both issuers cap bonus earnings at $1,500 in combined purchases per quarter, after which the rate falls back to the standard 1%. That ceiling produces a maximum bonus of $75 per card, or $150 if a cardholder runs both programmes in full. Activation is not automatic.

Why The Cap Matters More Than The Rate

A 5% rate sounds like five times the value of a flat 1% card, but the $1,500 ceiling compresses the real advantage. Once a cardholder crosses that threshold, every additional dollar earns 1%, so the effective rate on $3,000 of quarterly category spend is only 3%. Cardholders who routinely spend above the cap should treat the bonus as a fixed $75 rebate rather than an open-ended multiplier.

For Indian cardholders spending in rupees, the same maths applies after conversion and foreign exchange markup. On a $1,500 cap, the maximum bonus is roughly ₹6,600 at prevailing exchange rates, before any 2% to 3.5% forex fee that most US-issued cards levy on international transactions. That fee can wipe out a meaningful slice of the bonus on overseas spend.

Activation Deadlines And Practical Timing

Chase typically requires activation through the issuer's website or app, and the bonus applies only to purchases made after activation. Discover follows a similar model. Purchases made before activation earn the base rate and cannot be retroactively upgraded, which is the single most common reason cardholders lose out on the full $75.

The practical move is to activate in the final week of September, before the quarter opens, then route category spend to the relevant card and everything else to a flat-rate card. Splitting spend this way typically lifts a two-card blended rate above 3%, which is competitive against most premium travel cards carrying annual fees.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Maximum bonus is capped at $75 per card, per quarter, regardless of how much is spent.
  • Activation is mandatory and is not applied retroactively to earlier purchases.
  • Both cards carry no annual fee, so the bonus has no offsetting cost.
  • Overlapping categories between Chase and Discover reduce the benefit of holding both.
  • Foreign transaction fees on overseas spend can erode a large share of the bonus.
  • Unused category capacity cannot be carried into the next quarter.

Buyer Verdict

For a cardholder who can direct $1,500 of quarterly spend into the bonus categories, each card delivers a clean $75 rebate with no annual fee and no redemption complexity. The case for holding both weakens when the categories overlap, since the second card adds little beyond another $75 ceiling. A single 5% card paired with a flat 2% card for non-category purchases remains the more efficient two-card setup for most households.

Anyone planning large Q4 purchases, particularly electronics or holiday travel, should time them to land inside the activated window and inside the $1,500 cap. Spend beyond that point is better routed to whichever card in the wallet pays the highest flat rate.
