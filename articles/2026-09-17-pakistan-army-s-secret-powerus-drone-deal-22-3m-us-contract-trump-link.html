Pakistan Army's Undisclosed Drone Order: What We Know

The Pakistan Army has placed an undisclosed order for unmanned aerial systems (UAS) with POWERUS, a US drone manufacturer that recently secured a $22.3 million contract from the US Department of Defense. The order, first reported by Moneycontrol and later confirmed by Reuters and the Financial Times, follows a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) signed between POWERUS and the Pakistan Army. Neither the quantity nor the exact variant of drones has been disclosed, leaving defence analysts questioning the transparency of Pakistan's procurement process.

POWERUS has gained attention not only for its defence contracts but also for its reported links to former US President Donald Trump's sons, who are said to have financial interests in the firm. This connection adds a geopolitical dimension to the deal, potentially complicating US export approvals and raising questions about influence peddling.

POWERUS Drone Specifications and Pricing Context

While POWERUS has not publicly detailed the specific model sold to Pakistan, its flagship tactical UAS platforms typically fall in the medium-range, medium-endurance category. The $22.3 million US contract likely covers a batch of drones, ground control stations, and support services. For Pakistan, the unit cost remains undisclosed, but comparable systems from US and Chinese suppliers range from $1-5 million per unit depending on payload and endurance.

Real-World Buyer Implications for Pakistan

Pakistan's defence budget for 2026 stands at approximately PKR 2.1 trillion (around $7.5 billion), with a significant portion allocated to modernisation. The drone order, while small in absolute terms, signals a shift towards US suppliers after years of reliance on Chinese platforms like the CH-4 and Wing Loong. However, the lack of transparency raises red flags: without knowing unit costs, maintenance terms, or technology transfer provisions, it is impossible to assess value for money.

Operationally, POWERUS drones could enhance Pakistan's ISR (intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance) capabilities along contested borders. But integration with existing Chinese and Turkish systems may pose interoperability challenges. Furthermore, US export controls (ITAR) could restrict Pakistan's ability to modify or redeploy the drones, limiting long-term flexibility.

Hidden Risks and Geopolitical Fallout

The Trump family connection introduces a unique risk: if the former president returns to office or maintains influence, the deal could face heightened scrutiny from US lawmakers concerned about conflicts of interest. For Pakistan, this means potential delays or even cancellation if political winds shift. Additionally, the undisclosed nature of the order may violate Pakistan's own Public Procurement Regulatory Authority (PPRA) rules, which mandate transparency for defence purchases above certain thresholds.

  • Pros:

    • Potential for future US defence cooperation and training.
    • Diversification away from Chinese suppliers, reducing dependency.

    • Trump family links create political and reputational risks.

What Remains Hidden and Why It Matters

Critical details such as the number of drones, unit cost, delivery schedule, and offset obligations remain undisclosed. Without this information, Pakistani taxpayers cannot hold the military accountable for spending. For international observers, the deal raises questions about US arms export ethics and the blurring line between private business and foreign policy. As Pakistan deepens ties with a Trump-linked firm, the strategic calculus extends beyond the battlefield—it is a test of governance and transparency.
