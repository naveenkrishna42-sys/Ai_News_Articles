India's growth lags behind Modi's $30 trillion dream

India's growth rate falls short of Modi's ambitious target

Prime Minister Narendra Modi's vision of transforming India into a developed nation by 2047 faces a significant challenge. The country's current growth rate of 7% is below the 8% target needed to reach the $30 trillion economy by the year 2047. Economic experts and analysts are raising concerns about the feasibility of this ambitious goal.

Experts call for a new reform push to boost growth

Montek Singh Ahluwalia, a former chief economic advisor to the government, has suggested that India needs a new reform push similar to the 1991 economic liberalization to achieve the desired growth rate. He believes that structural reforms in key sectors like agriculture, infrastructure, and digital economy are crucial to bridge the gap between India's current growth and the target set by the government.

The Viksit Bharat 2047 book explores India's path to a developed nation

The book 'Viksit Bharat 2047' delves into the strategies and policies that India needs to adopt to become a developed nation by the year 2047. The book outlines a comprehensive plan that includes investments in infrastructure, education, and healthcare, among other sectors. However, the feasibility of the plan and the ability of India to achieve the target remains a topic of debate among experts.

India's growth rate may be too slow for Viksit Bharat

Despite India's growing economy, the current growth rate of 7% may be too slow to achieve the $30 trillion economy by 2047. Economic analysts and policymakers are grappling with the challenge of balancing growth with equity and sustainability. The question of who benefits from the growth and how it is distributed across the country is a key concern.
