Pakistan's 4-0 Defeat: A Black Mark on PCB's Record

Pakistan's cricketing woes reached a new low as they suffered their worst Test series defeat in 70 years, losing 4-0 to England. The Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB), led by Mohsin Naqvi, is now under intense scrutiny, with critics questioning the board's leadership and performance.

Ex-Pakistan Captain's Scathing Criticism

Former Pakistan captain Rashid Latif has not held back in his criticism of the current PCB leadership. He has called the board 'the worst in 70 years', highlighting the dismal performance of the team in England. Latif's comments come at a time when Pakistan is grappling with a series of poor performances, both at home and abroad.

Key Players Struggle in England

Pakistan's key players, including captain Babar Azam and pace bowler Shaheen Afridi, have struggled in England. Babar's average of 21.5 in the series is his lowest in Tests, while Shaheen's economy rate of 2.94 is the worst of his career. The lack of consistency from the top players has been a major concern for the PCB.

PCB's Handling of Players and Team Selection

The PCB's handling of players and team selection has also come under fire. The board's decision to rest key players for the second Test in England has been criticized, with many believing it was a missed opportunity to give the players a rest. The PCB's approach to team selection has been a major talking point, with critics questioning the board's strategy.

Broader Impact on Pakistani Cricket

The PCB's struggles have had a broader impact on Pakistani cricket. The team's poor performance in England has raised questions about the future of Pakistani cricket, with many believing that the team needs a change in leadership and strategy. The PCB's ability to bounce back from this series will be crucial in determining the future of Pakistani cricket.
