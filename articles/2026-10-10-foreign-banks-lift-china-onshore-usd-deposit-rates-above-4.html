Foreign banks in China push onshore USD deposit yields above 4%

Foreign banks operating in China are now offering onshore US dollar (USD) deposit rates that exceed 4%, according to BigGo Finance reporting. The move also follows a year-to-date cumulative increase of more than 1 percentage point, suggesting that USD funding costs and deposit demand have shifted meaningfully over 2026.

For commercial and individual savers who hold or temporarily park USD funds onshore, deposit yields are a direct lever on returns. But the practical value depends on the contract details: deposit term, compounding, payout frequency, and whether withdrawals and renewals are frictionless.

What a 1%+ rate increase changes for depositors

A cumulative rise of over 1 percentage point is not a cosmetic change. If your USD balance remains in the same instrument for a full period, a 1% higher rate can translate into a noticeable uplift in interest, especially when USD cash is otherwise earning far less in comparable locations.

Still, buyers should treat headline rates as an entry point, not a guarantee of net returns. In real life, the “effective” yield can be reduced by FX spread at conversion time, transfer fees, early withdrawal penalties, and any restrictions on how frequently you can roll over the deposit.

  • Tenor matters: a higher headline rate may apply only to longer maturities.
  • Compounding matters: annual vs. monthly interest payments can change effective earnings.
  • Liquidity matters: if you might need funds early, check penalty rules and notice periods.

Buyer checklist: how to evaluate foreign-bank USD deposits in China

Foreign banks’ onshore USD products typically target customers comfortable with USD governance and account administration. That makes process and costs as important as the interest rate. Use the checklist below when comparing offers across banks and deposit tenors.

First, verify the exact rate basis (nominal annual rate), maturity, and interest posting schedule. Second, confirm rollover terms: some deposits auto-renew at a new rate, while others require action. Third, audit fees: inbound/outbound wire charges, custody or account maintenance fees, and any account minimum requirements.

Finally, factor FX and operational risk. Even if the account is denominated in USD, your broader financial plan may involve converting to RMB or repatriating USD later. The FX spread and transfer mechanics can easily outweigh a small headline-rate difference, particularly for smaller balances.

Pros, cons, and real-world takeaways for cash management

The increase beyond 4% is a clear signal that USD deposit pricing is becoming more competitive among foreign banks onshore in China. For depositors with USD liquidity needs, this can be an opportunity to improve carry returns—if the operational terms are favorable.

  • Pros: Higher carry on idle USD funds; stronger negotiating leverage when comparing tenors across foreign banks.
  • Pros: Year-to-date gains of 1 percentage point+ improve forward return expectations if you can lock in at matching terms.
  • Cons: Rate lift may concentrate in longer maturities, reducing flexibility.
  • Cons: Net yield can shrink after FX spreads, transfer costs, and early withdrawal penalties.
  • Cons: Operational rules (rollover approvals, settlement timelines) can affect how quickly you can reallocate funds.

For anyone treating onshore USD deposits as a cash-management tool, the “4%+” headline is the start of diligence. The real decision should be based on net effective yield over your expected holding period, total fees, and how easily you can convert, withdraw, or roll without breaking terms.
