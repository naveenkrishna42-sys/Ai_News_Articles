Iran warns Red Sea chokepoint: How a crisis at Bab el-Mandeb could hit India

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran has issued a new warning regarding the Bab el-Mandeb strait, a critical Red Sea chokepoint.
  • The strait connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden, carrying about 12% of global trade.
  • A blockage or conflict could disrupt oil shipments, trade routes, and raise costs for India.
  • India relies heavily on the Red Sea route for crude oil imports and exports to Europe and Africa.

Iran's latest warning has thrust the Bab el-Mandeb strait into the global spotlight. As tensions between Tehran and Washington show no signs of cooling, the narrow waterway — a crucial artery for global trade — is now centre stage.

The Bab el-Mandeb strait, which translates to "Gate of Tears" in Arabic, connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden. It is a vital chokepoint for oil tankers and cargo ships moving between the Mediterranean and the Indian Ocean.

Why Bab el-Mandeb matters

Roughly 12% of global seaborne trade passes through this 29-kilometre-wide channel. That includes around 3.8 million barrels of crude oil and refined products daily, according to data from the US Energy Information Administration.

For India, the stakes are particularly high. A significant portion of the country's crude oil imports — especially from Iraq and Saudi Arabia — transit the Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb. Any disruption there would push up shipping costs and insurance premiums, eventually hitting fuel prices at home.

Trade routes at risk

Beyond oil, Indian exports to Europe and North Africa — including textiles, pharmaceuticals, and machinery — also depend on this route. Rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope would add weeks to transit times and sharply raise freight costs.

India's trade with the Middle East and the Mediterranean could face severe delays. The country's maritime trade planners are watching the situation closely, though officials have not yet confirmed any contingency measures.

Iran's strategic lever

Iran has long signalled that it can disrupt shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, but the Bab el-Mandeb is now receiving similar attention. Tehran has warned that the Red Sea chokepoint is a red line, hinting at potential action if the conflict with the US escalates further.

Iran has maintained close ties with Houthi forces in Yemen, who control parts of the coast near the Bab el-Mandeb. In past conflicts, Houthi fighters have targeted ships in the area, raising the spectre of mines, drones, or missiles threatening commercial vessels.

Global ripple effects

A crisis in the Red Sea would not spare the global economy. European nations rely heavily on this route for energy imports from the Gulf. Any blockage would strain supply chains already under pressure from geopolitical instability.

Insurance premiums for ships transiting the Red Sea have already risen in recent weeks. Some shipping lines are reportedly considering temporary reroutes, though no major disruptions have been reported yet.

The situation remains fluid. Iran's warning is a clear signal that the Red Sea could become a flashpoint in the broader US-Iran confrontation. For India, the immediate concern is the stability of energy supplies and trade routes that pass through this narrow stretch of water.

What to watch: Whether Iran or its allies take any tangible action in the Bab el-Mandeb, and how global navies — including India's — respond to safeguard shipping lanes.
