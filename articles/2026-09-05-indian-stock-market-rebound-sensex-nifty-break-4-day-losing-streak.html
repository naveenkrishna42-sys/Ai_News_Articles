Indian Stock Market Rebound: Sensex & Nifty Break 4-Day Losing Streak

Why the Indian Stock Market Rebounded Today

Indian benchmark indices snapped a four-day losing streak with a decisive upmove, as buying returned to banking, information technology, and automotive counters. The BSE Sensex climbed over 500 points in intraday trade before settling near the day's high, while the NSE Nifty 50 reclaimed the 24,800 zone after testing crucial support at 24,500 earlier in the week. The recovery came on the back of cooler crude oil prices, a softer US dollar index, and reassuring commentary from global central banks.

Market breadth turned decisively positive, with advance-decline ratios on both exchanges improving sharply compared to the previous four sessions. The relief rally signals that domestic institutional flows and long-only foreign investors used the recent correction as an accumulation opportunity rather than exiting positions.

Key Triggers Behind the Rebound

Three identifiable catalysts powered the recovery. First, a 1.2 percent drop in Brent crude to the $82 per barrel mark eased concerns about import-led inflation and improved the outlook for the current account deficit. Second, the US dollar index retreated from 103.5 to 102.8, making rupee-denominated assets more attractive for foreign portfolio investors. Third, better-than-expected services PMI data for August reinforced confidence in domestic growth resilience despite weak global manufacturing prints.

Additionally, rollover data from the September derivatives series showed long position additions in banking and consumption names, hinting at tactical bullish bets by proprietary desks ahead of upcoming macroeconomic data prints.

Sector Performance and Market Breadth

Banking heavyweight HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India contributed nearly 40 percent of the Sensex gains. The Nifty Bank index outperformed the broader market, rising over 1.1 percent, supported by expectations of stable asset quality and stable net interest margins in Q2 FY27. IT majors Tata Consultancy Services, Infosys, and Wipro rebounded 1.2 to 1.8 percent each on bargain hunting after a sharp seven-day correction.

Auto stocks surged on festive season optimism and improving rural demand indicators. Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra gained up to 2 percent, while two-wheeler leader Bajaj Auto added 1.4 percent. Metal stocks, however, remained underperformer due to weak Chinese demand signals, with Tata Steel and Hindalco closing marginally in the red.

What Investors Should Know Now

Short-term traders should monitor whether the Nifty sustains above the 24,750 pivot on closing basis, which would confirm trend reversal and open room for a test of 25,100 resistance. Long-term investors should view the rebound as confirmation that institutional money continues to treat every meaningful dip as a buying window, especially in large-cap quality names.

Investors must remain cautious about global triggers including US non-farm payrolls data and upcoming RBI policy commentary that could reintroduce selling pressure. Position sizing should reflect the elevated India VIX relative to its 12-month average, signaling that volatility regimes remain unpredictable.

Real-World Investor Highlights

  • Defensive rotation into FMCG and pharma stocks remains limited, suggesting risk appetite is returning
  • Mid-cap indices underperformed benchmarks, indicating large-cap leadership and selective participation
  • Rupee stability around ₹83.40 per dollar provided additional comfort to FII sentiment
  • Options data shows maximum Call writing at 25,000 strike, hinting at near-term resistance ceiling
