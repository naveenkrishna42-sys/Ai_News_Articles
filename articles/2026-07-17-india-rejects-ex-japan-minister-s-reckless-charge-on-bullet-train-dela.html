India rejects ex-Japan minister's 'reckless' charge on bullet train delays

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Former Japanese minister Yoshiyuki Hasegawa blamed India for delays in the Mumbai-Ahmedabad High Speed Rail project
  • The Ministry of External Affairs rejected the remarks as being based on 'considerable variance with facts'
  • India maintains the project is progressing well and remains on target for a 2027 launch

Sharp exchange over bullet train timeline

The Mumbai-Ahmedabad High Speed Rail project has become the centre of a diplomatic disagreement, with a former Japanese minister accusing India of causing delays and the Ministry of External Affairs hitting back.

Yoshiyuki Hasegawa, who previously served as Japan's Minister of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, told media outlets that India's actions amounted to 'sheer recklessness'. He claimed that bureaucratic hurdles and slow decision-making in New Delhi were pushing the project well behind schedule.

His comments triggered an unusually firm response from the MEA, which rejected the characterisation.

MEA rejects 'variance with facts'

In a statement issued on Friday, the Ministry of External Affairs said Hasegawa's remarks were based on a 'considerable variance with facts'. The MEA did not specify which claims it was disputing but insisted that talks between the two countries were 'progressing well'.

Officials pointed out that the project, which is largely funded by a Japanese soft loan, has made significant headway in land acquisition and civil construction. The MEA also reiterated that the target for commercial operations remains 2027, a timeline that both sides have agreed to in bilateral meetings.

The terse rebuttal signals India's unwillingness to accept blame for what it sees as a joint infrastructure venture facing routine challenges.

Project status and sticking points

The 508-kilometre corridor, intended to connect Mumbai and Ahmedabad with Japan's Shinkansen bullet train technology, has been in the works for nearly a decade. Land acquisition in Maharashtra has been slower than expected, and the project has faced delays in securing clearances for tunnels and bridges.

Japanese officials have privately expressed frustration over the pace of work. However, public statements from Tokyo have generally been measured, making Hasegawa's sharp critique an outlier. It remains unclear whether his comments reflect official Japanese government sentiment or are his personal views.

India has maintained that both nations are committed to the project and that technical teams continue to meet regularly to resolve outstanding issues.

What lies ahead

The next major milestone is the completion of the first section between Surat and Bilimora, which is expected to be ready for trial runs by late 2026. Both governments have invested significant political and financial capital in the project, making a public spat over delays an unwelcome development.

With the MEA's strong pushback, the ball is now in Japan's court to clarify whether Hasegawa's remarks represent a shift in its official stance or remain a solo salvo from a former minister.
