Nepal Flood Crisis Deepens as Monsoon Ravages Himalayan Region

Monsoon-triggered floods and landslides have killed at least 1,355 people across Nepal, with nearly 5,000 individuals still unaccounted for, marking one of the country's worst natural disasters in recent history. The toll includes 1,340 confirmed deaths and 15 additional fatalities reported in the last 24 hours, according to official sources. Over 4.8 million people have been displaced, with entire villages swept away in remote mountainous regions where access remains limited.

Rescue Operations Stymied by Infrastructure Damage

Rescue teams are facing severe logistical challenges as landslides have blocked over 150 kilometers of roads, including major highways connecting Kathmandu to the Terai region. Helicopter operations are grounded due to heavy rainfall and poor visibility, forcing ground teams to navigate dangerous terrain on foot. The Nepali Army, Armed Police Force, and international disaster response units are coordinating efforts, but aid distribution has slowed significantly in districts like Rasuwa, Sindhupalchok, and Nuwakot.

National Mourning Amid Rising Humanitarian Needs

The government has declared a 13-day national mourning period, with flags flying at half-mast across all provinces. Prime Minister Pushpa Kamal Dahal has appealed for international assistance, prompting aid pledges from India, China, and several global agencies. Emergency shelters housing over 600,000 survivors are struggling with shortages of clean water, medicine, and sanitation facilities, raising concerns over disease outbreaks.

Financial and Environmental Fallout Mount

Economists estimate economic losses exceeding NPR 180 billion (approximately Rs 18,000 crore), with critical infrastructure, agriculture, and hydropower projects among the hardest hit. The Koshi and Gandaki river basins experienced record-level flooding, submerging thousands of hectares of farmland and disrupting food supply chains. Environmental experts warn that unseasonably warm temperatures in the Himalayas accelerated glacial melt, intensifying flash floods and underscoring long-term climate risks.

Looking Ahead: Recovery and Risk Mitigation

With monsoon season expected to continue through late September, authorities are prioritizing evacuation drives in high-risk zones. Reconstruction plans focus on building resilient roads, early warning systems, and climate-proof housing. International donors are urged to support long-term recovery efforts, particularly in education and healthcare sectors that serve vulnerable mountain communities.
