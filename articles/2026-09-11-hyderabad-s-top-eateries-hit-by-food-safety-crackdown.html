Hyderabad’s Top Eateries Hit by Food Safety Crackdown

Key points
  • FSSAI inspection flagged critical hygiene lapses at three popular Hyderabad venues.
  • Penalties include fines, temporary closures, and mandatory corrective action plans.
  • Consumers can expect stricter monitoring and improved food safety standards moving forward.

Regulatory backdrop for Hyderabad’s food outlets

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) conducts periodic audits of restaurants, cafes and food courts across major cities. In Hyderabad, the municipal health department partners with FSSAI to enforce the Food Safety and Standards (Licensing and Registration) Act, 2011. Inspectors assess premises against a 100‑point checklist covering personal hygiene, storage practices, pest control and equipment sanitation.

Inspection trigger and scope

On 3 September 2026, a surprise audit targeted high‑traffic venues in the Banjara Hills and Jubilee Hills corridors. Inspectors focused on establishments with a seating capacity above 150 and a daily footfall exceeding 1,000 patrons. The audit report, released publicly on 9 September, listed three venues that failed to meet the mandatory 80‑point threshold.

Establishments under scrutiny

1. Sarvi Food Court

Located in a multi‑storey mall, Sarvi recorded repeated violations in cold‑chain maintenance and surface cleaning. The report noted that several freezers operated above the recommended 4 °C limit, raising the risk of bacterial growth.

2. 7 Sisters

This themed restaurant, known for its regional cuisine, was cited for inadequate pest control and the presence of unsealed food containers in the service area. Inspectors also observed cross‑contamination between raw and cooked items.

3. Habitat Café

Habitat Café, popular among freelancers, received fines for missing hand‑washing stations and improper waste segregation. The café’s espresso machine showed build‑up of mineral deposits, a breach of equipment hygiene standards.

Implications for consumers and operators

For diners, the immediate impact is heightened confidence that authorities are actively monitoring food safety. Operators, however, face operational disruptions and financial penalties that can affect profitability.

Path forward for Hyderabad’s dining sector

Restaurant owners are advised to adopt a continuous monitoring framework. This includes weekly temperature logs, quarterly pest‑control contracts, and staff training on personal hygiene. Investing in digital compliance tools can streamline record‑keeping and reduce the likelihood of surprise penalties.

Consumers should look for visible hygiene certificates displayed at entry points. Restaurants that publicly share recent inspection scores tend to attract a more discerning clientele.
