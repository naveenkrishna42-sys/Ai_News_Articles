Bay of Bengal Depression Set to Intensify into Cyclone Akvan

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued a critical warning regarding a depression currently centered over the Bay of Bengal. This weather system is projected to rapidly intensify into a severe cyclonic storm, to be named 'Akvan,' by November 17, 2026. The trajectory indicates a clear path towards the Andhra Pradesh coastline, posing a significant threat to coastal communities and infrastructure.

This development necessitates immediate attention from disaster management agencies and residents alike. The IMD's precise forecasts provide a crucial window for preparedness, mitigating potential losses and ensuring public safety.

From Depression to Severe Cyclonic Storm

The current depression, characterized by organized convection and sustained wind speeds, is drawing energy from the warm waters of the Bay of Bengal. Meteorological models consistently show a strengthening trend, leading to its classification as a severe cyclonic storm within the next 48-72 hours. This intensification means winds are expected to reach sustained speeds of 90-120 kmph, with gusts potentially exceeding 130 kmph, alongside extremely heavy rainfall.

Such rapid intensification is a common, yet dangerous, characteristic of cyclones forming in the pre-monsoon and post-monsoon seasons over the Bay of Bengal. The IMD's continuous monitoring provides real-time updates on its strength and movement, vital for accurate public advisories.

Projected Landfall and Impact Zones

Cyclone Akvan is forecast to continue its westward to northwestward movement, making its likely landfall on the Andhra Pradesh coast. The IMD's latest projections pinpoint the area between Nellore and Tuni as the probable landfall zone, expected around noon on November 20, 2026. This region is particularly vulnerable to storm surges, high winds, and torrential rainfall associated with severe cyclonic storms.

Beyond the immediate landfall area, adjacent districts in Andhra Pradesh and parts of Odisha are also bracing for significant rainfall and strong winds. Even Kolkata, far from the direct path, is expected to experience rain as the system influences broader weather patterns across eastern India.

IMD's Warnings and Preparedness Measures

The IMD's consistent and timely warnings are the cornerstone of disaster preparedness. Their advisories trigger a cascade of actions from state and local authorities, aiming to safeguard lives and minimize property damage.

Critical Alerts for Coastal Communities

The most immediate and critical alerts are directed at fishermen, who have been strongly advised not to venture into the Bay of Bengal. Those already at sea are urged to return to shore immediately. For residents in the projected path, warnings encompass heavy rainfall, destructive winds, and the potential for storm surges that can inundate low-lying coastal areas. These warnings are often accompanied by instructions regarding securing homes, preparing emergency kits, and following evacuation orders.

Governmental Response and Evacuation Protocols

Responding to the IMD's forecasts, state governments have initiated their disaster management protocols. Odisha has reportedly issued evacuation orders for vulnerable coastal areas, proactively moving residents to safer shelters. Andhra Pradesh authorities have intensified their warnings to fishermen and are activating district-level emergency response teams. These proactive measures, driven by expert meteorological predictions, are crucial in preventing casualties and ensuring an organized response to the impending natural calamity.
