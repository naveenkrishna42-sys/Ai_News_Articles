Oman intensifies oil spill containment in Dhofar; desalination plants safe

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil spill from a grounded tanker in Dhofar has spread over 400 square km.
  • Omani authorities are intensifying containment operations to limit environmental damage.
  • Desalination plants in the region are reported to be unaffected by the spill.

Spill spreads across 400 square km

Omani authorities have escalated efforts to contain a major oil spill in the Dhofar governorate after a tanker ran aground, with the slick now covering an estimated 400 square kilometres of sea. The spill, triggered by an explosion on board the vessel, has prompted a multi-agency response as teams work to prevent further environmental damage along the southern coastline.

Officials from the Ministry of Environment and Climate Affairs have not yet disclosed the exact volume of oil released, but satellite imagery and aerial surveys indicate the slick is drifting in a pattern that requires urgent intervention. Containment booms have been deployed at strategic points, while skimming vessels are operating in the affected zones.

Desalination operations unaffected

A key concern for residents and industries has been the proximity of the spill to desalination plants, which supply drinking water to large parts of the region. Authorities have confirmed that these facilities are currently operating normally, with no reported impact on water production or quality.

Engineers at the plants have been placed on heightened alert, and monitoring systems have been stepped up to detect any changes in seawater turbidity or hydrocarbon levels. The reassurance comes as a relief to coastal communities that rely heavily on desalinated water for daily use and agriculture.

Response and containment measures

The Omani government has mobilised specialised teams, including the Royal Oman Police Coast Guard and environmental response units, to tackle the spill. According to the Oman Observer, the operation is being coordinated from a command centre in Salalah, with support from regional partners and international experts.

Efforts are focused on protecting sensitive habitats, including mangrove forests and coral reefs, which are found along the Dhofar coast. Officials have also advised fishermen to avoid the affected areas, and temporary restrictions on fishing activities have been imposed in the vicinity of the spill.

Cause under investigation

The tanker was grounded after an explosion on board, the circumstances of which are still being investigated. While the crew has been safely evacuated, the vessel remains in a precarious position, and salvage teams are assessing options to stabilise it and prevent further leakage.

Weather conditions in the Arabian Sea are being closely monitored, as strong winds or currents could complicate containment operations. Authorities have not ruled out the possibility of the slick reaching shore in some locations, and clean-up crews are on standby to respond to any landfall.

The incident has drawn attention to the risks of maritime traffic in the region, which sees heavy movement of oil tankers and cargo ships. Environmental groups have called for stricter safety protocols, but the immediate focus remains on minimising damage from the current spill.

What to watch

In the coming days, the effectiveness of the containment operation will depend on sea conditions and the speed of salvage work. Authorities are expected to provide regular updates on the spill's trajectory and any potential impact on marine life. The investigation into the explosion will also be closely followed, as it may lead to new regulations for tankers operating in Omani waters.
