Bengaluru's Seshadri Road crippled by potholes, broken lights, garbage piles

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Seshadri Road in Bengaluru faces severe civic issues including large potholes.
  • Streetlights along the stretch have been non-functional for weeks.
  • Garbage piles have accumulated, leading to hygiene concerns.

Bengaluru's Seshadri Road, a key stretch in the city, is now a symbol of civic neglect. Residents say potholes have turned the road into a minefield. At night, the situation worsens because most streetlights along the stretch have been dead for weeks.

Garbage piles have mushroomed on the sidewalks. The stench is now a constant companion for those who live or work here. Commuters navigate the craters during the day, but after sunset, the darkness makes the drive treacherous.

Potholes and darkness: A daily hazard

Locals report that some potholes are nearly a foot deep. Two-wheelers often swerve dangerously to avoid them. Pedestrians have tripped and fallen, especially near the junctions with no lighting.

“It’s like playing Russian roulette every evening,” a shopkeeper on the road said, asking not to be named. “We’ve complained to the BBMP multiple times. They send a team, fill one hole, and leave the rest.”

Streetlight failures add to the danger

The streetlight failure is not a recent problem. Residents say it has persisted for over a month. Electrical wires dangle from some poles, raising the risk of electrocution during rain.

The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has not yet issued a formal statement on the timeline for repairs. Officials were unavailable for comment at the time of filing this report.

Garbage piles become a health risk

Piles of mixed waste—plastic, food scraps, and construction debris—line the road. Stray dogs rummage through the trash, scattering it further. Residents worry about disease outbreaks, especially with the monsoon approaching.

“The garbage truck hasn’t come in over a week. We’ve called the helpline, but nothing changes,” a resident said. The lack of regular collection has forced some to burn waste illegally, adding to air pollution.

Local civic activist groups have started a petition. They demand a comprehensive road repair plan, restoration of streetlights, and daily garbage collection. So far, they have gathered over 200 signatures.

What happens next

The BBMP is expected to respond after the next ward committee meeting. If no action follows, residents plan to hold a protest near the road. For now, Seshadri Road waits for a solution that seems stuck in bureaucratic potholes.
