Rupee posts strongest week since March on RBI support, oil swings

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rupee rose 20 paise to 95.30 against the US dollar in early trade on Friday.
  • The currency posted its strongest weekly performance since March, driven by suspected RBI intervention.
  • Analysts warn the rally faces tests from post-Fed US yield rises and lingering oil price worries.

The Indian rupee strengthened to 95.30 against the US dollar in early trade on Friday, gaining 20 paise, and capped its strongest weekly performance since March. The move comes as the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) suspected intervention provided a steady floor, even as global cues remained mixed.

Traders said state-run banks were seen offering dollars on behalf of the central bank, a familiar pattern that has kept the currency from sliding past key levels. The support has been particularly visible in recent sessions, with the rupee swinging in a tight band before closing nearly flat at 95.68 on Thursday.

Central bank support steadies the ship

The RBI's presence in the market has been the defining feature of the week. By absorbing excess dollar demand, the central bank has cushioned the rupee from sharp falls, even when oil prices spiked or the US dollar firmed overseas.

This intervention, traders note, is not about targeting a specific level but about curbing volatility. The result has been a currency that moves with less drama than its Asian peers, many of which have seen sharper swings this month.

Still, the rupee's gains remain fragile. The rally is being tested by a post-Federal Reserve rise in US Treasury yields, which typically pulls capital away from emerging markets. Higher yields make dollar-denominated assets more attractive, and that pressure is unlikely to fade quickly.

Oil and US yields keep traders on edge

Oil prices are the other lingering worry. India imports over 80% of its crude requirements, and any sustained jump in prices widens the trade deficit and weighs on the currency. Recent swings in global crude benchmarks have kept forex traders guessing.

Prachi Kele, Lead Economist at PL Capital, offered a cautious outlook on the currency's path. According to her, the rupee's trajectory will depend heavily on how global yields move and whether oil stays range-bound. She did not rule out further depreciation pressure if these factors turn adverse.

The US Federal Reserve's policy stance remains a key variable. While the Fed has signalled patience on rate cuts, markets are pricing in a gradual easing cycle. Any hawkish surprise could strengthen the dollar and push the rupee back toward the 96 mark.

What lies ahead for the rupee

For now, the RBI's toolkit appears adequate. The central bank has ample reserves to defend the currency, and its intervention strategy has earned it credibility among market participants.

But the fundamental pressures have not disappeared. The trade deficit is wide, foreign portfolio outflows have been intermittent, and global risk sentiment can shift quickly. The rupee's resilience this week is a testament to central bank support, not a sign that the storm has passed.

Market participants will now watch the next set of US economic data and the RBI's monthly bulletin for clues on policy direction. A break above 95.30 could open the door to further gains, but a reversal in oil or yields could just as easily erase the week's progress.

The coming sessions will test whether the rupee can hold its ground without constant central bank backing. For now, the floor is firm, but the ceiling remains uncertain.
