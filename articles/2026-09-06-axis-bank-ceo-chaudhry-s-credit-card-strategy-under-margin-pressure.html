Axis Bank's Credit Card Gambit: Growth Amid Margin Compression

Axis Bank's credit card portfolio continues expanding despite net interest margin headwinds, with CEO Amitabh Chaudhry prioritizing customer acquisition over immediate profitability. The bank's credit card loan book grew 16% year-on-year in FY2026, reaching ₹1.8 trillion even as NIM compressed to 3.4% from 3.8% in the previous fiscal.

Digital-First Acquisition Strategy

Chaudhry's 'unfinished agenda' centers on digitizing customer onboarding and leveraging data analytics for targeted offers. The bank launched three new premium cards in Q1 2026, partnering with major e-commerce platforms to drive co-branded acquisitions. Digital channels now account for 72% of new credit card customers, reducing acquisition costs by 28% compared to branch-led sales.

Premium Card Portfolio Expansion

Axis focuses on high-ticket credit segments that generate superior fee income. The Signature and Titanium card portfolios grew 22% YoY, contributing 45% of total credit card revenue despite representing only 18% of outstanding cards. This shift improves average revenue per user from ₹2,800 to ₹4,200 annually.

Margin Pressure Management Tactics

With NIM declining across the banking sector, Axis employs dynamic pricing models and tiered reward structures. The bank introduced income-based eligibility scoring, allowing it to approve 12-15% more high-limit applicants without increasing default risk. Credit cost ratios remained stable at 1.2%, supporting the aggressive growth strategy.

Competitive Positioning and Market Response

Axis Bank ranks fourth among private sector banks in credit card outstanding, trailing HDFC, ICICI, and SBI Card. However, its 32% growth in premium card segment outpaces industry average of 18%. Competitors responded with enhanced cashback rates and lower joining fees, compressing industry margins by 0.6% in H1 2026.

Technology Infrastructure Investment

The bank allocated ₹2,800 crore to payment technology infrastructure in FY2026, focusing on real-time fraud detection and personalized offer engines. These systems enable micro-segmentation of 45 million customers into 12,000 behavioral clusters, improving conversion rates from 3.2% to 5.8%.

  • Pros: Digital-first approach reduces acquisition costs; premium card focus increases fee income; strong data analytics improve targeting
  • Cons: NIM compression affects overall profitability; intense competition forces margin erosion; high technology investment impacts short-term returns
  • Key Insight: Axis prioritizes market share over margins, betting on long-term customer lifetime value rather than immediate credit income
