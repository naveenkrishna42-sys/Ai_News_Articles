Trump signals shift to economic pressure on Iran after new strikes

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says US is 'low-keying it' with Iran, hinting at reduced military escalation.
  • Reports suggest possible nuclear concessions in exchange for reopening the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Tehran raises stakes over Hormuz, while Washington weighs economic pressure as a primary tool.
  • The shift comes after new military strikes, signaling a potential policy recalibration.

Washington recalibrates its Iran strategy

US President Donald Trump has signaled a significant shift in his approach toward Iran, moving from overt military strikes to a strategy centered on economic pressure. In an interview with Axios, Trump said, "We are low-keying it" with Iran, a remark that analysts interpret as a deliberate de-escalation of military rhetoric in favor of sanctions and financial leverage.

The statement follows a series of new military strikes that had raised fears of a broader conflict in the region. The White House now appears to be walking a careful line, aiming to pressure Tehran without triggering a full-scale war.

The Hormuz factor

Central to this recalibration is the Strait of Hormuz, a vital chokepoint for global oil supplies. Reports from multiple outlets, including NDTV and Swarajya, suggest that Trump has floated the idea of nuclear concessions in exchange for the reopening of the strait, which Tehran has threatened to close in response to the strikes.

Iranian officials have raised the stakes by hinting at disruptions to shipping lanes, a move that could send shockwaves through global energy markets. The strait carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, making any disruption a matter of international concern.

Economic leverage over military might

The pivot to economic pressure is not new for the Trump administration, which has previously used sanctions to isolate Iran's economy. However, the timing of this shift is notable, coming just days after the latest military engagement. The message appears to be that Washington is willing to use force, but prefers a more measured approach.

Analysts point out that economic sanctions have already crippled Iran's oil exports, and further measures could target its banking sector or access to international financial systems. The goal, according to sources familiar with the matter, is to force Tehran to the negotiating table without escalating the conflict further.

Tehran's response and regional implications

Iran has not officially responded to Trump's latest overtures, but its actions in the Gulf suggest a willingness to test Washington's resolve. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz would be a drastic step, but one that Iran has threatened to take if its interests are not protected.

Regional players, including India and Gulf states, are closely monitoring the situation. Any prolonged disruption to shipping could impact oil prices and trade routes, with ripple effects across Asia and beyond.

What lies ahead

For now, the focus is on whether economic pressure can achieve what military strikes could not. The coming weeks will reveal whether Iran is willing to engage in negotiations or if the standoff escalates further.

Observers will be watching for concrete signals from both capitals, including any announcements on sanctions waivers or nuclear talks. The path to de-escalation remains uncertain, but the shift in tone from Washington offers a glimmer of hope for a diplomatic resolution.
