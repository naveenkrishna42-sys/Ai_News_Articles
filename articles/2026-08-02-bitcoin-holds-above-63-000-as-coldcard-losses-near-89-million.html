Bitcoin holds above $63,000 as Coldcard losses near $89 million

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin trades above $63,000, showing resilience despite market jitters.
  • Coldcard-related losses near $89 million, fueling debate on hardware wallet safety.
  • Analysts watch for support levels and broader crypto sentiment.

Bitcoin held its ground above the $63,000 mark on Sunday, even as losses tied to Coldcard hardware wallets crept toward $89 million. The cryptocurrency market, known for its sharp swings, appears to be absorbing the news without a major sell-off, at least for now.

The figure, which has been circulating among traders and security researchers, points to a growing concern over the safety of physical wallets, often touted as the most secure way to store digital assets. Coldcard, a popular brand among bitcoin holders, has not issued an official statement as of this writing, and details of how these losses occurred remain unclear.

Market holds steady

Bitcoin's price action over the past 24 hours has been relatively calm, with the asset trading in a narrow range above $63,000. This stability comes despite the worrying headlines, suggesting that many investors are either unaware of the scale of the losses or are choosing to look past them.

Data from major exchanges show steady trading volumes, though not at the levels seen during previous spikes. Analysts note that the market's reaction, or lack thereof, could be a sign of maturity, or simply a pause before the next move.

What are Coldcard wallets?

Coldcard wallets are hardware devices designed to keep private keys offline, away from the reach of hackers. They are a preferred choice for long-term holders who prioritise security over convenience. The brand has built a reputation for being 'paranoid' about security, which makes the reported losses all the more striking.

If the $89 million figure is accurate, it would represent one of the larger incidents involving hardware wallets in recent memory. However, without official confirmation, the exact nature of the losses—whether from physical theft, phishing, or a software vulnerability—remains speculative.

Community reactions

On social media, the crypto community is divided. Some users are calling for more transparency from Coldcard, while others are pointing out that user error, such as mishandling seed phrases, is often the real culprit in such cases. A few have even questioned whether the reported figure is being exaggerated.

Security experts, meanwhile, are advising caution. They recommend that users double-check their backup procedures and consider diversifying storage methods. The incident, they say, is a reminder that no single solution is foolproof.

What to watch

For now, the focus remains on whether Bitcoin can sustain its position above $63,000. A dip below this level could trigger a wave of selling, while a rally would likely shift attention away from the Coldcard news.

Investors should also keep an eye on any official response from Coldcard or regulatory bodies. The coming days will likely reveal more about the nature of these losses and whether they have any broader implications for the hardware wallet industry.
