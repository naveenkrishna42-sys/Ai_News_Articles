Prahlad Joshi takes charge as Education Minister after Dharmendra Pradhan resigns

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Prahlad Joshi appointed Education Minister after Dharmendra Pradhan's resignation.
  • Joshi acknowledged the portfolio is new to him, promising to learn quickly.
  • Resignation follows months of youth protests over NEET irregularities and job shortages.
  • Joshi's administrative experience and Karnataka roots seen as assets to manage crisis.

Context: A sudden change in leadership

Prahlad Joshi, a veteran BJP leader from Karnataka, officially took charge as India's Education Minister on Sunday, replacing Dharmendra Pradhan who resigned late Saturday night. The handover comes at a critical juncture, with the education sector facing intense scrutiny over examination reforms and employment-linked education policies.

Joshi, who previously held the Parliamentary Affairs and Coal portfolios, admitted the challenge openly. "This is a new subject for me," he told reporters after assuming office, adding that he would rely on experts and consult stakeholders to navigate the transition.

Background: Youth unrest and policy challenges

Dharmendra Pradhan's resignation was not sudden but the culmination of months of simmering discontent. The catalyst was widespread protests led by students and unemployed youth, particularly over alleged irregularities in the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET) and the lack of adequate job opportunities despite government skill development programmes.

Reports from the ground, including accounts from Delhi's Jantar Mantar where protesters gathered, painted a picture of deep frustration. Young demonstrators accused the government of prioritising cosmetic reforms over substantive change. The protests gained traction on social media, amplifying calls for accountability and systemic overhaul.

Analysts point out that the education portfolio has become a political minefield. The government's push for the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, while ambitious, has faced implementation hurdles, including resistance from states and confusion over curriculum changes. The resignation signals a recognition within the BJP that a fresh approach was needed.

Why it matters: Trust and credibility at stake

The appointment of Prahlad Joshi is not just a routine reshuffle. It is a calculated move by Prime Minister Narendra Modi to rebuild trust with a demographic that forms the backbone of India's future workforce. Youth unemployment, according to recent government data, hovers at over 10% for graduates, a figure that opposition parties have repeatedly targeted.

Joshi brings a reputation as a troubleshooter. His tenure as Parliamentary Affairs Minister saw him manage complex legislative logjams. He also hails from Karnataka, a state where the BJP faces stiff competition from the Congress in urban and semi-urban constituencies with high youth populations. His regional identity could help the government counter narratives of a Delhi-centric policy approach.

"The Prime Minister is signalling that he hears the youth," said a political observer who spoke on condition of anonymity. "Joshi is not an education specialist, but he is a crisis manager. The brief is clear: stabilise the sector politically while delivering visible outcomes."

What the new minister inherits

Joshi takes over a ministry with several unfinished files. The NEET controversy remains unresolved, with court cases pending and demands for a fresh investigation. The government's flagship Skill India Mission has missed targets in job placements, according to internal reviews. Additionally, the implementation of NEP 2020's three-language formula has stirred political rows in Tamil Nadu and other states.

On the positive side, the ministry has secured record budget allocations for education in recent years. School enrolment rates have improved, and digital infrastructure in higher education received a boost during the pandemic. Joshi's challenge will be to translate these inputs into outcomes that address public grievances.

What to watch: The next 100 days

Prahlad Joshi's first moves will be closely watched. He is expected to meet with student leaders and state education ministers in the coming weeks. A review of NEET procedures and a possible white paper on examination reforms are anticipated. The government may also announce new employment-linked incentives for educational institutions.

The opposition, particularly Congress, has already demanded a Joint Parliamentary Committee probe into NEET irregularities and the circumstances of Pradhan's resignation. Joshi's parliamentary skills will be tested as he defends the government's record while offering concessions. The coming months will reveal whether a change at the top can cool the streets and restore faith in India's education system.
