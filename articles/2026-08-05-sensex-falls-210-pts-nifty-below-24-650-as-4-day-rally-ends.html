Sensex Falls 210 Pts, Nifty Below 24,650 as 4-Day Rally Ends

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 210 points and Nifty slipped below 24,650, ending a four-day rally.
  • Profit booking and global cues likely weighed on investor sentiment.
  • Broader indices and sectoral performance varied, with some pockets showing resilience.

Rally Hits a Speed Bump

Indian equity benchmarks closed in the red on Tuesday, bringing a four-day winning streak to an abrupt halt. The BSE Sensex ended 210 points lower, while the NSE Nifty slipped below the 24,650 mark, leaving investors to digest the first notable pullback in a week.

The decline was broad-based but lacked the panic typically seen in sharp corrections. Market participants pointed to profit booking at higher levels as the primary trigger, with traders choosing to lock in gains after the recent run-up. Global cues also played spoilsport, as mixed signals from overseas markets dampened risk appetite.

What Dragged the Benchmarks

Heavyweight stocks in banking, financial services, and IT bore the brunt of the selling pressure. Index heavyweights like Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, and Infosys were among the top drags on the Sensex, according to provisional data. The Nifty's fall below the psychological 24,650 level added to the cautious mood on Dalal Street.

Analysts noted that the market had been trading at elevated valuations, making it vulnerable to sudden reversals. "The recent rally was quite sharp, so a breather was on the cards," said a Mumbai-based technical analyst, speaking on condition of anonymity. "The question now is whether this is a pause or the start of a deeper correction."

Sectoral Mixed Bag

While the headline indices fell, not every sector ended in the red. Defensive pockets such as FMCG and pharma showed relative strength, offering some cushion to the market. Meanwhile, metal and realty stocks witnessed selling, reflecting a risk-off tilt in investor preference.

The broader market, represented by the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices, also traded lower, though the losses were less severe than the frontline indices. This divergence suggests that retail and institutional investors are not exiting in a hurry but are rebalancing their portfolios.

Volatility and Volume Tell the Tale

Market breadth was negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. The India VIX, a measure of expected volatility, inched up, signalling a slight rise in anxiety among traders. However, volumes were not unusually high, indicating that the selling was orderly rather than panic-driven.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) and domestic institutional investors (DIIs) activity data for the day is yet to be released in full, but provisional figures suggest that domestic funds continued to provide support. The resilience in FII flows over the past month had been a key driver of the rally, and any reversal in that trend would be closely monitored.

What's Next for the Market

Investors now turn their attention to upcoming macroeconomic data and global central bank cues. A slew of economic releases, including industrial production and inflation figures, are scheduled in the coming weeks, which could influence the Reserve Bank of India's policy trajectory.

On the global front, movements in US bond yields and crude oil prices will remain key watchpoints. Any sharp spike in oil could stoke inflation worries and pressure the rupee, adding to domestic headwinds.

For now, traders are likely to stay nimble, with immediate support for the Nifty seen around the 24,500 zone. A close below that could trigger further profit booking, while a rebound above 24,800 would signal that the bulls are still in control.

The coming sessions will reveal whether Tuesday's dip is a mere blip or the beginning of a consolidation phase. Market veterans advise keeping an eye on global cues and institutional flows, as these will determine the trajectory in the near term.
