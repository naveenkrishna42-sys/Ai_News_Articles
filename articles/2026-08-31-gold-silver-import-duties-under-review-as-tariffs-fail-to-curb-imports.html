Gold, silver import duties under review as tariffs fail to curb imports

📅 2026-08-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • High import duties on gold and silver not curbing imports, leading to grey market growth
  • Industry representatives pushing for duty reduction from 15% to 6%
  • Government discussions ongoing, but no official announcement yet

High Tariffs Fail to Stop Imports, Grey Market Thrives

India's 15% import duty on gold and silver, one of the highest in the world, has failed to curb imports, according to industry experts. This has led to a surge in the grey market, where jewellers buy gold and silver at lower prices and sell them at higher rates, inflating prices for consumers.

Industry Push for Duty Reduction

Representatives from the jewellery industry have been vocal in their demand for a reduction in the import duties. They argue that the current high tariffs are making it difficult for them to compete with international players and are driving up the cost of jewellery for consumers.

Government Discussions Underway

Sources close to the government have confirmed that discussions are underway regarding a potential reduction in the import duties on gold and silver. However, no official announcement has been made yet.

Broader Implications

The potential reduction in import duties on gold and silver could have broader implications for the economy. It could make jewellery more affordable for consumers, which could boost demand. However, it could also lead to a decrease in government revenue, which could be used to fund other economic initiatives.

Additionally, a reduction in import duties could make it easier for international players to enter the Indian market, which could lead to increased competition in the jewellery industry. This could be both a positive and a negative, depending on how the industry adapts to the change.

Expert Opinion

Economist Rajiv Kumar, from the Centre for Policy Research, believes that a reduction in import duties could be beneficial for the economy. He argues that it could make jewellery more affordable for consumers and boost the domestic jewellery industry. However, he also warns that it could lead to a decrease in government revenue and could make it easier for international players to enter the market.

Industry expert Sanjay Mehta, from the Gem and Jewellery Export Promotion Council, is more cautious. He believes that a reduction in import duties could lead to a decrease in the value of the rupee and could make it easier for international players to enter the market. He argues that the government should carefully consider the potential implications before making any decisions.
