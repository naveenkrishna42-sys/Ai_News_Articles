Deal & Pricing Analysis

The BOB Card Etihad Travel Perks is positioned as a premium travel‑focused credit card. While many co‑branded cards charge a joining fee, BOB waives it entirely for new applicants. The standard annual fee is ₹4,999, but the first year is completely free, effectively giving cardholders a zero‑cost trial period. This pricing strategy mirrors the approach of other airline‑affiliated cards that aim to attract high‑spending travelers by removing the initial barrier. Compared to the market average of ₹2,500‑₹3,500 for similar travel cards, the BOB Etihad card sits slightly higher, yet the bundled benefits—especially the Etihad Miles earn rate and complimentary lounge visits—justify the premium for users who fly Etihad at least twice a year.

Real‑World Performance & Daily Use

In everyday use, the BOB Etihad card feels solid and reliable. The plastic is standard‑grade with a subtle Etihad logo embossing, and the chip‑and‑pin functionality works seamlessly across POS terminals in India and abroad. The standout feature is the mileage accrual: for every ₹100 spent on overseas purchases—such as flight bookings, hotel stays, or dining—cardholders earn 2 Etihad Miles, effectively doubling the return compared to many generic travel cards that offer 1 mile per ₹100. Domestic spends still earn at a respectable 1 mile per ₹100, ensuring that even routine grocery or utility bills contribute to the mileage pool. The four annual lounge visits are a tangible perk; users report easy access at major Indian hubs like Delhi, Mumbai, and Bangalore, as well as at Etihad’s own lounges in Abu Dhabi. The zero foreign currency markup eliminates the typical 2‑3% surcharge seen on many Indian cards, making overseas shopping noticeably cheaper. However, the high APR on revolving balances means that carrying a month‑end debt can quickly erode the value of earned miles, so disciplined payment habits are essential.

Competition & Market Alternatives

The closest rival in the same fee bracket is the HDFC Bank Diners Club Black Card, which charges an annual fee of ₹10,000 but offers a higher mileage earn rate (3 miles per ₹100 on international spends) and unlimited lounge access via Priority Pass. For users who prioritize lounge frequency over pure mileage accumulation, the HDFC option may be preferable despite the higher cost. Another alternative is the Axis Bank Vistara Signature Card, priced at ₹1,500 annually, which provides Vistara-specific benefits such as free seat upgrades and priority check‑in, but its earn rate is limited to 2 miles per ₹100 only on Vistara purchases, making it less versatile for general travel spend. Compared to these, the BOB Etihad card strikes a balance: a moderate fee, a strong earn rate on all international spends, and meaningful lounge access without requiring a premium tier subscription.

Who Should Buy This Deal

Frequent Etihad flyers who book at least two round‑trip international trips per year will reap the most value, as the mileage earn rate and lounge visits directly offset the ₹4,999 annual fee after the first year. Users who travel abroad for business or leisure and wish to avoid foreign transaction surcharges will also benefit from the 0% markup. Conversely, those who rarely fly Etihad, prefer cashback or flat‑rate rewards, or tend to carry a balance on their cards should look elsewhere, as the high APR and fee structure may outweigh the perks for low‑usage scenarios.

Final Buying Verdict

For loyal Etihad customers who travel internationally at least twice a year and pay their balance in full each month, the BOB Card Etihad Travel Perks delivers strong mileage earnings, lounge access, and travel protections that justify its modest annual fee after the waived first year. Occasional flyers or those who carry a balance should consider lower‑fee alternatives or cash‑back cards instead. Overall, the card offers a compelling value proposition for its target audience.
