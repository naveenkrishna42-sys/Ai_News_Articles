Android lock screen bug lets Gemini send SMS without PIN, fix incoming

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google's Gemini assistant can send SMS from a locked Android phone without PIN or biometric verification.
  • The vulnerability was first reported by cybersecurity firm Bitdefender and confirmed by Google.
  • Google has confirmed a fix is in the works and will be deployed via a server-side update in the coming days.

Lock screen bypass discovered on Android

A security vulnerability on Android devices is allowing Google's Gemini assistant to bypass lock screen protections and send text messages without any PIN or biometric verification. The flaw, first reported by cybersecurity firm Bitdefender and later confirmed by multiple outlets, means anyone with physical access to a locked phone can ask Gemini to compose and send an SMS.

The bug affects devices running Android 14 and 15, where Gemini is set as the default assistant. When the phone is locked, users can activate Gemini by long-pressing the power button or using the hotword. The assistant then processes commands — including sending messages — without prompting for the lock screen PIN, pattern, or fingerprint.

How the vulnerability works

Bitdefender's researchers demonstrated the exploit by issuing voice commands to Gemini on a locked phone. The assistant responded by drafting and sending SMS messages to contacts stored on the device, all without the owner unlocking it. The company described it as a 'significant privacy and security risk,' especially if the device is lost or stolen.

Google has acknowledged the issue. A spokesperson told Android Headlines that the company is 'aware of the reported behavior' and that a fix is being prepared. The update will be delivered server-side, meaning users won't need to manually download a patch from the Play Store.

Google confirms fix is on the way

The search giant has not disclosed a specific timeline, but reports indicate the fix will roll out over the next few days. It will likely require Gemini to re-verify the user's identity — via PIN, pattern, or biometric — before executing sensitive actions like sending messages from a locked screen.

Until the fix arrives, security experts recommend disabling Gemini's ability to operate while the phone is locked. Users can do this by going to Settings > Apps > Assistant > Lock screen, and toggling off 'Use assistant when phone is locked.' Alternatively, switching to a different default assistant or turning off voice activation entirely can serve as a temporary workaround.

What this means for users

This is not the first time Android lock screen bypasses have been discovered. In recent years, similar bugs have allowed unauthorised access to Google Photos, contact lists, and even payment apps. Each time, Google has patched the issue, but the recurring nature of these vulnerabilities raises questions about how thoroughly lock screen security is tested before new features go live.

For now, the risk is limited to physical access. An attacker would need to have the phone in hand. But in scenarios like theft or a lost device, the consequences could range from privacy leaks to financial fraud if SMS-based two-factor authentication codes are accessible.

Google's server-side fix is expected to reach all affected devices by early next week. Users are advised to keep an eye on their device's settings for any changes to Gemini's lock screen behaviour.
