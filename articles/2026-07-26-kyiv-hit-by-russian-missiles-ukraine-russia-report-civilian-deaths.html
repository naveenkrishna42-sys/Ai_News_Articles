Kyiv hit by Russian missiles; Ukraine, Russia report civilian deaths

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Russian missiles hit Kyiv overnight, causing fires and casualties.
  • At least two dead in Kyiv; ten killed at a drone exhibition near the capital.
  • Both Ukraine and Russia report civilian deaths in the latest strikes.

Russian missiles struck the Ukrainian capital Kyiv overnight, triggering fires and leaving at least two people dead. The attack comes as both Kyiv and Moscow report civilian casualties in the escalating conflict.

Missiles hit Kyiv overnight

Explosions were heard across Kyiv in the early hours of Saturday. Emergency services rushed to multiple sites as fires broke out in residential areas. Officials confirmed at least two civilians were killed, with several others injured.

The strikes hit several districts, damaging homes and infrastructure. Rescue teams worked through the night to extinguish fires and search for survivors. Ukraine's air force said it intercepted several missiles, but some got through.

Deadly attack on drone exhibition

Separately, a Russian attack on a drone exhibition near Kyiv killed ten people. The exhibition, showcasing unmanned aerial vehicles, was held in a suburb of the capital. Witnesses described scenes of panic as the strike hit the venue.

Ukraine's military said the attack targeted civilians and accused Russia of deliberately striking a non-military site. Moscow has not commented on the specifics of the strike but has previously denied targeting civilians.

Both sides report civilian deaths

Russia also reported civilian casualties from Ukrainian strikes. The Russian defence ministry said Ukrainian forces shelled a village in the Belgorod region, killing at least three people. Moscow blamed Kyiv for the attack.

Ukraine did not immediately comment on the Russian claims. The cross-border strikes have intensified in recent weeks, with both sides accusing each other of targeting civilians. The United Nations has called for restraint.

The overnight attacks come amid a broader escalation in hostilities. Ukraine has been pressing its allies for more air defence systems to counter Russian missile barrages. Western nations have pledged additional support, but deliveries have been slow.

Residents in Kyiv described a night of terror. "We heard the sirens and then the explosions. The whole building shook," said one local. Another said, "It's been months since we had such a close strike. We thought we were safer."

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned the attacks, calling them "acts of terror." He reiterated his call for more advanced air defence systems from Western partners. Russia has maintained that its strikes target military infrastructure, not civilians.

The conflict shows no signs of de-escalation. Both sides continue to trade heavy fire, with civilians bearing the brunt of the violence. International mediators have struggled to bring the warring parties to the negotiating table.

As dawn broke over Kyiv, rescue teams continued their work. The death toll may rise as crews sift through the rubble. The city remains on high alert for further strikes.

What happens next will depend on the response from both capitals. Ukraine is expected to push for more air defence systems at upcoming international meetings. Russia has signalled it will continue its campaign. The civilian toll continues to mount.
