Sanchez under fire as EU meets on Ceuta migrant crossings

📅 2026-08-04 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Spanish PM Sanchez faces criticism within EU over handling of Ceuta migrant crossings
  • EU commends Spain's swift response to the crisis but leaders seek coordinated action
  • Ceuta and Melilla are Spanish enclaves in North Africa, a recurring migration flashpoint

Sanchez under pressure at EU talks

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez arrived at the European Union summit on Tuesday facing a storm of criticism over the recent surge in migrant crossings into the North African enclave of Ceuta. The EU meeting, convened to address the escalating situation, has placed Madrid under a harsh spotlight as leaders debate how to respond to the growing pressure on the bloc's external borders.

While Brussels officials have publicly commended Spain's 'swift response' to the crisis, behind closed doors several member states have voiced sharp concerns. The criticism, according to diplomats, centres on the perceived lack of a coordinated European strategy, with some leaders arguing that Spain's handling of the arrivals has set a troubling precedent.

The Ceuta crisis explained

Ceuta, one of two Spanish enclaves on Morocco's Mediterranean coast, has long been a flashpoint for migration into Europe. Along with Melilla, the enclaves are the European Union's only land borders with Africa, making them a magnet for thousands of migrants and refugees seeking to enter the bloc.

The recent wave of crossings, which saw hundreds of people swim or wade across the border, has overwhelmed local authorities and triggered a diplomatic row between Madrid and Rabat. Spain has accused Morocco of failing to control its side of the frontier, while Morocco has denied any deliberate policy shift.

EU's mixed signals

Despite the public praise from EU institutions, the summit has exposed deep divisions among member states. Some southern European countries have sided with Spain, calling for more financial and logistical support for frontline states, while northern nations have pushed for stricter border controls and faster returns of irregular migrants.

Sanchez, for his part, has defended his government's actions, insisting that Spain acted decisively to protect its borders while upholding humanitarian obligations. However, his critics within the EU argue that the crisis in Ceuta is a symptom of a broader failure to forge a common migration policy, a charge his office has rejected as unfair.

What happens next

The EU summit is expected to issue a joint statement on migration, though the wording remains contested. Observers say the real test will come in the coming weeks as Spain seeks to repatriate migrants and rebuild trust with Morocco, whose cooperation is seen as vital to preventing another surge.

Officials have not yet confirmed the final numbers of migrants currently housed in Ceuta, nor the timeline for their transfer or return. What is clear is that the enclaves of Ceuta and Melilla will remain a litmus test for Europe's ability to manage its borders with Africa.
