Assam, Kerala, Puducherry Prepare for Assembly Elections

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Assembly elections are scheduled in Assam, Kerala, and Puducherry.
  • Voting will take place in multiple phases, with results expected later.
  • Key issues include employment, welfare schemes, and regional development.

Election Machinery Moves Into Gear

Polling stations are being readied across Assam, Kerala, and Puducherry as the three states prepare for assembly elections. The Election Commission has announced a multi-phase schedule, with voting to be held over the coming weeks. Counting of votes is expected to follow soon after, though the exact dates have not been officially confirmed yet.

In Assam, the campaign trail has been buzzing with rallies and roadshows. The state, which has seen a shifting political dynamic in recent years, will see a contest between the ruling coalition and opposition alliances. Key constituencies in the Brahmaputra Valley and the hill districts are expected to see tight fights.

Kerala: A Battle of Alliances

Kerala, meanwhile, is witnessing a three-cornered contest. The ruling Left Democratic Front, the Congress-led United Democratic Front, and the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance are all in the fray. The state has a history of alternating between the two major fronts, and the NDA has been trying to make inroads, particularly in the northern districts.

Welfare programmes, education, and healthcare have dominated the discourse here. But unemployment among the youth and the state's fiscal situation are also being discussed on the campaign trail. Coastal and plantation areas are being watched closely for voting patterns.

Puducherry: A Small Territory, Big Stakes

In Puducherry, the Union Territory's assembly polls will decide the fate of 30 seats. The territory has seen political turbulence in recent years, with changes in government and alliances. The contest is mainly between the ruling alliance and the opposition, but regional parties and independents could play a decisive role in a fragmented mandate.

Issues such as infrastructure, tourism, and the welfare of the Tamil-speaking population are expected to dominate. The Union Territory's unique administrative status often makes local governance a test case for Centre-state relations.

What to Watch

As the election dates draw closer, all eyes will be on the Election Commission's final schedule and the model code of conduct. Political parties are expected to release their manifestos in the coming days, outlining their promises on jobs, subsidies, and development.

Voter turnout in these three regions will be a key indicator of public mood. With no major national election due until later, these assembly results could set the tone for political narratives in the months ahead.
