Fed Expected to Hold Rates, but Hike Possible as Inflation and Iran War Loom

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Fed is widely expected to hold interest rates steady at its next meeting, but a rate hike is still a possibility.
  • Inflation remains high, and the ongoing war with Iran is adding uncertainty to the economic outlook.
  • Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has kept his cards hidden, leaving markets guessing about the central bank's next move.

Rate Decision Hangs in the Balance

The US Federal Reserve is set to announce its latest interest rate decision, and while a hold is the consensus expectation, a rate hike has not been ruled out. The central bank is grappling with persistent inflation and the economic fallout from the ongoing war with Iran.

Markets are bracing for a pivotal moment. The Fed has been under pressure to tighten policy, but the conflict in the Middle East has introduced fresh uncertainty. Analysts are split on whether the central bank will hold firm or surprise with a hike.

Inflation and War Cloud the Outlook

Inflation remains above the Fed's target, and the war with Iran has disrupted global supply chains, pushing energy prices higher. This combination has made the Fed's job harder. The central bank must balance the need to curb rising prices with the risk of stalling economic growth.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact impact of the conflict on the US economy, but the uncertainty is palpable. The Fed's statement will be scrutinised for any hints about future moves.

Warsh Keeps Markets Guessing

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has kept his cards close to his chest. Unlike previous meetings, where forward guidance was clearer, Warsh has offered little clue about the central bank's next step. This opacity has added to the volatility in financial markets.

Traders are now pricing in a range of outcomes. Some expect a hold with a hawkish tone, while others warn that a hike could come if inflation data does not cool. The decision will be closely watched by investors in India and around the world.

What happens next will depend on the Fed's assessment of the war's economic impact and whether inflation shows signs of easing. All eyes are on the announcement later this week.
