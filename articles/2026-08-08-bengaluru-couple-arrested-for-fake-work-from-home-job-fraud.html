Bengaluru couple arrested for fake work-from-home job fraud

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Couple arrested in Bengaluru for fake work-from-home job offers
  • Victims across India targeted; police investigating scale of fraud
  • Authorities urge job seekers to verify offers before paying

Couple held in Bengaluru for job scam

Bengaluru police have arrested a couple for allegedly running a fraudulent work-from-home job scheme that duped numerous job seekers across India. The accused, whose names have not been officially released, were taken into custody following a complaint from a victim who lost money to the fake offers.

The couple is believed to have advertised attractive remote jobs on various online platforms, promising high salaries and flexible hours. Once applicants showed interest, they were asked to pay registration fees or other charges, after which the accused stopped all communication.

How the scam operated

Preliminary investigations suggest the accused used multiple fake websites and social media accounts to lure candidates. They targeted people looking for easy entry into the work-from-home sector, a segment that has grown significantly in recent years.

Police sources said the couple collected money through bank transfers and digital wallets, making it difficult for victims to trace them. The exact amount swindled is yet to be ascertained, but officials have not ruled out a larger network operating behind the scenes.

Victims across India

Complaints have come in from several states, indicating the scam had a wide reach. Many victims were young professionals and students who had applied for data entry, customer support, and other remote roles advertised online.

One victim, who filed the initial complaint, told police that the couple had promised a monthly salary of up to Rs 40,000 for minimal work. After paying an advance fee of Rs 15,000, the victim received no further response from the accused.

Police investigation underway

The Bengaluru cyber crime wing is now examining the accused's digital footprints, including their phone records and bank statements. They are also checking whether the couple used stolen identities to open accounts or register websites.

Police have urged the public to be cautious while applying for jobs online, especially those that require upfront payments. They advise verifying the legitimacy of companies and avoiding offers that seem too good to be true.

What to watch

The accused are expected to be produced in court, where the police will seek their remand for further questioning. Investigators are also looking into possible links to other similar frauds reported in recent months, which could lead to more arrests.
