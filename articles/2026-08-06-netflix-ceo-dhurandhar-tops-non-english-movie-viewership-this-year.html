Netflix CEO: Dhurandhar tops non-English movie viewership this year

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ted Sarandos announced Dhurandhar as Netflix's top non-English movie of the year.
  • The film's success underscores the growing global appetite for Indian content.
  • Sarandos made the remark during a recent industry interaction, though specific viewership numbers were not disclosed.

Netflix co-chief executive Ted Sarandos has revealed that the Indian film Dhurandhar has emerged as the most-watched non-English movie on the streaming platform this year. The statement, made during a recent industry interaction, underscores the expanding global footprint of Indian cinema in the digital entertainment space.

A landmark achievement for Indian cinema

Sarandos, known for his data-driven insights into viewer behaviour, highlighted the film's performance without divulging specific viewership figures. The announcement positions Dhurandhar alongside a growing list of Indian productions that have found substantial audiences beyond domestic markets.

The film's success is particularly notable given the fierce competition on the platform, which hosts a vast library of international titles in multiple languages. Indian content, especially from Bollywood and regional industries, has been steadily climbing the streaming charts over the past few years.

What makes Dhurandhar stand out?

While the plot details and cast of Dhurandhar have been widely discussed in entertainment circles, its appeal appears to lie in its universal themes and high production values. The movie reportedly blends action, drama, and emotional depth, a combination that has historically resonated with global audiences.

Industry analysts point to Netflix's aggressive investment in Indian originals as a key driver behind such successes. The platform has commissioned dozens of films and series across Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, and other languages, betting on the country's storytelling traditions to attract subscribers worldwide.

Global streaming trends and Indian content

The recognition from Sarandos comes at a time when streaming platforms are increasingly looking beyond English-language markets for growth. India, with its vast population and rapidly increasing internet penetration, has become a critical battleground for services like Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, and Disney+ Hotstar.

Non-English titles have consistently performed well on Netflix, with South Korean series like Squid Game and Spanish films such as Money Heist setting benchmarks. Dhurandhar's achievement places it in this elite category, signalling that Indian movies can compete on equal footing with global hits.

Reactions from the industry

The news has sparked celebration among Indian filmmakers and actors, many of whom view it as validation of their craft. Trade analysts note that such recognition often leads to increased licensing deals and co-productions, as international studios seek to replicate the success formula.

However, some experts caution against reading too much into a single title's performance, arguing that streaming metrics are often opaque and subject to algorithmic variations. Nevertheless, the sentiment within the industry remains overwhelmingly positive.

What this means for Netflix in India

For Netflix, Dhurandhar's success reinforces its strategy of localising content while maintaining global production standards. The company has faced stiff competition in India from rivals offering cheaper plans and extensive regional libraries, but hits like this help justify its premium positioning.

Sarandos's public acknowledgment also serves as a marketing boost, potentially attracting more subscribers curious about the film. The platform is likely to leverage this momentum in its promotional campaigns for upcoming Indian releases.

As streaming consumption continues to evolve, the line between 'local' and 'global' is blurring. Dhurandhar may well be a harbinger of more Indian stories crossing borders, driven by platforms willing to take creative risks.

Looking ahead, all eyes will be on Netflix's next slate of Indian originals and whether they can sustain this upward trajectory. The coming months will reveal if this is a one-off triumph or the beginning of a sustained wave of Indian content dominating global charts.
