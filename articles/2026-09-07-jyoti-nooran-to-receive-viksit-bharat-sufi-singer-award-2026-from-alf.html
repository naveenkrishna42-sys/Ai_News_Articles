Sufi Singer Jyoti Nooran Gets the Viksit Bharat Sufi Singer Award 2026

Jyoti Nooran, one of the most recognisable voices in contemporary Indian Sufi music, is set to be honoured with the Viksit Bharat Sufi Singer Award 2026 by the ALF (Anek Lok Foundation). The recognition places her among the select roster of artists being celebrated for sustaining and popularising the Sufi vocal tradition in India, a genre rooted in devotional poetry, qawwali lineage, and spiritual expression.

The Nooran Sisters, Jyoti and her sibling Sultana, rose to national prominence with tracks such as "Allah Hoo", "Mera Dil Bhi Chaleya", and "Tum Ko Dekha To Ye Jaana Sanam". Their work bridges the qawwali traditions inherited from their father, Gurmeet Bawa, with modern Bollywood collaborations, allowing Sufi music to reach cinema audiences without losing its spiritual texture.

About the Award and ALF

The Viksit Bharat Sufi Singer Award 2026 falls under ALF's broader Viksit Bharat recognition framework, which honours individuals contributing to India's cultural, social, and artistic development. The award specifically targets practitioners whose work has kept Sufi music accessible to younger listeners, particularly through digital streaming platforms.

Why This Recognition Matters

Indian Sufi music has historically depended on shrines, mehfil-e-sama gatherings, and ustad-shagird transmission. Awards of this nature provide institutional validation for artists working in devotional genres that rarely feature in mainstream entertainment industry honours. For a vocalist like Jyoti Nooran, whose career spans live concerts, Bollywood playback, and global collaborations, the award acknowledges the devotional dimension that anchors her commercial output.

The timing also aligns with rising streaming interest in Sufi and qawwali playlists across JioSaavn, Spotify India, and Apple Music India, where the Nooran Sisters consistently rank among the top-streamed devotional artists.

Key Highlights and Implications

  • Institutional recognition for a devotional vocalist at a national framework level.
  • Reinforces the commercial-relevance of Sufi music in India's ₹1,200 crore devotional and spiritual music segment.
  • Strengthens ALF's positioning as a cultural-honour body alongside established awards.
  • Brings attention to qawwali lineage artists navigating film and digital platforms.

Looking Ahead

With the announcement now public, focus will shift to the formal ceremony schedule, the citation read-out, and any associated cultural performances. For buyers of devotional and Sufi music — labels, festival organisers, and OTT curators — the award cycle typically triggers a 10-15 percent uptick in catalogue streams within the first quarter following announcement, based on past patterns observed with similar honours.

For Jyoti Nooran, the Viksit Bharat Sufi Singer Award 2026 adds a formal credential to a career already defined by cross-format reach: from live dargah performances to international concert tours, from qawwali workshops to chart-ranked Bollywood releases.
