Four-year-old boy killed as speeding car hits two-wheeler near Mambakkam

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Incident occurred near Mambakkam on Thursday evening
  • Boy was riding pillion with family on two-wheeler
  • Car driver detained; investigation underway

A four-year-old boy was killed on Thursday evening when a speeding car rammed into the two-wheeler he was riding on with his family near Mambakkam, on the outskirts of Chennai. The child, identified as S. Dharun, died on the spot. His parents, who were also on the two-wheeler, sustained injuries and have been admitted to a nearby hospital.

Accident details

The incident took place on the Mambakkam–Medavakkam Road, a stretch that has seen several accidents in recent months. According to police, the family was returning home when a car, allegedly driven at high speed, lost control and hit their two-wheeler from behind. The impact threw all three occupants onto the road. The boy suffered severe head injuries and was declared dead on arrival at a government hospital.

Local residents, who rushed to the spot after hearing the crash, said the car was travelling at a dangerous speed. "The driver didn't even slow down after the hit. It was only when people blocked the road that he stopped," a witness told The Hindu.

Police response

The Mambakkam police have registered a case under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code and the Motor Vehicles Act. The car driver, whose identity has not been officially released, has been detained for questioning. A senior police officer said a thorough investigation is underway, including checking whether the driver was under the influence of alcohol.

"We are collecting CCTV footage from nearby shops and junctions to establish the exact sequence of events," the officer said. The two-wheeler has been seized for forensic examination.

Road safety concerns

The accident has once again highlighted the poor safety record of the Mambakkam–Medavakkam corridor. Residents have repeatedly complained about speeding vehicles, lack of speed breakers, and poor street lighting. In the past year, at least three fatal accidents have been reported on this stretch, according to local activists.

"Every time there is a death, officials promise action. But nothing changes. We need speed cameras and strict enforcement," said R. Kumar, a resident welfare association member.

What lies ahead

The boy's body has been handed over to his family after post-mortem. The police are expected to file a detailed report in the coming days. Meanwhile, the Greater Chennai Traffic Police have been urged to conduct a safety audit of the road and install traffic calming measures. The driver's remand hearing is likely to take place on Friday.
