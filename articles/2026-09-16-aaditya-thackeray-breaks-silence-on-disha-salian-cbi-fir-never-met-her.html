Why the Disha Salian CBI FIR Matters Again

The Central Bureau of Investigation has registered a fresh FIR in the death of Disha Salian, the former manager of late actor Sushant Singh Rajput, who died on 8 June 2020 after falling from a high-rise in Mumbai's Malad area. The original Mumbai Police probe had recorded it as an accidental death, and a magisterial inquiry closed the matter. The new FIR reopens a case that has been politically radioactive for six years.

Within hours of the news breaking, Shiv Sena (UBT) leader Aaditya Thackeray issued a public statement denying any connection to Salian. "I never met Disha Salian," he said, calling the FIR an act of character assassination timed for political advantage. His response marks the first direct comment from the former Maharashtra minister since the CBI action became public.

What the Fresh CBI FIR Contains

Reports indicate the FIR names several individuals from the film industry, including actor Sooraj Pancholi, Rhea Chakraborty and Dino Morea, alongside others whose roles are still being examined. The CBI has not publicly confirmed charges against any named person, and no arrests have been reported at the time of writing. Legal experts caution that an FIR is an investigation trigger, not a finding of guilt.

The Political Battle Behind the FIR

Shiv Sena (UBT) MP Sanjay Raut linked the CBI's move directly to Thackeray's ongoing campaign against the Fadnavis-led Maharashtra government. Raut's argument is straightforward: the timing of the FIR tracks the political calendar rather than any new forensic breakthrough. The ruling alliance has not formally responded to that charge.

For Maharashtra's voters, the case has become a proxy for the larger Sena split. Thackeray's camp frames the FIR as reputational warfare; the rival faction frames the reopening as overdue accountability. Neither side has produced new forensic evidence in the public domain.

Real-World Implications for the People Named

An FIR of this profile carries immediate consequences even before any charge sheet. Named individuals face travel scrutiny, media siege, endorsement risk and legal costs running into lakhs of rupees per month. For film-industry professionals, a CBI tag can freeze project signings for months, as seen during the 2020 Rajput investigation.

Buyers of news and legal commentary should note a key distinction: an FIR names suspects or witnesses, not convicts. Indian law presumes innocence until a competent court rules otherwise. Readers should track the CBI's charge sheet, not the headlines around the FIR.

Key Takeaways

  • Legal status: A fresh CBI FIR has been registered; no charges have been proven in court.
  • Thackeray's position: He denies ever meeting Salian and calls the FIR character assassination.
  • Political angle: Sanjay Raut alleges the probe is retaliation against Thackeray's campaign against the Fadnavis government.
  • Industry impact: Named film personalities face reputational and contractual risk before any trial begins.
  • What to watch: The CBI charge sheet, forensic findings, and whether the Bombay High Court intervenes.

What Happens Next

The CBI is expected to record statements and examine digital and call-record evidence from 2020. Any material contradiction with the original Mumbai Police investigation would be the first genuine legal turning point. Until then, the FIR remains a procedural step with outsized political weight.
