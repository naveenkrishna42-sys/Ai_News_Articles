Ex-cricketer questions Auqib Nabi's selection as India Test replacement

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Former cricketer questions Auqib Nabi's selection as replacement for Sri Lanka Tests
  • BCCI plans to standardise fitness test parameters after England tour issues
  • Selectors Ajit Agarkar and Gautam Gambhir face criticism over squad choices

Selection under scrutiny

India's squad selection for the upcoming Test series against Sri Lanka has sparked sharp criticism, with a former cricketer publicly questioning the decision to bring in Auqib Nabi as a replacement. The unnamed ex-player's remarks have added to a growing chorus of discontent over the team management's choices.

The criticism comes at a time when the Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) is already grappling with fitness concerns within the squad. Nabi's inclusion has been described as questionable, with the former cricketer suggesting that the move defied conventional selection logic.

Fitness standards under the microscope

The debate over Nabi's selection has coincided with reports that the BCCI is set to standardise its fitness testing parameters. The move follows what has been termed the 'England tour debacle', during which several players struggled to meet the required fitness benchmarks.

Sources indicate that the board will now implement uniform protocols for the Bronco and 2K base fitness tests across all levels. This comes after issues were flagged with players' conditioning, including a reported 'overweight' concern for one squad member, Rana.

Agarkar and Gambhir in the line of fire

Chief selector Ajit Agarkar and head coach Gautam Gambhir have borne the brunt of the criticism for Nabi's selection. Reports from NDTV Sports have highlighted the duo's role in choosing Nabi as a replacement for the Sri Lanka Tests, a decision that has baffled many within cricketing circles.

The former cricketer's remarks have amplified the pressure on the selection committee to justify its choices. With India's pace attack already weakened by injuries, the decision to opt for Nabi has raised questions about the team's long-term planning.

Injury woes continue to plague Indian cricket

The selection controversy is part of a broader pattern of fitness and injury concerns within the Indian team. From Jasprit Bumrah to Washington Sundar, several key players have faced breakdowns, prompting questions about the management of player workloads and recovery protocols.

An analysis by the Indian Express has pointed to systemic issues, noting that India's cricketers are increasingly prone to injuries. The Centre of Excellence (CoE) has also come under scrutiny after Bumrah's pull-out and Rana's fitness issues, raising doubts about the effectiveness of the board's rehabilitation programmes.

What lies ahead

As the BCCI moves to standardise fitness parameters, the focus will now shift to how the team management responds to the criticism. Nabi's performance in the Sri Lanka series, if he gets a game, will be closely watched by selectors and fans alike.

The coming weeks will reveal whether the board's new fitness protocols can prevent further breakdowns, and whether the selection committee can restore confidence in its decision-making. For now, the spotlight remains firmly on Agarkar, Gambhir, and the squad they have assembled.
