Kerala Literature Festival 2026 Opens in Kozhikode With Global Lineup

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 500 authors from 20+ countries are participating in KLF 2026.
  • Kozhikode’s beachfront venue hosts panels, workshops, and book launches.
  • The festival includes performances of Kathakali, Theyyam, and folk music.

Kozhikode Welcomes Global Literary Stars

The Kerala Literature Festival (KLF) 2026 opened in Kozhikode on Sunday, drawing thousands of book lovers to the city’s historic beachfront. The four-day event brings together more than 500 authors, poets, and thinkers from over 20 countries.

Day one saw packed sessions at the main venue, with the Arabian Sea providing a backdrop to conversations on fiction, poetry, and political writing. Organisers said the turnout exceeded expectations, with many attendees queuing up early for talks by prominent Indian and international writers.

A Platform for Diverse Voices

This year’s festival features a strong international presence. Authors from Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, the United Kingdom, and Brazil are among those scheduled to speak. Panels will cover topics ranging from climate change in literature to the rise of regional language publishing.

A dedicated children’s section offers storytelling sessions and illustration workshops. Organisers have also set up a translation booth, where volunteers help visitors explore works in Malayalam, Hindi, and English.

Culture and Performance Along the Beach

Evenings at KLF 2026 are reserved for cultural performances. Kathakali dancers, Theyyam artists, and folk musicians from different parts of Kerala are performing at an open-air stage near the venue. Festival director Ravi Deecee said the aim is to showcase Kerala’s artistic heritage alongside contemporary literature.

Local food stalls have been set up along the promenade, offering traditional snacks and refreshments. The festival organisers have arranged for extra public transport and parking to manage the crowds.

Notable Sessions and Launches

Several high-profile book launches are planned over the next three days. A session titled “Writing the Unspeakable” will feature authors discussing censorship and freedom of expression. Another panel, “The Future of Reading,” will examine how digital platforms are changing reading habits in India.

On Monday, a special session is dedicated to Malayalam literature, with veteran writers reading from their latest works. The festival also includes a poetry slam and a workshop on creative writing for young adults.

What to Watch

The festival runs until January 7. Visitors can expect more sessions on politics, history, and science. Organisers are already planning next year’s edition, with a focus on expanding the digital reach of the event for audiences unable to attend in person.
