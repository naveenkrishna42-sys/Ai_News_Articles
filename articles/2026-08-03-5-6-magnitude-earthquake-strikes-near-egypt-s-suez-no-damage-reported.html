5.6-Magnitude Earthquake Strikes Near Egypt's Suez, No Damage Reported

📅 2026-08-03 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • A 5.6-magnitude earthquake hit north of Egypt's Suez on Monday, with tremors felt in Cairo.
  • No damage or casualties have been reported, according to initial assessments.
  • The quake's epicenter was located in a relatively remote area, reducing immediate risk.

Earthquake Rattles Northern Egypt

A 5.6-magnitude earthquake struck north of Egypt's Suez city on Monday, sending tremors through the region and causing brief panic in Cairo. The quake, recorded at 00:37 GMT, originated in a relatively remote area near the Suez Canal, according to initial seismic data.

There have been no immediate reports of damage or casualties. Egyptian authorities have not yet issued a formal statement on the event, but preliminary assessments suggest the epicenter's location away from densely populated zones limited the impact.

Tremors Felt in Cairo

Residents in the capital, Cairo, reported feeling the ground shake for several seconds. Social media posts described buildings swaying and people evacuating offices and homes as a precaution.

The quake's magnitude has been reported variously as 5.3, 5.4, 5.5, and 5.6 by different monitoring agencies, reflecting slight variations in measurement methods. The United States Geological Survey (USGS) initially logged the event at 5.6, while regional observatories offered marginally different readings.

Region's Seismic History

Northern Egypt lies near the intersection of several tectonic plates, making it prone to occasional seismic activity. The Gulf of Suez and the Red Sea rift system are known sources of earthquakes in the area, though most are moderate and cause little damage.

Monday's tremor is among the stronger quakes recorded in the region in recent years. In 1992, a 5.8-magnitude earthquake near Cairo killed at least 370 people, a reminder of the potential risks. However, experts note that the depth and location of today's quake likely mitigated its effects.

No Tsunami Risk

Seismologists have ruled out any tsunami threat to coastal areas, including the Suez Canal and Mediterranean shoreline. The quake's epicenter was inland, and no abnormal sea-level changes have been observed.

Shipping traffic through the Suez Canal, a vital global trade artery, continues to operate normally. Port officials confirmed no disruptions to vessel movements following the tremor.

Authorities Monitoring Situation

The Egyptian National Seismic Network is monitoring aftershocks, though none have been reported in the hours since the main quake. Emergency services remain on standby across the Suez and Cairo governorates.

Local media have urged residents to remain calm and follow official guidance. The interior ministry has not announced any special measures, indicating the situation is under control.

Seismic activity in this part of Egypt is not uncommon, with small to moderate tremors recorded every few years. Monday's event, while noticeable, appears to have passed without significant consequence.

Officials are expected to release a detailed assessment in the coming hours. The public is advised to stay informed through official channels as monitoring continues.
