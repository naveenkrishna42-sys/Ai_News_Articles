US House votes to limit Iran war powers as oil crosses $100

📅 2026-07-24 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US House passes resolution to limit military action in Iran for first time since ceasefire collapsed
  • Some Republican lawmakers defect from party line to support the measure
  • Brent crude oil prices cross the $100 per barrel mark amid escalating tensions

House rebukes White House on Iran war powers

The United States House of Representatives has passed a resolution seeking to halt military action against Iran, marking the first time Congress has moved to limit the president's war powers since the breakdown of the ceasefire. The vote saw a significant number of Republican lawmakers cross party lines to support the measure.

The resolution, which passed with bipartisan support, directs the administration to seek congressional approval before committing US forces to further combat operations in Iran. Lawmakers cited growing public fatigue with the prolonged engagement and the absence of a clear exit strategy.

This is not the first time the House has taken such a step. Similar resolutions were introduced in previous sessions but failed to gain traction in the Senate. The current vote reflects deepening unease on Capitol Hill over the trajectory of the conflict.

Oil markets react sharply

As the House voted, global oil prices surged past the $100 per barrel mark for the first time in recent months. Brent crude touched $101.2 during intraday trading before settling slightly lower. Analysts attribute the spike to fears of supply disruptions from the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments.

India, which imports roughly 80% of its crude oil, faces immediate pressure on its current account deficit and retail fuel prices. Petrol and diesel rates in Indian metros have already risen by Rs 2-3 per litre over the past week, and traders expect further hikes if the conflict escalates.

The $100 oil threshold is a psychological marker that historically triggers policy responses from major consuming nations. The Indian government has not yet announced any intervention, but sources indicate the finance ministry is monitoring the situation closely.

Defections signal shifting political winds

The House vote exposed a rare rift within the Republican Party on national security matters. Several GOP lawmakers from districts with significant military and veteran populations argued that the conflict has dragged on without measurable gains. "We cannot continue to authorise open-ended military engagements without a clear mission," one defecting Republican said during floor debate.

Democrats, who have consistently pushed for war powers restrictions, rallied behind the resolution. House leadership framed the vote as a constitutional check on executive power, insisting that the decision to go to war belongs to Congress.

The White House has not yet issued a formal response to the vote. The resolution, even if passed by the Senate, would face a likely veto from the president, who has maintained that he has the authority to conduct military operations under existing authorisations.

What comes next

The Senate is expected to take up a companion resolution in the coming weeks. Its fate remains uncertain, as Republican leadership in the upper chamber has signalled opposition. Meanwhile, oil markets will watch for any signs of diplomatic movement or further escalation in the Gulf region. For India, the immediate concern is fuel prices and the broader economic impact of sustained $100-plus crude.
