US-Iran War: Analysts Warn of Strategic Escalation, Stalemate Risk

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Wars & Conflicts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Al Jazeera Centre for Studies report highlights strategic escalation in the US-Iran war.
  • Analysis focuses on conflict sustainability for both sides.
  • Report warns of potential stalemate and increased regional instability.

The US-Iran conflict is moving into a more dangerous phase, with both sides locked in a cycle of strategic escalation that tests their capacity to sustain a prolonged war. A new analysis from the Al Jazeera Centre for Studies warns that the current trajectory risks a bloody stalemate.

Escalation Dynamics and Strategic Calculations

The report, titled "Strategic Escalation and Conflict Sustainability in the US-Iran War," examines how both Washington and Tehran are calibrating their military and political moves. The analysts argue that each round of escalation is not just a tactical response but a calculated bid to change the strategic equation.

The US has been increasing pressure through a combination of direct strikes and support for regional allies. Iran, meanwhile, has relied on asymmetric tactics, including proxy forces and direct missile and drone attacks, to raise the costs for the American-led coalition.

According to the study, this pattern creates a feedback loop. A larger US strike invites a more sophisticated Iranian retaliation, which in turn demands a harsher American response. The risk is that both sides misjudge the other's red lines, leading to an unintended and uncontrollable spiral.

Sustainability: A Core Concern for Both Sides

A central theme of the Al Jazeera analysis is "conflict sustainability," the ability of each nation to maintain its military effort, economic stability, and domestic political support over time. For the US, the challenge is not just the financial cost of a distant war but the political will to sustain it through multiple presidential administrations.

For Iran, the calculation is even more stark. The war has placed immense strain on an already sanctions-crippled economy. The regime must balance its desire to project strength with the need to prevent internal unrest fueled by wartime hardship.

The report notes that while Iran can absorb punishment and continue low-level operations for years, it cannot win a conventional arms race against the US. Conversely, the US military can destroy Iranian assets but cannot easily eliminate the ideological and political roots of the conflict.

Regional Fallout and the Risk of a Wider War

The conflict is no longer contained to the Persian Gulf. The analysis highlights the expansion of the war's geography, with strikes hitting targets in Iraq, Syria, Yemen, and even Pakistan. This widening front increases the chances of a direct clash between the US and Iran that draws in other major powers.

The report also examines the role of proxies. Iran's network of allied militias across the region remains a key asset, but the war is degrading their capabilities. At the same time, the US is trying to bolster the defenses of Israel and Gulf Arab states, creating a more hardened and militarised region.

Analysts caution that the longer the war drags on, the more likely it becomes that a single miscalculation or a false-flag incident triggers a massive confrontation that neither side wants.

What comes next is uncertain. The Al Jazeera report suggests that without a credible diplomatic off-ramp, both the US and Iran will continue to escalate, each believing the other will blink first. The world is watching for any sign that either side is ready to step back from the brink.
