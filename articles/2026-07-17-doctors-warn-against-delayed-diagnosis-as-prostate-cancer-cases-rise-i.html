Doctors warn against delayed diagnosis as prostate cancer cases rise in India

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Prostate cancer cases are rising in India; delayed diagnosis is a major concern.
  • Urologist Dr. Sashiraj Singh identifies three common symptoms: frequent urination, weak urine stream, and blood in urine.
  • Myths about screening and treatment are causing men to delay seeking care, leading to advanced-stage detection.
  • Experts recommend annual PSA tests for men over 50, or earlier for those with family history.

Rising cases, late detection

Prostate cancer is becoming more common among Indian men, but many are still catching it too late. Doctors across the country are raising the alarm about delayed diagnosis, which is driving up mortality rates. Urologists say patients often ignore early warning signs or fall for myths that keep them away from screening.

According to recent reports, the number of new cases has climbed steadily over the past decade. Yet, awareness remains low. Most men only see a doctor when symptoms become unbearable, by which time the cancer may have spread.

Three everyday symptoms to watch for

Dr. Sashiraj Singh, a senior urologist, has highlighted three common symptoms that men should not ignore. The first is frequent urination, especially at night. The second is a weak or interrupted urine stream. The third is blood in the urine or semen.

“These are not normal signs of ageing,” Dr. Singh said. “If a man experiences any of these for more than two weeks, he must see a urologist.” He added that early stage prostate cancer often has no symptoms, making regular check-ups even more critical.

Myths that delay diagnosis

Several misconceptions are stopping Indian men from getting tested. One common myth is that prostate cancer only affects older men in Western countries. Another is that screening is painful or unnecessary. Some men also believe that treatment always leads to impotence or incontinence, so they avoid diagnosis altogether.

“These myths are dangerous,” Dr. Singh warned. “We have effective treatments today that preserve quality of life. But if you wait until stage four, options shrink.” He said many patients come in only after developing bone pain, a sign the cancer has metastasised.

Who should get screened and when

Doctors recommend that men over 50 get a prostate-specific antigen (PSA) blood test every year. Those with a family history of prostate or breast cancer should start at 40. The test is simple and widely available in Indian cities.

Dr. Singh stressed that a raised PSA does not always mean cancer. Infections or an enlarged prostate can also cause high levels. “Don’t panic. Just get it checked. Early detection saves lives,” he said.

Hospitals in metros like Delhi, Mumbai, and Bengaluru are seeing more men in their 50s and 60s with advanced prostate cancer. Doctors attribute this to a combination of diet changes, sedentary lifestyles, and lack of screening.

What lies ahead

The medical community is pushing for a national awareness campaign and subsidised PSA tests in primary health centres. Until then, the onus remains on individual men and their families. Watch for the symptoms, ignore the myths, and get tested — that simple step can make all the difference.
