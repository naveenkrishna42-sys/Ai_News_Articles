Jharkhand Exam Protests Intensify

📅 2026-08-16 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Students plan to burn effigies of Rahul Gandhi and CM Soren on August 16
  • Protesters plan to gherao CM's residence
  • Jharkhand government reopens dialogue with student protesters

Protests Over Recruitment Exam Row

Students in Jharkhand are intensifying their protests over the state's recruitment exam row. On August 16, they plan to burn effigies of Rahul Gandhi and Chief Minister Hemant Soren. The protests have been ongoing, with students expressing their frustration over the handling of government recruitment exams.

Demands and Threats

The protesters are demanding justice and fair treatment in the recruitment process. They have threatened to gherao the Chief Minister's residence, putting pressure on the government to address their concerns. The students are upset over the alleged irregularities and delays in the recruitment exams.

Some protesters have gone to extreme lengths to express their dissatisfaction. A protester recently shaved his head as a symbol of performing 'last rites' for the Chief Minister and Rahul Gandhi. The students are determined to get their voices heard and are using various forms of protest to achieve their goals.

Government's Response

The Jharkhand government has reopened dialogue with the student protesters in an attempt to resolve the issue. The government is trying to address the concerns of the students and find a solution to the recruitment exam row. The protesters, however, remain skeptical and are continuing their protests until their demands are met.

Protesters' Grievances

The protesters are primarily JPSC-JSSC aspirants who are upset over the handling of the recruitment exams. They have been paying with years of their lives, waiting for the exams to be conducted fairly and transparently. The students are demanding that the government take immediate action to address their grievances and ensure that the recruitment process is fair and just.

The protests have been gaining momentum, with more students joining in to express their solidarity. The situation remains tense, and it is unclear how the government will respond to the protesters' demands.

The next few days will be crucial in determining the outcome of the protests. Will the government be able to address the concerns of the students and find a solution to the recruitment exam row, or will the protests continue to escalate?
