Google and Epic End Battle: Third-Party Android App Stores Launch Next Week

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google and Epic Games have ended their legal dispute over app store policies.
  • Starting next week, third-party app stores will be allowed on Android devices.
  • This move breaks Google Play's long-held monopoly on Android app distribution.

Legal War Ends, New Era Begins

Google and Epic Games have called a truce. After years of courtroom battles over app store fees and policies, both companies have agreed to drop all litigation. The result: third-party Android app stores will be permitted on devices starting next week.

This settlement marks a dramatic shift. Google Play has been the default and often only way for most Android users to install apps. That monopoly is now broken.

What Changes Next Week

From next week, Android phone makers and users will be free to install and use app stores not controlled by Google. This includes stores like Epic's own game store, which was at the centre of the original dispute.

Developers will also gain more flexibility. They can now bypass Google's 30% commission on in-app purchases by directing users to alternative stores or direct payment methods. Google had previously fought hard to keep this system in place.

The Epic vs Google Timeline

The fight began in 2020 when Epic introduced a direct payment system in Fortnite, violating Google Play's rules. Google removed the game, and Epic sued. The case went through multiple hearings and appeals.

In 2024, a US court ruled that Google had illegally maintained a monopoly with its app store practices. The company was ordered to allow third-party stores. Google appealed, but the two sides have now reached a settlement instead of continuing the fight.

Neither company has disclosed the financial terms of the settlement. However, Epic has confirmed it will not have to pay any damages to Google.

What This Means for Android Users

For the average user, choice is the biggest gain. You will soon be able to download apps from stores like Samsung's Galaxy Store, Amazon's Appstore, or Epic's store without any system roadblocks. Security warnings may still appear, but they will not block installations.

Google has said it will implement new safety measures to protect users from malware in alternative stores. The company is expected to roll out a system that scans apps from all sources, not just Google Play.

Industry analysts believe this settlement could reshape the mobile app economy. Smaller developers may benefit from lower fees on third-party stores, while big players like Epic can offer games without sharing revenue.

Google's earlier loss in court had already set a precedent. The settlement just speeds up the implementation.

Watch for the first wave of third-party app stores to appear on Android phones from next Friday. Samsung and Xiaomi are expected to be among the first manufacturers to enable them by default.
