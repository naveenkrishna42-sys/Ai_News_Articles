New paper leak bill mandates 2-month probe window, fast-track trials

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • The new Bill prescribes a two-month window for completing probes into paper leaks.
  • Fast-track courts will be set up to ensure speedy trials in such cases.
  • The legislation aims to strengthen existing anti-cheating laws and curb exam malpractices.

Centre proposes stricter law to tackle exam malpractices

The Union government has introduced a new Bill in the Lok Sabha aimed at curbing paper leaks and other examination malpractices. The proposed legislation prescribes a two-month window for completing investigations and mandates the establishment of fast-track courts for speedy trials.

The Bill, which is set to be tabled on Monday, seeks to strengthen the existing anti-cheating framework. It comes in response to a spate of high-profile paper leak incidents that have disrupted recruitment exams and university admissions across multiple states.

Two-month deadline for probes

A key feature of the Bill is the strict timeline for investigations. Authorities will be required to complete their probe into any alleged paper leak within 60 days. This is intended to prevent cases from languishing for years, a common criticism of the current legal process.

If the investigation is not completed within the stipulated period, the Bill allows for an extension only under exceptional circumstances, subject to judicial oversight. Officials hope this will bring a sense of urgency to the enforcement machinery.

Fast-track courts to expedite justice

The legislation also provides for the setting up of fast-track courts dedicated to hearing cases related to exam malpractices. These courts will be expected to deliver verdicts within a defined timeframe, though the exact duration has not been specified in the available details.

Legal experts have pointed out that the success of this provision will depend on the availability of judges and infrastructure. Currently, many fast-track courts in the country are overburdened with pending cases.

Mixed reactions from experts and states

The Bill has drawn a mixed response. Some experts have questioned the efficacy of a fresh law when existing statutes have not been fully utilised. They argue that the focus should be on implementation rather than new legislation.

Separately, the Shiromani Akali Dal (SAD) has urged the Centre to allow states to hold their own recruitment exams. This demand reflects a broader concern about centralisation of the examination process, which some states say adds to delays and confusion.

Opposition parties are expected to scrutinise the Bill closely when it is taken up for debate in the Lok Sabha. The government has not yet indicated a timeline for its passage.

What to watch: The Bill's trajectory through Parliament and whether it receives bipartisan support. The test will be in its enforcement — if the two-month probe window and fast-track courts can actually deliver quicker justice in a system known for its backlog.
