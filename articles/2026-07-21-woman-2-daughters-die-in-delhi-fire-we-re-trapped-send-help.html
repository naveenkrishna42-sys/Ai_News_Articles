Woman, 2 Daughters Die In Delhi Fire: 'We're Trapped, Send Help'

📅 2026-07-21 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • A fire broke out at a house in Delhi's Lodhi Colony on Tuesday
  • A woman and her two daughters were killed in the blaze
  • The victims sent a desperate 'send help' message before being trapped

A woman and her two daughters died in a fire that broke out at their home in Delhi's Lodhi Colony on Tuesday morning. The victims sent a desperate message before being trapped, sources said.

Fire Breaks Out In BK Dutt Colony

The incident occurred at a house in BK Dutt Colony, also known as Lodhi Colony. Firefighters received a call around 6:30 am and rushed to the spot.

By the time they arrived, the blaze had engulfed a significant portion of the two-storey structure. Neighbours reported hearing screams and seeing smoke billowing from the upper floor.

'We're Trapped' Message Sent Before Death

According to preliminary reports, the woman managed to send a text message to a relative or friend, saying, "We're trapped, send help." The message was received minutes before the fire intensified.

The victims could not be rescued in time. Fire department officials said the flames and smoke made it impossible to reach them. The bodies were recovered after the fire was brought under control.

The deceased have been identified as a woman, aged around 35, and her two daughters, aged 12 and 8. Officials have not yet released their names pending family notification.

Cause Of Fire Under Investigation

The exact cause of the fire is not yet known. A short circuit is being suspected, but investigators have not confirmed this.

Police have registered a case of accidental death and are looking into the circumstances. Forensic teams have been called to the site to collect evidence.

Fire safety experts pointed out that many older homes in central Delhi lack proper fire exits and smoke alarms. This incident has raised fresh concerns about residential fire safety in the capital.

Neighbourhood In Shock

Residents of the quiet, tree-lined colony were in shock. Many gathered outside the house as firefighters worked to douse the flames.

"We heard screams around 6 am. By the time we came out, the whole floor was on fire. It was terrifying," a neighbour told reporters. "The family was very quiet. We never expected something like this."

Local authorities have announced an ex-gratia payment of Rs 5 lakh for the family of the deceased. Senior police officials have visited the spot and assured a thorough probe.

The fire department is expected to submit a detailed report in the coming days. Officials are also checking if the building had the required fire safety clearances.

What happens next: The investigation will focus on the electrical wiring and any structural violations. The tragedy has once again put the spotlight on fire safety compliance in residential areas across Delhi.
