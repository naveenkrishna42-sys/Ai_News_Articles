Bombay HC Warns of Oxygen Cylinders as Mangroves Shrink in Mumbai

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bombay High Court warned of severe oxygen crisis due to shrinking mangroves in Mumbai.
  • Court noted that mangroves are being destroyed at an alarming rate for development.
  • The observation came during a hearing on a petition regarding mangrove conservation.

Court Issues Stark Warning on Mangrove Destruction

The Bombay High Court on Wednesday warned that the rapid destruction of mangroves in Mumbai could force residents to carry oxygen cylinders for survival. The observation came during a hearing on a petition seeking protection of the city's remaining mangrove cover.

"One day, people will have to carry oxygen cylinders if this continues," a division bench of Justices Abhay Oka and S.S. Shinde remarked. The court expressed serious concern over the rate at which mangroves are being cleared for infrastructure projects and real estate development.

Mumbai's Green Lungs Under Threat

Mangroves act as natural carbon sinks and produce oxygen. They also protect the coastline from erosion and storm surges. Despite their ecological importance, large tracts have been lost in recent years.

Activists have repeatedly flagged illegal encroachments and dumping of debris in mangrove areas. The court noted that the state machinery has failed to prevent these violations effectively.

Government's Response Under Scrutiny

During the hearing, the state government submitted a status report on mangrove conservation efforts. The court, however, was not satisfied with the progress. "We are not seeing any concrete action on the ground. Only reports are being filed," the bench observed.

The court directed the Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority (MMRDA) and the forest department to submit detailed plans for mangrove restoration and protection. It also sought data on the exact extent of mangrove loss in the last decade.

Broader Implications for Urban Planning

The case highlights a growing conflict between development and environmental conservation in Mumbai. The city has one of the highest population densities in the world, and land is at a premium.

Environmentalists argue that short-term economic gains from clearing mangroves will lead to long-term ecological and health costs. The court's remarks underscore the urgency of balancing urban growth with environmental sustainability.

The bench has scheduled the next hearing for August 20, 2026. It has asked all concerned authorities to file compliance reports before that date.

What happens next: The court will review the government's restoration plans in the next hearing. Activists hope for strict enforcement of existing laws to prevent further damage.
