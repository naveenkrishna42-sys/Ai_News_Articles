RBI's FCNR(B) inflows surge to $73 billion, but rupee gains remain elusive

📅 2026-08-25 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • FCNR(B) inflows hit $73 billion, near $100 billion target
  • 76% of inflows are fresh money, reflecting strong diaspora confidence
  • Rupee's value unchanged despite large inflows due to RBI's conservative swap rates

Record Inflows Under FCNR(B) Scheme

The Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) FCNR(B) forex swap scheme has mobilised a record $73 billion in foreign exchange inflows. This marks a significant increase from previous years, with experts predicting that inflows could reach $100 billion by the end of August. The scheme, which allows NRIs and PIOs to invest in Indian debt instruments, has seen a surge in participation, with 35 million diaspora individuals contributing to the inflows.

Fresh Money Dominates Inflows

Analysis shows that 76% of the mobilised $73 billion is fresh money, indicating a strong confidence in the Indian economy among the diaspora. This fresh inflow is crucial for the economy, as it provides a steady stream of foreign currency that can be used to finance trade and investment.

Rupee's Value Unchanged Despite Large Inflows

Despite the record inflows, the rupee's value has remained unchanged. Experts attribute this to the RBI's conservative approach to setting swap rates. The RBI has been cautious in its approach, setting swap rates that are slightly higher than market rates, which has helped to curb speculative inflows but has also limited the potential for the rupee to appreciate.

Expert Opinions and Analysis

Economic analysts have varying opinions on the impact of the FCNR(B) inflows on the Indian economy. While some believe that the inflows will help to boost the economy by providing a steady stream of foreign currency, others argue that the conservative approach to setting swap rates has limited the potential for the rupee to appreciate. The ICRA, a leading credit rating agency, has predicted that inflows could near $100 billion by the end of August, highlighting the strong confidence in the Indian economy among the diaspora.
