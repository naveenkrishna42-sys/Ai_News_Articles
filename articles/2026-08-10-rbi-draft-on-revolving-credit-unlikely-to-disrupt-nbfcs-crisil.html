RBI draft on revolving credit unlikely to disrupt NBFCs: CRISIL

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • CRISIL assesses impact of RBI's draft revolving credit curbs on NBFCs
  • Agency says disruption to NBFC sector likely limited
  • Regulatory move part of broader RBI efforts to curb unsecured lending risks

The Reserve Bank of India's draft guidelines on revolving credit facilities are unlikely to cause major disruption to the non-banking financial company (NBFC) sector, according to credit rating agency CRISIL. The assessment offers a measured counterpoint to concerns that tighter norms could squeeze liquidity and alter lending dynamics.

What the draft proposes

The RBI's draft framework, issued for public consultation, seeks to rein in revolving credit lines such as overdrafts and cash credit facilities. It proposes that lenders reassess these limits at least once a year, and that any drawing beyond the sanctioned limit be treated as a separate, higher-cost facility.

For NBFCs, which rely heavily on such lines from banks and other financial institutions for their own funding, the changes could theoretically raise borrowing costs and introduce operational friction. However, CRISIL's analysis suggests that the actual impact on profitability and growth may be more contained than initially feared.

Why disruption may be limited

The rating agency points to several factors that could cushion the blow. Many NBFCs already maintain diversified funding sources, including bonds, commercial paper, and term loans, reducing their dependence on revolving credit. Additionally, the draft's annual review cycle aligns with existing internal risk management practices at larger, well-governed NBFCs.

CRISIL also notes that the proposed norms would primarily affect incremental utilisation beyond sanctioned limits, an area where most disciplined lenders and borrowers have limited exposure. As a result, the overall cost impact is expected to be modest, possibly translating into a small uptick in funding costs for some players.

Smaller NBFCs under closer watch

That said, the agency acknowledges that smaller NBFCs with weaker liquidity buffers could feel more pressure. These entities often depend on revolving credit as a flexible, low-cost funding tool, and may need to recalibrate their treasury operations or negotiate revised terms with lenders.

CRISIL's commentary comes at a time when the RBI has been tightening oversight of unsecured lending, including higher risk weights on consumer credit. The central bank's broader goal appears to be ensuring that credit growth does not outpace income growth, particularly in retail segments where stress has been building.

Industry response and next steps

Industry bodies and individual NBFCs are expected to submit their feedback to the RBI during the consultation period. Some have already voiced concerns about the administrative burden of annual reviews and the potential for unintended consequences on working capital availability for small businesses that depend on NBFCs for credit.

CRISIL's view, however, suggests that the sector—having weathered multiple regulatory shifts over the past decade—has the resilience to adapt without a significant hit to its core business. The final guidelines, which will follow stakeholder comments, are likely to incorporate some adjustments, further softening the impact.

Market participants will be watching the RBI's next move closely, particularly whether the final norms include any transition period or carve-outs for smaller lenders. For now, the consensus among analysts is that the draft, while signalling a stricter regulatory stance, is not a game-changer for the NBFC sector's fundamentals.
