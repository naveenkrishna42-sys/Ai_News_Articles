Kidney Disease: A Silent Threat

Kidney disease is a growing health concern in India, with an estimated 1.5 crore people suffering from chronic kidney disease (CKD). Unlike heart disease, kidney disease often causes no pain, making it a silent killer. A nephrologist has warned that certain everyday habits and foods could be silently damaging kidneys, increasing the risk of kidney failure.

Common Foods Linked to Kidney Disease

EatingWell has reported that certain common foods may increase the risk of kidney disease. These include processed meats, sugary drinks, and excessive salt intake. Processed meats contain high levels of sodium and nitrates, which can damage kidney cells. Sugary drinks can lead to obesity and high blood pressure, both of which can strain the kidneys. Excessive salt intake can cause fluid retention, leading to high blood pressure and kidney damage.

Everyday Habits Harming Kidneys

Hindustan Times reports that certain everyday habits could be silently damaging kidneys. These include smoking, excessive alcohol consumption, and lack of physical activity. Smoking can cause inflammation and damage to kidney cells. Excessive alcohol consumption can lead to high blood pressure and kidney damage. Lack of physical activity can lead to obesity and high blood pressure, both of which can strain the kidneys.

  • Smoking
  • Excessive alcohol consumption
  • Lack of physical activity

Parliamentary Panel Seeks Painkillers, Antibiotics Under Scanner

Outlook India reports that a parliamentary panel is seeking to bring painkillers and antibiotics under the scanner for their impact on kidney health. The panel is concerned that these medications can cause kidney damage, leading to chronic kidney disease. The panel is also seeking to regulate the use of these medications to protect kidney health.

Expert Opinion

Dr. Rajesh Kumar, a nephrologist, has warned that kidney disease is a growing health concern in India. He advises people to adopt a healthy lifestyle, including a balanced diet, regular physical activity, and avoiding smoking and excessive alcohol consumption. Dr. Kumar also recommends regular check-ups to monitor kidney health and detect any early signs of kidney damage.
