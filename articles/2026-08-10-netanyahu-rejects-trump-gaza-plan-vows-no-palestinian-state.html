Netanyahu rejects Trump Gaza plan, vows no Palestinian state

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Netanyahu publicly rejected Trump's 15-point Gaza peace plan
  • He ruled out Palestinian statehood during his tenure as PM
  • Israel will not withdraw troops until Hamas disarms, he said

Netanyahu draws a red line

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has bluntly dismissed the Gaza peace plan floated by former US President Donald Trump, declaring that a Palestinian state will not come into existence as long as he remains in office. The remarks, made on Sunday, mark a sharp rebuke of American mediation efforts and signal a hardening of Israel's stance.

Speaking to reporters, Netanyahu said the 15-point proposal put forward by Trump was unacceptable. He added that Israel knows how to stand up to American ideas, a pointed comment aimed at Washington's renewed push for a ceasefire and a long-term political framework in Gaza.

What Trump's plan proposed

The Trump plan, details of which emerged in recent days, reportedly includes a phased Israeli withdrawal from Gaza, a temporary administrative body, and a pathway toward eventual Palestinian self-governance. It was pitched as a compromise that could break the cycle of violence that has devastated the enclave since the latest conflict erupted.

But Netanyahu's response leaves little room for negotiation. He rejected the plan in its entirety, insisting that any pullout of Israeli forces would only occur after Hamas is fully disarmed. The prime minister framed the demand as a non-negotiable security prerequisite, not a political bargaining chip.

No state on his watch

The most striking element of Netanyahu's statement was his categorical refusal to countenance a Palestinian state. I will not allow a Palestinian state to be established as long as I am prime minister, he said, according to reports. This goes beyond previous equivocations and aligns him squarely with the far-right factions in his coalition.

Palestinian leaders have long viewed statehood as the core of any peace deal, and Netanyahu's words are likely to be met with anger in Ramallah and Gaza. The timing is also significant, coming as international mediators push for a durable truce and as humanitarian conditions in Gaza remain dire.

US-Israel tensions simmer

The rejection underscores growing friction between Netanyahu's government and Washington over Gaza policy. Trump's team had hoped the plan would be a diplomatic win, but the Israeli leader's public dismissal complicates those efforts.

Israeli officials have not confirmed any alternative framework, but Netanyahu's insistence on Hamas's disarmament as a precondition suggests he is in no hurry to end the military campaign. Analysts note that his political survival may depend on maintaining a tough security posture, especially with coalition partners demanding an even more aggressive approach.

What happens next

With Netanyahu's rejection now public, the onus shifts to Washington and regional mediators like Egypt and Qatar to salvage any diplomatic track. The coming weeks will show whether Trump's team revises its blueprint or abandons it altogether, and whether Israel's military operations in Gaza continue at their current intensity.

For now, the prospects of a negotiated settlement appear bleak, and the humanitarian situation in Gaza is likely to worsen as the political deadlock persists.
