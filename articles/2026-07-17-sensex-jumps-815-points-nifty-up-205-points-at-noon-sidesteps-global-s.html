Sensex Jumps 815 Points, Nifty Up 205 Points at Noon, Sidesteps Global Selloff

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex surged 815 points, Nifty gained 205 points by noon on July 17.
  • Indian markets outperformed global peers, sidestepping a broad global selloff.
  • Gains were broad-based, led by banking and IT stocks.
  • Analysts cited strong domestic fundamentals and FII inflows as key drivers.

Indian Markets Rally Despite Global Gloom

Indian equity benchmarks staged a strong rally on Friday, July 17, 2026, with the BSE Sensex jumping 815 points and the NSE Nifty adding 205 points by noon. The gains came even as most global markets struggled, indicating that domestic factors are keeping investor sentiment positive.

The Sensex was trading at 82,340 levels, while the Nifty hovered around 25,080. Market breadth was positive, with advances outpacing declines on both exchanges.

Banking and IT Stocks Lead the Charge

Banking stocks were the biggest contributors to the rally, with the Nifty Bank index rising over 2%. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India all gained more than 1.5%. Analysts attributed this to strong quarterly earnings expectations and improving asset quality.

IT stocks also saw robust buying, led by Infosys and TCS, which added nearly 2% each. The sector benefited from a weaker rupee and steady demand from key markets like the US and Europe.

Global Markets Under Pressure

While Indian markets soared, most Asian and European indices were in the red. Concerns over rising interest rates in the US and a slowdown in China weighed on global sentiment. The Dow Jones and S&P 500 futures were also trading lower ahead of the US open.

However, Indian markets managed to sidestep the selloff, thanks to strong domestic institutional buying and resilient economic data. Foreign portfolio investors have also been net buyers in Indian equities this month, adding to the positive momentum.

What’s Driving the Rally?

Market participants pointed to several factors behind the sharp upmove. The government’s focus on infrastructure spending and manufacturing has boosted sentiment. Additionally, corporate earnings for the June quarter have largely beaten expectations, providing a strong fundamental base.

The Reserve Bank of India’s decision to hold interest rates steady last month has also helped. Lower borrowing costs are expected to support consumption and investment in the coming quarters.

Technical analysts noted that the Nifty has broken above key resistance levels, which could trigger further short-covering and fresh buying. The index is now just 2% away from its all-time high.

Investors will watch for global cues and any unexpected policy moves from central banks. For now, the mood on Dalal Street remains upbeat, with most analysts expecting the rally to continue in the near term.
