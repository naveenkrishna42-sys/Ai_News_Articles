Thomas Tuchel: England became ‘too passive’ after scoring in World Cup semifinal loss to Argentina

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • England lost 2-1 to Argentina in the 2026 World Cup semifinal after taking an early lead.
  • Manager Thomas Tuchel said the team became 'too passive' after scoring.
  • Lionel Messi starred for Argentina, adding to his tournament legacy with another decisive performance.

England manager Thomas Tuchel did not mince words after his team's 2-1 loss to Argentina in the 2026 FIFA World Cup semifinal. The German coach said his side became "too passive" after taking an early lead, a tactical shift that allowed Argentina to seize control of the match.

Speaking to reporters after the game, Tuchel pointed directly to the period immediately following England's opening goal. "We scored and then we stopped doing what had got us ahead," he said. "We dropped too deep. We gave them space and invited pressure."

A lead that slipped away

England started the match with intensity and took the lead through a well-worked move in the first half. For a brief period, it looked as though the Three Lions might dictate the tempo against Lionel Messi's Argentina.

But the advantage did not last. Argentina equalised before halftime, capitalising on a spell of sustained pressure that Tuchel felt his side should have weathered better. Messi then produced a moment of quality in the second half to put Argentina ahead, a goal that ultimately decided the contest.

"Against a team like Argentina, you cannot afford to sit back and protect a one-goal lead," Tuchel said. "They have too much quality. You have to keep playing your game."

Messi's tournament legacy grows

The defeat was another chapter in a World Cup campaign defined by Messi's brilliance. The Argentine captain's performances have drawn comparisons to legends of the past, with statistical milestones piling up. According to tournament data, Messi now has 33 goal involvements (goals and assists) in World Cup matches since his debut in 2006, compared to 25 for France's Kylian Mbappé.

Further historical comparisons show Messi surpassing Pelé's tally of 10 goal involvements in World Cups, and moving ahead of Diego Maradona's 71. Messi now stands at 99, placing him in a stratosphere of his own. In the semifinal alone, he dictated the rhythm of Argentina's attacks and provided the decisive moment that sent his team to the final.

For England, the task of stopping Messi proved too heavy. Tuchel acknowledged the challenge. "When you give him time on the ball, he will punish you. We did that too often."

Tuchel's future with England

Despite the painful exit, there has been no public discussion of Tuchel's position being under threat. Former England striker Alan Shearer, writing after the match, argued that Tuchel's time with the national team should not be judged solely on this semifinal defeat.

Tuchel himself struck a defiant tone. "This is not the end. The team is young, talented, and growing. We will learn from this," he said.

The England manager will now have time to assess what went wrong in the knockout rounds. The team's defensive structure, which was a hallmark of Tuchel's tenure, came undone at the worst possible moment. Whether he adjusts his approach or sticks with the same system will be a key question in the months ahead.

Argentina, meanwhile, march on to the final. Messi will have one more chance to add to his extraordinary World Cup legacy. For England, the inquest has already begun.

Tuchel and his staff will review the match footage and prepare for the next international window. The focus will be on ensuring that England does not repeat the passive approach that cost them a place in the final.
