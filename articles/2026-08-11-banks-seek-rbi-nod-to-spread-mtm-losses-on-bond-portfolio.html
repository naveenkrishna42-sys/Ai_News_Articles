Banks seek RBI nod to spread MTM losses on bond portfolio

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Lenders have sought RBI approval to defer MTM losses on bond holdings.
  • The move aims to cushion the impact of rising bond yields on bank profitability.
  • RBI's decision will determine how banks manage their investment portfolios.

Banks seek regulatory relief on bond losses

Indian lenders have approached the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) with a request to spread their mark-to-market (MTM) losses on bond portfolios over multiple quarters, according to a report by The Economic Times. The move comes as rising bond yields have eroded the value of banks' held-for-trading and available-for-sale securities.

The proposal, if accepted, would allow banks to smooth the impact of these losses on their profit and loss statements, rather than absorbing them in a single quarter. This would provide temporary relief to lenders grappling with shrinking margins and elevated provisioning requirements.

Why MTM losses are pinching

MTM losses occur when the current market value of bonds falls below their book value. In a rising interest rate environment, bond prices decline, forcing banks to recognise losses on their investment portfolios. The extent of these losses has grown as the RBI maintained a hawkish stance to curb inflation, pushing yields on government securities higher.

For many banks, the bond portfolio constitutes a significant chunk of their assets. A sharp spike in yields can wipe out quarterly profits, especially for public sector lenders with large held-to-maturity books. By seeking permission to defer these losses, banks aim to avoid a sudden hit to their capital adequacy ratios.

Regulatory precedent and options

The RBI has in the past allowed banks to spread MTM losses under exceptional circumstances. In 2018, for instance, it permitted lenders to amortise losses on held-to-maturity securities over four quarters. A similar dispensation now could provide a cushion, though the central bank may weigh the risks of masking underlying asset quality issues.

Analysts note that while deferring losses offers short-term comfort, it does not address the root cause: structural mismatches in banks' asset-liability management. Some experts argue that a more durable solution lies in deepening the corporate bond market and encouraging banks to hedge their interest rate risk more actively.

Impact on profitability and capital

If the RBI accedes to the request, banks could report healthier near-term earnings, potentially boosting investor sentiment. However, the deferred losses would still need to be recognised eventually, creating overhangs on future balance sheets. Smaller lenders, which have thinner capital buffers, are likely to benefit the most from any such relaxation.

The central bank has not yet commented on the proposal. Its decision will hinge on broader financial stability considerations, including the need to maintain transparency in banks' financial reporting.

Market participants are watching for any signals from the RBI's monetary policy committee, which is scheduled to meet later this year. A shift in the rate cycle could either exacerbate or alleviate the pressure on bond portfolios.

Road ahead

The RBI's stance will be crucial in determining how banks navigate the current yield environment. While lenders await a formal response, they are also exploring internal measures such as trimming bond durations and increasing floating-rate lending.

For now, the industry is holding its breath. A favourable ruling could provide a much-needed breather, but the underlying challenges of rising rates and liquidity management remain.
