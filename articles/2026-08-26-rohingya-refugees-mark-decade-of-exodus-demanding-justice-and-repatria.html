Rohingya Refugees Mark Nine Years Since Exodus

Nine years ago, on August 25, 2017, a brutal military crackdown in Myanmar's Rakhine State forced over 740,000 Rohingya Muslims to flee across the border into Bangladesh. Today, thousands of these refugees gathered in the sprawling camps of Cox's Bazar to commemorate the anniversary and demand justice and safe repatriation.

Rally Demands: Safe Return and Justice

The refugees, waving the Rohingya flag and holding placards, chanted slogans demanding their right to return home, along with justice and equality. They accused the Myanmar military, known as the Tatmadaw, of genocide and called for the international community to intervene.

Mohammed Ehsan, a refugee leader, told TIVRA News, 'We want to go back to our ancestral homes with justice and security. We can't live like this, in these camps, forever.'

Progress Towards Repatriation: Slow and Uncertain

Bangladesh and Myanmar have signed repatriation agreements, but the process has been slow and voluntary returns have been negligible. According to the Bangladesh government, only around 1,000 refugees have returned to Myanmar since 2017, despite hundreds of thousands expressing a desire to do so.

International Pressure Mounts for Accountability

Human rights groups and the UN have documented widespread and systematic human rights abuses and war crimes by the Myanmar military against the Rohingya, including massacre, rape, and arson. The UN's Independent Investigative Mechanism for Myanmar is building cases against senior military leaders for the International Criminal Court.

  • Pros of continued international pressure: Ensures spotlight on Myanmar's human rights abuses, encourages accountability, and potentially influences repatriation discussions.
  • Cons of continued international pressure: Could strain relations between Myanmar and international donors, delay economic recovery and development.
