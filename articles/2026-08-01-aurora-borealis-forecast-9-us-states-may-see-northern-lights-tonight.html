Aurora Borealis Forecast: 9 US States May See Northern Lights Tonight

Key points
  • Up to nine US states could see the aurora borealis tonight due to a geomagnetic storm.
  • Scientists indicate more powerful solar waves are still arriving after the initial storm.
  • Forecasters have issued a guidance downgrade alert, urging skywatchers to check local conditions.
  • The celestial display is expected to boost tourism and economic activity in border states.

Skywatchers in Nine States Get a Rare Treat

Tonight, the night sky could turn into a canvas of green, purple, and pink as a geomagnetic storm makes the aurora borealis visible in up to nine US states. Forecasters at the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) have issued a watch, saying the display could be seen as far south as parts of the Midwest and Northeast. The storm, which began earlier today, is expected to peak in the evening hours.

This is not a typical event. The aurora, usually confined to high-latitude regions like Alaska and Canada, is being pushed further south by a strong burst of solar wind. The storm's intensity has caught the attention of both amateur astronomers and seasoned skywatchers, who are already scouting dark-sky locations away from city lights.

More Powerful Waves Are Coming

According to reports from Tech Times, the initial geomagnetic storm has arrived, but scientists warn that more powerful waves are still following behind. This means the aurora could become even more vivid in the coming hours, with the potential for multiple peaks of activity. The solar particles responsible for the display are travelling at high speed, and their interaction with Earth's magnetic field is expected to intensify.

Experts explain that the storm is the result of a coronal mass ejection (CME) from the Sun, which sent a cloud of charged particles hurtling toward Earth. When these particles collide with gases in our atmosphere, they emit light in different colours. The strength of the storm is measured on a scale from G1 (minor) to G5 (extreme), and tonight's event is currently rated at G3, with the possibility of reaching G4.

Guidance Downgrade Alert for Observers

Despite the excitement, the dars.gov.et website has issued a guidance downgrade alert. This means that while the aurora is possible, it may not be visible everywhere in the forecasted region. Cloud cover, light pollution, and the exact timing of the storm's peak could all affect visibility. Skywatchers are advised to check local weather forecasts and find a dark, open area with a clear view of the northern horizon.

For those hoping to catch a glimpse, the best time is typically between 10 pm and 2 am local time. Using a camera with a long exposure can help capture the aurora even when it is faint to the naked eye. Patience is key—the display can fade and reappear over the course of the night.

Economic Boost for Border States

The aurora forecast is not just a scientific curiosity. It has sparked a wave of tourism interest, particularly in states along the Canadian border. Hotels in places like northern Minnesota and Michigan's Upper Peninsula are reporting an influx of bookings from people hoping to see the lights. Local tourism boards are capitalizing on the event, promoting viewing parties and photography tours.

This phenomenon has a broader economic impact. A report from Outside Magazine suggests that aurora events can generate significant revenue for rural communities, which often lack other major attractions. Restaurants, gas stations, and lodges all benefit from the sudden surge in visitors. With more solar activity expected in the coming years as the Sun approaches its solar maximum, these events could become more frequent.

What to Expect in the Coming Days

Scientists are closely monitoring the situation as additional solar waves arrive. The storm could persist through the weekend, with the possibility of another peak on Saturday night. However, the intensity is difficult to predict, and officials caution that the aurora may not be visible in all areas.

Travel Tuesday tips from WTNH.com recommend checking real-time aurora forecasts and joining local astronomy groups for updates. For now, the advice is simple: look north, hope for clear skies, and enjoy the show. The Sun's activity is expected to remain elevated for the next several months, meaning more opportunities for skywatchers in the weeks ahead.
