Bengaluru Hotel Raids: Rotten Veggies, Expired Milk Found in Star Kitchens

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Health department formed 30 special teams to inspect hotels, restaurants, and food outlets across Bengaluru.
  • Raids at 3-star and 5-star hotels uncovered rotten vegetables, expired milk, and fungus on produce.
  • Officials have initiated action against violators under food safety norms.

Bengaluru's food safety drive has pulled back the curtain on what goes on inside the kitchens of some of the city's most prominent hotels. Health officials, acting on a series of inspections, have found rotten vegetables, expired milk, and visible fungus on produce in both 3-star and 5-star establishments. The findings have triggered swift action, with the health department forming 30 special teams to conduct raids across the city.

Raids Uncover Spoiled Stock

The inspections, carried out over the past week, targeted hotels, restaurants, and food outlets in various parts of Bengaluru. Officials reported that in several instances, kitchens were storing ingredients well past their usable dates. Expired milk cartons, wilted and rotting vegetables, and produce showing fungal growth were among the items seized.

"We found items that should have been discarded days ago," said a health department official involved in the raids. The teams also flagged poor storage practices and hygiene lapses, raising serious questions about the quality control measures at these establishments.

Action Against Violators

The health department has initiated proceedings against the offending hotels under the Food Safety and Standards Act. Penalties could range from fines to temporary suspension of licenses, depending on the severity of the violations. Officials have not yet confirmed the full list of hotels penalized, but sources indicate that both budget and luxury properties are among those caught.

This is not the first time Bengaluru's hospitality sector has faced scrutiny. However, the sheer scale of the current drive—with 30 dedicated teams fanning out across the city—signals a more aggressive stance by the authorities.

What the Inspections Covered

  • Storage conditions for perishables like dairy and vegetables
  • Expiry dates on packaged goods and beverages
  • Kitchen hygiene and pest control measures
  • Staff adherence to food handling protocols

The teams also collected samples from several outlets for laboratory testing to check for contamination beyond the visible spoilage. Results are pending, and further action may follow.

Public Trust at Stake

For a city that prides itself on its culinary diversity, these revelations are a sobering reminder of the risks diners face. Star ratings, it appears, do not guarantee fresh ingredients or clean practices. "Consumers assume a 5-star hotel means top-notch safety," noted a food safety expert tracking the raids. "But these inspections show that assumption can be misplaced."

The health department has urged the public to report any suspected violations, encouraging a culture of vigilance. Meanwhile, hotel associations have promised to cooperate with authorities and tighten internal audits.

As the special teams continue their sweep across Bengaluru, more violations are likely to surface. The coming weeks will reveal whether this crackdown leads to lasting change or merely a temporary scare for the city's hospitality industry.
