RBI flags common tricks banks use to mis-sell products to customers

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has identified specific deceptive sales practices used by banks to mis-sell financial products.
  • Common tactics include bundling unwanted insurance with loans and hiding high fees.
  • The central bank has urged customers to be vigilant and read all documents carefully before signing.

RBI warns banks over misselling tactics

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has published a detailed list of tricks that banks use to push the wrong financial products onto customers. The central bank's step is aimed at protecting ordinary savers and borrowers from being misled.

Bank officials often use high-pressure sales language, promising quick approvals or exclusive deals. The RBI's advisory makes it clear that these practices violate fair conduct norms.

Common tricks banks employ

One common practice is bundling. A customer applying for a home loan is told that a certain insurance policy is mandatory. In reality, it is not. The bank adds the premium to the loan amount, increasing the total repayment burden.

Another trick is the bait-and-switch. The bank advertises a low-interest loan but, at the time of signing, pushes a higher-cost product. The fine print often hides the real terms.

Banks also misrepresent investment products. A fixed deposit may be presented as a savings account, or a market-linked insurance plan is sold as a safe, guaranteed-return instrument. Customers only discover the truth when they need to withdraw money.

Hidden charges and misleading comparisons

Many loan offers come with hidden processing fees, prepayment penalties, or annual charges. The RBI has noted that these charges are not clearly disclosed upfront. Customers end up paying far more than they expected.

Banks sometimes compare their products against competitors' offerings in a selective manner. They highlight only the benefits of their own product while ignoring the drawbacks. This makes it difficult for customers to make an informed choice.

The central bank has also flagged the practice of selling credit cards without explaining the interest calculations. The interest-free period is often misunderstood, and late payment fees can quickly pile up.

What customers should do

RBI has advised customers to always read the offer document and terms and conditions before signing. Never rely solely on the salesperson's words. Ask for all charges in writing.

Customers should compare products from multiple banks using independent sources. Do not accept bundled products without checking if they are truly needed. It is also wise to check the bank's grievance redressal mechanism.

If a customer feels they have been mis-sold a product, they can file a complaint with the bank's internal ombudsman. If not satisfied, they can approach the RBI's Banking Ombudsman.

What happens next

The RBI is expected to tighten its monitoring of bank sales practices. Banks found violating norms may face penalties. For now, the central bank is relying on customer awareness as the first line of defence.
