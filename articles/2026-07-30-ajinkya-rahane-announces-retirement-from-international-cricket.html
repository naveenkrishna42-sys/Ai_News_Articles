Ajinkya Rahane announces retirement from international cricket

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ajinkya Rahane announced retirement from international cricket and all formats.
  • BCCI paid tribute to his distinguished international career spanning over a decade.
  • Rahane is best remembered for leading India to a historic Test series win in Australia in 2020-21.
  • He played 85 Tests, 90 ODIs, and 20 T20Is for India, amassing over 12,000 international runs.
  • His finest knocks include the Melbourne century in 2014 and the match-winning 112 at Lord's in 2014.

Ajinkya Rahane, one of Indian cricket's most dependable middle-order batters, has announced his retirement from international cricket and all formats. The decision brings the curtain down on a 14-year career that saw him play 85 Tests, 90 One-Day Internationals, and 20 T20 Internationals for India.

The Board of Control for Cricket in India paid tribute to Rahane, calling him a warrior who played some of the most memorable innings in Indian cricket's modern history. BCCI officials highlighted his role in several historic overseas victories.

A career defined by overseas grit

Rahane's Test career will be remembered for his ability to perform in challenging overseas conditions. He scored 5,085 runs in Tests at an average of 38.52, with 12 centuries and 26 fifties. His average in SENA countries—South Africa, England, New Zealand, and Australia—stood at a respectable 43.83.

His finest hour came during the 2020-21 Border-Gavaskar Trophy in Australia. After India were bowled out for 36 in Adelaide, Rahane stepped in as captain for the injured Virat Kohli. He scored a stunning 112 at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, leading India to a series-levelling win before the team scripted a famous 2-1 series triumph.

The Lord's hundred and other classics

Rahane's 103 at Lord's in 2014 remains one of the finest Test hundreds by an Indian batter in England. Chasing a tricky target, he batted with composure and class, taking India to a memorable victory. That knock, along with his 147 at the MCG later that year, cemented his reputation as a man for the big occasion.

In ODIs, Rahane scored 3,015 runs in 90 matches at an average of 35.47, including three centuries. He was a regular in the limited-overs side until 2018, often opening the batting. His T20I career was shorter, with 375 runs in 20 matches.

Leadership and legacy

Beyond his batting, Rahane captained India in six Tests, winning four and losing one. His calm demeanour and tactical acumen earned praise from teammates and critics alike. Under his leadership, India not only won in Australia but also drew a Test series in South Africa in 2021.

Rahane also played a key role in India's 2013 Champions Trophy win and was part of the squad that reached the 2015 World Cup semifinals. He played for Rajasthan Royals, Rising Pune Supergiant, Delhi Capitals, and Chennai Super Kings in the Indian Premier League.

What next for Rahane?

Rahane has not indicated any immediate plans for a coaching or commentary role. With his retirement, Indian cricket loses a batter who often put the team first, playing unselfish knocks in tough conditions. His legacy will be that of a quiet warrior who rose to the occasion when his country needed him most.

Fans and former cricketers will now watch to see if Rahane takes up a mentorship role in domestic cricket or the IPL. His experience and leadership qualities could be valuable for the next generation of Indian batters.
