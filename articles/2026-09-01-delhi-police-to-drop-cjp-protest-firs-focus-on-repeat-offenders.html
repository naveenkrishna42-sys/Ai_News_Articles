Delhi Police to drop CJP protest FIRs, focus on repeat offenders

📅 2026-09-01 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi Police to drop all FIRs against CJP protesters
  • Fresh FIRs to be filed against individuals with criminal history
  • Centre and Delhi government seek Supreme Court intervention to quash FIRs against NEET protesters

Delhi Police Shifts Focus to Repeat Offenders Amid Protest FIRs

Delhi Police has announced a significant shift in its approach to handling FIRs related to the ongoing protests by the Citizens for Justice and Peace (CJP). The police department has decided to drop all FIRs filed against protesters, but will file fresh cases against individuals who have a criminal history.

Centre and Delhi Government Seek Supreme Court Intervention

The Centre and the Delhi government have moved the Supreme Court seeking quashing of FIRs against youth protesters involved in the NEET-UG protests. The government argues that the FIRs are a violation of the right to peaceful protest and seeks 'complete justice' under Article 142 of the Constitution.

Analysis of the Police's Decision

The decision by Delhi Police to drop FIRs against CJP protesters is a strategic move aimed at de-escalating tensions and focusing on individuals who pose a threat to public order. However, critics argue that this approach may not address the root cause of the protests and could lead to a perception of selective justice.

Key Facts About the Protests

  • CJP protests have been ongoing for several months, with demands for justice for victims of police brutality and corruption.
  • NEET-UG protests were in response to the cancellation of the NEET-UG exam, which sparked nationwide protests.
  • Both sets of protests have seen widespread participation and have been met with a heavy-handed response from law enforcement agencies.

Impact on Public Perception

The decision by Delhi Police to drop FIRs against CJP protesters is likely to have a significant impact on public perception. While it may be seen as a step towards justice, it could also be interpreted as a lack of commitment to upholding the rule of law. The shift in focus to individuals with criminal history is a more targeted approach, but it may not address the broader issues of police brutality and corruption that fuel the protests.

Conclusion

The decision by Delhi Police to drop FIRs against CJP protesters is a complex and multifaceted issue. While it may be seen as a strategic move to de-escalate tensions, it also raises questions about the commitment to upholding the rule of law. The Centre and Delhi government's move to seek Supreme Court intervention to quash FIRs against NEET protesters highlights the broader issues of police brutality and corruption that need to be addressed.
