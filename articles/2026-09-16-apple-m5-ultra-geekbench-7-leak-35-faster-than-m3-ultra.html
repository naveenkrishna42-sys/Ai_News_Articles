Apple M5 Ultra Geekbench 7 Leak: 35% Faster Than M3 Ultra

✍️ By TIVRA News Tech & Gadgets Desk 📅 2026-09-16 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Apple's M5 Ultra outperforms the M3 Ultra by roughly 35% in early Geekbench 7 testing.
  • The chip reportedly beats AMD's 96-core Ryzen Threadripper PRO 9995WX in multi-core score with less than half the core count.
  • M6 benchmarks for an upcoming Mac mini also surfaced, signalling a faster refresh cycle across Apple silicon tiers.

Apple M5 Ultra Breaks Cover in Geekbench 7

The first Geekbench 7 result for Apple's M5 Ultra has surfaced, and the numbers are aggressive: roughly 35% faster than the M3 Ultra across the benchmark's composite score. The listing, first reported by Mezha and corroborated by subsequent leaks, points to a chip that widens Apple's lead in single-thread responsiveness while closing the gap on workstation-class multi-core rivals.

For buyers weighing a Mac Studio or Mac Pro purchase, this matters more than a synthetic score. Geekbench 7 introduces heavier mixed workloads and revised memory bandwidth weighting, making it a closer proxy for real editing, compilation, and simulation tasks than Geekbench 6 ever was.

M5 Ultra vs M3 Ultra vs Threadripper: The Numbers

Independent aggregators have compared the leak against AMD's 96-core Ryzen Threadripper PRO 9995WX. The claim circulating is that the M5 Ultra delivers about 11% higher multi-core output with less than half the core count. Treat vendor-adjacent leaks with caution, but the efficiency delta is the headline, not the raw win.

Why Core Efficiency Beats Core Count

AMD's Threadripper PRO wins on parallelism, not per-core throughput. A 96-core part drawing 350W in a workstation chassis is a different product category from a 32-core Apple SoC pulling a fraction of that power. For Indian studios paying commercial electricity tariffs, the thermal and power envelope directly affects total cost of ownership over a three-year cycle.

Memory bandwidth remains Apple's structural advantage. Unified memory removes the PCIe bottleneck that constrains discrete GPU workflows, which is why video editors and ML engineers consistently report better sustained performance on Ultra chips than raw Geekbench numbers suggest.

M6 Mac Mini Leaks Add Pressure

Separately, an M6 Geekbench listing tied to an upcoming Mac mini has appeared, with MacRumors and AppleInsider reporting gains over the M5. The timing is unusual: Apple typically spaces silicon generations by 12-18 months, and overlapping M5 Ultra and M6 leaks suggest an accelerated roadmap.

Buyers should read this as a warning against buying at the top of a cycle. If an M6 Mac mini lands soon, current M4 and M5 desktop pricing will correct sharply within a quarter.

Pros and Cons for Buyers

  • Pro: 35% generational uplift extends usable lifespan for 8K editing and local AI inference.
  • Pro: Best-in-class performance-per-watt reduces cooling and power costs in Indian offices.
  • Pro: Beating a 96-core Threadripper with fewer cores signals strong architectural efficiency.
  • Con: Geekbench 7 scores are synthetic; real-world gains will be narrower.
  • Con: Unconfirmed pricing could push Ultra configs past ₹6 lakh with memory upgrades.
  • Con: Imminent M6 leaks make waiting, not buying, the rational move for most users.

Verdict and Buying Advice

The M5 Ultra looks like a genuine workstation-class leap, but Geekbench 7 numbers alone should not trigger a purchase. Professionals with immediate 3D rendering or LLM fine-tuning needs will find the efficiency argument compelling; everyone else should wait for official Apple pricing and independent thermal testing before committing lakhs to a new Mac Studio.
