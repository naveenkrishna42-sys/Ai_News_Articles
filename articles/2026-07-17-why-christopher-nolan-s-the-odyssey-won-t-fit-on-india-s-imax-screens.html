Why Christopher Nolan's 'The Odyssey' won't fit on India's IMAX screens

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nolan filmed The Odyssey using IMAX film cameras, requiring 70mm projectors.
  • India's IMAX theatres use digital projection, unable to show the full frame.
  • Fans in India will see a cropped version, missing the director's intended visual scope.

The IMAX gap in Indian theatres

Christopher Nolan's latest epic, The Odyssey, is a technical marvel shot entirely on IMAX film cameras. But if you are in India and planning to watch it on an IMAX screen, you will not see Nolan's full vision. The reason lies in the hardware.

Nolan filmed the movie using large-format IMAX film, which requires 70mm projectors to project the image in its intended aspect ratio. India's IMAX theatres, however, are equipped with digital projection systems. They cannot handle the physical film print. This means the film will be shown in a cropped, digital version on Indian IMAX screens.

A recurring problem for Nolan films

This is not a new issue. Nolan's previous film, Oppenheimer, faced the same limitation. Only a handful of theatres worldwide, none in India, have the dual 70mm IMAX projectors needed for the full-frame experience. Indian audiences had to make do with a reduced aspect ratio.

For The Odyssey, the situation is identical. The director has stated in the past that digital IMAX loses about 26% of the image captured by the film negative. That lost detail is what Indian viewers will miss.

Why Indian theatres have not upgraded

Installing 70mm IMAX projectors is expensive. Each unit costs crores of rupees, and the film prints themselves are heavy and costly to transport. Multiplex chains in India have opted for digital IMAX because it is cheaper and allows for easier programming of multiple movies.

There is also the question of demand. The audience for Nolan's films in India is large, but multiplex owners are not convinced that upgrading to film projection for a single director's work is financially viable. The result is a consistent gap between what Nolan makes and what Indian screens can show.

What Indian fans are saying

Social media is buzzing with complaints. On platforms like X and Reddit, fans have been sharing their disappointment. Many are calling for dedicated 70mm screenings in major cities like Mumbai, Delhi, and Bengaluru.

Some fans are planning trips abroad to watch the film in its intended format. Others are simply settling for the digital version. The Indian Express review noted that while the story and performances are gripping, the visual scale is diminished on digital IMAX.

What comes next

Nolan's commitment to film is unlikely to change for his next project. Until Indian multiplex chains invest in 70mm IMAX projectors, his movies will remain incomplete experiences on Indian soil. For now, the best way to see The Odyssey in India is on the largest standard screen available — but even that will not match the director's original frame.
