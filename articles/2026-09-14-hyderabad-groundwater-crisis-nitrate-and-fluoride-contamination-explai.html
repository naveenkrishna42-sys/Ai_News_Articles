Hyderabad Groundwater Crisis: Nitrate and Fluoride Contamination Explained

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-14 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nitrate contamination in Hyderabad's groundwater is driven largely by sewage seepage and unregulated septic tank discharge, making deep borewells no safer than shallow ones.
  • Fluoride occurs naturally in Hyderabad's granite geology, but over-extraction concentrates it, pushing several zones past the 1.5 mg/L BIS permissible limit.
  • Standard RO purifiers remove 90-95% of nitrate and fluoride, but buyers must verify TDS rejection rates, membrane life and per-litre running costs before purchasing.
  • Households in affected zones should test borewell water annually and avoid using untreated groundwater for infant formula, given blue baby syndrome risk.

What the Hyderabad Groundwater Data Actually Shows

Groundwater across several Hyderabad zones carries nitrate and fluoride concentrations above Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) permissible limits, according to regional monitoring reported by The New Indian Express. The finding matters because roughly a third of households in the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) area still draw drinking water from borewells, particularly in peripheral circles where piped supply is intermittent.

Nitrate contamination above 45 mg/L and fluoride above 1.5 mg/L are the two thresholds that trigger regulatory concern under IS 10500. Both have been recorded in Hyderabad's unconfined aquifers, and both carry distinct health consequences that homeowners frequently underestimate until a family member is diagnosed.

Why Nitrate Levels Are Rising in Hyderabad's Aquifers

Sewage Seepage, Not Just Fertiliser

The common assumption is that agricultural runoff drives nitrate contamination. In Hyderabad's case, the dominant source is municipal: leaking sewer lines, unlined stormwater drains and thousands of septic tanks in the city's expanding western and northern peripheries. Nitrate is highly soluble and mobile, so it travels vertically through the soil profile into shallow aquifers within months, not decades.

Concentrations above 45 mg/L are particularly dangerous for infants under six months, where nitrate converts to nitrite and impairs haemoglobin's oxygen-carrying capacity — a condition known as blue baby syndrome or methaemoglobinaemia. Pregnant women and adults with compromised gastric acidity face elevated risk as well.

Fluoride: Geology Plus Over-Extraction

Hyderabad sits on granite and gneiss formations that naturally release fluoride into groundwater. The complication is over-extraction: as water tables fall, the remaining water has a higher dissolved mineral concentration, effectively raising fluoride levels without any new pollution source. Dental fluorosis appears first; skeletal fluorosis follows with prolonged exposure above 3-4 mg/L.

Buyer Implications: What Purifier Marketing Won't Tell You

Standard reverse osmosis membranes reject 90-95% of both nitrate and fluoride, which is why RO has become the default recommendation for borewell-dependent homes. But the economics vary sharply. A ₹9,000 RO unit with a 6-litre storage tank and 10-litre-per-hour recovery will struggle in a joint family; buyers should match capacity to household size, not to showroom discounts.

Ultrafiltration and UV purifiers do not remove dissolved nitrate or fluoride at all. They address bacteria and turbidity only. Any salesperson claiming otherwise should be asked for a third-party test certificate naming the specific contaminant and rejection percentage.

  • Pro: Domestic RO reliably cuts nitrate and fluoride to safe levels when membranes are maintained on schedule.
  • Pro: A ₹1,500 NABL test gives far more actionable data than any generic purifier claim.
  • Con: RO wastes 2-3 litres of reject water per litre purified, a real cost in water-scarce colonies.
  • Con: RO strips beneficial minerals; TDS controllers or remineralisation cartridges add ₹2,000-₹4,000 upfront.
  • Con: Cheap unverified purifiers with local membranes often deliver under 60% contaminant rejection.

Practical Action for Hyderabad Households

Test borewell water annually through an NABL-accredited laboratory, ideally before monsoon when concentrations peak. Households with infants should switch to tested packaged or municipal supply for drinking and formula preparation until results confirm nitrate below 45 mg/L. For purifier buyers, prioritise units with published rejection data, accessible service networks inside Hyderabad, and a documented AMC cost — not the lowest sticker price.

Longer term, the contamination trend points to a structural issue: Hyderabad's groundwater recharge cannot keep pace with extraction, and sewage infrastructure lags behind peripheral development. Until that gap closes, household-level treatment remains the only reliable safeguard.
