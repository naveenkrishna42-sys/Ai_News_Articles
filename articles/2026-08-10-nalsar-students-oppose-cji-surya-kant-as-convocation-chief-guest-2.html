NALSAR students oppose CJI Surya Kant as convocation chief guest

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Students at NALSAR University of Law are protesting the choice of Chief Justice Surya Kant as convocation chief guest.
  • The protest has been reported by multiple national media outlets, indicating significant student discontent.
  • The university administration has not yet issued an official response to the students' objections.

HYDERABAD: A section of outgoing students at NALSAR University of Law has objected to the institution's decision to invite Chief Justice of India Surya Kant as the chief guest for the upcoming convocation ceremony. The protest, which has gained traction among the graduating batch, has now been reported by several national media outlets, underscoring the depth of student sentiment on the matter.

The students' opposition appears to stem from concerns regarding the Chief Justice's tenure and certain judicial decisions. While the specific reasons have not been formally articulated in a collective statement, social media posts and informal student groups have amplified the dissent. The university administration has not yet responded to the students' demand for a change in the chief guest.

Student Dissent Takes Shape

The protest is not a sudden development. Students have reportedly been discussing the issue for weeks, with many feeling that inviting CJI Surya Kant sends a problematic signal at a time when questions are being raised about judicial independence and the role of the higher judiciary. Some students have also pointed to the Chief Justice's involvement in politically sensitive cases as a factor in their discomfort.

Graduating students have used internal forums and social media platforms to voice their concerns. A number of them have expressed disappointment that the university did not consult the student body before extending the invitation. The demand now is for the administration to either reconsider the choice or provide a clear rationale for it.

University's Silence Draws Criticism

So far, NALSAR's administration has maintained a studied silence. No official statement has been issued, and the Vice-Chancellor's office has not responded to queries from students or the press. This lack of communication has only intensified the frustration among the outgoing batch, with some students accusing the administration of being out of touch.

The convocation is a significant milestone for any student, and the choice of chief guest is often seen as a reflection of the institution's values. For many at NALSAR, a premier law school known for its progressive student body, the invitation to CJI Surya Kant feels like a betrayal of that ethos. The standoff has now become a talking point in legal education circles across the country.

Legal Community Weighs In

The controversy has also drawn reactions from alumni and legal professionals. Some have defended the university's decision, arguing that a Chief Justice's presence at a convocation is a matter of protocol and prestige, not an endorsement of individual judgments. Others have sided with the students, noting that institutions of higher learning must be sensitive to the views of their students.

This is not the first time a law school has faced such a situation. Similar protests have occurred at other institutions in the past, though rarely with this level of media attention. The debate touches on broader questions about the judiciary's accountability and the expectations that young legal professionals have of their future colleagues and seniors.

What Happens Next

The university will have to break its silence soon, as the convocation date is expected to be announced shortly. Students are planning to submit a formal representation to the administration, and if their demand is not met, they have hinted at a peaceful demonstration on campus. The outcome of this standoff will be closely watched by law schools across India, as it could set a precedent for how student voices are treated in such decisions.

For now, the ball is in the university's court. Whether it chooses to engage with the students' concerns or proceed with the original plan, the decision will have lasting implications for NALSAR's relationship with its own graduates.
