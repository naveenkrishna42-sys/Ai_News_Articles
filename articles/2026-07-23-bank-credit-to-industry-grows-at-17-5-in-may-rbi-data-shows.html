Bank Credit to Industry Grows at 17.5% in May, RBI Data Shows

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Industrial credit growth stood at 17.5% in May 2026, up from 12.3% a year ago.
  • Manufacturing and infrastructure sectors led the demand for loans.
  • Non-food bank credit overall rose 14.2% in the same period.

Bank credit to Indian industry expanded at a robust 17.5% in May 2026 compared to the same month last year, according to the latest data released by the Reserve Bank of India.

The growth marks a sharp acceleration from the 12.3% recorded in May 2025, signalling sustained demand for working capital and investment loans from the country's factories and infrastructure projects.

Manufacturing and Infrastructure Lead the Charge

The manufacturing sector was the primary driver, with credit growth surging past 20%. Textiles, chemicals, basic metals, and food processing units all drew higher loans, reflecting a pick-up in production and export orders.

Infrastructure lending also recorded double-digit growth, particularly for road and power projects. Banks have been actively funding the government's National Infrastructure Pipeline, which continues to funnel capital into highways, renewable energy, and urban transit.

Small and medium enterprises (SMEs) contributed to the uptick as well. Credit to the MSME segment grew by nearly 16%, helped by easier collateral norms and targeted government guarantee schemes.

Overall Credit Growth Stays Healthy

Non-food bank credit — a broader measure of lending to businesses and individuals — rose 14.2% in May. While slightly lower than the 15% seen in the previous month, it remains well above the pre-pandemic average of around 10%.

Retail loans continued to grow briskly, though at a slower pace than industrial credit. Home loans and auto loans saw steady demand, while personal loans moderated slightly as banks tightened underwriting standards.

Agriculture credit grew at a moderate 8%, as monsoons progressed and input costs stabilised.

Why This Matters

Strong industrial credit growth is a bellwether for economic activity. When companies borrow more, they typically invest in capacity, hire workers, and order raw materials — all factors that feed into GDP growth.

The RBI's monetary policy stance has remained accommodative, with the repo rate held steady at 6.25% in the June review. Banks have, in turn, passed on lower rates to corporate borrowers, making loans more attractive.

However, some analysts caution that the pace of credit growth may be tested if global demand weakens or commodity prices spike again. The central bank will watch credit quality closely as it balances inflation and growth objectives.

Deposit growth has not kept pace with credit expansion, leaving some lenders with tighter liquidity. A few banks have recently raised deposit rates to bridge the gap.

What to watch: the trajectory of industrial credit in the coming months, especially as the monsoon season and global economic trends influence business sentiment. The RBI's August monetary policy review will offer further clues on whether rates stay on hold or shift. Banks are likely to keep chasing quality corporate borrowers, even as they remain cautious on unsecured retail lending.
