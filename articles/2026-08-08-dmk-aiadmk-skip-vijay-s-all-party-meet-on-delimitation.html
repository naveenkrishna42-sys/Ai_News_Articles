DMK, AIADMK skip Vijay's all-party meet on delimitation

📅 2026-08-08 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • DMK, AIADMK, and allies boycotted the all-party meet convened by CM Vijay on delimitation.
  • Only 21 of 57 Tamil Nadu MPs attended the meeting, which rejected the Delimitation Bill.
  • AIADMK defended its absence, while the state plans to pass a resolution against delimitation.

Chennai, August 8: Tamil Nadu's political landscape witnessed a sharp divide on Saturday as the DMK, AIADMK, and their allies boycotted the all-party meeting convened by Chief Minister Vijay to discuss the contentious issue of delimitation. The meet, held in the state capital, saw attendance from only 21 of the 57 Members of Parliament representing Tamil Nadu, a turnout that underscores the deep rift among the state's political forces over the proposed Delimitation Bill.

The Chief Minister had called the meeting to forge a united front against the bill, which opposition parties argue would reduce Tamil Nadu's representation in Parliament. However, the absence of the two major Dravidian parties—traditionally fierce rivals but now united in their opposition—has raised questions about the effectiveness of the meeting.

A Divided House

The meeting was convened amid escalating tensions over the delimitation exercise, which is seen by many in the state as a threat to its political weight in the national legislature. The DMK and AIADMK, along with their respective allies, chose to stay away, citing various reasons. The AIADMK later defended its absence, stating that the party's position on delimitation was already clear and that it preferred to take its case directly to the people rather than participate in what it termed a symbolic exercise.

The DMK, on the other hand, did not issue an immediate statement, but party sources indicated that the decision to skip the meeting was a coordinated move with its allies. This rare alignment between the DMK and AIADMK, who have historically opposed each other, highlights the gravity of the delimitation issue for the state.

MPs Reject Delimitation Bill

Among the MPs who did attend, there was unanimous rejection of the Delimitation Bill. The attendees, including members from various political backgrounds, voiced strong opposition to the bill, which they argue is discriminatory and would penalize states that have successfully implemented family planning measures. The consensus at the meeting was that the bill, if passed, would significantly reduce Tamil Nadu's seats in the Lok Sabha, thereby diminishing its influence in national decision-making.

The Chief Minister, in his address, is understood to have emphasized the need for a united stand, but the absence of the major opposition parties has left the state's response fragmented. Political observers note that the boycott could be a strategic move by the DMK and AIADMK to distance themselves from any decision taken at the meeting, allowing them to criticize the outcome later.

Resolution on the Horizon

Following the all-party meeting, the Tamil Nadu government is set to pass a resolution against delimitation, a move that is expected to be tabled in the state assembly in the coming days. The resolution is likely to call upon the central government to reconsider the bill and to protect the state's interests. However, with the DMK and AIADMK having boycotted the meeting, it remains to be seen whether they will support the resolution or introduce their own motions.

The political atmosphere in the state is charged, with all parties gearing up for a prolonged battle over the issue. The delimitation debate has also sparked public protests, with several civil society groups and student organizations planning demonstrations in the coming weeks.

What Lies Ahead

As the state braces for a showdown with the Centre over delimitation, the immediate focus will be on the assembly session and the passage of the resolution. The DMK and AIADMK's next moves will be crucial, as their support—or lack thereof—will determine the strength of the state's opposition. With the bill still pending in Parliament, the coming months are likely to see heightened political activity in Tamil Nadu, as parties seek to mobilize public opinion and build pressure on the central government.

The delimitation issue has also brought to the fore questions about federalism and the balance of power between states and the Centre. For now, all eyes are on the state assembly and the political maneuvering that will follow.
