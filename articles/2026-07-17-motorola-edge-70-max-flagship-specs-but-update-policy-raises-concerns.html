Motorola Edge 70 Max: Flagship specs, but update policy raises concerns

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Motorola Edge 70 Max launched in India with flagship killer specs, big battery, and AI features.
  • Device is limited to two major Android OS upgrades and three years of security patches.
  • Critics say the update policy lags behind competitors like Samsung, OnePlus, and Google.

Motorola's latest flagship killer arrives

Motorola launched the Edge 70 Max in India on Thursday, packing top-tier specs, a large battery, and new AI features. The phone is positioned as a flagship killer, aiming to take on premium rivals at a more accessible price point.

The device features a high-refresh-rate display, a powerful chipset, and a camera system boosted by artificial intelligence. The Edge 70 Max also promises all-day battery life, a key selling point for heavy users.

Software update policy draws criticism

While the hardware impresses, the software update policy has raised eyebrows. Motorola has committed to only two major Android OS upgrades and three years of security patches for the Edge 70 Max.

This puts the phone behind competitors. Samsung offers four OS upgrades and five years of security patches for its Galaxy A and S series. OnePlus and Google also provide longer support for their devices.

Industry analysts note that for a phone marketed as a flagship killer, a longer update commitment is expected. Buyers who plan to keep the phone for more than two years may find the support window too short.

What this means for buyers

Consumers spending on a premium mid-range phone often expect it to stay secure and up-to-date for years. Motorola's policy means the Edge 70 Max will stop receiving major Android updates after 2028, and security patches will end in 2029.

This could affect resale value and long-term usability. For users who upgrade every two years, the policy may be acceptable. But for those who hold onto phones longer, it's a significant drawback.

Motorola's broader update problem

The Edge 70 Max isn't an isolated case. Motorola has historically offered shorter update windows than its competitors, even for its higher-end models. The company has not explained why it limits support, but it likely involves cost and resource allocation.

In contrast, brands like Samsung and Google have made longer support a key part of their value proposition. Google's Pixel series, for example, guarantees three OS upgrades and five years of security patches. Samsung's mid-range Galaxy A series matches that commitment.

What to watch

Motorola's update policy could become a deciding factor for savvy buyers. As more brands extend support, Motorola may face pressure to improve its offerings. For now, the Edge 70 Max is a strong hardware package held back by a weak software promise.

The phone will go on sale in India starting next week. Early reviews highlight its performance and battery life, but the update policy remains a sticking point. Whether Motorola revises its approach in future models remains to be seen.
