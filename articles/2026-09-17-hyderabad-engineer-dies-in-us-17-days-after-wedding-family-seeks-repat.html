Hyderabad Engineer Dies in US 17 Days After Wedding; Family Seeks Repatriation

Key points
  • The deceased, identified as Hussain, was a 28-year-old engineer from Hyderabad who had married on August 31 and returned to the US on September 3.
  • He was found dead in his sleep on September 17 at his home in the US; the cause of death remains unknown pending autopsy and toxicology reports.
  • The family has written to External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar and the Indian consulate seeking urgent help to repatriate the body.

A 28-year-old engineer from Hyderabad was found dead in his US home on September 17, just 17 days after his wedding, according to family members and police. The man, identified as Hussain, had married on August 31 and returned to the US on September 3 to resume work. His sudden death has left his family in shock and prompted an appeal to External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar for help in bringing his body back to India.

Who Was Hussain?

Hussain, a resident of Hyderabad's Old City, worked as a software engineer in the US. He had come to India for his wedding and returned to the US on September 3. According to a friend, he died in his sleep. The family was informed by his roommates. Hussain's father, a retired government employee, said his son was healthy and had no known medical conditions.

Circumstances of Death

Hussain was found dead in his apartment in the US on September 17. The local police have launched an investigation, and an autopsy is awaited to determine the cause of death. The family has not been given any official information about the circumstances. A friend told reporters that Hussain had spoken to his wife on September 16 and seemed fine.

Family's Plea for Repatriation

The family has written to External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar and the Indian consulate in the US, seeking urgent assistance to repatriate Hussain's body. The process can take weeks and cost lakhs of rupees. The family has also requested a probe into the death. "We want to know how he died. We want his body back," said Hussain's father.

Challenges for Indian Families

  • Financial Burden: Repatriation costs can exceed INR 5 lakh, a significant sum for middle-class families.
  • Bureaucratic Hurdles: Coordinating between US authorities, Indian consulate, and airlines is complex.
  • Emotional Toll: Families often struggle to get timely information and face delays in receiving the body.
  • Legal Complexity: If foul play is suspected, investigations can prolong the process.

What Can Be Done?

Families should immediately contact the Indian consulate and file a police report. They can also seek help from local Indian associations. The Ministry of External Affairs has a dedicated portal for repatriation requests. In cases of suspicious death, families should insist on a thorough investigation and keep all communication in writing.

Conclusion

Hussain's death is a tragic reminder of the vulnerabilities faced by Indian workers abroad. While the investigation continues, his family's plea underscores the need for streamlined repatriation procedures and support systems for families in distress.
