Beyond Charminar: 7 new experiences to try in Hyderabad

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hyderabad's offbeat experiences are gaining popularity beyond traditional landmarks.
  • New culinary and cultural activities offer fresh ways to explore the city.
  • Local entrepreneurs are driving the trend with innovative concepts.
  • These experiences cater to both residents and tourists seeking authenticity.

Hyderabad has never been short of iconic sights — the Charminar, Golconda Fort, and the Hussain Sagar lake draw crowds year-round. But beyond these familiar landmarks, a quieter revolution is unfolding. A new wave of experiences is reshaping how people engage with the city, moving past the usual tourist checklist.

From underground supper clubs to heritage walks with a twist, these activities are finding favour with locals and visitors alike. Telangana Today takes a look at seven trending experiences that are putting a fresh spin on Hyderabad's cultural and culinary scene.

Food trails that go beyond biryani

Hyderabad's culinary reputation rests on its biryani, but a new breed of food tours is digging deeper. Organised walks now take participants through old city lanes to sample lesser-known dishes — from rare kebabs to traditional sweets that have been made by the same families for generations.

These trails are not just about eating. They come with stories of the city's history, its communities, and the recipes that have survived centuries. For many, it is a way to taste the city's soul, one bite at a time.

Immersive art and craft workshops

In a city known for its pearls and bangles, craft workshops are gaining traction. Visitors can now try their hand at bangle-making, bidriware, or even kalamkari painting under the guidance of local artisans.

These sessions are designed to be hands-on, allowing participants to create their own souvenirs. The focus is on process, not just product, and the result is a deeper appreciation for the skills that have defined Hyderabad's crafts for centuries.

Rooftop experiences with a view

Several new venues have turned the city's skyline into a backdrop for dining and socialising. Rooftop cafes and lounges, many in the older parts of the city, offer views that juxtapose minarets with modern high-rises.

These spaces are not just about the view, though. They host curated events — from open-mic nights to classical music performances — creating a blend of old and new that is distinctly Hyderabad.

Heritage walks with a modern twist

Heritage walks are nothing new, but the latest versions are anything but conventional. Some trails now incorporate augmented reality, overlaying historical images onto current scenes. Others focus on themes like the city's cinema history or its lesser-known monuments.

These walks are led by young historians and storytellers who bring a fresh energy to the city's past. They are shorter, more interactive, and designed to fit into a busy day, making them accessible to a wider audience.

Secret supper clubs and pop-ups

A growing community of home chefs is hosting secret supper clubs, where diners gather at undisclosed locations for multi-course meals. The menus are often experimental, blending Hyderabadi flavours with global techniques.

These events are announced through social media and word of mouth, adding an element of exclusivity. For many attendees, the appeal lies in the intimacy — small groups, direct conversation with the chef, and a meal that feels personal.

Cycling and nature trails

For those who prefer the outdoors, cycling groups are organising regular rides through Hyderabad's greener pockets. Routes cover the Osman Sagar lake, the Durgam Cheruvu area, and the city's peri-urban fringes.

These rides are leisurely, often timed for early mornings, and end with breakfast at a local eatery. They offer a way to see the city's natural side while meeting like-minded people.

Wellness and mindfulness retreats

Finally, wellness is making its mark. Day-long retreats on the city's outskirts combine yoga, meditation, and local organic food. Some are held in heritage bungalows, others in farmhouses.

These retreats are deliberately low-key, with small groups and a focus on disconnecting. They are a response to the city's fast pace, offering a pause that many find restorative.

As these experiences grow in popularity, more entrepreneurs are likely to enter the space. The coming months could see even more innovation, as Hyderabad's creative class finds new ways to celebrate the city's rich past and vibrant present.
