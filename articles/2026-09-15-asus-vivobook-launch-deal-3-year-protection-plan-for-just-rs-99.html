Deal & Pricing Analysis

Asus India has rolled out a launch-period protection offer for its new Vivobook lineup, and the headline number is hard to ignore: Rs. 99 for a 2-year warranty extension that stacks on top of the standard one-year coverage, giving buyers three years of total protection. The offer is tied to the launch window of the new Vivobook models, with the launch date set for 16th September and the promotion running through 20th September 2026. In practice, that means the add-on must be purchased alongside a qualifying new Vivobook, not as a standalone product.

To judge the value, compare it with the open market. Third-party extended warranty plans for mainstream laptops typically cost between Rs. 3,000 and Rs. 8,000 for two additional years, depending on the machine's price and coverage terms. Even Asus's own standard extended warranty packages usually run into the low thousands. At Rs. 99, this is effectively a promotional giveaway designed to reduce purchase hesitation during the launch period. The catch is that the value is only realized if you were already planning to buy a Vivobook; the Rs. 99 is not a discount on the laptop itself, and the protection plan does not transfer to a different brand or model.

Real-World Performance & Daily Use

Vivobook laptops are positioned as everyday machines, and that is exactly how they behave. The latest models handle document work, browser-heavy multitasking, video calls, and streaming without drama. The 16:10 displays on newer SKUs give noticeably more vertical workspace than older 16:9 panels, which matters for spreadsheets and long documents. Keyboards are comfortable for extended typing, and the larger 70Wh batteries in the mid-tier models can stretch to a full working day if you keep brightness moderate and avoid sustained heavy loads.

Build quality is where the Vivobook line has historically shown its price point. Entry configurations use more plastic, while higher trims add aluminium lids and MIL-STD-810H durability testing. None of these are workstation-class machines, and sustained CPU-heavy tasks such as video export or gaming will cause fan noise and thermal throttling. That is precisely why the extended warranty matters: hinges, charging ports, and battery degradation are the most common failure points over a three-year ownership period, and covering them for Rs. 99 removes a meaningful long-term cost risk.

Competition & Market Alternatives

The obvious rivals in this segment are the HP Pavilion and Lenovo IdeaPad lines. HP frequently bundles its own extended warranty promotions, but the add-on cost is usually higher and the coverage window shorter. Lenovo's IdeaPad range often matches Vivobook on specifications and occasionally undercuts it on price, but its standard warranty extension pricing is similarly in the thousands of rupees. Where Asus pulls ahead with this offer is the sheer cost of the extension: no mainstream competitor is currently matching a two-year extension at Rs. 99. If you are comparing purely on hardware, the three brands trade blows on processor, RAM, and display quality. If you are comparing on total cost of ownership over three years, the Asus launch offer tilts the math in its favour.

Who Should Buy This Deal

This offer is aimed squarely at buyers who have already decided on a Vivobook and are purchasing during the launch window. Students, home-office users, and first-time laptop buyers who plan to keep the machine for three or more years will get the most out of it, since the extension covers the period when wear-related failures typically appear. Buyers who upgrade every 18 months, or who need dedicated GPU performance for gaming or professional creative work, should look elsewhere; the Vivobook is not built for those workloads, and the warranty add-on does not change that. The key action point is timing: the offer runs through 20th September 2026, and the Rs. 99 extension must be added at the point of purchase, so confirm eligibility with the seller before completing the order.
