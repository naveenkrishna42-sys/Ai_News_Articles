Delhi High Court Allows AJL To Pursue Property Release In National Herald Case

The Delhi High Court has declined to pass any restraint order preventing Associated Journals Limited (AJL) from seeking the release of properties attached by the Enforcement Directorate (ED) in connection with the National Herald case. The court's decision came while hearing petitions challenging the property attachments carried out under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA).

Background Of The National Herald Case

The National Herald case stems from allegations that AJL, the publisher of the now-defunct National Herald newspaper, received properties worth crores of rupees from the Young Indian entity between 2010 and 2014. The ED attached several properties alleging that they were proceeds of crime and linked to a money laundering syndicate. Subramanian Swamy, who had earlier filed a criminal defamation complaint against Sonia Gandhi and Rahul Gandhi in the case, later approached the courts seeking action.

Court Declines Status Quo Plea From ED

During the hearing, the ED requested the court to maintain status quo regarding AJL's right to file applications for the release of attached properties. However, the Delhi High Court refused to grant such a restraint, observing that AJL, as the original property owner, should not be prevented from invoking its statutory rights under Section 45 of the PMLA to approach the Adjudicating Authority for release of properties.

Implications For Ongoing Legal Proceedings

The ruling effectively keeps the legal pathway open for AJL to pursue the release of attached properties through appropriate constitutional and statutory mechanisms. The properties in question include commercial plots and residential premises located in Delhi and other parts of the country, with the ED claiming that they were transferred at undervalued rates to entities linked with AJL. The decision also sets a precedent regarding the balance between investigative agencies and property owners during the pendency of attachment proceedings.

Legal Provisions And Precedents Involved

Under Section 45 of the PMLA, a person whose property has been attached may file an application before the Adjudicating Authority for release, subject to conditions. The Supreme Court in Unitech Ltd v. Unreal Foundation Pvt Ltd (2020) had earlier emphasized that the right to seek release cannot be taken away merely because an investigation is ongoing. This judgment reinforces that principle by refusing to restrain AJL from pursuing its remedy.

  • Status Quo Denied: ED failed to convince the court to restrict AJL from filing release applications.
  • Statutory Right Upheld: AJL retains its right under Section 45 of the PMLA to approach the Adjudicating Authority.
  • Properties Still Attached: Multiple commercial and residential properties remain provisionally attached pending final outcome.
  • Legal Precedent Strengthened: Judgment aligns with prior rulings safeguarding property owners' rights during PMLA proceedings.
