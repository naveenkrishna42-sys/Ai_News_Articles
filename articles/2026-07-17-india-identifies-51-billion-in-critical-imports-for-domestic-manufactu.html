India identifies $51 billion in critical imports for domestic manufacturing push

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • India targets $51 billion in imports for domestic production under a new manufacturing push.
  • Over 100 products, including electronics and chemicals, are identified for local substitution.
  • The move aims to reduce supply chain vulnerabilities and shield the economy from external shocks.

New Delhi identifies $51 billion import hit list

The Indian government has drawn up a list of over 100 imported products worth $51 billion that it wants to manufacture locally, sources familiar with the matter told Reuters. The initiative is part of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's broader push to cut import reliance and protect the economy from global supply chain disruptions.

The identified items span sectors including electronics, chemicals, machinery, and pharmaceuticals. Officials believe domestic production of these goods could significantly reduce India's import bill and strengthen its manufacturing base.

Reducing dependence on China

A key driver behind the move is the desire to curb dependence on Chinese imports. Many of the products on the list are currently sourced primarily from China, making India vulnerable to geopolitical tensions and supply shocks. The government has been working on a strategy to gradually replace these imports with Made-in-India alternatives.

The plan aligns with the production-linked incentive (PLI) schemes already in place for sectors like electronics, automobiles, and textiles. However, this new push goes beyond existing incentives, focusing on specific products that are deemed critical for national security and economic stability.

Government's strategy and industry response

The selection process involved consultations with industry bodies and ministries. The government evaluated factors like domestic capability, technology availability, and potential for job creation. Sources said the final list includes items where India already has some manufacturing capacity but is not yet competitive enough to replace imports entirely.

Industry experts have welcomed the move but caution that implementation will be key. "The government must ensure that domestic manufacturers can match the quality and cost of imported goods," said a senior executive at an electronics manufacturing association. "Without that, the push could end up raising costs for downstream industries."

Challenges in execution

The government faces several hurdles. Many of the identified products require advanced technology and high capital investment. Domestic companies may need time to build capacity and achieve economies of scale. Additionally, trade agreements and global supply chain linkages could complicate efforts to completely replace imports.

Officials have indicated that the government may offer additional incentives, such as tax breaks and easier access to credit, to encourage local manufacturing. The plan is expected to be rolled out in phases over the next three to five years.

Impact on consumers and businesses

If successful, the initiative could reduce India's trade deficit and create millions of jobs. However, in the short term, consumers and businesses may face higher prices as domestic production scales up. The government is betting that the long-term benefits of self-reliance will outweigh these initial costs.

The $51 billion figure represents a significant portion of India's total imports, which stood at over $600 billion in the last financial year. Reducing even a fraction of this would be a major achievement for the Modi government's Atmanirbhar Bharat (Self-Reliant India) campaign.

The government is expected to release the full list of products in the coming weeks, along with a roadmap for implementation. Industry stakeholders will be watching closely to see which sectors get priority and what support the government provides.
