SBI Funds IPO Listing: History of Mega IPOs Shows Mixed Debut Returns

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • SBI Funds Management IPO closes, listing likely July 21.
  • Last 12 billion-dollar IPOs saw average listing gain of 7%.
  • 4 of those 12 IPOs listed with negative returns.
  • Analysts divided on SBI Funds' prospects given current market valuations.

The Rs 9,813 crore initial public offering of SBI Funds Management Limited — the country's largest asset manager — has concluded, and all eyes are now on its stock market debut, expected on July 21. The IPO, which opened for subscription last week, was met with robust demand from institutional investors. But retail investors, nursing memories of recent volatile listings, are asking a sharper question: what does history tell us about how this mega issue will perform on day one?

The answer, based on the track record of the last twelve IPOs that raised over Rs 7,500 crore (roughly $1 billion), is not straightforward. The average listing gain for these blockbuster issues stands at a modest 7%. Four of them — a full third — actually listed below their issue price.

The Billion-Dollar Club: A Mixed Bag

Between 2023 and mid-2026, twelve Indian companies raised over a billion dollars each from the public market. The cohort includes household names like Tata Technologies, Bajaj Housing Finance, and Hyundai Motor India. Their listing-day performance varied wildly.

Tata Technologies, for instance, delivered a stellar 140% pop on debut in late 2023, riding a wave of enthusiasm for engineering and digital services. Bajaj Housing Finance, listed in September 2024, also rewarded investors with a 20% gain. But others told a different story. Hyundai Motor India, which raised the largest-ever IPO in the country at Rs 27,870 crore, listed at a 1.5% discount to its issue price in June 2025. The stock has since recovered but remains a cautionary tale for those expecting automatic gains from size alone.

What Determines a Mega IPO's Debut

Analysts point to three factors that separate the winners from the losers in this club. The first is pricing. IPOs that leave some money on the table for investors tend to list higher. The second is sector sentiment. Asset management companies (AMCs) have been in favour, with HDFC AMC and Nippon Life India AMC trading at rich valuations.

The third factor is the broader market mood at the time of listing. The Nifty 50 has been volatile in recent weeks, oscillating between 24,000 and 25,000. A weak close on listing day could dampen gains.

"The SBI Funds IPO is priced at the higher end of the band. It leaves very little margin for error," said a Mumbai-based fund manager who did not wish to be named. "The AMC business has strong fundamentals — high margins, recurring income — but the valuation needs to be justified by performance."

Seven More IPOs Lined Up Next Week

The primary market remains active. At least seven other IPOs, across various sizes, are scheduled to hit Dalal Street in the coming week. Five listings are also lined up. This flood of supply could divert attention and liquidity away from any single stock.

For SBI Funds, the key number to watch is the grey market premium (GMP), an unofficial indicator of demand. In the days ahead of the listing, GMP has hovered in a narrow range, suggesting muted optimism rather than euphoria.

Whatever the outcome on July 21, the pattern from the last dozen mega IPOs is clear: big size does not guarantee a big debut. It guarantees scrutiny.

The listing will set the tone for other large IPOs slated for the remainder of 2026, including a potential offering from a state-owned energy giant. Market participants will watch closely to see if SBI Funds Management can break the pattern — or reinforce it.
