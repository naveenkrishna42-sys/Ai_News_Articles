Residents seek BHS status for Agaramthen sacred grove

Residents of Agaramthen, a small village near Chennai, have launched a campaign to secure Biosphere Reserve (BHS) status for the Agaramthen sacred grove. The grove, spanning over 100 acres, is home to a diverse range of flora and fauna, and is considered a significant cultural and ecological site.

Ecological and cultural significance of the grove

The Agaramthen sacred grove is not just a natural habitat but also a cultural and spiritual site. It is home to several endangered species, including the Indian peacock, the Indian pangolin, and various species of birds. The grove is also a significant site for the local community, who believe it to be a sacred place.

The grove's ecological significance is further underscored by its role in maintaining the region's water cycle. It acts as a natural water reservoir, providing water to the surrounding areas during the dry season.

Challenges in obtaining BHS status

The process of obtaining BHS status is complex and time-consuming. The Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) requires a detailed project report (DPR) and a management plan for the grove. The residents have been working on these documents, but the process has been slow and bureaucratic.

The residents also face challenges in terms of public awareness and support. Many people are not aware of the grove's significance, and there is a lack of political will to support the cause.

Broader impact and expert opinion

The Agaramthen sacred grove is a unique and valuable ecological and cultural site. Its protection and conservation are not just important for the local community but also for the larger ecosystem. The grove's role in maintaining the region's water cycle is crucial, especially in the context of climate change and water scarcity.

Environmental experts have welcomed the residents' efforts to secure BHS status for the grove. They believe that the grove's protection will not only benefit the local community but also contribute to the larger goal of biodiversity conservation.
