H-1B Visa $100,000 Fee Extended to 2027: What Indian Engineers Must Know

Key points

  • Employers with recent layoffs face closer scrutiny, effectively freezing fresh Indian hiring at cost-cutting US firms.
  • Indian IT services firms with high visa dependency face margin pressure, pushing more work to offshore centres in India.

H-1B Fee Extension: The Core Announcement

US President Donald Trump has extended the $100,000 H-1B visa fee for another year, pushing the restriction's validity till September 2027. The order also directs tighter scrutiny of employers that have carried out layoffs in recent months. For Indian engineers, technicians and IT services companies, this is not a one-time shock but a prolonged cost barrier that reshapes hiring economics for at least two more application cycles.

The fee is levied on new H-1B petitions filed by employers, not on individual applicants. That distinction matters enormously. A fresh graduate in Bengaluru eyeing a US role is affected; a professional already working in Texas on a valid H-1B is not, unless their employer files a fresh petition under specific conditions.

What the $100,000 Fee Actually Covers

Reports indicate the fee applies to new H-1B petitions, including those filed for candidates outside the US. Renewals and most transfers within the same category have been treated separately under the order's framework. Employers must also demonstrate that they have not displaced US workers, a clause that triggers deeper audits for companies with recent layoffs.

Impact on Indian Engineers and Technicians

1. Fresh Graduates and Early-Career Professionals

The hardest hit group is freshers and those with under five years of experience. Employers rarely justify USD 100,000 plus legal costs for a candidate without a proven US track record. Expect fewer entry-level offers, longer hiring pipelines, and a shift toward hiring locally in the US or remote from India.

2. Mid-Career and Senior Talent

Experienced engineers in AI, cloud, cybersecurity and semiconductor roles remain in demand. Companies will absorb the fee only where the skill premium is high. If your profile is replaceable by a US-based hire, your petition is unlikely to move forward. Specialisation is now the strongest visa strategy.

3. Indian IT Services Firms

Companies with heavy visa dependency face direct margin pressure. The logical response is more offshore delivery from Indian centres, which protects revenue but reduces on-site roles. Mid-tier IT firms with thinner margins will feel this more than large-cap players with existing US local hiring infrastructure.

Pros and Cons for Indian Professionals

  • Pro: Existing H-1B holders and renewal cases remain largely insulated from the new fee.
  • Pro: Demand for highly specialised talent still justifies employer spending.
  • Pro: Offshore and remote hiring from India could expand as firms cut on-site costs.
  • Con: Fresh petitions become prohibitively expensive for small and mid-size employers.
  • Con: Employers with layoffs face audits that delay or derail Indian hiring.
  • Con: The September 2027 timeline means uncertainty extends across at least two lottery cycles.

Buyer Guide: What This Means in Practical Terms

If you are planning a US move, treat the H-1B route as a premium, employer-funded channel rather than a default path. Compare alternatives: L-1 transfers within the same company, O-1 for exceptional talent, and Canada or Europe as stepping stones. For IT firms, the calculation is straightforward: USD 100,000 per new visa is roughly Rs 83-84 lakh, comparable to two to three years of an offshore engineer's fully loaded cost in India. That arithmetic will push more work to Indian delivery centres rather than US desks.

Negotiate clarity on who bears the fee before accepting any US offer. Verify your employer's layoff history, as it directly affects scrutiny risk. And build skills that are genuinely scarce in the US market, because the fee extension has made mediocrity expensive for everyone involved.
