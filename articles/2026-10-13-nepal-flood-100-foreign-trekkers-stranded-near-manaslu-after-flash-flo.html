Nepal Flood: 100+ Foreign Trekkers Stranded Near Manaslu After Flash Flood

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-10-13 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 100 foreign trekkers are stranded along the Budhi Gandaki valley in Gorkha after a flash flood destroyed trail bridges during peak autumn trekking season.
  • The Times of India reports a massive rock avalanche as the likely trigger, echoing Himalayan disasters such as Sikkim's 2023 South Lhonak outburst.
  • Five people are missing and hydropower projects on the river are damaged, raising repair costs and schedule risks for Nepal's power sector.
  • Bihar's Deputy CM expects no major impact at the state's Gandak barrage, easing immediate downstream flood fears in India.

What Officials Have Confirmed So Far

More than 100 foreign trekkers are stranded in a flood-hit region of northern Nepal, according to an official cited by Reuters. The Hindu reports that a flash flood surging down the Budhi Gandaki River swept through Gorkha district, forcing the evacuation of riverside villages. Telegraph India adds that five people are missing, with bridges and hydropower projects on the river damaged.

Timing explains the scale of the crisis: mid-October is the peak autumn trekking window, when post-monsoon skies draw the year's heaviest foreign footfall to routes such as the Manaslu Circuit. A single flood event in that corridor can therefore isolate large numbers of foreigners at once.

Why a Rock Avalanche Produces a Violent Flood Surge

The Times of India's reporting points to a massive rock avalanche as the probable cause, a failure mode Himalayan hydrologists have documented repeatedly. When a huge rock mass crashes into a steep river channel or a high-altitude lake, it displaces a wall of water and debris that carves downstream with immense force, collecting boulders, timber and silt along the way.

The closest recent parallel is the October 2023 South Lhonak Lake outburst in Sikkim, which destroyed the 1,200 MW Teesta III dam at Chungthang and killed dozens. The physics here are identical: sudden onset, minimal warning time for anyone in the valley, and destruction concentrated at bridges and riverbank infrastructure.

Implications for trekkers on the Manaslu route

The Manaslu Circuit follows the Budhi Gandaki gorge from Jagat upward through Philim, Deng and Namrung to Samagaun, using a chain of suspension and steel bridges to cross the river. Washed-out crossings strand groups on opposite banks even when nobody is injured, which is the most likely explanation for how so many trekkers were left stranded safely.

Licensed agencies and Nepali rescue services typically respond with helicopter lifts, though weather and landing sites dictate the pace of evacuation. Trekkers still moving in the valley should stay with their agency group, refuse any river-crossing attempt and track advisories from Nepal's tourism authorities and trekking associations.

Hydropower Damage and the Bihar Barrage Watch

Damage to hydropower projects in Gorkha carries real economic weight. The Budhi Gandaki basin hosts several schemes, including the long-planned 1,200 MW Budhi Gandaki storage project, and flood-borne silt routinely forces repairs costing crores of rupees while delaying commissioning schedules.

On the Indian side, Bihar's Deputy CM has said no major impact is expected at the state's barrage on the Gandak system at Valmikinagar. That assessment fits how such events unfold: as a surge travels hundreds of kilometres through Nepal's river network, peak discharge flattens sharply. Flood-monitoring agencies will still track gauge readings over the coming days.

What Happens Next for Rescues and Travel Plans

Immediate priorities are helicopter evacuation of the stranded trekkers and accounting for the five missing people. Satellite and drone surveys usually follow such events to pinpoint the avalanche source and check whether upstream slopes or lakes remain unstable.

Travellers planning Manaslu-region trips in coming weeks should expect rerouted itineraries, possible trail closures and stricter guide check-ins while damage assessments continue. For downstream Bihar, the immediate alarm has been played down by the state government; the longer test is rebuilding bridges and hydropower capacity in Gorkha before winter restricts access.
