BCCI's hunt for new SSM head hits roadblock after multiple candidates withdraw

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Multiple candidates have withdrawn from the race to head BCCI's SSM unit.
  • The search process has stalled, with no clear timeline for a new appointment.
  • The development comes amid scrutiny of the Centre of Excellence and injury management.
  • BCCI officials have not commented on the reasons behind the withdrawals.

The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) is facing a fresh hurdle in its efforts to revamp its medical and sports science setup. The search for a new head of the Sports Science and Medicine (SSM) unit has stalled after multiple candidates withdrew from the race, according to sources familiar with the matter.

The development adds to the growing scrutiny of the BCCI's Centre of Excellence (CoE) in Bengaluru, which has come under the spotlight following a spate of injuries to key players, including Jasprit Bumrah and Sai Sudharsan. The board had been looking to fill the top SSM position as part of a broader restructuring, but the process has now hit a wall.

Why candidates are pulling out

While the BCCI has not officially commented on the reasons, insiders suggest that the role's demanding scope and the high-stakes environment have given potential appointees pause. The SSM head is expected to oversee medical protocols, fitness regimes, and rehabilitation programmes across the national team and the CoE, a role that has become increasingly complex in the modern game.

Sources indicate that some candidates were uncomfortable with the level of autonomy granted, while others cited concerns over the board's decision-making structure. The withdrawals have forced the BCCI to go back to the drawing board, with no immediate timeline for a fresh appointment.

CoE under the microscope

The stalled search comes at an awkward time for the CoE, which has been fending off criticism over its handling of player injuries. VVS Laxman, the head of the CoE, has defended the centre's protocols, insisting there is no miscommunication between the BCCI CoE and the Indian team management. His remarks followed reports of a rift over the management of Bumrah's back issues and Sudharsan's recent setback.

The injury crisis has prompted BCCI secretary Devajit Saikia to schedule a meeting with Laxman to review the situation. The talks are expected to focus on the CoE's workload management strategies and the rising number of players reporting for national duty with pre-existing conditions.

IPL workload and the financial squeeze

A broader debate has also emerged over the role of the Indian Premier League (IPL) in the injury epidemic. Critics point out that players often push through pain during the franchise tournament due to contractual and financial pressures, only to be handed over to the national setup in a compromised state. The Economic Times recently highlighted how players are "dumped onto unsuspecting" national medical teams after the IPL, raising questions about the lack of coordination between franchises and the BCCI's medical staff.

This has intensified calls for a more centralised approach to player fitness, with the SSM head expected to act as a bridge between the IPL franchises and the national team. The absence of a permanent appointee, however, leaves a critical gap in that effort.

What lies ahead

The BCCI is likely to re-open the application process in the coming weeks, possibly widening its net to include international candidates. Saikia's meeting with Laxman could also yield interim measures to stabilise the CoE's operations in the meantime.

For now, the board faces a delicate balancing act: reassuring stakeholders of its commitment to player welfare while ensuring the next SSM head has the backing and clarity needed to make a real difference. The coming weeks will reveal whether the BCCI can break the impasse or if the search will drag on further, casting a longer shadow over its injury management record.
