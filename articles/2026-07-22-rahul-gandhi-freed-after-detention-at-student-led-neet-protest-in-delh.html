Rahul Gandhi freed after detention at student-led NEET protest in Delhi

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rahul Gandhi detained and later released after joining student protests in Delhi over NEET exam irregularities.
  • Union Minister Rajnath Singh accused the opposition of using student protests as a political tool.
  • Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan broke silence, facing calls for resignation over the NEET row.

Congress leader Rahul Gandhi was freed on Wednesday after being briefly detained by Delhi Police while participating in a student-led protest over alleged irregularities in the National Eligibility-cum-Entrance Test (NEET).

The protest, which drew hundreds of students and opposition supporters, centred on demands for a probe into reported question paper leaks and marking discrepancies in the medical entrance exam. Gandhi was taken into custody near Jantar Mantar but was released within hours, police sources confirmed.

Opposition joins students in black-clad protest

Earlier in the day, Gandhi and Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav led a group of opposition MPs dressed in black inside Parliament to show solidarity with the protesting students. The lawmakers demanded a parliamentary debate and a joint investigation into the NEET controversy.

Speaking to reporters after his release, Gandhi said, “I am used to getting punched. I am ready to take many more.” His remarks referenced past physical confrontations during political agitations and underscored his determination to back student demands.

Government hits back

Union Home Minister Rajnath Singh dismissed the opposition's move as opportunistic. “The opposition is using students' protests as a political tool to serve their own interests,” Singh told reporters in New Delhi. He added that the government had already ordered an inquiry into the exam irregularities and urged the opposition to wait for the findings.

The protest came amid mounting pressure on Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, who broke his silence on the row with a statement on social media. “We owe our students more than outrage. The government is committed to transparency and fairness in examinations,” Pradhan wrote, without directly addressing calls for his resignation that have grown louder within opposition ranks and among student groups.

NEET irregularities spark wider debate

The NEET exam, conducted by the National Testing Agency (NTA), is the gateway for admission to medical and dental colleges across India. Reports of question paper leaks and suspicious score jumps in certain states had already triggered protests in several cities before Wednesday's Delhi demonstration.

Student leaders have alleged that the NTA failed to ensure standardised testing conditions. They have demanded a Supreme Court-monitored probe and the release of detailed score data. The government has so far resisted these demands, maintaining that the NTA's internal review will address the concerns.

Opposition parties, including the Congress, the Samajwadi Party, and the Trinamool Congress, have sought to corner the government on the issue, framing it as a symptom of a broader breakdown in the public education system.

As the political slugfest intensifies, students have announced plans for larger rallies in the coming days. All eyes are now on the Supreme Court, which is expected to hear a batch of petitions related to the NEET irregularities later this week.
