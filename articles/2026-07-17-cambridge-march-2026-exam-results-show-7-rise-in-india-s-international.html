Cambridge March 2026 exam results show 7% rise in India’s international education demand

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cambridge International March 2026 exam entries from India rose 7% year-on-year.
  • Mathematics, science, and English remain the most popular subjects among Indian students.
  • The trend indicates rising preference for globally recognised qualifications and study abroad aspirations.

India sees 7% spike in Cambridge exam entries for March 2026

Cambridge International has reported a 7% increase in exam entries from India for the March 2026 series. The data, released on Tuesday, shows that over 1.2 lakh students from across the country sat for Cambridge IGCSE, O Level, and A Level examinations this cycle.

This marks the third consecutive year of growth in Indian registrations for Cambridge qualifications. Officials attribute the rise to a growing middle class that values international curricula and the perceived edge these credentials offer in university admissions abroad.

Mathematics and sciences dominate subject choices

Among the subjects, Mathematics remains the most opted-for, followed by Physics, Chemistry, and Biology. English as a Second Language and Economics also saw significant uptake. The popularity of STEM subjects aligns with national priorities and the global demand for skills in technology and engineering.

Schools in Maharashtra, Delhi-NCR, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu accounted for the bulk of entries. Many institutions now offer Cambridge programmes alongside CBSE or state board options, giving students flexibility in academic pathways.

Why Indian students are turning to Cambridge exams

Education experts point to several drivers. First, Cambridge qualifications are widely recognised by universities in the US, UK, Canada, and Australia. Second, the curriculum emphasises critical thinking and application-based learning, which students and parents find attractive.

Third, the number of international schools and private institutions offering Cambridge programmes has grown. In cities like Pune, Bengaluru, and Hyderabad, new campuses have opened, catering specifically to families seeking global education standards.

Impact on study abroad aspirations

The 7% rise mirrors a broader shift. Indian students increasingly view Cambridge A Levels as a stepping stone to top global universities. Admissions consultants note that a strong performance in these exams can strengthen applications, especially for competitive courses in medicine, engineering, and business.

At the same time, domestic universities like Ashoka, Jio Institute, and some IITs have started recognising Cambridge qualifications for direct entry. This dual acceptance — abroad and at home — adds to the appeal.

Cambridge International has also expanded its digital assessment options and support for schools, making it easier for institutions to offer the programme. The board plans to increase its presence in tier-2 and tier-3 cities, where demand is rising but supply of international curricula remains limited.

Officials have not yet released subject-wise breakdowns or performance data for the March 2026 series. That data is expected in the coming weeks.

What to watch: Whether the upward trend continues in the October 2026 series, and how universities in India and abroad adjust their admission criteria to accommodate the growing pool of Cambridge-qualified applicants.
