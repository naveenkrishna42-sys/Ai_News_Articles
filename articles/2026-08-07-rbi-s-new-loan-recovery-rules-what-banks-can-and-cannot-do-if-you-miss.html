RBI's new loan recovery rules: What banks can and cannot do if you miss an EMI

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has introduced new rules governing loan recovery practices for banks and NBFCs.
  • The guidelines clarify permissible actions, including penal charges and recovery agent conduct.
  • Borrowers will now have clearer recourse against unfair recovery tactics.

New framework for loan recovery

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has come out with a revised set of rules that spell out exactly what lenders can and cannot do when a borrower misses an EMI. The move is aimed at bringing more transparency and fairness to the loan recovery process, which has often been a grey area for both banks and customers.

The new guidelines, issued recently, apply to all commercial banks, including regional rural banks, as well as non-banking financial companies (NBFCs). They consolidate the existing instructions into a single framework, making it easier for borrowers to understand their rights and for lenders to follow a uniform code of conduct.

What lenders can do

Under the new rules, banks and NBFCs are allowed to levy penal charges if a borrower fails to pay an instalment on time. However, these charges must be reasonable and clearly disclosed at the time of loan sanction. The RBI has made it clear that penal charges are not a revenue-making tool but a deterrent against delayed payments.

Lenders can also initiate recovery proceedings, but only after following a defined process. This includes sending written notices to the borrower, giving them adequate time to respond, and considering any genuine hardship before taking further steps. The rules also permit lenders to engage recovery agents, but with strict conditions attached.

What lenders cannot do

The new framework explicitly prohibits recovery agents from using coercive tactics, harassment, or any form of intimidation. Agents cannot visit borrowers at odd hours, use abusive language, or make threatening phone calls. The RBI has also barred lenders from disclosing a borrower's default status to third parties, including employers or neighbours, except as required by law.

Another key restriction is on the use of penal charges. Lenders cannot compound penal charges, meaning they cannot charge interest on the penalty amount. Also, if a borrower repays the overdue amount, the penal charges must be reversed, and the loan account should be regularised without any further penalty.

Borrower's rights and grievance redressal

For the first time, the RBI has laid down a clear escalation mechanism for borrowers who feel they have been unfairly treated. Every lender must have an internal ombudsman or a dedicated grievance redressal cell to handle complaints about recovery practices. If the borrower is not satisfied with the lender's response, they can approach the RBI's integrated ombudsman scheme.

Borrowers also have the right to request a copy of their loan agreement and any communication related to recovery. Lenders are required to maintain a detailed log of all recovery actions taken, which can be reviewed by the RBI during inspections.

What this means for you

For the average borrower, these rules are a significant step towards levelling the playing field. If you are facing temporary financial stress and have missed an EMI, the new guidelines give you a clearer path to negotiate with your lender without fear of harassment.

The RBI has asked all lenders to update their internal policies and ensure that their recovery agents are trained on the new code of conduct. Banks have been given a short window to comply, and the rules will come into effect from the next financial year.

As these rules take shape, borrowers should familiarise themselves with the key provisions, especially the ones about penal charges and grievance redressal. Keeping a record of all communication with your lender is a good habit, as it could be useful if you need to escalate a complaint. The onus is now on lenders to prove that they are following the letter of the law, and borrowers have a stronger hand than ever before.
