Bengaluru

FIR Filed Against Illegal Posters Featuring Minister Gowda

📅 2026-08-21 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Police action initiated after illegal posters appeared in city limits.
  • Posters featured the image of Bengaluru Development Minister Gowda.
  • Authorities are investigating the source of the unauthorized campaign.

Illegal Publicity Sparks Police Probe

Bengaluru authorities have registered a First Information Report (FIR) following the appearance of unauthorized posters across the city. The posters prominently featured the photograph of Bengaluru Development Minister Gowda, triggering an immediate administrative response.

The incident has drawn attention to the persistent issue of illegal hoardings and banners cluttering public spaces. While political displays are common, these specific materials did not carry the necessary clearances required by the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP).

Unauthorized Displays Under Scrutiny

Local officials confirmed that the case was filed to address the violation of city advertising norms. The investigation aims to identify the individuals or groups responsible for commissioning and installing the posters without official permission.

Under current municipal guidelines, any public display of this nature requires a permit and the payment of prescribed fees. Unauthorized posters often face immediate removal by the civic body, followed by legal action against the perpetrators.

Regulatory Challenges in Bengaluru

The city has struggled for years with the proliferation of illegal signage. These displays often block footpaths, obstruct traffic signs, and deface public infrastructure, leading to frequent complaints from residents and civic activists.

The police are now reviewing CCTV footage from areas where the posters were spotted. They are attempting to trace the movement of the vehicles used to transport and install the materials during the late hours.

  • Municipal authorities have launched a drive to identify the printers involved.
  • Law enforcement officials are investigating potential breaches of the Karnataka Open Places (Prevention of Disfigurement) Act.
  • The minister's office has not yet issued a formal statement regarding the unauthorized use of his image.

The investigation is ongoing, and police are expected to track the source of the campaign through local printing hubs. Future updates will depend on the findings of the forensic analysis of the physical evidence recovered from the site.
