Goldman Sachs' India AI Enablers Basket: The 7 Core Names

Goldman Sachs has formally grouped 42 Indian listed companies into an "India AI Enablers" portfolio, and seven of them anchor the theme: HFCL, Polycab India, Netweb Technologies, Sterlite Technologies, Waaree Energies, Tata Power and one additional infrastructure-linked name. The basket has delivered roughly a 60% aggregate return in 2026, a performance that directly undercuts the global narrative that India is structurally "anti-AI."

For retail and institutional buyers, the significance is structural, not sentimental. Goldman's thesis treats AI as a capital expenditure cycle first and a software story second. Every GPU cluster in Mumbai or Hyderabad needs fibre backhaul, transformers, cooling, switchgear and uninterrupted power. India's listed industrial complex supplies exactly that.

Why These 7 Companies Qualify as AI Enablers

1. HFCL

HFCL manufactures optical fibre and cable and supplies telecom backhaul equipment, including 5G radio products. AI data centres are bandwidth-hungry, and inter-data-centre fibre demand scales with model training. HFCL's order book and margin trajectory are the metrics buyers should track.

2. Polycab India

Polycab dominates organised wires and cables, plus fast-growing FMEG and EPC segments. Data centre builds consume enormous copper and fibre cabling volumes. Polycab's pricing power and distribution reach make it the lowest-volatility AI proxy on this list.

3. Netweb Technologies

Netweb builds high-performance computing servers, private cloud systems and AI-optimised rack solutions, largely on NVIDIA platforms. It offers the most direct pure-play AI exposure among Indian hardware names, though valuation remains aggressive.

4. Sterlite Technologies

STL supplies optical connectivity and network integration for telcos and data centres globally. Its export mix adds currency and global capex sensitivity, a double-edged feature for Indian portfolios.

5. Waaree Energies

India's largest solar module maker benefits indirectly: hyperscale data centres increasingly contract renewable power, and Waaree supplies the generation hardware. Its inclusion signals Goldman's view that AI growth is an energy trade.

6. Tata Power

Tata Power's generation, distribution and rooftop solar businesses position it as the utility backbone for data centre corridors in Mumbai, Pune and Chennai. Regulated returns temper upside but stabilise the basket.

7. The Balance of the 42-Stock Portfolio

The remaining names span industrial gases, cooling systems, engineering consultancy and select IT services. Goldman weights the basket across connectivity, power and compute, mirroring global AI infrastructure indices.

Pros, Cons and Real-World Buyer Implications

  • Pro: The basket offers diversified AI exposure without direct US-listed semiconductor risk.
  • Pro: Order books at HFCL, Polycab and Netweb give visible revenue, unlike speculative AI startups.
  • Pro: Power and solar names hedge against data centre energy constraints.
  • Con: A 60% rally has compressed valuations; Polycab and Netweb trade at premium multiples.
  • Con: Execution risk is real — fibre and cable capacity additions take 12-24 months.
  • Con: Global AI capex slowdown would hit export-linked names like STL first.

Pricing Context and Value Justification

Indian investors cannot easily buy NVIDIA or Broadcom at scale. This basket is the practical domestic substitute. HFCL and Sterlite offer cyclical upside with execution risk; Polycab offers quality at a price; Netweb offers pure-play compute with the richest valuation; Tata Power and Waaree offer defensive energy exposure. A staggered, SIP-style allocation across the group makes more sense than chasing the 60% momentum print.
