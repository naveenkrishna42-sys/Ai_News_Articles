RBI's polymer note pilot faces hurdles from Chinese, Pakistani suppliers

The Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) pilot project for plastic banknotes, known as polymer notes, is encountering significant challenges from its primary suppliers, China and Pakistan. Bidders in the project have certified that no animal tallow is used in the production of these notes, a critical requirement for the RBI. However, the certification process has become a contentious issue, with some reports suggesting that the RBI is facing difficulties in obtaining the necessary approvals from these suppliers.

Animal tallow certification complicates plastic cash production

The use of animal tallow in the production of polymer notes is a significant concern for the RBI. Animal tallow is a by-product of the meat industry, and its use in the production of banknotes raises ethical and environmental concerns. The RBI has been working to ensure that the polymer notes are produced in a manner that is consistent with its guidelines on the use of animal products. However, the certification process has become a major hurdle, with some reports suggesting that the RBI is facing difficulties in obtaining the necessary approvals from its suppliers.

Global polymer banknote market adoption nears 19.2%

The global polymer banknote market is growing rapidly, with adoption nearing 19.2%. This growth is driven by the increasing demand for secure, durable, and environmentally friendly banknotes. The RBI's pilot project for polymer notes is a significant step in this direction, as it aims to introduce a new generation of banknotes that are more secure and durable than the current paper notes.
