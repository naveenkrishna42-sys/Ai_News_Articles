Cipla, INASL launch nationwide Hepatitis B screening drive on World Hepatitis Day

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cipla and INASL jointly launched a nationwide Hepatitis B screening campaign on World Hepatitis Day.
  • AIIMS experts highlighted early screening as critical to preventing hepatitis-related deaths.
  • Health Ministry reported over 21 crore people have been screened as part of India's fight against hepatitis.

Pharmaceutical major Cipla has partnered with the Indian National Association for the Study of the Liver (INASL) to roll out a nationwide Hepatitis B screening campaign. The initiative was launched on World Hepatitis Day, observed on July 28, to mark a renewed push against the viral infection that affects millions in India.

Early detection can save lives

Experts from the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) have stressed that early screening is the most effective tool to prevent hepatitis-related deaths. They said that a large number of cases go undetected until the disease has progressed to a chronic stage.

Most people infected with Hepatitis B do not show symptoms initially. This makes routine screening essential, especially for high-risk groups such as healthcare workers, pregnant women, and those with a family history of liver disease.

The campaign aims to reach remote and underserved areas where access to diagnostic facilities is limited. Cipla and INASL will set up screening camps and mobile units to test people for the Hepatitis B surface antigen.

India's progress in hepatitis control

The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare used the occasion to highlight the country's achievements in the fight against hepatitis. Officials said that over 21 crore people have been screened under various government programmes so far.

This large-scale screening effort is part of India's commitment to the World Health Organization's goal of eliminating viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030. The government has also been running vaccination drives and awareness campaigns alongside screening.

In a symbolic gesture, the Ripon Buildings in Chennai were illuminated in green on World Hepatitis Day. The green light is an international symbol of hope and solidarity in the fight against hepatitis.

Public-private partnership model

The collaboration between Cipla and INASL is a notable example of the public-private partnership model in healthcare. While the government provides the policy framework and infrastructure, private players bring in resources, logistics, and expertise.

Cipla has a long history of working on affordable medicines for hepatitis. The company has been involved in producing generic versions of antiviral drugs used to treat both Hepatitis B and C.

INASL brings academic and clinical expertise to the table. The association comprises hepatologists and liver specialists who will help design the screening protocols and ensure quality standards.

The campaign will also include training sessions for primary healthcare workers. They will be taught how to interpret test results and counsel patients who test positive.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of camps planned or the total budget for the initiative. More details are expected in the coming weeks as the campaign gathers momentum.

The next step will be to link those who test positive to treatment and care. Without follow-up, screening alone cannot reduce the disease burden. Cipla and INASL have said they are working on referral pathways to ensure timely medical intervention.
