CJP's Saurav Das alleges Puducherry Police harassed kin; cops deny

📅 2026-08-13 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Saurav Das alleges police 'acted like goondas' during visit to family home
  • Puducherry Police reject claim, say it was an Independence Day security drill
  • Das hints at Central government involvement; official calls allegation false

Allegation of intimidation

Saurav Das, a leader of the Congress-JP (CJP) party, has accused Puducherry Police of harassing his family members at their residence. In a strongly worded statement, Das claimed the officers 'acted like goondas' and attempted to intimidate his kin during a recent visit.

The politician also suggested a possible link between the police action and the Central government, though he did not provide specific evidence. His remarks have sparked a political row in the Union Territory, with opposition parties demanding a thorough inquiry.

Police response: 'Routine drill'

Puducherry Police have categorically rejected the allegations, calling them 'false and baseless'. An official spokesperson said the visit to Das's family home was part of a routine security drill conducted ahead of Independence Day celebrations.

According to the police, such drills are standard practice across the territory to assess preparedness and ensure public safety. The officer added that no harassment occurred and that the family was informed of the purpose of the visit at the time.

Political fallout

The incident has quickly become a flashpoint in local politics. CJP leaders have rallied behind Das, condemning the alleged behaviour and demanding action against the officers involved. Some have called for a judicial probe into the matter.

Meanwhile, ruling party members have defended the police, accusing Das of politicising a routine security exercise. The exchange has intensified ahead of upcoming local elections, with both sides using the episode to score political points.

What happens next

The Puducherry administration has yet to issue an official statement on the controversy. Das has said he will approach higher authorities if a satisfactory response is not forthcoming.

Observers expect the issue to be raised in the territorial assembly when it convenes next. The coming days will reveal whether this becomes a sustained political battle or fades into a footnote.
