Israeli settlers torch West Bank mosques after Palestinian killed

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Settlers torched two mosques in the West Bank after a clash that killed a Palestinian.
  • The attack occurred near Nablus, with significant damage reported to the buildings.
  • Israeli authorities have not yet confirmed arrests or an official response.

Attack on places of worship

Israeli settlers set fire to two mosques in the occupied West Bank on Sunday, hours after a deadly clash with Palestinians left one person dead. Witnesses reported seeing flames and thick smoke rising from the buildings near the city of Nablus.

The arson attack came as a direct escalation after a confrontation earlier in the day. One Palestinian was killed during the clash, though details of the incident remain unclear. Officials have not yet confirmed the identity of the deceased or the circumstances surrounding the death.

Damage and response

Firefighters were called to the scene but the mosques suffered extensive damage. Photographs from the area show charred walls and collapsed sections of the roofs. Local residents described the attack as a deliberate act of intimidation.

Israeli security forces have not announced any arrests. A spokesperson said the incident is under investigation but did not provide a timeline. No official statement has been issued by the government so far.

Broader context

The West Bank has seen a sharp rise in settler violence in recent months. According to UN data, attacks by settlers against Palestinian property and individuals have increased by over 40% compared to the same period last year.

This latest incident is likely to draw international condemnation. The European Union and several Arab nations have previously called on Israel to protect Palestinian civilians and hold perpetrators accountable. No reaction has come from the White House or the State Department yet.

Palestinian leaders have condemned the mosque burnings as a provocation. A spokesperson for the Palestinian Authority called on the international community to intervene, saying such attacks threaten to ignite a wider conflict. Hamas also issued a statement, warning of retaliation.

This is not the first time places of worship have been targeted. In 2010, settlers torched a mosque in the village of Beit Fajjar. In 2023, a similar attack occurred near Hebron. The pattern has drawn repeated criticism from human rights groups.

The Israeli military has yet to release a full report of Sunday's events. Local media reports suggest the initial clash began over land access near an outpost. Tensions have been simmering for weeks, with both sides accusing the other of aggression.

What happens next will depend on how Israeli authorities respond. If no arrests are made, the cycle of violence could deepen. The international community will be watching closely, with potential diplomatic fallout looming.
