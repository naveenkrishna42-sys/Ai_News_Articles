Scientists Discover Massive Ice Reserve in Utah Mountain

Scientists have discovered a significant ice reserve in a Utah mountain, measuring 1.55 million cubic meters. This discovery, equivalent to the volume of 600 Olympic swimming pools, was made using 232 gravity readings across the Timpanogos Rock Glacier.

Methodology and Findings

Researchers from the University of Utah used advanced gravity measurements to calculate the ice volume. By taking 232 readings across the mountain, they were able to map the subsurface ice and estimate its total volume.

Broader Implications

This discovery highlights the importance of studying mountain glaciers and rock glaciers. These formations can serve as natural water storage systems, especially in regions facing water scarcity. The Timpanogos Rock Glacier, located in Utah, may hold a billion tons of ice, making it a valuable resource for future water needs.
