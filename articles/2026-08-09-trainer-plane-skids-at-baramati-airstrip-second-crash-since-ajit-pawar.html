Trainer plane skids at Baramati airstrip, second crash since Ajit Pawar incident

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • A trainer plane skidded during landing at Baramati airstrip in Maharashtra on Sunday
  • This is the second such incident at the airstrip since Ajit Pawar's fatal crash in 2024
  • No casualties were reported in the latest incident involving a private firm's aircraft

Incident at Baramati airstrip

A trainer aircraft belonging to a private firm skidded while landing at the Baramati airstrip in Maharashtra on Sunday morning. The incident occurred around 8 am, prompting emergency response teams to rush to the spot.

Officials said there were no casualties in the latest mishap. The aircraft sustained damage, but the extent of the damage has not been officially confirmed yet.

Second incident since Ajit Pawar's crash

The Baramati airstrip has now witnessed two aviation incidents in recent years. The first was the crash in which Nationalist Congress Party leader Ajit Pawar died in 2024. The latest incident comes as a grim reminder of that tragedy.

Ajit Pawar, who was the deputy chief minister of Maharashtra at the time, died when his helicopter crashed near the same airstrip. The exact cause of that crash was never publicly released, though investigations were conducted by multiple agencies.

Response and investigation

Following Sunday's skidding, local police and airport authorities cordoned off the area. Fire tenders were deployed as a precaution, though no fire was reported.

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation has been alerted. Investigators are expected to examine the aircraft's landing gear and the runway conditions to determine what went wrong.

Authorities have not yet disclosed the name of the private firm that owns the trainer aircraft or the pilot's identity.

Safety concerns at regional airstrips

The repeated incidents at Baramati raise questions about safety protocols at smaller airstrips across Maharashtra. These facilities often handle a mix of private, training, and agricultural flights with limited infrastructure.

Aviation experts point to the need for stricter maintenance checks and better runway upkeep. However, no official statement has been issued by the state government or the aviation regulator on this matter so far.

Local residents, still scarred by the 2024 crash that claimed a prominent political leader's life, have expressed unease over the latest event. Police have urged calm and said the situation is under control.

What happens next

The DGCA's probe will focus on whether human error, technical failure, or runway conditions caused the skid. A formal report is expected in the coming weeks, and until then, training flights at Baramati are likely to remain suspended.
