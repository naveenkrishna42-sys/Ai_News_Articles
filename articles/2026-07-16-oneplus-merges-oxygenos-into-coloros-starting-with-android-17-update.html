OnePlus Merges OxygenOS into ColorOS Starting with Android 17 Update

📅 2026-07-16 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • OnePlus will move all devices to ColorOS starting with the Android 17 update.
  • The move signals deeper integration with parent company Oppo.
  • Reports of OnePlus exiting India have been denied by the company.

OnePlus Confirms Software Shift to ColorOS

OnePlus has officially announced that all its smartphones will transition to Oppo's ColorOS interface starting with the Android 17 update. The move ends the company's long-running OxygenOS skin, which had been a defining feature for the brand since its inception.

The announcement came via a company blog post on Thursday. OnePlus stated that the unified software platform will allow for faster updates and a more consistent experience across devices. Users currently on OxygenOS will receive the ColorOS build as an over-the-air update when Android 17 rolls out next year.

End of an Era for OxygenOS

OxygenOS was launched in 2015 and quickly became a favourite among tech enthusiasts for its clean, near-stock Android experience with useful customisations. Over the years, however, OnePlus gradually merged its software team with Oppo, leading to increasingly similar interfaces.

With Android 17, the two operating systems will become identical. OnePlus said existing features like Shelf and Zen Mode will be carried over, but the underlying code will be pure ColorOS. The company assured users that security patches and major OS updates will continue as scheduled.

Market Retreat and Reorganisation

This software shift comes amid reports that OnePlus is scaling back its global ambitions. Bloomberg and WIRED recently reported that the brand is pulling out of the United States and Europe, effectively ceding those markets to Oppo. OnePlus has not confirmed these reports, but the company has stopped launching new devices in several Western countries.

In India, however, OnePlus has denied any plans to exit. NDTV quoted a company spokesperson saying, 'Operations on track.' India remains OnePlus's largest market, and the brand continues to sell mid-range and flagship phones here through online and offline channels.

What This Means for Users

For current OnePlus owners, the transition to ColorOS will be seamless. The interface already shares many design elements with recent versions of OxygenOS. App drawers, quick settings, and notification panels are nearly identical. The main difference will be in pre-installed apps and default services, which will now align with Oppo's ecosystem.

OnePlus has promised at least three years of major Android updates and four years of security patches for devices launched after 2023. Older models will continue on OxygenOS until they reach their end-of-life date. The company has not said whether it will allow bootloader unlocking on ColorOS, a feature that was popular among developers.

Industry Reaction

Tech analysts have described the move as the final step in OnePlus's absorption into Oppo. CNET ran the headline 'It's Official: OnePlus Is Dead. Long Live Oppo,' reflecting the sentiment that the brand has lost its independent identity. OnePlus was founded in 2013 as a 'flagship killer' that offered high-end specs at lower prices. Over the years, it raised prices and expanded into multiple product categories, blurring the distinction with its parent company.

Despite the criticism, OnePlus continues to sell millions of phones annually, particularly in India and China. The company's decision to unify software is expected to reduce development costs and speed up update delivery.

OnePlus users can expect the Android 17 beta to begin testing early next year, with a stable rollout following in the second half of 2026. The company has not yet disclosed whether existing devices will retain the option to switch back to a stock Android experience.
