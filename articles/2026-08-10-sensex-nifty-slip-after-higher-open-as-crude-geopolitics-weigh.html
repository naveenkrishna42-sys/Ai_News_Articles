Sensex, Nifty Slip After Higher Open as Crude, Geopolitics Weigh

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty opened higher but slipped into the red by mid-session.
  • Rising crude oil prices and geopolitical concerns weighed on investor sentiment.
  • Analysts advise caution amid volatile global cues and earnings season.

Markets Reverse Early Gains

Indian equity benchmarks surrendered their opening gains on Monday, sliding into negative territory as a fresh spike in crude oil prices and simmering geopolitical tensions spooked investors. The Sensex, which had started the day on a firm footing, gave up all its advances by mid-session, while the Nifty too drifted lower, tracking weak cues from global peers.

Traders said the initial optimism, driven by positive global sentiment, quickly faded as concerns over supply disruptions and escalating conflicts resurfaced. The volatility index ticked up, reflecting nervousness among market participants ahead of key economic data due later in the week.

Crude's Climb and Global Jitters

The surge in international crude prices emerged as the primary drag, with benchmark Brent hovering near multi-week highs. Higher oil prices threaten to widen India's trade deficit and stoke imported inflation, a worry for a country that imports over 80 per cent of its crude requirements.

Geopolitical flashpoints, including ongoing tensions in the Middle East and Eastern Europe, added to the risk-off mood. Global investors have been cautious, pulling money out of riskier assets and seeking refuge in safe havens like gold and the dollar. Domestic institutional buying, however, provided some cushion to the downside.

Sectoral Hits and Stock Moves

Oil marketing companies bore the brunt of the selloff, with shares of HPCL, BPCL, and IOC declining in early trade. Aviation stocks also weakened on fears of higher fuel costs, while metal and realty counters saw mixed activity.

On the flip side, IT and pharma stocks bucked the trend, finding support from a weaker rupee, which boosts export competitiveness. Banking stocks were subdued, with heavyweight lenders like HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank trading flat to slightly negative.

What Analysts Are Watching

Market watchers say the near-term trajectory hinges on crude prices and any fresh geopolitical flashpoints. A sustained rise in oil could force the Reserve Bank of India to keep interest rates higher for longer, which would weigh on valuations.

Investors are also awaiting the upcoming monthly inflation print and corporate earnings from index heavyweights, which could provide direction. Technical analysts note that the Nifty has immediate support at the 24,800 level, while resistance is seen around 25,200.

Broader Market Picture

The broader indices underperformed the benchmarks, with midcap and smallcap stocks witnessing profit booking after a recent rally. The market breadth was negative, with more declines than advances on the BSE.

Foreign portfolio investors have turned net sellers in the past few sessions, while domestic funds have been absorbing the supply. This tug-of-war is likely to keep the market range-bound in the short term.

Outlook

Investors will keep a close eye on the movement of crude oil and any development on the geopolitical front. A de-escalation could trigger a sharp rebound, while further escalation may push the market into a deeper correction.

For now, analysts advise staying selective and focusing on quality stocks with strong fundamentals, as volatility is expected to persist until clarity emerges on these macro headwinds.
