Syria court sentences Assad, Najib to death in absentia

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Syrian court hands death sentence to Bashar al-Assad and Atef Najib in absentia
  • Trial marks first major judicial action against former regime figures since Assad's ouster
  • Sentences relate to crimes against humanity; details of execution timeline remain unclear

Landmark verdict delivered

A Syrian court has sentenced former president Bashar al-Assad and Atef Najib, a former security official, to death in a landmark trial. The verdict, delivered in absentia, marks the first time the ousted leader has been formally condemned by a domestic judicial process.

The charges centre on crimes against humanity, though the court's full reasoning has not been publicly released. Officials have not yet confirmed whether the sentences can be appealed or how they would be carried out, given that both men remain outside Syria.

Who is Atef Najib?

Atef Najib, a cousin of Assad and former head of the Political Security Directorate in Daraa, has long been a controversial figure. He was widely accused of ordering the arrest and torture of teenagers in 2011, an event that helped ignite the Syrian uprising.

Najib's role in the early crackdown made him a symbol of regime brutality. His sentencing alongside Assad signals the court's intent to hold mid-level officials accountable, not just the top leadership.

Trial in absentia raises questions

The death sentences were handed down without Assad or Najib present in court. Both men are believed to be outside the country; Assad fled to Russia in late 2024 after rebel forces captured Damascus.

Legal experts note that in absentia verdicts carry symbolic weight but face practical hurdles in enforcement. Syria's new authorities have not indicated how they plan to secure the extradition or surrender of the convicted figures.

International reactions

International media, including Al Jazeera, BBC, NDTV, and The Times of India, have reported the verdict as a historic step. However, no major government has yet issued an official statement on the sentencing.

The trial is being watched closely as a test of Syria's post-war judicial system. Many hope it will pave the way for broader accountability, while others caution that due process must be seen to be followed.

What happens next

The court's decision sets a precedent but does not resolve the fate of Assad or Najib. Observers expect diplomatic efforts to continue, with pressure likely on Russia to hand over the former president.

For now, the verdict stands as a powerful statement. Whether it translates into actual justice will depend on Syria's political will and international cooperation in the months ahead.
