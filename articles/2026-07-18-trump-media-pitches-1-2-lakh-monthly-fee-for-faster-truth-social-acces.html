Trump Media pitches $1.2 lakh monthly fee for faster Truth Social access

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump Media proposes $100,000 monthly subscription for first access to President Trump's Truth Social posts
  • Service targets financial institutions wanting 'market-moving' information before the public
  • Critics raise conflict of interest concerns over monetising presidential communications

Truth Social's premium tier: $1.2 lakh a month

Trump Media & Technology Group has proposed a $100,000 monthly subscription for Wall Street firms seeking priority access to President Donald Trump's posts on Truth Social, according to sources familiar with the plan. The proposed fee—roughly ₹83.6 lakh per month—would give paying clients a faster feed of the president's social media activity before it appears on the public timeline.

The pitch, described internally as a 'market-moving' information service, is aimed squarely at financial institutions that have been closely monitoring Trump's social media output for trading signals. A source told Reuters that company executives believe the demand is strong enough to command the premium price. 'People will pay because they have to,' one insider was quoted as saying.

Why Wall Street wants the feed

President Trump's Truth Social account has become a primary channel for policy announcements, personnel decisions, and business-related statements. Traders and analysts have long sought to get ahead of these posts, which can move stock prices, currency markets, and bond yields within minutes.

Under the proposed model, subscribers would receive Trump's posts via an API or direct data feed moments before they go live for the general public. This head start—possibly just a few seconds—could be enough for automated trading systems to execute orders based on the content before the broader market reacts.

Critics have pointed out that such a service would create an information asymmetry, effectively selling non-public presidential communications to the highest bidder. 'This raises serious questions about fairness and market integrity,' said a financial regulation expert who asked not to be named. The Securities and Exchange Commission has not commented on the proposal.

Conflict of interest concerns resurface

The plan adds to ongoing debates about President Trump's business interests overlapping with his official duties. Trump Media & Technology Group is majority-owned by Trump, meaning any revenue from the premium feed would directly benefit him financially while he holds the presidency.

CNN noted in a separate report that the same day brought several events highlighting conflicts of interest around the president. Ethics watchdogs have repeatedly called for clearer separation between Trump's businesses and his government role. The White House has not responded to requests for comment on the Truth Social fee proposal.

Trump Media has not confirmed the pricing publicly. A company spokesperson declined to comment on what they termed 'speculative reports.' The social media platform launched in 2022 and has struggled to gain the user base of larger competitors like X (formerly Twitter), which banned Trump after the January 6 Capitol riot and later reinstated him.

What comes next

The proposal is still in early stages and would require technical implementation as well as legal review. If rolled out, it could face scrutiny from regulators and lawmakers concerned about market manipulation and ethics. The coming weeks will show whether Trump Media can turn this pitch into a reality—and whether Wall Street is willing to pay the price.
