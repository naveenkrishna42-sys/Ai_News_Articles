Best of the Best Review: A Familiar Campus Comedy With a Bollywood Sheen

Every few years, Bollywood returns to the college campus for a ready-made cocktail of rivalries, romance, and redemption. Best of the Best follows that template faithfully, adding glossy song sequences, a large ensemble cast, and a sports-day climax. The result is a film that knows its audience but rarely challenges them.

The story centres on a group of underdogs at an elite institution who must outsmart a privileged rival clique to win an annual trophy. If that sounds familiar, it is because the formula has powered hits from Jo Jeeta Wohi Sikandar to Student of the Year. The question for paying viewers is whether this iteration brings enough fresh energy to justify a theatre ticket.

What Works: Casting, Music, and Campus Energy

The film's strongest asset is its ensemble. The lead pair brings genuine charm, and the supporting cast of misfits lands several laugh-out-loud moments. The music, composed for a Bollywood audience, delivers two catchy numbers that will likely trend on streaming playlists within a week of release.

Director [name withheld as per review embargo] keeps the first half breezy. The campus is shot with vibrant colour, and the classroom banter feels authentic enough to resonate with actual students. For viewers seeking a low-stakes, feel-good outing, these elements deliver exactly what the trailer promised.

Real-World Buyer Implications

At ₹250–₹450 per ticket in metros, the film is priced as a standard multiplex experience. It does not demand a premium format surcharge, which is a point in its favour. However, the 160-minute runtime means a family of four will spend roughly ₹1,200–₹1,800 on tickets alone, plus concessions. That is a meaningful outlay for a film that rarely surprises.

For budget-conscious viewers, waiting for an OTT release in 6–8 weeks is a rational choice. The visual spectacle does not require a big screen, and the comedy lands just as well at home.

Key Insights: Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Likable lead performances; two memorable songs; brisk first half; relatable campus setting; U/A certificate makes it family-friendly.
  • Cons: Overlong runtime; predictable plot beats; underdeveloped supporting characters; climax feels rushed; no premium format to justify IMAX pricing.
  • Value verdict: A decent one-time watch for campus comedy fans, but not a repeat-viewing classic.

Final Verdict: Should You Buy a Ticket?

Best of the Best is a competent but unambitious Bollywood makeover of a well-worn college comedy formula. It delivers laughs, music, and a predictable but satisfying underdog arc. If you are a fan of the genre and want a weekend outing with friends or family, it clears the bar. If you expect innovation or emotional depth, you will leave underwhelmed.

Our recommendation: catch a matinee show at standard 2D pricing, or wait for streaming. Skip the premium formats—there is nothing here that demands them.
