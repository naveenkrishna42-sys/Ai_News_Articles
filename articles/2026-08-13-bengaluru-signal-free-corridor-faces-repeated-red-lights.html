Bengaluru signal-free corridor faces repeated red lights

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • The signal-free corridor in Bengaluru is experiencing red lights at multiple intersections
  • Commuters face unexpected delays despite the corridor's purpose
  • Officials have not yet confirmed the cause or timeline for resolution

Promised seamless ride, delivered stop-and-go

Bengaluru's much-touted signal-free corridor, designed to let vehicles glide from one end to the other without a single halt, is instead turning into a series of red lights. Commuters report that at almost every intersection, the signal turns red just as they approach, forcing them to brake and wait. The corridor, which was meant to cut travel time drastically, now feels like any other congested stretch in the city.

The problem has been building over recent weeks, according to regular users. What was advertised as a smooth, uninterrupted drive has become a test of patience. Drivers who expected to zip through are now idling at signals that seem to be timed against them. The frustration is palpable, with many taking to social media to vent about the daily ordeal.

Signal timing under scrutiny

Local authorities have not yet explained why the signals are behaving this way. Some speculate that the timing algorithms may have been reset or are malfunctioning, while others point to possible coordination failures between adjacent junctions. The corridor was designed with synchronised signals to ensure a green wave for traffic moving at a set speed, but that seems to have broken down.

Traffic police officials have acknowledged the complaints but have not offered a concrete reason. They say they are looking into the matter, though no timeline has been given for a fix. Commuters, meanwhile, are left to guess whether the issue is technical or a deliberate change in policy.

Impact on daily commuters

For those who use the corridor daily, the impact is more than just lost minutes. Auto drivers and cabbies report reduced earnings as trips take longer. Office-goers, already battling Bengaluru's notorious traffic, find the signal-free corridor's failure particularly galling because it was supposed to be a relief.

The repeated halts also lead to higher fuel consumption and increased emissions, undermining the environmental benefits touted when the corridor was launched. A commuter waiting at a red light that stays red for over a minute can see the queue build up quickly, turning a smooth flow into a bottleneck.

What went wrong?

Engineers familiar with such systems suggest that signal-free corridors require precise timing based on average vehicle speed, traffic density, and time of day. Any deviation—whether due to a software update, a sensor fault, or manual overrides—can disrupt the entire sequence. Without official confirmation, these remain theories.

Some residents believe the issue may be linked to ongoing construction or maintenance work along the corridor, which could have forced temporary adjustments to signal timings. Others wonder if the system is being recalibrated for future traffic projections, but no such plan has been announced.

Waiting for a green light

Until a fix is implemented, commuters are advised to factor in extra travel time. The Bangalore Mirror's attempts to get a detailed response from the traffic management centre were met with vague assurances that the matter is being reviewed. There is no word yet on whether the signals will be re-synchronised or if a new timing plan is in the works.

For now, the signal-free corridor remains a promise unfulfilled. The next few days will show whether the authorities can get the lights to turn green in sequence again, or if the corridor's name becomes an irony that sticks.
