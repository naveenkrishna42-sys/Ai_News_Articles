Amex Resy Credit Expansion: What Actually Changed

American Express has widened the Resy dining credit on select cards to include hundreds of additional restaurants and venues, according to Forbes. The move directly addresses the biggest complaint cardholders have had since the credit launched: it was too narrow to use reliably. For a benefit that only pays out when you dine at a participating venue, breadth is everything.

The expansion matters because Amex statement credits are only as valuable as your ability to redeem them. A ₹10,000-style annual dining credit that sits unused is worth nothing. By adding hundreds of venues, Amex is converting a paper benefit into a usable one for cardholders in major metros and popular travel destinations.

Which Cards Carry the Resy Credit

The Resy credit is attached to select American Express cards, typically premium products where the annual fee runs into the tens of thousands of rupees equivalent in the US market. Cardholders should verify eligibility on their specific product before planning spend around it. The credit is separate from other Amex dining perks and follows its own enrolment and redemption rules.

Real-World Value: What the Credit Is Worth

On a premium Amex card, annual fees often exceed the equivalent of ₹50,000 to ₹75,000 in the US market. A dining credit worth a few hundred dollars can offset 20 to 40 percent of that fee if fully used. The expansion improves the odds of full redemption, especially for cardholders who travel to cities with dense Resy coverage.

The catch is behavioural. Credits only pay out if you were going to dine at those venues anyway. Spending money to trigger a credit is a net loss, not a gain. The expansion is genuinely valuable for people who already eat at Resy restaurants; it is marketing noise for everyone else.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Hundreds of new restaurants and venues now accept the credit, improving redemption odds in major cities.
  • Value is capped and resets annually; unused credit does not roll over.
  • Best suited to cardholders who dine out frequently and travel to metro areas.
  • Enrolment may be required, and credits can take a billing cycle to post.
  • The expansion strengthens the case for keeping a premium Amex card only if you can realistically use the full credit.

Should You Change Your Card Strategy?

If you already hold an eligible card, re-check the venue list in your city. The expansion may have added a restaurant you visit regularly, which turns a dormant benefit into real savings. If you are considering a new premium Amex card, model the full credit against your actual dining habits before applying.

Amex is clearly using the Resy ecosystem to make premium cards stickier. That benefits frequent diners, but it does not change the underlying maths: a credit you cannot use is not a reason to pay a higher annual fee.
