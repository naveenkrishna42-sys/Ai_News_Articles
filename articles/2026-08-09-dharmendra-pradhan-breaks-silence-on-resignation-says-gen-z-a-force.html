Dharmendra Pradhan breaks silence on resignation, says Gen Z a force

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Dharmendra Pradhan says he sought PM's approval to accept protesters' demands before resigning.
  • He called Gen Z a force that cannot be ignored, blaming some for misleading youth.
  • Pradhan hit back at Naveen Patnaik, asserting he will never disgrace Odisha.

Days after stepping down as Union Education Minister, Dharmendra Pradhan has finally broken his silence. In a series of interactions with the media, the senior BJP leader offered a detailed explanation for his resignation, which came amid widespread student protests across the country. He also took the opportunity to address critics, including Odisha's former chief minister Naveen Patnaik, and spoke at length about the political awakening of young India.

Why Pradhan quit

Pradhan said his decision to resign was not a retreat but a calculated move to de-escalate tensions. According to him, he had approached Prime Minister Narendra Modi to seek approval for accepting the demands put forward by the protesting students. When that approval was not forthcoming in the manner he expected, he chose to step down.

"I wanted to resolve the issue. I sought the PM's approval to accept the protesters' demands," Pradhan said, adding that his resignation was a way to take responsibility for the deadlock. The former minister maintained that his actions were guided by the need to protect the larger interest of students and the education system.

Gen Z cannot be ignored

The former minister reserved special mention for the younger generation, describing Gen Z as a formidable force in the country's democratic fabric. "Gen Z is a force that can't be ignored," he asserted, while cautioning that some elements had tried to mislead the youth for their own political gains.

Pradhan alleged that certain vested interests attempted to hijack the narrative of the student protests. He said the youth were right to raise their concerns, but insisted that some people tried to divert their energy towards destabilising the system. His comments come as opposition parties, including the Congress and regional outfits, have sought to corner the BJP over the handling of the protests.

War of words with Naveen Patnaik

The political slugfest took a personal turn when Pradhan responded to remarks made by Naveen Patnaik. The former Odisha chief minister had apparently taken a dig at Pradhan's resignation and his political future. Pradhan hit back sharply, saying he would never disgrace Odisha.

"Some people are trying to create a narrative that I have let down my state. That is false. I will never do anything to disgrace Odisha," he said. The exchange underscores the growing friction between the BJP and the Biju Janata Dal in the state, where both parties are locked in a fierce battle for political supremacy ahead of upcoming elections.

A message to protesters and party alike

Pradhan's remarks appear aimed at calming nerves within his own party as well. His resignation had triggered speculation about internal dissent and pressure from certain quarters. By framing his exit as a principled stand, he has tried to turn the narrative in his favour, positioning himself as a leader willing to make sacrifices for the sake of peace and dialogue.

He also urged protesters to return to the negotiating table, saying that the government remains open to discussions. However, he stopped short of criticising the Union government's handling of the crisis, choosing instead to emphasise the importance of constructive engagement.

As the dust settles, all eyes are now on the Prime Minister's Office and the Ministry of Education. Whether Pradhan's resignation leads to a policy shift or a new appointment remains to be seen. For now, the former minister has made it clear that he intends to remain a central figure in the political discourse, both in Delhi and in Odisha.
