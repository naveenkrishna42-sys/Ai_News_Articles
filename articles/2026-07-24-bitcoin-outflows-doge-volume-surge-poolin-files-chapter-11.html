Bitcoin Outflows, DOGE Volume Surge, Poolin Files Chapter 11

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin recorded outflows amid market uncertainty.
  • Dogecoin trading volume saw a notable increase.
  • Crypto mining firm Poolin filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection.

The cryptocurrency market had a mixed day on Friday, with Bitcoin facing outflows even as Dogecoin trading activity picked up. Meanwhile, a major player in the mining sector, Poolin, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in the United States, adding to the list of firms struggling in the post-crash environment.

Bitcoin Outflows Continue

Bitcoin's price remained under pressure as data showed continued outflows from digital asset investment products. Analysts pointed to persistent macroeconomic headwinds and regulatory uncertainty as key factors driving cautious sentiment among institutional investors.

The outflows come despite a broader market that has seen occasional rallies. Traders are watching the $20,000 level closely, with many expecting further volatility in the coming weeks. The trend suggests that large holders are still reducing exposure, even as retail interest shows signs of life in some altcoins.

Dogecoin Volume Picks Up

In contrast to Bitcoin's sluggishness, Dogecoin saw a significant spike in trading volume. The meme-inspired cryptocurrency recorded a notable increase in activity across major exchanges, though the reason for the uptick was not immediately clear.

Social media chatter around Dogecoin has been growing, with some users speculating about potential new use cases or endorsements. However, no official announcements were made. The volume surge appears to be driven by a mix of retail traders and algorithmic bots capitalising on short-term momentum.

Poolin Files for Chapter 11 Bankruptcy

In a significant development for the crypto mining industry, Poolin, a major mining pool operator, has filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the United States. The filing was confirmed on Friday, marking one of the largest bankruptcies in the sector this year.

Poolin had been struggling with liquidity issues following the crypto market downturn. The company cited unfavourable market conditions, high energy costs, and a drop in mining profitability as reasons for the filing. The Chapter 11 process will allow Poolin to restructure its debts while continuing operations.

Industry observers noted that the bankruptcy could have ripple effects on smaller miners who relied on Poolin's services. The firm had been a key player in the Bitcoin mining ecosystem, and its troubles underscore the ongoing shakeout in the mining sector.

Poolin's filing adds to a growing list of crypto companies that have sought bankruptcy protection in recent months. The industry is still grappling with the aftermath of the 2022 crash, and many firms are finding it difficult to recover.

What to watch: Markets will be eyeing Bitcoin's ability to hold key support levels, while Dogecoin's volume surge could signal a short-term rally. Poolin's bankruptcy proceedings will be closely followed for clues on the health of the broader mining industry.
