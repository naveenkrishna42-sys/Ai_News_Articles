Sensex ends flat, dips nearly 70 points in range-bound trade

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex closed 69.78 points lower at 82,453.72
  • Nifty settled at 24,941.50, down 18.50 points
  • Markets lacked direction due to mixed global signals

Indian equity benchmarks ended largely unchanged on Tuesday, with the BSE Sensex slipping nearly 70 points in a lacklustre session. The 30-share index closed at 82,453.72, down 69.78 points, while the broader NSE Nifty settled at 24,941.50, losing 18.50 points.

Traders described the day as range-bound, with no clear trigger to push the market in either direction. Volumes were moderate, and most sectoral indices moved in a narrow band.

Global cues offer no direction

Markets across Asia and Europe sent mixed signals during the day. While some indices edged higher on hopes of policy easing, others remained cautious ahead of key economic data due later this week.

Foreign portfolio investors remained net sellers in the cash segment, adding to the pressure. Domestic institutional buyers provided some support, but their purchases were not enough to lift the market decisively.

Banking and IT stocks weigh

Heavyweights in the banking and information technology sectors dragged the most. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and Infosys were among the top contributors to the Sensex's decline.

Auto and pharma stocks showed some resilience, paring losses in the final hour. However, gains in these pockets were insufficient to offset the selling in financials and tech.

Broader market holds up

The broader market fared slightly better than the benchmarks. The BSE midcap and smallcap indices ended in positive territory, indicating that select buying interest remained in smaller names.

Market breadth was positive on the BSE, with 1,876 stocks advancing against 1,698 declines. About 130 stocks touched their 52-week highs during the session.

Analysts said the lack of fresh triggers has kept the market in a consolidation phase. The upcoming monthly derivatives expiry later this week could add volatility.

Investors are now watching for cues from global central bank meetings and domestic inflation data due in the coming days. A breakout from the current narrow range may depend on these factors.
