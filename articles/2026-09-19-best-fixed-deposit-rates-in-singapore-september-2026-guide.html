Singapore Fixed Deposit Rates in September 2026: What Savers Are Actually Getting

Singapore's fixed deposit market has settled into a narrower band this September. The best headline rates available to retail savers sit around 1.70 per cent per annum, with entry-level minimum deposits starting from as low as $500 at several banks. That is a meaningful shift from the 3 per cent-plus era of 2023, and it changes how savers should think about locking up cash.

For anyone parking emergency reserves, CPF top-up proceeds, or a bonus payout, the question is no longer simply "which bank pays the most" but "which tenure gives me the best effective return without sacrificing liquidity."

Why the 1.70 Per Cent Headline Needs Reading Carefully

Tenure versus rate trade-off

A 1.70 per cent rate is frequently attached to a specific tenure, often 12 months or longer. A 6-month FD at 1.30 per cent rolled twice earns roughly 1.30 per cent annualised, not 1.70 per cent. Savers comparing offers must annualise the return over the actual holding period.

Minimum deposit tiers

The $500 entry point applies to a limited set of banks and usually to shorter tenures. Higher rates are often gated behind $10,000 or $20,000 placements. If you have $5,000, check whether a promotional rate requires a larger sum before you commit.

Promotional versus board rates

Many September 2026 offers are promotional, tied to fresh funds or online account opening. Board rates are lower. Confirm whether the rate holds for the full tenure or resets after an introductory period.

Fixed Deposit versus Alternatives in This Rate Environment

At 1.70 per cent, a $10,000 12-month FD returns about $170 before tax. Singapore Savings Bonds, T-bills, and high-yield savings accounts compete in the same space, and each carries different liquidity and effort costs. T-bills require auction participation; savings accounts demand salary crediting or card spend. Fixed deposits win on simplicity and certainty.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Rates up to 1.70 per cent p.a. are available, but typically on 12-month or longer tenures with fresh-fund conditions.
  • Minimum deposits start at $500, making FDs accessible to first-time savers and students.
  • SDIC coverage of $100,000 per depositor per bank means splitting large sums across banks is a practical safety step.
  • Early withdrawal almost always wipes out accrued interest, so only lock funds you will not need mid-tenure.
  • Monthly interest payout options suit retirees needing regular income; at-maturity payout suits compounding goals.

Buyer Guide: Choosing the Right FD in September 2026

Match tenure to your cash-flow timeline first, then compare rates. A 1.60 per cent 12-month FD that fits your plans beats a 1.70 per cent 24-month FD you may need to break. Verify whether the rate applies to fresh funds, and confirm the minimum placement before visiting a branch.

For sums above $100,000, split across two or more banks to stay within SDIC protection limits. For sums under $10,000, the difference between 1.30 per cent and 1.70 per cent is a few dollars a month, so convenience and app quality may matter more than the headline number.
