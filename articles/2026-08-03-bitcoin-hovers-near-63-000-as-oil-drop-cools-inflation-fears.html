Bitcoin hovers near $63,000 as oil drop cools inflation fears

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin is trading close to the $63,000 mark, supported by easing inflation worries driven by lower oil prices.
  • The cryptocurrency's recovery is being limited by weak investment flows into digital asset funds.
  • Market participants are watching for fresh catalysts to break the current range-bound movement.

Bitcoin is trading near the $63,000 level on Monday, finding some support from a decline in global oil prices that has helped calm inflation fears. The world's largest cryptocurrency, however, remains stuck in a tight range as weak inflows into digital asset products continue to cap any meaningful upside.

At the time of writing, Bitcoin was hovering around the $63,000 mark, reflecting a modest gain over the past 24 hours. The price action comes as Brent crude and WTI futures have slipped, easing concerns that persistent inflationary pressures would force central banks to keep interest rates higher for longer.

Oil's slide offers a tailwind

Falling energy prices are a double-edged sword for risk assets. On one hand, cheaper oil reduces input costs across the economy, which can translate into softer consumer price readings. For crypto traders, that scenario is favourable because it raises the odds of eventual monetary policy easing.

On the other hand, a sharp drop in crude can signal weakening global demand, which carries its own set of worries. So far, the market seems to be focusing on the inflation-relief narrative, with Bitcoin holding its ground above the psychologically important $60,000 support zone.

Flows remain sluggish

Despite the supportive macro backdrop, the recovery in Bitcoin's price has been anything but explosive. Data from fund managers and exchange-traded product issuers shows that daily net inflows into Bitcoin funds have been modest at best, with occasional days of outflows interrupting the trend.

This lack of conviction among institutional investors is keeping a lid on prices. Analysts note that while spot Bitcoin ETFs have been a major source of demand over the past year, the current environment of uncertainty around regulatory approvals and global growth is making fund managers cautious.

Trading volumes on major exchanges also remain subdued compared with the peaks seen earlier in the year. This suggests that the recent price stability is more a function of low selling pressure than of aggressive buying.

Range-bound market

Bitcoin has spent much of the last several weeks oscillating in a wide band between roughly $58,000 and $66,000. Each attempt to break above the upper end of this range has been met with selling, while dips towards the lower boundary have attracted bargain hunters.

This choppy, sideways action is typical of a market waiting for a fresh catalyst. For now, the combination of easing inflation expectations and tepid fund flows is keeping the price in equilibrium.

Options markets are showing a slight skew towards puts, indicating that some traders are positioning for downside protection. However, the implied volatility has dropped significantly from the levels seen during the spring, pointing to a market that is not expecting a major move in the near term.

What to watch

Investors will be keeping a close eye on upcoming US inflation data and the Federal Reserve's stance on interest rates. A cooler-than-expected consumer price index print could reignite risk appetite and drive fresh capital into crypto funds.

On the technical side, a sustained break above the $65,000 resistance level would likely signal the start of a new leg higher. Until then, expect Bitcoin to continue its range-bound dance, with oil prices and fund flows providing the rhythm.

The next few weeks will be crucial in determining whether Bitcoin can reclaim its upward trajectory or if it is destined to consolidate through the summer months. Market watchers are also monitoring any regulatory developments in the US and Europe that could shift the supply-demand dynamics for digital assets.
