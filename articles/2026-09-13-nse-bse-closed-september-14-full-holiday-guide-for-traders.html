NSE, BSE Closed September 14: Full Holiday Guide for Traders

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-13 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • NSE and BSE equity, F&O and currency segments are shut on September 14, with no trading, settlement or clearing activity.
  • Commodity exchanges such as MCX may operate a truncated evening session, so bullion and crude traders must confirm timings.
  • Mutual fund NAVs are not published on the holiday; units allotted on the next working day get that day's NAV.

Why the Exchanges Are Shut on September 14

India's two principal bourses, the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and the BSE, will remain closed on Monday, September 14, 2026, on account of a public holiday notified under the Negotiable Instruments Act. The Reserve Bank of India's annual holiday calendar and the exchange circulars issued at the start of the year align on this date, which is why no equity, equity derivatives, currency derivatives or interest rate futures session will be conducted.

For retail investors this is not a market event — it is a scheduled pause. There is no circuit filter to watch, no pre-open call auction and no closing bell. Trading resumes on the next working day with the usual 9:15 am pre-open and 9:15 am to 3:30 pm continuous session for equities, and 9:15 am to 11:30 pm for the commodity segment.

What Exactly Stays Closed — and What Does Not

The shutdown applies to the entire trading, clearing and settlement machinery of the exchanges. That means pay-in and pay-out obligations scheduled for September 14 are pushed to the next settlement cycle. Brokers cannot execute orders, and any order placed on a trading app will be rejected or queued depending on the platform's design.

Some services operate on a different clock. Commodity derivatives on the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX) often trade on exchange holidays because global bullion, crude and base metal markets remain open. In such cases MCX runs a shortened evening session, typically from 5:00 pm to 11:30 pm or 11:55 pm. Traders holding overnight positions in gold, silver or crude must verify the specific circular rather than assume a full closure.

Real-World Impact on Traders and Investors

An exchange holiday is a liquidity gap, not a valuation event. Your holdings do not lose or gain value because the market is shut; the last traded price simply carries forward. What does change is timing. Swing traders using stop-loss orders must remember that pending orders do not execute on a holiday, so gap risk on the reopening session is real, especially after a weekend plus a holiday.

Mutual fund investors should note that units are allotted at the NAV of the day the money is realised by the fund house, subject to cut-off times. If you submit a purchase on a holiday, the NAV applied is that of the next business day. Systematic investment plans dated September 14 will typically be processed on September 15.

  • Equity and F&O traders: No order execution, no intraday squaring off; overnight positions carry full gap risk into the next session.
  • Commodity traders: MCX may run a shortened evening session — verify timings before placing bullion or crude trades.
  • Mutual fund investors: NAV is not published; SIPs and lump sums get the next working day's NAV.
  • Derivative expiry watchers: Weekly and monthly expiries shift forward, which can compress time value for option buyers.
  • Long-term investors: No action needed; a single holiday has negligible impact on a multi-year portfolio.

How to Plan Around the Holiday

Treat the break as a housekeeping window. Reconcile contract notes, check margin balances so you are not caught short on reopening, and review any stop-loss levels that sit close to the last traded price. Brokers typically send margin shortfall alerts before a holiday, and funding a shortfall on a closed day can be difficult if banking channels in your state are also shut.

For anyone tracking the broader 2026 holiday list, the exchanges publish a consolidated annual calendar covering Republic Day, Holi, Good Friday, Independence Day, Diwali and other festivals. September 14 is one entry among roughly a dozen trading holidays in the year. Normal trading resumes the following session, with no change to contract specifications, lot sizes or margin requirements.
