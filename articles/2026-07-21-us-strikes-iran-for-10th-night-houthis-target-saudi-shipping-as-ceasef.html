US strikes Iran for 10th night, Houthis target Saudi shipping as ceasefire talks intensify

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • US strikes on Iran continue for the 10th night, with President Trump warning of retaliation for soldier deaths.
  • Houthi rebels in Yemen have threatened shipping lanes near Saudi Arabia, escalating regional tensions.
  • International mediators are pushing for a 10-day ceasefire to de-escalate the conflict.
  • Iran struck a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, while intelligence reports suggest US strikes are unlikely to change Tehran's stance.
  • India faces strategic dilemmas as the conflict expands, with calls for an independent foreign policy.

The United States has carried out fresh airstrikes against Iran for the tenth consecutive night, as the conflict in West Asia shows no signs of abating. US President Donald Trump has warned of further retaliation following the deaths of American soldiers, though intelligence reports suggest the strikes are unlikely to shift Tehran's calculus.

Strait of Hormuz under fire

Iran has struck a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. The attack raises fears of supply disruptions and a wider naval confrontation.

Meanwhile, Houthi rebels in Yemen have threatened shipping lanes near Saudi Arabia. The group, which is backed by Iran, has warned that vessels bound for Saudi ports are now targets, adding a new dimension to the conflict.

Ceasefire talks gain urgency

International mediators are pushing for a 10-day ceasefire to halt the escalation. Diplomatic channels have intensified, though no formal agreement has been reached.

The proposed truce would aim to create space for negotiations, but both sides remain far apart. Washington has insisted on an end to Iranian provocations, while Tehran demands a halt to US strikes before any talks can begin.

Intelligence assessments cast doubt

US intelligence reports have concluded that the ongoing airstrikes are unlikely to compel Iran to change its strategic posture. The assessments, reported by The Washington Post, suggest that Tehran views the strikes as a temporary challenge rather than an existential threat.

This evaluation complicates the White House's stated goal of forcing Iranian compliance through military pressure. As the bombing campaign enters its second week, analysts question whether the US has a clear endgame.

India caught in the crossfire

The expanding conflict has put New Delhi in a difficult position. India relies heavily on energy imports from the Gulf region, and any disruption to shipping in the Strait of Hormuz could have serious economic consequences.

Indian commentators have urged the government to pursue an independent foreign policy rather than aligning with any power bloc. The attack on Chabahar, a port in southeastern Iran where India has invested, has particularly rankled policymakers.

New Delhi has not yet issued a formal response to the latest developments, but officials are closely monitoring the situation.

What comes next hinges on the success or failure of the ceasefire push. If mediators can secure a 10-day pause, the region may get a chance to step back from the brink. If not, the cycle of strikes and retaliation could draw in more players and push the world closer to a broader war.
