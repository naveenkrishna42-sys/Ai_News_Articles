Six Planets, Perseids, ISS: Jordan's Sky Lights Up This Week

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Six planets will align in the morning sky on Wednesday, a rare celestial event.
  • The Perseid meteor shower is set to peak, offering bright fireballs.
  • The International Space Station will be visible, adding to the week's skywatching opportunities.

A Rare Morning Alignment

Skywatchers in Jordan are in for a treat this week as a rare six-planet alignment graces the pre-dawn sky. On Wednesday morning, Mercury, Mars, Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune, and Saturn will line up in a celestial arc, a spectacle that has not occurred in this configuration for years.

The alignment will be visible to the naked eye for the brighter planets, though a telescope or binoculars will help spot Uranus and Neptune. Astronomers advise heading to a location with a clear, unobstructed eastern horizon just before sunrise for the best view.

Perseid Meteor Shower Peaks

Adding to the excitement, the Perseid meteor shower is expected to peak around the same time, offering a dazzling display of shooting stars. Known for its fast, bright meteors, the Perseids are a favourite among amateur astronomers and casual stargazers alike.

Under ideal conditions, observers could see dozens of meteors per hour, though moonlight may interfere slightly. Experts recommend finding a dark spot away from city lights, lying back, and allowing the eyes to adjust for at least 20 minutes to catch the full show.

ISS Passes Over Jordan

In a further celestial bonus, the International Space Station (ISS) will be making several visible passes over Jordan this week. The orbiting laboratory will appear as a bright, fast-moving point of light, crossing the sky in a matter of minutes.

Pass times vary, but the station is often easiest to spot shortly after sunset or before dawn, when it reflects sunlight against a darker sky. Enthusiasts can check online trackers for precise timings for their specific location.

How to Watch

No special equipment is needed for the planet alignment or the meteor shower, though a pair of binoculars can enhance the experience. For the ISS, a clear sky and a good view of the horizon are key. It is advisable to check local weather forecasts, as cloud cover could obscure the events.

These events serve as a reminder of the dynamic nature of our solar system. With multiple phenomena occurring in the same week, it is an exceptional opportunity for families and schools to engage with astronomy.

As the week progresses, skywatchers should keep an eye on local astronomy groups for updates on exact visibility windows and any changes in conditions. The next such prominent alignment is not expected for some time, making this a must-see for those in Jordan and the wider region.
