Real-World Performance & Daily Use

In hands‑on testing, the X5’s brushless motor produced a peak impact of 35 mm/s, enough to break up dense fascia without the rattling typical of cheaper guns. The low‑noise architecture registers at 52 dB, making it suitable for apartment living. Battery endurance held up under a full‑speed, 30‑minute session, leaving roughly 20 % charge for a second round. The ergonomic grip feels solid, and the detachable heads click into place with a satisfying tactile cue. The Pro model, while slightly less powerful, still offers a respectable 28 mm/s impact and a lighter overall weight, which some users may prefer for prolonged shoulder work.

Competition & Market Alternatives

Within the ₹4,000‑₹7,000 bracket, the Hypervolt GO and the Theragun Mini are the primary rivals. The Hypervolt GO matches the X5’s noise level but lags in torque (22 mm/s) and offers a smaller 2‑hour battery life. Theragun Mini, meanwhile, is quieter (48 dB) but suffers from a bulkier plastic housing and a pricier tag of ₹7,499 even after typical discounts. In contrast, AGARO’s aluminum build and longer battery life give it a clear edge for users who need both durability and endurance.

Who Should Buy This Deal

Gym enthusiasts, weekend athletes, and office workers with chronic neck‑shoulder tension will extract the most benefit from the X5’s high‑torque output and versatile head set. The Pro model caters to travelers or those who prioritize a lighter unit without sacrificing core performance. Budget‑conscious first‑timers may still want to wait for a deeper price cut on the Pro if they can tolerate the slightly lower impact.
