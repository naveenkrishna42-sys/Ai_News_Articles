Guru Randhawa hits back at trolls over 'Fine Shyt' lyrics: 'Silly little slang'

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Guru Randhawa responds to criticism over 'Fine Shyt' lyrics, calling it 'silly little slang'
  • The song faces backlash for its portrayal of women and use of slang
  • Tony Kakkar reacts, saying it 'would have been fun' if they had trolled together

Punjabi singer Guru Randhawa has finally broken his silence over the controversy surrounding his latest track 'Fine Shyt', asking trolls to calm down and dismissing the criticism as much ado about nothing. The song, which has been trending for all the wrong reasons, drew flak for its lyrics and the way women are portrayed in the video.

In a cryptic social media post, Randhawa addressed the backlash head-on, calling the contentious words a "silly little slang". The singer's response, however, has done little to cool the debate, with fans and critics split over whether he is right to brush off the concerns.

The controversy so far

'Fine Shyt' released last week and quickly became a talking point, but not for its music. Netizens slammed the track for its suggestive lyrics, with many accusing the singer of normalising disrespectful language. The music video, too, came under fire for its depiction of women, sparking a wider conversation about sexism in Punjabi pop.

Randhawa, known for hits like 'Lahore' and 'High Rated Gabru', initially stayed quiet as the backlash grew. But on Monday, he took to social media to respond, urging detractors to see the lighter side. "It's just a silly little slang," he wrote, adding that people should not take the song so seriously.

Tony Kakkar weighs in

The controversy has now drawn in other musicians, with singer Tony Kakkar reacting to Randhawa's defence. In a post that raised eyebrows, Kakkar said it "would have been fun if we had trolled together", a remark that seemed to mock the entire situation while also offering a strange show of solidarity.

Kakkar, no stranger to online criticism himself, has faced similar backlash for his own songs in the past. His comment has only added fuel to the fire, with fans wondering if this is the start of a new trend where artists dismiss criticism as mere trolling.

Artistic freedom or casual sexism?

The debate over 'Fine Shyt' raises a familiar question: where does artistic freedom end and responsibility begin? Supporters of Randhawa argue that songwriters often use slang and colloquialisms to connect with younger audiences, and that critics are being overly sensitive. Detractors, however, point out that such lyrics can reinforce harmful stereotypes, especially when they objectify women.

This is not the first time a Bollywood or Punjabi track has faced such scrutiny. In recent years, several songs have been called out for their lyrics, leading to edits, apologies, and even legal complaints in some cases. The music industry, though, has largely continued to push boundaries, often citing creative licence.

What happens next

Randhawa's "calm down" message is unlikely to end the online storm. As the debate rages on, the singer has not announced any plans to change the song or its video. Whether the controversy will hurt his chart numbers or boost them remains to be seen — history suggests that outrage often drives streams.

For now, all eyes are on how the singer follows up. Will he double down, or will he offer a fuller explanation? The coming days will tell if this "silly little slang" becomes a footnote or a defining moment in his career.
