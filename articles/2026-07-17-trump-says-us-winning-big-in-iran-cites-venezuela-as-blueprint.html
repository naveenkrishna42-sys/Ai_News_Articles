Trump says US 'winning big' in Iran, cites Venezuela as blueprint

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says US is 'winning big' in Iran, using Venezuela as a strategy model.
  • He warns of escalating strikes on Iranian energy targets, including power plants and bridges.
  • Iran is reportedly seeking negotiations, but Trump warns Tehran to 'behave'.

Former US President Donald Trump has declared that the United States is 'winning big' in its confrontation with Iran, pointing to the strategy used in Venezuela as a blueprint for success. In a series of statements, Trump outlined an escalating military and economic pressure campaign that he claims will soon yield significant results.

'Will see fruits very soon'

Trump asserted that the US approach in Iran is mirroring the tactics that brought about political change in Venezuela. 'We're winning big in Iran. It's the same playbook as Venezuela — maximum pressure, targeted strikes, and cutting off their money. You will see the fruits very soon,' he said.

The former president did not provide specific evidence of progress but insisted that the strategy was working. His comments come amid heightened tensions in the Persian Gulf, where US forces have been deployed near strategic choke points.

Escalation in three days: 'Bridges, power plants'

Trump warned that the US would escalate its military campaign in Iran within three days. 'Next week comes bridges, power plants. We're going to hit their energy infrastructure hard. They need to understand that there is no safe haven,' he stated.

He also referenced a plan involving troops on the Hormuz Islands and a bombing campaign targeting what he described as 'Pickaxe Mountain' — a term likely referring to Iranian military installations. The specifics of these operations remain unclear, and US defense officials have not confirmed the details.

Port blockade and energy targets

In a separate development, Trump confirmed the resumption of a naval blockade on Iranian ports, aimed at cutting off oil exports and other trade. He threatened further strikes on energy targets if Tehran does not comply with US demands.

'We're blocking every port. No oil leaves Iran. And if they test us, we'll hit their refineries and pipelines. This is how you break a regime,' Trump said. The blockade has already disrupted global oil markets, with prices rising in response to the heightened risk of supply disruptions.

Iran seeks talks, Trump warns

Amid the escalating pressure, Trump claimed that Iran has been seeking negotiations with the United States. 'They want to talk. They're feeling the heat. But I'm telling them — behave. You don't get to threaten the world and then ask for a seat at the table,' he warned.

Iranian officials have not publicly confirmed any request for talks. However, diplomatic sources indicate that backchannel communications may be underway, though no formal negotiations have been announced.

The situation remains fluid, with both sides positioned for further confrontation. Analysts suggest that the coming days will be critical in determining whether the US escalates its strikes as promised or whether diplomatic channels open.

For now, Trump's rhetoric signals a continued hardline approach, with the Venezuela model serving as a template for what he hopes will be a decisive outcome in Iran.
