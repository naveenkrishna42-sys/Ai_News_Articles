Made by Google 2026: Pixel 11, Watch 5 and first tracker expected

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pixel 11 series expected to launch with Tensor G6 chipset, new colors, and design changes.
  • Pixel Watch 5 likely to feature upgraded health sensors and battery life.
  • Google's first tracker, possibly named 'HiLight', may debut alongside the Pixel 11 lineup.

What to expect from Pixel 11 series

Google's annual hardware event, Made by Google, is set to take place later this year, and leaks are already painting a detailed picture of what the tech giant has in store. The Pixel 11 series is expected to headline the show, with three models — the standard Pixel 11, a Pro variant, and a new 'HiLight' edition aimed at photography enthusiasts.

According to a report from 9to5Google, Google Contacts has revealed the color options for the Pixel 11 HiLight, hinting at a fresh palette that could include pastel shades and bold accents. The HiLight name itself suggests a focus on camera capabilities, possibly featuring improved low-light performance and advanced AI-driven imaging.

Tensor G6 performance

The Pixel 11 series is rumored to be powered by the Tensor G6 chipset, which has already appeared in benchmark leaks. Early scores suggest a significant leap in CPU and GPU performance compared to the Tensor G5, bringing the Pixel line closer to competitors like Qualcomm's Snapdragon flagship and Apple's A-series chips.

Pre-order dates are also circulating, with some reports suggesting that Google may open orders shortly after the event, following the pattern of previous launches. The Tensor G6 is expected to enhance on-device AI capabilities, enabling faster language translation, smarter photo editing, and more efficient battery management.

Pixel Watch 5 and the first Google tracker

Alongside the Pixel 11, Google is expected to unveil the Pixel Watch 5. While details are scarce, the smartwatch is likely to build on the success of its predecessor, with upgrades to health tracking, battery life, and possibly a new design. Fitness enthusiasts may see additional sensors for metrics like blood oxygen and stress levels.

More intriguingly, Google is reportedly working on its first-ever tracking device. While the name remains unconfirmed, it could compete directly with Apple's AirTag and Samsung's SmartTag. The tracker would integrate with Google's Find My Device network, which has expanded significantly over the past year to include third-party accessories.

What this means for the ecosystem

The introduction of a Google tracker would complete the company's hardware ecosystem, offering users a seamless way to locate keys, bags, and other belongings. It would also strengthen the case for Android users to stay within the Google ecosystem, rather than switching to rival platforms.

However, Google faces stiff competition. Apple's AirTag has dominated the market since its launch, and Samsung has a strong presence with its SmartTag line. Google will need to offer compelling features, such as UWB (ultra-wideband) support for precise location tracking, and robust privacy safeguards, to stand out.

The Made by Google event is also expected to feature updates to the Nest line of smart home devices, and possibly a new Chromecast or streaming dongle. But the Pixel 11 series and the tracker are likely to be the stars of the show.

As the event approaches, more leaks are expected to surface, revealing additional details about pricing, availability, and regional rollouts. Indian consumers will be particularly keen to know if the Pixel 11 series will launch in the country, given Google's growing focus on the Indian market.

Google has not yet confirmed the exact date for the event, but based on past years, it is likely to be held in October. The company will be hoping to build on the momentum of the Pixel 9 series, which received positive reviews for its camera and AI features.

With the Tensor G6 promising a major performance boost, and the addition of a tracker to the ecosystem, 2026 could be a landmark year for Google's hardware division. The next few months will reveal whether these leaks translate into reality, and how the market responds.
