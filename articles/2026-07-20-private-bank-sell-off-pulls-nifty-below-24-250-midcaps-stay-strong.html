Private Bank Sell-Off Pulls Nifty Below 24,250; Midcaps Stay Strong

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nifty fell below 24,250, dragged by heavy selling in private banking stocks.
  • Midcap and smallcap indices rose, showing resilience against the benchmark decline.
  • Private banks like HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank were among the top losers.

Mumbai, July 20: The Indian stock market ended Monday on a mixed note, with the benchmark Nifty slipping below the 24,250 mark as a sharp sell-off in private banking stocks weighed heavily on sentiment. However, midcap and smallcap indices defied the gravity, climbing higher and narrowing the overall market losses.

The Nifty 50 closed at 24,232, down 0.3% from the previous session. The Sensex also lost ground, shedding over 200 points to settle at 79,650. Traders pointed to profit booking in heavyweight lenders as the primary culprit.

Private Banks Take the Heat

Private sector banks bore the brunt of Monday's selling. HDFC Bank fell 1.8%, while ICICI Bank dropped 1.5%. Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank also ended in the red, losing nearly a percent each. Analysts attributed the slump to concerns over rising deposit costs and margin pressures in the sector.

“The private banking pack has been underperforming recently due to tighter liquidity conditions and a slowdown in loan growth,” said a market analyst at a domestic brokerage. “Investors are booking profits after the recent rally in these stocks.”

The Nifty Bank index declined 0.6%, making it the worst-performing sectoral index of the day.

Midcaps and Smallcaps Buck the Trend

While large-cap stocks struggled, midcap and smallcap indices painted a different picture. The Nifty Midcap 100 rose 0.4%, and the Nifty Smallcap 100 gained 0.6%. Stocks from sectors such as pharmaceuticals, chemicals, and consumer durables led the charge.

“The resilience in midcaps and smallcaps suggests that retail investor appetite for risk remains intact,” a fund manager noted. “These segments are seeing strong buying support, partly driven by positive earnings reports from smaller firms.”

Among midcap winners, companies like Dr Reddy’s Laboratories and Britannia Industries advanced over 2% each, while smallcap names such as Gujarat Gas and Tata Chemicals also posted solid gains.

Broader Market Mood and Global Cues

The overall market breadth remained positive, with more stocks advancing than declining on the BSE. Of the 3,500-odd stocks traded, around 1,800 ended higher. However, the mood was cautious as global markets traded mixed, with US futures pointing to a flat opening.

Foreign portfolio investors were net sellers to the tune of ₹1,200 crore on Monday, provisional data showed. Domestic institutional investors, however, stepped in with purchases worth ₹800 crore, providing some cushion to the market.

Crude oil prices stayed elevated above $85 a barrel, adding to inflationary worries. The rupee weakened slightly against the US dollar, closing at 83.50.

What to watch: Traders will now look to corporate earnings from major companies later this week, including Reliance Industries and Maruti Suzuki, for further direction. The Reserve Bank of India’s monetary policy minutes, due on Wednesday, could also influence rate-sensitive sectors like banking and auto.
