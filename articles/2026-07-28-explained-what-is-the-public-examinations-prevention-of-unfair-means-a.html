Explained: What is the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill

📅 2026-07-28 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Bill seeks to prevent unfair means in public examinations conducted by central agencies.
  • Opposition MPs criticised the Bill as an 'admission of failure' and a 'showpiece'.
  • TMC MP Abhishek Banerjee highlighted the recurring pattern of paper leak scandals each year.
  • BJP MP offered Gen Z-style praise for PM Modi during the debate.

What the Bill Proposes

The Lok Sabha on Tuesday debated the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026. The legislation aims to crack down on paper leaks, cheating, and organised malpractices in public examinations conducted by central government agencies. It proposes stringent penalties, including imprisonment and heavy fines, for those found guilty of leaking question papers or tampering with the examination process. The government has positioned the Bill as a measure to protect the integrity of competitive exams and the interests of millions of students who appear for them each year.

Opposition's Sharp Response

The Bill faced strong criticism from opposition benches. Several MPs termed it an 'admission of failure' by the government to prevent recurring paper leak scandals. Congress MP Gaurav Gogoi tore into the government during the debate, asking, "Who ordered you to use pellet guns?" He accused the ruling party of turning the Bill into a 'showpiece' rather than a substantive solution. Gogoi's remarks referenced past instances of police action against protesting students, drawing a sharp contrast between the government's tough talk on paper leaks and its handling of student unrest.

TMC MP Abhishek Banerjee pointed out the cyclical nature of the problem. "Every year, students get a new paper leak scandal," he said, highlighting that without addressing systemic flaws, the Bill would only offer temporary fixes. Banerjee argued that the government should focus on strengthening examination infrastructure and ensuring transparency rather than merely increasing punishment.

Unlikely Praise in the House

Amid the heated debate, a moment of unexpected praise came from the ruling benches. A BJP MP offered what was described as a 'Gen Z-style' compliment for Prime Minister Narendra Modi, saying "Modiji clocked it" during the discussion. The remark, which drew mixed reactions in the House, reflected the government's attempt to connect with younger voters. However, opposition members dismissed it as a distraction from the core issues raised by the Bill.

What Critics Are Saying

Legal experts and student groups have also raised concerns. Critics argue that the Bill's broad definitions could lead to misuse, potentially targeting innocent students or small-time operators while leaving the larger networks unscathed. They point out that many paper leaks involve organised syndicates with political or bureaucratic connections—actors the Bill may not effectively deter. The opposition has demanded a more comprehensive approach, including better surveillance of examination centres and stricter monitoring of printing and transportation of question papers.

The government, however, maintains that the Bill is a strong deterrent. It argues that existing laws are insufficient to tackle the scale and sophistication of modern exam fraud. Union ministers have cited examples of recent high-profile leaks in recruitment exams that affected lakhs of candidates, stressing the need for a dedicated legal framework.

What Happens Next

The Bill is expected to be taken up for a vote in the Lok Sabha later this week. It will then move to the Rajya Sabha for further scrutiny. The coming days will show whether the government can build consensus or if the opposition will force amendments. All eyes are on how this legislation evolves—and whether it can finally break the cycle of paper leaks that has plagued India's examination system for years.
