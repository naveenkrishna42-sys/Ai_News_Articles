MNRE Issues Branding Norms for PM Surya Ghar Rooftop Solar Scheme

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • MNRE has notified branding norms for PM Surya Ghar rooftop solar installations.
  • The scheme has crossed the 50-lakh household installation mark.
  • New rules mandate specific branding on equipment and installations.
  • Jammu & Kashmir emerges as a rising solar market with 39,160 installations.

The Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) has issued fresh branding norms for installations under the PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana. The directive, notified on Tuesday, aims to standardise the visual identity of rooftop solar systems across the country. It comes as the scheme crosses the 50-lakh household mark, a milestone that underscores the programme's rapid adoption.

What the New Branding Norms Entail

The MNRE notification specifies how the scheme's branding must appear on solar panels, inverters, and other system components. Installers and vendors are now required to display the official PM Surya Ghar logo and scheme name on all equipment. This applies to both new installations and those completed under the scheme's recent phases.

The norms are intended to create uniformity, making it easier for households to verify that their systems are part of the government initiative. Officials believe this will also help in quality checks and consumer awareness. The branding must be durable and visible, with specific guidelines on size and placement.

Scheme Crosses 50-Lakh Households

The notification arrives alongside news that the rooftop solar scheme has surpassed 5 million household installations. This milestone reflects the growing acceptance of solar energy in Indian homes, driven by subsidies and net-metering benefits. The pace has picked up significantly in the last year, with states like Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Uttar Pradesh leading the count.

Industry experts say the branding move could further boost trust among consumers. A standardised identity helps in distinguishing authorised installations from substandard ones. However, some vendors have expressed concerns about the additional compliance burden, though MNRE has assured that the process will be streamlined.

Jammu & Kashmir Emerges as a Solar Market

In a related development, Jammu & Kashmir has recorded 39,160 rooftop installations, positioning it as a rising solar market. This is notable given the region's challenging terrain and weather conditions. The administration has been pushing for renewable energy adoption, with local incentives complementing central scheme benefits.

The Union Territory's progress comes even as the Centre scrapped a 20 MW allocation under the PM-KUSUM scheme over lack of progress. That decision, reported earlier, highlights discrepancies in implementation across regions. While J&K's rooftop segment grows, ground-mounted projects have lagged, prompting the central action.

What the Branding Means for Consumers

For households, the branding norms are a reassurance of authenticity. When a solar system carries the official logo, it signals that the installation meets government standards. This is particularly relevant for those availing subsidies, as it creates a clear audit trail.

MNRE has also directed state nodal agencies to conduct random checks to ensure compliance. Non-compliant vendors could face penalties, including removal from the scheme's vendor list. The ministry has not yet specified a deadline for full compliance, but sources suggest a grace period of a few months.

As the scheme expands, the focus on standardisation is likely to intensify. The branding norms are a small but significant step in building a reliable solar ecosystem in India.

Looking ahead, the next few months will reveal how smoothly the norms are adopted on the ground. With the 50-lakh milestone behind, the government's target of 1 crore installations by 2027 remains ambitious but within sight. The coming quarters will test both the scheme's scalability and the industry's ability to meet new regulatory standards.
