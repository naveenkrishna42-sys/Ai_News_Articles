Navratri 2025: 8 Must-Visit Durga Temples In India For The Spiritual Souls

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Eight famous Durga temples across India are highlighted for Navratri 2025 visits.
  • Temples include Vaishno Devi, Kamakhya, Dakshineswar, and others.
  • Each site offers unique rituals and architectural styles.
  • Travelers are advised to plan ahead due to expected crowds.

As the auspicious nine nights of Navratri approach, millions of devotees across India prepare to honour Goddess Durga in her many forms. For the spiritually inclined, this is a time of fasting, prayer, and pilgrimage. If you are planning to immerse yourself in devotion, here are eight must-visit Durga temples that promise both divine blessings and a glimpse into India's rich cultural heritage.

Vaishno Devi, Jammu & Kashmir

Perched in the Trikuta Mountains, the shrine of Mata Vaishno Devi is one of the holiest sites in the country. The cave temple, located at an altitude of about 5,200 feet, attracts lakhs of pilgrims each year. The trek to the sanctum, spanning roughly 12 kilometres, is a test of faith and endurance, but the final darshan is said to be deeply rewarding.

During Navratri, the atmosphere is electric with chants of 'Jai Mata Di'. Special arrangements are made for the influx of visitors, and the yatra runs round the clock. Devotees often undertake the journey barefoot as a mark of respect.

Kamakhya Temple, Assam

Situated on the Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, the Kamakhya Temple is one of the oldest and most revered Shakti Peethas in India. Dedicated to Goddess Kamakhya, the temple is known for its unique tantric worship practices. The annual Ambubachi Mela draws huge crowds, but Navratri is equally significant here.

The temple's architecture, with its distinctive beehive-shaped shikhara, is a marvel in itself. During the nine nights, special pujas and homas are conducted, and the atmosphere is thick with the scent of incense and the sound of bells.

Dakshineswar Kali Temple, West Bengal

Located on the eastern bank of the Hooghly River, the Dakshineswar Kali Temple is synonymous with the mystic saint Ramakrishna Paramahamsa. The temple complex, built in the traditional Bengali style, houses the deity Kali in the main sanctum. Navratri, known here as Durga Puja, is the biggest festival of the year.

While the city of Kolkata erupts in grand pandal-hopping celebrations, the temple offers a more serene and traditional experience. The rhythmic beating of dhak and the glow of earthen lamps create a celestial ambience. It is a place where history and spirituality intertwine seamlessly.

Chamundeshwari Temple, Karnataka

Overlooking the city of Mysuru from the Chamundi Hills, the Chamundeshwari Temple is dedicated to the demon-slaying goddess. The towering gopuram, adorned with intricate carvings, is visible from miles away. The temple's origins date back centuries, and it has been patronised by the Wodeyar dynasty.

Navratri is celebrated with special grandeur in Mysuru, culminating in the famous Mysuru Dasara procession. The temple itself is bedecked with flowers and lights, and the evening aartis are a sight to behold. Climbing the 1,000 steps to the hilltop is considered an act of penance.

Ambaji Temple, Gujarat

Situated in the Aravalli ranges, the Ambaji Temple is one of the 51 Shakti Peethas and a major pilgrimage site for followers of the goddess. The temple enshrines a yantra rather than an idol, symbolising the goddess's presence. It is believed to be the spot where Sati's heart fell.

During Navratri, Ambaji becomes a hub of activity, with thousands of devotees performing the traditional garba dance in the temple premises. The surrounding town is painted in vibrant colours, and the air resonates with devotional songs. It is an experience that blends faith with festivity.

Kanaka Durga Temple, Andhra Pradesh

Perched on the Indrakeeladri Hill in Vijayawada, the Kanaka Durga Temple overlooks the Krishna River. The goddess is worshipped here as the bestower of boons, and the temple is particularly crowded during Navratri. Each day of the festival is associated with a different form of the goddess, and special alankaras (decorations) are prepared.

Devotees throng the hilltop, often taking the steps or the ropeway to reach the shrine. The view from the top is as breathtaking as the spiritual experience. The temple's annual calendar is packed, but Navratri remains the most auspicious period.

Kali Mandir, Kolkata (Kalighat)

One of the most famous Kali temples in India, Kalighat is believed to be where the toes of Sati's right foot fell. The temple's name is said to have given the city of Kolkata its own. The idol of Kali here is unique, with a golden tongue and a silver crown.

During Navratri, the temple witnesses an unending stream of devotees. The sacrifice of goats, though controversial, is still practised on certain days. For many, a visit to Kalighat is a crucial part of their spiritual journey in the city.

Maa Tarini Temple, Odisha

Situated near Ghatgaon in Keonjhar district, the Maa Tarini Temple is one of the four main Shakti shrines in Odisha. The goddess is depicted with two idols, and her worship involves unique tribal and tantric rituals. The temple is set in a lush forest, adding to its mystical aura.

Navratri here is marked by large fairs and animal sacrifices, drawing devotees from across the state. The temple's rustic charm and deep-rooted traditions offer a different kind of spiritual experience compared to the grander temples.

As you plan your pilgrimage for Navratri 2025, remember to check local travel advisories and temple schedules. The crowds will be immense, but the blessings are equally abundant. Whether you choose the snowy heights of Vaishno Devi or the riverbanks of Dakshineswar, each temple offers a unique path to the divine.

What remains key is the devotion in your heart. So pack your bags, keep your faith strong, and set out on a journey that is as much about self-discovery as it is about worship. The goddess awaits her children.
