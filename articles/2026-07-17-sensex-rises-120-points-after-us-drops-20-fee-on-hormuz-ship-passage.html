Sensex Rises 120 Points After US Drops 20% Fee on Hormuz Ship Passage

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex ended 120 points up after US dropped proposed 20% fee on Hormuz ship passage
  • Move eased fears of rising shipping costs and disruption in crude oil supplies
  • Broader market indices also traded in positive territory during the session

Market Closes Higher After US Policy Shift

Indian equity benchmarks closed in the green on Wednesday, with the Sensex gaining 120 points. The rally was driven by a key development overseas: the United States dropped its proposed 20% transit fee on ships crossing the Strait of Hormuz.

The decision removed a major overhang that had kept traders cautious in recent sessions. The Nifty also ended higher, though gains were modest compared to the Sensex.

Why the Hormuz Fee Mattered

The Strait of Hormuz is a vital chokepoint for global oil shipments. A 20% fee on vessels passing through would have raised shipping costs significantly, hitting import-dependent nations like India particularly hard.

Crude oil prices had edged up in anticipation of the levy. Wednesday's reversal brought some relief to markets, as lower freight costs support margins for Indian refiners and airlines.

Sectoral Performance

Banking and IT stocks led the charge. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Infosys were among the top contributors to the Sensex's gains. Oil marketing companies also saw buying interest after the Hormuz news.

Auto stocks were mixed. While Maruti Suzuki and Tata Motors rose, Bajaj Auto ended flat. Realty and metal indices traded in narrow ranges.

Broader Market Sentiment

The broader market mirrored the benchmark's positivity. The BSE Midcap index rose 0.3%, while the Smallcap index added 0.2%. Volumes were moderate, suggesting the move was more relief-driven than a broad-based rally.

Foreign institutional investors were net buyers in early trade, according to provisional data. Domestic institutions also picked up stocks, particularly in the financial space.

What Analysts Are Saying

Market participants viewed the US decision as a short-term positive. The fee had threatened to escalate trade tensions and disrupt supply chains in the Gulf region. Its withdrawal removes one layer of uncertainty.

However, analysts caution that the broader macroeconomic picture remains challenging. High inflation in developed economies and the possibility of further rate hikes continue to weigh on global risk appetite.

Outlook

The focus now shifts to domestic cues, including corporate earnings for the June quarter and the trajectory of monsoon rains. Investors will watch for any fresh policy announcements from the US or Gulf nations regarding shipping lanes.

For now, the Sensex has reclaimed some lost ground, but sustaining above the 62,000 mark will require consistent buying support from FIIs and a clear direction on oil prices.
