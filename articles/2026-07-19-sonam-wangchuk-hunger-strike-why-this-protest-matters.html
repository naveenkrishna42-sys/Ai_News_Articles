Sonam Wangchuk Hunger Strike: Why This Protest Matters

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sonam Wangchuk remains on hunger strike in hospital, with AIIMS experts backing urgent medical care.
  • CJP's Dipke claims Delhi Police is planning an overnight 'crackdown' on the protest site.
  • Former CJI K.G. Balakrishnan and opposition leaders condemn Wangchuk's removal from Jantar Mantar.
  • The protest highlights unresolved demands for Ladakh's statehood and Sixth Schedule protections.

Context: An Activist's Hunger Strike

Sonam Wangchuk, the Ladakhi engineer and climate activist known for the 'Ice Stupa' project, has been on a hunger strike for days. He was hospitalised after his health deteriorated, but he continues his fast. The protest, initially held at Jantar Mantar in Delhi, was abruptly dismantled by police, sparking a political firestorm.

The central issue is Ladakh's demand for statehood and inclusion under the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution, which would grant tribal communities greater autonomy over land and resources. Wangchuk's fast is the latest escalation in a movement that has simmered since Ladakh was made a Union Territory in 2019, a move many locals saw as a dilution of their political rights.

Background: The Ladakh Protests and Police Action

Wangchuk's hunger strike began as a peaceful sit-in at Jantar Mantar, a traditional protest site in New Delhi. On Friday night, however, Delhi Police removed him and other protesters, citing a Supreme Court order on noise pollution and public inconvenience. Wangchuk was then taken to Safdarjung Hospital, where he remains.

The Citizens' Justice Party (CJP), whose leader Dipke is coordinating with the protesters, has accused the police of planning an overnight 'crackdown' on the remaining demonstrators. Dipke claimed that police intend to clear the area forcibly, though Delhi Police has not confirmed this. The AIIMS medical board has backed urgent care for Wangchuk, but the activist has reportedly refused treatment, insisting his demands be met first.

Former Chief Justice of India K.G. Balakrishnan has publicly supported Wangchuk's hospitalisation, stating that his life must be saved. The intervention of a former CJI underscores the legal and ethical gravity of the situation.

Why It Matters: A Test of Protest Rights and Federal Balance

This is not just a local protest. Wangchuk's fast has become a national symbol of Ladakh's grievances. The region, strategically located near the borders with China and Pakistan, has long felt neglected by the central government. The 2019 abrogation of Article 370 and the subsequent reorganisation of Jammu and Kashmir into Union Territories left Ladakh without a legislature or elected government.

The demand for Sixth Schedule status is critical. It would allow Ladakh's tribal communities to control their land, forests, and local governance, preventing what activists call 'outsider exploitation' of the region's fragile ecology. Wangchuk's hunger strike has galvanised support from opposition parties, who see it as a test of the Narendra Modi government's commitment to democratic dissent.

The opposition has slammed the police action, with leaders calling it 'a shame' that a peaceful protester was forcibly removed. The BJP has defended the move, arguing that the protest violated court orders and that Wangchuk's health was at risk. This partisan divide reflects deeper tensions over how the central government handles regional autonomy movements.

What to Watch: Medical, Legal, and Political Trajectories

Wangchuk's health remains the immediate concern. If his fast continues, it could trigger a medical emergency, forcing the government into a difficult position. The AIIMS board's backing of urgent care suggests that doctors may soon have to intervene even against his will, a step that would carry legal and ethical implications.

Legally, the Delhi Police's action could face challenge in court. The right to peaceful protest is constitutionally protected, and the removal of protesters from Jantar Mantar may be seen as excessive. The CJP has indicated it will approach the courts to prevent further 'crackdowns'.

Politically, the protest could escalate. Other Ladakhi groups and opposition parties may organise solidarity protests, increasing pressure on the central government. The BJP's defence of the police action risks alienating voters in Ladakh, a region that has traditionally been less aligned with national parties.
