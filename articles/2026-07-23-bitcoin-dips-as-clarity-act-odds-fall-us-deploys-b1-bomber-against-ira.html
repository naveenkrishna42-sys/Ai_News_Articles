Bitcoin Dips as Clarity Act Odds Fall, US Deploys B1 Bomber Against Iran

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Odds of the US Clarity Act passing in Congress have tumbled, denting hopes for clear crypto regulation.
  • The US has deployed B1 bomber aircraft against Iran, adding to geopolitical tensions.
  • Bitcoin price edged lower, reflecting market jitters over both regulatory and military developments.

Clarity Act Odds Slip Amid Political Headwinds

The chances of the United States passing the highly anticipated Clarity Act — a bill meant to deliver clear federal rules for digital assets — have dropped sharply. Political analysts tracking betting markets say the probability of the legislation clearing Congress in its current form has fallen below 40%.

The setback comes as lawmakers remain deeply divided over key provisions, including stablecoin oversight and whether the Securities and Exchange Commission or the Commodity Futures Trading Commission should take the lead. Industry groups had pinned hopes on the act to end years of regulatory uncertainty.

B1 Bombers Deployed Against Iran

In a separate but market-moving development, the United States has deployed B1 Lancer bombers to strike targets inside Iran. The Pentagon confirmed the operation late Wednesday, describing it as a response to recent attacks on US personnel in the region. The move marks a significant escalation in hostilities.

Oil prices jumped on the news, while safe-haven assets like gold saw modest inflows. Cryptocurrency markets, already skittish from the Clarity Act news, reacted with a sharp but brief sell-off before partially recovering.

Bitcoin Price Reacts

Bitcoin price slipped by nearly 3% in the hours following the twin headlines. The digital asset traded around $63,400 at the time of reporting, down from intraday highs above $65,000. Analysts say the combined pressure of regulatory disappointment and geopolitical fear weighed heavily on sentiment.

Leveraged long positions took a hit, with over $120 million in BTC futures liquidated across exchanges. Traders are now eyeing the $62,000 support level as a critical line in the sand.

Ethereum and other altcoins also fell, though losses were less severe. The total crypto market cap dipped below $2.3 trillion before steadying.

What to Watch

All eyes are now on Washington and the Middle East. If the Clarity Act is formally shelved or significantly watered down, the regulatory vacuum could persist for months. Simultaneously, any further US military action against Iran could send oil and risk assets into a tailspin.

Bitcoin's next move will likely depend on whether the geopolitical crisis deepens or de-escalates. Traders should brace for continued volatility.
