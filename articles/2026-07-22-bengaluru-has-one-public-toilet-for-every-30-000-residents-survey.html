Bengaluru Has One Public Toilet for Every 30,000 Residents: Survey

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru has only one public toilet for every 30,000 residents, far below the national standard.
  • The survey covered nearly 200 wards and found severe shortages, especially in low-income areas.
  • Women face the worst lack of access, with very few female-only or well-maintained toilets available.

Bengaluru, often called India's Silicon Valley, is facing a basic civic crisis that no amount of tech can fix. A new survey has revealed that the city has just one public toilet for every 30,000 residents. That is a ratio far worse than the national standard of one toilet for every 500 to 1,000 people.

The findings, released by a city-based civic research group, paint a grim picture of sanitation access in the country's third most populous city. Researchers mapped public toilet availability across nearly 200 wards and found that most areas fall drastically short.

Severe Shortages in Low-Income Wards

The survey found that the shortage is not uniform. Several wards, particularly in the city's outer and low-income zones, have no functional public toilets at all. Residents in these areas are forced to rely on shared household facilities or resort to open defecation.

In some wards, the gap between the number of toilets needed and what is available is as high as 90 percent. This places a heavy burden on daily-wage workers, street vendors, and migrant labourers who spend long hours outside their homes.

Women Bear the Brunt of the Crisis

The lack of safe, clean public toilets affects women disproportionately. The survey noted that most existing public toilets are male-oriented, with very few female-only facilities. Women often have to walk long distances or queue for long periods, which leads to health issues and loss of work hours.

Many women simply avoid drinking water during the day to avoid using public toilets, a practice that leads to dehydration and urinary tract infections. The report calls for urgent installation of well-lit, women-only toilets across the city.

Maintenance and Safety Also a Concern

Even where public toilets exist, the survey found that maintenance is often poor. Many are locked, broken, or lack running water. Some are unsafe to use, especially at night, raising concerns about women's safety.

The researchers also noted that no clear system exists for regular cleaning or repairs. While the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has allocated funds for sanitation, the survey suggests that implementation on the ground remains weak.

What the City Needs

To meet the national standard, Bengaluru would need to build thousands of new public toilets. The survey recommends that the BBMP prioritise high-footfall areas such as bus stands, markets, and industrial zones.

It also suggests involving local resident welfare associations and self-help groups in maintenance, and using technology — such as a mobile app to locate and rate toilets — to improve accountability.

The findings come at a time when the city is already grappling with water shortages and waste management issues. Sanitation, experts say, is the missing piece of the puzzle that affects public health, dignity, and gender equality.

The BBMP has not yet formally responded to the survey. With municipal elections due next year, the issue is likely to become a key talking point for political candidates. Activists plan to take the findings to the Karnataka High Court if no action is taken within three months.
