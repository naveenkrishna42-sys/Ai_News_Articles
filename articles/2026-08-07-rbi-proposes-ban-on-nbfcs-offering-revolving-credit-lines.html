RBI proposes ban on NBFCs offering revolving credit lines

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has proposed a ban on NBFCs offering revolving credit products.
  • The move could affect fintech firms and digital lenders that rely on such credit lines.
  • Industry experts await final guidelines, with consultations expected.
  • Consumers may see changes in how credit cards and buy-now-pay-later schemes are offered.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has floated a proposal to prohibit non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) from offering revolving credit facilities. If implemented, the move would bar NBFCs from extending credit lines that allow borrowers to draw, repay, and redraw funds up to a sanctioned limit, a feature common in credit cards and certain digital lending products.

The proposal, reported by Financial Express, signals a tightening of the regulatory grip on a segment that has grown rapidly in recent years. NBFCs, including those backed by fintech firms, have increasingly used revolving credit to offer flexible borrowing options to consumers and small businesses.

What is revolving credit?

Revolving credit is a type of credit where the borrower has access to a set limit, can use funds as needed, and repays either in full or in installments. Interest is charged only on the amount actually used. Credit cards are the most common example, but many NBFCs have launched their own versions, often through digital apps.

Unlike a term loan, where the borrower receives a lump sum and repays it over a fixed period, revolving credit offers ongoing flexibility. This has made it popular among millennials and small business owners who prefer to borrow on demand.

Why is the RBI proposing this?

The RBI has not publicly detailed the rationale behind the proposal, but regulatory sources indicate concerns about consumer over-leveraging and the risk of unsecured credit piling up in the system. NBFCs, which are not subject to the same prudential norms as banks, have been able to offer these products with relatively lighter oversight.

There is also a worry about the growing interdependence between NBFCs and fintech platforms, which often market these credit lines aggressively. The central bank may be seeking to curb potential systemic risks that could emerge if a large number of borrowers default simultaneously.

Impact on fintech and consumers

If the ban comes into force, fintech companies that have built their business models around revolving credit lines could face significant disruption. Many such firms partner with NBFCs to offer 'buy now, pay later' (BNPL) options and instant credit lines, which would likely need to be restructured.

Consumers, too, may see a change in the availability of such products. While banks would still be allowed to offer revolving credit through credit cards and overdraft facilities, the range of options from non-bank players could shrink. This could reduce competition and, in turn, affect pricing and innovation in the market.

What happens next?

The RBI's proposal is at a consultative stage, and industry stakeholders are expected to respond before any final guidelines are issued. The central bank typically invites comments on such discussion papers, and the final rules may be modified based on feedback.

Observers believe the RBI may carve out exceptions for certain types of NBFCs, such as those focused on infrastructure or asset-backed lending, where revolving credit is not the primary product. However, for consumer-focused NBFCs, the writing appears to be on the wall.

As the consultation unfolds, the sector will be watching closely to see whether the central bank softens its stance or holds firm. The outcome will shape the future of digital lending in India and determine how fintech firms adapt to a more regulated environment.
