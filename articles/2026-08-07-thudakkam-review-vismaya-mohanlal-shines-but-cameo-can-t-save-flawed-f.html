Thudakkam review: Vismaya Mohanlal shines, but cameo can't save flawed film

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vismaya Mohanlal delivers a standout performance in Thudakkam
  • Mohanlal's cameo is brief and unable to salvage the film's issues
  • The film struggles with pacing and narrative coherence despite strong acting

A Promising Start, a Stumbling Middle

Thudakkam arrives with a title that promises a fresh beginning, and in many ways, it delivers—at least in its opening reels. The film introduces us to a world that feels familiar yet intriguing, setting up a story that seems ready to explore complex emotions. But as the narrative unfolds, the initial promise gives way to a sense of déjà vu, and the plot begins to lose its grip.

The director clearly has ambition, but the execution leaves much to be desired. Scenes that should land with emotional weight often feel rushed, while others drag on without purpose. It is a frustrating experience, because you can see the potential buried beneath the surface, waiting to be realised.

Vismaya Mohanlal: The Heart of the Film

If there is one reason to watch Thudakkam, it is Vismaya Mohanlal. She brings a natural ease to her role, infusing her character with a vulnerability that feels authentic. Her expressions speak volumes, and she manages to hold the screen even when the script lets her down.

In a film that wobbles in its storytelling, Vismaya's performance is a steady anchor. She navigates the emotional highs and lows with a maturity that belies her experience, making you wish the rest of the film matched her level of craft. It is a star-making turn, even if the vehicle around her is not worthy of her talent.

Mohanlal's Cameo: A Missed Opportunity

The big talking point, of course, is Mohanlal's cameo. Fans will flock to theatres hoping to catch a glimpse of the superstar, and he does not disappoint in terms of screen presence. His brief appearance is electric, reminding you of his immense charisma and why he remains a beloved figure in Malayalam cinema.

But here is the rub: the cameo feels tacked on, almost as if it was inserted to generate buzz rather than serve the story. It does not elevate the film or add depth to the narrative. Instead, it feels like a distraction, a moment that makes you wish the film had focused more on its core characters rather than relying on a familiar face to draw crowds.

What Went Wrong?

The flaws in Thudakkam are not hard to spot. The screenplay meanders, introducing subplots that go nowhere and characters that remain underdeveloped. The pacing is uneven, with the middle act particularly suffering from a lack of direction. You find yourself checking the time, waiting for the story to find its footing again.

There are also tonal shifts that feel jarring. The film tries to balance drama and light-hearted moments, but the transition is often clumsy. What could have been a tight, emotionally resonant drama ends up feeling like a patchwork of ideas that never quite coalesce.

The Technical Side

On a technical level, Thudakkam has its merits. The cinematography captures the mood of the setting well, and the background score adds a layer of texture to key scenes. However, these elements cannot compensate for the narrative shortcomings.

The editing, too, could have been tighter. Several scenes overstay their welcome, and a more disciplined approach might have helped the film maintain its momentum. As it stands, Thudakkam feels longer than it actually is, a testament to its pacing issues.

The Verdict

Thudakkam is a film that will likely divide opinions. For Vismaya Mohanlal's performance alone, it deserves a watch. But for those expecting a cohesive, engaging story, it may leave you wanting more. It is a reminder that even the most talented actors cannot save a film that is fundamentally flawed at its core.

As the credits roll, you are left with a sense of what could have been. The film has its moments, but they are few and far between. It is a middling effort that will be remembered more for its casting than its craft.

What remains to be seen is how audiences respond. Will the star power of Mohanlal's cameo be enough to draw crowds, or will word-of-mouth about the film's flaws keep them away? Only time will tell if Thudakkam finds its audience or fades into obscurity.
