Kuwait Rations Electricity After Iranian Strike Damages Power and Water Plants

📅 2026-07-21 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iranian attacks hit several power and water desalination plants in Kuwait.
  • Kuwait imposes electricity rationing and urges citizens to conserve energy.
  • Officials have not yet confirmed the full extent of damage or restoration timelines.

Kuwait is rationing electricity after Iranian strikes damaged several critical power and water desalination plants, plunging parts of the country into uncertainty. The attacks, part of a broader escalation between Iran and its regional rivals, have left Kuwait's grid struggling to meet demand.

Power Plants Hit in Overnight Strikes

Iranian attacks targeted key infrastructure overnight, hitting multiple power generation facilities and desalination plants. The strikes have directly disrupted the supply of electricity and fresh water, both essential for daily life in the Gulf state.

Kuwaiti authorities confirmed the damage but have not released a full list of affected sites. The government has not yet provided a timeline for repairs or restoration of full capacity.

Government Calls for Conservation

In response, the Kuwaiti government has issued an urgent appeal for citizens and residents to reduce electricity consumption. Officials have asked people to limit use of air conditioning, avoid running appliances during peak hours, and turn off unnecessary lights.

“We are facing an extraordinary situation,” a government spokesperson said, without giving further details. The plea for conservation suggests the damage is significant enough to strain the grid for days or weeks.

Water Supply Also at Risk

The damage to desalination plants adds a second layer of crisis. Kuwait relies almost entirely on desalination for its drinking water. With plants hit, authorities have warned of potential water shortages if the situation is not brought under control quickly.

Residents have begun stockpiling bottled water, and long queues have been reported at supermarkets. The government has assured the public that emergency reserves are being tapped, but rationing may be necessary.

Regional War Escalates

The attacks are part of a wider conflict between Iran and several Gulf states. Kuwait, which hosts a major US military presence, has been drawn into the fighting. The strikes mark a significant escalation, targeting civilian infrastructure rather than purely military assets.

International observers have condemned the attacks. The United Nations has called for an immediate de-escalation, but fighting continues across the region. Kuwait's allies have not yet announced any direct military support.

What happens next depends on whether Iran launches more strikes or if diplomatic channels can open. Kuwait's grid remains fragile, and the coming days will test the country's ability to keep lights and taps running.
