Sensex, Nifty week ahead: Q1 earnings, global tech selloff, US-Iran war among key triggers

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Q1 earnings season continues with major banks and IT companies reporting this week.
  • Global tech selloff and US-Iran war tensions add uncertainty to market direction.
  • Rising crude oil prices and FII activity will influence market sentiment.
  • IPO listings and West Asia developments are additional factors to watch.

Indian equity benchmarks Sensex and Nifty are set for a volatile week ahead as investors juggle a host of domestic and global factors. The primary triggers include the ongoing Q1 earnings season, a global technology stock selloff, escalating US-Iran tensions, and a sharp spike in crude oil prices. Market participants are also closely watching foreign institutional investor (FII) flows and a busy IPO calendar.

Q1 earnings season in focus

The first-quarter earnings reports will remain the dominant domestic driver this week. Several heavyweight companies, particularly from the banking and IT sectors, are scheduled to announce their results. Analysts say the performance of these sectors will set the tone for the broader market.

Banking stocks, which have a significant weight on the indices, are under particular scrutiny. Investors want to see if net interest margins and asset quality have held up in the current economic environment. Any disappointment could trigger a selloff in the banking pack and drag the Nifty lower.

Global tech selloff and US-Iran war fears

Global markets ended last week on a weak note, led by a sharp decline in technology stocks. The selloff was triggered by disappointing earnings from some of the largest US tech firms. This weakness could spill over to Indian IT stocks, which are already facing headwinds from a potential slowdown in client spending.

Adding to the uncertainty is the escalating conflict between the United States and Iran. The geopolitical risk has pushed crude oil prices higher, raising concerns about inflation and its impact on India's import bill. A sustained rise in oil prices could pressure the rupee and force the Reserve Bank of India to maintain a hawkish stance on interest rates.

FII activity and IPO calendar

Foreign institutional investors have been net sellers in recent sessions, and their behaviour this week will be critical. If FIIs continue to pull money out of Indian equities, it could cap any upside in the indices. Conversely, a reversal in FII flows could provide strong support.

On the primary market front, several initial public offerings are lined up for listing this week. The performance of these new listings will offer a read on retail investor sentiment and the overall appetite for risk.

Crude oil and West Asia developments

The sharp jump in crude oil prices is a key concern for the Indian economy. Higher oil prices increase input costs for companies and widen the current account deficit. Market watchers will track any diplomatic moves in West Asia that could de-escalate tensions and cool oil prices.

Domestic macroeconomic data, including industrial production figures, is also due later in the week. This data will provide a clearer picture of the health of the Indian economy amid global headwinds.

The week ahead promises to be a test of resilience for Indian markets. The direction will likely be determined by how corporate earnings hold up against global shocks.

Investors will watch the Nifty's ability to hold key support levels, while a break above resistance could signal renewed optimism. The interplay between domestic earnings and global cues will define the market's next move.
