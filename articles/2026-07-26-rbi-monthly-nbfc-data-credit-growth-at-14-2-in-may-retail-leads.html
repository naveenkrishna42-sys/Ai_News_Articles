RBI monthly NBFC data: credit growth at 14.2% in May, retail leads

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • NBFC credit grew 14.2% year-on-year in May 2026, the first monthly data release by RBI.
  • Retail lending was the primary driver, outpacing other segments.
  • The data provides granular, timely oversight of non-bank financial sector trends.

RBI publishes first monthly NBFC credit snapshot

The Reserve Bank of India released its maiden monthly data series on non-banking financial company (NBFC) credit on Tuesday. The data for May 2026 shows NBFC credit outstanding grew 14.2% year-on-year. This marks a new level of transparency in tracking the shadow banking sector.

The monthly series supplements the existing quarterly data. It gives policymakers and analysts a more frequent read on lending trends outside the traditional banking system. The May numbers set a baseline for future comparisons.

Retail lending fuels the growth engine

Retail loans formed the backbone of the expansion. Personal loans, home loans and vehicle finance recorded strong double-digit growth. The RBI data indicates that retail credit now accounts for over half of total NBFC loan books.

Small-ticket consumer loans and gold loans also contributed. NBFCs have been aggressively targeting the retail segment, where banks have tightened credit norms in recent quarters. This shift has helped non-bank lenders gain market share.

Wholesale and infrastructure credit remain subdued

While retail surged, wholesale lending to corporates grew at a much slower pace. Loans for infrastructure and real estate projects registered single-digit growth. NBFCs remain cautious on large-ticket exposures amid rising input costs and project delays.

Commercial vehicle and construction equipment finance, however, showed moderate improvement. Analysts say the divergence between retail and wholesale reflects a broader risk-aversion in the corporate lending space. The RBI's tight monetary stance has also raised funding costs for NBFCs.

Data granularity aids supervisory vigilance

The monthly data breaks down credit by sector, region and loan size. This level of detail was previously unavailable. The RBI can now spot early signs of stress in specific pockets, such as unsecured consumer loans or regional NBFCs.

For market participants, the data offers a real-time barometer of credit demand and NBFC health. The central bank has indicated it will release the series on a regular basis. This is part of a broader push to strengthen financial sector surveillance after the IL&FS crisis.

NBFCs have been raising capital through bonds and bank borrowings to fund the retail push. The 14.2% growth rate is higher than the overall banking credit growth of around 11% in the same period. This underscores the rising importance of NBFCs in India's financial intermediation.

The RBI has advised NBFCs to maintain adequate capital buffers and liquidity coverage. It is watching the unsecured retail segment closely for any signs of overheating. The monthly data will help in calibrating any preemptive regulatory measures.

Going forward, the RBI plans to expand the dataset to include asset quality metrics, such as non-performing loan ratios, in later releases. That will give a fuller picture of the health of NBFC credit. For now, the headline growth figure suggests the sector is on a solid footing, but the composition of that growth—retail-heavy and wholesale-light—warrants continued monitoring.
