Hydrogen Train vs Vande Bharat: Ticket Price Comparison

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • India’s first hydrogen train was flagged off by the Prime Minister.
  • The train is expected to have lower ticket prices than Vande Bharat.
  • It will initially operate on heritage and tourist routes.

Ticket Prices Compared

India’s first hydrogen train is here, and passengers are curious about the cost. Early estimates suggest the hydrogen train’s fares will be significantly lower than the Vande Bharat Express. Reports indicate a base fare of around ₹1.50 per kilometre for the hydrogen train, compared to Vande Bharat’s ₹2.50 to ₹3 per kilometre.

This makes the hydrogen train a cheaper option for long-distance travel. The exact pricing structure will be confirmed by Indian Railways once commercial operations begin on specific routes.

Route and Operations

The hydrogen train was flagged off by the Prime Minister during a function in Haryana. District-level events were held across the state to mark the occasion. The train is designed for non-electrified routes, making it ideal for heritage lines and hilly areas.

One of the first routes likely to see hydrogen trains is the Matheran Hill Railway in Maharashtra. This heritage line currently uses diesel locomotives. The shift to hydrogen will reduce pollution and noise, preserving the scenic experience for tourists.

Technology and Maintenance

The train uses hydrogen fuel cells to generate electricity. The only byproduct is water vapour, making it a zero-emission mode of transport. Indian Railways has partnered with multiple research institutes to develop the technology locally.

Refuelling stations will be set up at select depots. The initial rollout will be on short-distance tourist routes to test the infrastructure. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of trains or the full list of routes for commercial deployment.

Political Context

The launch had a political edge. The Prime Minister took a jibe at the Congress and the Aam Aadmi Party during the event, accusing them of neglecting railway modernisation. The opposition parties criticised the government for delays in the project.

The hydrogen train project was first announced in 2020. It faced several delays due to technology transfer issues and funding. The current administration has pushed for faster execution ahead of the next general elections.

What to Watch

Indian Railways will release the full timetable and fare chart for the hydrogen train in the coming weeks. The success of the Matheran trial will determine whether the technology is expanded to other heritage routes like the Nilgiri Mountain Railway. For now, passengers can expect a greener and cheaper ride.
