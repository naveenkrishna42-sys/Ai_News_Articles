Amit Shah: Bengal's Political Shift Unmatched Since Independence

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Amit Shah stated no state has seen political change like Bengal since Independence.
  • He said the West Bengal BJP government is working on the 'Sonar Bangla' promise.
  • Shah visited the 'Chicken's Neck' corridor and reviewed border security.
  • He announced a smart border grid plan to be first implemented in West Bengal.

Union Home Minister Amit Shah on Sunday asserted that the political transformation in West Bengal is unlike any other state has witnessed since India's Independence. Speaking during a visit to the state, Shah credited the BJP government in Bengal with fulfilling the promise of 'Sonar Bangla' — a vision of a golden Bengal.

'Unprecedented Political Change'

Shah's remarks came as he addressed a public gathering, where he drew a sharp contrast between the current administration and previous regimes. 'No state has witnessed such a political change since Independence,' Shah said, referring to the BJP's rise to power in the state.

The Home Minister did not elaborate on specific metrics but framed the shift as a fundamental realignment of Bengal's political culture. He argued that the BJP government has brought development and transparency where earlier there was stagnation.

Focus on 'Sonar Bangla' Promise

Shah emphasised that the West Bengal BJP government is actively implementing the 'Sonar Bangla' vision. This phrase, historically associated with Bengali cultural identity, was adopted by the BJP as a campaign slogan promising economic revival and good governance.

He did not provide a detailed timeline or project list but linked the concept to broader national development goals. The statement appears aimed at consolidating the party's base in a state where it faces stiff opposition from regional parties.

Border Security Review at 'Chicken's Neck'

Earlier in the day, Shah visited the India-Bangladesh border along the strategic 'Chicken's Neck' corridor. This narrow strip of land in northern West Bengal connects the country's northeast to the rest of India and is considered a vital security zone.

The Home Minister reviewed the security posture of Border Security Force (BSF) personnel deployed in the area. Officials familiar with the visit said Shah inspected border fencing, surveillance infrastructure, and troop readiness along the international border.

Smart Border Grid First in Bengal

Shah also announced that the central government's ambitious smart border grid project will first be implemented in West Bengal. The grid is designed to integrate advanced surveillance technologies, including sensors, cameras, and drones, to monitor the border in real time.

'The smart border grid will enhance our ability to detect and prevent illegal cross-border activities,' Shah said. The decision to prioritise West Bengal highlights the government's focus on securing this sensitive frontier, which has seen issues related to smuggling and infiltration.

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for the grid's installation. However, the announcement signals a push to modernise border management in the region.

The Home Minister's visit and statements come at a time when West Bengal remains a politically charged state. The BJP is working to consolidate its hold on power while the opposition accuses it of failing on key promises.

What to watch now: The implementation timeline of the smart border grid in West Bengal and whether the 'Sonar Bangla' development agenda translates into visible infrastructure and economic gains before the next election cycle.
