Karoline Leavitt steps down as White House press secretary

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Karoline Leavitt to leave post at end of month
  • She was the youngest press secretary in history
  • Trump announced the move; successor not yet named

White House podium sees change

President Donald Trump announced on Wednesday that Karoline Leavitt will step down as White House press secretary at the end of the month. The move brings to a close a 20-month tenure marked by combative briefings and a staunch defence of the administration's agenda.

Leavitt, who at 28 became the youngest person to hold the role, has been a fixture at the briefing room podium since early 2025. Her exit was confirmed by Trump in a statement, though no successor has been named yet.

A record-breaking tenure

Leavitt's appointment had broken barriers. She took over the job with no prior White House experience but quickly became one of the most recognisable faces of the administration. Her daily briefings often drew sharp exchanges with reporters, and she rarely shied from pushing back on tough questions.

During her time in office, she handled some of the most turbulent news cycles, including trade negotiations, immigration policy, and a series of high-profile cabinet shake-ups. Her style was unapologetic, and she earned both praise from conservatives and criticism from press advocates.

What she leaves behind

Her departure at month's end will leave a void in the communications team. Leavitt was known for her tight control over messaging and her ability to pivot quickly on breaking stories. She also modernised the briefing format, incorporating social media questions and direct video feeds.

Analysts note that her exit comes at a critical time, with midterm elections approaching and several policy battles heating up in Congress. The next press secretary will have to hit the ground running.

Reactions and next steps

No official reason was given for her departure. Some reports suggest she may be eyeing a role in a political action committee or a media venture, but nothing has been confirmed. Her office did not respond to requests for comment.

White House insiders say the search for a replacement is underway, with several senior communications aides considered frontrunners. The president is expected to make an announcement in the coming days.

As Leavitt prepares to leave, her legacy is likely to be debated for some time. She was a trailblazer in age and style, and her impact on the press secretary role will be felt for years.

The transition will be closely watched in Washington, where the briefing room remains a key battleground for public opinion. What happens next will depend on who steps into the role and how they handle the mounting pressures of the job.
