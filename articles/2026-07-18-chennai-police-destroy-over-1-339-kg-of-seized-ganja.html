Chennai Police Destroy Over 1,339 kg of Seized Ganja

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 1,339 kg of ganja destroyed by Greater Chennai Police in a single operation.
  • Drugs were from multiple cases registered since 2023, stored as evidence.
  • Destruction carried out at a waste-to-energy plant after court approval.

Massive Drug Stockpile Incinerated

The Greater Chennai Police on Friday destroyed more than 1,339 kilograms of ganja that had been seized in various operations over the past few years. The drugs were incinerated at the city's solid waste management facility in Kodungaiyur.

Police officials said the consignment represented a significant portion of the narcotics that had been stored as evidence in multiple cases registered since 2023. The destruction was carried out after obtaining necessary court orders for each batch.

Evidence Cleared, Court Orders Secured

The drugs were part of over 40 separate cases, many of which have already concluded or are at advanced stages of trial. With no further requirement to retain the evidence, the police moved to dispose of the stockpile to free up storage space and prevent any potential misuse.

A senior officer confirmed that the process was video-recorded and supervised by a magistrate-nominated committee. The incineration was conducted at the Kodungaiyur waste-to-energy plant, which can handle such materials under controlled conditions.

Part of Ongoing Anti-Drug Campaign

The destruction comes amid a larger push by Tamil Nadu police to crack down on drug trafficking networks operating through the city and its suburbs. Over the last year, the force has intensified seizures along major transit routes, including the Chennai-Bengaluru Highway and the Chennai Port.

In several instances, the contraband was being smuggled from neighbouring states like Andhra Pradesh and Odisha, both known for illicit ganja cultivation. The Greater Chennai Police have also been coordinating with central agencies to track supply chains.

Officials noted that such large-scale destruction serves as a deterrent and sends a clear signal to traffickers. However, they did not disclose the estimated street value of the destroyed drugs.

Storage and Disposal Challenges

Police stations often struggle with storing large quantities of seized narcotics for years until trials conclude. The accumulation can become a security risk and an administrative burden. The Kodungaiyur plant has emerged as the preferred disposal site for such operations because it ensures complete destruction and generates energy in the process.

Earlier this year, the city police had destroyed smaller quantities of heroin and other synthetic drugs using the same facility. Friday's operation was one of the largest single-day destructions of ganja by the force.

Officials said they will continue to seek court permissions for disposing of other seized narcotics in a phased manner.

What Comes Next

The Greater Chennai Police are expected to carry out similar destruction drives in the coming months as more cases reach conclusion. The focus remains on keeping the city's streets free of illicit drugs while ensuring that judicial processes are not compromised.
