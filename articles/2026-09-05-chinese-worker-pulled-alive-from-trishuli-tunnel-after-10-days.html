Chinese Worker Pulled Alive from Trishuli Tunnel After 10 Days

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-05 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Chinese national was rescued on day 10 from a Trishuli hydro-project tunnel that flooded on August 26, 2026
  • Nepali Army engineers, divers, and Chinese embassy officials coordinated the extraction using drill rigs and manual cutting tools
  • The survivor was airlifted to Kathmandu and admitted to hospital in stable but dehydrated condition
  • At least two more people were located alive in adjacent sections, raising hopes for further extractions

Background of the Trishuli Tunnel Flood

A Chinese national employed at an under-construction hydropower site in central Nepal was extracted alive on September 4, 2026, more than 10 days after a flash flood inundated the Trishuli tunnel network in Nuwakot district. The survivor had been trapped inside an approximately 800-metre section of the headrace tunnel when a sudden cloudburst on August 25, 2026 sent a torrent of debris-laden water through the worksite.

Initial search efforts focused on draining the lower shaft, but silt deposits and recurring seepage slowed progress. By day six, rescue coordinators switched to a vertical drilling approach after surface ground-penetrating radar indicated an air pocket near the tunnel crown.

Rescue Timeline and Verified Coordinates

Officials from Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA) confirmed the operation ran across three 18-hour shifts per day, sustained by teams drawn from the Nepali Army, Armed Police Force, and a technical liaison sent by the Chinese Embassy in Kathmandu.

Key milestones, as reported by Reuters and corroborated by The Guardian:

  • August 25, 2026: Cloudburst over the Trishuli basin; inflow estimated at 220 cumecs at peak
  • August 27, 2026: Pumping stations established at the lower portal; debris clearing begins
  • August 30, 2026: Acoustic and thermal sensors detect a faint response from the trapped worker
  • September 1, 2026: Vertical borehole reaches the air pocket; food, water, and an oxygen line are lowered
  • September 4, 2026 (Day 10): Worker extracted through a widened vertical shaft and airlifted to Kathmandu

Why Survival for 10 Days Was Physically Possible

Medical specialists consulted by the rescue team attributed the outcome to a combination of factors that are increasingly cited in confined-space survival literature. An elevated air pocket above the flood line prevented drowning, while tunnel temperatures around 16-18 °C slowed dehydration. The survivor reportedly rationed residual drinking water and sheltered behind a steel formwork panel that retained residual oxygen.

"Ten days is at the outer edge of documented tunnel-survival cases, but the physics line up when the air column is sealed from above and ambient temperature stays cool," noted a senior emergency-medicine consultant familiar with the case briefing.

Search for Additional Survivors Continues

Separately, Nepali Army divers recovered the body of one Nepali worker from the same tunnel earlier this week. Two more individuals, including a Nepali technician, have been located alive in adjacent sections, and extraction drilling is expected to continue through the week. Official casualty figures for the broader Trishuli basin floods remain under review by the Ministry of Home Affairs.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Vertical drilling bypassed silt-blocked horizontal access, cutting extraction time by an estimated 48 hours
  • Cross-border coordination with the Chinese Embassy enabled rapid supply of specialised drilling bits and medical evacuation assets
  • Survivor had access to a 2-metre air pocket and an estimated 3 litres of residual water
  • Acoustic sensors first pinpointed the survivor on day five, before visual confirmation on day eight
  • Two additional persons remain located alive; recovery expected within 72 hours as of publication
