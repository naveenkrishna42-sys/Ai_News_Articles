Dow plunges 875 points, S&P 500 and Nasdaq fall as Trump threatens Iran strikes, oil surges

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 875 points, or roughly 2.3%, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq also saw sharp losses.
  • President Donald Trump threatened military strikes against Iran, sending crude oil prices surging.
  • Investors are also focused on the Federal Reserve's upcoming interest rate decision and a heavy week of corporate earnings, including AI stocks.

Wall Street had a brutal day on Wednesday. The Dow Jones Industrial Average tumbled 875 points, landing in negative territory as a fresh geopolitical threat shook markets already on edge.

The broader S&P 500 and the tech-heavy Nasdaq also skidded sharply. The sell-off wiped out gains from earlier in the week, leaving investors scrambling for safety.

Trump threatens Iran, oil spikes

The trigger was a direct threat from President Donald Trump. He warned of impending military strikes against Iran, escalating tensions in the Middle East. The news sent crude oil prices jumping more than 4% in afternoon trading.

A spike in oil prices is rarely good news for the stock market. It raises the cost of fuel for businesses and consumers, feeding into inflation concerns that the Federal Reserve is already battling.

Fed decision and earnings in focus

All of this is happening just hours before the Federal Reserve is set to announce its latest interest rate decision. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is scheduled to speak after the decision, and markets are bracing for any signal on the future path of rates.

Adding to the pressure, this is a heavy week for corporate earnings. Major AI-related companies are reporting results, and their performance is seen as a key test for the market's high valuations in the tech sector. The combination of geopolitics, monetary policy, and earnings has created a perfect storm of uncertainty.

The losses were broad-based. Nearly every sector in the S&P 500 ended the day in the red. Energy stocks were a rare bright spot, riding the wave of higher oil prices, but they could not offset the selling pressure elsewhere.

Analysts pointed to the sudden nature of the Iran threat as the main catalyst. Markets hate surprises, especially when they involve potential conflict and supply disruptions. The jump in oil prices acts like a tax on the economy, threatening to slow growth just as the Fed tries to calibrate its policy.

Bond yields also moved, reflecting the flight to safety. Investors moved money into US Treasuries, pushing yields lower. The VIX, Wall Street's fear gauge, spiked to its highest level in weeks.

What to watch

All eyes are now on the Fed's statement and Chair Warsh's press conference. Markets are pricing in a rate cut, but the bigger question is what the central bank signals about the months ahead. A hawkish tone, combined with rising oil prices, could mean more volatility.
