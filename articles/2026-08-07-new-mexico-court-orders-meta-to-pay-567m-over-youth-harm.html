New Mexico court orders Meta to pay $567m over youth harm

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • New Mexico court orders Meta to pay $567 million in child safety lawsuit
  • Ruling addresses alleged harm to young users' mental health
  • Penalty framed as abatement of public nuisance, largest such ruling
  • Meta has not yet confirmed next steps, likely to appeal

Landmark penalty against Meta

A New Mexico court has ordered Meta Platforms to pay $567 million in a case alleging the social media giant harmed young users' mental health. The ruling, delivered on Thursday, marks one of the largest child safety judgments against a tech company in the United States.

The penalty is tied to claims that Meta's platforms, including Facebook and Instagram, contributed to a public nuisance by designing features that allegedly led to addictive behaviour among minors. The court's order frames the payment as an abatement of that nuisance, a legal remedy aimed at correcting ongoing harm.

What the lawsuit alleged

The lawsuit, filed by the state of New Mexico, accused Meta of failing to protect children from harmful content and manipulative design. State attorneys argued that the company prioritised engagement over user safety, exposing young people to content that could exacerbate anxiety, depression, and other mental health issues.

Court documents reportedly detailed internal Meta communications suggesting the company was aware of potential risks to younger users but did not take sufficient action. The state sought financial penalties and injunctive relief to force changes in platform design.

Reactions and implications

Advocates for child safety online have welcomed the ruling, calling it a significant step toward holding tech firms accountable. Legal experts note that the 'public nuisance' framing could set a precedent for other states pursuing similar cases against major platforms.

Meta has not yet issued a detailed public statement on the judgment. The company had previously argued that its platforms have built-in safety features and that it has invested heavily in child protection tools. Officials have not confirmed whether the firm will appeal, though legal observers expect a challenge given the scale of the payout.

A broader pattern

This is not the first legal setback for Meta over youth safety. The company has faced multiple lawsuits from US states and school districts alleging similar harms. In recent years, federal and state regulators have ramped up scrutiny of how social media companies design products for minors.

The $567 million figure, while substantial, is a fraction of Meta's annual revenue, which runs into tens of billions of dollars. Still, the ruling underscores a growing judicial willingness to impose financial consequences for alleged platform design flaws.

The court's decision could also influence ongoing legislative efforts in the US to regulate children's online safety. Several bills pending in Congress propose stricter design standards and higher penalties for violations, though none have yet become law.

What remains unclear is how Meta will respond operationally. The company may be forced to alter certain features for minors in New Mexico, or potentially nationwide, depending on the court's final order. Details of any mandated changes have not yet been released.

As the case moves toward possible appeal, child safety groups are likely to press for swift enforcement of the court's ruling. The outcome could shape how other states approach litigation against tech giants, and whether financial penalties become a routine tool in protecting young users online.

For now, all eyes are on Meta's next legal move and whether this judgment spurs similar rulings elsewhere. The coming months will reveal if this landmark penalty becomes a turning point in the broader fight over social media's impact on youth.
