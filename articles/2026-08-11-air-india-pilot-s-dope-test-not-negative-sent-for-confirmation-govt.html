Air India pilot's dope test 'not negative', sent for confirmation: Govt

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pilot's dope test 'not negative', sent for confirmation
  • Air India Phuket-Delhi flight faced rare flight-control event before 300-foot drop
  • Airbus and French agency to join probe into the incident

Pilot's test result under scrutiny

The government on Sunday said the dope test of the pilot involved in the turbulent Air India flight from Phuket to Delhi has come back 'not negative'. The sample has been sent for confirmation, officials said.

The development adds a new layer to an already complex investigation into the incident, which saw the aircraft suffer a rare flight-control event before a sudden 300-foot altitude drop.

What happened on the flight

The Air India flight, operating between Phuket and Delhi, encountered severe turbulence that led to a dramatic loss of altitude. Passengers on board reported moments of panic as the aircraft plunged before the crew managed to stabilise it.

Aviation experts have described the event as a 'rare flight-control event', distinguishing it from ordinary turbulence. The distinction matters because it shifts the focus from weather conditions to potential technical or operational factors.

Investigation widens

Probe teams are now looking beyond the immediate weather angle. Airbus, the aircraft manufacturer, and French aviation authorities are set to join the investigation, according to reports. Their involvement signals that the incident is being treated with serious technical scrutiny.

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has already begun a detailed review of the flight's data recorders and maintenance logs. The pilot's medical and professional records are also under examination.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the 'not negative' result points to a specific substance. They have, however, noted that common medications, including some over-the-counter drugs, can trigger false positives in initial screening tests.

Medicine advisory for pilots

In a related development, a medicine advisory for pilots has highlighted that common drugs may lead to false-positive results in dope tests. This has raised questions about the reliability of preliminary screenings in aviation settings.

The advisory, issued by medical experts, urges caution in interpreting test results without confirmatory analysis. It also underscores the need for pilots to report any medication they are taking, even those available without a prescription.

The confirmation test will be crucial in determining whether the pilot had any prohibited substance in their system. Until then, authorities are refraining from drawing conclusions.

What happens next

The confirmatory test results are expected to take a few days. In the meantime, the pilot has been taken off flying duties, a standard precautionary measure during such investigations.

Air India has said it is cooperating fully with the authorities. The airline has not commented on the pilot's status or the test results beyond the government's statement.

With Airbus and French agencies joining the probe, the focus will now be on piecing together the sequence of events that led to the near-catastrophic drop. The outcome of the confirmatory dope test will be a key factor in determining the next steps.
