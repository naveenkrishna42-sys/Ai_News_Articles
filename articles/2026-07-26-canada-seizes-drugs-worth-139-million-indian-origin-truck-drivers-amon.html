Canada Seizes Drugs Worth $139 Million, Indian-Origin Truck Drivers Among 21 Arrested

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Canadian police seized 1.7 tonnes of illegal drugs worth $139 million in a cross-border smuggling operation.
  • 21 people have been charged, including dozens of Indian-origin truck drivers.
  • The bust has revealed cartel brokers operating within Canada's trucking industry.

Canadian authorities have dismantled a major cross-border drug smuggling network, seizing illegal drugs worth $139 million (approximately Rs 1,150 crore) and arresting 21 people. Among those charged are several Indian-origin truck drivers, according to multiple reports.

The operation, led by the Ontario Provincial Police (OPP), targeted a smuggling racket that moved narcotics between Canada and the United States. The seizure included 1.7 tonnes of illegal drugs, one of the largest hauls in recent memory.

Cartel Brokers Inside Trucking Industry

Investigators said the bust uncovered cartel brokers operating within Canada's trucking industry. These individuals allegedly used commercial trucks to transport drugs across the border, exploiting the high volume of legitimate cross-border traffic.

The Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) had reportedly noted a lack of appetite for tackling such operations in 2019. However, the OPP's recent crackdown signals a shift in priorities.

The truck drivers arrested are accused of being key links in the supply chain, moving drugs from production hubs to distribution points in both countries.

Details of the Seizure

The drugs seized include cocaine, methamphetamine, fentanyl, and other narcotics. The street value of the haul is estimated at $139 million. Law enforcement agencies in both Canada and the US collaborated on the investigation.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact nationalities of all those arrested, but reports from multiple outlets highlight the involvement of Indian-origin individuals. The Times of India reported that dozens of Indian-origin men are among the 21 charged.

Impact on the Indian Diaspora

The arrests have sent shockwaves through the Indian diaspora in Canada, particularly within the trucking community. Many Indian-origin drivers operate in the sector, and the case has raised concerns about exploitation by criminal networks.

Community leaders have urged authorities to ensure fair treatment for those arrested and to investigate whether some drivers were coerced or unaware of the illegal cargo they were transporting.

Broader Implications

The bust highlights the growing sophistication of cross-border drug smuggling operations. Cartels are increasingly using legitimate industries like trucking to move illicit goods.

Canadian police have said the investigation is ongoing and more arrests are possible. They have also called for stricter oversight of the trucking industry to prevent further infiltration by organised crime.

The US Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) has praised the operation, noting that the drugs seized could have caused significant harm in American communities.

All 21 accused are expected to appear in court in the coming weeks. Their identities and specific charges have not been fully disclosed, but legal proceedings are likely to shed more light on the network's operations.

The case is expected to prompt a broader review of border security measures and the regulation of commercial transport between the two countries.
