Iran warns Gulf states of retaliation if US strikes its power plants

📅 2026-08-06 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran has threatened to 'black out' Gulf states if the US strikes its power plants
  • Tehran is seeking regional pressure on Washington to avoid fresh US strikes
  • US President Donald Trump says the Iran campaign is 'going very well' and praised Defense Secretary Hegseth

Tehran's warning to Gulf neighbours

Iran has issued a stark warning to Gulf states, threatening to 'black out' the region if the United States launches strikes on its power plants. The threat, reported by multiple outlets, signals Tehran's attempt to widen the calculus of any fresh US military action by targeting America's regional allies.

The warning comes amid escalating hostilities in the US-Iran conflict. A senior Iranian lawmaker has also declared that the US will soon be 'expelled from the region', adding to the rhetoric that has intensified in recent days.

Seeking regional pressure on Washington

Iran's strategy appears aimed at leveraging Gulf nations' vulnerability to apply pressure on the Trump administration. By threatening critical infrastructure across the Gulf, Tehran hopes these states will urge Washington to hold back from new strikes against Iranian power plants, according to reports.

The Islamic Republic has put its neighbours on notice, framing the issue as one of collective security. Gulf capitals, which host US military bases, now face a delicate balancing act between their alliance with Washington and their proximity to a conflict that could engulf them.

Trump's assessment of the campaign

US President Donald Trump, meanwhile, has described the ongoing campaign against Iran as 'going very well'. He also praised Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth for his handling of the military operations, as per comments reported by CNBC TV18.

The White House has not provided specific details on the scope of the strikes or their assessed impact. US officials have yet to confirm any timeline for further action, leaving room for speculation about the next phase of the conflict.

Regional fallout and uncertainty

The threat to 'black out' Gulf states underscores the high stakes of the confrontation. Analysts note that any attack on power grids could have catastrophic humanitarian and economic consequences, potentially drawing in countries that have so far stayed on the sidelines.

Despite the bellicose statements from Tehran, there are no confirmed reports of imminent Iranian retaliation. The situation remains fluid, with diplomatic channels reportedly open but showing little progress.

What happens next hinges on whether Washington heeds Tehran's implicit warning or presses ahead with further strikes. The Gulf states' response to Iran's pressure campaign will be closely watched, as their actions could shape the trajectory of this conflict.
