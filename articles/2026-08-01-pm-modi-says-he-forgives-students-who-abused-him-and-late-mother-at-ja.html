PM Modi says he forgives students who abused him and late mother at Jantar Mantar

📅 2026-08-01 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi said he and his late mother were abused at Jantar Mantar
  • He expressed a desire to forgive the students, calling them 'misguided children'
  • Modi stressed that abuses solve nothing and it is society's job to guide the youth

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has broken his silence on the recent incident at Jantar Mantar, where he claimed protesters abused him and his late mother. In a video message released on Friday, Modi said he wants to forgive the students involved, describing them as "misguided children" who need guidance, not anger.

The Prime Minister's comments come amid growing political tension over the protest, which has drawn sharp reactions from across the spectrum. Modi's tone, however, was measured, focusing on reconciliation rather than retribution.

A Personal Hurt, A Public Response

Speaking directly to the camera, Modi said the abuse at the Delhi protest site targeted not just him but also the memory of his mother, who passed away years ago. "My late mother and I were abused at Jantar Mantar," he stated, his voice steady but carrying an undercurrent of emotion. He did not specify the exact words used but referred to "foul language" that has reportedly shocked many.

The Prime Minister acknowledged the pain such personal attacks can cause, yet he pivoted quickly to a broader message. "Abuses never solve anything," he said, urging citizens to focus on constructive dialogue. His remarks appear designed to lower the political temperature rather than escalate it.

'Culture Shock' and the Call to Forgive

Reports from the protest suggest that some demonstrators, believed to be students, used language that even seasoned observers found jarring. Modi referenced this, noting a "culture shock" over "daughters" using such words—an apparent reference to female protesters. He did not name anyone, but the comment has sparked debate about protest decorum and generational shifts in activism.

Despite the provocation, Modi struck a paternalistic chord. "I want to forgive them," he said, adding that it is the responsibility of elders and leaders to steer the youth back to the right path. "Our job is to guide them, not to condemn them," he asserted, framing the incident as a teachable moment rather than a criminal matter.

Political Reactions and the Road Ahead

Opposition parties have seized on the incident, questioning the government's handling of protests and free speech. Some have demanded an inquiry into how the situation escalated, while others have accused Modi of playing the victim to deflect from larger issues. The Prime Minister's office has not announced any formal action against the protesters, suggesting a deliberate choice to de-escalate.

Social media, meanwhile, is divided. Some users have praised Modi's magnanimity, while others argue that forgiveness without accountability sets a dangerous precedent. The incident has also reignited discussions about the treatment of women in public spaces, with commentators noting the irony of "daughters" being at the centre of the controversy.

What Happens Next

As the video message circulates, all eyes are on the students' response and whether the government will take any follow-up steps. Modi's call for forgiveness may open the door to dialogue, but it remains unclear if the protesters are willing to engage. For now, the Prime Minister's message stands as an olive branch—one that could either defuse the standoff or be dismissed as political theatre. The coming days will reveal which path the nation takes.
