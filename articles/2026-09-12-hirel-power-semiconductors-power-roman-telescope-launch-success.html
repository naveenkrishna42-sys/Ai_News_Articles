Why HiRel Power Semiconductors Decided the Roman Telescope Mission

NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off in early September 2026, and the mission's success rested on components most consumers never see: HiRel (high-reliability) power semiconductors. These are not ordinary chips. They are radiation-hardened, hermetically sealed devices engineered to survive the vacuum, thermal cycling and charged-particle bombardment of space for years without maintenance.

The Roman Telescope will survey the sky with a field of view roughly 100 times larger than Hubble's, hunting dark energy and exoplanets. Every instrument on board — detectors, cryocoolers, reaction wheels and communication systems — depends on power conversion electronics that must not fail. A single commercial-grade MOSFET failure could end a multi-billion-dollar mission.

What Makes a Power Semiconductor 'HiRel'?

HiRel components are screened far beyond standard commercial or automotive grades. Manufacturers subject them to radiation tolerance testing (total ionising dose and single-event effects), extended temperature cycling, burn-in, and hermetic sealing. Typical qualified operating ranges span -55°C to +125°C or beyond, compared with 0°C to 70°C for commercial parts.

Buyer Implications: Cost, Lead Time and Qualification

For procurement teams in India's ISRO ecosystem, private space startups and defence electronics firms, HiRel components represent a hard trade-off. A commercial-grade power MOSFET might cost ₹50; its space-qualified equivalent can exceed ₹5,000 per unit after screening and documentation. Lead times stretch to six months or more, forcing design teams to lock in bills of materials early.

The value justification is straightforward: mission assurance. Insurance underwriters and launch providers increasingly demand traceable, qualified components. A failed ₹5,000 part can scrap a ₹1,000-crore satellite. The Roman Telescope's successful launch reinforces that this premium is not marketing — it is engineering necessity.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Radiation-hardened HiRel parts typically tolerate 100-300 krad total ionising dose, far beyond commercial chips.
  • Space-grade screening adds substantial cost — often 10x to 100x the commercial unit price.
  • Lead times of 26-52 weeks mean HiRel components must be ordered long before integration begins.
  • Hermetic sealing and extended temperature qualification (-55°C to +125°C) are standard, not optional.
  • India's growing private space sector will need domestic HiRel supply chains to reduce import dependence.

Pros and Cons of HiRel Components for Space Programmes

No component strategy is without trade-offs. Buyers should weigh these factors before specifying HiRel parts.

  • Pros: Proven radiation tolerance, hermetic reliability, traceable qualification documentation, lower mission failure risk, acceptance by insurers and launch providers.
  • Cons: Very high unit cost, long procurement lead times, limited supplier base, complex export control compliance (ITAR and equivalent Indian regulations), and reduced design flexibility once parts are locked.

The Road Ahead for India's HiRel Ecosystem

The Roman Telescope launch is a reminder that space missions are won or lost at the component level. India's semiconductor mission and defence electronics push create an opening for domestic HiRel fabrication and screening facilities. Until then, Indian buyers will continue paying import premiums and managing long lead times.

For design engineers, the practical lesson is simple: qualify early, budget realistically, and treat HiRel power semiconductors as mission-critical infrastructure rather than commodity line items. The Roman Telescope's success was built on that discipline.
