IIM Calcutta Creates Case Study on BJP’s West Bengal Poll Strategy

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • IIM Calcutta faculty created a detailed case study on BJP’s West Bengal election strategy.
  • The case examines grassroots mobilisation, coalition tactics, and communication methods.
  • It is intended for use in management and political strategy courses.
  • The study highlights data-driven decision-making and local leadership integration.

Kolkata: The Indian Institute of Management Calcutta has developed a strategic case study examining the Bharatiya Janata Party’s election management in West Bengal. The case, prepared by faculty members at the premier business school, focuses on the party’s organisational and campaign methods in the state.

The move signals a growing academic interest in electoral politics as a subject of management study. IIM Calcutta’s case is expected to be used in classrooms to teach students about large-scale campaign coordination and resource allocation.

Focus on Ground-Level Strategy

The case study looks at how the BJP built its presence in a state where it was traditionally a marginal player. It examines the party’s efforts to expand its organisational network beyond urban centres into rural and semi-urban areas.

Faculty members involved in the project have not disclosed specific electoral cycles or results under review. They have, however, confirmed that the study covers coalition management and the integration of local leaders into the party’s structure.

Key Elements of the Study

The case reportedly breaks down the BJP’s strategy into three broad areas: grassroots mobilisation, communication, and data analytics. It looks at how the party used booth-level committees and social media outreach to connect with voters.

Another section deals with the management of alliances and the handling of internal factionalism. The case also touches on the use of public rallies, door-to-door campaigns, and targeted messaging for different demographic groups.

Academic and Political Implications

IIM Calcutta’s decision to develop this case reflects a broader trend of Indian management schools engaging with real-world political scenarios. Similar cases have been written on election campaigns in other states, but this is one of the first focused on West Bengal.

The case is not an endorsement of any party or strategy. It is designed as a neutral analytical tool for students to study decision-making under uncertainty, resource constraints, and competitive pressure.

Political analysts say such studies can offer valuable lessons for campaign managers and policy planners. They also provide a structured way to understand how parties adapt to regional dynamics.

Reactions and Next Steps

Neither the BJP nor the Trinamool Congress has commented on the case study. IIM Calcutta has not announced a public release date, but the case is expected to be circulated among business schools and used in executive education programmes.

The institute may also publish a condensed version for wider readership. Faculty members are said to be working on supplementary teaching notes that will include discussion questions and simulation exercises.

What to watch: Whether other management institutes follow IIM Calcutta’s lead and develop similar case studies on election campaigns in other states, particularly in the run-up to the next general election.
