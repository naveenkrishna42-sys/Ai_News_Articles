Shiv Sena Corporator Surrenders Day After High Court Cancels Bail in Doctor Assault Case

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ramesh Mhatre surrendered to police on Sunday, a day after the Bombay HC cancelled his bail.
  • He was sent to judicial custody until August 3.
  • The Maharashtra IMA called off its July 20 strike but warned of an indefinite strike from August 9 if action is not taken.

A Shiv Sena corporator accused of assaulting doctors at a Thane hospital surrendered to police on Sunday, a day after the Bombay High Court cancelled his bail. Ramesh Mhatre was produced before a magistrate and sent to judicial custody until August 3.

The Alleged Assault

The incident took place earlier this month at a government-run hospital in Thane. Mhatre, along with his supporters, is accused of barging into the hospital and physically attacking doctors on duty.

The doctors alleged that the corporator and his men disrupted work and assaulted them after a disagreement over the treatment of a patient. The attack sparked widespread outrage among the medical community across Maharashtra.

Court Intervention

The Bombay High Court, while cancelling Mhatre's bail, made strong observations about the rising incidents of violence against healthcare professionals. The court urged authorities to take a strict stance against such attacks.

Senior advocate Milind Sathe, representing the state, argued that the corporator's release on bail could embolden others to commit similar offences. The court agreed and revoked the bail granted earlier by a lower court.

Mhatre had been out on bail since shortly after his arrest. The High Court's order came after an appeal by the state government challenging the lower court's decision.

Medical Community Reacts

The Indian Medical Association's Maharashtra chapter had called for a statewide strike on July 20 to protest the attack. Following Mhatre's surrender and the High Court's order, the IMA called off the strike.

However, the association has warned that it will go on an indefinite strike from August 9 if the state government fails to provide adequate security for doctors and ensure speedy justice in such cases. The IMA has also demanded the installation of CCTV cameras and security personnel in all government hospitals.

Doctors across the state have expressed relief at the High Court's decision, but many say this is just the first step. They are demanding stricter laws to protect healthcare workers from violence.

Political Fallout

The incident has also drawn political attention. The Shiv Sena, which Mhatre belongs to, has distanced itself from his actions. Party leaders have said that the corporator's behaviour does not reflect the party's stand.

Opposition parties have criticised the ruling alliance for not doing enough to prevent such attacks. They have demanded that the government take responsibility and ensure that doctors can work without fear.

The Thane police have registered a case against Mhatre and several unidentified persons under various sections of the Indian Penal Code, including assault and criminal intimidation.

What comes next will depend on the trial and the state government's response to the IMA's demands. The medical community is watching closely, and the August 9 deadline looms large.
