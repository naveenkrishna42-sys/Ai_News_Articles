Can a film star swing Tamil Nadu's election again?

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tamil Nadu has a history of electing film stars as chief ministers.
  • Current major parties are led by non-film personalities, but new star candidates are emerging.
  • The 2026 election may test whether the film-to-politics pipeline still works.

Tamil Nadu is heading to the polls, and a familiar question is resurfacing: can another film star sway the election? The state has a long history of turning matinee idols into political heavyweights. From M.G. Ramachandran to Jayalalithaa, the silver screen has often been a launchpad for the chief minister's office.

But the political landscape has shifted. The two major Dravidian parties — the DMK and the AIADMK — are currently led by M.K. Stalin and Edappadi K. Palaniswami respectively. Neither is a film star. Yet, new star candidates are entering the fray, hoping to tap into a deep well of fan loyalty.

The legacy of star power

MGR, as he was popularly known, was the first major film star to become chief minister in 1977. His successor, Jayalalithaa, also a leading actress, dominated politics for decades. Their success created a template: build a fan base, then convert it into votes.

That template has been tested before. Actors like Vijayakanth and Sarath Kumar have led parties, but neither reached the top post. Now, with Vijay, Rajinikanth, and others hinting at political roles, the question is whether they can break through.

New faces on the block

In the current election cycle, at least two prominent film personalities are running. Actor Vijay, who has a massive fan following, is contesting from a key constituency. Rajinikanth, after years of speculation, has launched a party and is fielding candidates.

Political analysts say star power alone is not enough. “Fans may cheer an actor on screen, but voting is about bread and butter issues,” said a Chennai-based political commentator. “The DMK and AIADMK have strong grassroots machines. A star needs more than charisma to win.”

What the voters say

On the streets of Chennai, opinions are divided. “I voted for Jayalalithaa because she worked for the people, not because she was an actress,” said a shopkeeper in T. Nagar. A young college student disagreed: “Vijay speaks about corruption and development. He connects with us.”

Both major parties are watching the star candidates closely. The DMK, which leads the ruling alliance, has not taken them lightly. Stalin has warned voters against “glamour politics”. The AIADMK, meanwhile, is trying to reclaim its own star legacy.

Challenges ahead

Winning an election in Tamil Nadu requires more than celebrity appeal. Caste equations, alliance politics, and local issues often outweigh star power. Moreover, the film industry itself is fragmented, with actors often backing different parties.

Rajinikanth, for instance, has remained vague on policy details. His party has not announced a full list of candidates. Vijay, on the other hand, has been more active, but his campaign has faced logistical hurdles.

The Election Commission has not yet released the final voter turnout figures, but early trends suggest a competitive race. The final result will show whether the film-to-politics pipeline still works — or if voters have moved on.

Whatever the outcome, one thing is clear: the romance between Tamil cinema and politics is far from over. The next few weeks will reveal if a new star can rise in Chennai's political sky.
