IRGC declares MOU 'dead' as strikes continue, two oil tankers ablaze near Hormuz

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • IRGC top brass declares MOU with US 'dead' as military strikes continue
  • Two oil tanker ships reported on fire in the Strait of Hormuz
  • Iran threatens 'zero hour' amid US plane deployments; Houthis warned to close Red Sea gateway

IRGC Declares MOU 'Dead' Amid Escalating Strikes

A senior commander of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has declared that a memorandum of understanding (MOU) with the United States is 'dead', as military strikes between the two sides continue. The announcement came on Friday, July 17, 2026, amid a sharp escalation in hostilities across the Middle East.

The IRGC official did not specify the exact nature of the now-defunct MOU, but the statement signals a complete breakdown in any remaining diplomatic channels. The development follows days of heightened tension, with both sides trading accusations and launching fresh attacks.

Two Oil Tankers Ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz

In a worrying development for global energy markets, two oil tanker ships have been reported on fire near the Strait of Hormuz. The narrow waterway is a critical chokepoint for oil shipments, and any disruption there can send shockwaves through world prices.

Maritime sources say the tankers were hit in what appeared to be coordinated strikes, though no group has claimed responsibility yet. The fires were still raging as of Friday evening, with rescue operations underway. The incident has already pushed crude oil futures higher in Asian trading.

Iran Threatens 'Zero Hour' as US Deploys Warplanes

Iran has raised the stakes by threatening a 'zero hour' response, though officials have not clarified what that would entail. The warning came after the United States deployed additional warplanes to the region, as reported by NDTV.

The US military has not commented on the scale of the deployment, but sources indicate it includes fighter jets and support aircraft. The Pentagon has described the move as a 'defensive posture' to protect American assets and allies in the region.

Houthis Told to Close Red Sea Gateway If US Hits Power Network

In a separate but connected development, exclusive sources told Reuters that Iran has directed the Houthi rebels in Yemen to close the Red Sea gateway if the US strikes Iran's power network. This would effectively block one of the world's busiest shipping lanes.

The Red Sea is strategically vital, handling about 12% of global trade, including oil and liquefied natural gas. A closure would force ships to take the longer route around Africa, raising costs and delaying deliveries.

Why the Red Sea Matters, and What It Means for India

The Times of India has highlighted how a Red Sea crisis could become India's next major economic headache. India relies heavily on the route for energy imports and exports to Europe and the Middle East.

Any prolonged disruption would hit India's trade balance, push up fuel prices, and strain the rupee. The government is reportedly monitoring the situation closely, though no official statement has been issued yet.

What Happens Next

The situation remains fluid. With the IRGC ruling out any diplomatic solution and the Strait of Hormuz now a flashpoint, the risk of a broader regional war is growing. Global markets and governments are bracing for further escalation, but no clear off-ramp is visible yet.
