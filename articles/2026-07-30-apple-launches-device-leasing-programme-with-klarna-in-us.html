Apple Launches Device Leasing Programme with Klarna in US

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Apple's new leasing programme, 'Apple Upgrade', is now available in the US.
  • The programme is powered by Klarna and offers temporary access to devices at half the MSRP.
  • Apple will not enable a 'restricted mode' for missed lease payments.

What is Apple Upgrade?

Apple has officially launched a device leasing programme in the United States. Called 'Apple Upgrade', it is powered by the fintech firm Klarna. The programme offers customers temporary access to the latest iPhones, iPads, and Macs at roughly half the manufacturer's suggested retail price (MSRP).

The move marks a significant shift in how Apple sells its hardware. Instead of buying outright or paying in instalments, users can now effectively rent devices for a set period. This brings Apple in line with trends seen in the automotive and furniture sectors, where leasing is common.

How the Leasing Works

Under the programme, customers pay a monthly fee that is about 50 percent lower than the device's MSRP spread over a standard loan. At the end of the lease term, they must return the device. There is no option to buy it outright at the end of the lease, according to reports.

The programme is designed for users who want the newest hardware every year without the upfront cost. Apple has partnered with Klarna to handle the credit checks and monthly billing. The scheme is currently limited to customers in the United States.

No 'Restricted Mode' for Missed Payments

One notable detail is that Apple will not enable any 'restricted mode' on devices whose lease payments are missed. This means that even if a customer stops paying, the device will continue to function normally. Apple has not explained how it plans to recover devices or enforce payment beyond standard credit reporting.

This is a departure from some other leasing or financing programmes in the tech industry, where non-payment can lead to the device being remotely locked or disabled. Apple's approach appears to rely more on the customer's creditworthiness at the outset rather than post-purchase enforcement.

What This Means for Consumers

For consumers, the programme lowers the barrier to owning the latest Apple hardware. A top-end iPhone, which could cost over ₹1.2 lakh in India, might now be accessible for a monthly fee that is substantially lower. However, the customer never owns the device.

The programme is ideal for those who upgrade frequently. But for users who keep their devices for three or four years, buying outright or through a traditional instalment plan may still be more economical. Apple has not yet announced whether this programme will expand to India or other markets.

Apple's new leasing programme with Klarna is a clear attempt to capture more recurring revenue. It remains to be seen how customers will respond to renting rather than owning. The company has not disclosed any plans for an international rollout, but if the US pilot succeeds, other regions could follow.
