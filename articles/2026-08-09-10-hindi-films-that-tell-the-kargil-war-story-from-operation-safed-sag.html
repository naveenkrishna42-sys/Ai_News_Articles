10 Hindi Films That Tell the Kargil War Story, From Operation Safed Sagar to Shershaah

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • The list spans 10 Hindi films, from Operation Safed Sagar to Shershaah.
  • Films cover both aerial combat and infantry bravery on the peaks.
  • The Kargil War remains a recurring theme in Bollywood war cinema.

The Kargil War of 1999 remains one of the most defining military conflicts in independent India's history. Over two decades later, Bollywood has repeatedly turned to the icy peaks of Kargil for stories of valour, sacrifice, and national pride. From the early dramatisation of air operations to the recent blockbuster Shershaah, Hindi cinema has built a small but significant sub-genre around the war.

Here are 10 Hindi films that portray the Kargil War, each offering a different lens — from the cockpit of a fighter jet to the frozen bunkers of Tiger Hill.

Operation Safed Sagar (2024)

The most recent addition to this list, Operation Safed Sagar, takes its name from the Indian Air Force's air campaign during the war. The film focuses on the precision strikes and aerial reconnaissance that played a crucial role in turning the tide against intruders.

While the film uses dramatic licence, it stays rooted in the actual sequence of events — from the initial detection of infiltration to the IAF's targeted sorties.

Shershaah (2021)

Shershaah brought the story of Captain Vikram Batra to a new generation. The film, starring Sidharth Malhotra, traces Batra's journey from a young aspirant to a Param Vir Chakra awardee who captured Point 4875 before being martyred.

The film's battle sequences, particularly the capture of the peak, were widely praised for their realism. It also became one of the most-watched Hindi films on streaming, cementing Batra's legacy in popular memory.

LOC Kargil (2003)

J.P. Dutta's LOC Kargil remains the most exhaustive cinematic treatment of the war. With an ensemble cast that included Sanjay Dutt, Ajay Devgn, Saif Ali Khan, and Abhishek Bachchan, the film chronicled multiple battles across the Line of Control.

Running over four hours, the film was a tribute to the soldiers of the Indian Army, even if its box office performance was modest.

Lakshya (2004)

Farhan Akhtar's Lakshya used the Kargil War as the backdrop for a coming-of-age story. Hrithik Roshan played Karan Shergill, a directionless young man who finds purpose as an army officer during the conflict.

The film's climactic sequences, set in the mountains, were shot with military cooperation and remain a benchmark for war cinematography in Hindi cinema.

Gunjan Saxena: The Kargil Girl (2020)

This biographical drama told the story of Gunjan Saxena, one of the first women pilots to fly in a combat zone. Janhvi Kapoor portrayed Saxena, who flew reconnaissance missions and rescue sorties during the war.

The film sparked debate over the portrayal of gender bias in the IAF, but it highlighted a lesser-known aspect of the conflict — the role of women in combat support.

Kesari: Chapter 2 (2025)

Though primarily a period drama, Kesari: Chapter 2 weaves in elements of the Kargil War through its legal and historical narrative. The film, starring Akshay Kumar, revisits the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and its aftermath, with the war serving as a parallel thread of national sacrifice.

Its inclusion in this list is for the way it connects past and present military honour.

Border (1997)

While Border predates the Kargil War, its depiction of the 1971 Indo-Pak war set the template for Bollywood's war films. Its influence on later Kargil films is undeniable — from the gritty battle sequences to the patriotic fervour.

Many critics consider it the spiritual predecessor to the Kargil war film cycle.

The Ghazi Attack (2017)

Another pre-Kargil war film, The Ghazi Attack is a naval war thriller. While it deals with the 1971 sinking of PNS Ghazi, its success proved that Indian audiences were hungry for war stories — a trend that later Kargil films rode on.

Its technical ambition paved the way for more realistic military dramas.

Paltan (2018)

J.P. Dutta returned to the war genre with Paltan, this time focusing on the 1962 Sino-Indian War. Though not about Kargil, its production design and attention to military detail drew comparisons to LOC Kargil.

Dutta's repeated exploration of soldierly sacrifice makes his work essential viewing for anyone interested in Indian war cinema.

Uri: The Surgical Strike (2019)

Strictly speaking, Uri is about the 2016 surgical strikes, not the Kargil War. But its box office success and critical acclaim reignited interest in military films, leading studios to greenlight more Kargil-centric projects.

It also set a new standard for action choreography and patriotic storytelling in modern Hindi cinema.

Why Kargil Endures in Cinema

The Kargil War offers a unique blend of high-altitude combat, clear-cut morality, and real-life heroism. Filmmakers have found it a fertile ground for exploring themes of duty, camaraderie, and national identity.

As new films continue to be announced — including rumoured projects on other Kargil heroes — it is clear that the war's cinematic journey is far from over. The next few years may bring even more stories from those frozen peaks.
