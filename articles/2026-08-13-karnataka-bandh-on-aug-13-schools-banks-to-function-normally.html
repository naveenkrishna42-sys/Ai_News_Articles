Karnataka bandh on Aug 13: Schools, banks to function normally

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Most organisations extend only moral support to the bandh call
  • Schools likely to stay open as KAMS withdraws closure call
  • Police on high alert in Mandya with CCTV monitoring key points
  • Bus operations expected to remain normal across the state

Bengaluru, August 12: The Karnataka bandh called for August 13 over the Cauvery water-sharing dispute is unlikely to disrupt normal life, with most organisations extending only moral support to the protest call. Schools, colleges, banks and public transport are expected to function as usual, according to reports from multiple sources.

Schools and colleges to remain open

The Karnataka Associated Management of Schools (KAMS), which had earlier floated the idea of a closure, has withdrawn its call. This means most private schools in Bengaluru and other districts will remain open on Thursday.

Government schools are also expected to function normally. Parents have been advised to confirm with individual institutions, but the general indication is that academic activities will proceed without disruption.

Banks, offices and transport unaffected

Banking operations are unlikely to be affected. The Indian Banks' Association has not issued any advisory for closure, and most branches will operate on regular hours.

Public transport, including BMTC buses in Bengaluru and KSRTC services across the state, is expected to run on schedule. The transport corporations have not announced any reduction in services.

Police on alert in Mandya

While the bandh has received only symbolic support in most parts, Mandya remains a sensitive spot. Police have been put on high alert, with a large crowd expected after 10 am. CCTV cameras have been installed at key locations to monitor the situation.

Additional forces have been deployed in the region, and authorities are keeping a close watch on any potential flashpoints.

Background of the bandh

The bandh has been called by pro-Kannada organisations and farmer groups to protest against the release of Cauvery water to Tamil Nadu. The issue has been a long-standing point of contention between the two states.

However, this time, the response has been tepid. Several industry bodies and trade associations have declined to participate, citing the economic cost of a shutdown. Even some farmer unions have distanced themselves from the call.

The muted response is a contrast to earlier bandhs over the same issue, which saw widespread closures and transport disruptions. This time, the mood appears to be more restrained.

Business establishments in Bengaluru are expected to open as usual, though some shops in Mandya and surrounding areas may remain shut as a precaution.

What to watch

All eyes will be on Mandya district, where the protest is expected to be the strongest. The police administration has said it will take action against anyone attempting to force closures or disrupt public order.

For now, the message from authorities is clear: the bandh will not be allowed to affect essential services or normal life. The day is likely to pass with only symbolic protests, barring any unforeseen escalation.
