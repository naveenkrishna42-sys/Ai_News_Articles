Siddaramaiah says politics corrupt, won't contest 2028 Assembly polls

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Siddaramaiah says politics has become corrupt
  • He will not contest the 2028 Assembly elections
  • Vows to continue public service despite stepping back from electoral politics

Karnataka Chief Minister Siddaramaiah has dropped a political bombshell. He announced on Sunday that he will not contest the 2028 Assembly elections. The veteran leader cited growing corruption in politics as the reason.

"Politics has become corrupt," Siddaramaiah said. He made the statement during a public event. The announcement caught many by surprise. Siddaramaiah has been a fixture in Karnataka politics for decades.

A blunt assessment of political decay

Siddaramaiah did not mince words. He described the current state of politics in stark terms. The Chief Minister said dishonesty has become widespread. He argued that the system needs cleaning up.

His comments come at a time when corruption allegations have hit several state governments. Karnataka has seen its share of controversies. Siddaramaiah himself has faced opposition charges of mismanagement. He has denied any wrongdoing.

The Chief Minister did not name any specific individuals or parties. But his broader critique was clear. He believes political morality has eroded.

Vow to continue service

Despite stepping away from electoral contests, Siddaramaiah said he will not retire from public life. "I will continue to serve the people," he vowed. He indicated he will work for social and developmental causes.

This is not a complete exit from politics. Siddaramaiah will remain Chief Minister until the next elections. But his decision to skip the 2028 race reshapes the political landscape in Karnataka. The Congress party will need to find a new face for the Chief Minister post in three years.

Supporters were visibly emotional at the event. Many had expected him to lead the party into the next election. Siddaramaiah has been a mass leader for the Congress. His decision creates a vacuum.

Reactions from political circles

The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) did not immediately respond to the announcement. Opposition leaders have often targeted Siddaramaiah on governance issues. They may now recalibrate their strategy.

Congress insiders said the party had no prior warning of the move. Senior leaders are likely to hold consultations. Siddaramaiah's decision could trigger a leadership race within the Karnataka Congress.

Political analysts said this is a rare instance of a sitting Chief Minister voluntarily stepping back from electoral politics. Most politicians cling to power until the end. Siddaramaiah's move will be studied for its implications.

Siddaramaiah has had a long career. He has served as Chief Minister multiple times. He has also held key portfolios in state and central governments. His decision marks the end of an era in Karnataka politics.

The Chief Minister's announcement also comes amid a broader debate on political ethics in India. Several states have seen corruption scandals in recent years. Siddaramaiah's remarks add to that discourse.

What happens next is uncertain. Siddaramaiah will continue to lead Karnataka for now. The Congress party will have to plan for a future without him as a candidate. Political observers will watch whether other leaders follow his example.
