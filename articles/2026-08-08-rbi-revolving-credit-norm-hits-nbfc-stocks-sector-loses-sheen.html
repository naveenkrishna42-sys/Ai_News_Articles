RBI revolving credit norm hits NBFC stocks; sector loses sheen

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's decision on revolving credit facilities triggers a selloff in NBFC stocks.
  • Shares of major non-banking financial companies declined sharply in trading.
  • Analysts say the move aims to curb risky lending but could pressure margins.

MUMBAI: Shares of non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) took a beating on Friday after the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) tightened norms around revolving credit facilities. The central bank's move, aimed at reining in unsecured lending, sparked a broad selloff in the sector, with several top NBFC stocks closing in the red.

The RBI's decision, announced late Thursday, requires lenders to treat revolving credit lines—such as credit cards and overdraft facilities—more conservatively. This means higher capital requirements for NBFCs, which could squeeze their profitability and slow down credit growth.

Stocks under pressure

The selloff was led by mid-sized NBFCs, though large players were not spared. Shares of Bajaj Finance, a market heavyweight, fell nearly 4% intraday before paring some losses. Other prominent names, including Mahindra & Mahindra Financial Services and Shriram Transport Finance, also witnessed sharp declines.

According to market analysts, the RBI's move signals a shift in regulatory stance. The central bank has been increasingly wary of the rapid growth in unsecured retail lending, which has outpaced overall credit growth in recent quarters. By making revolving credit more expensive to hold, the RBI aims to cool down this segment.

What the new norms entail

Under the revised framework, NBFCs will have to assign higher risk weights to revolving credit exposures. This is expected to raise their capital consumption, potentially forcing them to either raise fresh capital or reduce lending. For smaller NBFCs, the impact could be more pronounced, as their capital buffers are thinner.

The RBI has not specified a transition period, leaving lenders to adjust their portfolios quickly. This uncertainty added to the market's nervousness, with investors worried about near-term earnings hits.

Industry reaction

Industry bodies have expressed concerns that the move could dampen credit off-take, especially in the consumer segment, which has been a key driver of economic growth. Some NBFC executives, speaking off the record, said they were caught off guard by the timing, coming just ahead of the festive season when credit demand typically peaks.

However, the RBI's stance appears firm. The central bank has been vocal about the need to prevent asset quality deterioration, particularly in unsecured loans, where delinquencies have been creeping up. The move is part of a broader regulatory push to ensure financial stability.

Market outlook

Broader market indices also felt the heat, with the Nifty and Sensex slipping nearly 1% each, dragged by financials. The sector's underperformance is a stark contrast to its recent rally, which was driven by strong earnings and improving asset quality.

Analysts remain divided on the long-term impact. Some argue that the RBI's caution is warranted and that NBFCs will adapt by tightening underwriting standards. Others fear that the new norms could stifle innovation in digital lending, where revolving credit products are popular.

For now, investors are treading carefully. Trading volumes in NBFC stocks were significantly higher than the recent average, indicating that many are looking to exit or reduce their positions. The coming weeks will reveal whether the selloff is a temporary blip or the start of a deeper correction.

The RBI's next policy review, scheduled for October, will be closely watched for any further tightening. Until then, NBFCs will have to recalibrate their strategies to align with the new regulatory reality.
