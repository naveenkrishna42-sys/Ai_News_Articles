Hyderabad Police Dismantle Trafficking Ring After Rescuing Minor Girl

In a significant crackdown on human trafficking, Hyderabad Police have arrested eight individuals accused of trafficking and sexually exploiting a minor girl. The rescue operation was conducted late on Friday night in the Old City area, following a tip-off from local residents.

Details of the Arrests and Rescue Operation

According to officials, the victim, a 15-year-old girl, was trafficked from a neighbouring state and forced into commercial sexual exploitation. She was rescued during a coordinated raid by the Women and Child Protection Department and the Hyderabad Crime Branch.

Four of the accused—identified as Mohammed Ayub, Mohammed Furqan, Mohammed Saleem, and Mohammed Shafi—were apprehended at the scene. The remaining four, including two women, were arrested later in the day from different parts of the city. Preliminary investigations suggest the group operated a structured network involved in recruiting, transporting, and exploiting vulnerable minors.

Charges Framed Under Anti-Trafficking Laws

The accused have been charged under the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956, and relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code, including those related to kidnapping, assault, and criminal conspiracy. Child welfare authorities have taken the survivor into protective custody and are providing her with medical care and psychological support.

This case underscores the persistent challenge of child trafficking in urban centres like Hyderabad, where economic vulnerability and migration often expose young girls to exploitation. Police say further arrests are likely as the investigation unfolds.

Broader Crackdown on Trafficking Networks

The operation comes amid a state-wide initiative by Telangana Police to strengthen anti-trafficking mechanisms. Over the past six months, more than 40 individuals have been arrested in similar cases across the state, with over 25 survivors rescued.

Authorities say enhanced inter-agency coordination, including collaboration with NGOs and child protection agencies, has improved response times and outcomes in trafficking cases. The government has also announced plans to strengthen shelter homes and rehabilitation programmes for survivors.

Community Vigilance Key to Prevention

Local leaders and activists have praised the swift action but emphasised the need for greater community awareness to prevent such crimes. 'Traffickers often target families in distress, promising better opportunities for children,' said a spokesperson from a city-based child rights organisation.

Police have urged citizens to report suspicious activity through dedicated helplines and reiterated their commitment to protecting vulnerable children. The rescued girl's condition remains stable, and she continues to receive trauma counselling and educational support.
