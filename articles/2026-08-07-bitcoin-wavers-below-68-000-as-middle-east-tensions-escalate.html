Bitcoin wavers below $68,000 as Middle East tensions escalate

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin hovers below $68,000 as Middle East tensions escalate
  • Crypto markets remain volatile amid geopolitical uncertainty
  • Traders eye support levels and potential further downside

Bitcoin slipped further below the $68,000 mark on Friday, as escalating tensions in the Middle East kept risk assets under pressure. The world's largest cryptocurrency traded at around $67,800 during early European hours, reflecting a cautious mood across global markets.

Traders have been on edge for days, with headlines from the region driving sharp, short-lived moves. The latest flare-up has prompted investors to trim exposure to volatile assets, and digital currencies have not been spared.

Geopolitical jitters weigh on sentiment

The conflict in the Middle East has entered a more dangerous phase, with no clear end in sight. Each new development sends ripples through oil prices, equities, and now crypto, as traders reassess risk in real time.

Bitcoin's recent decline from its late-July peak above $70,000 underscores how quickly sentiment can shift. The asset had been buoyed by optimism around spot exchange-traded fund inflows and a softer dollar, but those gains have eroded as geopolitical fears take centre stage.

Market participants watch key levels

Analysts are now focusing on the $66,000 to $67,000 zone, a band that has acted as support in recent weeks. A break below that could trigger further selling, while a bounce would signal that buyers are still willing to step in.

Volume has been elevated, a sign that institutional players are actively repositioning. Some see this as a healthy correction, while others warn that a prolonged conflict could keep a lid on prices for weeks.

Altcoins follow bitcoin's lead

Ether, the second-largest cryptocurrency, has also slipped, trading below $3,500. Most major altcoins are in the red, with losses ranging from 1% to 4% over the past 24 hours.

Stablecoins, by contrast, have seen increased issuance, suggesting that some investors are parking funds on the sidelines rather than exiting the market entirely. That could provide a base for a rebound if tensions ease.

What to watch next

Traders will keep a close eye on any diplomatic moves or ceasefire talks, as a de-escalation could spark a rapid recovery. On the data front, US inflation figures due later this month will also influence the Federal Reserve's rate path, a key driver for crypto valuations.

For now, the market remains hostage to headlines. Until the geopolitical picture clears, bitcoin is likely to stay range-bound, with volatility the only constant.
