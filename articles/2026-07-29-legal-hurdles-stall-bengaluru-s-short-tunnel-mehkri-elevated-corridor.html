Legal hurdles stall Bengaluru's short tunnel, Mehkri elevated corridor

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Legal challenges have paused work on Bengaluru's short tunnel and Mehkri elevated corridor.
  • The projects are stuck over land acquisition and environmental clearance disputes.
  • Officials are hopeful for a resolution but no timeline has been set for construction to resume.

Two major infrastructure projects in Bengaluru, the short tunnel near the Central Business District and the Mehkri Circle elevated corridor, are currently stalled due to legal challenges. The projects, seen as crucial to decongesting some of the city's busiest roads, have hit a wall of litigation that shows no signs of quick resolution.

The Bengaluru Development Authority (BDA) and the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) are the agencies behind these projects. They are now waiting for court decisions that will decide the future of these multi-crore rupee undertakings.

The Short Tunnel Project

The short tunnel, a key part of the city's long-term traffic management plan, has run into trouble over land acquisition. Petitions have been filed challenging the acquisition of private properties for the project, arguing that the process has not followed due procedure.

The tunnel is designed to connect the busy areas of Shivajinagar and Vasanth Nagar, providing a swift bypass for commuters. However, with the legal cloud, the BBMP has been forced to put all construction activities on hold. Officials say that work cannot resume until the court gives a clear go-ahead.

Mehkri Circle Elevated Corridor

Meanwhile, the Mehkri Circle elevated corridor, another vital artery project, is facing its own set of legal hurdles. The project aims to create a flyover at one of the city's most congested junctions, but environmental activists have moved court against it.

The petitioners claim that the corridor will lead to the felling of a significant number of trees and will worsen air quality in the area. They argue that the authorities have not conducted a proper environmental impact assessment. The court has issued a stay on the project until the matter is heard in detail.

The BDA, which is handling the corridor, says it has already completed a majority of the design work. But with the stay in place, even preliminary site preparation has been halted. A senior official said the agency is preparing a response for the court, hoping to get the stay lifted soon.

What This Means for Commuters

For Bengaluru's already harried commuters, these delays mean more time spent in traffic. The short tunnel and the Mehkri corridor were expected to ease congestion on alternate routes, but with no end to the legal battles in sight, those benefits remain distant.

The city's traffic police have expressed concern, noting that these projects were part of a larger plan to manage the increasing vehicle population. They say that without these infrastructure upgrades, other roads will become even more choked.

Officials from both the BDA and BBMP have said they are doing their best to resolve the legal issues. However, they have not given any specific timeline for when work might restart. The courts are yet to schedule the next hearing for either case.

The total cost of both projects runs into hundreds of crores. The delays could lead to cost overruns, which will ultimately be borne by the state exchequer. For now, the construction equipment and materials sit idle at project sites, waiting for the legal fog to clear.

The coming weeks will be crucial. The agencies are expected to file their affidavits, and the courts will decide on the next steps. Until then, Bengaluru's traffic woes will continue to grow, with no immediate relief in sight.
