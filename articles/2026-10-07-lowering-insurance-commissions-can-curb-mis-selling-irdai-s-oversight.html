Introduction: The Commission‑Mis‑selling Link

Insurance mis‑selling remains a persistent concern in India, with numerous consumer complaints pointing to policies sold without proper suitability checks. A core driver of this problem is the commission structure that rewards agents heavily for first‑year sales, often at the expense of the policyholder’s actual needs. Reducing these commissions is increasingly viewed as a direct lever to curb mis‑selling practices.

How Commissions Fuel Mis‑selling

Agents typically earn a large upfront commission on the first premium, ranging from 25% to 40% of the annual premium for life insurance products, while renewal commissions are modest, usually between 2% and 5%. This disparity creates a strong incentive to close a sale quickly, even if the product does not match the buyer’s risk profile or financial goals. Consequently, agents may omit critical details, exaggerate benefits, or push high‑premium policies that generate larger upfront payouts.

IRDAI’s Regulatory Toolkit

The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI) holds the authority to reshape commission frameworks through guidelines, caps, and disclosure mandates. In recent years, IRDAI has introduced limits on commissions for certain products, mandated standardized benefit illustrations, and increased penalties for proven mis‑selling. Such measures aim to realign agent incentives with consumer protection objectives.

Key Regulatory Actions

  • Commission caps: IRDAI has set maximum permissible first‑year commissions for term plans at 25% of the premium, reducing the earlier higher limits.
  • Mandatory suitability assessments: Agents must document a customer’s financial needs before recommending a product.
  • Enhanced disclosure: Policy documents now require a clear, upfront summary of charges, benefits, and surrender values.
  • Grievance redressal: Faster turnaround times for complaint resolution and higher compensation awards for validated mis‑selling cases.

These steps collectively diminish the financial upside of pushing unsuitable policies while increasing the reputational and legal costs of non‑compliance.

Impact on Consumers and Insurers

For consumers, lower commission‑driven pressure translates into more transparent sales conversations and a higher likelihood of receiving policies that truly match their coverage needs. This can reduce the incidence of policy lapses, surrender losses, and financial strain caused by unsuitable purchases. Insurers, meanwhile, may experience a short‑term dip in new‑business acquisition costs as agents adjust to lower payouts, but the long‑term benefit lies in improved persistency ratios and stronger brand trust.

Way Forward: Balancing Incentives and Protection

Sustainable reform requires a nuanced approach: commissions must remain sufficient to attract and retain competent agents, yet not so high as to encourage mis‑selling. IRDAI could consider tiered commission structures that reward renewal and persistency, alongside ongoing market monitoring to detect emerging mis‑selling trends. Consumer education campaigns, coupled with robust grievance mechanisms, will further empower buyers to make informed choices.
