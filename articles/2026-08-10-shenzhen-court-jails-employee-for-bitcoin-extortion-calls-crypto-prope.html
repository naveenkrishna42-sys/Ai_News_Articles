Shenzhen Court Jails Employee for Bitcoin Extortion, Calls Crypto Property

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shenzhen employee sentenced for extorting Bitcoin worth $87,000 from a colleague.
  • Court ruling officially recognizes cryptocurrency as property in a criminal case.
  • Case highlights China's evolving legal stance on digital assets despite trading bans.

A court in Shenzhen has sentenced a company employee to prison for extorting Bitcoin worth approximately $87,000, in a ruling that marks a significant legal milestone in China's approach to cryptocurrency. The judgment, delivered earlier this week, explicitly recognized digital assets as property under Chinese law, a position that could reshape how similar cases are handled across the country.

The convict, whose identity has not been disclosed by the court, was found guilty of coercing a colleague into transferring a substantial amount of Bitcoin. The extortion reportedly took place under threats of physical harm, though specific details of the intimidation tactics have not been made public.

The Ruling and Its Legal Basis

The Shenzhen court's decision to classify Bitcoin as property is notable because it provides a clear legal framework for addressing crimes involving cryptocurrencies. Under China's criminal code, extortion is a serious offense, but the status of digital assets as property had previously been ambiguous in judicial practice.

In its written judgment, the court reasoned that Bitcoin holds economic value, can be controlled by individuals, and is transferable—criteria that align with the traditional definition of property. This reasoning, legal experts say, could now be cited as precedent in other courts dealing with theft, fraud, or extortion involving crypto.

The sentence handed down by the court has not been fully detailed in public records, but the imprisonment term reflects the gravity of the offense, which involved a sum equivalent to roughly ₹72 lakh in Indian currency.

China's Complex Crypto Stance

China has maintained a hardline stance against cryptocurrency trading and mining since 2021, when authorities banned all such activities as part of a broader crackdown on financial risks. Yet this ruling underscores a nuanced reality: while trading and mining are illegal, the underlying assets are not considered worthless or beyond legal protection.

The Shenzhen case is not the first time Chinese courts have dealt with crypto-related crimes, but it is among the most explicit in affirming property rights over digital tokens. In earlier instances, courts had treated Bitcoin as a form of virtual property in civil disputes, but this criminal case elevates that recognition to a more consequential level.

For victims of crypto-related crimes, the ruling offers a pathway to legal recourse. For perpetrators, it signals that courts will not shy away from punishing offenses even when the stolen asset exists only on a blockchain.

Implications for Future Cases

Legal observers in China note that this judgment could influence how police and prosecutors handle complaints involving cryptocurrency. Previously, some victims were told that digital assets fell outside the scope of property law, leaving them without effective remedies. This decision may change that dynamic.

The court's approach also aligns with broader global trends, where jurisdictions from the United States to Europe are increasingly treating crypto as property for legal purposes. However, China's unique regulatory environment means the ruling will likely apply mainly to criminal and civil disputes, not to legitimizing trading or investment activities.

Neither the court nor the convicted employee's legal team has issued public statements beyond the official judgment. The victim's identity has also been shielded from public view, consistent with standard privacy practices in such cases.

What to Watch

Observers will be watching whether China's Supreme People's Court issues guidelines to standardize the treatment of digital assets across lower courts. Any such move would clarify how property rights over crypto are enforced nationwide, potentially leading to more consistent verdicts in extortion, theft, and fraud cases involving Bitcoin and other tokens.

For now, the Shenzhen ruling stands as a clear signal: in the eyes of Chinese law, cryptocurrency is not a legal void—it is property, and crimes against it carry real consequences.
