Sensex Slides 571 Points as Nifty Hovers Near 22,450: 5 Key Factors

Market Overview

The benchmark Sensex dropped 571 points to close near 66,800, while the Nifty 50 hovered around 22,450, marking the eighth consecutive week of losses. The session wiped out roughly Rs 5 lakh crore of market capitalisation, reflecting broad‑based selling across sectors. Heavy volume in banking, energy and IT stocks underscored the intensity of the downturn.

Factor 1: Rising Global Oil Prices

Brent crude traded above $92 per barrel, driven by supply concerns and geopolitical tension in key producing regions. Higher oil prices raise input costs for Indian manufacturers and widen the current‑account deficit, prompting investors to shift to safer assets. The energy sector’s weight in the index amplified the impact on the overall market.

Factor 2: Sustained Foreign Institutional Investor Outflows

Foreign institutional investors recorded net sales of approximately Rs 12,000 crore in the cash segment during the session, continuing a trend seen over the past month. Persistent outflows reflect concerns over valuation premiums and tighter global liquidity. This selling pressure added to the downward momentum in large‑cap stocks.

Factor 3: Domestic Economic Concerns

Recent data showed a slowdown in manufacturing PMI and subdued GST collections, raising doubts about the pace of economic recovery. Additionally, RBI’s cautious stance on interest rates left markets wary of further tightening. These domestic factors eroded confidence in earnings growth prospects for the coming quarters.

Factor 4: Weak Global Cues and Technical Breakdown

Asian markets closed lower, and US futures indicated a risk‑off stance ahead of key inflation data. The Nifty breached the 22,500 support level, triggering algorithmic sell‑orders and stop‑loss activation. The combination of weak external cues and technical breakdown intensified the sell‑off, extending the losing streak to eight weeks.
