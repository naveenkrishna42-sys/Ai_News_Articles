Sensex and Nifty Close Lower Amid Weak Global Cues

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty closed in the negative zone on Tuesday.
  • Selling pressure was seen across banking, IT, and metal stocks.
  • Weak global cues contributed to the market downturn.
  • Investors await further domestic and global economic data.

Markets End Lower After Lackluster Session

Indian equity benchmarks closed Tuesday's trading session in the red, extending losses from the previous day. The BSE Sensex ended the day down over 300 points, while the NSE Nifty slipped below the 24,500 mark. Broader market indices also traded weak, reflecting cautious investor sentiment.

Traders cited weakness in global markets as a key trigger. Asian peers ended mostly lower, and European indices opened on a subdued note, adding to the selling pressure in domestic stocks.

Banking and IT Stocks Drag

Sectoral indices were largely in the red, with banking, financial services, and information technology stocks leading the decline. The Nifty Bank index fell nearly 1 percent, while the Nifty IT index also witnessed significant losses. Heavyweights like HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Infosys, and TCS were among the top drags on the benchmarks.

Midcap and smallcap stocks also felt the heat, though their losses were relatively contained compared to the frontline indices. Analysts noted that profit booking at higher levels was visible across sectors.

Global Cues Weigh on Sentiment

Weakness in global equities weighed heavily on the domestic market. US stock futures pointed to a soft opening on Wall Street later in the day, while Asian markets like Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng ended lower. Concerns over slowing economic growth and uncertainty around interest rate decisions by major central banks kept traders on edge.

Foreign institutional investors remained net sellers in the cash market, adding to the bearish tone. Domestic institutional buying, however, provided some support to the market, preventing a sharper fall.

What to Watch

Investors are now closely watching upcoming economic data releases and corporate earnings reports for further direction. Market participants will also track global crude oil prices and the movement of the Indian rupee against the US dollar. Any sharp move in these variables could set the tone for the next session.
