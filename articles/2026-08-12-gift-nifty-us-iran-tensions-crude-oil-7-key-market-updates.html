GIFT Nifty, US-Iran Tensions, Crude Oil: 7 Key Market Updates

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • GIFT Nifty signals a weak opening for Indian indices
  • Crude oil prices spike on US-Iran war fears
  • Global cues mixed as investors monitor geopolitical risks

Indian stock market investors woke up to a volatile mix of global cues on Wednesday, with geopolitical tensions and oil price movements taking centre stage. From GIFT Nifty trading hints to the escalating US-Iran conflict, here are seven key developments that could shape today's trading session.

GIFT Nifty Indicates Muted Start

GIFT Nifty, the leading indicator for Indian equity markets, pointed to a flat-to-negative opening for the NSE Nifty 50. The index was hovering around the previous close, suggesting that domestic investors are treading cautiously amid global uncertainties.

Analysts note that the lack of strong domestic triggers has left the market direction dependent on external factors. A weak start could extend into the morning session if selling pressure intensifies.

US-Iran Conflict Escalates

The overnight news flow was dominated by fresh hostilities between the United States and Iran. Reports of increased military posturing in the Middle East have rattled global markets, with investors flocking to safe-haven assets like gold and US Treasuries.

While the situation remains fluid, any further escalation could have a direct impact on oil supplies from the region. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for crude shipments, is being closely watched by traders worldwide.

Crude Oil Prices Surge

Brent crude futures jumped over 2% in early Asian trade, reflecting heightened supply risks. A sustained rise in oil prices is a negative for India, which imports nearly 85% of its crude requirements.

Higher crude prices could widen the country's trade deficit and stoke inflation, putting additional pressure on the Reserve Bank of India to maintain its hawkish stance. The rupee, too, may face depreciation pressure if oil continues to climb.

Impact on Indian Sectors

Oil marketing companies (OMCs) such as Indian Oil, BPCL, and HPCL are likely to see margin pressure if crude prices remain elevated. Conversely, upstream players like ONGC and Oil India may benefit from higher realisations.

Aviation stocks, already grappling with high fuel costs, could face further headwinds. IT and pharmaceutical sectors, which are less dependent on oil, might emerge as relative safe havens in today's session.

Global Cues Mixed

US markets ended Tuesday's session with modest losses, as geopolitical concerns overshadowed positive earnings reports. Asian markets opened lower on Wednesday, with Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's KOSPI both in the red.

European futures also pointed to a soft start, indicating that risk appetite remains fragile. Investors are now awaiting any diplomatic developments that could de-escalate the US-Iran standoff.

FII and DII Activity

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have been net sellers in recent sessions, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) have provided some support. The ongoing geopolitical tension could accelerate FII outflows, adding to market volatility.

Data from the exchanges showed that FIIs offloaded equities worth over ₹1,200 crore in Tuesday's session, while DIIs purchased shares worth ₹900 crore. This trend may continue if global risk sentiment deteriorates further.

What Investors Should Watch

Apart from crude oil and geopolitical headlines, investors will track the movement of the US dollar index and bond yields. A stronger dollar could weigh on emerging markets, including India.

Domestically, any statements from the government or RBI regarding the oil price situation will be closely monitored. The market is also likely to react to sector-specific news, especially in defence and energy.

As the session progresses, the key level to watch for the Nifty is its immediate support at the 24,500 mark. A breach below this could trigger further selling, while a recovery above 24,700 would signal resilience.

In the coming days, the trajectory of US-Iran relations and its impact on oil prices will remain the primary driver for Indian equities. Investors are advised to stay cautious and avoid taking aggressive positions until clarity emerges.
