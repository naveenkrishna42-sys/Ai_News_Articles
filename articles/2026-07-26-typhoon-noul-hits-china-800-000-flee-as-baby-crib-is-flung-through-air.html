Typhoon Noul hits China: 800,000 flee as baby crib is flung through air

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Typhoon Noul made landfall in southern China, triggering mass evacuations.
  • Over 800,000 residents have fled coastal areas ahead of the storm.
  • Visuals of a flying baby crib have gone viral, underscoring the typhoon's intensity.

Southern China is reeling after Typhoon Noul slammed into its coast, forcing more than 800,000 people to flee their homes. Dramatic visuals emerging from Guangdong province show the sheer force of the storm—including a baby crib being hurled through the air by fierce winds.

The typhoon made landfall early Sunday morning, bringing with it torrential rain and gusts that have uprooted trees and damaged buildings. Authorities had been bracing for the storm for days, issuing the highest red alert as Noul approached the densely populated region.

Mass evacuations under way

Local officials confirmed that over 800,000 residents have been moved to temporary shelters. The evacuations were largest in coastal cities like Shenzhen and Zhuhai, where flood risks were highest. Emergency teams have been going door-to-door in low-lying areas to ensure no one is left behind.

More than 340,000 people were specifically evacuated from the direct path of the typhoon, according to state media. Schools, offices and factories were shut down as a precaution. Train services and flights in and out of the region have been suspended until further notice.

Scary visuals capture storm's fury

Video clips circulating on social media show the terrifying force of Typhoon Noul. One particularly striking clip captures a baby crib being whipped into the air and smashed against a wall. The footage has drawn widespread attention, serving as a stark reminder of the storm's destructive power.

Other visuals show cars being tossed like toys, large trees bent almost to breaking point and sheets of rain lashing empty streets. Many residents have been sharing updates from inside their homes, where the roar of the wind is audible even through closed windows.

Emergency response underway

China's emergency management ministry has deployed relief teams to the worst-affected areas. Supplies including food, water and medical aid are being stockpiled in shelters. The military has also been put on standby to assist with rescue and recovery operations if needed.

Officials have warned that the typhoon could weaken as it moves inland, but the risk of flash floods and landslides remains high. Rivers in Guangdong are already swelling, and authorities are monitoring dam levels closely.

Power outages have been reported in several districts, with repair crews working to restore electricity as quickly as possible. The government has urged people to stay indoors and avoid unnecessary travel until the storm passes.

What to watch for

Weather agencies predict Noul will gradually lose strength as it moves north-east over the next 48 hours. However, the full extent of the damage will only become clear once the storm has passed and rescue teams can access affected areas. The focus now is on ensuring the safety of those evacuated and preventing further casualties.
