US and Saudi Arabia finalise civilian nuclear cooperation deal

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • The US and Saudi Arabia have signed a civilian nuclear cooperation agreement.
  • Israel has expressed fears the deal could ignite a regional arms race.
  • The agreement is seen as a major win for the Saudi kingdom.

Deal announced amid regional tensions

The United States has officially announced a civilian nuclear cooperation deal with Saudi Arabia. The agreement, confirmed by officials on Wednesday, marks a significant shift in energy diplomacy between the two nations.

Under the terms of the deal, the US will provide technical assistance and nuclear technology to Saudi Arabia for peaceful energy purposes. The arrangement is part of Washington's broader strategy to strengthen ties with Gulf allies.

The announcement was first reported by The Hindu and other major international outlets.

Israel voices alarm

Israel has reacted with concern to the news. According to a Reuters report, Israeli officials fear the Saudi nuclear deal could ignite a Middle East arms race.

Jerusalem has long opposed any nuclear programme in the region that could potentially be weaponised. While the agreement is strictly civilian, Israeli security analysts worry about long-term implications for regional stability.

The Israeli government has not issued an official statement yet. Sources indicate that backchannel discussions with Washington are underway.

A strategic win for Riyadh

The Wall Street Journal described the deal as a landmark approval by former President Donald Trump, calling it a big win for the Saudi kingdom. The agreement gives Riyadh access to American nuclear expertise without requiring it to forgo its own uranium enrichment capabilities.

This provision has been a sticking point in previous negotiations. Saudi Arabia has insisted on the right to enrich uranium domestically, a demand the US has resisted in the past with other nations.

Critics argue that the concession could set a dangerous precedent in the non-proliferation regime.

Global reactions and concerns

Al Jazeera and The Guardian have both reported on the deal, highlighting the controversy surrounding it. Questions remain about safeguards and monitoring mechanisms.

Under the agreement, Saudi Arabia must allow international inspections. However, the scope of these inspections and the timeline for implementation are not yet public.

The deal comes at a time when Iran's nuclear programme continues to be a flashpoint in the region. Experts warn that a Saudi nuclear capability, even if civilian, could trigger a cascade of nuclear ambitions among other Middle Eastern states.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact text of the agreement or its duration. Further details are expected in the coming weeks as the US State Department briefs Congress.

The next few months will be critical as the US administration faces scrutiny from both allies and adversaries over the terms of this civilian nuclear pact.
