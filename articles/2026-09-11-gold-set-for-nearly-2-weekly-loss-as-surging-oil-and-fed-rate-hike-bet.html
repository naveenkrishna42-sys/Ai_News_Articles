Why Gold Is Headed for a Nearly 2% Weekly Loss

Gold prices are on track to close the week with a loss of nearly 2%, with international spot gold falling more than 1% during the period. The twin triggers are a sharp rally in crude oil prices and a fresh repricing of US Federal Reserve rate hike expectations. When oil climbs, imported inflation risks rise, forcing the Fed to stay hawkish — a combination that strengthens the US dollar and raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset like gold.

Domestic Indian prices have mirrored the global slide, with 24K gold in major metros trading near Rs 78,500 per 10 grams and silver hovering around Rs 95,000 per kg. Retail buyers must remember that Indian bullion prices also carry import duty, dealer premiums and 3% GST, which is why the fall in rupee terms is often smaller than the fall in dollar terms.

What the Fed and Oil Story Means for Buyers

The Fed's hawkish tilt matters because gold pays no interest. When US Treasury yields rise, parking money in bonds becomes more attractive than holding bullion. A stronger dollar compounds the pain, since gold is priced in dollars globally — a stronger greenback makes the metal more expensive for buyers using other currencies, dampening demand.

Crude oil is the second lever. India imports over 80% of its oil requirement, so rising crude prices feed directly into inflation, the current account deficit and the rupee. A weaker rupee partly cushions domestic gold prices, but it also raises the effective cost for anyone converting dollars into bullion. This is why Indian retail gold prices often hold up better than international prices during a global sell-off.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Dip-buying is active: Physical demand from Indian jewellers and investors typically emerges whenever prices correct, providing a floor under the domestic market.
  • Silver is more volatile: Silver's industrial demand makes it swing harder than gold; the metal has seen sharp rebounds on dip-buying even as rate-hike bets rise.
  • USD/INR is the hidden variable: A 1% rupee depreciation can wipe out a large part of the dollar-price fall for Indian buyers.
  • Premiums and making charges matter: Jewellery carries 8-15% making charges plus 3% GST, so a 2% price dip may barely change your final bill.
  • CPI is the near-term trigger: A hot US inflation print could push gold below $2,480/oz; a soft print could revive the dip-buying rally.

Buyer's Playbook: What to Do Now

For investors, the current correction is a staggered-accumulation opportunity rather than a signal to go all-in. Splitting purchases across the next few weeks — ideally around the US CPI release — lets you average out volatility. Sovereign Gold Bonds and gold ETFs avoid making charges and GST on jewellery, making them more cost-efficient for pure investment exposure.

For jewellery buyers, the festive season is approaching, and dealers often bundle discounts on making charges. Compare the final landed price per gram across at least three jewellers, and check the day's USD/INR rate before locking a purchase. If the rupee weakens further, domestic prices could rebound even if international gold stays flat.

Watch the US CPI number closely. It is the single biggest near-term trigger for gold, and it will decide whether this weekly loss deepens into a larger correction or becomes a short-lived dip that buyers rush to exploit.
