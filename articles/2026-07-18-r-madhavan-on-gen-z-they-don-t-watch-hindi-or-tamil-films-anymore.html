R Madhavan on Gen Z: They don't watch Hindi or Tamil films anymore

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • R Madhavan observed that his son Vedaant and his peers do not watch Hindi or Tamil films.
  • The actor highlighted a generational shift in entertainment preferences among young Indians.
  • Madhavan's comments reflect a broader trend of declining interest in regional and Bollywood cinema among Gen Z.

Madhavan's observation on Gen Z viewing habits

Bollywood actor R Madhavan has made a candid observation about the current generation of youngsters. He says his son Vedaant's generation simply does not watch Hindi or Tamil movies anymore.

Speaking to the media, the actor shared his personal experience. He noted that young people today have very different entertainment choices compared to earlier generations.

Personal anecdote about son Vedaant

Madhavan pointed to his own son as an example. Vedaant, a budding swimmer, and his friends rarely engage with mainstream Indian cinema.

This is not a criticism but a fact, the actor clarified. He sees it as a natural evolution of tastes in a globalised world.

Shift in content consumption

The actor believes the shift is driven by the availability of global content on streaming platforms. Youngsters now have access to shows and films from around the world at their fingertips.

Hindi and Tamil films are no longer the default entertainment choice for many in this demographic. They prefer content that resonates with their global outlook, Madhavan suggested.

Industry experts have noted similar trends. Theatrical footfalls for regional and Bollywood films have seen a dip among younger audiences in urban centres.

Broader implications for Indian cinema

Madhavan's statement adds to a growing conversation about the future of Indian cinema. Filmmakers are increasingly trying to understand what draws Gen Z to the theatres or their screens.

The actor did not suggest that Indian cinema is dying. He merely pointed out that the audience is changing, and the industry must adapt.

Producers are now experimenting with shorter formats, web series, and genre-bending stories to capture this elusive demographic. The challenge is to make content that feels relevant without losing cultural roots.

Madhavan himself has been part of projects that blend traditional storytelling with modern themes. He remains optimistic about the medium's ability to evolve.

What happens next is uncertain. The film industry will likely continue to track these viewing patterns closely as it plans its next slate of releases.
