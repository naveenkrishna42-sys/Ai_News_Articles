Rahul Gandhi at Prayagraj: 'Degree means nothing in India today'

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rahul Gandhi addressed students at 'Chhatron ki Goonj' event in Prayagraj.
  • He said a degree means nothing in India today due to lack of jobs.
  • Targeted the Centre over unemployment, using 'dard, data, daulat' framework.

PRAYAGRAJ: Congress leader Rahul Gandhi turned his focus to the country's youth on Saturday, addressing a gathering of students at an event titled 'Chhatron ki Goonj' in Prayagraj. His message was blunt: a degree, he said, means nothing in India today.

The former Congress president used the occasion to launch a sharp critique of the central government, weaving his argument around three words — 'dard' (pain), 'data', and 'daulat' (wealth).

'Certificates have no meaning'

Gandhi told the assembled students that the current system has stripped academic qualifications of their value. "Every student here knows that the system is dishonest," he said, according to reports from the event.

His remarks come against the backdrop of persistent concerns over unemployment and underemployment among the country's educated youth. The Congress leader sought to connect the dots between educational credentials and the lack of corresponding job opportunities.

'Dard, data, daulat' — a three-part attack

The Congress leader structured his address around the alliterative triad. 'Dard' referred to the struggles and anxieties faced by students and job-seekers. 'Data' was used to point at official statistics, which he suggested fail to reflect the real scale of the crisis.

'Daulat' — wealth — was the third pillar. Gandhi implied that the concentration of wealth was linked to the systemic failures affecting the youth. He accused the government of using force to silence dissenting voices, a charge that has been a recurring theme in his recent public engagements.

A direct appeal to the youth

The event was designed as a dialogue with students, and Gandhi appeared to be making a direct appeal to this demographic ahead of upcoming electoral battles. His choice of words — colloquial, emotionally charged, and pointed — seemed aimed at resonating with a generation frustrated by the job market.

Political observers note that the Congress has been trying to position itself as the voice of the unemployed youth. Gandhi's Prayagraj address is the latest in a series of outreach efforts targeting students and first-time voters.

Voices from the ground

Reports from the venue suggested that many in the audience responded to Gandhi's framing. Some students were heard echoing his sentiment that the system was rigged against them. The event also saw participants speak about their own experiences of struggling to find meaningful employment despite holding degrees.

There was no immediate official response from the central government or the BJP to Gandhi's remarks. The ruling party has in the past dismissed his criticisms as politically motivated rhetoric.

What remains to be seen is whether this outreach translates into electoral gains. With state elections and a general election on the horizon, the youth vote is a prize both major political formations are aggressively courting. Gandhi's 'Chhatron ki Goonj' initiative is likely to travel to more cities, keeping the spotlight on jobs and education.
