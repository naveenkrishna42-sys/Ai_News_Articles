Sensex slips 188 points, Nifty ends lower as Tata stocks drag, crude stays high

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 188 points, Nifty down 0.25%
  • Tata group stocks among top losers
  • Crude oil prices stay high, pressuring sentiment
  • Broader market breadth negative

Indian equity benchmarks closed in the red on Wednesday, with the Sensex sliding 188 points and the Nifty ending lower as selling pressure in Tata group stocks and persistent strength in global crude prices kept investors cautious.

The 30-share Sensex settled at 78,954, while the Nifty closed near the 24,000 mark, down about 0.25 per cent. Market breadth was negative, with more than two stocks declining for every advancing one on the BSE.

Tata stocks drag

Tata Consultancy Services, Tata Motors, and Tata Steel were among the biggest drags on the indices. The decline in Tata group stocks came amid reports that the conglomerate is in discussions to acquire a stake in a struggling rival, though no official confirmation has been made.

TCS, the country's largest software services exporter, fell over 1 per cent, while Tata Motors and Tata Steel also ended lower. The combined losses in these three heavyweights accounted for a significant portion of the Sensex's decline.

Crude oil remains a worry

Global crude oil prices stayed elevated, with Brent trading above USD 85 a barrel, as supply concerns persisted. Higher crude prices are a double-edged sword for India, which imports over 80 per cent of its oil needs, putting pressure on the rupee and raising inflationary concerns.

Rising crude prices typically hurt sectors like aviation, paints, and lubricants, while benefiting upstream oil producers. Today, oil marketing companies ended mixed, with HPCL and BPCL closing lower, while ONGC gained marginally.

Broader market and sectoral performance

Sectorally, IT, auto, and metal stocks were the worst hit, while FMCG and pharma offered some support. The BSE Midcap index fell 0.4 per cent, while the Smallcap index slipped 0.6 per cent, indicating a risk-off mood among investors.

Among individual stocks, Vodafone Idea surged over 5 per cent after the company announced a tariff hike for its prepaid plans, while Zomato ended 2 per cent higher on renewed buying interest. Kotak Mahindra Bank, HDFC Bank, and Reliance Industries were among the top Sensex gainers, limiting the downside.

What's next for the market

Investors will now watch for cues from global markets, particularly the US Federal Reserve's stance on interest rates, and any movement in crude prices. The upcoming release of domestic inflation data later this week could also influence sentiment.

Analysts suggest that the market is likely to remain range-bound in the near term, with stock-specific action driven by corporate earnings and global cues. The Nifty has support at the 23,800-23,900 zone, while resistance is placed around the 24,200 level.

The coming sessions will be crucial as traders position themselves for the monthly derivatives expiry and ahead of the US inflation print due later this week.
