Centre Deploys NJORT as Multi-Institutional Probe Intensifies into Chandipura Virus Outbreak

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Centre deploys National Joint Outbreak Response Team (NJORT) to Gujarat
  • Multi-institutional probe launched to investigate the Chandipura virus outbreak
  • Gujarat health machinery placed on high alert; 22 children dead, seven under treatment

The Centre has deployed the National Joint Outbreak Response Team (NJORT) to Gujarat as a multi-institutional probe into the Chandipura virus outbreak intensifies. The move comes amid rising concerns over the viral encephalitis that has claimed at least 22 children in the state this monsoon, with seven more currently under treatment.

NJORT, a specialised rapid-response unit, is expected to assist state health authorities in containing the spread and strengthening surveillance. The deployment signals a coordinated central-state effort to tackle what health officials describe as a seasonal but deadly outbreak.

Multi-Institutional Probe Underway

The investigation involves multiple agencies, including the National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC), the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR), and the National Institute of Virology (NIV), Pune. These institutions are working together to identify the source of the outbreak, trace transmission patterns, and assess the effectiveness of current containment measures.

Preliminary findings suggest a link between the outbreak and sandfly breeding, which peaks during the monsoon. The virus is believed to be transmitted through the bite of infected sandflies, though the exact ecological factors driving this year's surge remain under study.

Gujarat on High Alert

Gujarat's health machinery has been placed on high alert, with district authorities instructed to report any suspected cases immediately. Hospitals in affected areas have been directed to maintain adequate stocks of essential medicines and ensure readiness of paediatric intensive care units.

Health teams have been deployed to conduct door-to-door surveys in affected villages, focusing on early detection and referral. The state government has also launched an awareness campaign to educate parents about the early symptoms of Chandipura virus, which include sudden fever, convulsions, and altered consciousness.

Monsoon-Sandfly Connection Explained

Experts point to the monsoon as a critical factor in Chandipura virus outbreaks. Rainfall creates ideal breeding conditions for sandflies, which thrive in moist, shaded environments. The virus, named after the Chandipura village in Maharashtra where it was first isolated in 1965, has historically caused sporadic outbreaks in central and western India.

This year's pattern is consistent with past trends, but the high fatality rate among children has heightened concern. Health officials emphasise that the first 24 hours after symptom onset are crucial, as timely medical intervention can significantly improve survival chances.

Treatment and Case Management

Currently, seven children are under treatment in hospitals, with their conditions reported as stable. There is no specific antiviral therapy for Chandipura virus, and treatment remains supportive, focusing on managing fever, seizures, and neurological complications.

Doctors are urging parents to seek immediate medical care if a child develops high fever with neurological symptoms, especially in areas where cases have been reported. Early hospitalisation and intensive care have been shown to reduce mortality in similar viral encephalitis outbreaks.

While the case fatality rate for Chandipura virus can be high, particularly in children, health authorities believe that improved surveillance and rapid response can help mitigate the impact. The deployment of NJORT is expected to bolster these efforts.

What to Watch

The coming weeks will be critical as the monsoon continues and the multi-institutional probe gathers more data. Health officials will closely monitor the effectiveness of containment measures and whether the outbreak shows signs of subsiding. The findings of the investigation are expected to inform future preparedness strategies for vector-borne viral outbreaks in the region.
