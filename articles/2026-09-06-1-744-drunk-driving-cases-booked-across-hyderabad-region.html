Enforcement Drive Covers Three Jurisdictions

The Hindu reported on 6 Sep 2026 that 1,744 persons were booked for drunk driving in Hyderabad, Cyberabad and Future City. The figure reflects coordinated checks by traffic police across the three jurisdictions. The report confirms a concentrated enforcement push rather than routine sporadic action.

Coverage of Hyderabad city, Cyberabad police commissionerate and Future City indicates a regional approach to road safety. Joint operations increase checkpoint density and reduce displacement of offenders between jurisdictions. For drivers, this means higher probability of interception during late night and weekend hours.

Legal Framework And Penalties For Drunk Driving In India

Drunk driving is punishable under Section 185 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, as amended in 2019. The law sets a legal blood alcohol limit of 30 mg per 100 ml of blood. Testing is done via breath analyser, and refusal to test is treated as an offence.

First Offence And Repeat Offence Consequences

A first conviction carries a fine of up to Rs 10,000 and imprisonment up to six months. A repeat offence within three years attracts a fine of up to Rs 15,000 and imprisonment up to two years. Courts may also order licence disqualification for a period of up to three years.

Administrative Actions By Police

Police can seize the vehicle and issue an immediate challan under the e-challan system. The vehicle is typically released to a sober authorised person after payment of fines and production of documents. Licence suspension proceedings are initiated separately by the Regional Transport Office on receipt of court orders.

Real-World Impact For Commuters And Fleet Operators

For private vehicle owners, a booking creates a criminal record entry and impacts insurance claims. Insurers treat drunk driving as a contributory negligence event and may reject or reduce claim payouts in related accidents. Fleet operators face operational downtime, driver deputation issues and reputational risk.

Employers with company vehicles are increasingly mandating zero-tolerance alcohol policies and pre-shift checks. Ride-hailing platforms face indirect pressure as customers shift to safer options during enforcement drives. Night economy businesses near checkpoints report altered customer movement patterns.
