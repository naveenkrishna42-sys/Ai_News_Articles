Banks' NIMs, deposit pricing in focus as brokerages drop RBI rate hike calls

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Brokerages have withdrawn predictions of RBI rate hikes, signaling a shift in monetary policy expectations.
  • Banks' net interest margins and deposit pricing strategies are now under greater scrutiny.
  • The change comes as inflation moderates and economic growth shows mixed signals.

The chorus of rate hike calls from brokerages has faded. After months of speculation that the Reserve Bank of India would tighten policy further, most analysts have now dropped their predictions. The focus has moved squarely to how banks manage their net interest margins (NIMs) and deposit pricing in a steady-rate environment.

Why rate hike calls vanished

Several factors drove the reversal. Inflation has softened more than expected, with retail inflation hovering within the RBI's comfort zone. At the same time, industrial production data has been patchy, giving the central bank room to hold fire.

"The probability of a rate hike in the next policy meeting has dropped sharply," a banking analyst at a Mumbai-based brokerage said. "The RBI's stance is now seen as neutral to accommodative."

Global cues also played a role. The US Federal Reserve has signalled a pause, reducing pressure on emerging market central banks to act. The RBI's own commentary in recent minutes has struck a balanced tone, with no urgency to move rates.

NIMs under the lens

With rates steady, banks' NIMs become the key metric to watch. NIMs—the difference between interest earned and interest paid—had expanded during the earlier tightening cycle as lenders repriced loans faster than deposits.

That benefit is now fading. Wholesale deposit rates have begun to inch up as competition for funds intensifies. Some banks have raised bulk deposit rates by 10-20 basis points in recent weeks. Retail deposit rates, however, remain sticky, offering a temporary cushion.

"The pressure on NIMs will be more visible from the September quarter onwards," warned a credit analyst tracking mid-sized private lenders. "Banks that rely heavily on wholesale funding will feel it first."

Deposit pricing becomes a tightrope walk

Banks face a delicate balancing act. They must attract deposits to fund credit growth, which has been robust, especially in the retail and MSME segments. But raising deposit rates too quickly would squeeze margins.

Public sector banks, which have a large low-cost current and savings account (CASA) base, are in a relatively better position. Private lenders, especially those with lower CASA ratios, are more vulnerable.

Some banks have started offering limited-period special deposit schemes to shore up their liability franchise. Others are leaning on certificate of deposits (CDs) to bridge the gap, though that adds to cost.

The RBI's liquidity management will be crucial. If the central bank continues to drain surplus liquidity, banks may have to raise deposit rates further to retain funds.

Credit growth vs margin trade-off

Loan growth remains healthy. Bank credit expanded by nearly 15% year-on-year in the latest fortnight, driven by personal loans, home loans, and working capital demand. But this growth is happening in a tighter liquidity environment.

"Banks cannot grow loans without growing deposits," a senior banker at a large private lender said. "If deposit growth lags, we have to either ration credit or pay more for funds. Both hit NIMs."

Some lenders are already becoming more selective in their lending, especially on unsecured loans, to protect margins. The shift could moderate credit growth in the coming months.

Market participants are now watching the next round of quarterly earnings closely. Management commentary on NIM guidance and deposit mobilization will be the key trigger for stock movements.

For now, the sector is in a wait-and-watch mode. With rate hike fears off the table, the spotlight is on how well banks navigate the trickier terrain of margin management and deposit mobilization in a stable rate world.

What to watch: The RBI's next monetary policy statement and any shift in its liquidity stance will set the tone for deposit pricing. Banks' quarterly results due in the next few weeks will offer the first concrete clues on NIM trajectory.
