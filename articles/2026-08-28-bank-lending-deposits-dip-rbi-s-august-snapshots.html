Bank Lending, Deposits Show Mixed Picture in August

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has released its fortnightly data painting a mixed picture of India's banking sector, with bank credit growth decelerating and deposit inflows moderating during the period ended August 15, 2026.

Credit Growth Slows Down

The pace of bank credit growth cooled, with gross bank credit growth at 13.4% year-on-year (YoY), down from 14.9% in the previous fortnight. This slowdown reflects reduced corporate demand for credit, possibly due to ongoing global economic uncertainties.

Expert analysis: "This slowdown in credit growth is concerning, as it indicates a softening in business sentiment. Companies are either holding back on expansion plans or prioritising cash preservation," says Dr. Meghana Kulkarni, Senior Economist at the Centre for Economic Data and Analysis.

Deposit Inflows Moderate

Bank deposits, which typically drive lending, grew by 12.9% YoY, down from 13.5% in the previous fortnight. This moderation could be due to reduced household savings or increased investment in alternative assets.

"The moderation in deposit growth suggests that households might be deploying their surplus funds elsewhere, such as in mutual funds or real estate," adds Dr. Kulkarni.

RBI Data: A Barometer of Banking Health

RBI's fortnightly data is a critical indicator of the health and direction of the banking sector. It provides real-time insights into lending and deposit trends, helping policy makers and industry stakeholders gauge economic sentiment and adjust their strategies accordingly.

Key Takeaways

  • Slowdown in bank credit growth suggests reduced corporate demand for loans or cautious business sentiment.
  • Moderation in deposit inflows could indicate reduced household savings or increased investment in alternative assets.
  • RBI data offers valuable insights into the banking sector's health and broader economic trends.

Experts Weigh In

While the latest data points to a slowdown in credit growth, experts are divided on the outlook for the coming months. Some, like Dr. Kulkarni, expect the trend to continue, while others hope that upcoming festive seasons and government initiatives will boost demand for credit.

"The coming months will be crucial to see if this is a temporary blip or a longer-term trend," says Dr. Kulkarni. "Policy makers and banks need to remain vigilant and ensure lending continues to support economic growth."

As the RBI continues to monitor and report on the banking sector's health, stakeholders will eagerly await the next set of fortnightly data to gauge the direction of the economy.
