Venezuela earthquake death toll crosses 5,000; IMF releases emergency funds

📅 2026-07-19 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Death toll from the twin earthquakes in Venezuela has exceeded 5,000, three weeks after the disaster.
  • International Monetary Fund has released emergency financial aid to support relief efforts.
  • A new report highlights that confusion and a lack of equipment severely hampered the initial response.
  • Rescue and recovery operations continue amid challenging conditions.

Three weeks after a pair of powerful earthquakes struck Venezuela, the confirmed death toll has crossed the 5,000 mark. The grim milestone was confirmed by local authorities on Saturday, as rescue teams continue to sift through rubble in the hardest-hit regions.

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) has announced the release of emergency financial aid to the South American nation. The funds are intended to support ongoing relief and recovery operations, though specific figures have not been disclosed by the organisation.

Report highlights response failures

A detailed assessment of the disaster response, released this week, paints a stark picture of confusion and critical equipment shortages. The report, prepared by an international humanitarian agency, notes that first responders were often unable to reach victims quickly due to damaged infrastructure and a lack of coordination.

“The initial hours were chaotic,” the report states, citing interviews with local officials and volunteers. It adds that heavy machinery, search dogs, and medical supplies were in short supply, slowing down efforts to pull survivors from collapsed buildings.

The findings have raised questions about the preparedness of Venezuelan authorities to handle a disaster of this scale. The twin quakes, which struck within hours of each other, devastated several cities and towns, leaving thousands homeless.

Relief efforts face logistical hurdles

International aid has begun to arrive, but distribution remains a major challenge. Damaged roads and bridges in mountainous areas have cut off some communities entirely. Relief workers report that fuel shortages, a chronic issue in Venezuela, are now hampering the transport of food, water, and medicine.

Local hospitals are overwhelmed. Many are operating without reliable electricity or running water. Field hospitals set up by foreign medical teams are providing crucial support, but they too are stretched thin.

The government has appealed for additional international assistance, including specialised rescue equipment and mobile medical units. Neighbouring countries, including Colombia and Brazil, have sent supplies and personnel.

Survivors struggle to rebuild

For the millions affected, daily life remains a battle. Temporary shelters are overcrowded, and there are growing concerns about the spread of waterborne diseases. Aid agencies are rushing to provide clean drinking water and sanitation facilities.

Many survivors have lost everything. In the city of Cumaná, one of the worst-hit areas, residents have begun clearing debris by hand, waiting for heavy equipment that has yet to arrive. “We have no government help here,” one resident told reporters. “We are on our own.”

The psychological toll is also immense. Grief counsellors are working with children and families, but the scale of the trauma far exceeds available resources.

What comes next

The IMF funds will provide a temporary cushion, but the road to recovery will be long and costly. The focus now is on preventing a secondary humanitarian crisis as the rainy season approaches, threatening further damage to unstable structures. Officials have not yet provided a timeline for completing search operations or a final death count.
