DMK Moves Supreme Court Seeking Urgent Cauvery Water Release by Karnataka

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • DMK has approached the Supreme Court seeking urgent directions to Karnataka for Cauvery water release.
  • Karnataka CM urges opposition and farmers to withdraw the August 13 bandh over the Cauvery issue.
  • Tamil Nadu CM agrees to defer Bengaluru visit amid Cauvery tensions; Mekedatu project pitched as permanent solution.

DMK Moves Top Court Amid Escalating Cauvery Row

The Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam (DMK) has moved the Supreme Court seeking directions to Karnataka for the urgent release of Cauvery water, pressing the court to intervene as the long-running dispute spirals into a fresh political flashpoint. The plea, reported by Live Law, comes at a time when both states are locked in a bitter public spat over water sharing, with Tamil Nadu insisting on immediate releases to save its standing crops.

The DMK's move signals a hardening of stance in Tamil Nadu, where the ruling party has faced pressure from farmers and opposition alike to secure the state's share of the river water. Legal experts suggest the Supreme Court's intervention could set the tone for how the dispute is managed in the coming weeks, particularly as the southwest monsoon remains erratic.

Karnataka's Bandh Call and Political Wrangling

In a parallel development, Karnataka Chief Minister has urged opposition parties and farmers to withdraw the proposed August 13 bandh over the Cauvery issue. The bandh, called by pro-Kannada outfits, threatens to disrupt normal life in Bengaluru and other parts of the state, adding a volatile layer to the water dispute.

The Chief Minister's appeal comes amid growing frustration in Karnataka over what it perceives as Tamil Nadu's aggressive legal and political tactics. However, the call for withdrawal has not yet yielded a clear response, with farmer groups insisting that their grievances over water sharing must be addressed first.

Tamil Nadu CM Defers Bengaluru Visit; DKS Calls Vijay 'Courageous'

Tensions have also spilled into cross-state political exchanges. Tamil Nadu Chief Minister's decision to defer a scheduled visit to Bengaluru, citing the Cauvery row, has drawn sharp reactions. Karnataka Deputy Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar, in a remark quoted by Hindustan Times, described Tamil Nadu BJP leader Vijay as a 'courageous leader' for agreeing to postpone the visit, a comment that has stirred speculation about shifting political equations.

Shivakumar's praise of Vijay, who has been critical of the DMK's handling of the Cauvery issue, appears aimed at widening the fault lines in Tamil Nadu's political landscape. Vijay's camp has also trained its guns on Shivakumar, with a minister in his outfit accusing the Karnataka CM of being beholden to DMK chief M.K. Stalin rather than the Congress high command.

Mekedatu Project Pitched as 'Permanent Solution'

Amid the legal and political maneuvering, Karnataka CM DK Shivakumar has once again pushed the Mekedatu project as the definitive answer to the Cauvery conundrum. Speaking to The Times of India, Shivakumar argued that the proposed reservoir across the Cauvery in Karnataka would ensure a regulated supply of water to Tamil Nadu while addressing Karnataka's own drinking water needs.

However, Tamil Nadu has consistently opposed the Mekedatu project, viewing it as an encroachment on its riparian rights. The Supreme Court had earlier allowed Karnataka to conduct a detailed project report, but the final clearance remains pending, making it a contentious issue that could further complicate the ongoing legal battle.

What Lies Ahead

The Supreme Court's response to the DMK plea will be closely watched, as will the fate of the August 13 bandh in Karnataka. With both states digging in, the coming weeks are likely to see more legal filings, political barbs, and public protests. The Mekedatu project, meanwhile, remains a distant solution, leaving the immediate question of water release unresolved.

As the Cauvery dispute continues to simmer, all eyes will be on the apex court to provide a clear directive that could either ease tensions or push the two states further into confrontation.
