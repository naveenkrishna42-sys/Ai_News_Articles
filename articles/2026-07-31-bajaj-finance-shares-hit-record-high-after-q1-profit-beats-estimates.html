Bajaj Finance shares hit record high after Q1 profit beats estimates

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bajaj Finance shares jumped 9% to hit a fresh all-time high in early trade.
  • Q1 profit exceeded street estimates, boosting sentiment across the Bajaj group.
  • Bajaj Housing Finance also raised its FY27 profitability outlook despite margin pressure.
  • Analysts are split on the stock's valuation, with some recommending 'buy' and others advising caution.

Mumbai: Bajaj Finance shares surged as much as 9% in early trade on Friday, hitting a fresh all-time high, after the non-banking financial company (NBFC) reported a stronger-than-expected profit for the June quarter. The stock's rally also lifted the broader indices, with the Sensex edging up 50 points to 77,970 and the Nifty gaining 28 points in early deals.

The sharp move came as investors cheered the company's Q1 numbers, which beat consensus estimates on the back of robust loan growth and improving asset quality. The stock's rally pushed its market capitalisation to a new peak, underscoring renewed optimism in the consumer finance space.

What drove the rally

The company's net profit for the quarter exceeded street expectations, helped by steady demand for consumer and small business loans. Net interest margin remained stable, while provisions for bad loans were lower than feared, analysts said.

The upbeat results also spilled over to other Bajaj group stocks. Bajaj Finserv, the parent company, jumped 6% in early trade, while Bajaj Housing Finance raised its FY27 profitability outlook despite ongoing margin pressure. The housing finance arm's revised guidance signals confidence in the group's ability to navigate a competitive lending environment.

Should you buy, sell, or hold?

The question on every investor's mind is whether the stock, after such a sharp run-up, still offers value. Brokerages are divided. Some have maintained a 'buy' rating, citing the company's strong franchise, digital capabilities, and headroom for growth in the under-penetrated consumer credit market.

Others, however, have turned cautious, pointing to the stock's rich valuation and potential headwinds from rising funding costs and regulatory scrutiny in the unsecured lending segment. A few have downgraded the stock to 'hold', arguing that the near-term upside is limited after the recent surge.

What analysts are watching

Key factors to monitor include the trajectory of credit costs, the pace of loan growth, and the company's ability to maintain margins in a rising interest rate environment. The management's commentary on asset quality, especially in microfinance and unsecured loans, will be crucial in the coming quarters.

For now, the market's reaction suggests that the positive surprise in Q1 earnings has outweighed concerns around valuation. But with the stock at a record high, the margin of safety has narrowed.

Broader market context

The rally in Bajaj Finance also provided a lift to the financial sector, with banking and NBFC stocks trading firm in early deals. The Sensex and Nifty held onto gains, though the broader market remained range-bound ahead of key global cues.

Investors will now parse the fine print of the earnings release and management commentary for signals on future growth. The company's annual general meeting, scheduled later this year, could offer more clarity on strategy and capital allocation.

As the session progresses, traders will watch whether the stock can sustain its gains or if profit-booking emerges at higher levels. The coming weeks will reveal if the market's optimism is justified or if the current valuation leaves little room for error.
