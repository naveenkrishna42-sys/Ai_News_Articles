Wakf Board Complains of Land Encroachment Near Hyderabad Airport

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Wakf Board has lodged a formal complaint regarding alleged encroachment on its property near the airport.
  • The complaint has been filed with the authorities, who have initiated an inquiry.
  • Officials have not yet provided details on the extent of the alleged encroachment.

The Telangana State Wakf Board has lodged a formal complaint with authorities over the alleged encroachment of its land parcels situated in the vicinity of the Rajiv Gandhi International Airport at Shamshabad. The move underscores growing concerns about unauthorized occupation of waqf properties, which are considered religious endowments under Islamic law.

According to sources familiar with the development, the complaint was filed recently, prompting revenue and police officials to take cognizance of the matter. The exact location and the extent of the land under dispute have not been officially disclosed, but the Board has urged swift action to protect its assets.

Nature of the Alleged Encroachment

The Wakf Board's complaint alleges that certain individuals or entities have illegally occupied portions of the land, which is designated for charitable or religious purposes. Such encroachments have been a recurring issue across the state, often leading to legal battles and administrative delays.

Officials familiar with the case said the Board has submitted documents and survey records to substantiate its claim. However, no formal statement has been issued by the Wakf Board or the district administration detailing the specific parcels or the timeline of the alleged occupation.

Authorities Begin Preliminary Inquiry

Following the complaint, revenue officials have reportedly initiated a preliminary inquiry to verify the ownership records and assess the ground situation. Police have also been alerted to prevent any potential law-and-order complications during the investigation.

An official from the Shamshabad revenue division, speaking on condition of anonymity, said that a field inspection would be conducted to determine the actual status of the land. "We have received the complaint and will follow due process," the official said, without elaborating on the specifics.

Recurring Issue of Waqf Land

The alleged encroachment near the airport is not an isolated incident. Waqf properties across Telangana have often been at the centre of disputes, with claims and counterclaims over their use and ownership. The state government had earlier formed committees to address such grievances, but implementation has been uneven.

In recent years, the Wakf Board has stepped up efforts to survey its holdings using modern technology, including GPS mapping, to curb illegal occupation. Yet, enforcement remains a challenge, particularly in fast-developing areas like the airport belt, where land values have skyrocketed.

Next Steps and Legal Recourse

The Board is likely to approach the civil court if the administrative machinery fails to resolve the matter, sources indicated. Under the Waqf Act, the Board has the authority to evict encroachers and recover possession of its properties, but such processes often require prolonged litigation.

Meanwhile, local residents and real estate stakeholders in the area are watching the development closely, as any legal action could affect land transactions in the vicinity. The district administration has not yet announced a timeline for completing the inquiry.

As the investigation unfolds, the focus will be on how quickly the authorities can verify the claims and whether the Wakf Board's efforts to reclaim its land yield tangible results. The case also highlights the broader need for a robust mechanism to protect waqf assets from encroachment, a challenge that persists despite existing legal provisions.
