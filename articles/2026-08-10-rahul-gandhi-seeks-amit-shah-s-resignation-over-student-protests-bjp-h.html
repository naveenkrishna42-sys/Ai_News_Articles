Rahul Gandhi seeks Amit Shah's resignation over student protests; BJP hits back

📅 2026-08-10 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rahul Gandhi accused Amit Shah of being 'either guilty or incompetent' over police action against student protesters
  • Gandhi demanded Shah's resignation and said neither PM nor HM have courage to address the House
  • BJP's JP Nadda hit back, claiming Gandhi is avoiding substantive debate; political slugfest escalates

Parliament turns combative over student protests

The monsoon session of Parliament witnessed a heated exchange on Monday as Congress leader Rahul Gandhi launched a blistering attack on Union Home Minister Amit Shah, demanding his resignation over the police action against student protesters. Gandhi's remarks drew an immediate and sharp retort from the Bharatiya Janata Party, turning the proceedings into a high-voltage political showdown.

Raising the issue during the session, Gandhi said the country wants to know whether Shah ordered the shooting of protesters. He asserted that the Home Minister must take responsibility for the actions of the police forces under his charge. "Either guilty or incompetent," Gandhi said, demanding a statement from Shah on the matter.

'Neither PM nor HM have courage'

Gandhi went further, alleging that neither Prime Minister Narendra Modi nor Home Minister Amit Shah had the courage to come to the House and address the concerns raised over the protests. He accused the government of avoiding accountability and sidestepping a direct discussion on the use of force against students.

"Not interested in Amit Shah's speech in Parliament on general topics," Gandhi said, making it clear that his demand was specific: a clear, on-record answer about the decision-making behind the police action. The Congress leader's remarks were aimed at cornering the government on a politically sensitive issue that has seen protests spread across multiple states.

BJP's counter-offensive

The BJP was quick to respond, with party president JP Nadda leading the charge. Nadda dismissed Gandhi's allegations as a diversionary tactic, claiming the Congress leader was avoiding substantive debate on key national issues. The BJP's counter-attack sought to turn the tables, accusing Gandhi of politicising a law-and-order situation for electoral gain.

Sources within the government indicated that the BJP would not yield ground, with party MPs instructed to highlight the Congress's own record on handling protests during its tenure at the Centre. The sharp exchange reflects the deepening political divide as both parties prepare for upcoming electoral battles.

A question of accountability

The core of Gandhi's demand rests on a question of accountability: who authorised the police action, and was the force used proportionate to the situation? The student protests, which began over issues related to educational policies and campus conditions, have escalated into a broader confrontation between demonstrators and authorities.

While the government has defended the police response as necessary to maintain public order, critics argue that the use of force against unarmed students raises serious questions about civil liberties. The demand for Shah's resignation has added a layer of political confrontation, with the opposition seeking to turn the protests into a referendum on the government's handling of dissent.

Officials have not yet confirmed any formal inquiry into the police action, and the Home Ministry has not issued a detailed response to Gandhi's allegations. The lack of clarity has allowed both sides to frame the narrative to their advantage, with the opposition citing public anger and the ruling party citing the need for firm action.

What lies ahead

As the monsoon session continues, the opposition is likely to press for a formal discussion on the protests, potentially forcing a vote or a walkout. The BJP, for its part, appears prepared to counter every allegation with a mix of denial and counter-charges. The coming days will test whether the government can weather the political storm or whether the demand for Shah's resignation gains traction beyond Parliament.

With both sides digging in, the student protests have become a flashpoint in India's political discourse, and the next move from either camp could set the tone for the remainder of the session.
