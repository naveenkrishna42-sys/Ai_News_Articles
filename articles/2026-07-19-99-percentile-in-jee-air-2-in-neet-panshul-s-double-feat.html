99 percentile in JEE, AIR 2 in NEET: Panshul's double feat

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Panshul achieved 99 percentile in JEE Main and All India Rank 2 in NEET UG 2026.
  • He attributes success to a disciplined routine, avoiding social media, and focused preparation.
  • NEET UG 2026 saw over 58% female qualifiers, with Shravani Kudale securing AIR 5.
  • Panshul's journey included overcoming challenges like the NEET retest and maintaining consistency.

A rare double achievement

A student from Rajasthan has stunned the academic world by securing a 99 percentile in JEE Main and an All India Rank (AIR) 2 in the NEET UG 2026 examinations. Panshul, whose full name has not been disclosed by authorities, has become the new toast of the education circuit. His feat is being hailed as extraordinary, given the divergent focus required for engineering and medical entrance tests.

Panshul's performance places him among a tiny fraction of students who have topped both streams. While the JEE Main tests aptitude for engineering, NEET UG demands deep knowledge of biology, chemistry, and physics. Balancing both is a nightmare for most aspirants.

Strategy and sacrifice

In interviews following the results, Panshul revealed his preparation blueprint. He stayed away from phone and social media for an entire year. "I treated every day as a battle. No distractions, only goals," he told reporters. His routine involved 12-14 hours of study, with breaks limited to short walks and meals.

He credited his teachers and parents for the support. But he also acknowledged the pressure. The NEET retest, triggered by a paper leak controversy, added to the stress. "The retest was a nightmare. I had to reset my strategy overnight," he said. He focused on revising core concepts and managing time during the exam.

NEET 2026: Women dominate qualifiers

The NEET UG 2026 results also highlighted a broader trend. Women formed over 58% of the total qualifiers, marking a significant shift in the medical entrance landscape. Among the top ranks, Shravani Kudale from Pune secured AIR 5. She started her medical entrance preparation in class 9 and credited her success to a disciplined approach.

Shravani, like Panshul, emphasized staying away from social media. "I stayed away from phone and social media for a year. It was tough, but it paid off," she said. Her story mirrors that of many top scorers who prioritize focus over fleeting online engagement.

The retest horror and reset

The NEET retest, ordered by the Supreme Court after allegations of a paper leak, disrupted plans for lakhs of students. Panshul described the retest as a "horror" that forced him to rethink his approach. He had to quickly adapt to a new set of questions and a changed environment. "I panicked initially, but then I told myself: this is just another exam," he recalled.

His reset strategy involved revisiting NCERT textbooks and solving previous years' papers. He also practiced meditation to stay calm. The result was a stellar performance that secured him AIR 2.

What sets Panshul apart

Experts say Panshul's double success is rare because JEE and NEET require different skill sets. JEE focuses on problem-solving speed and advanced mathematics, while NEET tests memory and application in biology. Panshul managed to excel in both by dividing his time intelligently. He spent mornings on physics and chemistry, afternoons on biology, and evenings on mathematics.

His parents, who run a small business in Rajasthan, said they never pressured him. "We just told him to do his best. He did more," his father said. The family is now weighing options—engineering or medicine. Panshul has not yet decided.

As the dust settles on the results, Panshul's story is a reminder that discipline and sacrifice can yield extraordinary outcomes. The next challenge for him is choosing a path. For now, he is enjoying the moment.
