CWG 2026: Tejaswin Shankar wins historic decathlon bronze for India

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tejaswin Shankar won bronze in men's decathlon at CWG 2026 with 7976 points.
  • He is the first Indian to win a decathlon medal in Commonwealth Games history.
  • Shankar competed despite carrying an injury, scripting a remarkable comeback.
  • The medal adds to India's strong Day 9 show, with Neeraj Chopra taking silver in javelin.

Tejaswin Shankar has done what no Indian has ever done before. On Friday, the 26-year-old from Delhi clinched the bronze medal in the men's decathlon at the Commonwealth Games 2026, finishing with a personal best of 7976 points. The achievement was all the more remarkable because Shankar competed with an injury that would have forced most athletes to withdraw.

The decathlon, a gruelling two-day test of ten track and field events, demands speed, strength, and endurance. Shankar's consistency across the disciplines saw him rise steadily through the standings, and by the time the final event — the 1500 metres — came around, the bronze was within reach. He held his nerve to cross the line and secure a podium finish that rewrites Indian athletics history.

A historic first for Indian athletics

No Indian had ever won a decathlon medal at the Commonwealth Games before Shankar's effort. The event has traditionally been dominated by athletes from Australia, England, and Canada, and for an Indian to break into that elite group marks a significant moment for multi-event athletics in the country.

Shankar's journey to this podium has been anything but smooth. Known for his high jump prowess — he holds the national record in that event — he transitioned to the decathlon only a few years ago. His rapid improvement has been tracked closely by athletics fans, and this bronze validates the decision to take on the most demanding event in track and field.

Injury adds to the achievement

The victory is even sweeter given the physical toll the competition took on Shankar. Reports from the athletes' village indicated that he was managing a niggle that required medical attention during the two-day event. Despite the discomfort, Shankar pushed through, posting strong marks in the throws and jumps to stay in medal contention.

His fourth-place standing after the first day had kept the medal race alive, and his performances on the second day — particularly in the discus and pole vault — proved decisive. The final points tally of 7976 was a personal best, underscoring that even at less than full fitness, Shankar can compete with the world's best.

A day of mixed fortunes for India

Shankar's bronze was one of several headline results for India on Day 9 of the Games. Javelin star Neeraj Chopra added a silver to his collection, continuing his remarkable run of podium finishes at major championships. Indian boxers also had a strong day, with several advancing to the later rounds.

The decathlon bronze, however, stands out for its historical significance. It adds a new chapter to India's growing prowess in athletics, a field where the country has traditionally struggled to match global standards. Shankar's achievement is likely to inspire a new generation of multi-event athletes back home.

Officials from the Athletics Federation of India have yet to comment on the extent of Shankar's injury or his plans for the upcoming season. But the athlete himself is expected to address the media in the coming days, where he will likely elaborate on his journey and what this medal means for Indian sport.

As the Commonwealth Games continue, Indian fans will be watching to see if more records fall. For now, Tejaswin Shankar's name is etched in the history books — a trailblazer in an event that India had long considered out of reach.
