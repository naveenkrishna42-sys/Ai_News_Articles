Cashback rates plunge 20% in Q2 2025

The credit card cashback market is in turmoil. In the second quarter of 2025, cashback rates on most credit cards dropped by an average of 20%. This is the steepest decline since the 2023 financial crisis. The decline was across all categories, from travel and dining to groceries and fuel.

Why the sudden drop?

Banks have cited rising operational costs as the main reason for the drop. They say they are passing on higher interest rates and increased compliance costs to customers. However, experts say this is just the tip of the iceberg.

Many banks have also cut back on promotional offers, which has led to a reduction in cashback rates. This is a common strategy to manage customer expectations and reduce churn.

Is this a trend or just a seasonal shift?

Experts are divided on whether this is a trend or just a seasonal shift. Some say that the drop is temporary and that cashback rates will rebound as the year progresses. Others say that this is the beginning of a new trend where cashback rates will remain low for the foreseeable future.

One thing is certain, though. The drop in cashback rates has led to a significant reduction in the value of cashback rewards. In 2025, the value of cashback rewards is expected to be lower than inflation, meaning that customers are not really saving money.

What does this mean for customers?

For customers, the drop in cashback rates means that they need to be more strategic with their spending. They need to focus on categories where the cashback rates are still high and avoid spending in categories where the cashback rates have dropped significantly.

Customers should also be aware of the fine print. Many credit cards have complex reward structures and hidden fees. It is important to read the terms and conditions carefully before applying for a credit card.
