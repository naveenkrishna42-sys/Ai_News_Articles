Delhi Metro Shuts 16 Stations: Commuters Stranded, Security Tightened

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) closed 16 stations indefinitely, citing security reasons.
  • Thousands of commuters were stranded, with long queues and confusion at affected stations.
  • Opposition parties criticized the government, alleging a crackdown on protests using plainclothes personnel.
  • Authorities have not specified when the stations will reopen.

NEW DELHI: Thousands of commuters were left stranded on Wednesday as the Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) abruptly shut down 16 stations across the city, citing unspecified security concerns. The closures, announced without prior warning, triggered chaos during peak morning hours, with long queues snaking outside barricaded gates and confused passengers scrambling for alternative transport.

The DMRC confirmed the decision in a brief statement, saying the stations would remain closed “till further instructions.” No timeline for reopening was provided, leaving daily travellers uncertain about their commutes. The affected stations span multiple lines, though officials have not disclosed the exact nature of the threat that prompted the move.

Commuters Fume, Demand Answers

At several closed stations, agitated passengers voiced their frustration. “Who will be responsible if we lose our jobs because of this?” one commuter asked, reflecting the anxiety of many who rely on the metro for timely travel. Auto-rickshaws and buses were overwhelmed as people scrambled for last-mile connectivity.

Social media erupted with complaints and photos of packed platforms at remaining open stations, as well as empty, cordoned-off entrances at the shuttered ones. The DMRC has not yet issued a detailed explanation, but sources indicate the move was precautionary following intelligence inputs about potential disruptions.

Political Slugfest Over Protest Crackdown

The station closures came amid heightened political tensions. Opposition parties accused the government of using the security pretext to crack down on ongoing protests in the city. Reports emerged of plainclothes personnel carrying nail-studded sticks in some areas, though authorities have not confirmed these accounts.

“This is an attack on the right to protest and a deliberate attempt to cripple the city,” a senior opposition leader said, demanding the immediate reopening of the stations. The ruling party, however, defended the action, stating that public safety was paramount and that any gatherings could be exploited by anti-social elements.

Uncertainty Looms Over Daily Life

For now, commuters have been advised to use alternative routes or check the DMRC’s official app for updates. Many were seen checking their phones repeatedly, hoping for news of a reopening. Local bus services reported a surge in ridership, leading to overcrowding and delays.

Business owners near the closed stations reported a sharp drop in footfall. “This is a huge blow. We depend on metro traffic,” a shopkeeper near one of the affected stations said.

The DMRC has not announced any review date for the closures. As the city waits, the focus remains on whether the security concerns will be resolved quickly or if Delhi’s daily rhythm will face prolonged disruption.
