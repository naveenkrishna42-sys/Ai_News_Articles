Netanyahu vows Iran will never get nuclear weapons, with or without deal

📅 2026-08-09 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Netanyahu vows to prevent a nuclear-armed Iran, with or without an agreement.
  • He rejects a US-proposed Gaza plan, adding to regional tensions.
  • IRGC chief allegedly claims Tehran will continue pursuing nuclear capabilities.

Netanyahu's Defiant Stance on Iran's Nuclear Programme

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has issued a stark warning, declaring that Iran will 'not have nuclear weapons'—with or without a diplomatic deal. His comments came amid escalating tensions in West Asia, where the spectre of a nuclear-armed Tehran has long haunted regional security calculations.

Speaking to reporters, Netanyahu dismissed any notion that Israel would accept a nuclear-capable Iran, even if international negotiations produced an agreement. 'With or without a deal, Iran will not get nuclear weapons,' he asserted, signalling that Israel reserves the right to act unilaterally if necessary.

IRGC Chief's Alleged Claim Adds Fuel to Fire

The Israeli leader's remarks followed an alleged statement by Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) chief, who reportedly claimed that Tehran would continue pursuing its nuclear programme. The IRGC's defiance, as reported by multiple outlets, underscores the deep mistrust between the two nations and the fragility of any diplomatic track.

Iran has consistently maintained that its nuclear activities are peaceful, but Israel and Western powers have long suspected a covert weapons programme. The alleged IRGC assertion only reinforces Jerusalem's conviction that Tehran cannot be trusted to honour any future accord.

Rejection of US Gaza Plan Complicates Regional Diplomacy

In a separate but related development, Netanyahu also rejected a US-proposed plan for Gaza, further straining ties with Washington. The Israeli PM's refusal to endorse the American initiative signals a hardening of his position on multiple fronts, potentially complicating broader regional diplomacy.

The rejection comes at a time when the US has been pushing for a ceasefire and a political solution in Gaza, following months of conflict. Netanyahu's stance suggests that Israel is unwilling to make concessions on security or territorial matters, even to its closest ally.

Regional Fallout and International Reaction

The developments have sent ripples across the international community, with world powers closely watching the escalating rhetoric. The UN and other mediators have urged restraint, but the Israeli PM's uncompromising language leaves little room for optimism.

Meanwhile, the situation on the ground remains volatile, with sporadic clashes reported along Israel's northern border. The convergence of the nuclear issue and the Gaza impasse creates a combustible mix that could destabilise the entire region.

What to Watch Next

Diplomatic efforts are expected to continue in the coming weeks, with US envoys shuttling between regional capitals. However, Netanyahu's firm stance suggests that Israel is prepared to act independently if diplomacy fails. The world will be watching whether Iran's leadership will respond with similar defiance or signal a willingness to negotiate.
