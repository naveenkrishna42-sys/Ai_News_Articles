Critical engine parts for Airbus A320neo, Boeing 737 MAX to be repaired in Hyderabad

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hyderabad facility to repair critical engine components for Airbus A320neo and Boeing 737 MAX.
  • Move strengthens India's position in aerospace maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) sector.
  • Repairs will be carried out at a specialised facility, details of the company not yet disclosed.

Hyderabad is set to become a hub for repairing critical engine components used in two of the world's most popular narrow-body aircraft — the Airbus A320neo and the Boeing 737 MAX. The development marks a significant step for India's aerospace maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) industry.

Strategic boost for MRO sector

According to a report by The Hindu, the repairs will be carried out at a facility in Hyderabad. Officials have not yet confirmed the name of the company that will operate the repair unit or the exact timeline for when operations will begin.

The move is expected to reduce dependence on overseas MRO facilities for Indian airlines. Currently, a large portion of engine component repairs for these aircraft types is sent abroad, leading to higher costs and longer turnaround times.

What components will be repaired

The specific engine components to be repaired have not been detailed in the initial announcement. However, the A320neo typically uses CFM International LEAP-1A or Pratt & Whitney PW1100G-JM engines, while the 737 MAX uses CFM International LEAP-1B engines. These are high-bypass turbofan engines that power the majority of modern single-aisle fleets globally.

Critical parts often include fan blades, compressor blades, turbine discs, and combustion chamber liners. These components require specialised tooling and certification for repair, making the Hyderabad facility's capability a notable achievement.

India's growing aerospace footprint

Hyderabad already hosts several aerospace and defence manufacturing units. The city's proximity to the Rajiv Gandhi International Airport and established industrial infrastructure make it a logical choice for such high-stakes repair work.

The Indian government has been actively promoting the MRO sector through policy reforms. In the 2021 budget, the government announced a revised MRO policy aiming to make India a global MRO hub. Tax incentives and streamlined customs procedures have been introduced to attract investment.

Industry experts believe that localising engine component repairs can lead to cost savings of 20-30% for airlines. It also reduces aircraft downtime since components can be shipped faster within the country.

Implications for airlines

IndiGo, India's largest airline, operates a large fleet of A320neo family aircraft. SpiceJet and Akasa Air also operate Boeing 737 MAX aircraft. These airlines have faced engine-related issues in the past, particularly with Pratt & Whitney engines on the A320neo. Having a local repair facility could help them manage maintenance schedules more efficiently.

Other carriers such as Vistara and Air India also use A320neo aircraft. The facility could potentially serve multiple airlines in the region, reducing their reliance on MRO providers in Singapore, Dubai, and Europe.

The announcement comes at a time when global supply chains are under strain. Localising critical repairs reduces vulnerability to geopolitical disruptions and shipping delays.

Officials are expected to release more details about the investment, job creation, and certification timelines in the coming weeks. The facility will need approvals from aviation regulators such as the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) and potentially the European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) or the US Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) to perform repairs on components used by international carriers.
