Oil Prices Slip on Hopes of Hormuz Reopening Deal

📅 2026-08-04 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil prices dropped for a second day on signs of progress toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz.
  • A Trump aide indicated a deal could be announced as early as Tuesday or Wednesday, ensuring 'freedom of movement'.
  • The strait handles a significant share of global oil shipments, making its closure a key supply risk.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent suggested an agreement could be reached within days.

Crude Slides on Hormuz Hopes

Oil futures extended their decline on Tuesday, with traders betting on a diplomatic breakthrough that could reopen the strategic Strait of Hormuz. The waterway, through which roughly a fifth of the world's petroleum passes, has been at the centre of heightened supply fears in recent weeks.

Prices fell as comments from US officials pointed to a possible deal between Washington and Tehran. Reports from multiple outlets, including CNBC and NDTV, suggested an agreement could be finalised imminently.

Signals of Progress

A senior aide to former President Donald Trump, who is leading the negotiations, indicated that a pact to reopen the strait could be announced "today or tomorrow". The official spoke on condition of anonymity, citing the sensitivity of the talks.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent separately told reporters that a deal might be struck "Tuesday or Wednesday", with the key condition being guaranteed "freedom of movement" for commercial shipping. The remarks were echoed by the BBC, which noted the market's two-day losing streak on the back of these signals.

Market Reaction

Brent crude, the international benchmark, fell sharply in early trading, while West Texas Intermediate also posted losses. The moves reflect a swift reversal from the price spikes seen earlier this month when the strait's closure was first reported.

Analysts said the market is now pricing in a lower risk premium, though caution remains. A full reopening would restore normal flows and ease concerns about supply disruptions that had pushed prices to multi-month highs.

What's at Stake

The Strait of Hormuz is a choke point between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, used by tankers from Saudi Arabia, Iraq, the UAE and other major producers. Any prolonged closure would have severe implications for global energy supplies.

While details of the proposed deal remain scarce, officials have stressed that the focus is on ensuring safe passage for vessels. Neither side has publicly confirmed the terms, and the situation remains fluid.

Geopolitical risk, however, has not vanished. Previous rounds of talks have faltered at the last moment, and the market's reaction suggests investors are cautiously optimistic rather than fully convinced.

Looking Ahead

Traders will now watch for official confirmation from both Washington and Tehran over the next 48 hours. A formal announcement could trigger further declines in crude prices, while any breakdown in negotiations would likely send them soaring once again.
