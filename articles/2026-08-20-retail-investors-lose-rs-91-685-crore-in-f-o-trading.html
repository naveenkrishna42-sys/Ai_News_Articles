Retail investors lose Rs 91,685 crore in derivatives trading

Retail investors suffered significant losses in derivatives trading in the fiscal year 2025-26, with a total of Rs 91,685 crore lost, according to a report by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). This marks the first decline in the derivatives trader base in four years.

88% of retail investors lost money

Nearly 88% of retail investors lost money in derivatives trading in FY26, despite strict regulations imposed by SEBI. The regulator's efforts to curb excessive trading and prevent losses have not been entirely successful.

Derivatives trader base falls for first time in four years

The number of retail investors participating in derivatives trading has declined for the first time in four years. This decline is attributed to the high losses incurred by investors and the strict regulations imposed by SEBI.

Retail traders pay Rs 25,000 crore in transaction costs

Despite the significant losses incurred, retail traders still paid Rs 25,000 crore in transaction costs in FY26. This highlights the high costs associated with derivatives trading, which may deter potential investors from participating.

Nearly 9 in 10 F&O traders lost money

Nearly 9 in 10 futures and options (F&O) traders lost money in FY26, with total losses amounting to Rs 91,685 crore. The decline in participation and the high losses indicate a challenging environment for retail investors in the derivatives market.
