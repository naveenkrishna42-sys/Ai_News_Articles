RBI FX Position Curbs Trigger $500 Million Losses for Indian Banks

✍️ By TIVRA News Business Desk 📅 2026-10-05 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI’s revised position limits curtailed banks’ ability to hedge overseas exposures, forcing unwinds at adverse rates.
  • The $500 million FX loss represents roughly 2‑3 % of the sector’s FY26 net profit and shaved ~15 basis points off the average CET1 ratio.
  • Banks are now accelerating internal VaR models, diversifying hedging instruments, and tightening counterparty limits to prevent recurrence.
  • Regulatory clarity and improved stress‑testing frameworks are expected to stabilize FX trading desks by FY27.

Overview of RBI Position Curbs

The Reserve Bank of India introduced stricter overseas position limits for banks in early FY26‑27, aiming to curb excessive foreign‑exchange speculation and enhance systemic stability. The new caps reduced the maximum net open position that a bank could maintain in any single foreign currency to 15 % of its Tier 1 capital, down from the previous 25 % threshold. The move was prompted by rising volatility in global currency markets and concerns over unhedged exposures that could amplify losses during sharp market moves.

Impact on Banks’ FX Trading Desks

With the limits tightened, many banks found their existing hedging strategies insufficient to cover outstanding overseas loans, export receivables, and overseas branch liabilities. Consequently, treasury teams were forced to unwind large FX positions at prevailing spot rates, which had moved against them due to the rupee’s depreciation against the dollar and euro. The aggregated mark‑to‑market loss reported by Business Standard stood at approximately $500 million for the quarter ending September 2026.

Key consequences included:

  • Sharp increase in daily Value‑at‑Risk (VaR) readings, prompting intra‑day limit breaches.
  • Heightened pressure on trading desks to liquidate positions, widening bid‑ask spreads and raising execution costs.
  • Operational strain as banks re‑balanced books across multiple currency pairs, leading to increased settlement fails.

Financial Implications and Capital Adequacy

The $500 million FX loss translates to roughly 2.8 % of the Indian banking sector’s FY26 net profit (estimated at ₹1.5 lakh crore, or about $18 billion). When expressed as a hit to capital, the loss reduced the average Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio by approximately 15 basis points across major public and private sector banks. For institutions with thinner capital buffers, the impact was more pronounced, pushing some closer to regulatory minima.

Risk Management Lessons and Outlook

The episode has prompted banks to overhaul their FX risk frameworks. Several lenders have:

  • Adopted more dynamic hedging models that adjust hedge ratios in real‑time based on volatility forecasts.
  • Expanded the use of cross‑currency swaps and forward‑rate agreements to diversify away from plain‑vanilla forwards.
  • Strengthened counterparty limits and increased collateral margins for over‑the‑counter trades.
  • Enhanced stress‑testing scenarios to include abrupt policy shifts like the RBI’s position‑cap changes.

Looking ahead, market participants expect the RBI to calibrate its stance based on macro‑prudential signals. If global FX volatility subsides, the regulator may gradually ease the caps, allowing banks to rebuild more efficient hedges. In the interim, the sector’s focus remains on preserving capital, improving transparency in FX exposures, and leveraging technology for faster risk analytics.
