Chennai hospital receives non-invasive ventilator, pulse oximeters

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A hospital in Chennai has been equipped with a non-invasive ventilator.
  • The facility also received a supply of pulse oximeters to monitor oxygen levels.
  • The equipment aims to improve care for patients with respiratory distress.

A hospital in Chennai has taken delivery of a non-invasive ventilator and a batch of pulse oximeters, according to officials familiar with the development. The equipment arrived on Saturday, marking a fresh addition to the facility's respiratory care capabilities.

The non-invasive ventilator will allow doctors to support patients with breathing difficulties without the need for an invasive airway. This method reduces the risk of infections and complications associated with intubation.

Critical equipment for respiratory care

The ventilator is designed for patients who can breathe on their own but need assistance. It works by delivering pressurised air through a mask, helping keep the airways open and improving oxygen exchange.

Pulse oximeters, which are small clip-on devices, measure the oxygen saturation level in a patient's blood. Nurses and doctors can use them to quickly detect drops in oxygen levels, a key indicator of worsening respiratory conditions.

Boosting preparedness

Hospital administrators said the new equipment will be used in the general ward and emergency department. They did not specify how many pulse oximeters were received or the exact model of the ventilator.

Chennai has seen periodic surges in respiratory illnesses, particularly during seasonal changes. The addition of non-invasive ventilation options gives clinicians more flexibility in managing patient loads without immediately resorting to intensive care beds.

How it helps patients

For a patient with moderate breathing trouble, a non-invasive ventilator can often stabilise their condition. This can reduce the need for heavy sedation and shorten hospital stays.

Pulse oximeters, meanwhile, allow for continuous monitoring. Nurses can track a patient's oxygen levels at the bedside and alert doctors if readings fall below a safe threshold.

The hospital did not disclose the funding source for the equipment or whether it was donated by a government agency or a private organisation. Officials have not yet confirmed the total cost or the timeline for installation.

Chennai's public health infrastructure has been under scrutiny in recent years, with demand for ventilators and monitoring devices often exceeding supply during outbreaks. The arrival of this equipment is a modest but concrete step toward filling that gap.

Doctors at the facility said they plan to train nursing staff on the new ventilator's operation over the coming week. The pulse oximeters are expected to be deployed immediately across the emergency and medicine wards.

No further deliveries have been announced. The hospital is expected to release a statement on the equipment's clinical impact after a month of use.
