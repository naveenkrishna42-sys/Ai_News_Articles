Tens of thousands flee as wildfires sweep France and Spain

📅 2026-07-24 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • More than 80,000 people evacuated in southwest France and Spain.
  • Spain declares national emergency as fires merge outside Madrid and in Avila.
  • Blazes fueled by extreme heatwave and prolonged drought conditions.
  • Criticism over patchy warnings and outdated evacuation plans in affected Spanish regions.

More than 80,000 people have been forced to flee their homes as wildfires rage across southwestern France and Spain. The blazes, fueled by a severe heatwave and prolonged drought, have merged into massive fire fronts outside Madrid and in the Avila region.

Spain declared a national emergency on Friday as the fires threatened several towns. The move allows the central government to coordinate resources and deploy military units to assist overwhelmed local fire crews.

France: Thousands evacuated as flames advance

In France, officials confirmed that tens of thousands of residents have been evacuated from the Gironde and Landes regions. The fires, which began earlier this week, have already destroyed thousands of hectares of pine forest.

Authorities set up emergency shelters in sports halls and schools. Many evacuees spent the night in temporary accommodation, uncertain whether their homes were still standing. Firefighters from across the country have been deployed, with water-bombing aircraft operating continuously since Wednesday.

The French government has not yet provided a full assessment of property damage. Officials say the priority remains containing the flames before winds pick up again over the weekend.

Spain: Fires merge near Madrid, national emergency declared

In Spain, the situation is equally dire. Two large fires that started separately near Madrid and in the province of Avila have now merged, creating a single massive blaze. The combined fire front has forced the evacuation of entire villages and suburban areas.

Spain's prime minister said the government would activate all available resources. The national emergency declaration allows for the rapid deployment of the Military Emergency Unit, which specializes in disaster response.

Local officials in Avila said the fire has already burned through more than 10,000 hectares. Many residents were given little time to gather belongings before evacuation orders were issued.

Criticism over patchy warnings and outdated plans

Reports from affected areas in Spain have highlighted gaps in the emergency response. Some residents said they received no official warning and had to rely on neighbors or social media to learn about the approaching flames.

In one town near Madrid, evacuees told local media that evacuation plans had not been updated in years. ThePrint reported that outdated protocols left many victims to fend for themselves as the fire advanced rapidly.

Spanish authorities have not yet commented on the criticism. Officials said they would review the response once the immediate threat is under control.

Heatwave and drought fuel extreme fire season

Meteorologists say the current heatwave is one of the most intense on record for July. Temperatures in parts of Spain exceeded 42 degrees Celsius earlier this week. Combined with months of below-average rainfall, vegetation has turned into tinder.

Climate scientists have warned that such extreme fire seasons are becoming more frequent. Southern Europe has experienced a series of devastating wildfires over the past five years, with 2022 and 2023 also seeing mass evacuations.

Firefighting efforts continue on both sides of the border. In France, authorities are hopeful that calmer winds forecast for Sunday will help contain the fires. In Spain, the priority remains preventing the merged blaze from reaching densely populated areas outside Madrid.

The coming days will be critical. Emergency services are racing against the weather, with forecasts showing no immediate relief from the heat. Thousands of displaced people remain in shelters, waiting for permission to return home.
