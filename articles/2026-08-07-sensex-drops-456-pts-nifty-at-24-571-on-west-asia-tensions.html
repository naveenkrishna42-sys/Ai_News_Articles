Sensex drops 456 pts, Nifty at 24,571 on West Asia tensions

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 456 points, Nifty settled at 24,571 amid West Asia tensions
  • Geopolitical concerns overshadowed positive domestic cues, dragging investor sentiment
  • Broader market indices also traded lower, with volatility expected to continue

Indian stock markets closed sharply lower on Friday, with the BSE Sensex declining 456 points and the NSE Nifty settling at 24,571, as escalating West Asia tensions weighed on investor sentiment. The benchmark indices, which opened on a cautious note, slid further during the afternoon session as geopolitical worries took centre stage, overshadowing any positive domestic triggers.

West Asia tensions spook investors

The latest flare-up in the West Asia region has injected fresh uncertainty into global markets, and Indian equities were no exception. Oil prices, a key concern for India's import-dependent economy, have been volatile, adding to fears of sustained inflationary pressure. Analysts said the market is pricing in the risk of supply disruptions, which could hurt corporate margins and delay rate cuts by the Reserve Bank of India.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the cash market, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) provided some support, but the selling pressure was too strong to be fully absorbed. The market breadth was negative, with more declines than advances on both the BSE and NSE.

Sectoral performance mixed

Among sectoral indices, IT stocks were the worst hit, followed by banking and financial services. The Nifty Bank index fell over 0.8%, dragged down by heavyweight lenders. On the other hand, defensive sectors such as FMCG and pharma managed to stay afloat, as investors sought safer bets in a volatile environment.

Midcap and smallcap stocks also traded lower, though the losses were relatively milder compared to the frontline indices. The volatility index, India VIX, rose sharply, indicating heightened market anxiety in the near term.

What analysts are saying

Market participants said the focus will remain on the geopolitical front, with any further escalation likely to trigger more sell-offs. However, some experts believe that the correction is a healthy one, given the recent run-up in prices. They also pointed to strong domestic fundamentals, including robust GST collections and improving corporate earnings, as factors that could limit the downside.

The upcoming US Federal Reserve policy meeting and domestic macroeconomic data releases will also be closely watched by traders. A softer stance by the Fed could provide some relief to emerging markets, including India.

Outlook for the coming weeks

With geopolitical risks still hanging over the market, volatility is expected to remain elevated in the short term. Investors are advised to keep a close eye on oil prices and any diplomatic developments in the West Asia region. A de-escalation in tensions could trigger a sharp rebound, while further escalation may drag the indices lower.

For now, traders are bracing for a choppy session on Monday, with the direction likely to be dictated by global cues and crude oil movement.
