Christopher Nolan calls Satyajit Ray one of India's greatest filmmakers

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Christopher Nolan praised Satyajit Ray as one of the greatest Indian filmmakers.
  • Nolan called the 'Apu Trilogy' 'absolutely incredible' and 'Pather Panchali' a mind-blowing film.
  • The director revealed Ray's work left a lasting impact on his cinematic vision.

Acclaimed filmmaker Christopher Nolan has described Satyajit Ray as one of the greatest Indian filmmakers of all time. The director of 'The Odyssey' and 'Oppenheimer' made the remarks during a recent interview, sparking fresh interest in Ray's body of work among global audiences.

Nolan specifically highlighted Ray's 'Apu Trilogy', calling it 'absolutely incredible'. He said the films, which follow the life of a young boy in rural Bengal, represent a high point in world cinema.

Nolan on 'Pather Panchali'

The British-American director reserved special praise for the first film in the trilogy, 'Pather Panchali'. He described it as an 'incredible film' that 'just blew my mind'.

Nolan revealed that the 1955 classic left a lasting impression on him. He spoke about how Ray's storytelling and visual style influenced his own approach to filmmaking.

The lasting impact of Ray's work

Nolan's comments add to a growing chorus of international filmmakers who have acknowledged Ray's genius. Directors like Martin Scorsese and Akira Kurosawa have previously spoken about Ray's influence on their work.

Ray, who also composed music for his films, remains a towering figure in Indian cinema. His works continue to be studied and celebrated across the world, decades after their release.

The remarks come as a new generation of viewers discovers Ray's films through streaming platforms and restored prints. The Criterion Collection has released several of Ray's works, making them accessible to a global audience.

A global voice for Indian cinema

Nolan's praise underscores Ray's unique position as a filmmaker who transcended national boundaries. His films, rooted in Indian culture, spoke to universal human experiences.

Industry observers say such endorsements from major Hollywood figures help bring attention to Indian cinema on the world stage. Ray, who received an Honorary Oscar in 1992, remains a benchmark for artistic excellence in filmmaking.

The director's comments have sparked discussions online, with fans and critics revisiting Ray's filmography. Many have noted the timeless quality of his work, which continues to resonate with contemporary audiences.

Nolan's admiration for Ray is not new. He has previously mentioned Ray's influence in interviews, but his latest remarks have drawn particular attention due to their timing around the release of his own ambitious project.

For now, the focus remains on how Ray's legacy continues to inspire one of the most celebrated directors working today. Nolan's words serve as a reminder of the enduring power of truly great cinema.
