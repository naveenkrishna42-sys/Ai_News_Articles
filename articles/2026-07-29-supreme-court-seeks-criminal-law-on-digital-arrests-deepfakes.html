Supreme Court Seeks Criminal Law on Digital Arrests, Deepfakes

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court termed digital arrest and deepfake fraud a 'serious national threat'.
  • Court asked the government to consider a standalone criminal law for such cybercrimes.
  • Existing IPC and IT Act provisions are deemed insufficient to curb these growing frauds.

The Supreme Court of India on Wednesday directed the central government to consider enacting a specific criminal law to combat the rising menace of digital arrest scams and deepfake fraud. A bench termed these emerging cybercrimes a 'serious national threat' that existing legal frameworks are failing to check.

The court observed that fraudsters are increasingly using technology to impersonate law enforcement officials and carry out 'digital arrests' — virtual detention of victims through video calls to extort money. Deepfakes, where manipulated audio or video is used to impersonate individuals, are also being deployed in such schemes.

Existing Laws Not Enough

The bench noted that while the Indian Penal Code and the Information Technology Act contain provisions dealing with cheating and impersonation, they are not tailored to the scale and sophistication of these new-age frauds. The court said a dedicated law would send a strong deterrent signal.

The government was asked to examine if a standalone criminal statute specifically defining digital arrest and deepfake fraud is needed. The court suggested that such a law could include stringent penalties and provisions for swift confiscation of proceeds of crime.

How Digital Arrest Scams Work

In these scams, fraudsters pose as police or CBI officers and inform victims that a parcel containing illegal items has been traced to their name. The victim is then kept on a video call and told they are under 'digital arrest' — not allowed to leave their home or contact anyone — until they pay a fine.

Many victims, fearing legal trouble, have transferred large sums of money. The court cited data showing that such scams have affected thousands across the country, with losses running into crores of rupees.

Deepfakes Add a New Dimension

Deepfake technology has added a dangerous layer. Fraudsters now create realistic videos of a victim's relative or a known official asking for money. The court said these crimes undermine trust in digital communication and require urgent legislative action.

The government has not yet responded with a timeline, but the court has fixed the matter for further hearing after eight weeks. The Law Ministry is expected to file a status report on the proposed legislation.

What happens next will depend on how quickly the government drafts a bill. The court has made it clear that waiting is not an option when technology is outpacing the law.
