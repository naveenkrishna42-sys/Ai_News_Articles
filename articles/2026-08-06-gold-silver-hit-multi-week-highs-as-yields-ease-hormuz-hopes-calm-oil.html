Gold, silver hit multi-week highs as yields ease, Hormuz hopes calm oil fears

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Precious metals climb as bond yields ease, boosting non-yielding assets.
  • Reopening hopes for Strait of Hormuz cap oil price rises, reducing inflation fears.
  • Gold touches seven-week high, silver follows suit in global markets.

Precious metals rally on softer yields

Gold and silver prices climbed sharply on Wednesday, reaching multi-week highs as global bond yields eased, making non-yielding assets more attractive to investors. The surge reflects a broader shift in market sentiment, with traders recalibrating their positions ahead of key economic data.

Gold touched a seven-week high, while silver mirrored the upward momentum, buoyed by a weaker dollar and renewed safe-haven demand. The rally marks a notable turnaround after weeks of consolidation, with analysts pointing to a mix of technical and fundamental triggers.

Yields ease, lifting bullion appeal

The drop in Treasury yields was the primary catalyst, reducing the opportunity cost of holding gold and silver. As yields slipped, fund flows rotated back into precious metals, reversing a recent trend of outflows.

Market participants are now watching central bank signals closely, with any hint of policy easing likely to extend the rally. However, the pace of gains has also raised caution about a potential pullback if yields rebound unexpectedly.

Hormuz hopes cap oil inflation fears

Meanwhile, hopes of a reopening of the Strait of Hormuz have tempered oil price spikes, easing concerns about imported inflation. Crude prices remained range-bound, providing relief to bullion investors who feared that rising energy costs could force tighter monetary policy.

The geopolitical development has been a key factor in the metals complex, as it reduces the risk of a sustained inflationary shock. Analysts note that while tensions persist, the market is pricing in a gradual normalisation of supply routes.

Domestic and global cues

In India, turmeric futures traded in a range of 22,178–22,966, reflecting broader commodity market volatility. The domestic precious metals market tracked global trends, with jewellers reporting steady buying interest at higher levels.

Globally, the gold trade is showing signs of revival, with volumes picking up after a sluggish period. The combination of lower yields and stable oil prices has created a supportive backdrop for bullion, though some analysts remain cautious about the sustainability of the move.

What to watch next

Investors will now focus on upcoming US economic data and Federal Reserve commentary for directional cues. A softer inflation print could reinforce the bullish case for gold, while any hawkish surprise may trigger profit-taking.

The Strait of Hormuz situation remains fluid, and any escalation could quickly alter the inflation calculus. For now, the metals market is enjoying a respite, but traders are advised to stay nimble as volatility is likely to persist.
