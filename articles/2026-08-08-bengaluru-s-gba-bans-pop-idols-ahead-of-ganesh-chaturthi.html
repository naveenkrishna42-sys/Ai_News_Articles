Bengaluru's GBA bans POP idols ahead of Ganesh Chaturthi

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • GBA prohibits sale and immersion of POP Ganesha idols in Bengaluru
  • Move aims to prevent water pollution during the festival
  • Eco-friendly clay idols recommended as alternatives

Pre-Festival Crackdown

With Ganesh Chaturthi just weeks away, the Gandhinagar Bull Temple Authority (GBA) in Bengaluru has announced a complete ban on plaster-of-Paris (POP) idols. The decision, made public on Saturday, is aimed at curbing the severe water pollution that follows the annual immersion of such idols in lakes and water bodies.

The ban covers the sale, storage, and immersion of POP idols within the GBA's jurisdiction. Traders and artisans have been asked to switch to eco-friendly alternatives like clay or paper pulp.

Why the Ban?

POP idols, often decorated with chemical paints, do not dissolve easily in water. Over the years, they have contributed to rising toxicity levels in Bengaluru's lakes, affecting aquatic life and making water unfit for use. The GBA's directive is part of a broader push by environmental groups and civic bodies to make festivals more sustainable.

Officials from the GBA have not yet confirmed the exact number of vendors currently stocking POP idols, but they have indicated that surprise checks will be conducted in the coming weeks.

Reaction from Stakeholders

Local artisans, especially those who have traditionally crafted POP idols, have expressed concerns about potential loss of income. However, several have acknowledged the environmental necessity and are already experimenting with natural materials.

Meanwhile, a section of devotees has welcomed the move, noting that the festival's spirit need not be tied to non-biodegradable materials. Community groups have also announced plans to set up artificial immersion tanks with treated water to minimise the impact.

Enforcement and Penalties

The GBA has warned that violators will face fines and possible seizure of stock. Specific penalty amounts have not been disclosed, but authorities have said they will work closely with the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) to ensure compliance.

Residents have been encouraged to report any sale of POP idols to the GBA's helpline. The authority has also released a list of approved eco-friendly idol makers to help buyers make informed choices.

What to Watch

As the festival approaches, the focus will be on how effectively the ban is enforced and whether the supply chain for eco-friendly idols can meet the demand. The GBA has promised a review of the ban's impact after the immersion processions, which could set a precedent for other cities in the state.
