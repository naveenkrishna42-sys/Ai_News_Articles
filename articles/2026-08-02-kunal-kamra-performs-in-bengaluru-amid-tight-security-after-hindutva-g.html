Kunal Kamra performs in Bengaluru amid tight security after Hindutva group objects

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kunal Kamra's show in Bengaluru proceeded under heavy security
  • Hindutva group had objected to the comedian's performance
  • Police deployed in large numbers to prevent any untoward incident
  • Show was held without any reported disruption

Show goes on under police watch

Comedian Kunal Kamra took the stage in Bengaluru on Saturday night, with hundreds of police personnel deployed outside the venue after a Hindutva group threatened to disrupt the event. The show, which was originally scheduled months ago, had become a flashpoint after right-wing activists demanded its cancellation.

Kamra, known for his sharp political satire and often controversial takes, performed to a packed auditorium in the city's central business district. Police barricades lined the approach roads, and attendees were frisked multiple times before being allowed inside. A senior police officer said the force was prepared for any contingency, though the night passed without any major incident.

Objections and counter-movements

The trouble began earlier this week when a local Hindutva outfit filed a complaint with the city police, alleging that Kamra's previous performances contained remarks that hurt religious sentiments. The group had announced plans to hold a protest outside the venue, prompting the comedian to post a defiant message on social media, saying he would not be bullied into silence.

In response, a coalition of civil liberties groups and student organisations organised a counter-petition, urging the police to allow the show to proceed. They argued that any ban would set a dangerous precedent for freedom of expression in the state. The police, for their part, maintained that they were not banning the event but merely ensuring law and order.

Security arrangements and public reaction

Over 300 personnel, including rapid response teams and women constables, were deployed in and around the venue. Snipers were positioned on nearby rooftops, and drones hovered overhead. Attendees said the security checks were the most extensive they had seen for any comedy show in the city.

Outside, a small group of protesters shouted slogans but were kept at a distance by the police. Some locals expressed frustration at the disruption. "We come here for entertainment, not for this politics," said one attendee, who did not wish to be named. Inside, the atmosphere was more relaxed, with Kamra cracking jokes about the security presence itself.

Legal and political implications

The episode is the latest in a series of confrontations between comedians and right-wing groups across India. Over the past year, at least three stand-up shows in different cities have been cancelled or moved after similar objections. Legal experts say the police are caught between upholding free speech and preventing public disorder.

"The law is clear on this — unless there is a credible threat to life or property, the show must go on," said a constitutional lawyer who tracks such cases. "But in practice, the police often buckle under political pressure." The state government has not issued any statement on the matter, though sources suggest that senior officials were monitoring the situation closely.

Kamra, who has faced multiple FIRs in the past for his comments on prime-time news anchors, did not directly address the controversy during the show. Instead, he stuck to his usual set, which includes impersonations of political figures and media personalities. The audience responded with loud applause, and the show ended around 11 pm without any untoward incident.

As the crowd filed out, many were seen discussing the irony of a comedy show requiring such heavy security. "This is what India has come to," one young woman remarked to her friend. The comedian himself later posted a one-word tweet: "Done."

What remains to be seen is whether the police will file a case against the Hindutva group for attempting to disrupt a lawful event, or whether the matter will be allowed to die down quietly. For now, artists and audiences alike are watching closely to see if this becomes a template for future shows in the city.
