Hyderabad man loses Rs 1 lakh in cyber fraud after phone software update

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Victim received a pop-up prompting a software update on his phone.
  • Following the prompt, he installed an app that gave fraudsters remote access.
  • Rs 1 lakh was siphoned from his bank account before he realised the fraud.
  • Police have registered a case and are investigating the incident.

Update trap: How the fraud unfolded

A Hyderabad resident fell victim to a cyber fraud that cost him Rs 1 lakh, after he followed a fake software update prompt on his phone. The incident, reported on Thursday, highlights a growing trend where fraudsters use system alerts as bait to install malicious software.

The victim, a man in his 40s living in the Kukatpally area, told police that a pop-up appeared on his phone screen claiming a critical system update was required. Believing it to be legitimate, he clicked on the prompt and followed the instructions, which led him to install an app from a link provided.

Within hours, he received multiple bank transaction alerts showing money being transferred out of his account. By the time he checked, Rs 1 lakh had been debited in multiple small transactions.

Remote access: The silent threat

Investigators suspect the app installed by the victim gave the fraudsters remote access to his phone. This allowed them to view his banking credentials, including OTPs and login details, without his knowledge.

Cyber crime experts point out that such fraudulent updates often mimic legitimate system notifications. They urge users to only install updates directly from official app stores or the phone's settings menu, never from pop-up links.

The victim approached the Cyber Crime police station, where a case has been registered under relevant sections of the Information Technology Act. Investigators are now tracing the transaction trail to identify the culprits.

Patterns of fraud on the rise

This incident is part of a broader spike in cyber fraud cases across Hyderabad. In recent months, several residents have reported losing money through fake customer care numbers, phishing links, and now, fraudulent software updates.

Police have repeatedly advised citizens to remain cautious of unsolicited pop-ups and to verify any request for personal or financial information. Banks, too, have issued warnings about sharing OTPs or downloading apps from unverified sources.

The Telangana Cyber Security Bureau has launched awareness campaigns in residential colonies and offices, but cases continue to surface, often with victims losing their life savings.

What the police advise

Officials recommend that anyone who suspects a fraudulent app should immediately disconnect their phone from the internet, uninstall the app, and change all passwords. They should also inform their bank to block transactions and report the matter to the cyber crime helpline.

For this victim, the loss is significant, but authorities say timely reporting can sometimes help recover funds if the money is still in transit. In this case, the transactions appear to have been completed swiftly, making recovery difficult.

The investigation is ongoing, with police examining call data records and the app's origin. They are also checking if the same fraudsters have targeted others in the city.

What to watch next

As cyber fraud methods evolve, police are urging citizens to adopt a 'verify before you trust' approach for all digital interactions. The Hyderabad Cyber Crime wing is expected to issue a public advisory detailing safe update practices in the coming days.
