Google TV Freeplay expands with 10,000-plus on-demand titles

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google TV Freeplay adds on-demand library with 10,000+ titles
  • Users can watch free movies and shows without downloading apps
  • Expansion targets cord-cutters in the US market

Bigger library, no subscription needed

Google TV Freeplay, the tech giant's free ad-supported streaming service, has added a vast on-demand library featuring more than 10,000 movies and television shows. The move transforms the platform from a purely live-channel experience into a hybrid service where users can pick and choose what to watch, when they want.

The new content is accessible directly through the Google TV interface, meaning viewers no longer need to hop between separate apps to find something to stream. This integration aims to simplify the browsing experience, putting a massive catalogue of free entertainment just a few clicks away.

What the expansion means for viewers

For cord-cutters, the addition is significant. Previously, Freeplay offered a lineup of free ad-supported streaming television (FAST) channels, which function like traditional broadcast TV but stream over the internet. Now, the service combines that live programming with a robust on-demand section, giving users greater flexibility.

The on-demand titles span various genres, though Google has not yet released a full breakdown of the catalogue. The company has confirmed that no apps are required to access this content, a key differentiator from other free streaming platforms that often rely on third-party integrations.

How it fits into Google's strategy

This expansion is part of a broader push by Google to make its TV platform a one-stop destination for free entertainment. By adding a large on-demand library, the company is positioning Freeplay as a serious competitor to other ad-supported services like Pluto TV, Tubi, and Freevee.

The timing is notable, as streaming fatigue has led many consumers to seek out free options that don't add to their monthly bills. Google appears to be betting that a seamless, integrated experience will draw users to its platform, even as premium streaming services raise prices.

Availability and rollout details

Currently, the updated Freeplay service is rolling out in the United States. Google has not specified when or if the expanded library will reach other markets, including India, where the company has been expanding its TV ecosystem through partnerships with manufacturers.

Users in the US can access the new on-demand section through the Google TV interface on supported devices, including smart TVs and streaming dongles. The service remains free, with ads supporting the content, though the frequency of ad breaks has not been disclosed.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the 10,000-title count includes regional or international content, but the initial rollout appears focused on Hollywood and mainstream programming.

As Google TV Freeplay grows, the next question is how it will compete with established free streaming giants and whether the company will expand this offering globally. Watch for announcements about international availability in the coming months.
