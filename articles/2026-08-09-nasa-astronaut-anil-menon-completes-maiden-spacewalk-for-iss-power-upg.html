NASA Astronaut Anil Menon Completes Maiden Spacewalk for ISS Power Upgrade

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • NASA astronauts Anil Menon and Jessica Meir performed a 6.5-hour spacewalk on August 8, 2026.
  • The spacewalk focused on preparing the International Space Station for upcoming solar array upgrades.
  • This was Indian-origin astronaut Anil Menon's first spacewalk, marking a personal and professional milestone.

Historic Spacewalk for Indian-Origin Astronaut

NASA astronauts Anil Menon and Jessica Meir stepped outside the International Space Station (ISS) on Saturday, August 8, 2026, for a 6.5-hour spacewalk. The primary objective was to prepare the orbital laboratory for a major solar array upgrade, a critical step in boosting the station's power capabilities.

For Menon, an Indian-origin astronaut, this marked his maiden spacewalk. The achievement was highlighted by multiple Indian news outlets, including Swarajya and The Hindu, underscoring the significance for India's space community.

Upgrading the ISS Power Grid

The spacewalk involved modifications to the station's existing power systems, setting the stage for the installation of new solar arrays. These upgrades are essential as the ISS continues to support a growing range of scientific experiments and international crew rotations.

NASA's official statement described the activity as a success, with both astronauts completing all planned tasks. The agency had earlier announced the spacewalk schedule, noting the precision required for such intricate work outside the station.

Menon's Journey to Space

Anil Menon, a physician and former NASA flight surgeon, was selected as an astronaut in 2021. His background includes serving as a medical officer for the SpaceX Crew Dragon missions, bringing a unique blend of clinical expertise and operational experience to the crew.

His first spacewalk is a personal milestone, but it also reflects the broader trend of increasing diversity in space exploration. Indian-origin astronauts have been part of NASA missions before, but Menon's active role in station maintenance highlights his integral position in the current crew.

What the Spacewalk Achieved

The 6.5-hour extravehicular activity (EVA) focused on preparing the ISS truss structure for the next pair of solar arrays. These arrays, part of a continuous upgrade effort, are designed to ensure the station has sufficient power for the next decade of operations.

NASA had earlier noted that the spacewalk would involve tasks such as installing support brackets and securing cables. The successful completion of these tasks is a prerequisite for future robotic or crewed missions to deploy the new arrays.

Reactions and Significance

Indian media celebrated Menon's achievement, with headlines like 'One more step' from space.com capturing the incremental progress of station maintenance. The Nagaland Post also covered the event, reflecting the wide interest in the mission.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact timeline for the next spacewalk, but NASA typically schedules follow-up EVAs within weeks to continue the solar array work.

As the ISS approaches its final decade of planned operations, each upgrade ensures the station remains a world-class research facility. For Menon, this spacewalk is likely the first of several, as the crew continues to adapt the station for future needs.

The next major milestone will be the actual deployment of the new solar arrays, a task that will require careful coordination between ground control and the orbiting crew. Watch for NASA's announcement on the next EVA date in the coming weeks.
