Bengaluru Metro fares exceed BMRCL's own projections, commuters demand review

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Metro fares in Bengaluru have risen above BMRCL's original projections, as per official documents.
  • Commuters have raised concerns over the fare hike, citing it as a burden on daily travel budgets.
  • Experts and activists call for a review of the fare structure to align with affordability and actual costs.

Fare Hike Surpasses BMRCL's Own Estimates

Bengaluru Metro commuters are facing a financial pinch as ticket prices have climbed higher than the Bangalore Metro Rail Corporation Limited's (BMRCL) own earlier projections. According to documents accessed by The New Indian Express, the current fares now exceed the maximum operational cost estimates that BMRCL had submitted to the state government during the project's planning phase.

The discrepancy has sparked fresh calls for a fare correction. Commuters argue that the revised charges, which were implemented in stages over the past year, are not only steep but also inconsistent with the affordability benchmarks the metro agency had originally promised.

Commuters Struggle with Rising Travel Costs

For many daily users, the metro was once a cheaper alternative to buses or autorickshaws. But with the latest fare revision, a trip from one end of the Purple Line to the other now costs significantly more than BMRCL's own cost-recovery model had suggested.

"We were told the fares would be capped at a certain level, but now we are paying nearly 30% more than what was projected," said a regular commuter from Whitefield. "This is not just an inconvenience; it's a financial strain for people who rely on the metro for work."

BMRCL had earlier set a fare formula based on distance slabs and operational expenses. However, internal documents show that the actual fares have overshot those slabs, leading to accusations of poor planning or deliberate overpricing.

Experts Question Fare Rationale

Transport analysts point out that the fare hike may be an attempt to recover higher-than-expected electricity costs and maintenance expenses. But they argue that the burden should not fall entirely on commuters.

"If the projections were wrong, BMRCL needs to explain why," said a city-based transport researcher. "The metro is a public service, not a profit-making enterprise. The fare should reflect what was promised, or at least be justified transparently."

The issue has also caught the attention of local activists, who have started a petition demanding a government audit of BMRCL's fare structure. They want the Karnataka government to step in and ensure that the metro remains affordable for the middle class and lower-income groups.

What Comes Next

BMRCL officials have not yet responded to the specific claims about the fare projections. However, sources within the agency indicate that a review of the current fare table is possible if public pressure mounts.

For now, Bengaluru's metro users are left waiting for a clear explanation — and a possible reduction in ticket prices that could ease their daily commute.
