Singapore FD rates hit 1.65%—What this means for savers

Key points
  • Fixed deposit rates in Singapore hit 1.65% in August 2026
  • This is the highest rate seen in over a decade

Singapore's Fixed Deposit Rates Hit 1.65%—A Decade High

Singapore's fixed deposit (FD) rates have reached a decade high of 1.65% in August 2026, according to MoneySmart. This marks a significant shift in the country's financial landscape, offering savers a higher return on their deposits compared to previous years.

Why This Matters for Savers

The rise in fixed deposit rates is a welcome development for savers in Singapore. With inflation rates remaining stable and the economy showing signs of recovery, banks are offering higher interest rates to attract deposits. This not only benefits individual savers but also contributes to the overall stability of the financial system.

Economic Factors Behind the Rate Hike

The increase in fixed deposit rates can be attributed to several economic factors. Firstly, the Singaporean economy has shown signs of recovery post-pandemic, with a stable inflation rate and robust GDP growth. Secondly, the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) has been implementing policies to support the economy, which has led to a rise in interest rates. Lastly, the demand for safe investment options has increased, prompting banks to offer higher returns on fixed deposits.

Comparison with Previous Years

In comparison to previous years, the current fixed deposit rates in Singapore are significantly higher. For instance, in 2016, the highest fixed deposit rate was around 1.2%. This year's rate of 1.65% represents a substantial increase, offering savers a better return on their investments.

What This Means for the Future

The rise in fixed deposit rates is a positive sign for the Singaporean economy. It indicates that the financial system is stable and that banks are confident in their ability to attract deposits. However, it is essential for savers to carefully consider their financial goals and risk tolerance before investing in fixed deposits. Additionally, the central bank's policies and economic conditions will play a crucial role in determining future interest rates.
