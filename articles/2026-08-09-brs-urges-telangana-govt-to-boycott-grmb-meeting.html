BRS urges Telangana govt to boycott GRMB meeting

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • BRS has formally requested the Telangana government to skip the GRMB meeting.
  • The party argues that the meeting could undermine the state's control over river water resources.
  • The government has not yet responded to the demand.

The Bharat Rashtra Samithi (BRS) has called on the Telangana government to boycott the upcoming meeting of the Godavari River Management Board (GRMB). The party contends that the meeting, scheduled to be held soon, could compromise the state's interests over the river's waters. Leaders from the opposition party have voiced strong objections, asserting that the board's proceedings often favour neighbouring states.

Party's Stance

Speaking to reporters in Hyderabad, senior BRS leaders argued that the GRMB has historically failed to address Telangana's legitimate concerns. They alleged that the board's decisions have repeatedly ignored the state's requirements, particularly regarding irrigation and drinking water projects. The party has therefore demanded that Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy's government stay away from the meeting as a mark of protest.

"The state cannot afford to be a silent spectator while its water rights are bargained away," a BRS spokesperson said, echoing the party's growing unease over the Centre's role in river water management.

Background of the GRMB

The Godavari River Management Board was constituted under the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation Act, 2014, to oversee the sharing of Godavari waters between Telangana and Andhra Pradesh. It operates under the purview of the Union Ministry of Jal Shakti. Since its inception, the board has been a point of friction between the two states, with periodic disputes over projects and allocations.

In recent years, the GRMB has taken up issues related to the construction of new barrages and the release of water for irrigation. Telangana has often accused the board of siding with Andhra Pradesh, a charge the latter has denied.

Political Reactions

The BRS's demand has drawn mixed responses from political circles. While some opposition leaders have supported the call for a boycott, others have questioned the party's motives, pointing to its own record during its decade-long rule in the state. The ruling Congress, however, has remained tight-lipped, with ministers avoiding direct comment on the matter.

Political analysts suggest that the BRS is using the GRMB issue to regain political traction after its electoral setbacks. The party has been seeking to position itself as the primary defender of Telangana's interests, and this move appears to be part of that strategy.

What Lies Ahead

The state government has not yet issued an official statement on whether it will heed the BRS's demand. A decision is expected closer to the meeting date. If the government chooses to boycott, it could escalate tensions with the Centre and Andhra Pradesh, potentially affecting water-sharing talks for the coming year.

Observers are watching closely to see if the Congress government, which has its own disagreements with the GRMB, will use this opportunity to take a firmer stance. The coming weeks will clarify whether the boycott call gains traction or fizzles out as political posturing.
