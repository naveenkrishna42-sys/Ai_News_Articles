Canada PM hits back after Trump slaps 50% tariff, calls US move 'unilateral'

📅 2026-07-22 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US President Donald Trump imposes a 50% tariff on Canadian imports, citing discrimination against American products.
  • Canada's Prime Minister calls the move 'unilateral' and gives a firm response, vowing to defend Canadian interests.
  • The tariff escalation raises fears of a broader trade war, with experts warning of global economic repercussions.

Canada rejects US tariff action

Canada's Prime Minister has strongly condemned the United States' decision to slap a 50% tariff on Canadian imports, calling the move 'unilateral' and unjustified. The firm response came shortly after US President Donald Trump announced the steep tariff, accusing Canada of discriminating against American products.

Speaking from Ottawa, the Canadian leader said the country would not be intimidated and would take 'necessary measures' to protect its economy and workers. Officials have not yet confirmed what retaliatory steps Canada might take, but the tone from the capital was clear: this will not go unanswered.

Trump's tariff rationale and global reaction

The White House justified the 50% tariff by pointing to what it described as long-standing Canadian trade barriers and policies that put US businesses at a disadvantage. President Trump has repeatedly argued that American products face unfair treatment in the Canadian market, particularly in sectors like dairy and lumber.

The announcement has drawn sharp reactions from international observers. Media outlets including The Guardian, CNN, and the BBC have noted that the move could be a warning shot to other trading partners. CNN described the tariff as 'a warning to the rest of the world', while The Guardian said it showed Trump's willingness to escalate trade disputes dramatically.

Pressure mounts on Carney

At home, Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney is under growing pressure to deliver a strong counterpunch. The opposition and business groups are demanding swift action to shield Canadian exporters from the impact of the new tariffs. The move threatens key industries, including automotive parts, lumber, and agricultural products.

Economists warn that a full-blown trade war between the two neighbours could disrupt supply chains and raise costs for consumers on both sides of the border. The Canadian dollar has already weakened against the US dollar in the wake of the announcement, reflecting market anxiety.

What this means for trade relations

The US-Canada trade relationship is one of the largest in the world, with billions of dollars in goods crossing the border daily. The 50% tariff represents one of the most aggressive trade actions taken by Washington against Ottawa in modern times. The move has been labelled 'unilateral' by Canadian officials, who argue it violates the spirit of the USMCA trade agreement.

Legal experts say Canada could challenge the tariff through the dispute resolution mechanisms of the trade pact, but that process could take months. In the meantime, both nations are bracing for a prolonged period of economic friction.

The coming days will be crucial. Canada is expected to announce its retaliatory measures soon, and all eyes will be on whether the two sides can de-escalate or if this marks the start of a deeper trade war.
