Scam 2010 First Look: Siddharth Bodke to Play Subrata Roy in Hansal Mehta Series

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Siddharth Bodke's first look as Subrata Roy revealed for Scam 2010
  • Hansal Mehta begins production on the Sony LIV series
  • Legal threat from Roy's family has not halted production

The first look of Siddharth Bodke as Subrata Roy in the upcoming series Scam 2010 is out, and it has already set the tone for what promises to be another gripping chapter in the Scam franchise. Directed by Hansal Mehta, the show is currently in production for Sony LIV, with the streaming giant confirming that the shoot will proceed despite a legal threat from the Roy family.

A New Face for a Controversial Tycoon

Bodke, who was seen earlier in Mehta's Scam 2003: The Telgi Story, steps into the shoes of Subrata Roy, the flamboyant founder of the Sahara India Pariwar. The first look shows Bodke in a sharp suit, a confident smirk, and a carefully styled demeanour that mirrors Roy's public persona during his heyday.

Roy, who passed away in November 2025, was a towering figure in Indian business and politics. His empire, built on a vast network of deposits from millions of small investors, collapsed under the weight of regulatory scrutiny. The series will reportedly trace his rise and fall, with the title referencing the year 2010 when the storm began brewing.

Production Goes Ahead Despite Legal Hurdles

The announcement of the series was met with resistance from Roy's heirs, who sent a legal notice objecting to the portrayal. However, Sony LIV has confirmed that production will move forward. The platform has not commented on the specifics of the legal notice, but industry insiders suggest that the creative team is confident in its approach.

Mehta, known for his meticulous research and nuanced storytelling, has a track record of turning real-life scams into compelling drama. His previous work in the franchise, Scam 1992: The Harshad Mehta Story, was a critical and commercial hit, and Scam 2003 followed suit. Expectations are high for this third instalment.

What to Expect from Scam 2010

The series is expected to explore not just Roy's business dealings but also his political connections and the legal battles that dragged on for years. The narrative will likely cover the Supreme Court's intervention, the jailing of Roy, and the eventual unraveling of the Sahara empire.

Bodke's casting is a deliberate choice, given his ability to portray complex characters with a mix of charm and menace. His performance in Scam 2003 was widely praised, and this role appears to be a step up in scale and intensity.

For Mehta, this is also a continuation of his collaboration with Bodke, who has become a familiar face in the director's universe of crime and intrigue. The show's writing team, which has not been fully disclosed yet, is expected to bring the same level of detail that made the earlier seasons so immersive.

The Legal and Ethical Tightrope

Biographical dramas always walk a fine line between fact and interpretation. In India, where defamation laws are strict, the makers have to be careful. The legal notice from the Roy family adds a layer of tension, but it also generates buzz, which is rarely bad for a streaming show.

The makers have not released a premiere date yet. Filming is underway, and the production team is keeping details under wraps. Given the track record of the franchise, fans can expect a release sometime in the next year.

For now, the first look has done its job: it has sparked curiosity, ignited debate, and reminded everyone that the Scam universe is far from done.

All eyes will now be on how the makers handle the legal challenge and whether they can deliver a story that does justice to one of the most complex financial scandals in independent India's history.
