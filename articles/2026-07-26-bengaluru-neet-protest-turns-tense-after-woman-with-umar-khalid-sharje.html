Bengaluru NEET protest turns tense after woman with Umar Khalid, Sharjeel Imam poster asked to leave

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Woman with poster of Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam at Bengaluru NEET protest
  • Police asked her to leave, leading to confrontation
  • Protest over NEET exam irregularities and alleged paper leaks

A protest in Bengaluru over alleged irregularities in the NEET examination took a tense turn on Saturday when a woman carrying a poster featuring Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam was asked to leave the gathering. The incident occurred near Town Hall, where students and parents had assembled to demand a probe into reported paper leaks and cancellation of the exam.

Poster sparks confrontation

Eyewitnesses said the woman, whose identity has not been disclosed, held a placard with images of the two activists, both of whom are currently in jail in connection with the 2020 Delhi riots conspiracy case. Police personnel at the scene approached her and requested she step away from the protest site.

Video footage from the event shows officers speaking to the woman, after which a brief scuffle ensued. Other protesters intervened, urging calm. The woman was eventually escorted out of the area. Police have not officially commented on the reason for her removal.

Protest demands

The larger demonstration, organised by student groups, focused on demands for a Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) inquiry into the NEET paper leak allegations and the cancellation of the exam in affected centres. Protesters argued that the integrity of the examination process had been compromised.

Several participants said they were not associated with the woman or her poster. One student, who did not wish to be named, said, “We are here for a fair exam. That poster has nothing to do with our demands.” The incident diverted attention from the core issue for a time, with both police and organisers working to de-escalate the situation.

Police response

Bengaluru Police have not issued a formal statement on the incident. Earlier in the day, additional forces were deployed around Town Hall as a preventive measure. Officials said the protest was largely peaceful barring the brief confrontation.

Sources indicated that the woman was not arrested but was asked to leave to avoid any potential law and order problems. The poster, which also carried slogans against the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test, was confiscated. No further action has been reported.

The NEET paper leak allegations have sparked protests in several cities, including Delhi, Mumbai, and Hyderabad. The National Testing Agency (NTA) has denied any systemic failure, though multiple state governments have called for a thorough investigation.

What happens next: The protest in Bengaluru is expected to continue, with student groups planning a larger demonstration next week if their demands are not met. Police are likely to maintain a visible presence at future gatherings.
