Four Arrested for Cheating Chennai Gold Merchant of Rs 63 Lakh

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Four people arrested for cheating a gold merchant in Chennai.
  • The fraud involved a fake purchase of gold worth Rs 63 lakh.
  • Police recovered some of the stolen gold and cash from the accused.

Fraud on a Gold Deal

Chennai police have arrested four men for allegedly cheating a gold merchant of Rs 63 lakh. The incident occurred earlier this week in the city’s commercial district.

The merchant, who runs a jewellery shop in T. Nagar, was approached by the suspects posing as bulk buyers. They expressed interest in purchasing a large quantity of gold coins and bars.

The Deception Unfolds

According to police, the accused negotiated the deal over several days. On the day of the transaction, they came to the shop with a bag they claimed contained the payment in cash.

The merchant handed over the gold, valued at Rs 63 lakh, after the suspects showed him a stack of currency notes. However, when he later checked the bag, he found that only the top notes were genuine. The rest was a bundle of cut paper.

The merchant immediately alerted the police, who tracked the suspects through CCTV footage and mobile phone records.

Arrests and Recovery

The four accused were arrested from different locations in Chennai within 48 hours. Police have identified them as residents of the city, aged between 25 and 40.

Investigators recovered a portion of the stolen gold, along with some cash, from the suspects. They are also probing whether the gang had carried out similar scams elsewhere.

“The accused had planned this meticulously. They used a decoy transaction to gain the merchant’s trust,” a senior police officer said, requesting anonymity.

Legal Proceedings

The four have been booked under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code for cheating and criminal conspiracy. They were produced before a local court and remanded in judicial custody.

Police are now investigating if more people were involved in the operation. They have also advised jewellers to verify bulk buyers thoroughly before handing over goods.

“We urge traders to be cautious. Such frauds are becoming common,” the officer added.

What Next

The accused remain in custody as the investigation continues. Police are working to trace the remaining stolen gold and identify any other victims of the gang.
