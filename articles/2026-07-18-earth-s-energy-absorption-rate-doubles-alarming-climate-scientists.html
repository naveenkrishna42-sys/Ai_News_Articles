Earth's energy absorption rate doubles, alarming climate scientists

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Earth's energy imbalance has more than doubled, according to NASA data.
  • Shrinking cloud cover is letting more sunlight reach the surface, reducing Earth's reflectivity.
  • Scientists warn this 'global dimming' reversal is accelerating global warming at an alarming rate.

Alarming Spike in Earth's Energy Imbalance

Earth is absorbing heat at a rate that has more than doubled, according to fresh NASA data. This energy imbalance — the difference between the energy the planet receives from the sun and what it radiates back into space — is now at levels that have caught many climate scientists off guard.

Researchers say the trend is accelerating faster than most climate models had predicted. The imbalance is measured in watts per square metre, and the latest figures show a sharp upward climb over the past decade.

Shrinking Cloud Cover Adds to the Heat

A key driver behind this worrying shift is a phenomenon some scientists are calling a reversal of 'global dimming'. For decades, pollution particles in the atmosphere helped reflect sunlight away, partially shielding the planet from some warming. But as air quality has improved, that shield has weakened.

Now, new data from The Economist and The Times of India highlight that shrinking cloud cover is letting more sunlight reach the Earth's surface. This reduces the planet's overall reflectivity, or albedo, acting as a powerful new amplifier of global warming.

Less cloud cover means more solar radiation is absorbed by land and oceans, rather than bounced back into space. The effect is particularly pronounced over the oceans, where darker waters soak up heat more efficiently than brighter, reflective surfaces.

NASA Data Confirms the Doubling

NASA's satellite measurements show that Earth's energy imbalance has not just grown — it has more than doubled in recent years. This is not a subtle shift. It represents a fundamental change in how the planet's energy budget is behaving.

Scientists are now racing to understand the full implications. The extra energy trapped in the system is driving more intense heatwaves, faster ice melt, and rising sea levels. The data suggests the planet is now absorbing roughly two times the net energy it was absorbing just a couple of decades ago.

What This Means for India and the World

For a country like India, already on the front lines of climate impacts, the news is stark. More absorbed energy means more extreme weather — fiercer cyclones, longer heatwaves, and unpredictable monsoons. The Indian Ocean is warming rapidly, and this new data suggests that trend will only accelerate.

Scientists have not yet confirmed whether this shift is a temporary spike or a permanent step-change in the climate system. But the trend line is clear: the planet is heating up faster than before, and the old assumptions about how quickly we have to act are being rewritten.

The next few years of satellite data will be critical. If the energy imbalance continues to rise at this pace, the 1.5°C warming target could become a distant memory sooner than anyone expected.
