Secret CIA Mission Paved Way for UAE's Access to Advanced AI Chips

📅 2026-07-20 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • CIA conducted a secret mission to evaluate UAE's security protocols before allowing advanced AI chip exports.
  • The UAE was rewarded with access to coveted AI technologies for supporting the US war in Iran.
  • The decision to raise the UAE's export status is expected to strengthen AI security in the region.

A secret CIA mission played a decisive role in clearing the way for the United Arab Emirates to obtain advanced American artificial intelligence chips, according to reports from The Wall Street Journal and other sources. The operation assessed whether the Gulf nation could be trusted with some of the world's most sensitive computing hardware.

The mission's findings directly influenced Washington's decision to elevate the UAE's export status, allowing it to bypass certain restrictions that had previously blocked access to cutting-edge AI processors. The move came as a reward for the UAE's support of the US military campaign in Iran.

The Covert Assessment

CIA officers conducted a classified evaluation of the UAE's security infrastructure, examining how the country planned to protect and monitor the use of advanced AI chips. The goal was to ensure the technology would not be diverted to adversaries or used in ways that could threaten US national security.

The assessment reportedly involved direct interactions with senior Emirati officials, including members of the royal family. The CIA needed to verify that the UAE had robust safeguards in place to prevent unauthorised access to the chips, which power everything from military simulations to commercial AI applications.

A Reward for Wartime Support

The timing of the decision was not coincidental. The UAE had provided critical support to the United States during the war in Iran, including allowing access to military bases and intelligence sharing. In return, Washington decided to relax export controls on advanced technologies.

The UAE's state-owned AI firm, Core42, welcomed the decision, stating it would strengthen AI security in the region. Company officials said the upgraded export status would allow the UAE to develop its AI capabilities without compromising its partnership with the United States.

Implications for Global Tech Trade

This development marks a significant shift in how the US manages the global trade of advanced semiconductors. For years, Washington has maintained strict controls on AI chip exports to prevent China and other rivals from acquiring the technology. The UAE's case shows that geopolitical alliances can override those restrictions.

Industry experts say the decision could set a precedent for other US allies seeking access to restricted technologies. Countries that demonstrate strong security protocols and align with US strategic interests may find it easier to obtain advanced chips in the future.

The CIA's involvement in the export approval process is itself noteworthy. Intelligence agencies rarely play such a direct role in commercial technology transfers, but the stakes involved with AI chips are considered high enough to warrant extraordinary measures.

What Comes Next

The UAE is expected to begin receiving shipments of advanced AI chips in the coming months. Emirati officials have said the technology will be used primarily for civilian purposes, including healthcare, climate modelling, and autonomous systems. But the dual-use nature of AI means the same hardware could eventually support military applications.

Washington will likely maintain close monitoring of how the chips are used, with the CIA's assessment serving as a baseline for future evaluations. Any breach of trust could quickly reverse the UAE's privileged access.
