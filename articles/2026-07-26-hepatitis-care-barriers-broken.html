Hepatitis in India

Millions of Indians may be living with hepatitis B without knowing it, according to a liver transplant surgeon. The virus is particularly dangerous due to its silent nature, often going undetected until severe liver damage has occurred.

Experts like Dr Shubham Vatsya, a gastroenterologist, are working to raise awareness about hepatitis B and C. They emphasize that vaccines exist and treatments are effective, yet many people remain unaware of the risks and options.

Understanding Hepatitis B and C

Hepatitis B and C are viral infections that attack the liver, causing damage over time. If left untreated, they can lead to liver cirrhosis, liver cancer, and even death. The fact that many people are asymptomatic makes it difficult to detect and treat the diseases.

A key concern is that people may be carriers of hepatitis B without knowing it. This 'silent epidemic' can be spread through bodily fluids, making it essential to take preventive measures.

Treatments and Vaccines

Lucky for those affected, vaccines and treatments are available for hepatitis B and C. These medical advancements have improved significantly over the years, offering hope to those living with the diseases.

However, despite the existence of effective treatments, hepatitis B and C remain deadly. This raises questions about the barriers to care and why many people are not receiving the medical attention they need.

World Hepatitis Day 2026

On World Hepatitis Day 2026, experts and organizations came together to raise awareness about the diseases. They emphasized the importance of education, prevention, and treatment in combating hepatitis B and C.

  • Experts stressed the need for increased awareness and education about hepatitis B and C.
  • They also highlighted the importance of preventive measures, such as vaccination and safe practices.
  • Additionally, they emphasized the need for improved access to treatment and care for those affected.

As the conversation around hepatitis continues, it is clear that there is still much work to be done to break down barriers to care and ensure that those affected receive the medical attention they need.

What's Next

As efforts to raise awareness and improve access to care continue, the focus will be on implementing effective strategies to combat hepatitis B and C. With the help of experts, organizations, and the public, it is possible to make a significant impact and reduce the number of people affected by these diseases.
