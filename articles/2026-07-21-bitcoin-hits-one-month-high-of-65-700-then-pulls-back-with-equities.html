Bitcoin Hits One-Month High of $65,700, Then Pulls Back With Equities

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin reached $65,700, its highest in a month, before sliding.
  • The pullback coincided with a dip in major stock indices.
  • Traders are watching for support at $62,000 and resistance near $66,000.

Bitcoin climbed to a one-month high of $65,700 on Monday before reversing course and falling back alongside broader equity markets. The world's largest cryptocurrency briefly touched the level during early Asian trading hours, only to shed gains as selling pressure emerged.

The pullback came as major stock indices in the US and Europe also edged lower. Traders pointed to profit-taking after the recent rally and lingering uncertainty over interest rate decisions as reasons for the retreat.

Price Action and Market Context

At the time of writing, Bitcoin was trading near $63,800, down about 2.9% from its intraday peak. The move higher earlier in the session broke above the $65,000 resistance level for the first time since mid-June.

Analysts said the rally lacked strong follow-through buying. “We saw a spike above $65,700 on relatively thin volume, and it didn't take much to trigger a reversal,” one trader noted. The broader crypto market also saw modest declines, with Ethereum slipping 1.5% to $3,450.

Correlation With Stocks

The simultaneous retreat in Bitcoin and equities reinforced the growing correlation between the two asset classes. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq both opened lower on Monday, dragged down by tech stocks.

Investors are awaiting key economic data later this week, including US GDP figures and personal consumption expenditure data, which could influence the Federal Reserve's policy path. A surprise rate hike or hawkish stance often pressures risk assets like Bitcoin.

“Bitcoin is increasingly trading like a risk-on asset, moving in tandem with equities,” said a market strategist. “Until we get clarity on rates, expect continued volatility.”

Technical Levels in Focus

Traders are now watching whether Bitcoin can hold above the $62,000 support zone. A break below that could signal a deeper correction toward $60,000. On the upside, the $66,000 level remains a key resistance point.

Open interest in Bitcoin futures rose during the rally, suggesting some speculative activity, but liquidations accelerated during the pullback. Over the past 24 hours, roughly $120 million in long positions were wiped out across major exchanges, data shows.

Macro factors, including the strength of the US dollar and bond yields, will likely dictate Bitcoin's next move. The dollar index edged up 0.2% on Monday, adding pressure on crypto prices.

What to watch: All eyes are on the upcoming US GDP and PCE data releases later this week. A softer-than-expected reading could reignite Bitcoin's rally, while strong data may trigger further selling.
