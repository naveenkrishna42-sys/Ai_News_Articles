CBI fines Rs 2.7 crore in corruption case

📅 2026-08-24 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ex-DGM, five others get 10-year RI in corruption case
  • CBI court fines Rs 2.7 crore
  • Case involves alleged irregularities in public procurement

CBI Court Imposes Rs 2.7 Crore Fine on Ex-DGM

The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) court has imposed a fine of Rs 2.7 crore on an ex-Deputy General Manager (DGM) and five others in a corruption case. The verdict comes after a lengthy trial that exposed alleged irregularities in public procurement processes.

Ten-Year Rigorous Imprisonment for Key Accused

The ex-DGM and five other accused have been sentenced to ten years of rigorous imprisonment. The court found them guilty of misusing their positions to secure unfair advantages in public procurement tenders.

Case Details and Broader Implications

The case, which has been under scrutiny for over two years, involved allegations of bribery, forgery, and corruption in the procurement of goods and services by a state government department. The CBI's investigation revealed a pattern of irregularities that had gone unchecked for several years.

The verdict is a significant step towards holding public servants accountable for their actions. It sends a strong message that corruption in procurement processes will not be tolerated. However, critics argue that the sentence is lenient, given the scale of the irregularities and the damage caused to public exchequer.

Expert Analysis and Critical Assessment

Legal experts have praised the CBI for its thorough investigation and the stringent verdict. They believe that the case sets a precedent for future procurement-related cases. However, there are concerns that the sentence may not be sufficient to deter similar offenses in the future.

Economists warn that such cases can have a chilling effect on the procurement process, leading to delays and increased costs. They urge the government to review and strengthen procurement laws to prevent such irregularities in the future.

  • Sentence: Ten years of rigorous imprisonment
  • Number of accused: Six (one ex-DGM and five others)
