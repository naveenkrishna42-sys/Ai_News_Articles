Chennai honours two women for environmental stewardship

The Dr M S Swaminathan Award for Environment Protection 2026 was conferred on two women in Chennai on Thursday, recognising their decades of work in conservation and sustainable development. The recipients, whose names were announced at the ceremony, were honoured for their distinct contributions to protecting India's natural heritage.

The award, instituted in memory of the legendary agricultural scientist, carries a citation and a cash prize. It seeks to keep alive Dr Swaminathan's belief that environmental security is inseparable from food security and rural livelihoods.

A legacy of science and service

Dr M S Swaminathan, often called the father of India's Green Revolution, passed away in September 2023. He championed the concept of sustainable agriculture long before it became a global buzzword, advocating for farming practices that work with nature rather than against it.

The award in his name was established to honour individuals and organisations that carry forward his vision. Previous recipients have included scientists, grassroots workers, and policy advocates who have demonstrated measurable impact in their fields.

Recognising diverse contributions

While the organisers did not disclose detailed citations, they emphasised that the two awardees were selected for their complementary approaches—one focused on ecological restoration, the other on community-led conservation. Their work spans different ecosystems, yet both share a common thread: involving local communities as active partners in environmental protection.

The selection committee, comprising eminent scientists and environmentalists, evaluated nominations from across the country over several months. The criteria included innovation, sustainability, and the potential for replicability in other regions.

An occasion for reflection

The ceremony, held at a city auditorium, drew scientists, students, and policymakers. Speakers underlined the urgency of scaling up grassroots solutions in the face of climate change, and called for stronger links between research institutions and field-level practitioners.

They also stressed the need to document and disseminate successful models, so that good practices can be adapted elsewhere. The event served as a reminder that environmental protection is not a standalone issue but is closely tied to agriculture, water security, and public health.

What lies ahead

As India works toward its net-zero commitments, awards like this highlight the importance of recognising unsung heroes. The two awardees are expected to use the platform to expand their ongoing projects, which focus on restoring degraded landscapes and building climate resilience in vulnerable communities.

Organisers have indicated that nominations for the next cycle will open early next year, with a call for more entries from underrepresented regions. The hope is that the award will continue to inspire a new generation of environmental leaders who view conservation as both a duty and an opportunity.
