Over 100 ABVP workers detained during Jharkhand Assembly march over student protests

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 100 ABVP workers detained during march to Jharkhand Assembly
  • Protest relates to police action on students amid exam row
  • MP Parimal Nathwani urges CM Hemant Soren to resolve demands via dialogue
  • Tamil Nadu passes anti-NEET resolution amid similar student unrest

Ranchi, August 11: Police detained more than 100 Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP) workers on Tuesday as they attempted to march towards the Jharkhand Assembly in protest against the recent police action on students. The detentions came amid escalating tensions over the ongoing exam row that has triggered widespread student demonstrations across the state.

The ABVP workers were held before they could reach the Assembly premises, with authorities citing prohibitory orders in the area. Eyewitnesses said the protesters were seated on the ground near the march route and were taken into custody peacefully, though the situation remained charged.

Context of the protests

The protests stem from a police lathi charge on students earlier this week, which has since become a flashpoint for wider anger over alleged irregularities in state examinations. Student groups have been demanding action against officials responsible for the police action, as well as transparency in the examination process.

Jharkhand has seen a series of protests in recent days, with students from various colleges and universities joining hands. The ABVP, the student wing of the Bharatiya Janata Party, has sought to position itself at the forefront of the demonstrations, accusing the state government of heavy-handedness.

Political reactions and calls for dialogue

In a significant development, Rajya Sabha MP Parimal Nathwani has urged Chief Minister Hemant Soren to resolve the students' demands through dialogue. Nathwani's appeal, reported by The Times of India, underscores the political sensitivity of the issue, with the opposition seeking to corner the ruling Jharkhand Mukti Morcha-led government.

Nathwani's statement also hinted at a broader political contestation, as he attacked the Opposition for what he termed as opportunistic posturing. The MP's intervention comes at a time when the Soren government is facing pressure from multiple quarters over its handling of the student unrest.

Wider unrest and anti-NEET resolution

The Jharkhand protests are not occurring in isolation. In Tamil Nadu, the state assembly passed an anti-NEET resolution on the same day, reflecting a growing regional backlash against centralised entrance examinations. The resolution, which urges the Union government to exempt the state from the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test, has added to the national discourse on examination reforms.

Meanwhile, in Jammu and Kashmir, the Leader of Opposition has extended support to the Jharkhand students, attacking the Opposition for what he described as inconsistent responses to student issues. This cross-regional solidarity suggests that the unrest in Jharkhand could resonate beyond state borders, influencing political narratives ahead of upcoming elections.

What happens next

The detained ABVP workers are expected to be released later tonight, but the underlying grievances remain unresolved. The Soren government has yet to announce a formal response to the students' demands, though sources indicate that a meeting with student representatives may be convened in the coming days.

All eyes will be on the Assembly session scheduled for later this week, where the opposition is likely to raise the issue. How the government balances firmness with conciliation will determine whether the protests escalate or subside.
