Mumbai court sentences 44 Somalis to life in prison for piracy

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • 44 Somali nationals convicted by a special court in Mumbai.
  • All sentenced to life imprisonment for piracy-related charges.
  • Verdict is among the most significant anti-piracy judgments in India.

Court delivers verdict in major piracy case

A special court in Mumbai has sentenced 44 Somali nationals to life imprisonment for piracy. The judgment was handed down on Tuesday, concluding a case that has drawn attention to India’s role in prosecuting maritime crime.

The convicted individuals were found guilty of hijacking vessels and holding crew members hostage. Officials have not yet disclosed the specific names of the ships involved or the exact dates of the attacks.

Prosecution details and evidence

The prosecution built its case on testimony from rescued crew members and naval personnel who intercepted the pirates. Investigators presented documents and communication records linking the accused to acts of armed robbery at sea.

Lawyers for the defence argued that their clients were fishermen forced into piracy. The court rejected this claim, stating that the evidence showed clear intent and organisation.

Legal framework and sentencing

India’s anti-piracy laws allow for stringent punishment, including life imprisonment, for those convicted of hijacking or attacking ships. The special court in Mumbai has handled several such cases in recent years.

This is one of the largest single groups of accused to be convicted in a piracy trial in India. The sentence reflects the severity with which Indian courts treat maritime crimes.

Reaction from authorities

Coast Guard and Navy officials have welcomed the verdict. They say it sends a strong signal to criminal networks operating in the Indian Ocean region.

“This judgment reinforces India’s commitment to protecting international shipping lanes,” a senior official said on condition of anonymity.

What comes next

The convicted men are expected to be held at a high-security prison in Maharashtra. Appeals are likely, though legal experts say the prosecution’s case was robust.

Indian authorities continue to monitor Somali pirate activity, which has declined in recent years but remains a concern for merchant vessels transiting key routes.
