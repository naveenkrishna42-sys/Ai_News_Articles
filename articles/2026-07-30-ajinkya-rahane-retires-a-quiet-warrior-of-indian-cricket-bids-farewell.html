Ajinkya Rahane retires: A quiet warrior of Indian cricket bids farewell

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ajinkya Rahane has retired from all international cricket.
  • The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) officially acknowledged his career.
  • Sachin Tendulkar and Virat Kohli led tributes, highlighting his composure and reliability.

Ajinkya Rahane, a name that rarely dominated headlines but always held the Indian batting line-up together, has announced his retirement from international cricket. The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) confirmed the development on Thursday, paying tribute to a career defined by quiet resilience rather than loud aggression.

The 38-year-old from Mumbai walked away with 5,077 Test runs, 11 centuries, and a captaincy legacy that includes one of India's greatest overseas victories — the 2020-21 Border-Gavaskar Trophy win in Australia.

Never in the spotlight, always on call

Rahane's career was a study in understatement. While contemporaries like Virat Kohli and Rohit Sharma drew attention for their strokeplay, Rahane was the man holding the other end. He was never the most flamboyant batsman in the dressing room, but he was often the most reliable.

His technique against the moving ball, especially in seaming conditions abroad, made him a vital cog in India's Test side. From the Lord's hundred in 2014 to the match-winning 112 at the Melbourne Cricket Ground in 2020, Rahane's best knocks came when the team needed them most.

'You showed that composure isn't the opposite of aggression'

Sachin Tendulkar led the tributes on social media, writing that Rahane "showed that composure isn't the opposite of aggression." The message struck a chord because it perfectly captured the essence of Rahane's game — a calm, unruffled presence in the eye of a storm.

Virat Kohli called Rahane his "favourite batting partner." The two shared numerous stands in the middle, often rescuing India from precarious positions. Kohli's tribute highlighted the trust and understanding the pair developed over years of playing together.

A captain for the ages

Perhaps Rahane's greatest contribution came when he wasn't in the limelight. In 2020, when Kohli returned home for paternity leave after the first Test of the Australia series, Rahane took over the captaincy. India had been bowled out for 36 in Adelaide. The series seemed lost.

Rahane led from the front, scoring a century in Melbourne to level the series. The team went on to win the decider in Brisbane, breaching the Gabba fortress. It remains one of India's greatest series victories, and Rahane was its quiet architect.

He captained India in six Tests, winning four. His leadership style was understated but effective, earning respect from teammates and opponents alike.

The numbers and the legacy

Rahane's Test average of 38.45 does not tell the full story. He averaged 43.15 in overseas Tests, a significant jump that reflected his value away from home. In England, Australia, South Africa, and New Zealand, he was often India's most dependable batsman.

His 11 Test centuries include seven outside India. Among Indian batsmen with at least 20 overseas innings, only Rahul Dravid and Sunil Gavaskar have a higher average in SENA countries (South Africa, England, New Zealand, Australia).

In limited-overs cricket, Rahane played 90 ODIs and 20 T20Is, scoring two ODI centuries. He was a regular in the middle order before being phased out as India built a younger white-ball squad.

What happens next for Rahane remains unclear. He has not announced plans to take up coaching or commentary. For now, the cricketing world is left to reflect on a career that was more about substance than style, more about duty than drama.

The BCCI's tribute noted that Rahane "always answered the call." That, in the end, may be his finest epitaph.
