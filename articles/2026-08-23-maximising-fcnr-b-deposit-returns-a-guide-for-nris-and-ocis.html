Maximising FCNR (B) Deposit Returns: A Guide for NRIs and OCIs

📅 2026-08-23 📂 Credit Cards & Cashback Original source ↗
Key points
  • FCNR (B) deposits allow NRIs to hold funds in foreign currencies.
  • Interest earned on these deposits is fully tax-exempt in India.
  • PSU banks often offer competitive long-term rates compared to private lenders.

Understanding FCNR (B) Deposits

Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) accounts, or FCNR (B), allow Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) to maintain deposits in designated foreign currencies. Unlike NRE accounts, these deposits are not subject to exchange rate fluctuations on the principal amount, as the funds remain in the original currency of deposit.

The primary advantage for account holders is the complete tax exemption on interest earned in India. This makes the instrument a preferred choice for individuals looking to park excess foreign currency while keeping it fully repatriable.

Comparing Interest Rates: PSU vs. Private Sector

Interest rates for FCNR (B) accounts are linked to global benchmarks like the SOFR (Secured Overnight Financing Rate). Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) often adjust their rates conservatively, focusing on stability and long-term liquidity. In contrast, private sector banks occasionally offer higher promotional spreads to attract premium NRI banking customers.

Investors should check the specific currency yield curves before choosing a lender. While major currencies like the USD, GBP, and EUR are standard, some private banks offer broader currency options. Always verify if the bank provides an online dashboard to track real-time yield adjustments, as global central bank policies can influence these rates frequently.

Key Features and Eligibility

  • Tenure: Deposits typically range from one year to five years.
  • Repatriation: Both principal and interest are fully and freely repatriable.
  • Eligibility: Valid only for NRIs and OCIs.
  • Taxation: Interest income is exempt from income tax in India; check local tax laws in your country of residence for reporting requirements.

How to Open Your Account Online

Most major Indian banks allow NRIs to initiate the account opening process through their digital portals. You will need your passport, valid visa or OCI card, and proof of non-resident status.

After visiting the bank’s official NRI portal, select the FCNR (B) product page. Fill out the e-form and upload self-attested copies of your documents. Many banks now use Video KYC to verify your identity, eliminating the need for physical branch visits. Once the account is activated, you can transfer funds via SWIFT from your overseas bank account to the designated FCNR account number.

Strategic Recommendation

FCNR (B) deposits are ideal for NRIs who wish to avoid the risk of rupee depreciation while earning tax-free interest on their foreign currency holdings. This account is best suited for conservative savers or those planning to repatriate their funds back to their country of residence in the near future. If you are indifferent to currency risk and seek higher absolute returns, you might consider NRE fixed deposits instead, though these are subject to exchange rate volatility.
