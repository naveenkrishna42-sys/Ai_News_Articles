Modi's Message to Xi: Peace on the Border Comes First

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has drawn a direct line between peace along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) and the future of India-China relations, telling Chinese President Xi Jinping that differences between the two nations should not be allowed to become disputes. The remarks came on the sidelines of the BRICS summit, where both leaders welcomed what they described as steady progress in bilateral ties after years of friction.

For New Delhi, the sequencing is deliberate. Diplomatic normalisation, resumed direct flights, and eased investment screening only hold if the border remains calm. That position, articulated repeatedly since the 2020 Galwan clash, has now become the operating principle of India's China policy.

The 'Three Mutuals' Framework Explained

Modi told Xi that ties must be guided by the "three mutuals" — mutual respect, mutual sensitivity, and mutual interest. The formulation is not new, but its repetition at a leadership-level meeting gives it fresh weight as a negotiating template.

In practical terms, mutual respect covers sovereignty and territorial integrity; mutual sensitivity addresses each side's core concerns, including the border and Pakistan; and mutual interest anchors cooperation in trade, climate, and multilateral forums. Indian officials have privately used the framework to argue that China cannot expect market access while massing troops along the LAC.

What Both Sides Have Actually Delivered

Since the October 2024 disengagement agreement at Depsang and Demchok, both armies have pulled back from several friction points. Direct flights resumed in 2025, and visa processing for business travellers has widened. However, troop levels in the Ladakh sector remain well above pre-2020 norms, and the de-escalation phase — pulling heavy equipment and additional brigades back to peacetime locations — is still unfinished.

Why This Matters Beyond the Border

India's trade deficit with China crossed $99 billion in recent years, and New Delhi has kept strict scrutiny on Chinese FDI through the Press Note 3 route. Any thaw directly affects electronics, solar module, and pharmaceutical supply chains where Indian manufacturers depend on Chinese inputs.

For global buyers and supply-chain managers, the signal is cautious optimism. A calmer LAC reduces the risk of sudden customs disruptions or port delays that hit shipments through Shanghai, Ningbo, and Shenzhen. It does not, however, remove the structural dependence that Indian policymakers are trying to reduce through PLI schemes and domestic component manufacturing.

BRICS and the Wider Diplomatic Chessboard

Xi used the same summit to call on BRICS nations to play a "due role" in bringing peace to West Asia — a reference to the Israel-Iran and Gaza theatres. That push positions BRICS as an alternative mediation platform to Western-led diplomacy, with India walking a careful line between its Quad partnerships and its BRICS membership.

Modi's emphasis on border peace therefore serves two purposes: it protects India's sovereignty interests while keeping New Delhi credible as a bridge power that can talk to both Washington and Beijing.

Key Real-World Takeaways

  • Border tranquillity is now formally the precondition, not the by-product, of India-China engagement.
  • Disengagement is partial; de-escalation and de-induction of heavy forces remain the next test.
  • Trade normalisation will stay selective, with Press Note 3 scrutiny unlikely to disappear soon.
  • BRICS is emerging as a parallel diplomatic venue, complicating India's balancing act.
  • For businesses, watch LAC developments as a leading indicator of customs and shipping stability.

The Road Ahead

Expect incremental confidence-building measures: more border personnel meetings, possible resumption of Kailash Mansarovar Yatra, and technical talks on river data sharing. A full return to pre-2020 normalcy is unlikely without verified de-escalation on the ground.

Modi's message is clear and transactional — peace on the border buys progress everywhere else. Whether Beijing reciprocates with matching force reductions will determine if this thaw endures or remains a seasonal pause.
