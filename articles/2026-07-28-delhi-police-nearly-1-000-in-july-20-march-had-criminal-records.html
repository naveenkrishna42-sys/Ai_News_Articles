Delhi Police: Nearly 1,000 in July 20 March Had Criminal Records

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi Police identified nearly 1,000 individuals with criminal records among those who participated in the July 20 march near Parliament.
  • The police have not yet disclosed the nature of the crimes or whether any arrests have been made.
  • The march was organized to protest against the government's policies on agricultural reforms.

Police Reveal Criminal Records Among Protesters

Nearly 1,000 people who took part in the July 20 march near Parliament had criminal records, Delhi Police have said. The disclosure comes weeks after the protest, which drew thousands to the streets.

Police officials confirmed that the individuals were identified through a review of records and surveillance footage. The exact number of participants with criminal backgrounds has not been officially released, but sources say the figure is close to 1,000.

Nature of Crimes Not Disclosed

The Delhi Police have not specified the types of crimes these individuals were involved in. It is unclear if the records include minor offences, serious felonies, or cases that are still pending.

Officials have also not said whether any arrests have been made in connection with the march. The police are likely to share more details in the coming days.

Context of the July 20 March

The July 20 march was organized by farmer unions and other groups to protest against the government's agricultural reforms. The protest was largely peaceful, but there were reports of minor skirmishes between protesters and police.

The march took place near the Parliament building, a high-security zone. The police presence was heavy, and the area was cordoned off to prevent any untoward incidents.

Opposition Questions Police Claims

Opposition leaders have questioned the timing and motive behind the police's revelation. They argue that the government is trying to vilify the protest movement by highlighting the criminal records of some participants.

“This is a classic diversion tactic,” said a senior opposition leader, speaking on condition of anonymity. “Instead of addressing the genuine concerns of farmers, the police are being used to smear the entire movement.”

The ruling party has defended the police, saying that the law will take its course. “We have full faith in the Delhi Police to ensure that all participants are dealt with according to the law,” a party spokesperson said.

What Happens Next

The Delhi Police are expected to file a detailed report on the matter. It remains to be seen if any legal action will be taken against the individuals with criminal records.

The protest movement, meanwhile, continues to gather momentum. Farmer unions have announced another march next month, which is likely to draw even larger crowds.
