What happened on the IndiGo Delhi–Chennai flight

Multiple Indian media reports say an IndiGo flight operating the Delhi–Chennai sector was diverted to Hyderabad after smoke was reported onboard. The trigger was reportedly a passenger’s power bank that began to smoke mid-flight, prompting the crew to prioritise ventilation and safety checks before landing.

Incidents like this are operationally serious even when no one is injured. Lithium-ion devices can fail in ways that go beyond a “hot gadget” to include thermal runaway—an accelerated heating event that can produce smoke quickly in a closed cabin environment.

Why a power bank can turn into a flight safety issue

Most power banks use lithium-ion cells paired with protection electronics that should stop overcharge, over-discharge, short circuits, and over-current. When those protections are missing, bypassed, or overwhelmed—often due to counterfeit cells, physical damage, water ingress, or manufacturing defects—the device may overheat.

For buyers and travellers, the uncomfortable reality is that “capacity” (mAh) can be marketed aggressively while safety engineering varies widely across brands and tiers. A high-capacity unit also typically contains more stored energy, which can increase the severity of failure if safeguards fail.

Buyer-relevant risk factors passengers may not notice

Even careful users can face risk if a product is poorly made or has an internal fault. Look for warning signs: swelling, a cracked casing, intermittent charging, a hot back during normal use, or a burning/chemical smell—any of which should lead to immediate discontinuation.

Airline and airport rules often limit battery size and require safe packaging, but they cannot prevent failures caused by defects or user-damage. The practical takeaway: choose safer products and handle them like “energy devices,” not phone accessories.

Practical steps for passengers during travel

When smoke or heating is reported onboard, crews generally follow emergency protocols: isolate the affected item if safe, increase monitoring, and assess for visible smoke and heat sources. Passengers should not attempt ad-hoc interventions around an actively failing battery.

Before boarding, ensure your power bank is in good condition and protected from accidental shorts—especially if it’s stored with coins, keys, or loose metallic items. Avoid using the power bank while it is inside a bag without a protective sleeve or pouch if you can’t visually confirm its condition.

What to do if your power bank gets unusually hot

  • Stop using it immediately and disconnect cables.
  • Do not try to “cool it down” while charging continues.
  • If you see swelling, discontinue use permanently and keep it isolated and away from flammable materials.
  • Inform cabin crew if anything is noticed onboard; do not handle it further yourself.

What to look for when buying a power bank after such incidents

After an onboard diversion linked to a power bank incident, buyers should shift from “fast charging only” to “fault protection first.” A strong product should include reputable cell sourcing, multiple layers of protection, and clear compliance markings rather than vague claims.

In many markets, the most common consumer failure modes are not the brand “name,” but the internals: cheap cells, inadequate protection boards, and overstated specs. Since you may not open the casing to inspect cells, your best proxy is credible certifications, transparent ratings, and a manufacturer with a documented safety approach.

Key insights for value-for-money buyers

“More mAh” is not automatically better. A 10,000mAh power bank may be sufficient for most smartphone trips, while frequent travellers with laptops should buy for real charging needs and ensure the device supports the relevant voltage/current profiles.

  • Pros: Verified protection circuits reduce the chance of runaway failures; reputable ratings improve compatibility and charging efficiency.
  • Pros: Moderate thermal performance is a practical indicator you can test in the first few charges.
  • Cons: Budget products may advertise high capacity and fast charging while cutting safety margin.
  • Cons: Even genuine products can fail if physically damaged, exposed to moisture, or used with incompatible cables.

For manufacturers and buyers: the post-incident checklist

For airlines, diversion decisions follow safety protocols triggered by smoke or abnormal heat, regardless of whether a device is a phone battery, power bank, or other lithium unit. For consumers, the incident is a reminder that battery safety is not theoretical—product selection and handling habits directly affect risk.

At TIVRA News, we treat these events as buyer guidance: choose brands that can back safety features, verify ratings, and avoid damaged or suspicious units. The best “upgrade” after a power-bank fire story is not a higher wattage—it’s a safer power bank with proven protection behaviour and credible documentation.
