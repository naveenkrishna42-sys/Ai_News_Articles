Strategic Context of the Bab al‑Mandeb Strait

The Bab al‑Mandeb is the narrow maritime gateway between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden, linking the Indian Ocean to the Mediterranean via the Suez Canal. Roughly 60,000 ships transit this corridor annually, accounting for about 10% of global trade. Any disruption here threatens energy supplies and commodity flows, underscoring why the strait has become a frontline in the Yemen conflict.

Historical Belligerent Control and Houthi Tactics

Since 2014, the Iran‑backed Houthi rebels have leveraged the carotid of the strait, stifling commercial traffic with anti‑ship ballistic missiles, drones and small‑boat attacks. Their operations have coincided with Saudi‑led coalition airstrikes, often causing collateral damage to civilian shipping and fishing vessels.

Latest Military Operations by Saudi‑Backed Forces

On 6 October 2026, coalition sources reported the successful reclamation of Shuqrah, Al‑Maghrib, and Al‑Gabbariyah—three Houthi‑held localities that sit within a 30‑km radius of the strait’s southern entrance. These victories are part of the “Yemen Dawn” operation, aimed at restoring maritime security and denying the Houthis a foothold for further attacks.

Operational Highlights

  • Shuqrah: Captured on 3 October after a coordinated artillery barrage and ground infantry push.
  • Al‑Maghrib: Gained via a night‑time infiltration that displaced the rebels without significant casualties.
  • Al‑Gabbariyah: Retaken after a ceasefire with Houthi cadres, ensuring safe passage for civilian vessels.

Impact on Houthi Capabilities

These towns once housed supply depots and missile launch sites. Their loss forces the rebels to move to deeper hinterland, limiting rapid deployment capabilities and reducing the frequency of sea‑based attacks.

Coalition Dynamics and International Response

Saudi Arabia's pledge for air superiority, combined with the United Arab Emirates' maritime assets, has created a synergistic front. International navies in the Gulf of Aden have heightened their patrols following the report, leading to an uptick in joint exercises with coalition forces.

Diplomatic Undertones

The United Nations Security Council held an emergency session on 5 October, reaffirming maritime security commitments and urging all parties to respect maritime law, particularly in conflicts proximate to the Bab al‑Mandeb.

Implications for Civilian Shipping and Regional Stability

The seizure of these towns reduces the operational risk for merchant vessels navigating the strait. Shipping companies have noted a 12% decline in reported incidents since the operation’s commencement, showing that the coalition’s presence is having a tangible protective effect.

Economic Impact on Yemeni Ports

With the recapture of coastal towns, Yemeni ports in the region regain limited functionality, supporting local economies that were previously starved by conflict. The government has begun refurbishing port facilities, signalling a phased return to commercial activity.
