India's forex reserves surge to record $729 billion

📅 2026-08-28 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's forex reserves hit record $729.33 billion
  • RBI's dollar hunt ends early due to sustained capital inflows
  • RBI allows banks to tap FX swap window more frequently

India's Forex Reserves Hit Record High

India's foreign exchange reserves have surged to a record $729.33 billion, marking a $12.42 billion jump from the previous week. This latest data from the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) underscores the country's growing financial resilience and strong capital inflows.

Why This Matters

The surge in forex reserves is a positive sign for India's economy. It indicates that the country is attracting significant foreign investment, which can be used to finance imports, support the rupee, and fund government expenditures. This is particularly important given India's current account deficit and reliance on foreign capital to bridge the gap.

RBI's Dollar Hunt Ends Early

The RBI's dollar hunt, a program to accumulate foreign currency reserves, has ended ahead of schedule. This early termination is attributed to sustained capital inflows, which have made the program redundant. The RBI has also allowed banks to tap the foreign exchange swap window more frequently, further facilitating capital inflows.

Broader Impact

The increase in forex reserves has several implications. It provides a financial cushion against external shocks, such as a depreciation of the rupee or a slowdown in global capital flows. It also enhances India's ability to participate in international financial markets, such as bond issuances and foreign direct investment.

Expert Opinion and Analysis

Economists and financial analysts are optimistic about the latest data. They believe that the sustained capital inflows are a sign of India's growing attractiveness as an investment destination. However, they also caution that the country must manage its current account deficit and ensure that the capital inflows are used productively.
