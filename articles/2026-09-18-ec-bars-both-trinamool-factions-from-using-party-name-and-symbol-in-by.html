EC Bars Both Trinamool Factions from Using Party Name and Symbol

The Election Commission of India issued a directive on 17 September 2026 preventing both the Mamata Banerjee‑led Trinamool Congress faction and the rival group from using the party’s registered name ‘All India Trinamool Congress’ and its emblem, the ‘Flowers & Grass’ symbol, in the forthcoming byelections. The order cites violations of the Model Code of Conduct and internal party disputes that threaten the integrity of the electoral process.

Legal Basis of the Order

Under Section 12A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, the EC can deregister or restrict the use of a party’s name and symbol if it determines that the entity is unable to maintain a unified structure. The commission noted that repeated public clashes, parallel rallies, and competing claims to the TMC brand created confusion among voters, warranting interim restraint.

Impact on the Trinamool Factions

For the Mamata‑led camp, the restriction forces a temporary rebranding effort while retaining control of the party’s organisational machinery. The rebel faction, which claims legitimacy based on a separate congress, faces the same limitation, weakening its ability to campaign under a recognised banner. Both sides must now rely on alternative identifiers approved by the EC for the byelection ballots.

Political Reactions and Allegations

Rahul Gandhi, Congress leader, denounced the move as a collusion between the EC and the BJP, describing it as ‘theft’ of a political identity. He warned that such actions erode trust in independent institutions and could be replicated against other regional parties. The BJP has denied any coordination, stating the EC acted solely on statutory grounds.

Implications for Electoral Fairness

Analysts note that while the EC’s intent is to prevent voter confusion, the timing—coinciding with high‑stakes byelections—raises perception of partisanship. If the factions cannot convey their distinct platforms under recognizable symbols, voter turnout and informed choice may suffer, especially in constituencies where TMC traditionally commands a strong base.

Possible Outcomes and Way Forward

The EC is expected to review the proposed alternative names and symbols within a week. Approval would allow the factions to contest the byelections under new identifiers, while rejection could prolong the stalemate and potentially lead to legal challenges. Observers suggest that a mediated settlement, possibly involving internal party elections, could restore the original brand and avert further institutional strain.

Key Real‑World Highlights

  • EC’s order marks a rare instance where both factions of a major regional party are simultaneously barred from core identifiers.
  • Mamata‑led TMC’s protest underscores tensions between state leadership and central electoral authorities.
  • Opposition’s accusation of EC‑BJP collusion highlights growing skepticism about institutional neutrality.
  • The situation may set a precedent for handling intraparty disputes during active election cycles.
