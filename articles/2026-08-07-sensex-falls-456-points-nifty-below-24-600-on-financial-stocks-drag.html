Sensex Falls 456 Points, Nifty Below 24,600 on Financial Stocks Drag

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 456 points, closing lower in a volatile session.
  • Nifty slipped below the 24,600 mark, dragged by financial stocks.
  • Broader market showed mixed trends; global cues and sectoral rotation influenced trading.

Indian equity benchmarks ended Friday's session in the red, with the Sensex tumbling 456 points and the Nifty slipping below the key 24,600 level. The decline was led by financial stocks, which bore the brunt of selling pressure throughout the day. The volatility was palpable as traders adjusted positions ahead of the weekend, keeping volumes moderate.

The 30-share Sensex closed at its day's low, while the broader Nifty settled at 24,587, down 0.8 percent. Market participants pointed to profit-booking in recent outperformers, particularly in the banking and financial services space, which had seen a strong run in the preceding sessions. The mood on Dalal Street remained cautious, with global cues offering little support.

Financial Stocks Lead the Decline

Financial heavyweights, including major private and public sector banks, were the primary drag on the indices. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India were among the top losers on the Sensex, collectively shaving off more than 200 points. The Nifty Bank index fell over 1 percent, underperforming the broader market.

Analysts attributed the weakness to a mix of factors—rising concerns over deposit growth, margin pressures, and profit-taking after a recent rally. "Financials had run up sharply, so some cooling off is natural," said a Mumbai-based dealer, requesting anonymity. The selling was broad-based within the sector, with mid-sized lenders also facing pressure.

Broader Market Shows Mixed Trends

While the headline indices fell, the broader market painted a slightly different picture. The BSE Midcap index managed to end flat, while the Smallcap index posted marginal gains, suggesting that retail investors remained selective. Advances and declines were almost evenly matched on the BSE, indicating that the sell-off was not indiscriminate.

Sectoral indices were a mixed bag—IT and pharma stocks bucked the trend, offering some respite. Technology stocks, in particular, gained on the back of a weaker rupee, which typically boosts export-oriented companies. Meanwhile, auto and FMCG stocks ended lower, tracking the overall weakness.

Global Cues and Rupee Movement

Overseas markets provided a weak handover, with Asian peers trading lower through the day. Concerns over global growth and uncertainty around central bank policies kept investors on edge. European markets were also expected to open soft, adding to the cautious sentiment.

The Indian rupee, however, showed resilience, trading in a narrow band against the US dollar. A stable currency helped limit foreign fund outflows, though provisional data suggested that foreign institutional investors were net sellers in the cash market. Domestic institutions, on the other hand, were believed to be buyers, providing some cushion.

What to Watch Next Week

Market participants will now turn their attention to upcoming macroeconomic data, including inflation figures and industrial production numbers, due next week. Any surprise in these readings could influence the Reserve Bank of India's policy trajectory, which remains a key driver for rate-sensitive sectors like banking.

Additionally, global events—particularly the US Federal Reserve's stance and oil price movements—will be closely monitored. A sustained fall in crude prices could ease inflationary pressures, while any spike may add to concerns. Traders are likely to remain nimble, with the Nifty's immediate support seen near the 24,400 level.

For now, the market's direction hinges on how financial stocks fare in the coming sessions. A recovery in that space could lift sentiment, but further weakness may trigger deeper cuts. Investors are advised to watch global cues and sectoral rotations closely.
