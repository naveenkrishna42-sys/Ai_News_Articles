US military confirms end of latest Iran strikes, 11th consecutive night of attacks

📅 2026-07-22 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US military confirms end of latest round of strikes on Iran
  • This marks the 11th consecutive night of US attacks
  • Secretary of State Rubio labels Iran 'main troublemaker' in the region
  • Trump threatens to attack 'Pickaxe Mountain' site
  • Iran denies any nuclear activity at the threatened site

Strikes Continue for 11th Night

The United States military announced on Wednesday that its latest round of air strikes against Iran have concluded. This marks the 11th successive night of attacks, signaling a sustained military campaign that shows no immediate signs of de-escalation.

Officials have not yet confirmed specific targets or the extent of damage inflicted during the overnight operations. The Pentagon stated the strikes were aimed at degrading Iranian military capabilities, though details remain classified.

Rubio's Blunt Assessment

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio did not mince words when describing Iran's role in the region. He labelled Tehran the 'main troublemaker' in West Asia, adding that the country is 'in a lot of trouble'.

Rubio also warned against Iran's demands regarding the Strait of Hormuz, calling them a 'dangerous precedent'. The strategic waterway is critical for global oil shipments, and any disruption could send shockwaves through energy markets worldwide.

Threats Against 'Pickaxe Mountain'

The situation escalated further when President Donald Trump threatened to attack a site referred to as 'Pickaxe Mountain'. The cryptic reference has not been officially explained by the Pentagon or the White House.

Iranian officials were quick to respond, denying that any nuclear activity is taking place at the site Trump mentioned. Tehran has consistently maintained that its nuclear programme is for peaceful purposes, a claim repeatedly rejected by Washington and the International Atomic Energy Agency.

Regional Fallout

The sustained US bombing campaign has drawn sharp reactions from neighbouring countries and global powers. Several nations have called for restraint and a return to diplomatic channels, but the White House has shown no willingness to halt military operations.

Iran's allies in the region, including groups in Iraq, Syria, and Yemen, have condemned the strikes. Analysts warn that the longer the campaign continues, the higher the risk of a broader regional conflict that could draw in multiple actors.

The United Nations has yet to convene an emergency session, though diplomatic sources indicate that several member states are pushing for one. Russia and China have both criticised the US action, calling it a violation of international law.

What to Watch

All eyes are now on whether the US will announce a 12th night of strikes or if diplomatic backchannels will finally yield any progress. The world watches as the clock ticks toward what could be an even more dangerous phase of this conflict.
