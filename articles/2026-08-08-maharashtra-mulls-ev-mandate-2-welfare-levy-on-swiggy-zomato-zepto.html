Maharashtra mulls EV mandate, 2% welfare levy on Swiggy, Zomato, Zepto

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Maharashtra government proposes EV-only delivery fleets for Swiggy, Zomato, Zepto
  • A 2% welfare levy on each order is planned to fund rider benefits
  • Platforms may need to absorb costs or pass them to consumers
  • Draft policy awaits stakeholder consultations before final approval

MUMBAI: Maharashtra is preparing a sweeping policy that could require food and grocery delivery platforms such as Swiggy, Zomato, and Zepto to switch their entire fleets to electric vehicles and pay a 2 per cent welfare levy on every order. The proposal, which has not been made public yet, is part of a broader state push to formalise gig worker protections and cut urban emissions.

Two senior state officials familiar with the draft told The Indian Express that the plan is at an advanced stage. It would apply not just to the three major platforms but to any company operating last-mile delivery services in the state. The levy, calculated on the order value, would fund a dedicated welfare corpus for delivery riders, covering insurance, health benefits, and accident compensation.

The EV mandate is equally ambitious. The policy sets a target for platforms to convert a significant share of their two-wheeler fleets to electric within a fixed timeframe. Companies that fail to comply could face penalties, though the exact enforcement mechanism is still being worked out.

Rider welfare fund takes shape

The proposed 2 per cent welfare levy is at the heart of the plan. It mirrors similar initiatives in other states, but Maharashtra's version is notable for its scope and the explicit link between platform operations and rider welfare. The fund would be administered by a state-appointed body, with contributions tracked digitally to ensure transparency.

Officials argue that the levy is necessary because delivery platforms have grown rapidly but rider protections have lagged. "These companies have built billion-dollar businesses on the back of gig workers, but many riders lack basic social security," one official said, speaking on condition of anonymity. The state believes a small per-order charge can create a sustainable safety net without burdening any single stakeholder.

For the platforms, the financial impact could be significant. A 2 per cent levy on millions of orders across Maharashtra would run into hundreds of crores annually. Whether the cost is absorbed by the companies or passed on to consumers remains an open question. Industry watchers expect a mix of both, with possible price hikes on delivery charges.

EV transition poses logistical hurdles

The EV mandate is a more complex ask. Delivery partners typically own their vehicles or lease them, and the cost of an electric two-wheeler is still higher than a petrol model. Charging infrastructure in dense urban areas like Mumbai and Pune is patchy, and range anxiety is a real concern for riders who clock long hours.

Platforms have already experimented with EV fleets in select cities, but a full transition across Maharashtra would require massive investment. Companies would need to subsidise vehicle purchases, set up battery-swapping stations, and ensure riders have access to affordable financing. The policy draft suggests a phased rollout, but it does not specify the timeline or how the transition costs will be shared.

Rider unions have welcomed the welfare levy but are cautious about the EV mandate. Many riders fear that forced EV adoption without adequate support could squeeze their earnings. "We want better benefits, but we also need to be able to afford the vehicles," said a rider who has worked for multiple platforms in Mumbai.

Industry reaction and next steps

The proposal has not been formally shared with the companies, but sources indicate that informal talks have begun. Swiggy, Zomato, and Zepto have not issued public statements, though they are likely to lobby for changes. The industry's main concerns are the levy's rate, the EV transition timeline, and the lack of clarity on who bears the upfront costs.

The policy is still in draft form and faces several layers of approval. State officials will hold consultations with platforms, rider groups, and EV manufacturers before finalising the rules. Any final decision will also need to be vetted by the state cabinet, which could take months.

What happens next is uncertain. The state could soften the mandate, or the platforms could launch a legal challenge. But the direction is clear: Maharashtra is intent on making delivery platforms contribute more to the welfare of the workers who power them. The coming months will show whether the policy survives contact with the industry, and at what cost to consumers.
