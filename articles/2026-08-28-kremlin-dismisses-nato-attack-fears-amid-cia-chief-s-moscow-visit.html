Kremlin dismisses CIA chief's visit as 'scare stories'

Russian officials have dismissed claims that CIA Director John Ratcliffe's secretive visit to Moscow was to warn Russia against attacking NATO territory, calling the allegations 'scare stories'. The Kremlin's denial comes amid growing concerns about the escalating tensions between NATO and Russia.

CIA chief's secretive Moscow trip

Ratcliffe's trip to Moscow, which was kept under wraps, has sparked speculation about the nature of his discussions with Russian officials. Some reports suggest that Ratcliffe delivered a bleak assessment of Russia's war efforts, but the CIA has not commented on the details of his visit.

Russian response to NATO attack fears

Russian President Vladimir Putin has previously stated that he would not allow NATO to attack Russian territory, but the Kremlin's denial of the CIA chief's visit as a warning against NATO attacks has raised eyebrows. The Kremlin's response has been seen as an attempt to downplay the seriousness of the situation and to avoid escalating tensions further.
