Tips for Indian Journalists Investigating Cryptocurrency

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • GIJN published a guide for journalists investigating cryptocurrency.
  • Covers blockchain basics, tracing transactions, and finding scam victims.
  • India's crypto market growth makes such reporting increasingly relevant.

What the Guide Covers

The Global Investigative Journalism Network (GIJN) has released a comprehensive guide aimed at reporters looking into cryptocurrency. Titled 'Reporter's Guide to Investigating Cryptocurrency', the resource breaks down how digital currencies work and how journalists can track them.

The guide explains blockchain technology in plain language. It walks reporters through reading public ledgers, identifying wallet addresses, and following money trails across exchanges.

Why It Matters for Indian Media

India has seen a surge in crypto trading and investment, especially among younger demographics. With regulatory uncertainty still hanging over the sector, scams and unregistered platforms have proliferated.

The GIJN guide offers tools to verify claims made by crypto projects. It shows how to check whitepapers for red flags and use open-source intelligence to trace suspicious transactions.

Practical Steps for Reporters

One section focuses on finding victims of crypto fraud. It advises journalists to monitor Telegram groups, Reddit threads, and local social media channels where aggrieved investors often share stories.

Another part deals with interviewing regulators and law enforcement. The guide suggests asking specific questions about pending cases and enforcement actions rather than generic policy statements.

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

The guide warns against over-reliance on self-proclaimed experts. Many crypto influencers push narratives that benefit their holdings. Reporters are advised to cross-check claims with independent data sources.

It also cautions against sensationalism. Not every price drop signals a rug pull, and not every rally is organic. The guide emphasises the need for context and data-driven reporting.

Tools and Resources

GIJN lists several free and paid tools for tracking crypto transactions. These include blockchain explorers like Etherscan and more advanced analytics platforms used by forensic accountants.

The guide also points to databases of known scam addresses and lists of sanctioned entities. These can help reporters quickly assess whether a project has been flagged by authorities.

For Indian journalists, the guide is particularly timely. The country's crypto market is estimated to be worth several crore rupees, with millions of users. Yet regulatory clarity remains elusive, leaving room for both innovation and abuse.

What Happens Next

The GIJN guide is available for free online. Indian newsrooms may consider using it for internal training sessions. As the crypto ecosystem evolves, such resources will become essential for journalists covering finance and technology.
