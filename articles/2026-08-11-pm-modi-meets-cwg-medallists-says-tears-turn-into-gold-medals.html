PM Modi meets CWG medallists, says 'tears turn into gold medals'

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi met Indian CWG medallists at his residence and congratulated them for their achievements.
  • He lauded Lovlina Borgohain for protesting against a distorted map of India at the Games.
  • The PM fed laddus to weightlifter Mirabai Chanu during the interaction, a gesture that went viral.

A meeting of champions

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday met the Indian medallists of the recently concluded Commonwealth Games at his official residence, in a warm and informal interaction that blended pride with personal touches. The gathering was an opportunity for the nation's leadership to acknowledge the grit and determination of the athletes who brought home laurels.

During the interaction, Modi addressed the assembled sportspersons with a message that struck an emotional chord. He spoke of the sacrifices and struggles behind every medal, using a phrase that resonated deeply: “From every drop of tears, gold medals are dripping.” The remark was aimed at capturing the unseen toil and emotional investment that goes into an athlete's journey.

Lovlina's stand on national honour

A significant moment of the meeting came when the Prime Minister lauded boxer Lovlina Borgohain for her objection to a distorted map of India at the Games. The Assam-born pugilist had reportedly raised the issue when an incorrect representation of the country was displayed, a stance that Modi publicly commended. He described her act as a reflection of the deep sense of patriotism that Indian athletes carry with them, even while competing on foreign soil.

Lovlina, who won a bronze medal in the 75kg category, has been in the spotlight for her performances in the ring, but this particular gesture off it has earned her widespread admiration. The PM's words underscored how the athletes are not just competitors but also ambassadors of the nation's integrity.

Laddus and light moments

The interaction was not all serious talk. In a lighter vein, Modi was seen feeding laddus to weightlifter Mirabai Chanu, a gesture that quickly became the talking point of the day. The Manipuri lifter, who clinched a gold medal with a record-breaking effort, accepted the sweet with a smile, and the moment was captured by cameras, adding a human touch to the ceremonial occasion.

Such gestures have become a hallmark of Modi's interactions with sportspersons, blending warmth with respect. For Mirabai, who has battled injuries and personal challenges to reach the top, the moment was a recognition of her resilience as much as her achievement.

A broader message for Indian sports

The meeting also served as a platform for the PM to reflect on the broader growth of Indian sports. He emphasised that the country's athletic talent is no longer confined to a few disciplines, with medal winners emerging from boxing, weightlifting, badminton, athletics, and table tennis, among others. The diversity of achievements, he suggested, is a sign of a healthy and expanding sporting culture.

Modi's words come at a time when India is preparing for future international competitions, including the Asian Games and the Olympics. The government's focus on sports infrastructure and athlete support was reiterated, though specific policy announcements were not part of this interaction.

The athletes, for their part, expressed gratitude for the Prime Minister's time and encouragement. Many shared their experiences from the Games, including the challenges of competing in a high-pressure environment. The exchange was described by those present as candid and motivating.

As the meeting concluded, the focus now shifts to the next set of challenges for these athletes. With the Asian Games on the horizon, the expectation is that the momentum from the Commonwealth Games will carry forward. The PM's message of turning tears into gold is likely to remain a motivating refrain for the squad as they prepare for future battles on the global stage.
