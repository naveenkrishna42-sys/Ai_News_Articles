Ram Navami 2026 in South India: A Guide to Key Temples and Kalyanam Traditions

Ram Navami, the festival celebrating the birth of Lord Rama, falls on April 1, 2026. In South India, the day is marked not just by prayers but by grand Kalyanam (celestial wedding) ceremonies of Lord Rama and Goddess Sita. Temples across the region prepare for weeks, drawing lakhs of devotees.

The festival holds special significance in states like Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Kerala. Each temple adds its own regional flavour to the rituals, from ornate processions to classical music performances.

Bhadrachalam Temple: The Epicentre of Celebrations

The Bhadrachalam Temple in Telangana is arguably the most famous venue for Ram Navami in South India. The Sita Rama Kalyanam here is a state-sponsored event, attended by dignitaries and thousands of pilgrims. The ceremony is conducted with Vedic chants and a ceremonial procession of the deities.

Devotees believe that attending this Kalyanam brings marital bliss and prosperity. The temple remains packed from early morning, with special queues for darshan. The local government arranges free meals and accommodation for visitors during the festival.

Rameswaram and Srirangam: Icons in Tamil Nadu

In Tamil Nadu, the Ramanathaswamy Temple in Rameswaram holds a special place. The temple, associated with the Ramayana, conducts a grand Kalyanam in its sprawling corridors. Devotees take a holy dip in the 22 wells before attending the ceremony.

The Srirangam temple near Tiruchirappalli also hosts a vibrant Ram Navami. The festival here includes a procession of the utsava murthy (processional deity) on a decorated vahana (vehicle). Traditional nadaswaram (pipe instrument) and thavil (drum) performances accompany the rituals.

Tirupati and Kerala: Unique Regional Practices

The Kodandarama Temple in Tirupati, dedicated to Rama holding a bow, sees a massive turnout. The temple authorities organise a week-long series of events, including discourse on the Ramayana and cultural programmes. The Kalyanam is performed at an auspicious hour determined by the temple priests.

In Kerala, the Thriprayar Temple in Thrissur district is notable. Here, the festival is part of the larger Rama Navami celebrations that include a unique offering of 'payasam' (sweet porridge). The temple follows a strict ritual calendar, and devotees often observe a full-day fast.

Rituals and Observances Across the Region

Common to all these temples is the recitation of the Ramayana, particularly the Sundara Kanda and Yuddha Kanda. Many households in South India set up a small kalyanam mandapam at home, performing a symbolic wedding. Women dress in traditional silk sarees, and families prepare special prasadam like panakam (jaggery water) and vadapappu (soaked lentils).

Fasting is observed from sunrise to sunset by many devotees. The day concludes with aarti and distribution of prasad. Temples often release official schedules a week before the festival, which can be checked on their websites or local dailies.

Travel arrangements should be made early, as roads and trains near major temples get congested. The celebrations are a blend of religious fervour and community bonding, reflecting the enduring legacy of the Ramayana in South Indian culture.

What to watch: The exact timings of the Kalyanam at Bhadrachalam and Rameswaram will be announced closer to the date by temple trusts. Devotees planning to visit should confirm darshan slots and accommodation availability at least two weeks in advance.
