Bitcoin steadies near $65K as $1.1B ETF inflows boost mood; CPI next

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin trades near $65,000, supported by $1.1 billion in crypto ETF inflows.
  • Investors now focus on upcoming US CPI data for clues on Federal Reserve policy.
  • ETF inflows signal renewed institutional interest despite recent market volatility.

ETF inflows steady the ship

Bitcoin is holding its ground near the $65,000 mark, with the latest wave of exchange-traded fund (ETF) inflows providing a cushion against recent selling pressure. Data shows crypto ETFs have absorbed roughly $1.1 billion over the past week, a clear sign that institutional appetite remains intact even as the broader market digests mixed macroeconomic signals.

The inflows mark a turnaround from the outflows seen in early August, when a spike in geopolitical tensions and a stronger dollar had knocked sentiment. Traders say the steady bid from ETF buyers has helped Bitcoin reclaim its 50-day moving average, a level many chartists track closely.

“It’s not a euphoric rally, but the fact that money is coming back into these vehicles suggests the dip was bought,” said one Mumbai-based crypto fund manager, who asked not to be named. “The question now is whether the macro data cooperates.”

CPI in the crosshairs

All eyes are now on the US Consumer Price Index (CPI) report due later this week. A hotter-than-expected print could force the Federal Reserve to keep interest rates higher for longer, a scenario that typically pressures risk assets including cryptocurrencies.

Economists polled by major agencies expect headline CPI to have risen 0.2% month-on-month in July, with core inflation also seen ticking up modestly. But recent energy price moves have injected uncertainty into those forecasts.

“Bitcoin has traded like a risk asset again over the past month, so the CPI number will likely set the tone for the next leg,” noted a Singapore-based analyst. “If inflation surprises to the upside, we could see a quick retest of the $62,000 support zone.”

Institutional money versus retail caution

The ETF inflows stand in contrast to retail trading volumes, which have remained subdued on Indian exchanges. Local platforms report a dip in new user sign-ups since the regulatory clarity around taxation and reporting requirements took effect.

Still, the institutional flows are being watched closely by domestic players. Several Indian family offices have increased their crypto allocations through offshore funds, according to industry sources, even as the domestic regulatory framework remains in flux.

The latest $1.1 billion inflow figure is the largest weekly total since May, when Bitcoin last traded near $70,000. That period was followed by a sharp correction, and some veterans warn the current rally could be similarly fragile.

What to watch

Beyond the CPI print, traders are also monitoring the US dollar index and Treasury yields, both of which have shown a strong inverse correlation with Bitcoin this year. A dovish signal from Fed Chair Jerome Powell at the Jackson Hole symposium later this month could provide additional tailwinds.

For now, Bitcoin appears comfortable in a range between $63,000 and $66,000. A close above the upper end on rising volume would likely attract fresh momentum buyers, while a break below the lower end could trigger another round of ETF outflows.

The coming 48 hours will be decisive. If CPI comes in line or cooler, the current bid could extend toward $68,000. If it surprises hot, expect volatility to return quickly.
