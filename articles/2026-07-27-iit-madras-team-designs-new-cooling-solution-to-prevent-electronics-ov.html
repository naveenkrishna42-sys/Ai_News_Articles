IIT Madras team designs new cooling solution to prevent electronics overheating

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • IIT Madras researchers have created a new cooling design for electronics.
  • The design uses micro-channels and nanofluids to dissipate heat more effectively.
  • This innovation aims to prevent overheating in devices like smartphones and laptops.
  • The team tested the design in lab conditions and found significant temperature reduction.

Researchers at the Indian Institute of Technology Madras have developed a new cooling design to protect electronic devices from overheating. The innovation addresses a growing problem as gadgets become smaller and more powerful, generating more heat in tighter spaces.

How the cooling design works

The design uses micro-channels etched into a heat sink, combined with a nanofluid coolant. The nanofluid contains nanoparticles that enhance heat transfer, drawing heat away from critical components like processors and batteries.

According to the team, this approach improves thermal management efficiency significantly compared to conventional air cooling methods. The system is compact enough to fit inside smartphones, laptops, and other portable electronics.

Lab tests show promising results

In experiments conducted at the IIT Madras campus, the new design reduced operating temperatures by up to 15 degrees Celsius under heavy load conditions. The researchers used standardised testing procedures to measure performance.

They compared their design with existing cooling solutions and found it outperformed them in both heat dissipation and energy consumption. The team published their findings in a peer-reviewed engineering journal.

Why overheating is a critical issue

Overheating can cause permanent damage to electronic components, reduce battery life, and even pose safety risks like fires. As devices get thinner, manufacturers struggle to fit effective cooling systems inside them.

The IIT Madras solution offers a way to keep temperatures in check without adding bulk. This could help extend the lifespan of devices and improve user experience, especially for gamers and heavy users.

Next steps for the research

The researchers are now working with industry partners to explore commercial applications. They plan to scale up the design for larger electronics like servers and electric vehicle batteries.

Patent applications have been filed for the technology. The team expects prototypes to be ready for field testing within two years, though no timeline has been set for mass production.
