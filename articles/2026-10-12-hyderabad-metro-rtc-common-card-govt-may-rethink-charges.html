Hyderabad Metro-RTC Common Card: Govt May Rethink Charges

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-10-12 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Telangana government to reconsider pricing structure of the integrated Hyderabad Metro-Telangana RTC common mobility card following stakeholder feedback.
  • The common mobility card enables seamless travel across Hyderabad Metro Rail and RTC buses but faces criticism over fare calculations and convenience charges.
  • Officials indicate a review will focus on balancing operational sustainability with affordability for daily commuters using multi-modal public transport in the city.

Background: The Common Mobility Card Initiative

The Telangana government introduced the Hyderabad Metro-Telangana RTC common mobility card to streamline travel across the city's metro rail and bus services. Launched with the aim of promoting unified digital payments and reducing dependency on cash or multiple tickets, the card allows passengers to travel across both modes without purchasing separate passes. However, concerns over fare structuring and additional service charges have emerged since its rollout.

Integration Across Modes

The card operates on a stored-value model, deducting fares based on distance traveled in the metro and flat or zone-based rates for RTC buses. Users can top up the card at metro stations, RTC depots, and designated retail outlets. Integration was seen as a step toward creating a unified transport ecosystem in alignment with national common mobility standards.

Reasons Behind the Reconsideration

Public and political feedback highlighted perceived inconsistencies in fare calculation, especially for short-distance metro rides combined with bus travel. Critics argue that the cumulative cost of using both services via the card often exceeds the sum of individual tickets, undermining the value proposition. Additionally, concerns were raised about the transparency of convenience fees and maintenance charges levied during top-ups.

Operational Challenges

RTC and Hyderabad Metro Rail authorities defended the pricing as necessary to cover infrastructure and operational costs. However, officials now acknowledge that user experience metrics and ridership data suggest room for recalibration. A government source indicated that a committee comprising transport and finance departments is analyzing fare models and interoperability benchmarks used in other Indian metro cities.

Implications for Commuters

For daily commuters, especially those traveling across zones or combining metro and bus journeys, a revised pricing model could lead to savings of up to 15–20 percent per trip. The government has not ruled out waiving certain surcharges or introducing slab-based discounts for frequent users. Experts suggest that aligning the card’s pricing with existing smart card schemes in Delhi, Mumbai, or Bengaluru may improve adoption rates.

Potential Adjustments

Possible changes under review include revisiting convenience fees on digital recharges, introducing monthly pass options, and capping daily charges for multi-leg journeys. Officials also hinted at enhancing merchant integration to expand top-up accessibility beyond metro stations and major bus stands.

Looking Ahead

The outcome of the review is expected to influence the broader rollout of the National Common Mobility Card (NCMC) framework in Hyderabad. A balanced approach could strengthen public transport usage and set a precedent for other state-level transit integrations. Commuters will be notified of any revised tariffs before implementation, likely within the next quarter.
