Why women are more prone to fungal infections in monsoon and how to prevent them

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • High humidity in monsoon creates ideal conditions for fungal growth, especially in women.
  • Tight synthetic clothing and dampness are major triggers for infections like vaginal yeast and ringworm.
  • Prevention includes wearing breathable fabrics, keeping skin dry, and avoiding prolonged wetness.

The monsoon season brings relief from the heat, but it also ushers in a host of health concerns, particularly for women. Fungal infections spike during this time, and medical experts point to a combination of biological and environmental factors that make women more vulnerable.

High humidity and constant dampness create the perfect breeding ground for fungi. Women, due to their anatomy and certain lifestyle habits, often bear the brunt of this seasonal shift. The most common infections include vaginal yeast infections, ringworm, and athlete's foot.

Why women are at higher risk

Dr. Neha Sharma, a gynaecologist based in Mumbai, explains that the vaginal environment is naturally moist and warm. During the monsoon, the added humidity disrupts the pH balance, allowing Candida, a type of fungus, to overgrow. "Women are more prone because their reproductive tract provides a conducive environment for fungal growth, especially when combined with damp clothing," she says.

Another reason is the widespread use of tight-fitting synthetic underwear and leggings. These fabrics trap heat and moisture, worsening the problem. Women who wear wet clothes for extended periods, such as after rain or exercise, are at an even greater risk.

Symptoms to watch for

Common signs of a fungal infection include itching, redness, a white discharge with a cottage-cheese texture, and a burning sensation during urination or intercourse. Skin infections may appear as red, circular rashes with raised edges, often on the inner thighs, under the breasts, or in the groin area.

If left untreated, these infections can spread and cause significant discomfort. Doctors advise seeking medical attention at the first sign of persistent symptoms rather than relying on over-the-counter creams, which may not be effective for all types of fungi.

Prevention tips from experts

Prevention is largely about keeping the skin dry and clean. Dr. Sharma recommends switching to loose-fitting cotton underwear, which allows the skin to breathe. "Avoid synthetic fabrics during the monsoon. Change out of wet clothes immediately, and never sit in damp underwear for hours," she says.

Other practical measures include:

  • Using a separate towel for intimate areas and washing it frequently.
  • Avoiding scented soaps, douches, or feminine hygiene sprays that can irritate the skin.
  • Wearing open-toed footwear or breathable sandals to prevent foot infections.
  • Maintaining a diet low in sugar, as fungi feed on sugar and can multiply faster.

For women prone to recurrent infections, doctors sometimes recommend probiotics or specific antifungal treatments as a preventive measure during the monsoon. However, these should only be taken under medical guidance.

When to see a doctor

Most fungal infections are treatable with antifungal creams or oral medications. But if symptoms persist beyond a week, or if there is severe pain, swelling, or fever, a doctor should be consulted. Pregnant women and those with diabetes or weakened immune systems need to be especially cautious.

Fungal infections are not just uncomfortable—they can also lead to secondary bacterial infections if the skin breaks due to scratching. Early diagnosis and treatment are key to avoiding complications.

As the monsoon continues, women need to stay vigilant. Simple changes in clothing and hygiene routines can make a significant difference. With the right precautions, it is possible to enjoy the rains without worrying about infections.
