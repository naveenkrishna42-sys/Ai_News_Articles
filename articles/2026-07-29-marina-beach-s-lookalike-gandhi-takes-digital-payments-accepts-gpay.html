Marina Beach's lookalike Gandhi takes digital payments, accepts GPay

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A lookalike of Mahatma Gandhi at Marina Beach in Chennai now accepts digital payments like GPay.
  • The man, known locally as the 'living Mahatma', has been a fixture on the beach for years.
  • The shift to digital payments reflects the broader adoption of cashless transactions in India.

The man who is Gandhi on Marina Beach

For years, a familiar figure has walked the sands of Chennai's Marina Beach. Dressed in a simple white dhoti and carrying a wooden staff, he bears an uncanny resemblance to Mahatma Gandhi. Tourists and locals alike stop to take photographs with him, and some offer a few rupees as a token of respect or gratitude.

Now, that exchange has gone digital. The man, known simply as the 'living Mahatma', has started accepting payments through Google Pay (GPay). A small QR code card hangs around his neck, next to his spectacles. Visitors can scan and pay without reaching for their wallets.

A small card, a big change

The shift happened recently. A volunteer helped him set up the digital wallet and printed the QR code. The man, who is in his 60s, admits he does not fully understand the technology. But he knows the scans bring in money. “Earlier, people would give coins or small notes. Now, they show their phones. I just show them the card,” he said.

His daily routine remains unchanged. He arrives at the beach by 6 am, walks along the shore, and sits under a tree near the Gandhi statue. He does not ask for money. But the donations have become steadier since he started using the QR code. On some days, he collects around Rs 300 to Rs 500. On weekends, it can touch a thousand rupees.

How the idea came about

A group of college students from a nearby engineering college noticed him struggling to count small change. One of them suggested the digital option. They helped him link his Aadhaar card to a payment app and printed a laminated card. The students visit once a week to check his balance and help him withdraw cash.

The man does not have a smartphone. The students use their own phones to process the payments. He trusts them completely. “They are like my grandchildren,” he said.

Living a simple life

Despite the digital upgrade, his life remains frugal. He lives in a small rented room in Triplicane, about two kilometres from the beach. He eats at a local temple that offers free meals. The money he collects goes towards his rent and medical expenses. He has no family in the city.

He has been coming to Marina Beach for over 15 years. His resemblance to Gandhi was first noticed by a tourist from Gujarat. The tourist asked for a photo, and soon others followed. Over time, he became a local landmark. Some call him “Gandhi Thatha” (Gandhi grandfather).

He does not try to imitate Gandhi's speeches or mannerisms. He simply stands or walks quietly. That stillness, he believes, is what draws people. “I don't need to say anything. They see me and they remember Bapu,” he said.

Chennai Corporation officials are aware of his presence but have not officially recognised him. He operates without a permit. No one has asked him to leave.

What comes next

The students plan to get him a basic feature phone with UPI capability. That would allow him to receive payments without depending on others. They are also looking into a small donation box with a digital display. For now, the QR code works.

As digital payments spread across India, even the most traditional figures are adapting. On Marina Beach, the living Mahatma now accepts GPay — one scan at a time.
