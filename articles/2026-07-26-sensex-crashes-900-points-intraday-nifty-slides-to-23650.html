Sensex crashes 900 points intraday, Nifty slides to 23650

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 900 points intraday, Nifty dropped to 23,650 level
  • Global sell-off and FII outflows among key triggers
  • Weak quarterly earnings from index heavyweights added pressure

Indian equity markets witnessed a brutal sell-off on Friday, with the BSE Sensex plunging over 900 points during intraday trading. The broader Nifty50 index slipped to the 23,650 mark, wiping out gains from earlier in the week.

The sharp decline comes amid a confluence of negative triggers that spooked domestic investors. From weak global cues to sustained selling by foreign institutional investors (FIIs), the day's trade turned into a rout by mid-session.

Global cues weigh heavy

Markets across Asia were under pressure after overnight losses on Wall Street. Weak economic data from the US and renewed fears over interest rate hikes in developed economies dampened risk appetite.

Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng both fell over 1%, setting a bearish tone for Indian indices right from the opening bell. The sell-off in global markets was led by technology stocks, which also impacted Indian IT shares.

FII selling continues unabated

Foreign portfolio investors have been net sellers in Indian equities for the past several sessions. Provisional data shows FIIs offloaded shares worth over Rs 3,000 crore in the previous session alone.

The relentless selling by foreign funds has been a major drag on the market's momentum. Analysts attribute this to the strengthening of the US dollar and rising bond yields in developed markets, making emerging markets like India less attractive.

Weak quarterly results add to woes

Disappointing earnings from some index heavyweights further accelerated the decline. Several companies reported results that missed street estimates, triggering sharp falls in their stock prices.

Sectors like banking, auto, and metals were among the worst hit. Heavyweights such as Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank saw significant selling pressure, pulling the benchmarks down sharply.

The Nifty Bank index fell over 2% in intraday trade, reflecting the broad-based nature of the sell-off. Midcap and smallcap stocks also bled, with the broader indices falling in tandem with the benchmarks.

What triggered the intraday crash?

Traders pointed to a combination of factors that converged on Friday. Weak global cues at the open were followed by sustained FII selling in the cash market. As the day progressed, heavy index options expiry-related positions added to the volatility.

Additionally, fresh concerns over domestic inflation data and its impact on monetary policy kept sentiment fragile. The Reserve Bank of India's stance on interest rates remains a key monitorable for the markets.

While the market did recover some ground from the day's lows in late afternoon trade, the overall tone remained weak. The Volatility Index (VIX), a measure of market fear, spiked over 10% during the session.

Investors are now closely watching the next set of quarterly earnings and global central bank meetings for cues. With no immediate trigger for a reversal, traders expect volatility to remain elevated in the coming sessions.
