US Open Drama: Star Power Fades as Doubles Draws Open Up

📅 2026-08-27 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Serena Williams and Carlos Alcaraz eliminated in mixed doubles quarterfinals.
  • Gael Monfils and Elina Svitolina demonstrate chemistry to secure a path forward.
  • The tournament highlights a clear divide between exhibition-style hype and tactical doubles cohesion.

The Cost of Star-Studded Pairings

The US Open mixed doubles draw often serves as a playground for tennis royalty, but the recent elimination of Serena Williams and Carlos Alcaraz proves that individual brilliance does not automatically translate to court synergy. While the pairing generated immense media interest, their exit at the hands of Flavio Cobolli and Belinda Bencic highlights the technical gap between singles icons and specialized doubles players.

Tennis is fundamentally a game of geometry and communication. When two singles players team up, they often struggle to cover the net effectively or anticipate partner movement. Alcaraz and Williams, despite their immense skill, lacked the rhythm required to dismantle a team that functions as a cohesive unit.

The Power of Real-World Chemistry

Contrasting this exit is the progress of Gael Monfils and Elina Svitolina. Unlike the celebrity "super-teams" assembled for marketing purposes, this married couple brings a unique advantage: years of lived experience and intuitive understanding. Their success in the tournament is no fluke.

In professional tennis, doubles success relies heavily on non-verbal cues and knowing exactly where your partner is at all times. Monfils and Svitolina play with a synchronized energy that is rarely seen in ad-hoc pairings. Their advancement suggests that in the high-stakes environment of a Grand Slam, structural cohesion outweighs raw individual ranking.

A Shift in Tournament Dynamics

The early exits of high-profile players like Novak Djokovic and Aryna Sabalenka alongside Williams and Alcaraz have stripped the bracket of its biggest draws. For tournament organizers, this is a double-edged sword. While the loss of marquee names hurts immediate ticket demand, it opens the door for doubles specialists to finally capture the spotlight.

  • Strategic Disadvantage: Singles players often try to "win" every point from the baseline, which is a losing strategy in doubles.
  • Tactical Realities: The serve-and-volley game remains the gold standard for doubles, a skill set that has dwindled in the modern singles era.
  • Market Correction: Fans are realizing that "star power" does not guarantee a deep run in specialized formats.

The remainder of the US Open will likely favor those who prioritize the nuance of the format over individual flair. As the tournament progresses, the focus must shift toward the tactical adjustments required to navigate the net-heavy reality of professional doubles. Whether Monfils and Svitolina can maintain this momentum against dedicated doubles specialists remains the primary question for the closing stages.
