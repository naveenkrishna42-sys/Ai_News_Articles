ASEAN Foreign Ministers Voice 'Serious Concern' Over Middle East Hostilities

📅 2026-07-21 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • ASEAN foreign ministers voiced 'serious concern' over renewed US-Iran hostilities
  • US Secretary of State Marco Rubio attended the ASEAN Foreign Ministers' Meeting
  • South China Sea tensions also dominated discussions
  • Ministers urged restraint and adherence to international law

ASEAN Ministers Meet Amid Escalating Middle East Crisis

Southeast Asian foreign ministers gathered in Vientiane, Laos, on Monday for what officials described as one of the most consequential meetings in the bloc's recent history. The shadow of a renewed conflict between the United States and Iran loomed large over the discussions.

In a joint statement issued after the closed-door session, the Association of Southeast Asian Nations expressed 'serious concern' over the escalating hostilities in the Middle East. The ministers warned that the crisis could have 'grave implications for global peace, security, and economic stability.'

Rubio Attends, Pushes for Unified Position

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio attended the meeting, making a strong push for ASEAN member states to take a clearer stance against what Washington describes as Iranian aggression. Rubio's presence underscored the Biden administration's efforts to rally allies even as it conducts airstrikes against Iranian targets.

Diplomats said Rubio briefed the ministers on the latest developments and urged them to condemn Iran's alleged role in attacks on commercial shipping in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Oman. Several ASEAN members, however, remained cautious, preferring a neutral posture that avoids picking sides in the US-Iran confrontation.

South China Sea Tensions Add to the Mix

The Middle East crisis was not the only flashpoint on the agenda. The perennial issue of the South China Sea also dominated discussions, with several ministers voicing alarm over continued militarisation and aggressive maritime patrols.

Philippine Foreign Secretary Enrique Manalo called for a 'rules-based order' and urged all parties to abide by the 2016 Permanent Court of Arbitration ruling. China, a major trading partner for many ASEAN states, was not directly named but its activities in the disputed waters were clearly the subject of pointed exchanges.

Vietnam and Malaysia echoed similar concerns, while Cambodia and Laos, both closer to Beijing, urged dialogue and restraint. The bloc's inability to reach a consensus on the South China Sea issue has long been a source of frustration for some members.

Trade and Economic Fallout

Beyond security, the ministers discussed the potential economic fallout from a broader Middle East war. Any disruption to oil supplies from the Persian Gulf could send energy prices soaring, hitting import-dependent ASEAN economies hard.

Thailand and Indonesia, both net oil importers, pushed for contingency planning. The ministers agreed to task senior officials with studying the economic impact and reporting back before the next summit in November.

What Next for ASEAN?

The bloc is now expected to issue a more detailed communique later this week after bilateral meetings conclude. The challenge for ASEAN will be to maintain its traditional stance of non-interference while addressing the very real security and economic threats posed by the Middle East crisis.

All eyes will be on whether the ministers can agree on a unified response or whether the bloc's consensus-driven model will once again produce a watered-down statement that satisfies no one.
