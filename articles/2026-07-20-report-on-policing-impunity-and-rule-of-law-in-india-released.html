Report on Policing, Impunity, and Rule of Law in India Released

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Report by The Hindu Centre for Politics and Public Policy explores policing and impunity in India.
  • Focus on tensions between citizens and state institutions regarding rule of law.
  • Highlights accountability gaps and challenges in law enforcement.
  • Released on March 1, 2024, as part of ongoing policy research.

A new report titled ‘Citizens and the State: Policing, Impunity, and the Rule of Law in India’ has been released by The Hindu Centre for Politics and Public Policy. Published on March 1, 2024, the study examines the complex relationship between ordinary citizens and law enforcement agencies.

The report zeroes in on how policing practices intersect with issues of impunity and the broader adherence to legal norms. It draws on case studies and policy analysis to map out where the system falls short.

Core Findings on Policing and Accountability

The study highlights persistent gaps in police accountability. It notes that citizens often face hurdles when seeking redress against misconduct by law enforcement personnel.

Instances of custodial violence and delayed investigations are cited as symptoms of a deeper institutional problem. The report argues that without robust oversight, impunity becomes embedded in daily policing.

Tensions Between Citizens and the State

The document explores how these dynamics erode trust in the rule of law. It points to a cycle where lack of accountability breeds public cynicism, which in turn weakens cooperation with authorities.

Researchers emphasise that the rule of law is not merely about written statutes but about consistent enforcement. When citizens perceive the system as biased or ineffective, the social contract frays.

Recommendations for Reform

The report calls for stronger independent oversight bodies to monitor police conduct. It suggests greater transparency in investigations and disciplinary proceedings.

Community-based policing models and better training for officers are also recommended. The authors argue that legal reforms alone will not suffice without cultural change within police forces.

One key proposal is the establishment of citizen-friendly complaint mechanisms. Such systems, the report says, can help bridge the gap between the public and the state.

The findings come at a time when debates over police powers and civil liberties are intensifying across India. Multiple high-profile cases have brought the issue of impunity into public focus.

The Hindu Centre for Politics and Public Policy is known for its non-partisan research. This report is part of a series aimed at informing policymaking on governance and justice.

What happens next will depend on whether these recommendations gain traction among lawmakers and police leadership. The report is expected to be circulated among relevant stakeholders for discussion.
