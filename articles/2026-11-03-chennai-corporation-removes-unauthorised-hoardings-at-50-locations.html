Chennai Corporation removes unauthorised hoardings at 50 locations

  • The action targets structures erected without proper permits, disrupting public spaces and posing safety hazards.
  • Businesses with legitimate permits can continue operations; unauthorised advertisers face forced removal and potential penalties.

Background and Rationale

Chennai Corporation has directed the removal of unauthorised hoardings and advertisements across 50 locations within its jurisdiction. This action responds to persistent violations of municipal bye-laws governing advertising, construction, and public space usage. The installations ranged from temporary billboards to permanent structures erected without proper permits, disrupting the urban fabric of multiple wards. The Hindu reported the enforcement, noting that these structures frequently posed safety hazards, blocked pedestrian pathways, and created visual clutter inconsistent with Chennai's planned development vision.

Regulatory Framework

The enforcement operates under Section 33 of the Chennai Municipal Corporation Act and related provisions of the Tamil Nadu Municipalities and Town Panchayats Act. Advertisers require prior approval through the corporation's online portal, specifying dimensions, location, lighting, and materials. Unauthorised structures lack documentation of these approvals, making removal straightforward under existing law. The corporation has clarified that removal constitutes a corrective measure rather than a punitive action.

Practical Impact Assessment

For businesses, the action creates immediate operational considerations. Companies holding legitimate permits can maintain their advertising presence without interruption. Unauthorised advertisers face forced removal, potentially losing months of invested capital. For residents, the removal improves walkability, reduces visual noise, and enhances property values in affected areas. The corporation estimates that compliance improvements could increase foot traffic by 15-20% in cleaned zones based on comparable municipal data from other Indian cities.

  • Pros: Enhanced public aesthetics, improved pedestrian flow, stronger civic identity, reduced fire and structural hazards, better property values
  • Cons: Disruption to unauthorised advertisers, increased compliance costs, potential legal challenges from property owners, transition friction during phased removals

Compliance Roadmap for Businesses

Organisations planning advertising placements in Chennai should follow a structured sequence: first, register on the corporation's online portal; second, submit detailed application forms including site plans and material specifications; third, await approval which typically takes 7-14 days; fourth, install only upon receiving written clearance; fifth, maintain records of all correspondence for audit purposes. Non-compliance triggers removal orders, fines up to ₹5,000 per violation, and potential prosecution under criminal provisions.

Looking Ahead

The corporation intends to expand enforcement operations to additional wards in subsequent phases. Stakeholders should monitor official announcements regarding inspection schedules and new permit quotas. Organisations that engage proactively with ward-level offices secure priority processing times. Residents concerned about visual pollution should file complaints through the corporation's helpline, ensuring timely action.
