BCCC chief joins Bengaluru anti-encroachment drive

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BCCC chief participates in demolition drive against encroachments
  • Move signals tougher stance on illegal structures in Bengaluru
  • Drive targets public land, footpaths, and stormwater drains

Bengaluru's anti-encroachment campaign got a significant push on Thursday as the chief of the Bruhat Bengaluru City Corporation (BCCC) personally joined the demolition drive. The top civic official's presence on the ground marks a shift from routine administrative oversight to direct involvement in the contentious issue of illegal structures.

The drive, which has been underway for several weeks, targets encroachments on public land, footpaths, and stormwater drains across the city. These structures have long been a source of public frustration, causing traffic bottlenecks and flooding during monsoon. The BCCC chief's participation is seen as a message that the corporation is serious about reclaiming civic space.

What the drive entails

Teams equipped with earthmovers and police protection have been systematically clearing illegal constructions, ranging from temporary shanties to permanent commercial buildings. Officials said the focus is on structures that block drainage or violate building norms.

Residents in affected areas have expressed mixed reactions. Some welcomed the action, saying it would ease congestion and improve sanitation. Others, however, raised concerns about the lack of notice and the impact on small businesses that depend on these structures for livelihood.

"We have been demanding action for years," said a local resident, who did not wish to be named. "But we also want the corporation to ensure that those displaced get alternative arrangements, especially if they are poor."

Previous attempts and challenges

This is not the first time Bengaluru has attempted such a drive. Past efforts have often stalled due to legal hurdles, political pressure, or administrative apathy. However, the BCCC chief's hands-on approach suggests a determination to see this through.

Municipal officials have been directed to prepare a detailed list of all encroachments, with priority given to those on stormwater drains and major roads. The corporation has also set up a helpline for citizens to report illegal structures, though the response has been slow so far.

Public response and next steps

The drive has gained attention on social media, with many citizens posting photographs of demolished sites and tagging the corporation's official handle. Some have called for the drive to be extended to all 198 wards, while others have demanded transparency in the selection of targets.

The BCCC chief has assured that the drive will be carried out in a fair and systematic manner, with no bias towards influential individuals. "We will not spare anyone," the chief was quoted as saying in a brief interaction with reporters on site.

Officials have not yet confirmed how many structures have been demolished so far, or the total area reclaimed. A detailed report is expected to be released after the drive concludes.

The corporation is also planning to use the reclaimed land for public amenities such as parks, parking lots, and footpaths, though specific plans have not been finalised.

As the drive continues, all eyes will be on how the corporation balances firm action with due process. The coming weeks will reveal whether this is a one-off show of force or the beginning of a sustained campaign to reclaim Bengaluru's public spaces.
