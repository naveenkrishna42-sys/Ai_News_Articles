New 'beefy' bird-like dinosaur discovered in New Mexico's San Juan County

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • New dinosaur species discovered in San Juan County, New Mexico
  • Described as 'beefy' and bird-like, adding to known diversity
  • Researchers highlight evolutionary links between birds and dinosaurs

Discovery in the Badlands

Scientists in New Mexico have unveiled a newly identified dinosaur species, a 'beefy' bird-like creature whose fossils were recovered from the rugged terrain of San Juan County. The announcement, made this week, adds a fresh chapter to the region's rich prehistoric record.

The researchers, whose work was reported by Source New Mexico, described the animal as having a stout, robust build—an unusual combination for a theropod, the group that includes birds and their closest dinosaur relatives.

A Duck-Like Walk, A Dinosaur's Bones

Local news outlets, including the Roswell Daily Record, have picked up on the playful observation that 'if it walks like a duck—it's a dinosaur.' The creature's bird-like traits, particularly in its limbs and hips, suggest it moved in a manner reminiscent of modern waterfowl, though its heavy frame would have made it far more imposing.

The fossil was unearthed in the fossil-rich badlands of the San Juan Basin, a region already known for yielding significant Cretaceous-era specimens. The new species bolsters the area's reputation as a hotspot for understanding the transition from dinosaurs to birds.

What Makes It 'Beefy'?

Paleontologists have not yet released the full scientific description, but the informal tag 'beefy' hints at a body plan that was stockier and more muscular than typical bird-like dinosaurs. This suggests an animal that was likely a fast, powerful runner or an adept predator, rather than a delicate, agile flyer.

The discovery underscores a key evolutionary story: birds are not just related to dinosaurs—they are dinosaurs. The San Juan County find provides another data point in how these creatures diversified in size, shape, and behaviour before the mass extinction 66 million years ago.

Local Pride, Global Interest

For New Mexico, the announcement is a point of pride. The state has a long history of significant paleontological finds, and this new dinosaur joins a growing list of species that call the region home. The researchers have not yet stated whether the fossils will be displayed at a local museum, but interest is already high.

The scientific community will now await the formal peer-reviewed description, which is expected to provide a name for the species and detailed analysis of its anatomy. Until then, the 'beefy' bird-like dinosaur remains a tantalising glimpse into a lost world.

What's Next

The team is expected to publish their full findings in the coming months, which could reveal more about the creature's diet, habitat, and evolutionary relationships. Paleontology enthusiasts and researchers alike will be watching closely as this new piece of the dinosaur-bird puzzle falls into place.
