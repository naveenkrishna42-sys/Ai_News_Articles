Messi hits back at critics: 'Nothing was handed to us' after Argentina reach World Cup semi-finals

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Messi rejects claims Argentina benefited from favorable refereeing after reaching the semi-finals.
  • Over 10 million sign a petition calling for FIFA to kick Argentina out of the tournament.
  • Social media trends like 'VARgentina' and wild conspiracy theories fuel the controversy.

Lionel Messi has hit back at critics questioning Argentina's path to the World Cup semi-finals, insisting the defending champions have earned their place on merit. The captain's comments come as a petition demanding FIFA expel Argentina from the tournament crossed 10 million signatures, with accusations of biased refereeing dominating online chatter.

Messi's blunt message to doubters

Speaking after Argentina's quarter-final victory, Messi dismissed the growing chorus of complaints. 'Nothing was handed to us. We fought for every ball, every match, every inch,' he said. 'People see what they want to see.'

The comments were a direct response to the 'VARgentina' taunt that has trended on social media throughout the tournament. Critics point to several penalty calls and VAR decisions that went Argentina's way, including a contentious spot-kick in the previous round.

Petition crosses 10 million signatures

A Change.org petition calling on FIFA to expel Argentina from the World Cup has gathered over 10 million signatures, making it one of the largest sports-related petitions in history. Organisers cite 'systematic referee bias' and claim Argentina has received preferential treatment from match officials.

FIFA has not commented on the petition. The world body typically does not respond to online campaigns and has no mechanism to remove a team mid-tournament based on fan sentiment.

Social media goes wild with conspiracy theories

The controversy has spawned a cottage industry of internet theories. From 'VAR' to 'Vantara' — a fictional divine intervention — to chants of 'Har Har Mahadev' supposedly influencing cosmic forces, Indian social media users have offered some of the most creative explanations for Argentina's run.

One viral thread claimed Argentina's matches were being officiated by a secret FIFA committee that included Messi's childhood coach. Another suggested the ghost of Diego Maradona was manipulating VAR technology from beyond the grave. None of these theories have any basis in fact.

What the critics are actually saying

Mainstream criticism has been more measured but persistent. Several former players and pundits have questioned the consistency of VAR decisions in Argentina's matches. A widely shared analysis showed that Argentina had benefited from more VAR reviews than any other team in the knockout stages.

Opponents argue that while Messi's greatness is undisputed, the team's recent results have been shaped by moments of official generosity. Argentina's fans counter that every champion team gets tight calls in their favour, and that the outrage is driven by jealousy.

What happens next

Argentina now prepare for a semi-final clash that could silence their critics — or fuel them further. A win would put them within touching distance of back-to-back World Cup titles. For now, Messi's message is clear: judge us on the pitch, not on petitions or memes.
