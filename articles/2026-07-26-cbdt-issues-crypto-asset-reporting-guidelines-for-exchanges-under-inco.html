CBDT issues crypto asset reporting guidelines for exchanges under Income-tax Act 2025

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Crypto exchanges must collect tax residency and identification details of users under the new CBDT framework.
  • Individual investors face no additional filing burden; reporting obligations are on exchanges.
  • The guidelines sharpen oversight of cross-border crypto transactions and aim to bring clarity to the tax reporting regime.

The Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT) has issued a fresh set of guidelines on crypto asset reporting for exchanges operating in India, bringing them under the ambit of the Income-tax Act 2025. The new framework mandates that platforms collect detailed information about their users, including tax residency and identification documents.

The move is aimed at tightening compliance and ensuring that crypto transactions are traceable for tax purposes. Exchanges are now required to maintain and submit structured reports on user activity to the tax authorities.

What the new rules mean for exchanges

Under the CBDT's framework, crypto exchanges will be responsible for gathering and verifying the tax residency status of every user. This includes collecting Permanent Account Number (PAN) or equivalent foreign tax identification numbers for non-residents.

Platforms must also record wallet addresses and transaction histories. The reporting requirement covers both fiat-to-crypto and crypto-to-crypto trades. The data is to be submitted periodically in a prescribed format, though the exact frequency has not been specified in the initial notification.

Exchanges that fail to comply could face penalties under the Income-tax Act, though officials have not yet confirmed the exact quantum of fines.

No fresh burden on individual investors

For individual investors, the guidelines do not introduce any new compliance obligations. The reporting responsibility rests solely with the exchanges. Taxpayers are not required to file any additional forms or declarations regarding their crypto holdings as a result of this directive.

The CBDT has clarified that the framework is designed to streamline information flow rather than impose extra paperwork on retail participants. Existing tax rules on crypto gains — taxed at 30% with no deduction for losses — remain unchanged.

Cross-border oversight sharpened

One of the key features of the new guidelines is the enhanced scrutiny of cross-border crypto transactions. Exchanges dealing with foreign users or routing transactions through overseas wallets must report these separately.

The CBDT is looking to track capital flows that move through digital assets, especially where tax residency mismatches could occur. This is part of a broader push by Indian regulators to align with global standards set by the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) on virtual asset service providers.

Industry observers say the guidelines bring much-needed clarity to a space that has operated in a grey area for years. However, some compliance experts have pointed out that the operational burden on smaller exchanges could be significant.

What happens next

Exchanges have been given a transition period to update their systems and begin collecting the required data. The CBDT is expected to issue further clarifications on the reporting format and deadlines in the coming weeks. Market participants are watching closely for any additional rules on taxation of crypto derivatives or staking income, which remain outside the scope of this notification.
