Velachery's Pothole Menace: A Persistent Infrastructure Failure

Velachery, a bustling residential and commercial hub in South Chennai, is grappling with a severe pothole problem that has turned its arterial roads into obstacle courses. Despite being one of the city's fastest-growing neighbourhoods, the area's infrastructure has failed to keep pace. The New Indian Express reports that the Chennai Corporation has promised to begin repair work soon, but for the thousands of daily commuters, that promise rings hollow after months of neglect.

The Ground Reality: Roads That Defy Description

Key stretches such as the 100 Feet Road, Velachery Main Road, and the link roads connecting to Taramani and Guindy are peppered with craters, some deep enough to swallow a two-wheeler's wheel. During the recent northeast monsoon, these potholes became water-filled traps, leading to frequent breakdowns and minor accidents. Residents report that even ambulances and fire engines have been delayed during emergencies.

Corporation's Response: 'Work Will Begin Soon'

Officials from the Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC) have stated that tenders for re-laying several stretches have been floated and work will commence shortly. However, no specific timeline or budget allocation has been made public. This lack of transparency has fuelled scepticism among locals who have heard similar promises in previous years. In 2025, a similar announcement was made, but only patchwork repairs were carried out, which washed away within weeks.

Buyer Implications: What This Means for Residents and Investors

For homebuyers and investors, Velachery's pothole problem is more than an inconvenience; it's a red flag for infrastructure reliability. Property prices in the area have seen a steady rise over the past decade, with apartments commanding ₹8,000–12,000 per sq ft. However, persistent road issues can dent the area's appeal, especially for families with school-going children and professionals commuting to the IT corridor.

Potential buyers should factor in additional vehicle maintenance costs—suspension damage, tyre replacements, and alignment issues—which can set back a car owner by ₹15,000–20,000 annually. Two-wheeler riders face even higher risks, with increased chances of skidding and injury.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Commuter Woes: Daily travellers spend an extra 30–45 minutes on the road, burning more fuel and losing productive hours.
  • Safety Risks: Potholes have led to a spike in two-wheeler accidents, especially at night when visibility is low.
  • Economic Impact: Local businesses report reduced footfall as customers avoid the bumpy rides.
  • Property Market: While Velachery remains desirable, unresolved infrastructure issues could slow price appreciation in the short term.

What Needs to Change: Expert Perspective

Urban planners argue that piecemeal repairs are not enough. A durable solution requires proper drainage, quality materials, and timely execution. The GCC's proposed use of mastic asphalt and polymer-modified bitumen could extend road life, but only if applied correctly and maintained regularly.

Citizens can play a role by reporting potholes through the GCC's mobile app and following up on complaints. Community groups in Velachery have already started documenting the worst stretches and submitting them to the corporation.

The Road Ahead
