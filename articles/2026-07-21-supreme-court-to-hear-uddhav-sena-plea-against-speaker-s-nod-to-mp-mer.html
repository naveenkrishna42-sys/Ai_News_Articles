Supreme Court to hear Uddhav Sena plea against Speaker's nod to MP merger

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shiv Sena (UBT) moved Supreme Court against Lok Sabha Speaker's approval of merger of six of its MPs with the Shinde faction
  • The petition challenges Speaker Om Birla's decision on what the Uddhav Thackeray camp calls a 'unilateral merger'
  • Supreme Court agreed to consider the plea, with the matter listed for hearing

The political battle between the two Shiv Sena factions has reached the Supreme Court again. The Uddhav Thackeray-led Shiv Sena (UBT) has filed a plea challenging Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla's decision to approve the merger of six of its MPs with the Eknath Shinde faction.

The petition was mentioned before the court on Tuesday, pressing for an urgent hearing. The Supreme Court has agreed to consider the matter, marking the latest legal twist in a dispute that has reshaped Maharashtra's political landscape over the past year.

Speaker's decision under scrutiny

Speaker Birla's ruling cleared the way for six Shiv Sena MPs to merge with the Shinde-led faction. The Uddhav camp has called this a 'unilateral merger' that bypassed due process. The party argues that the Speaker's approval violates parliamentary norms and the anti-defection law.

The Thackeray faction contends that the merger was not a decision taken by the party's authorised leadership. In their petition, they have asked the court to examine whether the Speaker's discretion was exercised correctly under the Tenth Schedule of the Constitution.

Legal history of the Sena split

The Shiv Sena split formally began in June 2022 when Eknath Shinde led a rebellion against Uddhav Thackeray, taking a majority of MLAs with him. The Supreme Court later upheld the Maharashtra Governor's decision to call for a trust vote, which led to Thackeray's resignation as Chief Minister.

Since then, both factions have been locked in legal battles over party symbols, legislative membership, and control of the party organisation. The Election Commission recognised the Shinde faction as the real Shiv Sena in February 2023, awarding it the 'bow and arrow' symbol.

What the Uddhav camp is arguing

The Uddhav Thackeray faction's petition challenges the very basis of the Speaker's approval. They argue that a merger under the anti-defection law requires at least two-thirds of the party's legislators to agree. The faction claims that the six MPs who moved to the Shinde camp did not represent the party's legislative strength.

The petition also flags procedural irregularities. It states that the Speaker did not give the Uddhav faction a chance to present its objections before passing the order. The Thackeray camp has asked the court to set aside the merger approval and restore the MPs' original party affiliation.

This is not the first time the Sena feud has reached the top court. In earlier hearings, the Supreme Court had declined to stay the Election Commission's decision on the party symbol but had ordered status quo on other aspects of the dispute.

Political implications

The merger has direct consequences for the arithmetic in the Lok Sabha. The Shinde faction now has a stronger claim to the Shiv Sena's seats in Parliament. This matters for the ruling NDA coalition, of which the Shinde-led Sena is a part.

Analysts point out that the Speaker's decision, if upheld, could set a precedent for how future intra-party mergers are handled in the Lok Sabha. It also comes as Speaker Birla is set to rule on a similar matter involving rebel TMC MPs merging with the NCP, adding another layer of political sensitivity.

The Supreme Court has not yet announced a date for the hearing. The Uddhav faction is expected to push for an early listing, given the political stakes and the proximity of upcoming sessions of Parliament.

For now, both sides are watching the court's next move closely. The decision could either consolidate the Shinde faction's position or force a fresh round of legal and political uncertainty for the Shiv Sena.
