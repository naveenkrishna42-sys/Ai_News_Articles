Assam floods: Death toll hits 68, over 5 lakh displaced as waters recede

📅 2026-07-28 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 68 people have died in Assam's worst flooding in years, with over 5 lakh people affected across the state.
  • Congress chief has attacked Amit Shah over the BJP's 'flood-free Assam' promise, as relief operations continue.
  • Actor Adil Hussain revealed his nieces are stranded in Sivasagar, expressing frustration and anger over the situation.
  • Floodwaters are beginning to recede in some areas, but many districts remain submerged and relief efforts are ongoing.

The floodwaters that have ravaged Assam for weeks are finally beginning to recede. But for the millions who have lost homes, livestock and livelihoods, the damage is done.

At least 68 people have died in what officials are calling the state's worst flooding in years. Over 5 lakh people remain affected across 26 districts, with relief camps still housing tens of thousands.

Congress targets BJP over broken promise

The political blame game has intensified. The Congress chief directly attacked Union Home Minister Amit Shah on Monday, reminding him of his 2021 campaign pledge to make Assam 'flood-free' within five years.

“Where is the flood-free Assam now?” the Congress leader asked at a press conference in Guwahati. He accused the BJP government of failing on its core promise while millions suffer.

BJP leaders have defended their record, pointing to ongoing relief operations and a central assistance package. But with water levels still high in several districts, the criticism is gaining traction.

Human toll: 'It took everything from us'

In the worst-hit areas, entire villages have been submerged for weeks. Farmers have watched their paddy fields turn into lakes. Families have lost stored grain, cattle and in many cases, their homes.

“It took everything from us,” one survivor from Dhemaji district told local reporters. His family now lives in a government relief camp with little more than the clothes they were wearing.

Bollywood actor Adil Hussain, who hails from Assam, spoke publicly about his own family's ordeal. He revealed that his nieces remain stranded in Sivasagar district, their homes completely under water.

“I feel terribly frustrated, angry, sad and depressed,” Hussain told The Times of India. His statement has drawn attention to the slow pace of rescue efforts in remote areas.

Rescue and relief operations under strain

The National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and state agencies have deployed boats and helicopters for rescue missions. But the scale of the disaster has stretched resources thin.

Officials say over 1,500 relief camps are now operational. However, reports from several districts indicate shortages of drinking water, food and medicine.

The situation has been compounded by breaches in embankments along the Brahmaputra and its tributaries. Many roads remain cut off, making it difficult to reach isolated communities.

What happens next

With the monsoon still active, the risk of fresh flooding remains. The India Meteorological Department has forecast more heavy rain in the region over the next 48 hours.

Authorities have warned that the death toll may rise as floodwaters recede and more bodies are recovered. The focus now is on preventing waterborne diseases in relief camps and restoring road connectivity to cut-off areas.
