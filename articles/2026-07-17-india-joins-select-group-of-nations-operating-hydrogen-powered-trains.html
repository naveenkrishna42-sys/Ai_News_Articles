India joins select group of nations operating hydrogen-powered trains

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's first hydrogen-powered train flagged off by PM Modi in Haryana
  • PM Modi called it a Make in India success and highlighted development despite oil supply crisis
  • India joins a select group of nations operating hydrogen-powered trains
  • Foundation stone laid and projects worth Rs 14,700 crore dedicated to the nation in Jind

India has joined a select group of nations now operating hydrogen-powered trains. Prime Minister Narendra Modi flagged off the country's first such train in Haryana on Friday, calling it a testament to the Make in India initiative.

First hydrogen train flagged off in Haryana

The Prime Minister flagged off the train at an event in Jind, Haryana. He also laid the foundation stone and dedicated to the nation development projects worth Rs 14,700 crore in the region.

The launch positions India alongside countries like Germany, France, and China that have already introduced hydrogen-powered trains. These trains emit only water vapour, making them a zero-emission alternative to diesel locomotives.

PM Modi highlights Make in India success

Speaking at the event, PM Modi said the hydrogen train is a shining example of the Make in India programme. He noted that the country's development did not come to a standstill despite the ongoing global oil supply crisis.

"India's development did not come to a standstill despite oil supply crisis," PM Modi said, according to sources present at the event. He added that the launch of the hydrogen train shows the government's commitment to green energy and self-reliance.

Why India's first hydrogen train is special

The hydrogen-powered train runs on fuel cells that convert hydrogen into electricity. The only byproduct is water vapour, which means the train produces no harmful emissions. This makes it a key part of India's push to decarbonise its railways.

Indian Railways operates one of the largest rail networks in the world. The introduction of hydrogen-powered trains is expected to significantly cut down on diesel consumption and reduce the carbon footprint of the railways.

The train was developed under the Make in India initiative, with Indian engineers and manufacturers playing a key role in its design and production. This is a departure from earlier times when India relied heavily on imported technology for such projects.

Development projects worth Rs 14,700 crore

Along with the train launch, PM Modi dedicated several development projects to the nation. These include new railway lines, road projects, and other infrastructure initiatives worth Rs 14,700 crore.

The projects are expected to boost connectivity and economic growth in Haryana and surrounding regions. The foundation stone for these projects was also laid at the same event in Jind.

What happens next

The hydrogen train will undergo trial runs on select routes before being introduced into regular service. Indian Railways plans to expand the fleet of hydrogen-powered trains in the coming years, with several more routes identified for deployment. The success of this pilot will determine the speed at which hydrogen technology is adopted across the network.
