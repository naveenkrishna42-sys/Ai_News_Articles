WhatsApp rolls out web calls, waiting rooms and call transfer features

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Users can now make and receive voice and video calls directly from the WhatsApp web and desktop apps.
  • A new call transfer feature allows users to switch an active call between their phone and computer without dropping it.
  • A waiting room feature lets group call hosts control who joins, adding a layer of privacy and moderation.

Browser calling goes live

WhatsApp has officially launched a browser-based calling feature, allowing users to make and receive voice and video calls directly from the web and desktop versions of the app. The move eliminates the need to keep a phone nearby for calls, syncing conversations across devices.

The feature is rolling out globally over the coming weeks. Users will see a call tab in the web interface, mirroring the mobile experience. The company confirmed that calls will be end-to-end encrypted, maintaining the same security standard as the mobile app.

Call transfer between devices

Alongside browser calling, WhatsApp introduced a call transfer feature. Users can switch an active call from their phone to their computer or vice versa with a single tap. The transition is seamless — the call does not drop or lose audio quality.

The feature addresses a common pain point for users who start a call on mobile but want to move to a larger screen or a headset connected to a laptop. It works for both voice and video calls. WhatsApp has not specified whether the feature will support transfers between other paired devices, such as tablets, in future updates.

Waiting rooms for group calls

WhatsApp also added a waiting room feature for group calls. Hosts can now see who is trying to join and admit participants individually. This gives call organisers more control, especially in large or public groups where unknown numbers might try to connect.

The waiting room acts as a virtual lobby. Participants see a screen indicating they are waiting to be let in. The host receives a notification and can admit or deny entry. The feature is optional — hosts can turn it off for casual calls.

Kunal Shah, founder of Cred, was spotted testing the browser calling feature with Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg. The test call, shared on social media, demonstrated the new functionality. It is unclear if this public test was part of a broader beta programme or a one-off demonstration.

Why this matters

WhatsApp has been gradually building out its desktop and web capabilities. The addition of direct calling removes a key limitation — users previously had to rely on third-party workarounds or switch to other platforms for computer-based calls. The new features bring WhatsApp closer to competitors like Zoom and Google Meet, which already offer browser-based calling with advanced controls.

The waiting room feature, in particular, positions WhatsApp as a more serious tool for professional and semi-formal group conversations. It could appeal to small businesses, educators, and community organisers who use WhatsApp for coordination.

All three features are rolling out to users on the latest version of WhatsApp. The company is expected to extend the waiting room functionality to one-on-one calls in a future update. No timeline has been given for that expansion.
