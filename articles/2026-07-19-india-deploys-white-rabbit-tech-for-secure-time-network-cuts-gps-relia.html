India deploys White Rabbit tech for secure time network, cuts GPS reliance

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Union Minister Pralhad Joshi commissioned the White Rabbit Technology-based Indian Standard Time Dissemination Demonstration Network in Bengaluru.
  • The network aims to secure time synchronisation for critical sectors like telecom, power grids, and financial markets.
  • It reduces dependence on GPS-based timing, which is vulnerable to jamming, spoofing, and geopolitical disruption.

New network aims for 'One Nation, One Time'

India has taken a significant step toward securing its official timekeeping. Union Minister for Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution, Pralhad Joshi, commissioned the White Rabbit Technology-based Indian Standard Time (IST) Dissemination Demonstration Network in Bengaluru. The move is part of the government's 'One Nation, One Time' initiative.

The network uses White Rabbit technology, an open-source, deterministic Ethernet-based protocol originally developed at CERN. It can synchronise clocks with sub-nanosecond accuracy over long distances. This is far more precise than standard Network Time Protocol (NTP) servers.

Why India needs its own secure timing backbone

Most critical infrastructure in India currently relies on the Global Positioning System (GPS) for time synchronisation. This creates a vulnerability. GPS signals are weak and can be jammed or spoofed by hostile actors. A disruption to GPS timing could cripple telecom networks, power grids, stock exchanges, and data centres.

The new network provides a terrestrial alternative. By distributing IST via fibre optic cables, it remains immune to satellite signal interference. The demonstration network in Bengaluru will test the feasibility of scaling this technology across the country. Officials have stated that a nationwide rollout could eventually protect all critical infrastructure from time-based cyberattacks.

What White Rabbit technology brings

White Rabbit achieves its accuracy by combining Synchronous Ethernet (SyncE) and Precision Time Protocol (PTP). It compensates for latency and jitter in fibre networks. This makes it suitable for industries requiring microsecond-level precision, such as 5G telecommunications and high-frequency trading.

The network is also designed to be interoperable with existing systems. It does not require a complete overhaul of current infrastructure. This lowers the cost of adoption for public and private entities.

Impact on key sectors

For telecom operators, accurate time synchronisation is essential for 5G network handovers and beamforming. The Reserve Bank of India and stock exchanges require precise timestamps for audit trails and transaction settlement. Power grid operators need synchronised phasor measurement units to prevent cascading failures.

By securing IST distribution, the government aims to reduce systemic risk. A single trusted time source eliminates discrepancies between multiple unverified clocks. This also aids law enforcement and forensic investigations where timestamps are evidence.

Reducing foreign dependency

GPS is owned and operated by the United States Department of Defense. While it is freely available, its signals can be degraded or denied during conflicts. India's own satellite navigation system, NavIC, provides positioning but its timing services are still being integrated. The White Rabbit network offers an independent, sovereign time distribution method.

The Council of Scientific & Industrial Research (CSIR) and the National Physical Laboratory (NPL) are the technical partners behind the demonstration. They will collect performance data over the next several months before recommending a full-scale deployment.

What to watch now: The government is expected to release a white paper on the pilot results in six months, which will determine the timeline for expanding the network to major financial and industrial hubs across India.
