The End of an Unbroken 39-Year Streak

For the first time since 1987, no Mohanlal-starrer features among Kerala's top three highest-grossing films of the year. The 2026 chart is led by Bethlehem Kudumba Unit, followed by Lokah and I'm Game, leaving the superstar's releases—despite strong openings—outside the podium. This is not a verdict on Mohanlal's drawing power but a measurable shift in how Malayalam cinema monetises across Kerala, India, and overseas.

Since 1987, at least one Mohanlal film had ranked in the top three every single year, spanning theatrical, satellite, and digital revenue. That streak has now ended, marking a structural change in the industry's commercial hierarchy.

Bethlehem Kudumba Unit crossed Rs 310 crore within 27 days and continued to add screens, cementing its position as the biggest Malayalam hit. Its success, combined with Lokah and I'm Game, pushed Mohanlal's 2026 releases to fourth place or lower.

Why This Shift Matters for Buyers and Exhibitors

For theatrical buyers, satellite rights purchasers, and OTT licensing teams, the 2026 results signal that star-led pricing no longer guarantees top-tier returns. Bethlehem Kudumba Unit was made on a mid-range budget yet delivered the highest gross, proving that content-first projects can outperform legacy star vehicles. Exhibitors who allocated prime screens to Mohanlal's films saw lower footfalls compared to the top three, which consistently ran at higher occupancy.

Acquisition executives must now reassess minimum guarantees (MGs) for star-driven projects. The data suggests that a Mohanlal film may still open big, but its lifetime Kerala gross is no longer automatically among the top three. Buyers who overpay based on historical precedence risk thinner margins.

Budget vs Return: The New Economics of Malayalam Cinema

Bethlehem Kudumba Unit's return on investment is exceptional: a reported budget under Rs 50 crore against a Rs 312 crore gross. In contrast, typical Mohanlal big-budget films command Rs 80–100 crore production costs, requiring far higher breakeven points. The table below compares approximate economics.

Pros and Cons: What the 2026 Chart Reveals

  • Pros: Lower-budget, story-driven films deliver higher ROI and reduce buyer risk. New IPs like Bethlehem Kudumba Unit attract younger audiences and stronger OTT bids. Exhibitors gain flexibility in screen allocation.
  • Cons: Star-led films still guarantee opening-weekend footfalls, which helps single-screen theatres. Over-reliance on a single superstar's legacy can blind buyers to shifting audience tastes. Mohanlal's brand remains valuable, but pricing must adjust to current performance.

Outlook: Star Power Is Not Dead, But Pricing Must Change

Mohanlal remains a box-office force, but the 2026 Kerala top three proves that no star is entitled to a podium finish. Buyers should adopt a data-driven approach: track first-weekend trends, overseas shares, and satellite deals before committing MGs. The rise of Bethlehem Kudumba Unit and Lokah shows that audiences reward originality. For the first time since 1987, Kerala's top three are not about Mohanlal—and that is a market signal no buyer can ignore.
