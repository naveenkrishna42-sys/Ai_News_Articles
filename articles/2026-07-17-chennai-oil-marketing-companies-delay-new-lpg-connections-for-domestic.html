Chennai: Oil Marketing Companies Delay New LPG Connections for Domestic Users

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil marketing companies in Chennai have not released new LPG connections for domestic consumers.
  • The delay is causing inconvenience to households that have applied for new connections.
  • Officials have not provided a specific timeline for when the backlog will be cleared.

New LPG Connections Stalled in Chennai

Oil marketing companies (OMCs) in Chennai have not issued new liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) connections to domestic consumers, leaving many households in limbo. The development was reported by The Hindu on Thursday, highlighting a growing backlog of applications.

The delay affects families who have applied for new connections, often as a shift from traditional fuels like firewood or kerosene. Consumers say they have been waiting for weeks, with no clear timeline from the companies involved.

What Consumers Are Facing

Residents in several parts of Chennai have expressed frustration. Many have submitted applications and paid the required fees, only to be told that connections are not being processed at the moment. "We applied over a month ago, but there is no update. We are stuck with cylinders from the black market, which are expensive," said a resident of T. Nagar, who did not wish to be named.

The issue is particularly acute in areas where piped natural gas is not available. For these households, LPG remains the primary cooking fuel. The lack of new connections forces some to rely on informal suppliers, raising safety and cost concerns.

OMCs Yet to Comment

Officials from the three main OMCs—Indian Oil Corporation, Bharat Petroleum Corporation, and Hindustan Petroleum Corporation—have not issued a statement explaining the delay. Sources within the industry suggest that the hold-up could be due to administrative bottlenecks or supply chain issues, but nothing has been confirmed.

The companies have not provided a timeline for when they will resume issuing new connections. This lack of communication has added to the uncertainty for consumers.

Impact on Domestic Consumers

For many Chennai households, LPG is not just a convenience but a necessity. The delay in new connections affects families that may have recently moved or are setting up new homes. It also impacts those trying to switch from subsidised kerosene to cleaner LPG as part of government initiatives.

Consumer rights groups have urged the OMCs to expedite the process. "The government has been promoting LPG as a clean fuel, but if companies are not issuing new connections, it defeats the purpose. This needs immediate attention," said a representative from a local consumer forum.

The situation also raises questions about the efficiency of the distribution network in Chennai. With the city growing, demand for new LPG connections is expected to rise, making the current bottleneck a matter of concern.

What Happens Next

As of now, no official announcement has been made about when new LPG connections will be issued. Consumers are advised to check with their local distributors for updates. The OMCs may need to address the backlog soon to avoid further inconvenience.

All eyes are on the companies to provide clarity on the issue. Without a resolution, households in Chennai will continue to face difficulties in accessing a basic cooking fuel.
