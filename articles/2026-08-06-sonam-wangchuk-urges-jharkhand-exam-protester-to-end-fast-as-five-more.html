Sonam Wangchuk urges Jharkhand exam protester to end fast as five more join stir

📅 2026-08-06 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sonam Wangchuk urged a Jharkhand exam protester to end hunger strike
  • Five more aspirants joined the fast in Ranchi on Wednesday
  • Jharkhand CM Hemant Soren has opened doors for talks with protesters

Ranchi, August 5: Climate activist Sonam Wangchuk has appealed to a young job aspirant in Jharkhand to call off her hunger strike, even as five more protesters joined the fast on Wednesday. The stir, now in its second week, centres on alleged irregularities in the Jharkhand Public Service Commission (JPSC) and Jharkhand Staff Selection Commission (JSSC) examinations.

The protester at the centre of the appeal, a woman whose identity has not been officially disclosed, has been fasting at the protest site in Ranchi. Her resolve, she said, stems from a promise to her daughter. "My daughter says 'mummy, let's go', but I can't leave without justice," she was quoted as saying in local media reports.

Wangchuk's appeal

Wangchuk, known for his climate activism and educational reforms in Ladakh, reached out to the protester through a video message. He urged her to prioritise her health and life, saying the fight for justice should not come at the cost of a young mother's wellbeing. His appeal, however, has not yet changed her decision.

The activist's intervention adds a new layer to a protest that has already drawn attention from across the country. Five more aspirants joined the hunger strike on Wednesday, taking the total number of fasting protesters to at least a dozen, according to organisers.

Protest gathers momentum

The agitation has brought together job aspirants from diverse backgrounds — from Devendra Nath Mahto to Abhijeet Dipke — who are now being described as the new faces of India's youth protests. Their demands include a re-examination of the JPSC and JSSC papers, a probe into alleged question paper leaks, and action against officials found guilty of malpractice.

The protest site in Ranchi has become a meeting point for aspirants, many of whom have travelled from distant districts. They sit in groups, some holding placards, others discussing the next course of action. The mood is tense but determined.

Government reaches out

In a significant development, Jharkhand Chief Minister Hemant Soren has opened doors for talks with the protesters. According to official sources, the government has invited representatives of the agitators for a dialogue, though no date has been fixed yet. Soren's move is being seen as an attempt to defuse the situation before it escalates further.

The state government has maintained that it is committed to ensuring a fair recruitment process. However, protesters remain sceptical, pointing to past instances where assurances did not lead to concrete action.

What lies ahead

As the hunger strike enters its critical phase, all eyes are on the proposed talks between the protesters and the state government. The onus is on the administration to offer a credible roadmap — one that addresses the aspirants' concerns without undermining the integrity of the examination system. For now, the women and men fasting in Ranchi have made it clear: they will not move until justice is served.
