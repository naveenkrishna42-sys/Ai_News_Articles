India garners $17.4 billion via FCNR(B) deposits under RBI swap window

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • India has received $17.4 billion in FCNR(B) deposits under the RBI's special swap window.
  • The initiative is part of efforts to bolster foreign exchange reserves and improve the balance of payments.
  • Banks are offering high returns to NRIs, but experts warn of leverage risks.
  • The finance ministry has assigned deposit mobilisation targets to public sector banks.

RBI swap window attracts $17.4 billion from NRIs

India has pulled in $17.4 billion through Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) or FCNR(B) deposits under a special swap facility opened by the Reserve Bank of India. The window, designed to attract dollar inflows, has seen strong participation from non-resident Indians.

The central bank's move is aimed at fortifying the country's foreign exchange reserves. Officials believe the inflows will help stabilise the rupee and improve the overall balance of payments position.

Banks lure NRIs with higher returns

Public and private sector banks have been aggressively marketing FCNR(B) deposits to NRIs, offering interest rates that are significantly higher than those available in other global markets. The RBI's swap window allows banks to convert these dollar deposits into rupees at favourable rates, reducing their own currency risk.

However, some analysts have flagged risks. A report in The Economic Times quoted experts warning that the high rates offered could lead to leverage issues if the rupee depreciates sharply. Banks may face margin pressure if they have to hedge these positions at higher costs later.

Finance ministry sets targets for PSBs

The finance ministry has stepped in to accelerate the process. According to Business Standard, the ministry has assigned specific FCNR(B) deposit mobilisation targets to public sector banks. This directive aims to ensure that the banking system collectively meets the government's forex inflow goals.

The targets are part of a broader strategy to pre-empt any potential external sector stress. India's foreign exchange reserves have been under pressure in recent months due to global monetary tightening and capital outflows.

Swap window strengthens balance of payments

The $17.4 billion figure, reported by Moneycontrol, marks a strong start for the RBI's latest push. The Telegraph India reported that the total inflows could cross $20 billion as more deposits are booked. The swap mechanism allows banks to swap their FCNR(B) deposits with the RBI at a fixed rate, effectively locking in the cost of funds.

This policy is reminiscent of a similar window opened in 2013, which had successfully attracted over $26 billion and helped stabilise the rupee during the taper tantrum. The current effort is seen as a preemptive measure to build a buffer against global volatility.

Risks remain despite early success

While the early response has been encouraging, experts caution that the strategy is not without downsides. The high interest costs on these deposits could eat into bank margins. Additionally, if the rupee remains under pressure, the cost of hedging for banks could rise, potentially reducing the net benefit of the inflows.

Bankers, however, remain optimistic. They point out that the RBI's swap window provides a clear exit route, reducing the risk of sudden outflows. The deposits typically have a tenure of three to five years, giving the central bank time to manage the country's forex position.

Moving forward, the success of this initiative will depend on global interest rate trends and the stability of the rupee. The RBI and the finance ministry are likely to monitor the inflows closely and may extend the swap window if needed.
