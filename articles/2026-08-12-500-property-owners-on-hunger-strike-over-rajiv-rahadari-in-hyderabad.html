500 property owners on hunger strike over Rajiv Rahadari in Hyderabad

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • More than 500 property owners are on hunger strike in Hyderabad over Rajiv Rahadari land compensation.
  • The protest is against alleged inadequate compensation for land acquired for the project.
  • Officials have not yet responded to the protesters' demands.

Hyderabad: Over 500 property owners have begun an indefinite hunger strike here, protesting against the compensation offered for land acquired for the Rajiv Rahadari project. The protesters, mostly farmers and small landowners, have gathered at a makeshift camp near the project site, demanding a higher payout and better rehabilitation terms.

The Rajiv Rahadari, a major infrastructure initiative aimed at decongesting the city's traffic, has required the acquisition of substantial land parcels across several suburban areas. While the project is expected to bring long-term benefits, the affected owners argue that the current compensation package does not reflect the market value of their properties.

Protesters' demands

According to protest organisers, the landowners are not against the project itself but are seeking a fair deal. They claim that the government's valuation, based on old ready reckoner rates, falls far short of current real estate prices, particularly in areas that have seen rapid development in recent years.

"We are not opposing development," said a spokesperson for the agitating owners, speaking on condition of anonymity. "But we cannot accept a compensation that forces us to take a huge loss on our ancestral land. We want the government to revise the rates and include a one-time settlement for loss of livelihood."

The protesters have also demanded that the authorities provide alternative plots for those who have lost their primary residences, and ensure timely payment of the enhanced compensation once agreed upon.

Government's position

Officials from the Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority (HMDA), which is overseeing the Rajiv Rahadari project, have not yet issued a formal response to the strike. However, sources indicate that the government is open to dialogue and has constituted a committee to look into the grievances of the affected owners.

Earlier this year, the state government had announced a revised compensation policy for infrastructure projects, which included a higher multiplier for land value. But the protesters say the policy has not been applied uniformly, and that several owners have not received the promised amounts.

In a related development, some owners have moved the Telangana High Court seeking intervention. The court has reportedly asked the government to file a status report on the compensation process, though no final orders have been passed yet.

Impact on project timeline

The hunger strike, now in its fourth day, has slowed down work on certain stretches of the Rahadari where the agitation is centred. Construction has been halted in at least two segments as a precautionary measure, according to project engineers.

Officials, however, maintain that the overall timeline of the project will not be affected, as only a small percentage of the total land remains under dispute. They estimate that over 80% of the required land has already been acquired and handed over to the contractors.

The Rajiv Rahadari is a flagship project of the state government, envisioned to provide a seamless corridor connecting the eastern and western parts of the city, with underpasses and flyovers at key junctions. Once completed, it is expected to reduce travel time significantly and boost economic activity along the corridor.

Local political leaders have also weighed in, with some opposition parties extending support to the agitators, while others have urged the government to resolve the issue quickly to avoid any law-and-order problem.

The situation remains tense, with the protesters vowing to continue their fast until their demands are met. Medical teams have been stationed at the site to monitor the health of the participants, several of whom are elderly.

As the standoff continues, all eyes are on the state government's next move. A delegation of protesters is expected to meet with senior officials later this week, and a breakthrough, if any, would likely come after that. The coming days will show whether the government chooses to revise its compensation package or stick to its earlier stance, and how soon the project can return to full steam.
