Sonam Wangchuk breaks 26-day fast, protest leader vows to continue agitation

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sonam Wangchuk broke his 26-day long fast at Jantar Mantar in the presence of BJP leaders JP Nadda and Jitendra Singh.
  • CJP founder Dipke expressed relief but vowed to continue the protest until Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigns.
  • The agitation over education policy and regional issues remains unresolved despite the fast ending.
  • Government officials have not yet commented on the resignation demand or next steps.

Context: A 26-Day Fast Comes to an End

Sonam Wangchuk, the well-known education reform activist and engineer, broke his 26-day-long hunger strike at Delhi's Jantar Mantar on Thursday. The protest, which began on June 28, had drawn attention to demands for changes in the National Education Policy and issues specific to Ladakh and other Himalayan regions.

Wangchuk ended his fast in the presence of senior BJP leaders JP Nadda and Jitendra Singh. The event was marked by a palpable sense of relief among supporters, but the underlying political tension remains far from resolved.

Background: Why the Protest Began

Wangchuk's fast was the culmination of months of campaigning by the Citizens for Justice and Peace (CJP), an organisation founded by activist Dipke. The agitation initially focused on the implementation of the National Education Policy 2020 in Ladakh and other northern regions, but quickly escalated to include demands for the resignation of Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan.

Protesters allege that the education policy sidelines regional languages and fails to address the unique needs of Himalayan communities. The demand for Pradhan's resignation stems from accusations of mishandling the policy rollout and ignoring repeated appeals from local stakeholders.

Why It Matters: Symbolism Versus Substance

The breaking of Wangchuk's fast is a significant moment, but it does not signal an end to the agitation. CJP founder Dipke made this clear in his statement soon after the event. He expressed relief that Wangchuk's health was no longer at immediate risk, but was unequivocal that the protest would continue until Pradhan steps down.

This distinction matters because it underscores a central dynamic of the protest movement: the personal sacrifice of a prominent figure like Wangchuk provides moral weight, but the organisational machinery of the CJP is determined to sustain pressure on the government. The fast may have been a dramatic tactic, but the underlying political demands remain unchanged.

Government Response Remains Unclear

The presence of Nadda and Singh at the fast-breaking ceremony suggests that the BJP leadership is keen to manage the optics of the situation. However, neither leader made any public commitment regarding the protesters' core demands.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether any formal dialogue is underway with the CJP or whether the government plans to address the resignation demand. The education ministry has so far maintained a studied silence on the matter.

What to Watch: The Next Phase of the Agitation

With Wangchuk's fast over, the immediate question is how the CJP will sustain momentum. Dipke has indicated that the protest at Jantar Mantar will continue indefinitely, with a series of planned events and public outreach programmes.

The coming days will test whether the movement can retain public attention without the dramatic element of a hunger strike. It will also reveal whether the government is willing to engage in substantive negotiations or intends to wait out the agitation.

The demand for Pradhan's resignation remains a red line for the protesters. If the government refuses to budge, the CJP may escalate its tactics. If a compromise is reached, it could reshape the political dynamics of education policy in India's Himalayan belt.

For now, the situation at Jantar Mantar remains fluid. The fast may be over, but the protest is very much alive.
