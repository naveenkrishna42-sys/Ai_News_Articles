9 IPOs worth Rs 7,700 crore to hit Dalal Street next week

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nine IPOs worth Rs 7,700 crore are set to open next week on Dalal Street.
  • Shiprocket and Milky Mist are among the prominent issuers in the pipeline.
  • Eight new listings are also scheduled for the coming week.
  • The primary market remains active despite broader market volatility.

Mumbai: The primary market is bracing for a busy week as nine companies prepare to launch their initial public offerings (IPOs), collectively aiming to raise around Rs 7,700 crore. The pipeline includes marquee names such as logistics firm Shiprocket and dairy major Milky Mist, alongside several other issuers across sectors.

Investment bankers said the coming days will test investor appetite for new-age businesses and traditional consumer brands alike. The total mobilisation, pegged at roughly Rs 7,681 crore, underscores sustained interest in equity capital markets despite choppy secondary market conditions.

Shiprocket and Milky Mist lead the charge

Shiprocket, a logistics technology platform backed by prominent venture capital funds, is among the most anticipated offerings. The company provides shipping solutions to e-commerce sellers and has seen rapid growth in recent years. Its IPO is expected to draw strong demand from institutional investors, given the expanding online retail ecosystem in India.

Milky Mist, the Tamil Nadu-based dairy and food products company, is also set to tap the market. The firm is known for its paneer, curd, and cheese products, and has built a strong regional brand presence. The offering will provide an exit opportunity for early private equity investors and raise capital for expansion.

Other issuers and sector spread

The remaining issuers include Dhoot Transmission, which makes cables and wiring harnesses for the automotive and consumer durables sectors. The company's IPO was earlier reported to be part of the Rs 7,400 crore-plus mobilisation for the week. Other names in the pipeline span sectors such as specialty chemicals, engineering components, and financial services.

Merchant bankers said the mix reflects broad-based interest from promoters to go public, aided by a favourable regulatory framework and robust domestic liquidity. The sheer number of issues in a single week is rare, and market participants will be watching how retail and institutional segments absorb the supply.

Eight listings on the cards

In addition to the fresh issues, eight companies are scheduled to make their trading debut on the stock exchanges next week. These listings include firms that completed their share sales in recent weeks and will now see price discovery in the secondary market.

Listing day performance will be a key indicator of sentiment. Recent trends have been mixed, with some stocks listing at steep premiums over their issue prices while others saw muted openings. Analysts said grey market premiums, though unofficial, can offer a preliminary read on demand.

Market context and investor caution

The IPO wave comes at a time when the benchmark indices have shown resilience, but volatility persists due to global cues such as US interest rate expectations and crude oil prices. Domestic institutional flows have remained supportive, and retail participation in IPOs has stayed strong, with many issues being subscribed multiple times over.

However, experts advise careful stock selection. Some recent listings have traded below their issue prices within weeks of debut, prompting investors to focus on company fundamentals rather than hype. The sheer number of offerings also creates competition for investor mindspace and capital.

What to watch next week

With the issue calendar packed, the coming week will be a test of market depth. Successful closures and strong subscription numbers could encourage more companies to accelerate their IPO plans in the coming months. Conversely, any weak response might prompt issuers to defer or reprice their offerings.

Investors and analysts will also track listing gains, anchor investor participation, and the response from foreign portfolio investors, whose appetite for Indian equities has been uneven this year. The primary market's ability to absorb this supply will offer a clearer picture of risk appetite in the months ahead.
