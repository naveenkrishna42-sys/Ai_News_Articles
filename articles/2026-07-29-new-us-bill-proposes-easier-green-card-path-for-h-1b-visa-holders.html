New US Bill Proposes Easier Green Card Path For H-1B Visa Holders

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • The bill offers a Green Card pathway to H-1B holders after seven years of continuous US residency.
  • It is seen as a 'commonsense fix' to counter Trump-era immigration policies that critics call 'systemic attacks'.
  • Indian professionals, who form the largest group of H-1B visa holders, stand to benefit significantly.
  • The proposal has sparked debate, with some calling it a 'traitorous' move and others praising it as a long-overdue reform.

A new bill in the US Congress aims to create a more straightforward path to permanent residency for H-1B visa holders, a move that could benefit lakhs of Indian professionals stuck in the green card backlog.

US Senator [Name not provided] reintroduced the legislation this week, proposing that immigrants who have lived in the country for seven continuous years without a criminal record should be eligible for a Green Card. The bill has been described by its sponsor as a 'commonsense fix' to what he called 'systemic attacks' on legal immigration during the Trump administration.

What the Bill Proposes

The proposed law would bypass the current, cumbersome system that ties H-1B workers to their employers and forces them to wait decades for a Green Card, especially those from India. Under the current rules, country-wise caps mean Indians often face wait times exceeding 50 years.

The new framework would instead offer a residency-based route. If an H-1B holder has lived and worked in the US for seven years, pays taxes, and has no criminal record, they could apply for a Green Card directly. This would effectively delink the visa holder from a single employer, giving them greater job mobility and security.

Political Reactions and Debate

The bill has already stirred strong reactions. Critics, particularly from conservative circles, have labelled the senator a 'traitor' for proposing what they see as an amnesty for foreign workers. Some argue it undermines the principle of merit-based immigration and could depress wages for American workers.

Supporters, however, argue that the current system is broken and punishes highly skilled workers who contribute to the US economy. They point out that Indian tech professionals make up the majority of H-1B visa holders and are often forced to live in a state of permanent uncertainty. Industry bodies have also backed the bill, calling it a vital step to retain top talent that might otherwise leave for Canada or other countries with friendlier immigration policies.

Why This Matters for Indians

India is the largest source of H-1B visa holders. For decades, Indian professionals have been caught in a bureaucratic nightmare—unable to change jobs without risking their visa status, and unable to plan their lives due to the green card backlog. This bill, if passed, would offer a clear, predictable path to permanent residency.

Analysts note that the bill is unlikely to pass quickly given the current political climate in Washington. However, its reintroduction signals that the issue remains alive and has bipartisan appeal in some quarters. For the lakhs of Indians waiting in line, it offers a glimmer of hope.

What Comes Next

The bill has been referred to the Senate Judiciary Committee for review. No timeline for a vote has been announced. Observers expect intense lobbying from both supporters and detractors in the coming weeks.
