Heart patients must now be tested for kidney disease

📅 2026-08-30 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • New ESC guidelines recommend universal screening for CKD in heart disease patients.
  • The guidelines highlight the growing link between cardiovascular and renal health.

New Guidelines Recommend Universal Screening for CKD in Heart Disease Patients

The European Society of Cardiology (ESC) has released new guidelines recommending that all patients with heart disease be tested for chronic kidney disease (CKD). This move is part of a broader effort to address the growing link between cardiovascular and renal health.

Why This Matters

The link between heart disease and kidney disease is well-established. Patients with heart disease are at a higher risk of developing CKD, and those with CKD are at a higher risk of developing heart disease. The new guidelines aim to address this bidirectional relationship by recommending universal screening for CKD in heart disease patients.

Key Recommendations

The new guidelines recommend that all patients with heart disease be tested for CKD, regardless of their age, sex, or other risk factors. The guidelines also recommend that patients with CKD be tested for heart disease, and that both conditions be managed together.

Screening for CKD

The guidelines recommend that all patients with heart disease be tested for CKD using a combination of screening tools, including the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR), and serum creatinine levels. The guidelines also recommend that patients with CKD be monitored regularly for signs of worsening kidney function.

Management of CKD

The guidelines recommend that patients with CKD be managed using a combination of lifestyle modifications, medication, and, if necessary, dialysis or kidney transplantation. The guidelines also recommend that patients with CKD be monitored for signs of cardiovascular disease, and that both conditions be managed together.

Expert Opinion

Dr. Sanjay Gupta, a cardiologist at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Delhi, says that the new guidelines are a significant step forward in addressing the link between heart disease and kidney disease. "These guidelines recognize that both conditions are closely linked and that managing one can have a significant impact on the other," he says. Dr. Gupta also notes that the guidelines are likely to have a significant impact on clinical practice, with many more patients being screened for CKD and both conditions being managed together.

Broader Impact

The new guidelines are likely to have a significant impact on clinical practice, with many more patients being screened for CKD and both conditions being managed together. The guidelines are also likely to have a significant impact on public health, with many more patients being able to avoid the complications of both heart disease and CKD.
