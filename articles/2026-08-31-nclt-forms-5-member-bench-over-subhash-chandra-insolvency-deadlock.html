NCLT forms 5-member bench over Subhash Chandra insolvency deadlock

📅 2026-08-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • NCLT forms 5-member bench to resolve deadlock in Subhash Chandra insolvency case
  • Creditors seek ₹6.5 crore recovery against ₹22,006 crore claims
  • Canara Bank and Union Bank move NCLAT against Chandra insolvency plan

The Deadlock

The National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) has formed a 5-member bench to resolve a deadlock in the insolvency case of Subhash Chandra. The deadlock arose after the tribunal's original bench failed to reach a consensus on the repayment plan proposed by creditors.

The creditors have proposed a recovery plan worth ₹6.5 crore against the total claims of ₹22,006 crore. This significant discrepancy has led to the formation of the 5-member bench.

The Verdicts

The original bench of the NCLT was unable to reach a consensus on the repayment plan. The third member of the bench issued a verdict that differed from the original bench's verdicts, further complicating the situation.

This is the first time in the history of the NCLT that a 5-member bench has been formed to resolve a deadlock in an insolvency case. The formation of the bench indicates the complexity and importance of the Subhash Chandra insolvency case.

The Broader Impact

The deadlock in the Subhash Chandra insolvency case has significant implications for the insolvency and bankruptcy code in India. The case highlights the challenges faced by the NCLT in resolving complex insolvency cases.

The formation of the 5-member bench is a step towards ensuring a fair and just resolution of the case. It also sends a message to other creditors and debtors that the NCLT is committed to resolving insolvency cases in a timely and efficient manner.

The Legal Battle

In a twist to the Subhash Chandra insolvency case, Canara Bank and Union Bank have moved the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) against the insolvency plan. The banks argue that the plan is unfair and does not adequately address their claims.

The move by Canara Bank and Union Bank to the NCLAT is a significant development in the Subhash Chandra insolvency case. It indicates the banks' determination to ensure that their claims are adequately addressed in the insolvency plan.

Expert Opinion

Legal experts have expressed concern over the deadlock in the Subhash Chandra insolvency case. They argue that the case highlights the need for clearer guidelines and procedures for resolving insolvency cases.

Experts also note that the formation of the 5-member bench is a positive development. It indicates the NCLT's commitment to resolving insolvency cases in a fair and just manner.
