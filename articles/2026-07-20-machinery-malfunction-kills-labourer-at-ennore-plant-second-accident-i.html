Machinery malfunction kills labourer at Ennore plant; second accident in a week

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A 28-year-old labourer was killed in a machinery malfunction at a private plant in Ennore.
  • The incident is the second industrial accident in the Ennore area in the past week.
  • Police have registered a case and are investigating the cause of the malfunction.

Chennai: A 28-year-old labourer died on Monday after a machinery malfunction at a private industrial plant in Ennore. The incident marks the second fatal industrial accident in the area within a week.

The victim has been identified as Ramesh, a native of Andhra Pradesh who had been working at the plant for the past three years. He was operating a heavy-duty conveyor belt system when the equipment suddenly malfunctioned, crushing him.

Colleagues alerted security, but it was too late

Colleagues working nearby heard the commotion and rushed to help. They alerted plant security and attempted to free Ramesh from the machinery. By the time they managed to stop the equipment, he had sustained severe injuries.

He was rushed to a nearby government hospital, where doctors declared him dead on arrival. Local police have registered a case of unnatural death and are investigating the circumstances that led to the malfunction.

Second fatal incident in a week

This is the second industrial accident in Ennore in the past seven days. Last week, a worker at another private unit in the same industrial corridor died after falling into an open chemical vat. Labour unions have raised concerns over safety standards at factories in the region.

Ennore, a key industrial hub on the northern outskirts of Chennai, houses several heavy industries, including chemical plants, thermal power stations, and manufacturing units. Accidents have been reported periodically, with workers often citing inadequate safety training and lack of protective gear.

Police probe and company response

Police have seized the malfunctioning machinery for forensic examination. They are also questioning the plant supervisor and other workers to determine if negligence played a role. Officials have not yet confirmed whether the company had complied with mandatory safety protocols.

The plant management issued a brief statement expressing condolences to the family. They said they are cooperating with the investigation and will take corrective measures. No compensation details have been announced so far.

Labour activists have demanded a thorough inquiry into both accidents. They argue that repeat incidents point to a systemic failure in enforcing safety regulations. The state labour department is expected to conduct an inspection of the facility in the coming days.

Last week's accident had also prompted calls for stricter oversight. Workers at nearby units have expressed fear and uncertainty, saying they feel unsafe on the job.

Authorities are yet to release a detailed report on either incident. The focus now remains on the forensic examination of the machinery and the broader safety audit of industrial units in the Ennore belt.
