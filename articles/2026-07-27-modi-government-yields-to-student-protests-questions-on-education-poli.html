Modi Government Yields to Student Protests; Questions on Education Policy Linger

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Student protests in India, called the 'Cockroach' movement, have led to a government policy reversal.
  • The protests were led by Gen Z and first-time voters, challenging the government on education and dissent.
  • Analysts say the government's concession has punctured its image of invincibility.
  • The protests have raised questions about the future of education policy and freedom of speech in India.

The Indian government has reversed a controversial education policy after weeks of massive student protests across the country. The movement, dubbed the 'Cockroach' protests by some media outlets, saw tens of thousands of students take to the streets in cities from Delhi to Chennai.

The protests were sparked by a new education bill that critics said would centralize control over university curricula and restrict academic freedom. The government initially dismissed the protesters as a 'lazy generation' and even used the term 'cockroach' in internal communications to describe them, a phrase that later leaked and fueled public outrage.

Government U-Turn

Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s government announced a full withdrawal of the bill late last week. The decision came after reports that the protests were spreading to rural areas and threatening to disrupt the ruling party's electoral base.

In a televised address, the Education Minister said the government had heard the 'voice of the youth' and would form a new committee to draft a more inclusive policy. Protest leaders, however, remain skeptical, demanding a complete overhaul of the consultation process.

“They are treating us like cockroaches, but we have shown we can be a swarm they cannot ignore,” said a student leader from Delhi University, speaking on condition of anonymity.

A Generation Awakens

Political analysts say the protests mark a significant shift in India's political dynamics. The movement was largely driven by Gen Z and first-time voters, many of whom had previously been seen as apolitical or disengaged.

“This is the first time a purely student-led movement has forced a policy reversal in decades,” said Dr. Ananya Sharma, a political analyst at the Centre for Policy Research in New Delhi. “It signals that the government’s aura of invincibility has been punctured.”

The protests also highlighted deep frustrations over employment prospects, rising tuition fees, and a perceived crackdown on dissent. Social media played a key role in organizing the demonstrations, with hashtags like #ReclaimOurEducation and #NotYourCockroach trending for weeks.

Questions on Dissent

Even as the protests wind down, questions remain about the state of democratic debate in India. Critics argue that the government's initial response—calling the protesters lazy and even criminalizing some demonstrations—reflects a troubling trend of intolerance toward dissent.

“The policy reversal is a victory, but the battle for academic freedom is far from over,” said a professor at Jawaharlal Nehru University, who did not wish to be named. “We need to ensure that this isn't just a tactical retreat, but a genuine change in how the government engages with students.”

The government has not yet responded to these concerns. Officials have only said that the new committee will include student representatives, a detail that protest organizers say is crucial.

What Happens Next

The newly formed committee is expected to submit its recommendations within six months. Student leaders have vowed to continue monitoring the process and to protest again if their demands are not met. The coming months will test whether this movement leads to lasting reforms or remains a flash in the pan.
