Chennai Reroutes 700 Vinayaka Idols for Immersion

The Greater Chennai Corporation has confirmed that around 700 Vinayaka idols will be diverted away from the Pattinapakkam and Palavakkam immersion points and redirected to Ennore and Kovalam. The move covers the Ganesh Chaturthi visarjan window and is aimed at easing congestion on the ECR and Marina corridor while protecting sensitive shoreline stretches.

For devotees, the practical effect is simple: if your pandal or home idol was traditionally immersed at Pattinapakkam or Palavakkam, you will now travel to a different ghat. Planning the route in advance matters, because the diverted fleet includes both small household idols and larger community pandal murtis that need crane and tanker support.

Why the Corporation Is Diverting Idols

Pattinapakkam and Palavakkam sit on heavily used coastal stretches. On visarjan day, thousands of vehicles converge on the ECR and the Marina service roads, creating bottlenecks that spill into neighbourhoods for hours. Shifting 700 idols to Ennore in the north and Kovalam in the south spreads the load across the city instead of concentrating it on two points.

Water quality is the second driver. Idols made with plaster of Paris, synthetic paints and non-biodegradable ornaments leave residue behind. Concentrating immersion at fewer, better-managed ghats lets the Corporation deploy containment screens, debris collection crews and immersion tanks more effectively.

What Changes for Devotees

Devotees assigned to Ennore should budget extra travel time from south and central Chennai, since the drive crosses the city. Those routed to Kovalam face a shorter run from the southern suburbs but should expect queueing on the ECR approach. Both points will operate with staggered timings rather than a single peak window.

Real-World Implications for Residents

Residents along the ECR and Marina belt will see relief on visarjan day, with fewer processions terminating at Pattinapakkam and Palavakkam. Traders and eateries near those two points, however, should expect lower footfall than in previous years.

Ennore and Kovalam will absorb the additional traffic. Local residents near both ghats should plan errands around the immersion window, and small businesses near the new points can expect a short, sharp spike in demand for water, snacks and parking.

Pros and Cons of the Diversion Plan

  • Pro: Spreads idol traffic across north and south Chennai instead of two overloaded coastal points.
  • Pro: Better containment of plaster, paint residue and floral waste at fewer, managed ghats.
  • Pro: Reduced pressure on the Marina and ECR service roads during peak visarjan hours.
  • Con: Longer travel for south Chennai devotees reassigned to Ennore.
  • Con: New congestion risk around Ennore and Kovalam if timings are not staggered properly.
  • Con: Confusion at pandal level if route changes are not communicated clearly in Tamil and English.

What Devotees Should Do Before Visarjan

Confirm your assigned immersion point with your pandal committee or local ward office. Carry your own collection bags for flowers and offerings so the ghats stay clear. If you are travelling to Ennore from the southern suburbs, leave well before the stated window rather than at the peak.

Pandal organisers moving large murtis should coordinate crane and transport slots in advance, since both Ennore and Kovalam will run on fixed schedules. Smaller household idols can use the designated immersion tanks where provided, which is faster than waiting for the sea point.
