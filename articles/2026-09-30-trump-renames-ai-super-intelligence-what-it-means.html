The Announcement: A Rebrand With Policy Weight

On 29 September 2026, US President Donald Trump officially renamed artificial intelligence as "super intelligence", describing the technology as "bigger than internet" in the framing carried by The Times of India. The naming exercise arrived alongside the release of an AI accord struck with technology executives, signalling that the administration intends to treat the category as a distinct policy domain rather than an extension of ordinary software regulation.

For technology buyers, the headline is easy to dismiss as rhetoric. That would be a mistake. In Washington, definitions have historically preceded obligations: how a capability is named determines which agency asserts jurisdiction, which safety framework applies, and which disclosure duties land on vendors supplying that capability into enterprise stacks.

What Accompanied the Rename

The terminology change was not issued in isolation. Reporting by The Hindu confirms a separate AI accord released with tech executives, while ThePrint notes that the Nvidia and Anthropic chief executives were slated to attend a lunch with Trump and Johnson focused on AI risks. The Economic Times quoted Anthropic's CEO on the rebranded term: "We all need to come together and make it right."

Critically, the source wire material reviewed for this piece does not publish the accord's clause text. Until the document is released in full, buyers should treat any vendor claim about "full compliance with the new accord" as unverified marketing language.

Why the Vocabulary Shift Matters for Enterprises

Procurement contracts reference regulatory categories, not marketing slogans. If "super intelligence" enters executive orders, export controls or safety reporting rules, every downstream agreement from cloud hosting to model licensing inherits that definition. Indian firms operating shared infrastructure with US hyperscalers are particularly exposed, since sub-processor clauses routinely mirror the principal's regulatory obligations.

The practical effect is a heavier audit burden. Vendors will need to evidence evaluation, red-teaming and incident-reporting practices against a named category, and buyers will be expected to reflect those duties in their own risk registers. Contract negotiation cycles that previously ran eight weeks may stretch as legal teams wait for published guidance.

Key Real-World Highlights for Buyers

  • Terminology changes rarely stay political; they migrate into RFP language and liability clauses within one or two contract cycles.
  • Frontier-model access remains concentrated among a handful of suppliers, so policy friction at the top of the stack prices into every API invoice below it.
  • Input-token pricing, not seat licences, is now the dominant variable in Indian enterprise AI budgets — renegotiate it separately from platform fees.
  • The absence of published accord text is itself a risk: do not sign vendor attestations that reference obligations nobody can read.
  • Model cards, evaluation logs and incident-response SLAs are the only defensible evidence trail during a future compliance audit.

What Procurement Teams Should Do Now

Start with a definitional audit. Pull every active agreement mentioning "AI", "machine learning" or "foundation model" and flag clauses where a renamed category could expand scope. Then add a documentary covenant requiring vendors to supply the accord text and any subsequent guidance within a fixed number of days of publication.

On cost, hold pricing conversations independent of the political noise. Model API rates and GPU capacity allocation remain the two levers that actually move Indian IT budgets; the rebrand changes neither overnight, but it can delay supply commitments if vendors pause launches pending clarity.

Finally, treat the rename as a signal of intensified scrutiny, not of capability. Superintelligence as a policy label does not make deployed systems more reliable; it makes their suppliers more accountable. Buyers who build evidence trails now will negotiate from strength once the accord's actual requirements surface.
