Why Visit Ram Temples on Dussehra?

Dussehra, the festival that celebrates Lord Rama's triumph over the demon king Ravana, holds deep spiritual meaning for millions of Hindus. On this day, devotees mark the victory of good over evil by visiting temples dedicated to Lord Rama. For Dussehra 2025, which falls on October 2, a guide from Outlook India highlights five key Ram temples across the country that offer a profound experience.

Each of these sites has its own history and rituals. Pilgrims often combine temple visits with local fairs and processions, making the journey part of the celebration. Here is a look at the five temples recommended for Dussehra 2025.

Ayodhya Ram Mandir: The Spiritual Epicenter

The newly constructed Ram Mandir in Ayodhya has become a major pilgrimage destination. This temple, built on the site believed to be Lord Rama's birthplace, features intricate carvings and a towering structure. On Dussehra, the temple holds special aartis and discourses on the Ramayana.

Authorities expect large crowds, so planning ahead is crucial. The temple complex includes a museum and gardens, allowing visitors to spend the day exploring. Local guides often share stories of the temple's journey from a disputed site to a grand shrine.

Bhadrachalam Temple: Where Rama Married Sita

In Telangana, the Bhadrachalam Temple is revered as the spot where Lord Rama married Sita. The temple's architecture reflects a blend of Dravidian and Vijayanagara styles. On Dussehra, the temple conducts a special Kalyanotsavam, or wedding ceremony, reenacting the divine union.

Devotees believe that attending this ceremony brings blessings for marital harmony. The temple town itself is lively with street vendors selling prasad and souvenirs. Visitors should note that accommodation may be limited during the festival, so booking early is wise.

Rameswaram Ramanathaswamy Temple: A Coastal Pilgrimage

Located on an island in Tamil Nadu, the Ramanathaswamy Temple is one of the twelve Jyotirlinga shrines and is closely linked to Lord Rama. According to legend, Rama worshipped Shiva here to atone for killing Ravana. The temple's long corridors and 22 wells, each with different water, attract pilgrims year-round.

On Dussehra, the temple holds a special abhishekam, or ritual bathing of the lingam. The coastal setting adds a serene backdrop, but visitors must check the tide timings as access to certain areas can be affected. The temple is also a short distance from the sea, where devotees take a dip before entering.

Kodandarama Temple, Karnataka: A Hilltop Retreat

In the town of Tirumakudlu Narasipura, Karnataka, the Kodandarama Temple sits on a hill overlooking the Kaveri River. This temple is known for its unique idol of Lord Rama holding a bow, or kodanda. The temple's location offers panoramic views of the river and surrounding greenery.

Dussehra here is marked by a procession where the idol is taken around the hill. The temple is less crowded than others, making it ideal for those seeking a quieter experience. Visitors can also explore the nearby Khettalli village for traditional crafts.

Ram Jhula, Rishikesh: A Spiritual Bridge

While not a temple in the traditional sense, Ram Jhula in Rishikesh is a suspension bridge over the Ganges that connects two ashrams. It is named after Lord Rama, who is said to have meditated in the area. The bridge itself is a popular spot for pilgrims walking between the Parmarth Niketan and Swarg Ashrams.

On Dussehra, the area hosts a large evening aarti on the riverbank. The atmosphere is vibrant with chanting and lamps floating on the water. Visitors should be prepared for crowds on the bridge and plan to arrive early for a good view of the ceremony.

For those planning a visit, checking temple websites for updated timings and any special entry requirements is essential. Many temples enforce dress codes and restrict mobile phone use inside the sanctum. Travelers should also book transport and accommodation weeks in advance, as Dussehra sees a surge in pilgrimage traffic.

As Dussehra 2025 approaches, these five sites offer distinct ways to connect with the spirit of the festival. Whether at the grandeur of Ayodhya or the quiet of a hilltop shrine, the journey itself becomes part of the devotion. Watch for local announcements on special events, as many temples add cultural programs during this period.
