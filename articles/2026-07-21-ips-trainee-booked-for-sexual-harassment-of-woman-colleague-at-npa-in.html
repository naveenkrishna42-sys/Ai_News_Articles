Trainee IPS Officer Booked for Harassment

Hyderabad: A trainee Indian Police Service (IPS) officer at the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Police Academy (NPA) has been formally booked for the alleged sexual harassment of a woman colleague. The case has sent shockwaves through the premier training institution, known for grooming the country's top police brass.

According to sources, the woman officer filed a complaint detailing a pattern of unwanted behaviour. She has accused her male batchmate of assault, stalking, and making crude gestures. In a particularly disturbing allegation, she claimed the accused threw condoms at her.

Alleged Suicide Attempt at Academy

The incident took a dramatic turn when the accused trainee was reportedly found unconscious in his room. Multiple news reports have described this as an alleged suicide attempt. The officer was immediately rushed to a nearby hospital for medical treatment.

Academy officials have not yet released an official statement on the condition of the accused officer. The exact circumstances surrounding the incident remain under investigation. Security and medical protocols at the academy are understood to have been activated swiftly.

Legal Action and Internal Inquiry

Following the woman's complaint, the local police registered a First Information Report (FIR) against the trainee. The charges include sections related to sexual harassment and criminal intimidation under the Indian Penal Code. The case is being investigated by the Hyderabad police in coordination with the academy administration.

An internal inquiry has also been initiated by the NPA. The academy is likely to review its internal complaint committee mechanisms and the code of conduct for officer trainees. The accused officer’s career hangs in the balance, pending the outcome of both the criminal investigation and the internal disciplinary proceedings.

What Happens Next

The investigation will now focus on gathering forensic evidence and witness statements. The woman officer has been offered counselling and support. The academy will need to decide on the trainee's suspension or termination from the course. Legal experts say the case could set a precedent for how sexual harassment complaints are handled within elite government training institutions.
