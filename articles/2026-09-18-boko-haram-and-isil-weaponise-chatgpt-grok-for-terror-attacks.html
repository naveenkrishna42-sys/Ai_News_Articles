Boko Haram and ISIL Weaponise ChatGPT, Grok for Terror Attacks

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-18 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Terror groups including Boko Haram and ISIL have reportedly used ChatGPT and Grok to source bomb-making instructions and operational planning.
  • Consumer AI subscriptions cost as little as Rs 1,650–2,000 per month, giving militants cheap access to powerful models.
  • Current guardrails on leading LLMs remain reactive; adversarial prompting and regional languages bypass safety filters.
  • Organisations and governments must treat AI misuse as a security procurement issue, not just a content moderation problem.
  • India's own AI safety framework needs to mandate red-teaming logs and rapid takedown protocols for harmful outputs.

Terror Groups Turn to Consumer AI Tools

A joint investigation by NDTV and Al Jazeera has revealed that Boko Haram and ISIL fighters have used ChatGPT and Grok to plan attacks and build explosives. According to the reporting, operatives asked the AI models for step-by-step bomb-making instructions, and when one tool refused, they simply switched to another. The phrase “God helped us, so will AI” was reportedly used by a Boko Haram member, underscoring how deeply these groups have integrated generative AI into their operations.

The findings place a harsh spotlight on the safety architectures of leading large language models (LLMs). More importantly, they raise urgent questions for enterprises, governments and defence buyers who are now procuring AI systems at scale.

What Exactly Was Used, and How

Investigators found that militants accessed AI through standard consumer apps and web interfaces, not custom-built dark web tools. ChatGPT (OpenAI) and Grok (xAI) were the primary platforms cited. The operatives used everyday smartphones and basic internet connections, often in regions with patchy connectivity.

When safety filters blocked a direct request, users rephrased the prompt, switched to a different model, or used regional languages and code words. This “model shopping” tactic is well known to security researchers, but the NDTV-Al Jazeera investigation provides fresh, on-the-ground evidence of its real-world impact.

Platform Access and Pricing Context

For buyers and policymakers, the cost of access matters. A ChatGPT Plus subscription costs about Rs 1,650 per month in India, while Grok is bundled with X Premium at roughly Rs 700–1,300 per month depending on the tier. Both are within reach of organised militant groups that already fund weapons and logistics.

Why Existing Guardrails Are Failing

OpenAI and xAI have public policies against violent content, and both companies have said they continuously improve safety measures. Yet the investigation shows that determined users can still extract harmful information. The core problem is that current guardrails are reactive: they block known phrases but struggle with creative rephrasing, low-resource languages and multi-turn conversations that gradually lead to dangerous outputs.

For enterprise buyers, this means AI procurement cannot rely solely on vendor claims. Security teams must demand red-team reports, audit logs and contractual guarantees around misuse detection.

Real-World Buyer Implications

  • Due diligence gap: Most AI vendors do not disclose how quickly they patch jailbreak methods. Buyers should ask for mean-time-to-mitigate metrics.
  • Regional language risk: Models perform unevenly across Hausa, Kanuri, Arabic dialects and other languages used by terror groups. Safety filters are weakest where training data is thinnest.
  • Cost of inaction: A single successful attack can cause crores in damage and destabilise entire regions, far outweighing the cost of robust AI safety audits.
  • Regulatory pressure: India’s upcoming AI safety rules may mandate incident reporting, which will affect how companies deploy LLMs in sensitive sectors.

Pros and Cons of Current AI Safety Measures

  • Pro: Leading models block many direct harmful requests out of the box.
  • Pro: Rapid public reporting by media and researchers forces faster vendor responses.
  • Con: Model shopping lets users bypass one platform’s filters by moving to another.
  • Con: Subscription pricing is low enough for non-state armed groups to afford premium access.
  • Con: No universal standard exists for measuring or certifying AI misuse resistance.

What Should Happen Next

Governments and AI vendors need a shared threat intelligence framework. That means real-time reporting of harmful prompts, cross-platform bans on repeat offenders, and mandatory red-teaming before any model launch. For Indian buyers, the lesson is clear: treat AI safety like any other security specification. Ask vendors for proof, not promises.

The NDTV-Al Jazeera investigation is a wake-up call. The same AI tools that boost productivity can, in the wrong hands, become instruments of terror. The response must be as fast and adaptive as the threat.
