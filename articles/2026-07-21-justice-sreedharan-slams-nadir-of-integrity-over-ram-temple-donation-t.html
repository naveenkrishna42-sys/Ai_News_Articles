Justice Sreedharan Slams ‘Nadir of Integrity’ Over Ram Temple Donation Theft

📅 2026-07-21 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Justice Atul Sreedharan condemned public indifference to a donation theft at the Ram Temple.
  • He remarked that nothing can shame those unfazed by such an act, calling it a 'nadir of integrity'.
  • The observation came in a split verdict on bulldozer demolitions by the Allahabad High Court.

Justice Sreedharan’s Strong Remarks on Temple Theft

Justice Atul Sreedharan of the Allahabad High Court made sharp observations on Tuesday regarding the theft of donations meant for the Ram Temple. He stated that people who remain unfazed by such an act have reached the 'nadir of Indian integrity'.

The judge expressed deep disappointment that a society which prides itself on faith could be so indifferent to the misappropriation of offerings made at a sacred site. 'Nothing can shame people unfazed by Ram Temple donation theft,' he remarked during the proceedings.

Split Verdict on Bulldozer Justice

The comments came in the backdrop of a split verdict delivered by a division bench of the Allahabad High Court on the issue of bulldozer demolitions. Justice Sreedharan and another judge differed on a proposed two-year ban on razing properties of accused persons.

Justice Sreedharan was part of the bench that delivered a split verdict on the controversial practice. The case has drawn significant attention as demolitions of homes and properties owned by individuals accused of crimes have continued despite legal scrutiny.

Dissenting View on Executive Discretion

In his separate opinion, Justice Sreedharan termed the bulldozer demolitions as a 'retributive exercise of executive discretion'. He argued that such actions are carried out with impunity to satiate the 'bloodlust of society'.

The judge emphasised that demolitions without due process undermine the rule of law. His remarks signal a strong judicial pushback against extra-legal measures, even as the other judge on the bench held a different view.

The High Court’s split decision means the matter is likely to be referred to a larger bench for final resolution. Legal experts say this could set a precedent on how courts balance executive action with constitutional safeguards.

Broader Implications for Judicial Scrutiny

Justice Sreedharan’s observations on both the temple donation theft and bulldozer demolitions reflect a growing judicial concern over public apathy and executive overreach. His comments on the integrity nadir resonate with those who see a disconnect between societal values and actions.

The verdict has sparked debate in legal and political circles. While some support the need for strict action against criminals, others argue that demolitions violate basic rights.

The Allahabad High Court is expected to list the matter for further hearing before a larger bench. The outcome will be closely watched as it could define the limits of executive discretion in property demolition cases.
