Stage play and art exhibition highlight mental health awareness in Chennai

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A stage play and art exhibition were held in Chennai as part of a mental health awareness event.
  • The event used creative mediums to encourage open dialogue on mental health.
  • Organizers aimed to reduce stigma through accessible, community-focused programming.

Chennai event blends art and theatre for mental health

Chennai's cultural calendar saw a purposeful addition on Friday as a stage play and an art exhibition took centre stage to mark a mental health awareness event. Organised by local groups, the initiative sought to use creative expression as a bridge to discuss emotional well-being, a topic often shrouded in silence.

The venue buzzed with visitors moving between the play and the exhibition, both designed to spark reflection. Organizers said the arts offer a softer entry point into conversations that many find difficult to start.

Art as a conversation starter

The art exhibition featured works by emerging and established artists, each piece touching on themes of anxiety, resilience, and recovery. Curators arranged the display to guide viewers through a narrative of struggle and hope, avoiding didactic tones in favour of visual storytelling.

Several artists were present to interact with attendees, explaining the personal motivations behind their creations. For many visitors, the exhibition was a first step toward understanding mental health as a shared human experience rather than a private burden.

The play: a mirror to everyday struggles

The stage play, performed by a local troupe, portrayed characters navigating stress, family expectations, and the pressure to appear fine. The script, written with input from mental health professionals, avoided melodrama and instead focused on quiet, recognizable moments.

Audience members responded with visible emotion during key scenes, and a moderated discussion followed the performance. This post-show dialogue allowed viewers to voice their own experiences, a move organisers said was central to the event's purpose.

Community response and future plans

Attendance exceeded initial expectations, with many attendees staying beyond the scheduled hours to engage with counsellors present on site. Volunteers distributed informational pamphlets and details of local helplines, ensuring that the event's impact extended beyond the venue.

Organisers noted that such events are vital in a city where mental health services are expanding but awareness still lags. They emphasised that the arts are not a substitute for professional care but a complement to it.

The event is part of a broader push by Chennai-based groups to normalise mental health discussions. Similar programmes are reportedly being planned for other neighbourhoods in the coming months.

As the evening wound down, participants left with a mix of introspection and resolve. Organisers hope the conversations sparked here will ripple outward, reaching homes and workplaces where mental health remains a taboo topic.

What to watch: whether this model of combining theatre and visual art gets adopted by other cities, and if sustained funding will allow such initiatives to become regular fixtures in Chennai's cultural scene.
