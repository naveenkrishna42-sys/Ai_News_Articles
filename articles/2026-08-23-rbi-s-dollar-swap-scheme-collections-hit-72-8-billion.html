RBI's dollar swap scheme collections hit $72.8 billion

📅 2026-08-23 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's dollar swap scheme collections exceed $72.8 billion
  • RBI reports $72.85 billion via FCNR(B), OFCB, ECB Swap facility
  • RBI records $65 billion in foreign currency deposits from 35 million diaspora

RBI's dollar swap scheme collections exceed $72.8 billion

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has reported that collections under its dollar swap scheme have exceeded $72.8 billion. The scheme, which was launched to provide foreign currency liquidity to banks, has seen significant inflows from various sources.

Forex inflows via FCNR (B), OFCB, ECB Swap facility

The RBI has seen forex inflows of $72.85 billion via the FCNR (B), OFCB, and ECB Swap facility. This facility allows banks to borrow foreign currency from the RBI at a discount, which they can then lend to customers at a higher rate. The inflows have been driven by the demand for foreign currency from banks to meet their customers' needs.

Foreign currency deposits from diaspora

The RBI has also recorded USD 65 billion in foreign currency deposits from 35 million diaspora. This is a significant increase from the previous year, and it reflects the growing confidence of Indian diaspora in the Indian economy. The deposits have been made in various forms, including FCNR-B deposits, which are non-resident external (NRE) deposits held in Indian banks.

Why RBI ended its foreign currency swap scheme early

The RBI ended its foreign currency swap scheme early due to the significant inflows it received. The scheme was launched in April 2026 to provide foreign currency liquidity to banks, but it was ended in July 2026 after receiving $72.85 billion in inflows. The RBI decided to end the scheme early to prevent any potential liquidity issues.

What happens next?

The RBI's decision to end the foreign currency swap scheme early has raised several questions about the future of foreign currency liquidity in India. The RBI will need to monitor the situation closely and take necessary steps to ensure that there is enough foreign currency liquidity available in the market. The government and the RBI will also need to work together to address the concerns of the Indian diaspora and ensure that they continue to have confidence in the Indian economy.
