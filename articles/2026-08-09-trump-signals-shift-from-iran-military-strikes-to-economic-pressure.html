Trump Signals Shift From Iran Military Strikes To Economic Pressure

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says US is 'only semi-negotiating' with Tehran and going 'low key'.
  • WSJ reports Trump willing to end Iran war without a formal nuclear deal.
  • Economic pressure expected to replace military operations as primary strategy.
  • Strait of Hormuz remains a key sticking point in any potential agreement.

In a notable shift from his earlier hardline military posture, President Donald Trump has indicated that the United States is moving away from active strikes on Iran, signalling a preference for economic pressure and a possible exit from the conflict without a formal nuclear agreement. The remarks, made in an interview with Axios, suggest a recalibration of Washington's strategy in the region.

‘Low Keying It’ With Tehran

Trump told Axios that the US is “low keying it” with Iran, a phrase that marks a sharp departure from the bellicose rhetoric that preceded the outbreak of hostilities. He also stated that the US is “only semi-negotiating” with Tehran, hinting at a less formal, more pragmatic approach to dialogue.

The comments come amid a flurry of reports from outlets including Al Jazeera and The Times of India, which cite administration sources confirming a reduction in military operations. The White House has not issued an official statement on the matter, but the president's own words have set the tone for what appears to be a strategic pivot.

Nuclear Deal Off the Table?

According to a Wall Street Journal report, Trump is now willing to end the war with Iran without securing a comprehensive nuclear deal. This marks a significant concession, as the administration's initial casus belli was Tehran's alleged pursuit of nuclear weapons. The president's flexibility suggests that the primary objective has shifted from disarmament to regional stability and de-escalation.

Officials familiar with the discussions have indicated that the US is exploring a framework that would focus on verifiable constraints rather than a full treaty. However, no formal proposal has been presented to Iranian authorities, and Tehran has yet to respond publicly to the reported shift.

Hormuz Remains the Flashpoint

While the nuclear issue appears to be receding in urgency, the Strait of Hormuz continues to be a critical sticking point. Reports suggest that Trump is unwilling to compromise on the free navigation of the strait, through which a significant portion of global oil supply transits. Any deal, informal or otherwise, would likely hinge on Iran's commitment to keeping the waterway open.

The president's remarks have been met with cautious optimism in some quarters and scepticism in others. Critics argue that abandoning the nuclear demand undermines the original justification for the conflict, while supporters see it as a pragmatic step toward reducing US involvement in another Middle Eastern quagmire.

Economic Leverage Over Bombs

Trump's emphasis on economic pressure over military operations aligns with his long-held preference for using sanctions and trade measures as tools of statecraft. The administration is reportedly preparing a new round of sanctions targeting Iranian oil exports and financial institutions, aiming to choke off revenue streams without resorting to further strikes.

This approach, however, carries risks. Iran has historically responded to economic strangulation with provocations, including attacks on shipping and regional proxies. The coming weeks will test whether the 'low key' strategy can achieve what sustained military pressure has not.

What Happens Next

As the US recalibrates its approach, all eyes are on Tehran's response and whether informal backchannels can yield a tangible de-escalation. The president's willingness to walk back his nuclear demands may open a window for diplomacy, but the path remains fraught with mistrust and the ever-present spectre of miscalculation.
