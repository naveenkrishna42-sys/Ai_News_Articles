US PERM green-card freeze: What happens to 401(k), stocks & property?

Key points
  • A PERM/green-card filing freeze affects work-authorisation pathways, not ownership of 401(k), stocks, or property
  • Your 401(k) and brokerage outcomes depend on account type, vesting, and how soon you distribute or move assets
  • Returning to India is manageable for assets—watch tax reporting, withdrawals, and estate/planning gaps

What the “green-card suspension” actually changes for asset holders

When the US discusses suspending or freezing parts of the employment-based green-card process (often PERM-linked routes), the immediate impact is on immigration processing and timelines. For many Indian professionals, the bigger question is not whether you “lose” money, but what happens to the assets you already own—especially a US 401(k), taxable stocks, and property—if you decide (or are forced) to return to India.

In most cases, the government action does not confiscate personal investments. Your 401(k) and brokerage accounts are contractual and legally owned through the plan/custodian. The practical risk shifts to work status uncertainty, which can affect contributions, employer match, vesting of equity, and whether you can stay long enough to benefit from tax-advantaged growth.

Commercial buyers and long-term planners should treat this as a “cash-flow and timing” issue: you may need a plan for distributions, tax withholding, and how you’ll hold or transfer assets while you live abroad.

401(k): Ownership stays with you, but rules change when you leave

A 401(k) is generally held in your name (through the plan). If you return to India, you usually do not lose the account. The real decision is what you do with it when you stop being employed by the US company: leave it in the plan (if allowed), roll it over to an IRA, or withdraw under the plan rules and US tax framework.

Key buyer-style benchmarks: the “best” route depends on (1) your current age and whether you meet plan distribution rules, (2) whether you have access to employer plan-to-IRA rollovers, (3) whether you want ongoing US tax-deferred growth versus immediate liquidity, and (4) your expected tax treatment in India.

Also watch employer match. If contributions pause due to job loss or end of employment, you stop building new retirement balances. However, existing balances continue compounding, and any vesting already completed remains yours.

Stocks and brokerage accounts: The “freeze” doesn’t freeze ownership

Public stocks held in a brokerage account are typically straightforward. A PERM suspension may limit your ability to work for a particular employer, but it does not usually remove your brokerage access or sell your holdings. If you hold exchange-listed shares, the share registry and brokerage custody remain independent of immigration processing.

Where outcomes can differ is with employer equity: RSUs and stock options have vesting schedules tied to employment and sometimes to continued service through the vesting cliff. If you leave, unvested equity can be forfeited under the grant agreement—even if you “own” the grant document.

For taxable brokerage portfolios, your return to India affects tax reporting more than the investment itself. Dividend income, capital gains, and realised gains will need correct reporting across jurisdictions, and your broker’s ability to support foreign tax documentation can vary by custodian.

Equity vesting: the real clock is employment, not status

If you’re evaluating whether to stay in the US until a particular vesting date, think in terms of “what is already vested” versus “what is contingent.” For RSUs, vesting can be time-based; for options, it can be both time- and exercise-window-based after termination of employment. This is where immigration timeline uncertainty becomes a financial planning constraint.

  • Pro: Market risk and custody controls are separate from green-card processing.
  • Con: Unvested RSUs and options may be lost if employment ends before vesting.
  • Pro: You can keep diversified holdings and rebalance even after moving abroad.
  • Con: Cross-border tax filings can become more complex for dividends and capital gains.

US property: Title matters more than immigration paperwork

If you own US real estate, the most important determinant of what happens when you return to India is title and mortgage obligations, not your immigration status. Ownership, deed records, and lien arrangements remain in place. The “risk” category is typically operational: property taxes, mortgage servicing, insurance, and whether you can manage the property remotely.

If you have a mortgage, leaving the US doesn’t absolve payment responsibilities. Foreclosure risk is tied to missed payments, not green-card programme status. If the plan is to rent out the property while you’re in India, you’ll want to model vacancy, maintenance, and property management fees.

On the tax side, property ownership influences US reporting and can also affect your Indian tax position depending on holding period and income classification (rent, gains). Engage cross-border tax guidance early if you’re planning to sell shortly after returning.

Practical action plan for buyers and long-term investors

People affected by PERM-linked freezes can treat the next 6–12 months as a decision window: protect downside, preserve tax advantages where feasible, and avoid “forced mistakes” like untimely withdrawals. This is not about panic; it’s about aligning immigration uncertainty with finance operations.

Start with a checklist: confirm what you can do with your 401(k) upon employment exit, review your grant agreements for RSUs/options vesting terms, inventory brokerage holdings and dividend settings, and ensure your US property is managed with documented payment and vendor coverage.

If India is the likely destination, prepare for compliance: plan your transition timeline for account permissions, beneficiary designations, and US address/tax documentation used by custodians.
