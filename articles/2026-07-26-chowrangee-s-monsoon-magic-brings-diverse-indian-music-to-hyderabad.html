Chowrangee’s Monsoon Magic brings diverse Indian music to Hyderabad

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chowrangee’s Monsoon Magic event held in Hyderabad
  • Celebrates India's musical diversity with folk, classical, and contemporary acts
  • Event aims to showcase unity in musical traditions across regions

Monsoon beats echo across Hyderabad

Hyderabad’s cultural calendar added another vibrant chapter this weekend as Chowrangee hosted its ‘Monsoon Magic’ event, a celebration of India’s vast musical landscape. The gathering drew music lovers from across the city, who braved the damp weather to enjoy a lineup that spanned genres and regions.

The event featured performers from different parts of the country, each bringing a distinct flavour. From the soulful strains of Hindustani classical to the energetic rhythms of folk music, the programme was designed to reflect the country’s sonic richness. Organisers said the aim was to highlight how diverse traditions can coexist and even complement each other.

A stage for regional talent

Artists from states such as Rajasthan, Punjab, and Tamil Nadu took the stage, offering audiences a taste of their native sounds. The monsoon theme tied the performances together, with several pieces evoking the season’s mood through ragas and lyrics that speak of rain and renewal.

One performer, a folk singer from Rajasthan, drew cheers with a rendition of a traditional rain song. Another, a classical vocalist from the Carnatic tradition, presented a composition that blended melodic patterns from both north and south India. The audience responded with enthusiasm, clapping and swaying to the beats.

Community and connection through music

Beyond the performances, the event served as a meeting point for musicians and listeners. Conversations between sets touched on the challenges of preserving folk traditions in a rapidly urbanising country. Several artists noted that platforms like Chowrangee’s Monsoon Magic are vital for keeping these art forms alive.

“It’s not just about entertainment,” said one organiser. “It’s about creating a space where people can experience the depth of our musical heritage.” The event also included informal workshops where attendees learned basic rhythms from folk drummers, adding an interactive layer to the evening.

Hyderabad’s growing cultural footprint

Hyderabad has long been a city where tradition and modernity meet. Events like this reinforce its role as a hub for the arts. Chowrangee, known for its focus on indie and folk music, has steadily built a reputation for curating experiences that go beyond mainstream entertainment.

The Monsoon Magic edition was no exception. It drew families, young professionals, and seasoned music enthusiasts, all united by a love for melody. The rain, far from being a deterrent, seemed to enhance the atmosphere, with droplets adding a natural percussion to the live performances.

What lies ahead

With the monsoon season still underway, organisers are already looking at potential follow-up events. They hope to expand the format to include more regional traditions and possibly collaborate with other cultural organisations across India. For now, the echoes of the evening’s music linger — a reminder that in a country of many tongues, rhythm remains a common language.
