Iran billboard taunts Trump after Lindsey Graham's death

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • A billboard in Tehran taunts Donald Trump after Lindsey Graham's death
  • Graham was a key Trump ally and influential voice on foreign policy
  • The incident raises questions about US-Israel relations and regional stability

Billboard appears in Tehran after Senator's death

A billboard in Iran's capital has appeared to taunt former US President Donald Trump following the death of Senator Lindsey Graham. The digital display, photographed in Tehran on Friday, shows the text 'Who is D nexT' — a clear reference to Graham's passing and Trump's circle of allies.

The sign has drawn immediate attention from diplomats and analysts. It comes just days after Graham, a Republican from South Carolina and a vocal Trump supporter, died under circumstances that officials have not yet confirmed.

Graham's role in US politics and foreign policy

Lindsey Graham was a fixture in Washington for decades. He initially criticised Donald Trump during the 2016 Republican primaries but later became one of his most loyal defenders in the Senate. His influence stretched far beyond domestic politics, particularly on matters of national security and foreign relations.

Graham was a strong advocate for Israel and a hawkish voice on Iran. He often pushed for tougher sanctions and military deterrence against Tehran. Analysts say this makes the billboard's timing and message particularly pointed.

Reactions in Washington and beyond

The US State Department has not officially commented on the billboard. But several lawmakers have condemned it as a provocative gesture at a sensitive moment. Senator Tom Cotton called it 'a disgraceful act from a regime that thrives on intimidation'.

In Israel, officials are watching closely. Graham was seen as a key pillar of bipartisan support for Jerusalem in the US Congress. His death leaves a gap in that coalition, and questions are already being asked about whether Israel's standing in Washington will be affected.

The 'Three Amigos' dynamic

Graham was part of what some called the 'Three Amigos' — a trio of Trump allies that included him, Senator Ted Cruz, and former UN Ambassador Nikki Haley. The group was instrumental in shaping Trump's foreign policy vision, especially on Iran and the Middle East.

With Graham gone, the remaining 'Amigos' face a recalibrated political landscape. Trump himself has not yet responded publicly to the billboard. But those close to him say he is deeply affected by Graham's death and views the Iranian taunt as a personal insult.

What the billboard signals

Iranian messaging through public displays is not new. In 2020, after the US killed General Qasem Soleimani, billboards in Tehran depicted a similar style of threat. This latest display suggests Tehran is testing the political waters after Graham's death.

Experts say the billboard could be a signal of confidence from Iran's leadership. It may also be an attempt to exploit what they see as a moment of weakness or distraction in US politics.

What happens next depends on how Washington responds — and whether Trump or the Biden administration view this as a line that cannot be crossed.
