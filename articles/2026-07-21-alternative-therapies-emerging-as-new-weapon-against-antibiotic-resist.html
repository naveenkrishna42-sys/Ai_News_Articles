Alternative therapies emerging as new weapon against antibiotic resistance

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Antibiotic resistance is increasing, threatening the effectiveness of life-saving drugs.
  • Alternative therapies, including bacteriophages and biofilm-disrupting compounds, are being studied as potential solutions.
  • Children are particularly vulnerable to resistant infections, according to recent global studies.

As antibiotic resistance continues to climb worldwide, researchers are turning to alternative therapies to outsmart superbugs. From brewing techniques to biofilm disruption, scientists are exploring unconventional methods to tackle infections that no longer respond to standard drugs.

Growing threat to children's health

A recent global study has found that antibiotic-resistant bacteria are increasingly leaving children vulnerable to common infections. The findings, reported in The Guardian, highlight a worrying trend where once-treatable conditions are becoming harder to manage in young patients.

Rising antimicrobial resistance in children threatens the effectiveness of life-saving antibiotics, according to experts. This development has prompted urgent calls for new approaches to treatment.

Exploring alternative pathways

Researchers at Georgia State University are investigating how brewing processes and biofilm formation could offer clues to fighting resistant bacteria. Their work, titled "Outsmarting Superbugs: From Brewing to Biofilms," suggests that compounds found in fermentation might disrupt bacterial communication and growth.

Alternative therapies may offer a new way to fight antibiotic resistance, according to a report by Down To Earth. Scientists are looking beyond traditional drug development into methods that weaken bacteria without killing them outright, reducing the pressure for resistance to evolve.

Bacteriophages and enzyme therapies

One promising avenue is the use of bacteriophages—viruses that specifically target and destroy bacteria. These phages have been used in limited cases, particularly in Eastern Europe, and are now gaining attention in Western medicine as a last-resort treatment.

Enzyme-based therapies that break down bacterial biofilms are also being tested. Biofilms are protective layers that bacteria form, making them resistant to antibiotics. Disrupting these layers could restore drug effectiveness.

Expert Q&A: Solutions exist

In an expert Q&A published by The Conversation, researchers stressed that solutions to antibiotic resistance do exist. They pointed to a combination of stewardship, new drug development, and alternative therapies as key strategies.

However, experts cautioned that these alternatives are not yet widely available. Clinical trials and regulatory approvals remain significant hurdles before such treatments can reach hospitals and clinics.

What happens next

Governments and health agencies are ramping up funding for alternative therapy research. The next few years will be critical to determine whether these approaches can be scaled up to meet the growing threat of superbugs.
