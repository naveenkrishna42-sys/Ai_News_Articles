Sensex, Nifty Close Lower on Financials Drag, Crude Oil Pressure

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty ended in the red, reversing intraday gains.
  • Financial stocks were the biggest drag on the benchmarks.
  • Rising crude oil prices added to market jitters.
  • Broader market indices also witnessed selling pressure.

Indian equity benchmarks closed lower on Friday, snapping a two-day winning streak, as selling pressure in financial stocks and a spike in global crude oil prices dampened investor sentiment. The BSE Sensex ended 312 points, or 0.4 per cent, lower at 78,456, while the NSE Nifty 50 slipped 89 points, or 0.37 per cent, to settle at 23,712.

Intraday, the market had shown some resilience, with the Sensex touching a high of 78,890 in early trade. However, the momentum fizzled out by afternoon as financial heavyweights came under heavy selling.

Financials Lead the Decline

The financial sector was the primary culprit behind the market's fall. Private sector banks, public sector lenders, and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) all traded in the red, pulling the Nifty Bank index down by over 0.8 per cent.

HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India were among the top losers on the Sensex, collectively shaving off nearly 250 points from the index. Analysts attributed the weakness to profit-booking after a recent run-up in banking stocks, as well as concerns over margin pressures in a rising interest rate environment.

Crude Oil Prices Weigh on Sentiment

Adding to the gloom, global crude oil prices firmed up, with Brent crude trading above $82 a barrel. Higher oil prices are a double-edged sword for India, a net importer of crude, as they widen the trade deficit and stoke inflationary pressures.

This, in turn, could delay the Reserve Bank of India's rate cut cycle, a key expectation that has been supporting equity valuations. The rise in oil also hit oil marketing companies, with HPCL, BPCL, and IOCL all closing lower.

Broader Market Under Pressure

The broader market also witnessed selling, with the BSE Midcap index down 0.3 per cent and the Smallcap index losing 0.5 per cent. Market breadth was negative, with about 1,800 stocks declining against 1,500 advances on the BSE.

Sectorally, only IT and FMCG managed to close in the green, as investors sought refuge in defensive plays. Metal stocks, too, were under pressure following a dip in global commodity prices.

What Analysts Are Saying

Market observers noted that the pullback was largely expected after a strong rally earlier in the week. "The market is consolidating in a range, and any negative trigger – be it oil or global cues – is being used by traders to book profits," said a senior analyst at a domestic brokerage.

They added that the upcoming US Federal Reserve meeting and domestic inflation data will be key factors to watch in the near term. The market's next direction hinges on these events, as well as on the progress of the monsoon and its impact on rural demand.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the cash market on Friday, offloading shares worth approximately ₹1,200 crore, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) bought shares worth a similar amount, providing some support.

The rupee, meanwhile, closed flat at 83.45 against the US dollar, as the Reserve Bank of India's intervention likely cushioned any sharp fall.

With the earnings season largely behind, the market is likely to take cues from global macros and domestic policy expectations. Investors will now be watching the US inflation print and the RBI's commentary in its upcoming policy review, which could set the tone for the next leg of the market.
