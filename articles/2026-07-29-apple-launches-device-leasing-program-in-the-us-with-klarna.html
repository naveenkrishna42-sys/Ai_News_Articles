Apple launches device leasing program in the US with Klarna

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Apple's new leasing program is available in the United States.
  • Customers can lease iPhones, iPads, and Macs instead of buying them.
  • The program is run in partnership with the payment company Klarna.
  • Users can upgrade to newer devices at the end of the lease term.

New option for Apple customers

Apple has launched a device leasing program in the United States. The program, called Apple Upgrade, lets customers lease iPhones, iPads, and Macs instead of buying them outright. It is run in partnership with the payment company Klarna.

The move gives consumers a new way to access Apple hardware. Instead of paying the full cost upfront or through an installment plan, users now have a lease option that ends with them returning the device.

How the lease works

Under the program, customers pay a monthly fee to use the device. At the end of the lease term, they can either return the device or upgrade to a newer model. The exact terms and monthly costs depend on the device model and the lease duration.

Apple has not yet detailed the full pricing for all devices. But sources indicate the program is designed to make the latest hardware more accessible. It removes the need for a large upfront payment.

Partnership with Klarna

The program is powered by Klarna, a Swedish fintech company known for its buy-now-pay-later services. Klarna will handle the leasing agreements and payments. This is not a loan or a traditional installment plan — it is a lease.

Apple has not confirmed whether the program will come to other countries. For now, it is limited to customers in the United States. The company has also not said if the program includes AppleCare or other services as part of the monthly fee.

Why this matters

The leasing model is common in other industries, such as cars and home appliances. But it is relatively new for consumer electronics at scale. Apple is now betting that a growing number of users prefer access over ownership.

Analysts say this could boost Apple's recurring revenue and lock users into the ecosystem. If a customer leases an iPhone, they are more likely to stay with Apple when the lease ends. The program also reduces e-waste, as returned devices can be refurbished and resold.

Apple has not yet released official numbers on early sign-ups. But the company confirmed the program went live this week on its website and in select Apple Store locations.

What to watch next

Industry watchers will be looking at how quickly Apple expands this program outside the US. If successful, it could reshape how millions of people buy their smartphones and computers. Apple has not given a timeline for international rollout.
