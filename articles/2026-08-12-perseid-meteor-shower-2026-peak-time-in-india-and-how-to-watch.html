Perseid meteor shower 2026: Peak time in India and how to watch

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Perseid meteor shower is expected to peak on the night of August 12-13, 2026.
  • Skygazers in India can expect the best views after midnight, when the radiant is high.
  • The shower coincides with a new moon, promising dark skies and optimal viewing conditions.

When to watch the Perseids in India

The Perseid meteor shower, one of the most anticipated celestial events of the year, reaches its peak tonight. For skywatchers in India, the best time to look up is after midnight and in the pre-dawn hours of August 13. The radiant point, located in the constellation Perseus, will be well above the horizon during these hours, offering a clearer view of the meteors.

According to astronomers, the shower is expected to produce up to 60-70 meteors per hour under ideal conditions. This year, the peak coincides with a new moon, ensuring darker skies and a better chance of spotting fainter streaks.

Where to look

The meteors will appear to radiate from the northeast, but they can be seen across the entire sky. Experts recommend finding a dark location away from city lights, lying flat on your back, and allowing your eyes to adjust to the darkness for at least 20 minutes. No special equipment is needed—just patience and a clear view of the sky.

While the Perseids are visible from both hemispheres, their visibility in India is best during the early morning hours, when the radiant is higher. In urban areas, light pollution may reduce the number of visible meteors, so a trip to the outskirts is advisable.

What makes the Perseids special

The Perseids are caused by debris from Comet Swift-Tuttle, which Earth passes through every August. As the comet's particles enter the atmosphere at high speed, they burn up, creating bright streaks of light. This year, the shower follows a recent solar eclipse, adding to the excitement for astronomy enthusiasts.

NASA has also announced live streaming of the event for those unable to step outside. The Virtual Telescope Project will host an online observation session on August 12, allowing viewers worldwide to watch the shower remotely.

Tips for the best experience

  • Find a dark spot with an unobstructed view of the sky.
  • Give your eyes at least 20 minutes to adapt to the dark.
  • Avoid looking at your phone or any bright screens.
  • Bring a reclining chair or blanket for comfort.

What to expect tonight

Forecasters predict clear skies over much of India, though local weather conditions may vary. The shower's activity is expected to peak around 2 a.m. IST, but meteors may be visible from as early as 10 p.m. The most spectacular fireballs often appear after midnight.

For those who miss tonight's peak, the shower will remain partially active for a few more nights, though at a reduced rate. So even if you cannot step out tonight, there is still a chance to catch a few shooting stars over the coming days.

As the Perseids fade, attention will shift to the next major celestial event—the Orionid meteor shower in October. But for now, tonight offers a rare chance to witness one of nature's most dazzling displays. So set your alarms, head to a dark spot, and look up.
