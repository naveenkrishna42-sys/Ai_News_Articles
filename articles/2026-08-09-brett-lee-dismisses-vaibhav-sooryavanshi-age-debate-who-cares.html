Brett Lee dismisses Vaibhav Sooryavanshi age debate: 'Who cares?'

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Brett Lee dismisses age debate, says 'Who cares?'
  • Sooryavanshi's age has become a major talking point in cricket circles
  • Former players like Dhawan, Rahane, Uthappa weigh in on the young batter

Lee's blunt message on age talk

Australian pace legend Brett Lee has brushed aside the endless speculation over Vaibhav Sooryavanshi's age, saying the focus should be on the teenager's talent rather than his birth certificate. "Everyone away from India asks if he's 15. Who cares?" Lee said, shutting down a debate that has followed the young batter's rapid rise.

Lee's comments come amid a chorus of opinions from former cricketers, each offering their own take on the 15-year-old's potential and the pressures that come with early stardom. The former speedster's dismissal of the age question signals a growing fatigue within the cricket fraternity over a narrative that refuses to die.

Dhawan's verdict on skill vs age

Former India opener Shikhar Dhawan, known for his own flamboyant strokeplay, weighed in with a measured assessment. "Having skill is one thing," Dhawan said, hinting that temperament and consistency would determine whether Sooryavanshi lives up to the hype. His remarks suggest that while the raw ability is undeniable, the journey from prodigy to proven performer is fraught with challenges.

Dhawan's comments add to a growing list of veterans who have publicly endorsed the youngster's talent while cautioning against premature coronation. The subtext is clear: Indian cricket has seen many teenage sensations fade away, and the real test begins now.

Uthappa's warning: 'Remember Kambli, Shaw'

Robin Uthappa struck a more cautionary note, invoking the names of Vinod Kambli and Prithvi Shaw—two players whose early promise was derailed by a mix of injuries, form slumps and off-field issues. "Remember Kambli, Shaw," Uthappa warned, urging Sooryavanshi and his handlers to guard against complacency and the pitfalls of fame.

The reference to Shaw is particularly telling, given that the Mumbai batter was once hailed as the next big thing after his U-19 World Cup exploits. Uthappa's warning serves as a reminder that talent alone is rarely enough to sustain a long international career.

Rahane points to family support

Ajinkya Rahane, known for his calm and composed approach, highlighted a different aspect: the role of family. The veteran batter pointed out that a strong support system at home can make all the difference for a young player navigating the glare of the spotlight. "Family factor is huge," Rahane said, delivering his verdict on what could determine Sooryavanshi's trajectory.

Rahane's observation underscores how the environment around a player often shapes their response to success and failure. For a 15-year-old suddenly in the national conversation, having grounded parents and mentors could be as crucial as any coaching input.

What lies ahead for Sooryavanshi

As the debate over his age continues to simmer, Sooryavanshi's performances on the field remain the only definitive answer to his critics. The coming months will reveal whether he can translate domestic promise into sustained excellence across formats and against tougher opposition.

For now, the cricket world watches closely—some with admiration, others with skepticism. But as Lee succinctly put it, the number on a passport matters little if the bat does the talking.
