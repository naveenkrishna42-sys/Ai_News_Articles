Modi and Trump at ASEAN Summit: What the Manila Agenda Means for India

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-15 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Modi and Trump attend the Manila ASEAN Summit with maritime rules, tariffs and conflicts on the agenda
  • India-ASEAN trade near $130 billion faces a goods deficit and an unfinished pact review
  • Connectivity projects and supply chain shifts carry the sharpest commercial implications
  • ASEAN consensus on Myanmar and the South China Sea remains fragile under Philippine chairmanship

Why Manila matters for New Delhi

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and US President Donald Trump are both slated to attend the ASEAN Summit in Manila, placing the 11-member bloc — now including Timor-Leste — at the centre of competing strategic calculations. For India, the summit is the highest-profile multilateral platform this year to convert the Act East Policy from diplomatic attendance into trade, connectivity and maritime deliverables. The Philippines' chairmanship also keeps the South China Sea firmly on the table.

Maritime security and conflict management

Expect firm language on the South China Sea, where ASEAN members remain divided over how firmly to push back against Beijing's claims. India has consistently backed freedom of navigation and UNCLOS-based rules, and Manila gives Modi room to align with claimant states without being dragged into a bilateral confrontation. The Myanmar crisis, Afghanistan and the Ukraine war will test whether the bloc can still agree on a unified chair's statement.

Economic ties: trade, tariffs and supply chains

India-ASEAN merchandise trade has hovered near $130 billion, but New Delhi's goods deficit with the bloc remains a friction point in the ongoing review of the ASEAN-India Trade in Goods Agreement. Trump's presence adds urgency: Washington's tariff pressure over Indian purchases of Russian oil, and its push to reroute supply chains away from China, will dominate the margins. India is pitching itself as a trusted alternative for electronics assembly, pharma and critical-mineral processing.

What businesses should actually watch

The commercial signal lies in connectivity — the India-Myanmar-Thailand trilateral highway, ASEAN-India maritime cooperation and the India-Middle East-Europe Economic Corridor. Progress on digital payment linkages and skilling partnerships would cut transaction costs faster than any communiqué. Nalanda University's emerging role as an academic hub for Southeast Asia, flagged by the MEA, is a soft-power asset that supports long-term talent pipelines.

Key real-world takeaways

  • The Modi-Trump sideline meeting will shape the tariff and Russian oil dispute more than any plenary text.
  • ASEAN consensus is fragile; a diluted chair's statement on Myanmar or the South China Sea would weaken the summit's leverage.
  • India's Act East push now runs through economic deliverables, not summit photo-ops.
  • For buyers and corporates, connectivity and digital-payment announcements carry direct cost and lead-time implications.
