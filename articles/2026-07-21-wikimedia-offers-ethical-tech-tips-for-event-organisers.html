Wikimedia Offers Ethical Tech Tips for Event Organisers

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Wikimedia published a set of ethical tech recommendations for events.
  • Tips cover data privacy, environmental impact, and accessibility.
  • The guide aims to help organisers make responsible technology choices.

Wikimedia, the organisation behind Wikipedia, has released a new set of guidelines aimed at helping event organisers make more ethical technology choices. The tips, published under the banner of 'Ethical Tech Tips for Events', focus on three core areas: privacy, sustainability, and inclusivity.

The guide is designed for a wide audience, from small community meetups to large conferences. It offers practical advice rather than abstract principles, with the goal of reducing the negative impact of technology on both people and the planet.

Privacy First: Data and Surveillance

One of the central themes of the Wikimedia guide is data privacy. Organisers are urged to avoid using tools that track attendees without explicit consent. The tips recommend using open-source software where possible and minimising the collection of personal data.

The guide specifically advises against using facial recognition systems or mandatory app downloads. It suggests that if an event app is used, it should allow users to opt out of non-essential data sharing. Wikimedia also warns against using third-party platforms that may sell or misuse attendee information.

Reducing the Environmental Footprint

Sustainability is another key pillar. The tips encourage organisers to consider the environmental cost of digital tools. Streaming high-definition video may seem standard, but it consumes significant energy. The guide recommends offering lower-resolution options and using content delivery networks that rely on renewable energy.

For physical events, the advice extends to reducing electronic waste. Organisers are urged to avoid handing out branded USB drives or other gadgets that will likely be thrown away. Instead, the guide suggests using reusable or digital materials.

Inclusivity and Accessibility

The third major focus is on making events accessible to everyone, regardless of their technical ability or physical limitations. The guide emphasises the need for captioning on live streams, sign language interpretation, and materials in multiple languages.

It also warns against using platforms that require high-end hardware or fast internet connections. Such requirements can exclude participants from regions with poor connectivity or those using older devices. The tips suggest testing all tools with a diverse group before the event.

Wikimedia also highlights the importance of choosing platforms that support screen readers and other assistive technologies. The goal is to ensure that no one is left out due to the choice of tech stack.

Practical Steps for Organisers

The guide provides a checklist for organisers to follow. This includes auditing all software for privacy policies, opting for open-source alternatives like BigBlueButton over proprietary tools, and ensuring that recorded sessions are stored securely.

Another tip is to provide clear privacy notices. Attendees should know what data is being collected, how it will be used, and how long it will be kept. The guide also recommends deleting data after the event unless consent has been given for longer retention.

Wikimedia's advice is not just for tech events. It applies to any gathering that uses digital tools, from academic conferences to local club meetings. The organisation hopes that by following these tips, event organisers can set a new standard for responsible technology use.

As digital tools become more embedded in our daily lives, the choices made by event organisers can have a ripple effect. The Wikimedia guide is a step toward making those choices more conscious and less harmful.

What comes next? The organisation plans to update the guide periodically as technology evolves. Event organisers are encouraged to share their own experiences and feedback, which may help shape future versions of the tips.
