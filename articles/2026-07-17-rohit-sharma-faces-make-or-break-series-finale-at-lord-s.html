Rohit Sharma faces make-or-break series finale at Lord’s

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rohit Sharma’s poor run of form has persisted through the current series, with his latest dismissal coming after a long struggle.
  • Debate over the opener’s spot in India’s ODI World Cup squad is intensifying as selectors weigh experience against current form.
  • The Lord’s decider could be Rohit’s final chance to prove his worth before crucial selection decisions are made.

When Rohit Sharma walks out at Lord’s on Sunday, it may not just be a series decider for India. It could be the last time the veteran opener gets to settle the debate over his own place in the side.

The 39-year-old has been struggling for rhythm at the crease for weeks. His latest dismissal — caught off a delivery he would have dispatched to the boundary a few seasons ago — was a microcosm of his recent slide. The wait for a big score grows longer by the innings.

Numbers that refuse to lie

Rohit’s returns in the ongoing series have been underwhelming. He has crossed fifty only once in the last seven innings across formats, and that knock came on a flat deck where others around him cashed in far more freely. Against quality pace attacks, his footwork has appeared hesitant, his timing off.

The struggles are not just about runs. Rohit’s strike rate, once his calling card in limited-overs cricket, has dipped noticeably during the powerplay. The aggression that defined his game — putting pressure back on the bowler from the first ball — has given way to cautious prods and edges.

Debate has been raging over whether India can afford to carry a struggling opener into the ODI World Cup. With the tournament less than four months away, every innings is being scrutinised. And at Lord’s, the scrutiny will be at its peak.

Selection jigsaw getting tighter

India’s top-order options have never been more crowded. Shubman Gill has made the No. 3 slot his own. Yashasvi Jaiswal is waiting in the wings, fresh off a domestic season where he piled on runs at will. Ishan Kishan, too, has staked a claim with a string of impactful knocks lower down.

Rohit’s experience, leadership and ability to dominate on his day still count for plenty. But selectors are now weighing those intangibles against cold, hard numbers. The selectors have not publicly indicated a deadline, but sources within the camp suggest the Lord’s game is being viewed as a critical checkpoint.

“No one is doubting Rohit’s class,” a team insider was quoted as saying. “But at this level, you need to show it on the park, not just in the nets. The management wants to give him every chance, but there is a limit.”

What a big knock at Lord’s would mean

A vintage Rohit innings at cricket’s most famous address would silence the noise. The ground holds special memories for him — he scored his first Test double-century there in 2021, a knock that announced his arrival as a genuine all-format great. Another Lord’s special, this time in the blue of India’s ODI side, would buy him the time and trust he needs.

But if the struggles continue, the calls for change will become impossible to ignore. India’s bench strength means the team will not be weaker without him — but it will lose an aura that few current players can match.

Rohit Sharma has overcome slumps before. The question is whether he can summon one more resurrection when it matters most.

What to watch: All eyes at Lord’s will be on how Rohit handles the new ball in the first 10 overs. If he gets a start, the pressure shifts back to the selectors. If he doesn’t, the debate over his World Cup spot will only get louder.
