Japan Reclassifies Bitcoin, Crypto as Financial Assets in Landmark Vote

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Japan's parliament voted to reclassify Bitcoin and cryptocurrencies as financial assets under the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act.
  • The change brings digital assets under stricter regulatory oversight, including disclosure and investor protection rules.
  • The law is expected to impact taxation, with potential alignment to capital gains treatment for other financial assets.

Parliament Passes Historic Bill

Japan's parliament has voted to reclassify Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies as financial assets, a landmark decision that places digital currencies under the country's Financial Instruments and Exchange Act. The bill, passed on Wednesday, marks one of the most significant regulatory shifts for crypto in Asia's third-largest economy.

The move effectively ends the previous legal ambiguity that treated cryptocurrencies more as a payment method or commodity. From now on, crypto assets will be subject to the same regulatory framework as stocks, bonds and other financial instruments.

What the New Law Means

Under the revised law, crypto exchanges and custodians must comply with stricter disclosure requirements, investor protection rules and anti-fraud provisions. The Financial Services Agency (FSA) will now have broader authority to supervise and sanction market participants.

Industry insiders say the reclassification could also simplify tax treatment. Currently, crypto gains are taxed as miscellaneous income, with rates as high as 55%. The new framework is expected to align crypto taxation with capital gains, which are taxed at a flat 20% for most investors.

Market Reaction

Bitcoin's price saw a modest uptick following the announcement, gaining roughly 2% in Asian trading hours. Analysts attribute the subdued reaction to the fact that the bill had been widely expected for weeks.

Japan has long been a testbed for crypto regulation. In 2017, it became one of the first countries to legally recognise Bitcoin as a payment method. The latest move signals a shift toward treating digital assets as mainstream investment vehicles rather than niche alternatives.

Global Implications

The decision could influence other developed economies that are still debating how to classify crypto assets. The European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) framework and the US Securities and Exchange Commission's enforcement actions have both struggled with the question of whether crypto is a security, a commodity or something else entirely.

Japan's approach offers a clear, legislative answer: crypto is a financial asset, subject to securities-like rules. That clarity could attract institutional investors who have been hesitant due to regulatory uncertainty.

What Critics Say

Not everyone is celebrating. Some crypto advocates argue that treating digital assets as financial instruments could stifle innovation and impose compliance costs on smaller projects. Others worry that the new rules could push some crypto businesses out of Japan altogether.

The FSA has said it will issue detailed guidance over the coming months to address these concerns. The agency has also indicated it will take a phased approach to enforcement, giving firms time to adapt.

What Happens Next

The law takes effect on a date yet to be announced, likely within six months. The FSA is expected to release its implementation roadmap by the end of the year. Investors and exchanges will be watching closely for the final tax treatment and reporting requirements.
