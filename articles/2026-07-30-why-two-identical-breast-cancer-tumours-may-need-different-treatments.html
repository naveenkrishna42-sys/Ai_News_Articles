Why Two Identical Breast Cancer Tumours May Need Different Treatments

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Identical-looking tumours can have different molecular profiles, affecting treatment response.
  • Personalised medicine based on genetic testing is becoming crucial in breast cancer care.
  • Study highlights need for routine molecular profiling to avoid ineffective treatments.

Same Appearance, Different Biology

Two breast cancer tumours may appear identical under a microscope, yet they can demand entirely different treatment strategies. This is not a rare anomaly, but a growing concern that is reshaping oncology.

A new report from Express Healthcare reveals that the visual similarity of tumours often masks distinct molecular signatures. These differences can determine whether a patient responds to hormone therapy, targeted drugs, or chemotherapy.

Molecular Profiling Over Microscope

Doctors have long relied on histopathology — examining tissue samples under a lens — to classify breast cancer. But the study underscores that this approach has limits.

For instance, two tumours classified as the same subtype may have different genetic mutations. One might have a HER2 amplification, while the other does not. Without molecular testing, a patient could receive an ineffective or even harmful treatment.

Experts now recommend routine genomic profiling for all breast cancer patients. This helps identify actionable mutations and tailor therapy accordingly.

Implications for Indian Patients

In India, breast cancer is the most common cancer among women, with over 1.5 lakh new cases reported annually. The cost and accessibility of molecular testing remain barriers.

However, several hospital chains and diagnostic labs are now offering affordable panels. The study's authors argue that investing in such tests can save both lives and money by avoiding trial-and-error treatments.

Doctors also stress that patients should ask about molecular testing. A tumour's appearance alone is no longer a reliable guide for care.

Toward Personalised Medicine

The findings reinforce a broader shift in cancer care: away from one-size-fits-all protocols and toward personalised medicine. Treatment decisions are increasingly based on a tumour's genetic blueprint rather than its shape or size.

Clinical trials are now testing drugs based on biomarkers, not just cancer type. The result is better outcomes and fewer side effects.

Yet challenges remain. Not all mutations have approved therapies, and some tests are still too expensive for widespread use.

What is clear is that the era of treating all breast cancers the same way is ending. The next step is making molecular profiling a standard part of diagnosis, not an optional extra.
