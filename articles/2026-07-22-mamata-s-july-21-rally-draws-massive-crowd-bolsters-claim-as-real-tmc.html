Mamata’s July 21 Rally Draws Massive Crowd, Bolsters Claim as Real TMC

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mamata Banerjee’s July 21 rally at Kalighat saw a huge turnout, asserting her leadership of the Trinamool Congress.
  • The rally countered a separate event by TMC rebels, who held their own Martyrs’ Day gathering.
  • Mamata urged INDIA bloc unity and called the rebel faction the BJP's 'B-team'.
  • Rebel MLA Madan Mitra gave Mamata a one-month ultimatum to distance herself from Abhishek Banerjee.

Show of Strength at Kalighat

Kolkata’s Kalighat ground turned into a sea of blue and white on July 21. Mamata Banerjee’s annual Martyrs’ Day rally drew a crowd that organisers said was among the largest in recent years. The footfall was a direct response to the rebel Trinamool Congress faction that had held its own rally earlier in the day.

The chief minister used the platform to make a simple point: she is the real face of the party. The sheer size of the gathering, she argued, proved that the loyalty of the party’s grassroots workers remains with her. The event was designed to project unity, with senior leaders and MPs sharing the stage.

Countering the Rebel Narrative

The day began with a separate rally by dissident MLAs at another venue in the city. That gathering, however, failed to generate the same energy. Rebel MLA Madan Mitra, a former minister, used the occasion to serve an ultimatum. He gave Mamata Banerjee one month to drop party general secretary Abhishek Banerjee from key decision-making roles.

Mitra’s demand was clear: return to the party’s old guard or face a continued split. The rebel faction has been positioning itself as the true inheritor of the Trinamool legacy. But Wednesday’s rally at Kalighat was a pointed rebuttal. The message from the crowd was unambiguous—they stand with Mamata.

Political Rhetoric and Bloc Strategy

From the podium, Mamata Banerjee did not mince words. She accused the rebel faction of being the BJP’s “B-team”, claiming they are being propped up to keep Suvendu Adhikari, the Leader of Opposition, politically relevant. “They are not fighting for the party. They are fighting for a handful of power-hungry people,” she said.

The chief minister also turned her focus to national politics. She urged the INDIA bloc to remain united against the BJP. “We must fight together. There is no room for division,” she said. Her remarks came amid speculation about strain within the opposition alliance. By calling for unity, she signalled that her focus remains on the 2026 assembly elections.

Grassroots Verdict

For party workers, the day was a morale booster. Many who spoke to reporters said they had come to “protect the real TMC”. The presence of first-time voters and women in large numbers was noticeable. Organisers claimed the crowd exceeded expectations, though no official count was provided.

The rebel faction’s rally, by contrast, drew a much smaller audience. Political analysts noted that the dissidents have failed to build a mass base. Their support remains limited to a handful of disgruntled MLAs. Wednesday’s events suggest that the battle for the party’s soul is far from over, but the numerical advantage clearly lies with Mamata Banerjee.

What happens next: The rebel faction is likely to hold more meetings in the coming weeks. Mamata Banerjee’s next test will be the party’s organisational elections, where the real battle for control could play out.
