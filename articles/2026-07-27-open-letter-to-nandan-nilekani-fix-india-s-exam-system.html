Open Letter to Nandan Nilekani: Fix India's Exam System

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • The letter addresses Nandan Nilekani, known for Aadhaar and digital governance, to lead exam reform.
  • It points to issues like paper leaks, coaching industry pressure, and high-stakes stress.
  • The letter suggests tech-based solutions for secure, fair, and continuous assessment.

The Open Letter That Has Sparked Debate

An open letter addressed to Nandan Nilekani has stirred a much-needed conversation about the state of India's examination system. The letter, published by NDTV, describes the current situation as having reached 'a breaking point.' It calls on Nilekani, the Infosys co-founder and architect of Aadhaar, to bring his expertise in technology and governance to reform how millions of students are tested every year.

The letter does not name its author but is written in a tone of urgent appeal. It argues that the exam system has become a source of immense anxiety and unfairness, rather than a genuine measure of learning. The plea is for Nilekani to lead a task force or advisory group that can design a more robust, transparent, and student-friendly assessment framework.

Systemic Failures and the Coaching Industry

The letter highlights several deep-rooted problems. Paper leaks have become almost routine, disrupting the lives of lakhs of students who prepare for months. The reliance on a single, high-stakes exam for college admissions or government jobs creates immense pressure. This has fuelled a multi-crore coaching industry that often teaches to the test, not to build understanding.

Students from smaller towns and rural areas are at a disadvantage. They lack access to expensive coaching centres or reliable internet for online tests. The letter argues that the current system rewards rote memorisation and coaching shortcuts, not critical thinking or creativity. It calls for a shift towards continuous assessment and competency-based testing that is fairer to all.

Technology as a Solution, Not a Gimmick

The open letter suggests that Nilekani's experience with Aadhaar and digital public infrastructure can be applied to exams. It proposes a secure, decentralised testing platform that uses biometric authentication to prevent impersonation. Blockchain could be used to store results and question papers, making tampering nearly impossible.

The letter also advocates for adaptive testing, where the difficulty of questions adjusts based on the student's performance. This would reduce the need for lengthy, one-size-fits-all papers. It recommends that states and central boards collaborate on a common digital framework, rather than maintaining separate, often leak-prone systems. The goal is to make exams a tool for learning, not a gatekeeping barrier.

What Nilekani's Role Could Be

Nandan Nilekani has not publicly responded to the letter. However, his track record suggests he is open to such challenges. He led the Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) and helped build the India Stack, a set of open APIs that revolutionised digital payments and identity verification. The letter argues that a similar approach could transform the exam ecosystem.

It stops short of demanding a specific appointment. Instead, it asks Nilekani to use his influence to convene experts from education, technology, and psychology. The letter stresses that any reform must be inclusive, especially for students with disabilities and those from disadvantaged backgrounds. It warns against treating technology as a magic bullet without addressing underlying issues of equity and mental health.

The open letter has already generated discussion on social media and among education activists. Many agree that the system needs a fundamental overhaul, not just incremental fixes. The ball is now in Nilekani's court, and the country is watching to see if he will take up the cause.
