Indian-origin man faces losing US citizenship over 3-decade-old identity fraud

📅 2026-08-12 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Indian-origin man accused of identity fraud after living in the US for 30 years
  • He was deported in 1995 but later became a US citizen in 2008
  • US authorities are seeking to revoke his citizenship over the alleged fraud

A long-hidden past surfaces

For more than three decades, an Indian-origin man built a life in the United States. He entered the country years ago, eventually becoming a naturalised citizen in 2008. Now, federal authorities allege that his path to citizenship was built on a lie — and they want to strip that citizenship away.

The case, which has drawn attention in both India and the US, centres on accusations of identity fraud. According to court records, the man was deported from the US in 1995. Yet, years later, he managed to secure American citizenship under a different identity. How he slipped through the system remains a key question.

The accusation: identity fraud

US prosecutors have accused the man of using a false identity to obtain immigration benefits. The alleged fraud spans years, involving documents and personal details that did not match his original records. If proven, the charge could lead to denaturalisation — a rare but serious legal step.

Denaturalisation is not a criminal penalty but a civil proceeding. It essentially nullifies a person's citizenship, leaving them vulnerable to deportation. In recent years, US authorities have stepped up efforts to identify individuals who acquired citizenship through fraudulent means.

A timeline of key events

  • Entry into the US: over 30 years ago
  • Deportation order: 1995
  • Citizenship grant: 2008
  • Current legal action: citizenship revocation sought

Authorities have not disclosed the man's current whereabouts or whether he has been detained. Officials have not yet commented on the specifics of the alleged fraud, citing the ongoing legal process.

Broader immigration crackdown

The case comes amid a wider US crackdown on immigration fraud. In recent months, the Justice Department has pursued similar actions against individuals accused of using fake IDs to gain entry. One such case involved a Pakistani man whose citizenship was sought to be revoked in a Texas district court.

Advocacy groups have called for transparency and due process in such cases. The National Association for Protection of Americans (NAPA) has urged US authorities to ensure that deportees and those facing denaturalisation are treated fairly. The organisation has highlighted concerns about the speed and opacity of the process.

What happens next?

The man's legal team is expected to contest the charges. A court hearing will determine whether the citizenship revocation proceeds. If the court rules against him, he could face deportation to India — a country he left decades ago.

Legal experts note that denaturalisation cases are complex and often take months, if not years, to resolve. The outcome will hinge on evidence of intent — whether the man knowingly deceived authorities or was a victim of circumstances.

For now, the case serves as a reminder of the long arm of immigration law. As US authorities continue to scrutinise old records, more such cases may surface. The coming months will reveal how this particular story unfolds — and what it means for others who entered the US under questionable circumstances.
