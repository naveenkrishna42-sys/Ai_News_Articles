Alphabet Q2 Earnings Beat Estimates, But Record Capex Spooks Investors

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Alphabet reported Q2 2026 revenue above analyst expectations, led by enterprise AI services.
  • The company announced a significant increase in capital expenditure for 2026, spooking investors.
  • GOOGL stock dropped in after-hours trading despite the earnings beat, reflecting capex concerns.

Alphabet Inc. delivered a strong second-quarter earnings report on Wednesday, beating revenue estimates as enterprise adoption of its artificial intelligence tools surged. But the celebration was short-lived. Investors focused on a sharp hike in planned capital expenditure for 2026, sending shares of the Google parent company lower in after-hours trading.

The mixed market reaction underscores a growing tension on Wall Street: how much is too much to spend on the AI arms race?

Enterprise AI Drives Revenue Beat

Alphabet reported Q2 2026 revenue that exceeded consensus estimates, driven largely by a surge in enterprise AI services. Google Cloud saw accelerated growth as businesses integrated the company's generative AI models into their operations. The company's core advertising business also held steady, contributing to a solid top-line performance.

CEO Sundar Pichai highlighted the momentum during the earnings call, pointing to strong demand from corporate clients. The beat marks the third consecutive quarter where Alphabet has outperformed on revenue, signaling that its heavy bet on AI is beginning to pay off in commercial contracts.

Capex Hike Rattles Investors

Despite the earnings beat, the market's attention quickly turned to Alphabet's updated capital expenditure forecast. The company outlined a significantly higher capex plan for 2026, citing investments in data centers, AI chips, and infrastructure to support its expanding cloud and AI businesses.

The news triggered a sell-off in after-hours trading. GOOGL stock dropped by more than 4% as analysts and investors digested the scale of the spending. The move raises the question of whether Alphabet is burning through cash too quickly in its race against competitors like Microsoft and Amazon.

“The market is rewarding the revenue beat but punishing the spending outlook,” one analyst noted. “Investors want to see a clearer path to returns on these massive investments.”

Spiralling AI Costs and Market Jitters

Alphabet is not alone in facing this scrutiny. The broader tech sector has seen a pattern where earnings beats are overshadowed by rising capital intensity. Microsoft and Amazon have also reported higher capex in recent quarters, but Alphabet's latest forecast appears to have caught investors off guard.

The company is spending heavily on custom tensor processing units and expanding its global data center footprint. Internal estimates suggest the AI infrastructure buildout will accelerate through 2027. While the long-term strategy is clear, the near-term financial drag has left some shareholders uneasy.

Google's management defended the spending as essential to maintaining its competitive edge. Executives argued that the enterprise AI opportunity is still in its early stages and that under-investing now would be a bigger risk.

What to Watch

All eyes will be on Alphabet's next earnings call for signs that the capex cycle is peaking. Investors will also watch for growth in cloud margins as a measure of whether the AI spending is translating into profitable revenue. The stock's near-term direction may depend on how quickly the market rebalances its view of spending versus growth.
