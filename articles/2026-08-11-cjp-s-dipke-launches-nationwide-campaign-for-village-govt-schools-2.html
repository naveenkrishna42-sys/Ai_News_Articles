CJP's Dipke launches nationwide campaign for village govt schools

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • CJP founder Abhijeet Dipke announces nationwide campaign for village government schools.
  • Campaign begins with a 'social audit' of rural schools on August 15.
  • Dipke questions why new Parliament and PM house exist but not new schools in villages.

Abhijeet Dipke, founder of the CJP, has announced a nationwide campaign to overhaul government schools in rural India. The move comes amid growing concerns over the state of basic amenities in villages, with Dipke questioning the disparity between urban infrastructure and rural neglect.

Speaking to reporters, Dipke pointed out that while the country has seen new Parliament buildings and a new Prime Minister's residence, government schools in many villages continue to lack even basic facilities. His remarks underscore a widening gap in educational infrastructure that has long been a point of contention for activists and educators.

Social audit on August 15

The campaign will kick off with what Dipke describes as a 'social audit' of rural schools, scheduled for August 15. The initiative aims to inspect the condition of schools across the country, focusing on availability of clean drinking water, functional toilets, and classroom infrastructure.

Dipke has urged panchayat heads and local bodies to participate actively in the audit, emphasizing that the responsibility of improving schools lies not just with the government but also with the community. He has called on citizens to document and report the state of their local schools, creating a grassroots-level database of deficiencies.

Panchayat heads urged to repair schools

In a direct appeal, Dipke has asked panchayat heads to take immediate steps to repair and renovate government schools in their respective areas. He noted that many schools suffer from broken windows, leaking roofs, and inadequate furniture, which directly impacts the learning environment for children.

The CJP founder stressed that the campaign is not merely about demanding government action but about fostering a sense of ownership among villagers. He believes that local participation is crucial to ensuring sustainable improvements in the long run.

Broader questions on rural education

Dipke's campaign comes at a time when rural education in India faces significant challenges. Despite various government schemes, many schools in remote areas still struggle with basic infrastructure and teacher shortages. The social audit is expected to highlight these issues on a national scale.

The campaign has already drawn attention from political circles, with some viewing it as a potential precursor to a larger movement on rural development. However, Dipke has maintained that the focus remains squarely on education and the welfare of children in villages.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the government will respond to the audit findings. The CJP has stated that the results of the August 15 audit will be compiled and presented to relevant authorities, with a demand for immediate remedial action.

What to watch

The success of the campaign will depend on the level of public participation in the social audit and the subsequent response from both panchayats and state governments. As August 15 approaches, all eyes will be on how many villages take part and what the findings reveal about the true state of rural education in India.
