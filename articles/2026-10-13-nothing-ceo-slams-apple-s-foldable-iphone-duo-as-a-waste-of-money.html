Nothing CEO Slams Apple's Foldable iPhone Duo as a 'Waste of Money'

  • Industry estimates place the iPhone Duo at $1,999–$2,399 (roughly ₹1.7–2 lakh), up to double Apple's own top-end slab iPhone.

Pei's Attack Lands Days Before Pre-Orders

Carl Pei, chief executive of London-headquartered smartphone maker Nothing, has publicly dismissed Apple's first foldable — branded the iPhone Duo — as “a waste of money.” The remark, reported by BigGo Finance and picked up by PhoneArena, arrives days before pre-orders open on October 16. It turns Apple's biggest hardware bet of the year into an open industry feud.

The timing is deliberate. Forbes has already ranked iPhone Duo prices across global markets, and Visual Capitalist has charted the same data, signalling unusual scrutiny for a first-generation device. Pei's intervention adds a combative voice from a founder who once built OnePlus into the original “flagship killer.”

Why This Criticism Carries Weight

Pei is not a fringe commentator. He co-founded OnePlus before launching Nothing in 2020, and his company has deliberately skipped foldables, betting instead on transparent design and aggressive pricing. Nothing's flagship Phone (3) launched at US$799 (₹79,999), a price that undercuts any credible foldable by a wide margin.

His argument mirrors long-standing buyer concerns: heavier bodies, fragile hinges, visible creases and upgrade costs that are hard to justify. Nothing's position is that most users extract more value from a conventional slab phone than from a device that folds, however elegant the engineering.

What Buyers Are Actually Getting

Reporting through 2025 and 2026 pointed to a book-style foldable with an inner display of roughly eight inches, a crease-free panel supplied by Samsung Display and a slim cover screen. PhoneArena adds that Apple will offer a special in-store extra for buyers who show up on day one, a retail tactic the company has used in past launches to drive footfall.

Forbes' market-by-market ranking shows the real cost varies sharply by region. Indian buyers should expect official pricing well above dollar conversions once customs duty and GST apply — a gap that can equal the price of an entire mid-range phone.

The Pricing Problem at the Heart of the Feud

Industry estimates through 2026 placed the iPhone Duo in the $1,999–$2,399 band, up to about double Apple's own top-end slab phone, the iPhone 17 Pro Max. Samsung's premium foldables occupy a similar band, which is precisely Pei's point: the category charges a heavy tax for a different shape.

For buyers, the maths matters more than the rhetoric. If a foldable's inner screen genuinely replaces a tablet for reading, editing and travel, the premium can be rationalised. If it mostly stays folded, a slab flagship delivers equivalent performance at half the cost.

The Buyer's Verdict

Pei's claim is opinion, but it aligns with measurable pain points. Foldables still trade battery life and durability for screen area, and first-generation devices carry resale and repair risk. Apple's build quality and app optimisation could offset much of that — yet only real-world crease tests, battery benchmarks and six-month resale data will settle the argument.

  • Key positives: Apple's first foldable with expected deep iOS app continuity, a reported crease-free ~8-inch inner panel, an exclusive in-store launch perk and Apple's strong service and resale network.
  • Key risks: estimated $1,999–$2,399 pricing (₹1.7–2 lakh), first-generation hardware unknowns, unproven foldable battery life, and Samsung's seven-year head start with the Z Fold line.
  • Value alternative: Nothing's Phone (3) at US$799 targets buyers who want flagship performance without paying for the fold.

What Happens Next

Pre-orders open on October 16, and early sell-through will show whether Apple has converted curiosity into demand. If queues match the pull of the in-store perk, Pei's framing will look premature; if returns mount, Nothing's scepticism will age well. Either way, the foldable debate now has two of the industry's sharpest operators on opposite sides.
