Samsung and Google unveil Android XR glasses with 9-hour battery life

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Samsung Galaxy Glasses run on Android XR, co-developed with Google
  • Battery life rated at 9 hours for mixed use
  • Device integrates tightly with Galaxy phones, watches, and buds

The first Android XR glasses arrive

Samsung and Google have taken the wraps off the Galaxy Glasses, the first consumer device running Android XR. Unveiled at Samsung’s Unpacked event, the smart glasses aim to bring the Galaxy ecosystem into everyday eyewear.

The device marks a direct entry into a market currently dominated by Meta’s Ray-Ban Stories line. Unlike bulky headsets, these look and feel like a pair of regular spectacles.

Battery life that lasts the workday

One of the headline numbers is the 9-hour battery life. Samsung claims this covers mixed usage — a blend of notifications, voice commands, and occasional augmented reality overlays. That puts it ahead of most current smart glasses, which typically struggle to cross the 4- to 5-hour mark.

Charging is handled through a custom case, similar to wireless earbuds. The glasses themselves pack a Qualcomm chipset optimised for low-power XR workloads.

Deep integration with Galaxy devices

The Galaxy Glasses are not standalone devices. They pair primarily with Samsung phones, but also work with Galaxy Watches and Buds for seamless handoff. Notifications appear as floating text in the wearer’s field of view, and calls can be answered via bone conduction speakers built into the temples.

Google’s Android XR platform brings native access to Google Maps, Google Photos, and YouTube. Users can get turn-by-turn directions projected onto the real world or view photo slideshows without pulling out a phone.

Camera and AI features

A 12-megapixel camera sits on the right temple, capable of recording 1080p video. Google’s Gemini AI powers on-device translation, object recognition, and real-time captioning. Samsung has also added its own Galaxy AI layer for tasks like summarising messages or identifying landmarks.

Privacy was a clear focus. A small LED glows when the camera is active, and there is a hardware switch to physically disable the camera and microphone.

Going up against Meta

Meta has sold millions of its Ray-Ban Stories and newer Ray-Ban Meta glasses. Samsung and Google are betting that tighter ecosystem lock-in and longer battery life will persuade users to switch.

Pricing has not been officially confirmed, but industry watchers expect it to land around the Rs 25,000–30,000 range in India. Availability is slated for later this year, starting with the US and South Korea.

Developers will get access to the Android XR SDK from next month. That could determine how fast the app ecosystem grows — and whether the glasses become a daily essential or a niche gadget.
