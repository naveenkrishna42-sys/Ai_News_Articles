GHMC 10-day monsoon drive from August 11 to tackle waterlogging

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • GHMC will launch a 10-day monsoon drive starting August 11.
  • The drive focuses on desilting drains and clearing blockages to prevent waterlogging.
  • Residents can report issues through GHMC's helpline and mobile app.

Pre-monsoon preparedness gets a push

The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) is set to launch a 10-day special monsoon drive starting August 11. The initiative aims to tackle waterlogging and ensure smooth drainage across the city ahead of heavy rains.

Officials said the drive will focus on desilting stormwater drains, removing encroachments, and clearing garbage that often blocks water flow. Teams have been instructed to work in all zones, with special attention to low-lying areas that have historically faced flooding.

What the drive will cover

The drive will involve round-the-clock monitoring of critical points, including underpasses, culverts, and major road stretches. GHMC staff will also inspect and repair damaged manholes and replace missing grates to prevent accidents during rains.

In addition, the civic body will deploy mobile pumps and vacuum machines in vulnerable neighbourhoods. These will be stationed at identified spots to quickly remove accumulated water after a downpour.

How residents can help

GHMC has urged citizens to cooperate by not dumping solid waste into drains or stormwater channels. Residents can report blockages, overflowing manholes, or water stagnation through the GHMC helpline or the MyGHMC mobile app.

Officials said complaints registered during the drive will be prioritised and resolved within a stipulated time frame. Regular updates on the progress of the drive will be shared on GHMC's official social media handles.

Past issues and this year's approach

Hyderabad has witnessed severe waterlogging in several areas during previous monsoon seasons, leading to traffic snarls and inconvenience. The GHMC has faced criticism for lack of preparedness in some instances, prompting this year's more structured approach.

The 10-day window is seen as a preparatory phase before the peak of the monsoon. The civic body has also coordinated with the Hyderabad Metro Water Board and the Disaster Response Force to ensure quick response during emergencies.

Officials have not yet confirmed how many teams will be deployed, but they indicated that all 150 GHMC circles will be covered. The drive is expected to conclude on August 20, with a review meeting to assess the outcomes.

What to watch

Over the next two weeks, residents will be watching how effectively GHMC translates its plan into action. The success of the drive will likely set the tone for the rest of the monsoon season, and any lapses could draw sharp public scrutiny.
