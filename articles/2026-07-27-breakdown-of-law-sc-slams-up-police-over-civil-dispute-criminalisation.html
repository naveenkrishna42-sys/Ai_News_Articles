‘Breakdown of law’: SC slams UP Police over civil dispute criminalisation

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court termed UP Police's action a 'breakdown of law' for criminalising a civil property dispute.
  • The court noted that multiple FIRs were filed against the same parties in different districts, abusing legal process.
  • A bench of Justices ordered the consolidation of cases and warned against misuse of police power.

The Supreme Court on Tuesday sharply criticised the Uttar Pradesh Police for converting a civil property dispute into multiple criminal cases, describing the practice as a 'breakdown of law'. A bench of Justices remarked that the police action amounted to an abuse of the legal process and undermined the rule of law.

Multiple FIRs over same property dispute

The case involved a property disagreement between two private parties. Instead of treating it as a civil matter, the police registered several First Information Reports across different districts of Uttar Pradesh against the same individuals. This led to the petitioners being forced to appear before multiple courts, facing repeated harassment.

The bench noted that such tactics are increasingly being used to pressure litigants. 'This is a clear breakdown of law. You cannot turn a civil dispute into a criminal case just because you have the power to register an FIR,' the court observed.

Abuse of police machinery

The Supreme Court pointed out that the police machinery was being misused to settle personal scores. The judges expressed concern over the rising trend of filing multiple FIRs in different jurisdictions based on the same set of facts. This not only clogs the criminal justice system but also causes immense hardship to the accused.

The court stated that the police must act as neutral arbiters and not as tools in the hands of one party. 'When a civil remedy is available, the police must not rush to register criminal cases,' the bench emphasised.

Directions for consolidation

To address the immediate grievance, the Supreme Court directed that all FIRs arising from the same property dispute be consolidated and transferred to a single police station. The court also ordered that no further FIRs be registered against the petitioners in connection with this matter without prior approval from the supervising officer.

The bench warned that if such misuse continues, it may consider imposing costs on the errant police officers. The matter has been listed for further hearing after six weeks to ensure compliance with the court's orders.

Legal experts say this judgment sends a strong message to law enforcement agencies across the country. The distinction between civil wrongs and criminal offences must be respected to prevent harassment of citizens.

What happens next will depend on how strictly the Uttar Pradesh Police implements the Supreme Court's directions. The court has made it clear that it will not tolerate any further abuse of the legal process in this case.
