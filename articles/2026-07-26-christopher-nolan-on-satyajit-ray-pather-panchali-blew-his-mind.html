Christopher Nolan on Satyajit Ray: Pather Panchali 'blew his mind'

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Christopher Nolan called Satyajit Ray one of the greatest Indian filmmakers.
  • Nolan said Pather Panchali from The Apu Trilogy 'blew his mind'.
  • The director highlighted Ray's storytelling and visual style as deeply influential.

Nolan's admiration for Ray

Christopher Nolan, the acclaimed director of Oppenheimer and Inception, has spoken at length about his admiration for Indian cinema legend Satyajit Ray. In a recent interview, Nolan described Ray as 'one of the greatest Indian filmmakers' and singled out Pather Panchali from The Apu Trilogy as a film that left a deep impression on him.

The Hollywood director said the film 'blew his mind' and called it 'absolutely incredible'. Nolan's comments have generated buzz in film circles, with many noting the global recognition of Ray's work.

The impact of Pather Panchali

Nolan elaborated on why Pather Panchali had such a lasting impact on him. He praised Ray's ability to tell a deeply human story with minimal resources, highlighting the film's visual storytelling and emotional depth. 'It just feels so authentic and real,' Nolan said, according to reports.

The Apu Trilogy, which includes Pather Panchali (1955), Aparajito (1956), and The World of Apu (1959), is widely regarded as Ray's masterpiece. The films follow the life of a young boy named Apu from childhood to adulthood, set against the backdrop of rural Bengal.

Global recognition for Ray

Satyajit Ray remains one of India's most internationally celebrated filmmakers. His work has influenced directors across the world, including Martin Scorsese, Akira Kurosawa, and now Christopher Nolan. Ray received an honorary Oscar in 1992, just a year before his death.

Nolan's latest comments add to the growing chorus of global filmmakers acknowledging Ray's genius. The director's praise comes at a time when Indian cinema is gaining more international attention, with films like RRR and All Quiet on the Western Front (though not Indian) winning Oscars.

What Nolan said about Ray

In the interview, Nolan was asked about filmmakers who have influenced his work. He did not hesitate to name Ray. 'Satyajit Ray's Pather Panchali is a film that blew my mind. The way he captures life, the simplicity, the complexity — it's all there,' Nolan said.

He also praised The Apu Trilogy as a whole, calling it 'absolutely incredible'. Nolan's comments were shared widely on social media, with fans of both directors celebrating the cross-cultural appreciation.

This is not the first time Nolan has spoken about Indian cinema. He has previously cited Indian filmmakers like Satyajit Ray and Mani Ratnam as influences, though his comments on Ray this time were particularly detailed.

What this means for Indian cinema

Nolan's praise is a reminder of the enduring legacy of Satyajit Ray. For a new generation of filmmakers and audiences, it highlights the timeless quality of Ray's storytelling. The recognition from a contemporary master like Nolan also underscores the universal appeal of Ray's films.

Indian cinema continues to evolve, but the foundations laid by pioneers like Ray remain relevant. Nolan's words may inspire more viewers to revisit The Apu Trilogy and discover why these films are considered classics.

As for what comes next, Nolan is reportedly working on his next project, details of which remain under wraps. Whether his admiration for Ray will influence his future work is yet to be seen, but the connection between the two directors is now firmly in the spotlight.
