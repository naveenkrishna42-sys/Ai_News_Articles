VK honours Karnataka changemakers at Bengaluru ceremony

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • VK Excellence & Honor event held in Bengaluru
  • Karnataka's changemakers felicitated for their contributions
  • Ceremony highlights achievements across multiple sectors

Recognising unsung heroes

BENGALURU: In a ceremony marked by quiet dignity and loud applause, the VK Excellence & Honor initiative felicitated a diverse group of Karnataka's changemakers at a city venue on Thursday. The event, which drew community leaders, professionals, and young achievers, sought to put the spotlight on individuals whose work often goes unnoticed in the daily din of headlines.

The honourees spanned fields as varied as education, healthcare, social entrepreneurship, and the arts. While the organisers did not disclose the full list of awardees, those present spoke of the rigorous selection process that prioritised grassroots impact over celebrity status.

A celebration of impact

The gathering was not merely a ceremonial affair. Between the award presentations, short films and testimonials highlighted the tangible difference the honourees have made in their communities. From running free tuition centres in rural belts to pioneering low-cost sanitation solutions in urban slums, the stories shared resonated deeply with the audience.

One awardee, a teacher from a government school in north Karnataka, received a standing ovation for her decade-long work in bringing digital literacy to first-generation learners. Another, a young entrepreneur from Mangaluru, was recognised for developing a mobile app that connects small farmers directly with wholesale buyers, cutting out exploitative middlemen.

Voices from the event

In his address, a senior organiser stressed that the initiative was born out of a simple belief: that excellence must be seen to be believed. "We are surrounded by people solving problems with limited resources and immense creativity," he said. "This is our small way of saying their work matters."

Several attendees noted that such events are particularly significant in a state as large and diverse as Karnataka, where urban prosperity often overshadows rural challenges. "It reminds us that progress is not just about the big tech parks in Bengaluru," said a city-based social worker who attended the ceremony. "It is also about the quiet, persistent efforts happening in smaller towns and villages."

Building on the momentum

The organisers have indicated that this is not a one-off exercise. Plans are already afoot to expand the scope of the initiative in the coming years, with a focus on identifying more young changemakers and providing them with mentorship and seed funding. Details of the next edition are expected to be announced later this year.

As the evening wound down, the mood was one of cautious optimism. If Thursday's ceremony is any indication, Karnataka's changemakers are no longer content to work in the shadows. And the VK Excellence & Honor platform appears determined to keep bringing them into the light.

The coming months will show whether this recognition translates into sustained support for the honourees, and whether the initiative can inspire similar efforts across other states.
