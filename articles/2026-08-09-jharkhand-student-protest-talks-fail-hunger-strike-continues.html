Jharkhand student protest: Talks fail, hunger strike continues

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Student delegation and Jharkhand government talks conclude without resolving deadlock.
  • Doctors visiting hunger-striking protesters report stable vitals but weight loss.
  • CM Hemant Soren vows justice, says 'we caught the theft'.
  • Protesters use creative methods like rate cards and poems to press demands.

Ranchi: A meeting between a student delegation and the Jharkhand government concluded on Sunday without a breakthrough, leaving thousands of protesters on an indefinite hunger strike in a tense standoff over alleged irregularities in state recruitment exams.

The talks, held in the state capital, were expected to ease the deadlock that has gripped the region for days. However, both sides emerged without a concrete agreement, and the protest at the hunger strike site continues.

Health of protesters a growing concern

Doctors who visited the protesters on Saturday said their vitals remain stable, but weight loss has been recorded among those on the fast. Medical teams have been monitoring the group, which includes several student leaders, as the strike enters its second week.

The condition of one student leader, whose health has reportedly deteriorated, has added urgency to the situation. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of protesters currently on hunger strike, but local reports suggest it is in the dozens.

Government's response and CM's assurance

Chief Minister Hemant Soren has publicly backed the students, stating, "We caught the theft, will deliver justice." His remarks, made at a public event, were seen as an attempt to reassure the protesters while the government works on a legal response.

The state government has formed a committee to investigate the alleged irregularities, but students have demanded immediate action, including cancellation of the exams and re-testing. The government has not yet agreed to these terms.

Creative protests draw attention

Amid the serious standoff, protesters have turned to creative methods to press their demands. From 'rate cards' listing alleged costs of exam papers to poems criticising the system, the protest site has become a canvas of dissent.

These efforts have drawn public sympathy and media attention, with many locals visiting the site to show support. Social media has also amplified the movement, with hashtags trending nationally.

What lies ahead

The failure of talks has set the stage for a prolonged confrontation. Student leaders have vowed to continue the hunger strike until their demands are met, while the government has appealed for patience, citing the need for due process.

All eyes are now on the next round of negotiations, though no date has been announced. The coming days will test both the students' resolve and the government's ability to balance legal procedure with public pressure.
