BDA tightens rules for builders modifying building plans in Bengaluru

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BDA has introduced new checks for builders requesting plan modifications.
  • The move aims to reduce unauthorized changes and ensure compliance with approved plans.
  • Builders must now submit additional documentation and face stricter scrutiny.

New rules for plan changes

The Bangalore Development Authority (BDA) has introduced stricter checks for builders seeking modifications to approved building plans. The new measures are designed to curb unauthorized alterations and ensure that any changes comply with existing regulations.

Builders will now have to submit additional documentation, including structural stability certificates and revised drawings, before the BDA considers their requests. Officials said the move aims to prevent deviations that could compromise safety or violate zoning laws.

Why the crackdown?

Recent inspections by the BDA revealed several instances where builders made significant changes to approved plans without proper approval. These included adding extra floors, altering setbacks, and changing the building's use.

Such violations not only flout building bylaws but also pose safety risks to occupants and surrounding properties. The new checks are expected to act as a deterrent, making it harder for builders to bypass regulations.

Impact on builders

Real estate developers have expressed concern that the tighter norms could delay project timelines. Many argue that minor modifications are sometimes necessary due to site conditions or client requirements.

However, BDA officials maintain that the new process is not meant to be obstructive but to ensure accountability. Builders who comply with the rules will still get approvals, though the process will be more thorough.

What the new process entails

Under the updated procedure, builders must submit a detailed justification for each proposed modification. The BDA will also conduct site inspections to verify that the changes align with the submitted documents.

Any deviation found during inspection could lead to penalties or revocation of the plan approval. The authority has also warned that repeat offenders may face stricter action, including blacklisting.

Residents' welfare associations have welcomed the move, saying it will help maintain the city's planned development. They hope the checks will reduce instances of illegal constructions that often burden local infrastructure.

The BDA has not yet specified how long the new approval process will take. Builders are advised to factor in additional time when planning projects.
