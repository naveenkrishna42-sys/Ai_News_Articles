Pakistan Accused of Killing 30 Unarmed Protesters in Kashmir

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 30 unarmed protesters have been killed by Pakistani forces in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK), according to accusations.
  • The Indian Ministry of External Affairs rejected the recent Pakistan-organised polls in PoK as a cosmetic exercise.
  • Clashes erupted on election day, with local reports claiming 14 activists killed and dozens injured.
  • Pakistan minister Khawaja Asif called the protesters 'enemies like India', escalating tensions.

Pakistan stands accused of fatally shooting at least 30 unarmed protesters in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK), in what witnesses describe as a brutal crackdown. The violence, which erupted on the day of controversial local elections, has drawn sharp condemnation from New Delhi and human rights groups.

The Guardian reported that the area was "filled with dead bodies" as security forces opened fire on demonstrators. Local activists claim the death toll may be higher, with many injured and dozens missing. Officials in PoK have not yet confirmed the exact number of casualties.

MEA Rejects PoK Polls as 'Cosmetic Exercise'

The Indian Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) dismissed the Pakistan-organised elections in PoK as a "cosmetic exercise to camouflage illegal occupation." In a strongly worded statement, the MEA reiterated that the entire region of Jammu and Kashmir is an integral part of India.

India has consistently opposed any political process in PoK, which it considers territory illegally occupied by Pakistan. The latest crackdown has only reinforced New Delhi's position that the polls lacked legitimacy and were conducted under duress.

Election Day Clashes Leave Dozen Dead

On the day of the polls, violence escalated as security forces clashed with protesters. Telegraph India reported that at least 14 activists were killed and dozens more injured. Eyewitnesses described chaotic scenes with security forces using live ammunition to disperse crowds.

The protesters were demanding greater autonomy and an end to alleged repression by the Pakistani administration. The elections, touted by Islamabad as a democratic exercise, were marred by boycotts and allegations of rigging.

Pakistan Minister Calls Protesters 'Enemies'

In a provocative statement, Pakistan minister Khawaja Asif declared that "protesters of Azad Kashmir are enemies like India." The comment has further inflamed tensions, with critics accusing the government of treating legitimate dissent as treason.

Asif's remarks came amid growing unrest in PoK, where many residents feel alienated from the political process. Human rights organisations have called for an independent investigation into the killings.

The international community has yet to issue a formal response, but the incident is likely to escalate diplomatic tensions between India and Pakistan. The United Nations has previously called for restraint in the region.

Authorities in PoK have imposed a curfew and suspended mobile internet services to prevent further unrest. It remains unclear when the situation will normalise.

The coming days will reveal whether the Pakistani government will allow an independent probe into the shootings or continue to suppress dissent. The world is watching closely as the death toll mounts and calls for justice grow louder.
