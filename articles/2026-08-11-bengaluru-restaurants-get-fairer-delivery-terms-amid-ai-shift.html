Bengaluru restaurants get fairer delivery terms amid AI shift

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • New delivery terms offer more equitable revenue sharing for restaurants
  • AI-driven demand forecasting and dynamic pricing are reshaping operations
  • Platforms and eateries adapt to tech-led efficiency and transparency

Bengaluru's restaurant industry is witnessing a quiet but significant shift. Delivery platforms are revising terms with eateries, offering fairer revenue splits and more transparent contracts. The change comes as artificial intelligence (AI) increasingly powers everything from order prediction to delivery routing.

For years, restaurants have grumbled about high commissions and opaque algorithms. Now, with AI enabling better demand forecasting and cost optimisation, platforms are finding room to ease margins — a move that could redefine the food-tech equation in India's startup capital.

What the new terms mean

Under the revised agreements, restaurants are expected to retain a larger share of each order. Sources indicate that commission rates have been recalibrated, with some eateries seeing reductions of up to 5 percentage points. In a sector where thin margins are the norm, this is a meaningful breather.

The changes are not uniform, though. Larger chains with high order volumes are reportedly getting the most favourable deals. Smaller, independent kitchens are seeing more modest adjustments, but even they are benefiting from clearer fee structures and reduced hidden charges.

Platforms are also tying better terms to performance metrics. Restaurants that maintain quick preparation times and low cancellation rates are being rewarded with lower commissions. This, industry watchers say, pushes eateries to improve efficiency rather than just absorb costs.

AI's growing role in food delivery

Artificial intelligence is the engine behind these changes. Platforms now use machine learning algorithms to predict demand by area, time of day, and even weather. This allows them to optimise delivery fleets, reduce idle time, and cut per-order logistics costs — savings that are being passed on, at least partly, to restaurants.

Dynamic pricing is another area where AI is making inroads. Surge pricing during peak hours is becoming smarter, balancing customer willingness to pay with restaurant capacity. This helps platforms maintain profitability without squeezing restaurant margins further.

AI is also improving menu engineering. By analysing customer preferences and ordering patterns, platforms can suggest menu tweaks that boost sales. Some Bengaluru restaurants have reported a 15-20% increase in average order value after adopting these insights.

What this means for diners

For customers, the shift could translate into more stable pricing and better service. With restaurants earning more per order, they are less likely to cut corners on ingredients or packaging. Faster delivery times, driven by AI-routed logistics, are also expected to become the norm.

However, not everything is rosy. Some restaurant owners worry that AI-driven terms could become a double-edged sword. If platforms use algorithms to continually adjust commissions, smaller players could find themselves at a disadvantage. Transparency in how these metrics are calculated remains a key concern.

Industry reaction and outlook

Restaurant associations in Bengaluru have welcomed the move but are calling for more regulatory oversight. They want standardised contracts that prevent platforms from unilaterally changing terms. The National Restaurant Association of India has been pushing for a code of conduct, and this development could add momentum to that demand.

Food-tech analysts note that this is part of a broader global trend. In markets like the US and Europe, platforms are moving toward more collaborative models with restaurants, driven by both regulatory pressure and the need for sustainable growth. Bengaluru, with its dense restaurant ecosystem and tech-savvy consumer base, is well-positioned to lead this shift in India.

For now, the immediate focus is on implementation. Restaurants are reviewing their new contracts, and platforms are fine-tuning their AI systems. The coming months will reveal whether this fairer terms experiment holds up under real-world pressure — and whether it spreads beyond Bengaluru to other Indian cities.

What to watch: how small, independent restaurants fare under the new AI-linked terms, and whether regulators step in to formalise these changes industry-wide.
