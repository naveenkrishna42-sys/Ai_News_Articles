Trump threatens to 'hit Iran hard' after attack on US bases in West Asia

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says US will deliver a 'hard' response to Iran after missile attacks on American bases.
  • Iran restarted hostilities after a brief lull, striking US positions in Jordan and Iraq.
  • Oil prices surged globally following Trump's threat of retaliation.
  • US and Saudi Arabia conducted strikes on Iraq, raising fears of a wider regional war.

Trump warns of retaliation

United States President Donald Trump has threatened to 'hit Iran hard' after Iranian missile attacks targeted American bases in Jordan and Iraq. In a statement on Wednesday, Trump said Iran would 'get a beating' for the assault, which injured several US personnel.

The sharp escalation marks a new phase in the West Asia conflict, which had seen a brief pause in direct US-Iran hostilities. Trump's warning came hours after the attacks, with the president vowing a 'disproportionate response'.

Why Iran restarted the fight

Iran's decision to resume attacks after a lull has puzzled analysts. According to reports, Tehran appears to be testing the limits of Washington's patience after the US halted its own strikes earlier this month.

Some experts suggest Iran aims to disrupt US-Saudi coordination in the region. The attack on Jordanian and Iraqi bases is seen as a message that Tehran can still project power despite American pressure.

Oil prices surge

Global oil markets reacted swiftly to Trump's threat. Crude prices jumped by over 5% in Asian trading on Wednesday, reflecting fears of supply disruptions in the energy-rich region.

Analysts warn that a full-scale US-Iran war could send oil prices above $150 a barrel, hitting economies worldwide. The Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for oil shipments, remains a flashpoint.

Is war spreading?

The US and Saudi Arabia have conducted joint strikes on targets inside Iraq, according to reports. This has raised concerns that the conflict is expanding beyond Iran's borders.

Iraqi officials have condemned the strikes as a violation of sovereignty. Meanwhile, Iran-backed militias in Iraq and Syria have vowed to retaliate against any further US aggression.

The situation on the ground remains fluid. US forces in Jordan and Iraq have been placed on high alert, while diplomatic channels between Washington and Tehran appear to be closed.

What happens next depends on the scale of Trump's promised retaliation. Any direct strike on Iranian soil could trigger a wider war that draws in regional powers. For now, the world watches as the two sides brace for a possible confrontation.
