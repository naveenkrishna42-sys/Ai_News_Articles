Avika Gor hospitalised with dengue after shooting with 104-degree fever

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Avika Gor hospitalised due to dengue
  • She continued working with 104-105 degree fever
  • Husband Milind Chandwani provided health update

Actress Avika Gor has been admitted to hospital after contracting dengue, with her husband Milind Chandwani revealing that she continued shooting even as her fever touched 104 degrees. The update, shared on Saturday, has drawn concern from fans and colleagues across the entertainment industry.

Worked through high fever

According to Milind Chandwani, Avika was determined to meet her professional commitments despite her worsening condition. She reportedly shot for her projects with a temperature ranging between 104 and 105 degrees Fahrenheit, a decision that left her physically drained.

"She was unable to move or eat," Chandwani said, describing the severity of her state before hospitalisation. The couple, who married in 2023, have been open about their life together on social media, making this health scare particularly worrying for their followers.

Dengue symptoms and treatment

Dengue, a mosquito-borne viral infection, is characterised by sudden high fever, severe headaches, pain behind the eyes, joint and muscle pain, and fatigue. In severe cases, it can lead to a dangerous drop in platelet count and require hospitalisation for monitoring and supportive care.

While the specific hospital where Avika is being treated has not been disclosed, sources indicate that she is under medical supervision and receiving the necessary care. The actress's team has not yet issued an official statement regarding her current health status.

Career and public response

Avika Gor first gained national recognition as a child artist in the television serial Balika Vadhu, where her portrayal of Anandi earned her widespread acclaim. She has since appeared in films such as 1920: London and U-Turn, and has a substantial following on social media platforms.

Fans have flooded her social media timelines with get-well-soon messages, while industry peers have expressed their support. The incident has also sparked conversations about the pressure on performers to prioritise work over health, with several actors commenting on the demanding schedules prevalent in Indian television and film production.

Health warnings from experts

Medical professionals have repeatedly cautioned against ignoring persistent high fever, especially during the monsoon and post-monsoon seasons when dengue cases typically surge. Working through a fever of 104 degrees or higher can exacerbate dehydration and increase the risk of complications, they note.

In Avika's case, early hospitalisation has likely reduced the risk of severe dengue, which can be life-threatening if not treated promptly. Doctors recommend immediate medical attention when fever is accompanied by severe body ache, rashes, or a drop in platelet count.

As the monsoon season continues across several Indian states, health authorities have intensified vector-control measures, but individual precautions remain crucial. The coming days will be critical for Avika's recovery, with her family requesting privacy during this time.

Milind Chandwani has assured well-wishers that she is receiving the best possible care, but has not provided a timeline for her discharge. Fans and colleagues alike will be hoping for a swift and complete recovery for the young actress.
