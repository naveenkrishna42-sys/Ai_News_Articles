Vijay wins Tamil Nadu: How a film star stormed to power in 2026

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Actor Vijay led his party to a clear majority in the Tamil Nadu 2026 assembly elections.
  • His campaign focused on anti-corruption, welfare schemes, and youth empowerment.
  • The victory marks a historic shift from traditional Dravidian parties to a celebrity-driven movement.

Tamil Nadu woke up to a political earthquake on Tuesday. Actor Vijay, a superstar of Kollywood with a fan base that rivals any political dynasty, swept the 2026 assembly elections. His party, Tamizhaga Vetri Kazhagam (TVK), won a clear majority, ending decades of dominance by the DMK and AIADMK.

The victory was not a fluke. Vijay's campaign, launched less than two years ago, tapped into a deep well of voter frustration with corruption and dynastic politics. He promised a clean break, and millions of young voters believed him.

The making of a political star

Vijay's entry into politics was long anticipated. For years, his films carried strong social messages, often portraying him as a champion of the common man. When he formally announced his party in 2024, the machinery was already in place. Fan clubs, with lakhs of members, turned into grassroots campaign units overnight.

His rallies drew crowds that dwarfed those of veteran politicians. He spoke in simple Tamil, rarely attacking rivals directly. Instead, he focused on a promise: a corruption-free government that would deliver jobs, free education, and healthcare for all.

The campaign that clicked

The TVK manifesto was a mix of populism and pragmatism. It promised Rs 2,000 monthly dole for women, free bus travel for students, and a special fund for small businesses. But the real draw was Vijay himself. He did not just campaign; he performed. His roadshows were choreographed like film sequences, with music, lights, and thousands of supporters in branded T-shirts.

Social media amplified the frenzy. TVK's digital team ran targeted ads, live-streamed events, and countered negative narratives within hours. The DMK and AIADMK, both led by ageing leaders, struggled to keep pace.

What this means for Tamil Nadu

Vijay's win is a clear rejection of the status quo. The DMK, led by M.K. Stalin, had been in power since 2021. But allegations of corruption in the state's liquor policy and rising unemployment eroded its support. The AIADMK, still rebuilding after its 2021 defeat, failed to offer a credible alternative.

Analysts say this election marks a generational shift. The electorate is younger, more aspirational, and less tied to caste-based vote banks. Vijay drew support across communities, a feat rare in Tamil Nadu's polarised politics.

“People voted for a face they trust, not a party symbol,” said political commentator N. Sathiya Moorthy. “Vijay represents hope, not ideology.”

The road ahead

Governing is different from campaigning. Vijay will have to manage a state with a Rs 7 lakh crore debt, a struggling manufacturing sector, and water disputes with neighbouring states. His cabinet, largely inexperienced, will be tested from day one.

He has promised to be a hands-on chief minister, accessible to the public. But the real challenge lies in delivering on his campaign promises without breaking the bank. The business community is watching closely, wary of populist spending.

For now, the mood in Chennai is celebratory. Supporters burst firecrackers outside TVK offices, chanting Vijay's name. The actor, now chief minister-elect, has asked them to stay peaceful. “Our work has just begun,” he said in his victory speech.

What happens next: Vijay will be sworn in within a week. His first budget, due in July, will be the first real signal of his government's priorities. Investors and voters alike are waiting to see if the star can deliver on the big screen of real politics.
