New TB Vaccines Could Save 7.3 Million Lives by 2050: Gavi

Gavi's Ambitious Plan to Roll Out New TB Vaccines by 2029

The Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunisation (Gavi) has announced a target of 2029 for the introduction of next-generation tuberculosis (TB) vaccines, a move that could prevent up to 7.3 million deaths by 2050. This breakthrough comes after decades of reliance on the century-old BCG vaccine, which offers limited protection against pulmonary TB in adults. The new vaccines, currently in late-stage clinical trials, have shown promise in preventing active TB disease and could revolutionise global health efforts.

TB remains one of the world's deadliest infectious diseases, claiming over 1.5 million lives annually. The COVID-19 pandemic exacerbated the crisis, disrupting diagnosis and treatment services and leading to a rise in cases. Gavi's plan, if successful, would mark a significant turning point in the fight against TB, particularly in low- and middle-income countries where the burden is highest.

Why These Vaccines Matter: Lives and Economic Impact

The potential impact of new TB vaccines extends far beyond health. According to Gavi, widespread vaccination could avert 50 million TB cases and generate $1.6 trillion in economic benefits by 2050. These gains would come from reduced healthcare costs, increased productivity, and the prevention of catastrophic expenses that push families into poverty. For countries like India, which accounts for over a quarter of global TB cases, the vaccines could be transformative.

Dr. Soumya Swaminathan, former Chief Scientist at the World Health Organization, has emphasised that new TB vaccines are essential to achieving the End TB Strategy targets. "We need a vaccine that is effective in adolescents and adults, and we are hopeful that the current candidates will deliver," she said. However, she cautioned that equitable access must be ensured to prevent a repeat of the COVID-19 vaccine inequity.

Challenges and Opportunities for India

India bears the highest TB burden globally, with an estimated 2.6 million cases in 2022. The government has set an ambitious goal to eliminate TB by 2025, but experts argue that without a new vaccine, this target is unrealistic. The introduction of new vaccines could accelerate progress, but challenges remain in distribution, cold-chain infrastructure, and public acceptance.

Dr. Lucica Ditiu, Executive Director of the Stop TB Partnership, noted, "India has the manufacturing capacity and political will to lead the way. But we must invest in delivery systems and community engagement to ensure that vaccines reach those who need them most." Gavi's advance market commitment (AMC) model, which guarantees a market for vaccines, could incentivise manufacturers to produce affordable doses for low-income countries.

Comparing the Old and the New: BCG vs. Next-Generation TB Vaccines

The BCG vaccine, developed in 1921, protects infants against severe forms of TB but is less effective in preventing pulmonary TB in adults. New vaccine candidates, such as M72/AS01E and VPM1002, aim to address this gap. M72/AS01E, developed by GlaxoSmithKline, showed 50% efficacy in a phase 2b trial, while VPM1002, a recombinant BCG vaccine, is being tested for safety and efficacy in multiple countries.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • High efficacy: M72/AS01E showed 50% efficacy in preventing active TB, a major improvement over BCG.
  • Affordability: Gavi's AMC could reduce the price to under $10 per dose, making it accessible to low-income countries.
  • Economic returns: Every dollar invested in TB vaccines could yield $16 in economic benefits.
  • Challenges: Cold-chain requirements, vaccine hesitancy, and weak health systems could hinder rollout.
  • India's role: Indian manufacturers like Serum Institute are poised to produce vaccines at scale, ensuring supply security.

The Road Ahead: Funding and Global Cooperation

Gavi's plan requires substantial funding. The alliance has estimated that $1.5 billion is needed for vaccine development and delivery over the next five years. Donor countries and philanthropic organisations have pledged support, but gaps remain. The upcoming replenishment conference in 2025 will be critical for securing commitments.

Meanwhile, the World Health Organization is updating its guidelines for TB vaccine introduction, and countries are preparing for regulatory approvals. If all goes well, the first new TB vaccines could be rolled out in high-burden countries by 2029. This timeline, while ambitious, offers a beacon of hope for millions affected by TB.
