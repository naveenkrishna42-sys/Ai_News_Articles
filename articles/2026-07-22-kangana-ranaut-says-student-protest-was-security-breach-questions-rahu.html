Kangana Ranaut says student protest was 'security breach', questions Rahul Gandhi

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kangana Ranaut called the student protest a 'security breach' and said MPs feared an attack.
  • She questioned Rahul Gandhi for moving the protest to the PM's residence.
  • The protest was related to the NEET-UG 2026 row, with BJP urging talks and Opposition demanding action.

BJP MP Kangana Ranaut has stoked a fresh political row by calling a student protest near Parliament a 'security breach'. She claimed that several MPs were frightened and believed they were about to be attacked.

The actor-turned-politician also took aim at Congress leader Rahul Gandhi, questioning his decision to move the protest to the Prime Minister's residence. Her remarks come amid escalating tensions over the NEET-UG 2026 controversy.

MPs feared mob attack, says Kangana

Speaking to reporters, Kangana described the atmosphere inside Parliament during the student march. "We were scared of a mob attack. MPs were frightened. We thought the protesters would storm the building," she said.

She alleged that the security arrangements were inadequate and that the protest crossed a line. "This was a clear security breach. No one should be allowed to intimidate Parliament," she added.

The protest, organised by students demanding a probe into the NEET-UG 2026 paper leak, had turned chaotic near the Parliament complex. Police had to use barricades and water cannons to control the crowd.

Rahul Gandhi under fire

Kangana specifically questioned Rahul Gandhi's role in the protest. "Why did Rahul Gandhi move the protest to the PM's house? What was the intention? To threaten the Prime Minister?" she asked.

She accused the Opposition of using students for political gain. "They are not concerned about the students. They just want to create chaos and target the government," she said.

Rahul Gandhi had earlier met the protesting students and led a march towards the Prime Minister's residence, demanding a Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) probe into the NEET-UG 2026 irregularities.

BJP urges dialogue, Opposition stands firm

While Kangana's comments have added to the political heat, the BJP leadership has taken a more conciliatory tone. Senior party leaders have urged the protesting students to engage in dialogue with the government.

"We are open to discussions. The government is ready to address all genuine concerns. But violence and intimidation cannot be tolerated," a BJP spokesperson said.

The Opposition, however, remains adamant. Congress and other parties have demanded the resignation of Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, holding him responsible for the NEET-UG 2026 fiasco. Actress and BJP MP Hema Malini, when asked about the resignation demand, refused to comment, saying, "Protesting won't get any work done."

What next?

The government is expected to hold a meeting with student representatives in the coming days to defuse the situation. The Opposition has announced plans to raise the issue in Parliament. The NEET-UG 2026 controversy is far from over, and both sides are digging in for a long confrontation.
