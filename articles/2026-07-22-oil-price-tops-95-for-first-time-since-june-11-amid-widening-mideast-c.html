Oil price tops $95 for first time since June 11 amid widening Mideast conflict

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Brent crude oil price crossed $95 for the first time since June 11, marking a five-week high.
  • The surge is driven by widening conflict in the Middle East, threatening key oil transit routes.
  • US downplays Iran diplomacy, adding to supply concerns and market volatility.
  • Analysts expect further price increases if disruptions continue.

Brent crude crosses $95 as supply fears grip market

Oil prices surged past the $95 mark on Wednesday, hitting a five-week high not seen since June 11. The rally comes as the Middle East conflict widens, threatening two key oil transit routes and rattling global markets.

Brent crude futures climbed over 3% in early trading, with analysts pointing to escalating geopolitical risks. The latest spike was triggered by reports that the US has downplayed the possibility of diplomatic progress with Iran, further squeezing hopes of de-escalation in the region.

Two key trade routes under threat

The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes, is now in the crosshairs of the conflict. Separately, disruptions have been reported along another critical shipping lane, compounding fears of a supply crunch.

"The market is pricing in a real risk of physical supply outages," said a commodities strategist tracking the developments. "When two major chokepoints are simultaneously threatened, the premium on crude rises sharply."

US stance adds to uncertainty

Washington's decision to step back from direct engagement with Tehran has removed a key safety valve. Traders had earlier hoped that renewed talks might ease tensions, but those expectations have faded.

The US administration has instead signaled a tougher posture, leaving little room for a quick resolution. This has left the market exposed to every new flashpoint in the region.

What analysts expect next

Forecasters are split on whether the rally has more room to run. Some see $100 as a realistic target if hostilities escalate further. Others caution that a diplomatic breakthrough—however unlikely now—could trigger a sharp reversal.

Investors are also watching for any coordinated release of strategic petroleum reserves by major consumers. Such a move could temporarily cap prices, but it would not address the underlying supply risk from the conflict.

Retail fuel prices in India have not yet been officially revised, but any sustained rise above $95 will add to inflationary pressures. The government is widely expected to monitor the situation closely before making any policy moves.

For now, all eyes remain on the next twist in the Middle East. If no diplomatic off-ramp appears, oil prices could stay elevated—and volatile—for weeks to come.
