Solar eclipse and Perseid meteor shower to dazzle skies on August 12

A Rare Celestial Double-Header

Skywatchers are in for a treat on August 12, when a partial solar eclipse and the peak of the Perseid meteor shower coincide in a single day. This rare alignment of celestial events has been described by astronomers as the stargazing highlight of the year.

The partial solar eclipse will see the Moon pass between the Sun and Earth, casting a shadow that partially blocks the Sun's disc. Meanwhile, the Perseids, one of the most anticipated meteor showers of the year, will reach its peak activity, offering a dazzling display of shooting stars.

According to reports from BBC Sky at Night Magazine and other outlets, this is not just a two-event spectacle. As many as four rare celestial occurrences are expected to align on the same date, making it a once-in-a-year opportunity for astronomy enthusiasts.

What Astronomers Say

A US astronomer has shared key insights on how to make the most of this unusual day. While meteor showers are best viewed with the naked eye in dark skies, solar eclipses require proper eye protection to avoid serious injury.

The astronomer emphasised that the eclipse will be visible in specific regions, and timing will be critical. Those in the path of the eclipse should use certified solar viewing glasses or indirect projection methods, such as pinhole projectors, to safely observe the event.

For the meteor shower, the advice is simpler: find a dark location away from city lights, allow your eyes to adapt to the darkness, and look towards the constellation Perseus, from which the meteors appear to radiate.

Four Events, One Date

Beyond the eclipse and the meteor shower, reports from ThePrint and other sources indicate that two additional celestial phenomena will also occur on August 12. While specific details of all four events have not been fully confirmed, astronomers suggest that such a convergence is extremely rare.

This alignment offers a unique opportunity for both seasoned stargazers and casual observers. The key, experts say, is preparation. Knowing the exact times of the eclipse in your location, and having the right equipment ready, can make all the difference between a memorable experience and a missed one.

For those in India, the visibility of the eclipse will depend on geographic location. Skywatchers are advised to check local timings and weather forecasts in the days leading up to August 12.

How to Prepare

  • Check local eclipse timings and visibility maps for your region.
  • Use certified solar filters or pinhole projectors for the eclipse — never look directly at the Sun.
  • For the meteor shower, find a dark spot away from urban light pollution.
  • Allow 20–30 minutes for your eyes to adapt to the dark before the meteor peak.
