CINTAA in turmoil: 8 members resign, allege lack of transparency

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Eight elected members of CINTAA have resigned, citing lack of transparency.
  • They have demanded fresh elections for the organisation.
  • CINTAA has denied 'false' dissolution claims, calling them misinformation.
  • Allegations have been directed at Poonam Dhillon and Padmini Kolhapure.
  • Upasana Singh has responded, calling an EGM 'illegal'.
  • The crisis has triggered an internal power struggle within the 68-year-old body.

Resignations rock CINTAA

The Cine & TV Artistes' Association (CINTAA), a 68-year-old body representing television and film actors, is facing an internal crisis after eight of its elected members resigned en masse. The members have demanded fresh elections, alleging a lack of transparency in the functioning of the organisation.

The resignations have sent shockwaves through the industry, with the dissenting members claiming that decision-making processes have become opaque. They have accused the current leadership of bypassing democratic norms, a charge that has now become the centre of a bitter public spat.

Allegations against Dhillon and Kolhapure

According to reports, the eight members who stepped down have specifically raised concerns about the role of senior actors Poonam Dhillon and Padmini Kolhapure, who are associated with the association's executive committee. The allegations suggest that the duo, along with others, have been running the body without adequate consultation with the wider membership.

The resigning members have called for a complete overhaul, insisting that only a fresh election can restore faith in the institution. They have also hinted at deeper procedural irregularities, though they have not yet provided specific evidence in public.

CINTAA hits back at 'false' claims

In response, CINTAA has strongly denied what it calls 'false' dissolution claims, with officials stating that a 68-year-old institution cannot be undermined by misinformation. The association has asserted that it remains functional and that the resignations are a minority action that does not reflect the sentiment of the broader membership.

The leadership has also pushed back against the demand for immediate elections, arguing that the existing mandate should be respected. This has led to a sharp exchange of statements, with both sides accusing the other of bad faith.

Upasana Singh calls EGM 'illegal'

Amid the chaos, actor Upasana Singh, who is reportedly aligned with the current leadership, has dismissed the proposed extraordinary general meeting (EGM) demanded by the dissenting members. She has called the EGM 'illegal', stating that it does not follow the association's constitution.

Her remarks have added fuel to the fire, with the resigning members countering that the EGM is a legitimate democratic tool. The dispute now hinges on procedural interpretation, with legal experts suggesting that the matter may need to be resolved through arbitration or the courts.

A deepening rift

The crisis has exposed a deep rift within CINTAA, which has historically been a unifying force for actors in the Hindi film and television industry. The association has played a key role in negotiating with producers and safeguarding artistes' rights, making this internal strife particularly concerning for its members.

Several industry insiders have expressed worry that the prolonged infighting could weaken the body's bargaining power. However, both factions have claimed that they are acting in the best interest of the members, leaving the rank and file confused about the way forward.

What happens next

With the resignations now on the table, all eyes are on the association's next move. The leadership has a window to address the transparency concerns, but the demand for fresh elections is unlikely to fade.

If the two sides fail to reach a compromise, the matter could escalate to the courts or lead to a formal split. For now, the industry is watching closely to see whether CINTAA can resolve this crisis without further damaging its reputation.
