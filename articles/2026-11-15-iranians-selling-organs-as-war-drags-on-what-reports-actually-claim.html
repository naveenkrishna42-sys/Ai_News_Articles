Iranians Selling Organs as War Drags On: What Reports Actually Claim

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-15 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Reports cite kidney, liver tissue and testicular sales, but scale remains unverified
  • Iran runs the world's only state-sanctioned compensated kidney donor program since 1988
  • Unregulated surgeries carry severe infection, rejection and exploitation risks
  • Buyers of such claims should demand named sources, hospital records and NGO verification

What the Reports Claim

The Times of India, citing Iran International and other outlets, reports that Iranian workers are selling organs — including kidneys, liver tissue and, according to some claims, testicular tissue — to pay debts and cover rent. The reports tie the trend to a conflict with the US and Israel that they say has run since February, alongside a deepening cost-of-living crisis.

None of the aggregated snippets name individual sellers, hospitals, or documented case files. The figures are therefore claims, not confirmed data, and readers should treat viral figures circulating on social platforms with caution.

Why the Claims Are Plausible

Iran's economy has faced years of sanctions, currency depreciation and inflation, which compress real wages even for employed workers. When savings collapse and credit dries up, distressed households historically turn to asset sales — and the human body becomes the last asset available to the poorest.

Iran's Unique Organ Donation Background

Iran operates the world's only state-regulated compensated living-unrelated kidney donation program, established in 1988. The government pays donors a stipend and provides insurance, which historically kept the country nearly self-sufficient in transplants. This existing infrastructure makes organ-related claims structurally plausible — but regulated donations and covert black-market sales are legally and medically distinct.

Medical and Ethical Risks for Sellers

Nephrectomy carries surgical risks including hemorrhage, infection and anaesthesia complications, and donors face reduced renal reserve for life. Unregulated procedures remove safeguards: no independent donor advocate, no long-term follow-up, and no verified consent. Brokers profit while liability stays with the seller.

What Remains Unverified

No casualty figures, payment amounts, clinic names or seller testimonies have been independently confirmed in the material reviewed. Before amplifying such reports, journalists and readers should demand named sources, hospital records, and corroboration from bodies such as the WHO's transplant monitoring programmes or verified NGOs.
