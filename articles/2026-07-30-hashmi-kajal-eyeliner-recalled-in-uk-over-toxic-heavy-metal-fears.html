Hashmi Kajal Eyeliner recalled in UK over toxic heavy metal fears

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • UK authorities recalled Hashmi Kajal Eyeliner for containing excessive lead and antimony.
  • Heavy metals in cosmetics can accumulate in the body, causing long-term health issues.
  • Experts warn consumers to check ingredients and opt for safer alternatives.

Recall in the UK

British regulators have pulled a popular Indian-origin eyeliner from shelves. Hashmi Kajal Eyeliner was found to contain dangerously high levels of toxic heavy metals, including lead and antimony.

The UK's Office for Product Safety and Standards issued the recall earlier this week. Consumers who bought the product have been advised to stop using it immediately and return it for a refund.

What was found

Tests on the kajal revealed lead levels far exceeding safe limits for cosmetics. Antimony, another heavy metal linked to respiratory and skin issues, was also detected in concerning quantities.

Lead is a neurotoxin that can cause developmental problems, especially in children and pregnant women. Antimony can irritate the eyes and skin, and long-term exposure may damage the heart and lungs.

Why this matters

Heavy metals in cosmetics are not a new problem. Many traditional kajals and eyeliners, particularly those made in South Asia, have been flagged for contamination over the years.

The issue is that these metals are not always listed on labels. Consumers assume they are buying a safe product, but impurities from raw materials or manufacturing processes can slip through.

Health risks to watch for

Short-term exposure to contaminated kajal can cause eye irritation, redness, and watering. Long-term use may lead to lead accumulation in the body, affecting the nervous system and kidneys.

Doctors warn that even small amounts of lead can be harmful over time. Children who use or are exposed to such products face higher risks because their bodies absorb metals more readily.

What consumers can do

Experts recommend checking cosmetics for certification marks from trusted regulators like the US FDA or EU authorities. Avoid products that do not list ingredients clearly.

Switching to brands that voluntarily test for heavy metals is a safer bet. Natural or organic labels do not automatically mean metal-free, so scrutiny is key.

Regulatory bodies in India have not yet commented on this recall. The company behind Hashmi Kajal has not issued a public statement as of now.

Watch for updates from the UK Office for Product Safety and Standards, and check your makeup bag for any recalled batches.
