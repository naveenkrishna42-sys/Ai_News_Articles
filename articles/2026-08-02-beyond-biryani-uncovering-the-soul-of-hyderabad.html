Beyond Biryani: Uncovering the Soul of Hyderabad

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hyderabad's cultural identity extends far beyond its culinary fame
  • The Qutb Shahi tombs and other monuments reveal the city's layered history
  • Local initiatives are working to preserve and showcase Hyderabad's intangible heritage

Hyderabad is often reduced to a single dish. Ask any tourist what draws them to the city, and the answer is almost always the same: biryani. But beneath the layers of saffron rice and slow-cooked meat lies a metropolis with a far richer, more complex identity. The city that was once the seat of the Qutb Shahi dynasty and later the Asaf Jahi rulers has a heritage that spans centuries, and yet, much of it remains overshadowed by its culinary fame.

This is not a story about food. It is about the monuments, the stories, and the people that give Hyderabad its true character—a character that survives in the shadow of Charminar, in the quiet corners of the Old City, and in the efforts of those determined to keep the city's history alive.

The Qutb Shahi Tombs: A Silent Legacy

On the outskirts of the city, away from the bustle of Laad Bazaar and the crowds at Golconda Fort, lie the Qutb Shahi tombs. These structures, built over a span of nearly a century, are the final resting place of the rulers who shaped Hyderabad's early destiny. For years, they stood neglected, their domes and arches crumbling under the weight of time and weather.

Recent restoration efforts have brought some of them back to life. The tombs now host sound-and-light shows, and guided walks are becoming increasingly popular among locals and visitors alike. Yet, for many Hyderabadis, these monuments remain a blind spot—known, but rarely visited.

Beyond the Tourist Trail

The city's heritage is not confined to its grand structures. In the bylanes of the Old City, artisans still practice crafts that have been passed down through generations. Pearl merchants, lac bangle makers, and weavers of the famous Pochampally sarees continue their trade, often unnoticed by the casual visitor. Their work is a living testament to a culture that refuses to be erased.

Local historians and heritage enthusiasts are increasingly stepping in to bridge the gap between the city's past and its present. Walking tours, heritage trails, and storytelling sessions are being organised with the aim of introducing residents to the Hyderabad that exists beyond the guidebooks. These efforts are not just about preservation; they are about creating a sense of ownership and pride among the city's youth.

The Hyderabad of Today

Hyderabad's rapid growth as a tech and business hub has brought with it a new set of challenges. Old neighbourhoods are giving way to high-rises, and the city's skyline is changing faster than its history can be documented. The tension between development and preservation is palpable, and it is not always resolved in favour of the latter.

Yet, there is hope. The same energy that drives the city's economic ambitions is now being channelled into cultural initiatives. Museums are being revamped, archives digitised, and public art installations are springing up in unexpected places. The city is slowly learning to look at itself in a new light—not just as a destination for business or food, but as a place with a story worth telling.

A City of Many Layers

Hyderabad's soul is not a single narrative. It is a patchwork of influences—Persian, Mughal, Telugu, and Deccani—each leaving its mark on the city's language, architecture, and customs. The result is a place that defies easy categorisation, and that is precisely its strength.

To know Hyderabad is to walk through its crowded markets, to listen to the call to prayer echoing from its minarets, and to sit with the elderly men who have watched the city change over decades. It is to understand that the city's identity is not fixed, but fluid—constantly evolving, yet deeply rooted.

As the city moves forward, the question is not whether it will preserve its heritage, but how it will choose to do so. The answer will determine whether future generations will know Hyderabad for more than just its biryani.

For now, the efforts of historians, artists, and ordinary citizens offer a glimpse of what is possible. The city is waking up to its own history, and the journey has only just begun.
