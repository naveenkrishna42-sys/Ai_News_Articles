Study Abroad 2025: Complete List of Entrance Exams for Indian Students

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Several standardized tests like IELTS, TOEFL, GRE, GMAT, and SAT are required for admission to foreign universities.
  • Eligibility criteria vary by exam and institution, with most requiring a minimum score and academic background.
  • Students should plan ahead for registration deadlines and test dates to meet application timelines.

Understanding the Exam Landscape

For Indian students aspiring to pursue higher education abroad in 2025, the path is paved with a series of standardized tests that serve as gateways to universities across the globe. These exams evaluate a candidate's academic readiness, language proficiency, and critical thinking skills, forming a crucial part of the application process.

While each country and institution has its own set of requirements, a common thread runs through most applications: a combination of English language proficiency tests and subject-specific or aptitude-based assessments. Knowing which exams to take and when can significantly streamline the application journey.

English Proficiency Tests: The First Hurdle

For non-native English speakers, proving language proficiency is non-negotiable. The International English Language Testing System (IELTS) and the Test of English as a Foreign Language (TOEFL) are the two most widely accepted exams worldwide. Most universities in the UK, Australia, Canada, and the US accept either of these scores.

IELTS is often preferred for Commonwealth countries, while TOEFL is more common in the US. Both tests assess listening, reading, writing, and speaking skills, but they differ in format and scoring. Candidates should check specific university requirements, as some may have a preference or minimum score thresholds that vary by course and level of study.

Graduate Admissions: GRE and GMAT

For those aiming for master's or doctoral programs, the Graduate Record Examination (GRE) is a standard requirement for a wide range of disciplines, including engineering, sciences, and humanities. The GRE General Test measures verbal reasoning, quantitative reasoning, and analytical writing skills.

For business school aspirants, the Graduate Management Admission Test (GMAT) is the go-to exam. It focuses on analytical writing, integrated reasoning, quantitative, and verbal skills, tailored to assess the abilities needed in management education. Many top-tier MBA programs globally require a competitive GMAT score, though an increasing number are also accepting GRE scores as an alternative.

Undergraduate Admissions: SAT and ACT

Undergraduate applicants, particularly those targeting US universities, will likely need to take the Scholastic Assessment Test (SAT) or the American College Testing (ACT). The SAT covers evidence-based reading, writing, and math, while the ACT includes an additional science reasoning section.

While many US colleges have made these tests optional in recent years, a strong score can still enhance an application, especially for scholarships. Indian students planning to apply to undergraduate programs in the US, Canada, or even some European institutions should consider taking these exams well in advance.

Eligibility and Requirements: What You Need to Know

Eligibility criteria for these exams vary. For IELTS and TOEFL, there is no strict educational prerequisite, but candidates are typically expected to have completed secondary education. For GRE and GMAT, a bachelor's degree in any discipline is generally required, though some programs may have specific subject prerequisites.

Each exam has its own scoring system, and universities often set minimum score requirements. For instance, a common IELTS band requirement for postgraduate courses is 6.5 overall, while top US universities might expect TOEFL scores above 100. Similarly, competitive GRE scores often fall in the 310-330 range, and GMAT scores above 700 are considered strong for leading business schools.

Planning Your Test Schedule

Given that these exams are offered multiple times a year, students have flexibility in choosing test dates. However, it is crucial to register early, as slots can fill up, especially during peak application seasons (October to January). Test results are typically available within two to three weeks for computer-based exams and around five weeks for paper-based ones.

Students should also factor in time for score reporting to universities, which can take an additional few weeks. It is advisable to take the exams at least six months before application deadlines to allow ample time for any retakes if needed.

What to Watch Next

As the 2025 admission cycle unfolds, students should keep a close eye on university-specific updates and any changes to test formats or requirements. Some institutions are increasingly adopting flexible policies, such as accepting multiple exam types or waiving tests for certain applicants. Staying informed and preparing strategically will be key to navigating the study abroad journey successfully.
