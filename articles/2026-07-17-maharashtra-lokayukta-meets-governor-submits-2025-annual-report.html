Maharashtra Lokayukta meets Governor, submits 2025 annual report

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Lokayukta called on the Governor at Raj Bhavan, Mumbai
  • Annual report for the year 2025 formally submitted
  • Meeting is part of statutory requirement under Lokayukta Act

The Maharashtra State Lokayukta met Governor Bhagat Singh Koshyari at Raj Bhavan in Mumbai on Monday and formally presented the annual report for the calendar year 2025.

The meeting, which lasted around 30 minutes, was part of the statutory requirement under the Maharashtra Lokayukta and Upa-Lokayuktas Act. The report details the anti-corruption body's work over the past year.

Report covers investigations and recommendations

The 2025 annual report includes a summary of complaints received, investigations conducted, and recommendations made by the Lokayukta. It also highlights cases where action was taken against public officials for corruption or maladministration.

Officials at Raj Bhavan said the Governor accepted the report and noted its importance in strengthening accountability in governance. The Governor is expected to table the report in the state legislature during the upcoming session.

Statutory requirement fulfilled

Under Section 10 of the Lokayukta Act, the authority must submit its annual report to the Governor by March 31 each year. This year's submission comes a few months after the deadline, though no official explanation for the delay was provided.

The Lokayukta, a retired high court judge, has previously flagged concerns about inadequate staffing and lack of independent prosecution powers. It remains unclear if the 2025 report addresses these issues.

What the report contains

The document is divided into chapters covering complaint trends, departmental inquiries, and systemic recommendations to improve transparency in state departments. The Lokayukta also included a section on suo motu cognisance cases taken up during 2025.

Details of the number of complaints or specific cases have not been made public yet. The report is typically confidential until tabled in the legislature.

The meeting at Raj Bhavan comes amid ongoing discussions about strengthening anti-corruption mechanisms in the state. The Governor and the Lokayukta are expected to meet again after the report is presented to the legislature.

The next step will be for the state government to respond to the Lokayukta's recommendations. The report is likely to be tabled in the winter session of the Maharashtra legislature later this year.
