Madhya Pradesh High Court: Literary Expression No Crime in Urdu Poem Case

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Madhya Pradesh High Court quashed a case against a teacher for reciting an Urdu poem.
  • The court ruled that literary expression is not a crime under the law.
  • The judgment reaffirms the importance of freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution.

The Madhya Pradesh High Court has quashed a criminal case against a teacher who was accused over the recitation of an Urdu poem. The court held that literary expression does not constitute a crime. The ruling came in response to a petition filed by the teacher challenging the FIR lodged against him.

Case Background

The teacher, posted at a government school, had recited an Urdu poem during a literary event. A complaint was filed alleging that the poem contained objectionable content. Police registered a case under various sections of the Indian Penal Code, including those related to promoting enmity between groups.

The teacher moved the High Court seeking quashing of the FIR. He argued that the poem was a work of literary expression and did not violate any law. His counsel submitted that the recitation was part of a cultural programme and lacked any malicious intent.

Court's Observations

Justice G.S. Ahluwalia, hearing the petition, observed that literary expression cannot be curbed. The judge said that merely because a poem is in Urdu, it cannot be presumed to be offensive. The court noted that the poem did not contain any incitement to violence or hatred.

The bench emphasised that freedom of speech and expression is a fundamental right under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution. It stated that unless the expression falls within the reasonable restrictions laid down in Article 19(2), the state cannot interfere. The court found that the poem in question did not meet the threshold for any of those restrictions.

Legal Precedents Cited

The High Court referred to earlier Supreme Court judgments on the scope of artistic and literary freedom. It reiterated that the right to express oneself through poetry, art, or literature is integral to a democratic society. The court warned against the tendency to file cases based on subjective interpretations of creative works.

Justice Ahluwalia remarked that if every literary piece is subjected to criminal scrutiny, it would stifle creativity. The court observed that teachers, in particular, should not be harassed for engaging in cultural activities. It directed the immediate quashing of the FIR and all proceedings arising from it.

Reaction and Implications

Legal experts have welcomed the judgment, saying it sends a strong message against frivolous litigation. They note that the ruling reinforces the boundaries of free speech in the context of literary works. The teacher's counsel expressed relief, stating that the decision protects the rights of educators and artists alike.

The state government has not indicated whether it will appeal the order. The High Court's decision is likely to be cited in similar cases where creative expression is alleged to be offensive.

In the coming months, the judgment may influence how lower courts handle complaints against poets, writers, and performers. The case underscores the judiciary's role in balancing individual freedoms with public order concerns.
