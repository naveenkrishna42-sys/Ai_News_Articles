Trump backs Infantino, says replacing FIFA chief would be a 'terrible mistake'

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump calls potential Infantino exit a 'terrible mistake'
  • Three confederations have signed an open letter to football family
  • Pressure mounts on FIFA leadership ahead of key meetings

US President Donald Trump has weighed into the growing turmoil surrounding FIFA president Gianni Infantino, describing any move to replace the embattled Swiss-Italian administrator as a “terrible mistake.”

Trump’s comments come as Infantino faces mounting pressure from three continental football confederations that have jointly signed an open letter to the football family, demanding change at the top of world football’s governing body.

Open letter rattles FIFA

The open letter, published on UEFA’s official website, is addressed directly to the football family and signals rare public dissent from within FIFA’s own ranks. While the full contents have not been made public, sources indicate the letter calls for accountability and fresh leadership.

Three confederations — widely understood to include UEFA, though the others have not been officially named — have put their weight behind the call. The move is seen as an unprecedented challenge to Infantino’s authority, who has led FIFA since 2016.

Trump’s intervention

Trump’s backing, delivered in a statement that did not specify the context or audience, adds a geopolitical layer to an already tense standoff. The US president has maintained a public friendship with Infantino, who has overseen FIFA’s expansion into North America, including the 2026 World Cup hosted jointly by the US, Canada, and Mexico.

“Replacing Gianni would be a terrible mistake,” Trump said. The remarks were first reported by Al Jazeera and later picked up by the BBC and other outlets. The White House has not elaborated on what prompted the statement or whether it was made in an official capacity.

Why Infantino is under fire

Infantino’s tenure has been marked by both commercial growth and persistent controversy. Critics have pointed to governance issues, questions over transparency, and disputes with European football bodies over the proposed breakaway Super League and the expanded Club World Cup format.

The open letter from the three confederations is the most coordinated pushback yet, though none of the signatories have publicly called for Infantino’s resignation in so many words. FIFA has not issued an official response to the letter, and Infantino himself has remained silent on the matter.

What happens next

The coming weeks are likely to determine whether the open letter translates into concrete action. FIFA’s congress is scheduled for later this year, and any formal motion to challenge Infantino’s leadership would need the backing of a significant bloc of member associations.

For now, the football world watches closely. Trump’s endorsement may bolster Infantino’s position in some quarters, but it is unlikely to silence his European critics. The question is not whether Infantino will survive — but how much longer the uneasy peace within FIFA can hold.
