FSSAI bars Dabur from '100%' claims on food products

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • FSSAI has prohibited Dabur from selling food products with '100%' claims
  • The directive covers products like honey, ghee, and coconut oil
  • The regulator found the claims misleading to consumers

India's food safety regulator has barred Dabur from selling food products that carry '100%' claims on their labels, in a move that could reshape how the country's consumer goods giants market their staples.

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) issued the directive after concluding that such claims were misleading, according to multiple reports. The order applies to products including honey, ghee, and coconut oil, where the '100%' tag has been a common marketing tool.

What the directive says

The regulator's order effectively halts the sale of Dabur products that use the '100%' label, unless the claims can be substantiated to the authority's satisfaction. While the exact reasoning behind the decision has not been made public in full, the move signals a stricter interpretation of labelling rules under the Food Safety and Standards Act.

Dabur, one of India's largest fast-moving consumer goods companies, has not yet issued a detailed public response. The company has traditionally leaned heavily on purity and natural positioning for its food range, making the directive a significant blow to its marketing playbook.

Why it matters for consumers

For years, shoppers have been drawn to products that promise absolute purity. A jar of honey or a bottle of coconut oil labelled '100%' carries an implicit guarantee that nothing else is inside. The FSSAI's intervention suggests that such absolute claims may not always hold up to scrutiny.

Regulators in several countries have tightened rules around food labelling, and India appears to be following that trend. The directive could force companies to either prove their claims with lab tests or drop the language altogether.

Industry-wide implications

The order is unlikely to be limited to Dabur alone. Other brands in the honey, ghee, and edible oil segments often use similar '100%' or 'pure' claims. If the FSSAI applies the same standard across the board, the entire sector may need to revisit how it communicates with consumers.

Legal experts suggest the ruling could set a precedent. Companies that fail to comply risk penalties, including fines or suspension of product licences. The regulator has not yet specified a timeline for compliance, but the directive is effective immediately.

Dabur's share price is expected to face pressure when markets open, though the company has not yet commented on the financial impact. Analysts note that food products constitute a small but growing part of the company's portfolio, which is dominated by healthcare and personal care items.

What to watch next

The coming weeks will reveal whether Dabur challenges the order in court or opts for a quiet relabelling exercise. The FSSAI's next move—whether it extends the scrutiny to other companies—will also be closely watched by the industry.
