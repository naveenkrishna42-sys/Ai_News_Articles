Bollywood’s Two-Part Movie Trend: From Ramayana to Dhurandhar

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bollywood has seen a rise in two-part film releases, with major titles like Ramayana and Dhurandhar following this trend.
  • The two-part format allows for deeper storytelling and larger box office returns, but also carries risks of audience fatigue.
  • Upcoming two-part films include Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana and the action film Dhurandhar.

The Two-Part Phenomenon in Bollywood

Bollywood is increasingly embracing the two-part film format. Recent announcements and releases show that several major projects are being split into two installments. This approach allows filmmakers to expand narratives and maximize commercial potential.

Among the most anticipated is Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana, a mythological epic that will be released in two parts. Another notable entry is Dhurandhar, an action-packed film that also adopts the split-release strategy.

Why Bollywood Is Splitting Films

The two-part model offers distinct advantages. It gives directors more screen time to develop characters and plotlines without cramming everything into a single runtime. For audiences, it builds anticipation and often leads to higher engagement across both releases.

From a financial standpoint, two parts can double the box office window. Studios can generate revenue from ticket sales, merchandise, and digital rights over an extended period. However, the strategy is not without risks. If the first part fails to impress, the second part may struggle at the box office.

Key Films That Have Used This Format

Several Bollywood films have successfully used the two-part structure. The Baahubali franchise, though originally made in Telugu, set a benchmark with its two-part release in Hindi. KGF: Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 also demonstrated the power of this model.

Other examples include War and its upcoming sequel, though not initially planned as two parts, the trend is clear. Ramayana and Dhurandhar are now joining this list, promising grand storytelling on a larger canvas.

The Road Ahead for Two-Part Blockbusters

The success of upcoming two-part films will depend on execution. Audiences are becoming more selective, and a weak first part can kill interest in the sequel. Filmmakers must ensure each part stands on its own while contributing to a cohesive whole.

With budgets soaring and expectations high, the two-part format is likely here to stay. It offers a way to tell complex stories without compromise, but only if the content delivers.

As Bollywood prepares for Ramayana and Dhurandhar, all eyes will be on how these films perform. Their success or failure could shape the future of multi-part releases in Indian cinema.
