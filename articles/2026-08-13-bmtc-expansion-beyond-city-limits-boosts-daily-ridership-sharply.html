BMTC expansion beyond city limits boosts daily ridership sharply

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BMTC daily ridership crosses 31 lakh, a 17% jump from last year
  • Expansion beyond city limits and new routes fuel the surge
  • Officials credit improved connectivity and frequency for growth

Ridership crosses 31 lakh mark

Bengaluru's public bus operator, BMTC, has reported a sharp rise in daily ridership, crossing the 31 lakh mark. This represents a 17% increase compared to the same period last year, a surge officials attribute largely to the corporation's recent expansion beyond city limits.

The growth comes as BMTC has aggressively extended its network into peri-urban areas and neighbouring districts, tapping into a commuter base that previously relied on private vehicles or informal transport. The new routes have added thousands of passengers daily, reshaping the city's public transport dynamics.

Expansion beyond city limits

Over the past year, BMTC has launched multiple intercity and suburban services, connecting Bengaluru with towns like Devanahalli, Hoskote, and Bidadi. These routes, once considered fringe, now see packed buses during peak hours, with daily commuters and students forming a significant chunk of the ridership.

Officials note that the expansion was a strategic response to the city's sprawling growth. "People are moving to the outskirts for affordable housing, but they still work in the city. We saw an opportunity to serve that corridor," a senior BMTC official said, though specific numbers for individual routes were not disclosed.

Improved frequency and connectivity

The surge is not just about new roads but also about better service. BMTC has increased bus frequency on many existing routes, reduced waiting times, and introduced more AC services on long-distance stretches. These changes have made public transport a more viable option for those who once shunned it.

Passenger feedback has been largely positive, with many citing reliability as a key factor. "Earlier, I used to drive my bike to work. Now, the bus is on time, and I save on fuel and parking," said a regular commuter on the Whitefield route, reflecting a broader trend.

Challenges remain despite growth

However, the surge has not come without strain. Overcrowding on certain popular routes has been reported, and BMTC is working to add more buses to meet demand. The corporation has also faced operational challenges, including driver shortages and maintenance backlogs, which officials say are being addressed.

Financial sustainability is another concern. While ridership is up, operational costs have risen too, and the corporation continues to rely on state subsidies to keep fares affordable. Experts suggest that the current growth trajectory could help BMTC break even in the coming years, but that will depend on managing costs effectively.

What this means for Bengaluru

The ridership increase is a positive sign for the city's efforts to reduce traffic congestion and air pollution. BMTC's expansion aligns with broader state policies promoting public transport, and the numbers suggest that citizens are willing to shift modes when services are reliable.

Yet, the challenge lies in scaling up infrastructure to match demand. Upcoming metro corridors and suburban rail projects may complement BMTC's network, but integration remains a work in progress. For now, the bus operator is focused on consolidating its gains and plugging gaps in coverage.

Looking ahead, BMTC plans to introduce more electric buses on the new routes and expand into additional peripheral areas. The next few months will be critical as the corporation evaluates the sustainability of this growth, with officials hinting at further route additions before the year ends.
