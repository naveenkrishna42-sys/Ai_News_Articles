Daayra Cleared: CBFC Passes Kareena Kapoor Khan Film With One Word Change

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-14 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • The CBFC granted Daayra a U/A certificate with no visual cuts, trimming only a single spoken word replaced by 'Minister'.
  • A zero-cut certification for a gritty drama signals strong box-office and OTT potential for the Kareena Kapoor Khan starrer.
  • For exhibitors, the U/A rating widens the addressable audience without the commercial penalty of an A certificate.

Kareena Kapoor Khan's upcoming drama Daayra has cleared its most consequential hurdle. The Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC) has passed the film with a U/A certificate, and the examining committee ordered no visual cuts whatsoever. The sole intervention is an audio substitution: one spoken word has been replaced with the term 'Minister'.

What the CBFC Actually Changed

According to the certification details, the committee's objection was limited to a single dialogue term deemed sensitive in the political context of the scene. The replacement word, 'Minister', is a neutral, formal substitution that preserves the sentence's meaning and rhythm. Crucially, no shot, frame or sequence was deleted, blurred or muted.

For a film reportedly built on tense, character-driven drama, that outcome is rare. Censor boards typically demand visual trims in films that touch on political or institutional subject matter. A clean visual pass suggests the narrative avoids direct, identifiable references while still carrying dramatic weight.

Why a Zero-Cut U/A Matters Commercially

Certification is not paperwork; it is pricing. A U/A rating lets families and younger viewers into the hall without an age gate, which broadens the opening-weekend audience. An 'A' certificate, by contrast, typically shrinks the multiplex audience by a meaningful margin, especially for a female-led drama that relies on word of mouth.

Zero visual cuts also protect the director's cut. Every deleted frame is a marketing liability, feeding social media chatter about a 'censored' film. With only one word altered, the makers can market Daayra as the version the director intended, which matters for critical reception and awards-season positioning.

What Buyers and Exhibitors Should Watch

  • Screen allocation: A U/A drama with a bankable star can command prime evening and weekend slots, but exhibitors should track advance bookings before locking premium screens.
  • Runtime and show count: Certified runtime directly determines daily show density. A leaner runtime allows more shows per screen, lifting per-screen revenue.
  • OTT window: A clean U/A certification strengthens the film's streaming value, since family-friendly titles travel better across platforms and territories.
  • Marketing angle: The 'no cuts' certification is itself a talking point. Distributors can use it to signal an uncompromised, adult-leaning drama without the A-rating penalty.
  • Risk factor: Political-adjacent dramas can attract scrutiny after release. A single-word substitution reduces, but does not eliminate, that exposure.

The Kareena Kapoor Khan Factor

Kareena Kapoor Khan remains one of the few Hindi film stars whose name alone secures theatrical footfalls, particularly in metro multiplexes. Her recent filmography has leaned toward high-concept, performance-led projects rather than pure spectacle. Daayra fits that trajectory, and a smooth certification removes the last procedural obstacle between the film and its release calendar.

Bottom Line for the Trade

The CBFC's decision is a best-case outcome for all stakeholders: producers keep their cut, exhibitors get a broader-certificate title, and audiences get an unaltered visual experience. One word changed is a footnote; zero frames lost is a commercial advantage. The film's eventual performance will now depend on content and marketing, not certification friction.
