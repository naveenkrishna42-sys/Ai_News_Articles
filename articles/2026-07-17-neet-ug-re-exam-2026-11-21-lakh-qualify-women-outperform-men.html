NEET UG 2026 Re-Exam Results: Key Numbers and Trends

The National Testing Agency (NTA) on Thursday released the results of the NEET UG 2026 re-examination, with 11.21 lakh candidates qualifying for medical and dental seats across India. This marks a significant development in a testing cycle marred by controversy, as the original exam was scrapped over irregularities.

Women candidates outperformed their male counterparts in the re-test, continuing a trend seen in recent years. Officials have not yet released a gender-wise breakdown of the pass percentage, but preliminary data indicates higher qualification rates among female aspirants. The total number of candidates who appeared for the re-exam was higher than the original test, though exact figures are yet to be confirmed by the NTA.

Toppers and Regional Performance

Punjab's Aryan Gupta and Haryana's Pranshul Bansal emerged as joint toppers of the NEET UG 2026 re-exam, according to multiple media reports. Their achievement underscores the competitive edge of candidates from northern India, but the results also highlight strong performances from other regions.

Telangana produced eight students in the top 138 ranks, with Sahyu securing All India Rank (AIR) 13. This points to a deepening of medical education preparation in southern states. Analysts suggest that coaching hubs in Hyderabad and other cities have contributed to this trend, though the NTA has not released state-wise data yet.

Why the Re-Exam Happened and What It Means

The NEET UG 2026 re-examination was ordered after the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) found evidence of paper leaks and other irregularities in the original test held in May. The Supreme Court mandated a fresh exam for all candidates, a rare move that affected over 20 lakh aspirants originally.

The decision had far-reaching consequences. Students faced a delay of nearly two months in results, which pushed back the counselling schedule. Medical colleges, already grappling with faculty shortages, now must compress their admission timelines. The controversy also eroded trust in the NTA, which has faced repeated allegations of mismanagement in recent years.

For candidates who qualified, the re-exam offered a clean slate. But for those who performed better in the original test, the re-test may have been a setback. Legal experts have noted that while the Supreme Court's intervention ensured fairness, it also set a precedent that could be invoked in future exam-related disputes.

What to Watch Next

The immediate focus shifts to the counselling process, which is expected to begin within the next two weeks. The Medical Counselling Committee (MCC) will release schedules for state and central quota seats. Delays in counselling could push the academic session start date into late 2026, affecting internships and postgraduate admissions.

Longer-term, the NTA faces calls for systemic reform. The government has indicated it may introduce a single national entrance exam for all professional courses, but no timeline has been announced. For now, the 11.21 lakh successful candidates must navigate a compressed admission cycle, while the rest await another attempt next year.
