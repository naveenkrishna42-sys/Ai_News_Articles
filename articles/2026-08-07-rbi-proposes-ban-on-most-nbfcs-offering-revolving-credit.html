RBI proposes ban on most NBFCs offering revolving credit

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Draft norms would restrict revolving credit to certain large NBFCs
  • Move targets credit cards, overdrafts, and similar facilities
  • Public comments sought before final rules are notified

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has floated draft regulations that would bar most non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) from offering revolving credit facilities, a move that could reshape the consumer lending market.

Revolving credit includes products like credit cards, overdraft lines, and other facilities where borrowers can draw, repay, and redraw funds up to a sanctioned limit. Currently, many NBFCs offer such products, often in partnership with banks.

What the draft says

Under the proposed norms, only NBFCs that meet specified criteria—such as a minimum net owned fund threshold and a track record of prudent lending—would be permitted to extend revolving credit. Most others would have to wind down existing facilities or convert them into fixed-tenor loans.

The RBI said the objective is to mitigate risks associated with unsecured, revolving exposures and to ensure that entities offering such products have adequate capital and risk management frameworks. The draft is open for public comments until a specified date, following which the central bank will finalise the rules.

Impact on the sector

Industry experts say the proposal, if implemented, would disproportionately affect smaller NBFCs that rely on revolving credit products for customer acquisition. Larger, systemically important NBFCs are expected to qualify under the proposed criteria, but mid-sized and smaller players may have to exit the segment.

Banks, which are already permitted to offer credit cards and overdrafts, are unlikely to be impacted. However, co-branded credit card arrangements involving NBFCs could come under scrutiny.

What it means for consumers

For borrowers, the change could mean fewer options for flexible credit, particularly from non-bank lenders. Those who currently use NBFC-issued credit cards or overdraft facilities may see those products discontinued or migrated to term loans with fixed repayment schedules.

The RBI has not yet specified how existing customers would be treated during the transition, but it has indicated that the final rules would provide adequate time for compliance.

Industry response

Some NBFCs have privately expressed concern that the proposal could stifle innovation and financial inclusion, especially for first-time borrowers who rely on smaller lenders. Others have welcomed the move, saying it would bring discipline to a segment that has seen rapid growth.

The RBI's discussion paper on the subject had earlier flagged that revolving credit facilities can lead to over-leveraging and higher delinquencies, particularly in a rising interest rate environment.

The central bank has not yet announced a timeline for final notification. It is expected to review comments from stakeholders before issuing the final guidelines.

All eyes will now be on the final rules—and on how lenders adjust their product suites in the interim.
