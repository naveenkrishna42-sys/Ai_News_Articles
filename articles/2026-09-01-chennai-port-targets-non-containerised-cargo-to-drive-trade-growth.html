Chennai Port targets non-containerised cargo to drive trade growth

📅 2026-09-01 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chennai Port aims to increase non-containerised cargo handling
  • Expansion could boost trade and economic activity in the region
  • Port faces challenges in competing with other major ports in India

Chennai Port's Strategic Shift

Chennai Port, India's fourth-largest port by volume, is pivoting its strategy to focus on non-containerised cargo. This strategic move comes as the port seeks to boost trade and economic activity in the region. The port authority is exploring various options to increase the handling of non-containerised cargo, which includes bulk cargo, breakbulk cargo, and project cargo.

Why Non-Containerised Cargo?

The shift towards non-containerised cargo is driven by several factors. Firstly, non-containerised cargo often has higher value per unit weight compared to containerised cargo. This can result in higher revenue for the port. Secondly, non-containerised cargo can be more flexible in terms of handling and storage, which can be beneficial for certain types of goods. Finally, non-containerised cargo can help to diversify the port's revenue streams, reducing its reliance on containerised cargo.

Challenges Ahead

Despite the potential benefits, the port faces several challenges. One of the main challenges is the lack of infrastructure to handle non-containerised cargo. The port currently has limited facilities for bulk cargo handling, breakbulk cargo handling, and project cargo handling. Additionally, the port is competing with other major ports in India, such as Mumbai Port and Kolkata Port, which have more experience and infrastructure in handling non-containerised cargo.

Expert Opinion

Port analyst Rajiv Kumar from the Institute of Maritime Studies says, 'Chennai Port's strategic shift towards non-containerised cargo is a bold move. It shows the port's commitment to diversifying its revenue streams and boosting trade and economic activity in the region. However, the port will need to invest heavily in infrastructure to compete with other major ports in India.'
