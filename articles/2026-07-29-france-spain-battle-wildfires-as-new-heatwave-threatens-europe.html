France, Spain battle wildfires as new heatwave threatens Europe

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • A new heatwave is raising wildfire risks across southwestern Europe.
  • France has ordered 4,000 additional evacuations as a blaze near Bordeaux grows to four times the size of Paris.
  • The fires are threatening the M51 nuclear missile site and the Rafale fighter jet assembly line.
  • Spain allowed thousands to return home but remains on high alert for new fires.
  • Over a third of a million people have been displaced across the region this summer.

PARIS/MADRID — France and Spain are scrambling to contain massive wildfires as a fresh heatwave sweeps across southwestern Europe, threatening lives, homes and critical military infrastructure.

In France, authorities ordered 4,000 more people to evacuate on Wednesday as a blaze near Bordeaux swelled to an area four times the size of Paris. The flames are now encroaching on sensitive defence installations, including the M51 nuclear missile storage site and the Dassault Aviation assembly line for Rafale fighter jets.

Fires threaten nuclear and military sites

The fire near the Atlantic coast has already consumed more than 20,000 hectares of pine forest. French officials confirmed the flames are advancing toward the military zone, though they said no nuclear material is immediately at risk.

“We are deploying every available resource to protect these critical sites,” a French interior ministry spokesperson said. Hundreds of firefighters backed by water-bombing aircraft are working to create firebreaks.

The M51 missile site, located in the Aquitaine region, houses France's submarine-launched nuclear warheads. The Rafale assembly plant is a key national asset, producing fighter jets for both the French Air Force and export customers.

Spain on edge after mass evacuations

Across the border, Spain allowed thousands of evacuees to return to their homes in the provinces of Zaragoza and Teruel after earlier fires were partially contained. But authorities warned that a new heatwave, with temperatures expected to exceed 40°C, could reignite the danger.

“The situation remains extremely volatile,” said a spokesperson for Spain's emergency services. “We are monitoring every hotspot and have crews on standby.”

In the past week, fires in Spain have scorched over 30,000 hectares, forcing the evacuation of entire villages. The flames have been fuelled by dry vegetation, low humidity and strong winds.

Regional crisis deepens

The new evacuations in France bring the total number of people displaced by wildfires across southwestern Europe this summer to more than a third of a million. The region has been hit by successive heatwaves since June, with temperatures repeatedly breaking records.

Climate scientists have linked the extreme heat to broader changes in global weather patterns. European Union officials have activated the bloc's Civil Protection Mechanism to coordinate cross-border firefighting assistance.

Portugal and Italy are also on high alert, with several large fires reported in recent days. Greek authorities have warned of extreme fire risk in the coming week.

Firefighting teams from Germany, Poland and Sweden have been deployed to the worst-hit areas under a mutual aid agreement. The French government has also requested help from the European fleet of firefighting aircraft.

Local residents described scenes of panic as flames approached their homes. “We had five minutes to grab our documents and leave,” one evacuee near Bordeaux told local media. “The sky was orange, and the air was choking.”

Meteorologists predict the heatwave will persist for at least another week, with little chance of rain. The French weather service has issued red alerts for several southern departments, warning of “dangerous” conditions.

Officials have not yet confirmed the full extent of property damage or financial losses. Emergency services continue to battle multiple active fronts, and containment remains days away.

As the mercury climbs again, authorities across the region brace for what could be the worst wildfire season on record. The immediate focus remains on saving lives and protecting critical infrastructure, but questions are growing over Europe's long-term preparedness for such extreme events.
