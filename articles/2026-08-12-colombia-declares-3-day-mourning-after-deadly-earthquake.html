Colombia declares 3-day mourning after deadly earthquake

📅 2026-08-12 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Colombia's government has announced three days of national mourning following a deadly earthquake.
  • Rescue teams continue to search for survivors, pulling a woman alive from the rubble after 36 hours.
  • China has offered humanitarian relief assistance to Colombia.

National mourning declared

Colombia's government has declared three days of national mourning following a devastating earthquake that struck the country, leaving scores dead and many more injured. The announcement came as rescue workers raced against time to find survivors amid the debris of collapsed buildings.

The quake, which struck with little warning, has caused widespread destruction in several towns and cities. Local authorities have described the situation as "critical", with hospitals overwhelmed and power lines down in many affected areas.

Rescue efforts continue

In a glimmer of hope, rescue teams pulled a woman alive from the rubble 36 hours after the earthquake struck. Her rescue has buoyed spirits among the weary volunteers and emergency personnel who have been working around the clock.

Elsewhere, a baby was saved after rescuers heard a faint whisper from beneath the debris. The infant, pulled out safely, has become a symbol of resilience in the face of tragedy. Yet for every such miracle, there are families still waiting for news of their loved ones.

International support pours in

China has offered humanitarian relief assistance to Colombia, according to state media reports. The offer includes search-and-rescue teams, medical supplies, and emergency funds to help the South American nation cope with the disaster's aftermath.

Other nations and international organisations have also expressed condolences and pledged support. The United Nations has said it is ready to coordinate aid efforts if requested by the Colombian government.

A tale of two Colombias

The earthquake has exposed the stark divide between urban and rural Colombia. In affluent neighbourhoods of major cities, buildings constructed to modern seismic standards largely withstood the tremors. In poorer, informal settlements, homes made of fragile materials crumbled instantly.

This disparity has reignited debates about inequality and disaster preparedness in the country. Analysts note that while Colombia has made strides in building codes, enforcement remains weak in marginalized areas, leaving the most vulnerable populations at greatest risk.

What happens next

As the mourning period begins, the focus will shift from rescue to recovery. Authorities are expected to conduct detailed assessments of the damage in the coming days, which will determine the scale of international aid needed. For now, the nation grieves, and the world watches closely.
