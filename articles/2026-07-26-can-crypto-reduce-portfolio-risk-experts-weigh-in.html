Can crypto reduce portfolio risk? Experts weigh in

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Experts say crypto can reduce portfolio risk with proper allocation and rebalancing.
  • A small, disciplined investment, not speculative trading, is key.
  • Periodic rebalancing and long-term holding are recommended strategies.

The crypto question

Does adding cryptocurrency to your investment portfolio make it less risky? According to financial experts, the answer is yes — but only if done correctly.

The key is not to treat crypto as a get-rich-quick bet. Instead, a disciplined approach with a small allocation and regular rebalancing can actually lower overall portfolio volatility.

Small allocation, big difference

Experts recommend keeping crypto exposure to between 1% and 5% of your total portfolio. This small slice can provide diversification benefits without exposing you to the full brunt of crypto's notorious price swings.

“The idea is to have an asset that moves differently from stocks and bonds,” said one analyst quoted in the report. “When done right, crypto can act as a hedge.”

But the crucial word is “right.” Simply buying Bitcoin or Ethereum and hoping for the best is not a strategy. It is speculation.

Rebalancing is not optional

Periodic rebalancing is essential. If crypto prices soar, your allocation could balloon to 10% or 20% of your portfolio, defeating the purpose of risk reduction. Selling some holdings to bring the percentage back down locks in gains and maintains the intended risk profile.

Conversely, if crypto crashes, rebalancing means buying more at lower prices. This disciplined approach forces investors to sell high and buy low, a basic principle many ignore.

Long-term holding over trading

Day trading crypto is a losing game for most retail investors. Experts stress that the risk-reducing benefits come from long-term holding, not short-term speculation.

“The volatility is real, but if you hold for years, the correlation with traditional assets tends to drop,” the report notes. This low correlation is what makes crypto a potential diversifier.

However, correlation can spike during market crises, so crypto should never be the only hedge in a portfolio.

What to watch next

Regulatory clarity remains a wild card. India and other major economies are still shaping their crypto policies, which could affect how the asset behaves relative to stocks and bonds.

For now, the message is clear: crypto can reduce risk, but only for those who treat it as a small, disciplined part of a broader strategy — not a lottery ticket.
