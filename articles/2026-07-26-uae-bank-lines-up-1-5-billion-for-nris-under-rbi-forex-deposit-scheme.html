UAE bank lines up $1.5 billion for NRIs under RBI forex deposit scheme

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • UAE's top bank offers $1.5 billion for NRIs in RBI's forex deposit scheme
  • Scheme targets dollar and other foreign currency deposits from non-resident Indians
  • Move aims to boost India's foreign exchange reserves and attract diaspora funds

Abu Dhabi: The largest bank in the United Arab Emirates has lined up $1.5 billion specifically for Non-Resident Indians (NRIs) looking to park their money in the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) foreign currency deposit programme. The move comes as India pushes to shore up its forex reserves through targeted schemes aimed at its vast overseas diaspora.

The offer, first reported by The Economic Times, signals growing competition among Gulf lenders to capture NRI savings. The bank, which has not been named in the report, is expected to roll out the facility in the coming weeks.

What the RBI scheme offers

The RBI's foreign currency deposit programme allows NRIs to open accounts and hold deposits in foreign currencies such as US dollars, pounds sterling and euros. These deposits earn interest in the same currency, protecting savers from rupee depreciation.

The scheme has been a key tool for India to attract foreign capital without taking on exchange rate risk. For NRIs in the UAE, where salaries are often paid in dirhams, the programme offers a way to repatriate savings without conversion costs.

The $1.5 billion commitment from the UAE bank is one of the largest single pledges by a foreign lender under this programme.

Why UAE banks are betting big on NRIs

The UAE hosts one of the largest concentrations of Indian expatriates, estimated at over 3.5 million. Remittances from the Gulf region account for a significant chunk of India's inward flows, often exceeding $20 billion annually.

By offering dedicated funds for NRI deposits, UAE banks are positioning themselves as intermediaries between the Indian government and the diaspora. The move also helps these lenders strengthen their relationship with high-net-worth clients who often maintain multiple accounts across borders.

Banking analysts note that the $1.5 billion allocation is a strategic bet on the stability of the Indian rupee and the RBI's ability to manage external debt.

Impact on India's forex reserves

India's foreign exchange reserves have seen fluctuations in recent months, driven by global rate hikes and commodity price volatility. The RBI has been actively seeking new sources of dollar inflows to cushion the currency.

Programmes like this one allow the central bank to build a buffer without issuing sovereign bonds or drawing down existing reserves. For the government, every billion dollars in NRI deposits helps narrow the current account deficit.

The UAE bank's commitment, if fully subscribed, would add roughly 0.5% to India's current forex kitty of around $600 billion.

What NRIs should watch out for

Interest rates on these deposits will be determined by the RBI and the bank, likely pegged to prevailing international benchmarks such as SOFR or LIBOR. NRIs should compare rates across lenders and check for any early withdrawal penalties.

Tax treatment remains a key consideration. Interest earned on NRE (Non-Resident External) accounts is tax-free in India, but the same does not apply to all foreign currency deposit variants. Experts recommend consulting a chartered accountant before committing large sums.

Currency risk, though minimised by the foreign currency denomination, still exists if the depositor needs to convert back to rupees at a future date.

The roll-out is expected within the next quarter. NRIs in the UAE should watch for official announcements from the bank and the RBI regarding specific interest rates, tenure options and minimum deposit thresholds.
