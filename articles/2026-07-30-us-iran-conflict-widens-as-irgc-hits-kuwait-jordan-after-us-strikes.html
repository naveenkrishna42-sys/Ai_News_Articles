US-Iran Conflict Widens as IRGC Hits Kuwait, Jordan After US Strikes

📅 2026-07-30 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran's IRGC says it has hit targets in Kuwait and Jordan following deadly US strikes on Iranian positions.
  • US troops were killed and F-35 jets destroyed in an IRGC strike on an American base in Jordan.
  • US launched retaliatory strikes on Iranian targets after foiling a missile attack on its forces.
  • President Trump says he will hit Iran hard after the attack and was briefed on an incident involving an Egyptian tanker.

Conflict Escalates Beyond Borders

The simmering US-Iran conflict has taken a dramatic turn, with Tehran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claiming responsibility for attacks on Kuwait and Jordan. This marks a significant expansion of the battlefield, moving beyond the usual flashpoints in Iraq and Syria.

The IRGC statement came hours after the United States launched strikes on Iranian targets. The Pentagon said the operation was a direct response to a foiled missile attack on American forces in the region, though it did not provide further details on the location of that incident.

American Base Hit, Casualties Reported

The most damaging blow for Washington came in Jordan. An IRGC strike on a US military base in the kingdom reportedly killed several American troops and destroyed multiple F-35 fighter jets. The exact number of casualties has not been officially confirmed by US Central Command, but the loss of advanced aircraft represents a serious tactical setback.

This is the first time the IRGC has publicly claimed a direct hit on Jordanian soil, a country that has long served as a stable rear base for US operations in the Middle East. The attack raises questions about the vulnerability of American assets even in allied nations.

Trump Vows Retaliation, Tanker Incident Eyed

President Donald Trump has responded with characteristic bellicosity, stating he will “hit Iran hard” in response to the attacks. The White House said Trump was also briefed on a separate incident involving an Egyptian tanker, though officials have not yet confirmed any link between that event and the broader conflict.

The president's statement did not specify the nature of the planned retaliation. However, US military assets in the Persian Gulf and the Arabian Sea are on high alert, and defense analysts expect a significant escalation in the coming hours.

Regional Fallout and What Comes Next

The widening of the conflict to Kuwait and Jordan has alarmed Gulf states. Both countries host US military personnel and facilities, and their direct involvement in the fighting could trigger a broader regional war. Diplomatic channels remain open, but no immediate ceasefire talks have been announced.

The coming days will likely see a furious cycle of retaliation. Washington must decide whether to strike Iranian soil directly or continue targeting IRGC positions in proxy theaters. Tehran, emboldened by its ability to strike US bases, is unlikely to back down. The world watches as the Middle East teeters on the edge of a wider conflagration.
