NEET protest at Bengaluru's Freedom Park leads to FIR over posters

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Protesters gathered at Freedom Park to oppose NEET examination system.
  • Controversial posters at the event sparked political tensions.
  • Police registered an FIR regarding the posters; no arrests reported.

Protest at Freedom Park draws crowds

A sizable crowd gathered at Bengaluru's Freedom Park on Monday to protest against the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET). The demonstration, part of a broader movement against the medical entrance exam, saw participants from student groups and political organisations.

The protest remained largely peaceful for most of the day. However, the event took a turn when certain posters displayed at the venue sparked a sharp political reaction.

Posters ignite political row

The posters, which appeared at the protest site, contained slogans and imagery that drew criticism from rival political factions. Leaders from the ruling party accused the protest organisers of using the platform to spread divisive messages.

Opposition leaders countered, alleging that the posters were planted to discredit the protest. They demanded an impartial inquiry into the matter. The exchange of accusations quickly escalated into a full-blown political row.

FIR registered by police

Bengaluru police registered an FIR late on Monday evening in connection with the controversial posters. The case has been filed under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code.

Police officials have stated that they are reviewing CCTV footage and gathering statements from witnesses. No arrests have been made so far. Authorities have not confirmed the exact wording or content of the posters that led to the complaint.

What happened at the protest

The demonstration at Freedom Park was one of several held across the state demanding the scrapping of NEET. Students argued that the exam places undue pressure on aspirants and favours those with access to expensive coaching.

The protest included speeches and a march within the park. Organisers claimed the turnout exceeded expectations, though police did not provide an official crowd estimate.

Political reactions pour in

State BJP leaders condemned the posters, calling them an insult to national symbols. They demanded strict action against those responsible. The Congress party, which has been organising anti-NEET agitations, distanced itself from the posters.

“We do not support any form of provocative material. Our protest is peaceful and lawful,” a Congress spokesperson said. Meanwhile, other parties called for a thorough investigation to identify the individuals behind the posters.

The incident has deepened the existing rift between the ruling and opposition camps over the NEET issue. The exam has been a flashpoint in Karnataka politics for months.

What to watch next

The police investigation is in its early stages. Political temperatures are expected to remain high as both sides await the findings. The next round of anti-NEET protests is already being planned, with organisers vowing to avoid any controversy.
