China, Pakistan Urge US-Iran Talks as West Asia Tensions Escalate

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • China and Pakistan jointly urge US and Iran to return to negotiating table
  • Chinese UN envoy says US actions have pushed the region to 'dangerous precipice'
  • Trump administration reinstates blockade of Hormuz Strait, disrupting global oil trade

China and Pakistan have made a joint appeal for the United States and Iran to resume talks, as the conflict in West Asia continues to escalate. The call comes amid a fresh wave of US strikes on Iranian targets and the reinstatement of a naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz by the Trump administration.

China warns of ‘dangerous precipice’

China’s UN envoy, speaking at an emergency session of the Security Council, accused the United States of pushing the region to a ‘dangerous precipice’ with its ongoing military campaign against Iran. The envoy urged Washington to immediately halt what he described as ‘acts of aggression’ and return to diplomacy.

“The US has taken the entire Middle East to the brink of a wider war,” the envoy said. “This is not a path to peace. It is a path to catastrophe.” China has consistently called for restraint from all sides, even as the US has intensified its bombing campaign in response to Iranian-linked attacks on American forces in the region.

Pakistan backs diplomatic push

Pakistan’s foreign ministry issued a statement echoing Beijing’s call, saying that ‘dialogue and diplomacy remain the only viable options’ to de-escalate the crisis. Islamabad has deep ties with both Saudi Arabia and Iran, and has sought to position itself as a mediator in the wider conflict.

“Pakistan believes that the use of force will only deepen the cycle of violence,” the statement read. “We urge both Washington and Tehran to show restraint and return to the negotiating table without preconditions.” The joint call from Beijing and Islamabad reflects growing anxiety among Asian capitals about the economic and security fallout from the war.

Hormuz blockade disrupts global oil markets

The Trump administration has reinstated a full naval blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway through which roughly a fifth of the world’s oil passes. The blockade is intended to cut off Iranian oil exports, but it has also disrupted shipments from other Gulf states, sending crude prices soaring.

China, the world’s largest oil importer, has been hit particularly hard. Beijing has called for ‘normal passage’ through the strait and warned that the blockade amounts to an act of economic warfare. Analysts say the blockade could push China to deepen its strategic partnership with Iran, including through increased military cooperation.

The US Navy’s Fifth Fleet, based in Bahrain, has been enforcing the blockade with destroyers and patrol aircraft. Iranian commanders have threatened to retaliate by targeting US warships and closing the strait to all traffic, though they have so far not carried out that threat.

What to watch

Diplomatic efforts are expected to intensify in the coming days, with the UN Security Council likely to hold further emergency sessions. Whether Washington and Tehran are willing to return to talks, however, remains uncertain. Both sides have indicated they will only negotiate from a position of strength, making a quick resolution unlikely. The global oil market, meanwhile, remains on edge.
