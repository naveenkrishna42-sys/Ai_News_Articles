Trump made $1.4 billion from crypto in a year; Justin Sun link emerges

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Donald Trump's crypto earnings hit $1.4 billion in a single year.
  • Justin Sun, founder of Tron, is suspected to have facilitated key deals.
  • The scale of the earnings raises questions about crypto's role in political finance.

Donald Trump has reportedly earned $1.4 billion from cryptocurrency ventures in the past year, a staggering sum that has drawn scrutiny to the intersection of digital assets and political fundraising. The figure, disclosed in financial documents reviewed by The Guardian, marks one of the largest single-year crypto hauls by a political figure globally.

The revelation has focused attention on Justin Sun, the Chinese-born founder of the Tron blockchain. Sun, a controversial figure in the crypto world, is suspected by investigators of being the architect behind several of Trump's most lucrative token deals. Neither Trump nor Sun has commented on the specific allegations.

A year of explosive growth

Trump's crypto earnings come primarily from a series of ventures launched over the past 18 months. These include a non-fungible token collection, a decentralized finance platform, and a memecoin that saw explosive trading volume during the 2026 midterm campaign season.

The $1.4 billion figure dwarfs the earnings of most publicly traded companies in the same period. Analysts say the sum is unprecedented for a former president actively eyeing another White House run. The money flowed through a complex web of shell companies and crypto wallets, making it difficult to trace the ultimate beneficiaries.

The Sun connection

Justin Sun's alleged involvement stems from his role as an advisor to a shell entity that handled the tokenomics for Trump's NFT project. Sources close to the matter say Sun personally negotiated the fee structure that allowed Trump to pocket a disproportionate share of trading fees and token sales.

Sun has a history of high-profile partnerships, including a failed attempt to buy a banana taped to a wall for $6.2 million. His Tron network has been criticised for its centralised control and frequent technical glitches. If confirmed, the Trump-Sun tie-up would mark one of the most significant crossovers between mainstream politics and the crypto fringe.

Regulatory concerns

The scale of the earnings has alarmed regulators. The Securities and Exchange Commission is believed to be examining whether the token offerings violated securities laws. Trump has not registered any of the crypto products with the SEC, relying on exemptions that critics say are being stretched.

Congressional Democrats have called for a formal investigation. "This is not a donation; it's a profit," said Senator Elizabeth Warren in a statement. "If a former president can make $1.4 billion in unregulated crypto schemes, the system is broken." Republicans have largely stayed silent, though a few have expressed concern about the optics.

What Sun stands to gain

If Sun indeed orchestrated the deals, his motives are not purely altruistic. Tron's native token, TRX, has seen a surge in trading volume since the Trump ventures went live. The association with a US political heavyweight could lend legitimacy to a platform often dismissed as a playground for speculators.

Sun also gains access to a powerful network. Trump's base includes millions of retail investors who are new to crypto, a demographic that Sun's projects have long courted. The symbiotic relationship benefits both men financially, but the ethical lines remain blurred.

What happens next

With the 2028 election cycle approaching, Trump's crypto earnings are likely to become a central issue. Expect the SEC to issue subpoenas in the coming weeks. Justin Sun's travel to the United States, already restricted due to previous regulatory run-ins, may become even more complicated. The full extent of the partnership may only emerge through lawsuits or whistleblowers.
