Supreme Court warns against politicising Ram Mandir fund theft case

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court termed the Ram Mandir fund theft case as a simple crime, not a political issue
  • Court warned against politicising the matter and suggested setting up a Special Investigation Team (SIT)
  • Opposition parties had demanded a probe into Ayodhya finances over donation theft allegations

The Supreme Court on Monday stepped into the controversy surrounding the alleged theft of donations meant for the Ram Mandir in Ayodhya, calling it a straightforward criminal case and warning against any attempt to politicise the matter.

A bench headed by the Chief Justice of India made it clear that the court will not allow the case to be turned into a political slugfest. “This is only a case of commission of crime. Do not politicise it,” the bench observed.

Court suggests SIT probe

During the hearing, the Supreme Court suggested the formation of a Special Investigation Team (SIT) to look into the allegations of fund misappropriation. The court indicated that a dedicated team would ensure a fair and thorough investigation, insulated from external pressures.

The suggestion comes amid rising political temperatures over the issue. Opposition parties have been demanding a comprehensive probe into the finances of the Ram Janmabhoomi Teerth Kshetra Trust, which oversees the temple’s construction and donations.

Political fallout

The controversy erupted after reports surfaced that a significant amount of donations collected for the Ram Mandir construction had been allegedly stolen or misappropriated. Opposition leaders quickly seized on the reports, calling for a high-level inquiry and accusing the trust of a lack of transparency.

“We need a full-fledged investigation into how the money was handled. The devotees who donated have a right to know where their money went,” a senior opposition leader said. The demand for a probe has been echoed by several parties, who have also questioned the role of trust officials.

The ruling party, however, has dismissed the allegations as politically motivated. Party spokespersons have argued that the trust is managed by respected figures and that any wrongdoing should be dealt with by law, not in the court of public opinion.

Court’s firm stance

The Supreme Court’s remarks on Monday sought to cut through the political noise. The bench emphasised that the case should be treated as a simple crime — theft — and not as a tool for political point-scoring.

“Let the investigation proceed on its own merits. The court will not allow this to become a platform for political debate,” the Chief Justice said. The observation was seen as a rebuke to both sides attempting to use the case for political advantage.

The court also noted that the faith of millions of devotees should not be undermined by such controversies. The sanctity of the temple project, it said, must be preserved.

What lies ahead

The Supreme Court is expected to issue formal orders on the formation of the SIT in the coming days. Until then, the case will remain under judicial scrutiny, with all parties directed to cooperate with the investigation.

The development marks a significant turn in a case that had quickly spiralled from a financial irregularity into a full-blown political controversy. With the court now firmly in control, the focus shifts to the investigation — and away from the war of words.
