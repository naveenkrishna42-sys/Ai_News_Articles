Two arrested for using manja thread kites in New Washermenpet

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two people were arrested in New Washermenpet for flying kites with manja thread.
  • The banned nylon thread can cause severe injuries to humans and animals.
  • Police have warned of strict action against the use of manja thread.

Chennai police arrested two men on Monday for flying kites using banned manja thread in the New Washermenpet area. The arrests come as part of an ongoing crackdown on the use of the hazardous nylon thread, which has been prohibited due to the severe risk it poses to humans and birds.

The accused were found flying kites with the sharp, glass-coated thread in the afternoon, prompting local residents to alert the authorities. Police teams reached the spot and took the two men into custody. They have been booked under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code and the Environment Protection Act.

Manja thread: a recurring menace

Manja thread, often coated with powdered glass and other abrasive materials, is widely used during kite-flying festivals, especially in the run-up to Pongal. Despite repeated bans by the state government and the National Green Tribunal, the thread continues to appear in markets and on rooftops across the city.

The thread is notorious for causing deep cuts to pedestrians, cyclists, and two-wheeler riders. Several deaths have been reported in the past due to throat slits caused by manja. Birds, particularly kites and pigeons, are also frequent victims, often dying after getting entangled in the sharp line.

Police intensify patrolling

The Greater Chennai Police have been conducting regular patrols and surprise checks in residential neighbourhoods, especially during the kite-flying season. Officials say that flying kites with manja thread is a cognisable offence, and those caught will face legal consequences.

“We have received multiple complaints from the public about manja thread injuries,” a senior police officer said. “Our teams have been instructed to act swiftly against anyone found using it.” The officer added that the police are also coordinating with local vendors to trace the supply chain of manja thread in the city.

Public response and awareness

Residents of New Washermenpet and surrounding areas have welcomed the arrests, expressing hope that such actions will deter others from using the dangerous thread. Many have called for stricter enforcement, noting that the ban has often been flouted with impunity.

Animal welfare groups have also urged the authorities to remain vigilant. They point out that the thread is a major threat to the city's bird population, and that the recent arrests send a clear signal to those who disregard the law.

In recent years, the Tamil Nadu government has encouraged the use of cotton or nylon-free threads during festivals, but the practice has yet to catch on widely. Awareness campaigns have been launched in schools and public spaces, but the message often fades once the festive season ends.

What happens next

The two accused are expected to be produced before a magistrate later today. Police have said they will seek strict punishment to serve as a deterrent. Meanwhile, officials have hinted at more intensive checks in the coming weeks, particularly in areas known for kite flying.

The public, too, has a role to play. Those who spot anyone using manja thread can report it to the police control room, and authorities have promised swift action. As the next kite-flying season approaches, the focus will remain on ensuring that the skies stay safe for everyone — on the ground and above it.
