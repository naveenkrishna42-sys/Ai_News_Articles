Experts Urge WHO to Declare Climate Crisis a Global Public Health Emergency

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Environment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 200 health experts and organisations have petitioned the WHO to declare a global public health emergency due to climate change.
  • The petition highlights increasing health impacts like heat-related deaths, infectious disease spread, and air pollution.
  • WHO has not yet responded officially, but the call adds pressure for stronger global health action on climate change.

A coalition of over 200 health experts and organisations has formally urged the World Health Organization (WHO) to declare the climate crisis a global public health emergency. The call, reported by The Guardian, comes amid mounting evidence that rising temperatures are directly harming human health across the world.

The petition argues that climate change is no longer just an environmental issue. It is a medical crisis unfolding in real time. Heatwaves, the spread of vector-borne diseases, and respiratory illnesses from air pollution are already overwhelming healthcare systems in several countries.

Health Impacts Accelerating

The experts point to data showing a sharp rise in heat-related deaths in India, Europe, and parts of Africa. Dengue and malaria are appearing in regions where they were previously rare. The WHO has not yet issued a formal response, but the timing of the petition is significant.

Global temperatures have risen by over 1.2 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels. Even a small additional increase, the experts warn, could push millions more people into health emergencies. The petition specifically asks the WHO to activate its highest alert level, which would unlock international funding and coordination mechanisms.

What a Public Health Emergency Declaration Would Mean

If the WHO declares a global public health emergency, it would trigger a coordinated international response. This could include faster deployment of vaccines, heat-health action plans, and stricter air quality standards. The declaration would also pressure governments to treat climate adaptation as a health priority.

India, one of the most climate-vulnerable nations, has already seen record-breaking heatwaves in recent years. Hospitals in several states reported a surge in heatstroke cases last summer. Experts say a WHO emergency declaration could accelerate India's own heat-health action plans and funding for cooling centres.

Political and Economic Hurdles

Not everyone is convinced. Some governments fear that declaring a public health emergency could lead to trade restrictions or divert resources from other health crises. The WHO has previously declared emergencies for Ebola, Zika, and Covid-19, but never for a slow-moving crisis like climate change.

The petition's authors acknowledge these concerns but argue that the scale of the threat demands bold action. They note that climate change already kills an estimated 150,000 people annually, a figure that is expected to rise sharply in the coming decades.

The WHO's executive board is expected to discuss the petition in its next meeting. No date has been set yet. Officials have not confirmed whether the request will be taken up immediately or referred to a committee.

What happens next depends on political will. The experts have made their case. The world's health body now has to decide whether to sound the alarm.
