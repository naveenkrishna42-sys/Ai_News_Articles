Delhi HC stays FSSAI order barring Dabur from '100% pure' claims

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi HC stays FSSAI order against Dabur's '100%' claims on food products
  • Interim relief granted to Dabur, which had moved court against the regulatory directive
  • Case highlights ongoing scrutiny of advertising claims in India's food industry

Court grants interim relief to Dabur

The Delhi High Court has stayed a Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) order that barred Dabur from using '100 per cent' claims on its food products. The interim stay comes as a breather for the FMCG major, which had moved the court challenging the regulator's directive.

Dabur had approached the High Court after FSSAI directed the company to halt the sale of food products carrying '100%' labels. The regulator's order raised questions about the accuracy and substantiation of such claims under the country's food safety norms.

While the court has not yet delivered a final verdict, the stay means Dabur can continue using its existing labels for now. The matter is likely to be heard further in the coming weeks.

What the FSSAI order said

FSSAI had issued the directive asking Dabur to withdraw '100 per cent' claims from its food products. The watchdog's move was part of a broader scrutiny of misleading advertising and labelling practices in the packaged food sector.

According to the regulator, such absolute claims require rigorous scientific backing. In the absence of clear substantiation, they could mislead consumers about the purity or composition of a product.

Dabur, however, argued that its claims were genuine and supported by internal quality checks. The company contended that the order was disproportionate and could harm its brand equity.

Legal and industry implications

The Delhi High Court's stay is significant not just for Dabur but for the wider food and beverage industry. Many companies routinely use superlatives like '100%' or 'pure' in their marketing, and the case could set a precedent for how such claims are treated.

Legal experts say the ruling underscores the need for a balanced approach—one that protects consumer interests without stifling legitimate advertising. The FSSAI's stance reflects growing regulatory vigilance, but courts are also examining whether such bans are backed by adequate evidence.

For Dabur, which sells products ranging from juices to honey, the interim relief allows business continuity. However, the company may still need to revisit its labelling if the final order goes against it.

What happens next

The case will now proceed in the Delhi High Court, with both sides expected to file detailed submissions. The final judgment could clarify the standards for using '100%' claims on food products in India.

Industry observers will watch closely, as the outcome could influence labelling norms across the sector. For consumers, the dispute highlights the importance of reading labels carefully—even when a product carries an absolute promise.
