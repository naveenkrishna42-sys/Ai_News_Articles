TCS iON Admits to Power Failure at Jaipur NEET-PG Exam Centres

TCS iON, the company managing the computer-based testing for the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test for Postgraduate (NEET-PG) 2026, has admitted to a power failure at exam centres in Jaipur. The company has expressed its 'deep regret' over the incident and is working to fix it 'urgently'.

AIIMS CRE Exam Cancelled Due to Power Failure

The power failure at the Jaipur exam centres has also affected the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS) Common Recruitment Examination (CRE) exam, which has been cancelled. This is the second time in a row that the Jaipur exam centre has faced a power failure, raising concerns about the reliability of computer-based testing.

Back-to-Back Glitches at Jaipur Exam Centre Raise Concerns

The back-to-back glitches at the Jaipur exam centre have brought computer-based testing under scrutiny. Experts are questioning the reliability of the system, especially after the recent incidents. The power failure is not the only issue that the exam centre has faced. There have been reports of technical problems and paper leaks, which have further complicated the situation.

PIB Flags Misleading Claims Over NEET PG Paper Leak in Jaipur

The Press Information Bureau (PIB) has flagged misleading claims over the NEET PG paper leak in Jaipur. The PIB has issued a statement clarifying that there is no evidence of any paper leak. The statement has been issued to set the record straight and to ensure that the candidates are not misled.

Over 2.63 Lakh Candidates Appear for NEET PG 2026 Nationwide

Despite the glitches and the power failure, over 2.63 lakh candidates have appeared for the NEET PG 2026 nationwide. The exam was conducted in a computer-based format, which is a significant change from the traditional pen-and-paper method. The shift to computer-based testing has been a topic of debate, with some experts arguing that it is more reliable and efficient, while others are concerned about the potential for technical issues.
