RBI Financial Inclusion Index rises 4.48% in FY26, shows broad-based growth

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's Financial Inclusion Index rose 4.48% in FY26 to 69.2 from 66.2 in FY25.
  • All three sub-indices — access, usage and quality — showed uniform improvement.
  • Growth was broad-based across urban and rural areas, with significant gains in digital payments and insurance penetration.

The Reserve Bank of India's Financial Inclusion Index rose 4.48% in the financial year 2025-26, reaching 69.2 from 66.2 in the previous year. This marks the second consecutive year of accelerated growth after a slower 2.9% increase in FY25.

The index, which measures the depth and reach of banking, investment, insurance and pension services across the country, has climbed steadily since its base year of 2017. The latest reading puts it nearly 19 points above the 2017 baseline of 50.

All three pillars show uniform gains

The index is built on three sub-indices: access (35% weight), usage (45% weight) and quality (20% weight). For FY26, each sub-index recorded a proportional rise. The access sub-index improved to 75.1, usage moved to 63.8, and quality touched 70.4.

This uniform movement suggests that the expansion is not limited to one dimension, said officials familiar with the data. The RBI noted that the growth was "broad-based" across all geographical regions and population segments.

Rural and urban growth converge

A key takeaway from the data is that rural areas matched urban centres in the pace of financial inclusion. The number of basic savings bank deposit accounts per 1,000 adults rose in both settings, while the percentage of adults with at least one active loan account also improved.

Digital payments infrastructure expanded rapidly. The number of ATMs per 1,00,000 adults and the number of banking outlets in villages with population below 2,000 both saw notable increases. The RBI said the ratio of digital transactions to total transactions crossed 80% for the first time.

Insurance and pension penetration improve

Insurance penetration, measured as the number of life and non-life policies per 1,000 adults, showed a steady upward trend. The pension sub-index also improved, driven by higher enrolment in the Atal Pension Yojana and the National Pension System.

Quality indicators such as the percentage of adults with at least one active credit account and the share of districts with more than 90% financial inclusion coverage both rose. The RBI pointed to improved customer grievance redressal mechanisms as a contributing factor.

What the index measures

The Financial Inclusion Index is a composite measure that captures three dimensions: availability of banking services, actual usage by customers, and the quality of services including financial literacy and consumer protection. It draws data from multiple sources including banks, insurance companies, pension funds and regulatory filings.

The index is updated annually. The RBI has been publishing it since 2021 to track the progress of financial inclusion beyond simple account penetration. The current reading of 69.2 means India still has room to improve, especially on usage and quality compared to access.

What to watch next

The RBI will now focus on deepening usage in under-penetrated districts and improving last-mile delivery in the north-eastern states. The next index reading, due in July 2027, will show whether the uniform growth can be sustained as the base gets higher.
