Delhi Police Detain 18 CJP Leaders at IGI Airport in Major Crackdown

Delhi Police Launch Coordinated Crackdown on CJP Leaders

In a dramatic early morning operation, Delhi Police detained 18 leaders of the Chief Electoral Officer Watch (CJP) at Indira Gandhi International Airport, marking one of the largest single-location arrests in recent memory. The operation, which began around 6:30 AM, involved over 100 officers deployed across Terminals 2 and 3, effectively paralyzing passenger movement for nearly two hours.

The detentions come amid escalating tensions over alleged irregularities in the ongoing state assembly elections and CJP's planned protest at Jantar Mantar demanding transparency in the electoral process. Police sources confirmed that the arrested individuals were transported in three SUVs and dispersed to separate lockups in North, South, and East Delhi to prevent coordinated resistance.

Operational Tactics and Response

According to eyewitness accounts and footage shared by journalists present at the scene, plainclothes officers moved in swiftly upon the arrival of a delegation of CJP leaders who had flown in from Mumbai and Bangalore. The group included prominent activists and legal observers monitoring the election process. Video clips show uniformed personnel forming human chains and directing passengers away from the main exit gates.

The Indian Express reported that some detainees were held at the airport lounge for over 90 minutes before being moved. Legal experts have raised concerns over whether the detentions violated the Supreme Court's interim order dated October 8, which directed authorities not to impede peaceful assembly or arrest individuals without sufficient cause.

National Outcry and Media Reaction

Rahul Gandhi, speaking outside the AICC headquarters, condemned the arrests as "an assault on democratic dissent" and announced a march to the Supreme Court demanding immediate release of the detainees. Journalists covering the event were briefly detained but later released after intervention by the Press Club of India.

International media outlets including Reuters and Al Jazeera, along with domestic publications such as The Hindu and Alt News, have flagged what they describe as a pattern of press restrictions and alleged police excesses during the past week of protests. Rights organizations like Amnesty International have called for an independent investigation into the use of force.

Legal Ramifications and Next Steps

All 18 detainees were produced before the Metropolitan Magistrate’s Court in Rouse Avenue, where bail pleas were filed by their legal representatives. As of press time, no formal charges have been disclosed, though authorities indicated that sections related to unlawful assembly and obstruction may apply under the Indian Penal Code. Meanwhile, CJP has reiterated its 24-hour ultimatum to the Delhi Police to confirm a date and time for their proposed Jantar Mantar demonstration.

Real-World Impact on Democratic Discourse

This incident underscores growing friction between civil society watchdogs and law enforcement agencies during critical electoral periods. For voters and observers tracking the integrity of the democratic process, such confrontations raise questions about accountability mechanisms and the space available for civic engagement. The coming days will likely see renewed legal battles and potential interventions by constitutional bodies.
