PM Modi's BRICS Gesture to Xi Jinping: What the Video Reveals About India-China Ties

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-13 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi interrupted his own BRICS address to ask after Xi Jinping, a gesture that signals India's continued push for stable bilateral channels despite unresolved border friction.
  • Xi skipped the dinner hosted by PM Modi on Day 1, underlining that symbolic warmth has not translated into restored diplomatic normalcy.
  • Xi's five-point action plan on AI, smart manufacturing and 'Greater BRICS' expansion carries strategic implications for India's tech and trade positioning.

A Pause That Said More Than the Speech

Prime Minister Narendra Modi stopped mid-address at the BRICS summit to ask after Chinese President Xi Jinping, prompting an exchange captured on video and circulated widely by NDTV and other outlets. The moment was brief, but in summit diplomacy such pauses are read closely. India and China share a 3,488-km contested frontier and have yet to fully normalise ties after the 2020 Galwan clash.

The gesture matters because it was unscripted. Prepared remarks rarely accommodate spontaneous welfare checks on a rival head of state. New Delhi's messaging here is consistent with its stated position since 2024: border peace is the precondition for broader engagement, but the door stays open.

Why Xi Skipped the Leaders' Dinner

On Day 1, Xi did not attend the dinner hosted by PM Modi, a detail NDTV flagged separately. Protocol absences at multilateral summits are rarely accidental. They allow a leader to withhold personal endorsement without triggering a formal diplomatic incident.

Read alongside the speech pause, the two events form a familiar pattern: public civility, private distance. For Indian businesses with China-linked supply chains, this is the operative reality — engagement continues at the working level even as the political ceiling stays capped.

Xi's Five-Point Action Plan: The Substance Behind the Optics

Xi used the summit to advance a five-point plan centred on AI cooperation, smart manufacturing, trade facilitation and a 'Greater BRICS' framework. Chinese state messaging and The Hindu's reporting both emphasise technology as the bloc's next integration frontier.

For India, the plan cuts two ways. Multilateral AI standards-setting could reduce dependence on Western frameworks. But a China-anchored technology stack inside BRICS risks locking Indian firms into Chinese hardware, cloud and payment rails — a concern that has shaped India's telecom and app policies since 2020.

What This Means for Indian Buyers and Industry

Indian electronics and EV manufacturers import substantial Chinese components — cells, PCBs, rare-earth magnets and machine tools. Any BRICS trade-facilitation track that lowers tariffs or speeds customs clearance could cut landed costs on these inputs by 3-7% within a single fiscal year.

The counter-risk is standardisation. If BRICS adopts Chinese-origin AI and smart-manufacturing protocols, Indian MSMEs may face retooling costs in the ₹5-15 lakh range per production line to stay compliant. That is real money for a tier-2 supplier in Pune or Coimbatore.

  • Positive signal: Modi's spontaneous check on Xi keeps a personal channel open, useful for de-escalation.
  • Negative signal: The dinner skip shows Xi is not ready to convert optics into concessions.
  • Commercial upside: AI and trade facilitation could lower input costs for Indian importers.
  • Commercial risk: Chinese technology standards may impose compliance costs on Indian MSMEs.
  • Watch point: Whether border talks resume at the same pace as summit-level engagement.

The Bottom Line for India

Symbolism at BRICS does not substitute for border disengagement, and both Delhi and Beijing know it. The video of Modi pausing is worth noting precisely because it is an exception in a relationship defined by caution.

Indian buyers, importers and manufacturers should treat the summit as a signal, not a settlement. Track the AI and trade workstreams for cost implications, but keep contingency sourcing for critical Chinese inputs. Until the frontier sees verifiable de-escalation, the diplomatic warmth on display remains tactical rather than structural.
