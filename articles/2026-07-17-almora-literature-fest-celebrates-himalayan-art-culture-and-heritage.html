Almora literature fest celebrates Himalayan art, culture and heritage

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • The festival focuses on Himalayan art, culture and heritage.
  • It brings together writers, artists and thinkers from across India.
  • The event highlights the unique cultural identity of the Kumaon region.

A celebration in the hills

The Almora Literature Festival has returned to the Himalayan town, drawing writers, artists and cultural enthusiasts from across India. Held against the backdrop of the snow-capped peaks, the festival aims to spotlight the rich artistic and literary traditions of the region.

Organisers said this year's edition places a special emphasis on the cultural heritage of the Kumaon hills. The event has become a platform for voices from the mountains, often overshadowed by mainstream literary gatherings in the plains.

Voices from the mountains

Panels and discussions at the festival cover a wide range of topics — from folk tales and oral traditions to contemporary writing in Hindi and regional languages. Authors and poets are reading from their works, while local musicians perform traditional Kumaoni songs.

One session focused on the preservation of Pahari languages, which face the threat of decline in an increasingly connected world. Speakers argued that literature festivals in smaller towns can play a crucial role in keeping these languages alive.

Art and craft on display

Beyond literature, the festival features exhibitions of local handicrafts, including woollen shawls, copperware and wood carvings. Artisans from nearby villages have set up stalls, demonstrating techniques passed down through generations.

Workshops on traditional painting styles and storytelling techniques are also part of the programme. The idea, organisers said, is to create a space where art and conversation feed into each other.

A growing movement

The Almora Literature Festival is part of a wider trend of literary events emerging in smaller Indian towns. Unlike the major festivals in metros, these gatherings often have a more intimate, community-driven feel.

Attendance has grown steadily over the years. Locals and tourists alike have been seen crowding the venues, making the most of the cool October weather and the chance to engage with ideas that matter to the hills.

What happens next: The festival will continue for two more days, with sessions on environmental writing and the role of women in Himalayan literature scheduled. Organisers hope the conversations started here will echo well beyond the valley.
