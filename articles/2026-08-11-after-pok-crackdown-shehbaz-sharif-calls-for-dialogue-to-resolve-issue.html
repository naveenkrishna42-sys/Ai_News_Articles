After POK crackdown, Shehbaz Sharif calls for dialogue to resolve issues

📅 2026-08-11 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shehbaz Sharif advocates dialogue to resolve issues after PoK crackdown.
  • Pakistan's stance shifts from calling protesters 'enemies' to 'patriotic brothers'.
  • PML-N on back foot in PoK, calls protesters 'patriots' ahead of elections.
  • AJK polls peaceful, PML-N poised to form government, says Rana Sanaullah.

Sharif's Call for Dialogue

Pakistan Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif has said that 'issues' should be resolved through dialogue, in a statement that comes days after a crackdown in Pakistan-Occupied Kashmir (PoK). The remark marks a notable shift in tone from the government's earlier强硬 approach.

Speaking to media, Sharif did not specify which issues he was referring to, but his words are being read as an attempt to de-escalate tensions in the region. Analysts see this as a strategic move ahead of the upcoming elections in PoK.

From 'Enemies' to 'Patriots'

The change in rhetoric is stark. Earlier, protesters in PoK were labelled as 'enemies' by Islamabad. Now, they are being called 'patriotic brothers'. This U-turn has raised eyebrows, with critics calling it a desperate bid to win support.

Shehbaz Sharif's party, the PML-N, appears to be on the back foot in PoK. The party has been forced to recalibrate its stance, acknowledging the protesters' grievances. This shift is seen as a response to mounting pressure on the ground.

Protesters Now 'Patriots'

The PML-N's flip-flop is evident. Just weeks ago, the same protesters were denounced as troublemakers. Now, party leaders are calling them 'patriots'. This has confused many, but it also signals a recognition of the protesters' influence.

Political observers note that this is a classic pre-election manoeuvre. With polls approaching, the PML-N is trying to mend fences with a population it had earlier alienated. Whether this will work remains to be seen.

Elections in PoK

The Ajit Jhelum Kashmir (AJK) polls are being held amidst this backdrop. Rana Sanaullah, a senior PML-N leader, has claimed that the polls are peaceful and that his party is poised to form the government.

However, the opposition has disputed these claims, alleging irregularities. The situation on the ground remains tense, with many residents still angry over the crackdown. The election results will be a key indicator of the public mood.

What's Next?

All eyes are now on the election outcome. If the PML-N wins, it could consolidate its position, but if it loses, the fallout could deepen the crisis. The dialogue offer from Sharif may be a first step, but it is unclear if it will be followed by concrete action.

The coming weeks will reveal whether this shift in rhetoric is just a tactical move or a genuine change in policy. For now, the people of PoK are watching closely.
