Sensex falls 188 pts to second day of losses; Nifty closes at 24,436

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 188 points, Nifty ended at 24,436, extending losses to a second day
  • Rising crude oil prices and the Hormuz deadlock weighed on investor sentiment
  • Tata Group stocks came under pressure; Bharti Airtel, Hindalco, TCS, M&M among top movers

Indian stock markets closed in the red for a second consecutive session on Wednesday, as investor sentiment remained clouded by rising crude oil prices and geopolitical tensions around the Strait of Hormuz. The BSE Sensex dropped 188 points, while the NSE Nifty settled at 24,436, slipping below the 24,450 mark during intraday trade.

The benchmark indices had opened on a cautious note and struggled to find direction through the day. Selling pressure intensified in the afternoon, pulling the Sensex down over 200 points at one stage before a marginal recovery trimmed the losses.

Crude oil and Hormuz deadlock weigh on markets

The surge in global crude oil prices emerged as a key overhang on domestic equities. Traders pointed to the ongoing deadlock in the Strait of Hormuz — a critical chokepoint for oil shipments — as a trigger for the risk-off mood. Higher crude prices typically raise concerns about inflation and fiscal pressure on India, which imports a large share of its oil requirements.

Oil marketing companies and aviation stocks were among the early losers, though some recovered later in the session. Market analysts said the uncertainty around the Hormuz situation could keep volatility elevated in the near term.

Tata Group stocks under pressure

Shares of several Tata Group companies traded weak through the day, adding to the broader market decline. The group's stocks have been in focus recently, though Wednesday's fall was part of a wider sell-off rather than a company-specific trigger.

Among the Nifty constituents, Bharti Airtel, Hindalco, TCS and M&M emerged as the top movers — with some gaining while others dragged the index. The mixed performance reflected the lack of a clear directional bias, with investors cherry-picking sectors.

Sectoral trends and market breadth

IT stocks showed resilience in early trade, but profit-taking in metal and auto counters weighed on the indices. Banking stocks remained subdued, with private lenders and PSU banks both trading in a narrow range.

Market breadth was slightly negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. Midcap and smallcap indices also traded lower, mirroring the weakness in the benchmark indices.

Foreign institutional investors have been cautious over the past few sessions, and Wednesday's close is likely to keep them on the sidelines until there is more clarity on the global oil supply situation.

What to watch next

Investors will now keep a close eye on crude oil price movements and any diplomatic developments around the Hormuz strait. A prolonged deadlock could push oil prices higher, putting additional pressure on the rupee and bond yields.

Domestic macroeconomic data and global cues, particularly from the US Federal Reserve, will also shape market direction in the coming sessions. Traders are likely to stay cautious, awaiting fresh triggers before taking large positions.
