iPhone 18 Pro BOM cost may rise 40%, squeezing Apple margins

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • TrendForce estimates iPhone 18 Pro BOM cost could rise by nearly 40%.
  • Apple may sacrifice margins to sustain shipment volumes amid higher component prices.
  • iPhone 18 Pro expected to launch in September, with demand driving the timeline.

Component costs climb sharply

A new estimate from TrendForce suggests the bill of materials (BOM) for Apple's upcoming iPhone 18 Pro could be nearly 40% higher than its predecessor. The research firm's analysis, reported by GSMArena, points to rising prices for key components such as chipsets, displays, and camera modules. The jump is significant even by the standards of recent iPhone generations, where incremental cost increases have been the norm.

The BOM figure covers the total cost of parts and materials that go into each device, excluding assembly, software, and other overheads. A 40% surge would mark one of the steepest single-generation increases in recent memory. While Apple has historically negotiated favorable pricing with suppliers, the current supply chain environment appears to be testing that leverage.

Margins under pressure

According to TrendForce, Apple may have to absorb a portion of these higher costs rather than pass them entirely to consumers. The firm suggests the company could sacrifice some margin to sustain shipment volumes, especially in a market where smartphone demand remains price-sensitive. This would be a departure from Apple's usual strategy of protecting its industry-leading margins, which have long been a Wall Street favorite metric.

Industry watchers note that component inflation has been a persistent issue across the tech sector, driven by factors like advanced manufacturing costs and supply chain bottlenecks. For Apple, the challenge is balancing premium pricing with consumer expectations. The company has not officially commented on the TrendForce report, and final pricing for the iPhone 18 Pro remains unconfirmed.

September launch expected

Despite the cost pressures, the iPhone 18 Pro is still expected to launch in September. Reports from Forbes and Bloomberg indicate that Apple is preparing for a major release event, with demand reportedly strong enough to drive the timeline. In India, where Apple has been expanding its retail footprint, the company is said to be gearing up to launch up to nine devices, including the iPhone 18 Pro and new MacBook Pro models with M6 chips.

Bloomberg has also reported that Apple is conducting a retail employee lottery in preparation for the launch, a sign that the company expects significant footfall and sales activity. This suggests that while component costs are rising, Apple remains confident in its ability to move units, even if it means thinner margins per device.

What this means for buyers

For consumers, the key question is whether the higher BOM will translate into higher retail prices. If Apple decides to absorb the costs, the iPhone 18 Pro could retail at a price similar to the iPhone 17 Pro, preserving its competitive position. If not, buyers could see a notable price hike, particularly in markets like India where import duties already inflate costs.

Analysts are divided on the likely outcome. Some argue that Apple has room to raise prices given the brand's loyalty and the device's premium positioning. Others point to the global smartphone market's sluggish growth, which may force Apple to keep prices stable to avoid dampening demand. The company's final decision will likely depend on how the next few months of production and sales data shape up.

As the September launch approaches, more details are expected to emerge on pricing, component specifications, and availability. Apple's official announcement, likely in the coming weeks, will provide the clearest picture of how the company plans to navigate this cost surge.
