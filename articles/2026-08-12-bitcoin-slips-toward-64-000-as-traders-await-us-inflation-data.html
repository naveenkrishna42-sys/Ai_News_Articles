Bitcoin slips toward $64,000 as traders await US inflation data

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin traded near $64,000, down from recent highs, as risk appetite cooled.
  • Traders are focused on Wednesday's US inflation report for clues on Fed rate moves.
  • Analysts say a hot print could pressure crypto, while a cool one may boost it.

Bitcoin slipped toward the $64,000 mark on Tuesday, extending a cautious pullback as traders held their breath ahead of a crucial US inflation report due Wednesday. The world's largest cryptocurrency was last seen changing hands at around $64,200, down roughly 2% over the past 24 hours, according to data from The Block.

The slide comes after a volatile stretch that saw Bitcoin swing between $62,000 and $67,000 over the past week, as markets grapple with mixed signals on the US economy and the Federal Reserve's next move.

Inflation test looms large

Wednesday's consumer price index (CPI) report is expected to show a modest uptick in headline inflation, with economists forecasting a year-on-year rise of around 3.2%. Core inflation, which strips out volatile food and energy prices, is seen holding steady at 3.3%.

But the headline number is what matters most for crypto traders. A hotter-than-expected print could reignite fears of sticky inflation, prompting the Fed to keep interest rates higher for longer. That scenario has historically weighed on risk assets, including Bitcoin, which often trades like a high-beta tech stock in times of macro stress.

"The market is in a wait-and-see mode," said one digital asset trader in Mumbai. "Everyone knows the CPI number will move the needle, so there's not much appetite to take big positions before it lands."

Rate-cut hopes on the line

The inflation data arrives at a delicate juncture. Futures markets currently price in a near-certainty of a rate cut at the Fed's September meeting, but the size of that cut remains an open question. A soft CPI reading could cement expectations of a 50-basis-point move, while a hot one might force traders to dial back to 25 basis points.

For Bitcoin, the stakes are high. The asset has rallied nearly 40% this year on the back of strong spot ETF inflows and growing institutional adoption. But it has also shown sensitivity to shifts in rate expectations, with sharp sell-offs in April and June when rate-cut bets were pushed back.

"If CPI comes in hot, we could see Bitcoin test the $62,000 support level again," said a Singapore-based analyst. "But if it's cool, a rally back toward $67,000 is very much on the cards."

Liquidity and leverage build-up

Adding to the tension is a build-up in leverage across crypto derivatives markets. Open interest in Bitcoin futures has climbed to multi-month highs, suggesting that a flurry of liquidations could follow whichever direction the CPI report pushes prices.

Data from Coinglass shows that over $500 million in leveraged positions could be wiped out if Bitcoin moves more than 3% in either direction, a scenario that has played out repeatedly in recent months. Such cascading liquidations often amplify moves beyond what fundamentals would suggest.

Meanwhile, broader market sentiment remains mixed. While institutional interest continues to grow—with several major asset managers adding Bitcoin exposure to their portfolios—retail trading volumes have cooled from the peaks seen earlier this year.

What to watch

All eyes now turn to Wednesday's CPI release at 8:30 am ET. Expect heightened volatility across crypto and traditional markets in the hours following the print, with Bitcoin likely to set its near-term direction based on the data.

Beyond the inflation report, traders will also monitor weekly jobless claims and a speech by a Fed official later in the day for additional clues on the central bank's thinking. A clear break above $67,000 or below $62,000 could define the next leg of Bitcoin's trend heading into the autumn.
