Average Credit Card Interest Rate This Week at a Glance

For the week ending September 14, 2026, the average credit card interest rate in India sits in the 36% to 42% annualised band. Most mass-market and co-branded cards price revolving credit at roughly 3.0% to 3.5% per month, which translates to an annual percentage rate (APR) near 40% when compounded daily.

Premium metal cards and super-premium travel products sometimes quote a marginally lower monthly rate, but they compensate with higher annual fees. The headline number matters less than the daily periodic rate applied to your outstanding balance.

Why the Average Rate Is a Blunt Instrument

An average APR tells you where the market sits, not what you will pay. Issuers set rates by card variant, credit profile and internal risk models. A salaried borrower with a 780-plus CIBIL score on a mid-tier card can be charged 3.0% monthly, while a sub-prime profile on the same product may be charged 3.5%.

Interest is calculated on the daily outstanding balance, then compounded monthly. That means a rate cut of 0.25% per month changes your annual cost by roughly three percentage points, which is meaningful on a Rs 2 lakh revolving balance.

Revolving vs EMI vs Balance Transfer

Revolving credit is the most expensive way to carry a balance. Converting a large purchase to a no-cost EMI typically costs a one-time processing fee of Rs 99 to Rs 499 plus GST, which is far cheaper than 40% APR for a 12-month horizon.

Balance transfer offers at 0% for 3 to 6 months can work, but the 1% to 3% transfer fee plus GST eats into the saving. Run the numbers before shifting debt.

Practical Benchmarks for Indian Cardholders

  • 30-day cost of Rs 1 lakh: At 3.25% monthly, interest is about Rs 3,250, plus 18% GST on the interest.
  • Minimum due trap: Paying only 5% of the outstanding keeps the account current but leaves the rest revolving at full APR.
  • Cash withdrawals: Attract interest from the transaction date, with no interest-free period.
  • Reward offset: Even a 5% cashback card cannot out-earn a 40% APR if you revolve a balance.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Average APR holds steady in the 36%-42% band week on week.
  • Premium cards shave 1-3 percentage points off the mass-market rate.
  • EMI conversion is almost always cheaper than revolving credit.
  • GST at 18% applies on interest, late fees and most charges.
  • CIBIL score directly influences the rate your issuer assigns.

Buyer Implications and Value Justification

If you are comparing cards this week, do not choose on APR alone. A card with a 39% APR and no annual fee can beat a 37% APR card with a Rs 5,000 annual fee if you never revolve a balance. Conversely, if you routinely carry debt, the lower-rate card pays for itself within two billing cycles.

Check the most recent statement for the exact daily periodic rate, the compounding method and the cash advance terms. Those three data points determine your true cost far more than any published average.
