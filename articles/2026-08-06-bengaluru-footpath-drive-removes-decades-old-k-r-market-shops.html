Bengaluru footpath drive removes decades-old K.R. Market shops

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Decades-old shops near K.R. Market demolished in footpath clearance drive
  • Vendors and traders left without alternative arrangements
  • Civic officials cite pedestrian movement and congestion as reasons

Bengaluru's decade-old street commerce near the bustling K.R. Market has been swept away in a single civic action. A footpath clearance drive, carried out by the city's civic body, has removed shops that lined the pavements around the market for years, leaving traders and customers in shock.

The drive, which began early in the morning, saw JCB machines and civic workers dismantling the makeshift structures. Shop owners, many of whom had been operating from the same spot for decades, were given little notice before their livelihoods were uprooted.

Shops that outlived generations

Several of the affected establishments had been running for more than 30 years, serving as landmarks in the dense commercial neighbourhood. From flower vendors to hardware sellers, the shops catered to a steady stream of customers who relied on the market's easy access.

One trader, who requested anonymity, said his father had started the business in the 1980s. "We have never seen anything like this. We were told to pack up in a few hours," he added, standing amid the debris of what was once his shop.

Reasons cited by the authorities

Civic officials said the drive was part of a broader effort to clear footpaths for pedestrian movement. The stretch near K.R. Market, one of the city's oldest and busiest trading hubs, had become congested with encroachments, they noted.

Officials argued the shops were obstructing footpaths and contributing to traffic chaos in the area. However, they have not yet confirmed whether any alternative arrangements would be made for the displaced vendors.

Traders demand rehabilitation

Traders' associations have condemned the move, calling it arbitrary and lacking foresight. They argue that the shops were not merely encroachments but part of the market's character and economy, providing employment to hundreds of families.

Some vendors have demanded that the civic body allot them space in a designated hawking zone or a nearby market complex. Others fear that without formal rehabilitation, they will be forced to shut down permanently.

A city's balancing act

The action highlights the tension between urban planning and informal commerce in Bengaluru. While the need for clear footpaths is undeniable, the manner of execution has raised questions about sensitivity towards small traders.

Similar drives in other parts of the city have often led to protests and legal challenges. This time, the speed and scale of the operation have intensified the criticism.

Local residents have mixed feelings. Some welcome the cleaner footpaths, while others worry about losing the convenience and character that the market offered.

As the dust settles, the immediate future of these vendors remains uncertain. The civic body has not announced any follow-up measures, and the traders are planning to approach the courts or the state government for relief.

What happens next will depend on whether the authorities offer a rehabilitation package or stick to their position. For now, the empty pavements near K.R. Market stand as a stark reminder of the cost of urban order.
