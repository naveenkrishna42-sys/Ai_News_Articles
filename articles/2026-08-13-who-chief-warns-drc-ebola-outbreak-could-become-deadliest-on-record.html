WHO Chief Warns DRC Ebola Outbreak Could Become Deadliest on Record

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • WHO chief warns DRC Ebola outbreak could become deadliest on record
  • Ebola deaths in DRC surpass 2,000, with ongoing transmission
  • Health agencies revise containment strategies in response to challenges

WHO Chief Issues Stark Warning

The head of the World Health Organisation has raised the alarm over the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, warning that the current epidemic is on track to become the deadliest on record. The outbreak, which has already claimed more than 2,000 lives, continues to spread in a region plagued by conflict and weak healthcare infrastructure.

In a statement, the WHO chief described the situation as deeply concerning, noting that the virus is not yet under control. The warning comes as health workers on the ground struggle to reach affected communities due to insecurity and community mistrust.

Death Toll Crosses 2,000

Official figures now show that Ebola deaths in the DRC have surpassed 2,000, making this the second-largest outbreak in history, behind only the 2014-2016 West Africa epidemic that killed over 11,000 people. The current outbreak, which began in August 2018, has been concentrated in North Kivu and Ituri provinces, where armed groups have repeatedly attacked treatment centres.

Health officials have confirmed that the virus is still spreading, with new cases being reported in recent weeks. The WHO has warned that without a significant scale-up of response efforts, the outbreak could continue for many more months.

Challenges in Containment

One of the biggest hurdles has been the lack of access to affected areas. Militia violence has forced aid workers to suspend operations at times, and several Ebola treatment centres have been vandalised or attacked. This has allowed the virus to slip through the cracks, with some cases going undetected and spreading within communities.

Community resistance has also hampered response efforts. Many locals remain sceptical of health workers and government officials, fuelled by misinformation and historical grievances. This has led to delays in seeking treatment and, in some cases, hidden burials that increase the risk of transmission.

Revised Strategy by Health Agencies

In response to these challenges, health agencies have revised their containment strategy. The new approach places greater emphasis on community engagement, working with local leaders and traditional healers to build trust and improve case detection. It also involves the use of experimental vaccines and treatments, which have shown promise in reducing mortality rates.

The WHO has called for increased international support, both financial and logistical, to bolster the response. However, funding gaps and the diversion of global attention to other health crises have slowed the pace of the intervention.

What Lies Ahead

The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether the outbreak can be brought under control. Health officials are racing to vaccinate more people and trace contacts, but the volatile security situation remains a wild card.

If the current trajectory continues, this outbreak will surpass the deadliest on record, a grim milestone that no one wants to see. The world is watching, and the response in the DRC will test the global community's commitment to preventing another Ebola catastrophe.
