Ancient cosmic pile-up challenges black hole formation theories

The James Webb Space Telescope has uncovered a cosmic phenomenon that may rewrite our understanding of black hole formation. Astronomers have discovered three supermassive black holes crammed into a single galaxy, which existed just 1.2 billion years after the Big Bang. This discovery suggests that black holes may have formed much earlier and grown faster than previously believed.

Why this matters

This finding challenges the current understanding of how supermassive black holes form and evolve. If black holes can form and grow so quickly in the early universe, it implies that the seeds of these cosmic giants must have been much larger or formed under different conditions than we thought.

Key findings and implications

The discovery was made by analyzing data from the James Webb Space Telescope, which has unprecedented capabilities for observing the early universe. The three black holes were identified through their gravitational effects on surrounding matter, a technique known as gravitational lensing.
