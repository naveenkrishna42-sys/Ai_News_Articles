Kirana body urges Hyderabad police to spare genuine traders in FAST crackdown

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hyderabad police recently conducted a crackdown using the Hyderabad-FAST system.
  • The kirana merchants' body has appealed to police to avoid targeting genuine traders.
  • The association seeks clarity on the criteria used to identify violators.

Hyderabad's kirana merchants have appealed to the city police to exercise restraint in the ongoing enforcement drive linked to the Hyderabad-FAST system, asking that genuine traders not be caught in the net meant for violators. The appeal comes days after a series of crackdowns that have left many small shopkeepers nervous.

Hyderabad-FAST and the recent crackdown

The Hyderabad-FAST system, a digital platform used by the police to track and act against repeat offenders and those with pending warrants, has been central to recent operations. While the technology is designed to flag suspects, the merchants' body claims that some legitimate business owners have been unnecessarily inconvenienced during the raids.

Police officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of people detained or the specific criteria that triggered the alerts. However, the swift action has prompted the kirana association to seek clarity on how the system distinguishes between a genuine trader and a habitual offender.

Merchants' appeal for fairness

In a formal representation to the police commissioner, the merchants' body requested that field officers verify credentials thoroughly before taking action against shopkeepers. They argued that most kirana stores operate on thin margins and cannot afford legal hassles arising from mistaken identity or outdated records.

"We are not asking for any leniency for those who break the law," a representative of the association said. "But our members are law-abiding citizens. A single wrong alert can ruin years of hard work." The body has also sought a helpline or a quick redressal mechanism for traders who receive notices under the Hyderabad-FAST flag.

Police response and way forward

Police sources indicate that the drive is part of a broader effort to clean up specific pockets of the city where stolen goods and illegal trade are suspected. They maintain that the Hyderabad-FAST system is only a tool, and ground-level verification is always carried out before any arrest.

Still, the association has urged the police to issue clear guidelines to sub-inspectors and constables so that genuine traders are not summoned repeatedly. They have also asked for a list of documents that shopkeepers should keep handy to prove their bona fides during checks.

The dialogue between the merchants and the police is expected to continue in the coming days, with both sides keen to avoid friction. For now, the kirana owners are hoping that their appeal leads to a more measured approach.

What remains to be seen is whether the police will issue a formal circular or amend their operational protocol in response to the association's concerns. The merchants, meanwhile, are advising their members to stay calm and cooperate with authorities while the matter is being resolved.
