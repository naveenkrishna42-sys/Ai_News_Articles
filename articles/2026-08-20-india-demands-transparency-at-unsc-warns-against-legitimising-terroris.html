India Demands Transparency at UNSC, Warns Against Legitimising Terrorists

📅 2026-08-20 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • India calls for overhaul of UNSC working methods to ensure greater transparency
  • New Delhi warns against using Council membership to legitimise or de-list terrorists
  • Palestinian envoy expresses support for India's push for non-permanent UNSC seat

India Pushes for UNSC Transparency Reforms

India has called for a comprehensive overhaul of the United Nations Security Council's working methods, demanding greater transparency in how the body operates. New Delhi's position emphasizes that membership in the world's most powerful diplomatic body must not serve as a tool to legitimise terrorist organisations or facilitate their removal from international sanctions lists.

The statement from the Indian delegation comes at a time when the legitimacy and effectiveness of the Security Council continue to face scrutiny from member nations seeking more equitable representation.

Counter-Terrorism Measures at the Forefront

Speaking during the Security Council's review of its own working methods, India underlined that the Council's membership carries responsibilities that must align with the global fight against terrorism. The Indian position specifically addressed concerns about potential misuse of Council mechanisms to benefit designated terrorist entities.

New Delhi has consistently maintained that any reform process must ensure the Council cannot be exploited to undermine international counter-terrorism efforts. This stance reflects India's broader diplomatic philosophy of zero tolerance toward terrorism in all its forms.

Palestinian Backing for India's Position

The Palestinian envoy to the United Nations has publicly backed India's push for a non-permanent seat on the Security Council. The envoy stated solidarity with New Delhi's reform agenda, acknowledging India's commitment to multilateralism and fair representation within the UN system.

This expression of support highlights India's growing diplomatic partnerships across different regional groups at the United Nations. The backing from Palestine adds to the broader coalition of nations advocating for changes in how the Security Council conducts its business.

Calls for Systematic Change

India's demand for transparency extends beyond counter-terrorism concerns. The delegation has argued that the Council's working methods require systematic updates to reflect contemporary global realities. This includes more predictable scheduling of meetings, clearer criteria for agenda items, and improved communication with non-member states.

These reform proposals align with longstanding demands from a significant number of UN member countries who feel the Council's internal procedures lack the openness expected of a body with such sweeping powers under international law.

Discussions on Security Council reforms have persisted for decades without fundamental changes to the body's structure or operations. India continues to advocate for meaningful transformation while engaging constructively within existing frameworks.

What Comes Next

India is expected to continue building support among UN member states for its reform proposals during the current session.
