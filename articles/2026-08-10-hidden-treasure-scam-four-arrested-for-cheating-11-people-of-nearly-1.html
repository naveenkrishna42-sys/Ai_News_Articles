Hidden treasure scam: Four arrested for cheating 11 people of nearly ₹1 crore in Hyderabad

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Four individuals arrested for allegedly running a hidden treasure scam in Hyderabad
  • 11 victims cheated of nearly ₹1 crore in total
  • Police investigation ongoing; more details awaited

Four held in Hyderabad treasure hunt fraud

Hyderabad police have arrested four individuals accused of running an elaborate hidden treasure scam that duped 11 people out of nearly ₹1 crore. The arrests were made following a complaint filed by a victim who realised he had been swindled after weeks of being fed promises of buried riches.

The accused allegedly lured victims with claims of a secret treasure hidden at a specific location, convincing them to invest money for excavation costs, permissions, and other fabricated expenses. Police said the gang operated systematically, targeting people through word of mouth and personal networks to build trust before striking.

How the scam unfolded

According to investigators, the accused would approach potential victims with a convincing story about a hidden cache of gold and valuables, supposedly left behind by ancestors or discovered through old maps. They promised a share of the treasure in exchange for upfront payments to cover logistics and legal hurdles.

Over time, the victims paid varying amounts, believed to total close to ₹1 crore collectively. The gang allegedly kept the deception alive by staging fake digging sessions or showing doctored documents, stringing victims along for months.

Police action and victim impact

The arrests were made after one of the victims approached the police with detailed transactions and communication records. Following a preliminary investigation, the four suspects were taken into custody. Officials have not yet confirmed whether the entire amount has been recovered or if additional members are involved.

Many of the victims are believed to be middle-class individuals who had saved up for years, making the financial blow particularly severe. Police have urged anyone with similar experiences to come forward, as they suspect the number of affected people may be higher than the 11 currently known.

Modus operandi under scrutiny

Investigators are now examining the gang's methods, including how they selected targets and crafted believable narratives around the treasure. The use of fake documents and staged evidence appears to have been central to their approach, which police say was sophisticated enough to evade suspicion for several months.

Officials have also noted that such scams often rely on exploiting greed and desperation, and they have cautioned the public against responding to unsolicited offers involving hidden wealth. The case has been registered under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code, and the accused are expected to be produced before a local court.

What to watch

The investigation is ongoing, with police likely to probe whether the gang had prior criminal records or links to similar frauds elsewhere. Further arrests cannot be ruled out as authorities trace the flow of funds and any accomplices who may have helped in executing the scheme.
