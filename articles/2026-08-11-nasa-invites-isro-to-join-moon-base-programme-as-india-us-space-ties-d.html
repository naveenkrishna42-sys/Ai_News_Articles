NASA invites ISRO to join moon base programme as India-US space ties deepen

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • NASA has formally invited ISRO to participate in its lunar base programme.
  • The invitation signals deepening space cooperation between India and the US.
  • ISRO's role in the programme is expected to be announced after further discussions.

In a significant move that underscores the growing strategic partnership between New Delhi and Washington, NASA has officially invited the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) to join its ambitious moon base programme. The development, reported by Moneycontrol, comes as part of the broader Artemis framework, which aims to establish a sustainable human presence on the lunar surface by the end of this decade.

The invitation is being seen as a major endorsement of India's space capabilities, particularly after the success of the Chandrayaan-3 mission, which made India the first country to land near the lunar south pole. While the specific contours of ISRO's participation are yet to be finalised, officials indicate that Indian expertise in cost-effective lunar missions and robotic exploration could play a key role in the programme's early phases.

A natural extension of Artemis

India signed the Artemis Accords in June 2023, a US-led initiative to establish norms for peaceful space exploration. Since then, the two countries have deepened their collaboration, with joint missions like NISAR (NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar) satellite slated for launch in the near future. The moon base invitation is the latest and most ambitious step in this trajectory.

NASA's lunar base concept, known as the Artemis Base Camp, envisions a long-term habitat near the lunar south pole, where water ice is believed to be accessible. The base would support extended stays for astronauts and serve as a staging point for deeper space exploration, including potential missions to Mars.

What India brings to the table

ISRO's strengths lie in its ability to deliver high-impact missions at relatively low cost. The Chandrayaan-3 mission, for instance, had a budget of under Rs 600 crore, a fraction of what similar missions typically cost elsewhere. Such frugal engineering is exactly what NASA needs as it looks to build a sustainable presence on the Moon without ballooning budgets.

Additionally, India's growing private space sector, with companies like Skyroot Aerospace and Agnikul Cosmos, could contribute to the programme through launch services and component manufacturing. The invitation is expected to open new avenues for Indian industry in the global space supply chain.

Geopolitical undertones

The move also carries clear geopolitical weight. As China accelerates its own lunar ambitions, including plans for a crewed moon landing by 2030, the US is keen to build a coalition of like-minded nations for space exploration. India, with its non-aligned history but pragmatic foreign policy, is a valuable partner in this effort.

Analysts note that the invitation is not just about science and technology; it is about signalling a shared vision for the future of space governance. By bringing ISRO into the fold, NASA is reinforcing a rules-based order in space, countering the more opaque approach taken by Beijing.

Next steps and expectations

Formal negotiations between the two space agencies are expected to begin in the coming months. The scope of India's involvement could range from supplying robotic landers and rovers for pre-base reconnaissance to contributing life-support systems or even crewed modules in later stages, though the latter remains a distant prospect.

ISRO chairman, in a recent public address, hinted at the organisation's readiness to take on new challenges, though he stopped short of confirming specific roles. "We are exploring all options and are committed to ensuring that India's participation is meaningful and mutually beneficial," he had said.

For now, the invitation is a clear signal that India's space programme has arrived on the world stage. As the details unfold, all eyes will be on how this partnership shapes the next chapter of lunar exploration.
