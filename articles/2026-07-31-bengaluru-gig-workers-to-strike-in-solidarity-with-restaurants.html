Bengaluru Gig Workers to Strike in Solidarity with Restaurants

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gig workers in Bengaluru announce strike in solidarity with restaurants
  • Demands include better pay, working conditions, and transparency
  • Potential disruption to food delivery services in the city

Strike Call Announced

Gig workers across Bengaluru have announced a strike in solidarity with restaurants, a move that could disrupt food delivery services in the city. The decision comes after weeks of growing friction between delivery partners, restaurant owners, and the platforms that connect them.

The workers, who operate for various food delivery apps, say they are stepping back from work to press for better pay and improved working conditions. The strike is expected to affect thousands of orders daily if a resolution is not reached soon.

Demands and Grievances

At the heart of the protest are long-standing grievances over shrinking earnings and rising operational costs. Delivery partners argue that reduced base fares and increased fuel prices have made it difficult to sustain a livelihood.

Restaurant owners have also voiced concerns over high commission fees charged by platforms, which they say eat into already thin margins. The solidarity strike is aimed at drawing attention to these shared challenges.

Workers are also demanding greater transparency in payout calculations and a fair mechanism for resolving disputes. Many say they have been left with little choice but to stop work to make their voices heard.

Impact on Services

The strike is likely to lead to longer delivery times and fewer available riders during peak hours. Some platforms may temporarily suspend services in certain zones if the situation persists.

Customers in Bengaluru have been advised to plan for potential delays. Several restaurants have expressed support for the workers, with some even offering to pause online orders during the strike period.

Talks and Next Steps

Representatives of the gig workers have indicated that they are open to negotiations with platform companies. However, they have made it clear that the strike will continue until their core demands are addressed.

Platform companies have not yet issued a formal response. Industry watchers expect that pressure from both workers and restaurants could bring stakeholders to the table in the coming days.

The development highlights the growing bargaining power of gig workers in India's tech capital. As the gig economy expands, such collective actions may become more frequent unless systemic issues are tackled.

All eyes are now on the platform companies to see if they will offer concessions. A prolonged strike could have ripple effects on the broader gig economy across other Indian cities.
