Hariyali Teej 2026: Top Rajasthan Temples for Festive Darshan

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hariyali Teej is celebrated during the monsoon month of Shravan, marking the union of Shiva and Parvati.
  • Major temples in Jaipur, Udaipur, Jodhpur, and Pushkar witness special decorations and crowds.
  • Devotees observe fasts and offer prayers for marital bliss and well-being.
  • Temple authorities expect long queues and advise early arrival on the festival day.

A Festival of Swings and Prayers

Hariyali Teej, the green festival of swings, falls on the third day of the bright fortnight in the Hindu month of Shravan. This year, it arrives in mid-August, when Rajasthan's parched landscape transforms under the first heavy rains. For devotees, the day is dedicated to Goddess Parvati and her reunion with Lord Shiva. Married women observe a strict fast, praying for their husbands' longevity, while unmarried women seek blessings for an ideal life partner.

The festival gets its name from 'hariyali' or greenery, and temples across the state are decked with fresh leaves, marigolds, and traditional rangoli. The swing, or 'jhoola', becomes the centrepiece of celebrations, often adorned with flowers and placed near the deity's sanctum. In cities like Jaipur and Udaipur, the air fills with the sound of bhajans and the clang of temple bells from early morning.

Jaipur's Historic Shrines

The state capital offers some of the most iconic venues for Teej darshan. The Govind Dev Ji Temple, located within the City Palace complex, sees thousands of devotees queuing from dawn. The temple's intricate architecture and the idol of Lord Krishna, believed to be particularly benevolent on this day, draw crowds from across the state. Special 'shringar' or adornment ceremonies are held, and the atmosphere remains charged till late evening.

Another key destination is the Moti Doongri Ganesh Temple, perched on a hilltop. While primarily dedicated to Lord Ganesha, the temple's scenic location and festive decorations make it a favourite among families. The nearby Laxmi Narayan Temple, also known as Birla Mandir, offers a more serene experience with its white marble façade and evening aarti that overlooks the city.

Udaipur and the Lake City Charm

In Udaipur, the Jagdish Temple stands as a prime example of Indo-Aryan architecture and becomes a hub of activity on Teej. Dedicated to Lord Vishnu, the temple's three-storeyed structure is adorned with brass images and intricate carvings. Devotees often combine their visit with a boat ride on Lake Pichola, making the spiritual journey a visual treat. The temple's location in the old city adds to the festive buzz, with local markets selling bangles, ghewar, and traditional leheriya dupattas.

The Karni Mata Temple, also in Udaipur, offers a different kind of reverence. Known for its resident rats, which are considered sacred, the temple attracts curious tourists and devout locals alike. On Hariyali Teej, special pujas are conducted, and the temple trust arranges for free distribution of prasad. The cable car ride to the temple provides a panoramic view of the city, making it a memorable visit.

Jodhpur and Pushkar's Sacred Spots

Jodhpur's Chamunda Devi Temple, situated atop the Mehrangarh Fort, is another significant site. The temple's association with the royal family of Marwar lends it historical weight, and on Teej, the fort complex witnesses a blend of tourism and local devotion. The climb to the temple is steep, but the view of the blue city from the top is considered a reward in itself. For those who prefer a less strenuous visit, the Umaid Heritage and other smaller neighbourhood temples also host special aartis.

Pushkar, the ancient holy town, offers the Brahma Temple, one of the very few in the world dedicated to Lord Brahma. On Hariyali Teej, the temple sees an influx of pilgrims who combine their visit with a dip in the sacred Pushkar Lake. The town's ghats, lined with steps and temples, create a unique spiritual atmosphere that is both tranquil and vibrant. Local priests note that the monsoon season adds a special significance to the rituals, as the lake's water level rises with the rains.

What to Expect This Year

Temple authorities across Rajasthan have begun preparations, expecting larger crowds than usual as travel has fully resumed post-pandemic. Devotees are advised to reach temples early, preferably before 8 am, to avoid long queues. Several temples have announced live streaming of the morning aarti for those who cannot visit in person. Local police have also been roped in for crowd management at major shrines, especially in Jaipur and Udaipur.

As the festival approaches, check with individual temple trusts for updated timings and any specific entry restrictions. The monsoon may cause intermittent rains, so carrying an umbrella is prudent. With the right planning, a visit to these temples on Hariyali Teej can be a deeply fulfilling experience, blending faith, culture, and the unique charm of Rajasthan's monsoon.
