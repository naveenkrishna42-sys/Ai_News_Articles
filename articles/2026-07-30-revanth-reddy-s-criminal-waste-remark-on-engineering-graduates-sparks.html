Revanth Reddy's 'criminal waste' remark on engineering graduates sparks political row

📅 2026-07-30 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Telangana CM Revanth Reddy said engineering education for many graduates is a 'criminal waste' as they lack basic skills.
  • Reddy questioned if engineering graduates can even write a job application.
  • The opposition in Telangana has slammed the CM for the remarks, calling them insulting to students and the education system.

Telangana Chief Minister Revanth Reddy has ignited a political firestorm with his blunt assessment of engineering graduates in the state. Speaking at a public event, the Chief Minister described the current state of engineering education as a 'criminal waste', asserting that many graduates lack the fundamental skills required for employment.

Reddy questioned the very competency of these graduates, asking whether they could even draft a basic job application. The remarks, which were widely reported, have drawn sharp condemnation from opposition parties.

CM's blunt critique of engineering skills gap

Revanth Reddy did not mince words when addressing the quality of engineering graduates. He argued that the large sums of money spent on engineering education are not yielding returns, as a significant number of graduates are unemployable. The Chief Minister's comments highlight a long-debated issue in India's higher education sector.

By labelling the current system a 'criminal waste', Reddy has put the spotlight back on the disconnect between academic curricula and industry requirements. His pointed remark about graduates being unable to write a job application underscores the severity of the skills gap as perceived by the state government.

Opposition parties cry foul

The Chief Minister's statements were met with immediate backlash from political rivals in Telangana. Opposition leaders accused Reddy of insulting the state's students and demeaning the hard work of thousands of engineering graduates and their families.

They argued that such sweeping generalisations from the highest office are demoralising and do not reflect the reality that many engineering graduates from Telangana have gone on to successful careers in India and abroad. The opposition is demanding an apology from the Chief Minister, calling his remarks insensitive and politically motivated.

Government's parallel education initiative

In a separate but related development, the Telangana government, under Chief Minister Reddy, announced a new programme to sponsor annual foreign study tours for government school teachers. This initiative appears aimed at improving the quality of teaching at the foundational level.

While the government promotes teacher exposure to global best practices, the Chief Minister's harsh words for the current engineering graduates have created a contradictory narrative. Critics question how the state plans to fix the engineering skills gap while simultaneously dismissing the existing talent pool.

The row over Reddy's remarks is unlikely to die down soon. The opposition is expected to raise the issue in the upcoming assembly session, demanding a clarification or an apology. Meanwhile, the state's education department will have to grapple with the Chief Minister's damning assessment as it formulates policy for technical education.

All eyes are now on the Chief Minister's next public statement to see if he will elaborate on his plan to address the 'criminal waste' he identified, or if the controversy will force a retreat.
