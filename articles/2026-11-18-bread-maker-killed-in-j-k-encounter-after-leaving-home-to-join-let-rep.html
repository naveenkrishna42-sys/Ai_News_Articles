Bread Maker Killed in J&K Encounter After Leaving Home to Join LeT: Reports

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Deceased described as LeT commander Latteef Bhat's accomplice; body retrieved from encounter site
  • CCTV footage reportedly shows militants near Kulgam CRPF checkpost a day before the attack
  • Prolonged search operations disrupt daily life; Kashmir politicians warn against collective punishment

What the Reports Say

A bread maker was killed in an encounter in Jammu and Kashmir's Kulgam district after leaving home roughly a week earlier, intending to join Lashkar-e-Taiba (LeT) ranks and attack Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) personnel, ThePrint reported. The report describes the deceased as an accomplice of LeT commander Latteef Bhat, whose associates have been the focus of the ongoing anti-terror operation.

The sequence of events, as reported, indicates the man traveled to a militant contact point before security forces launched the cordon-and-search operation that led to the exchange of fire.

The Kulgam CRPF Attack Context

The encounter follows a CRPF attack in Kulgam. The Hindu and NDTV cited CCTV footage showing Lashkar terrorists near a CRPF checkpost a day before the attack, while The Times of India reported on the commander's movements captured on camera. This footage forms the backbone of the investigative trail linking the deceased to the planning stage.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • The deceased had left home about a week before the encounter, indicating premeditated movement rather than a chance encounter.
  • CCTV evidence near the checkpost gives investigators a verifiable timeline to reconstruct the attack chain.
  • Security forces continue search operations across Kulgam, with the body of Bhat's alleged accomplice retrieved from the site.
  • No official casualty breakdown or full charge-sheet detail has been confirmed yet, so attribution remains report-based.

Civilian Impact and Political Reaction

TheWire reported that prolonged search operations have stifled normal life, with Kashmir politicians warning against collective punishment. For residents, that translates into restricted movement, shuttered markets, interrupted school attendance, and delayed commutes — the everyday cost of a manhunt that can stretch for days.

For buyers of local goods and services, the disruption hits daily-wage earners hardest: bread vendors, transporters, and shopkeepers lose trading hours while curfew-like conditions hold. The political pushback centres on ensuring operations remain targeted rather than sweeping, a distinction that shapes how long disruptions last.
