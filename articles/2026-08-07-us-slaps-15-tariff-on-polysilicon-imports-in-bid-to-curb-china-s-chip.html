US Slaps 15% Tariff on Polysilicon Imports in Bid to Curb China's Chip Edge

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • US announces 15% tariff on polysilicon imports, a key material for semiconductors and solar panels.
  • Move is aimed at reducing dependence on China, which controls a significant share of global polysilicon production.
  • Tariff is part of broader trade actions targeting Chinese tech supply chains.
  • Industry analysts say the move could raise costs for US solar and electronics manufacturers in the short term.

Washington Targets Polysilicon in New Trade Action

The United States has announced a 15% tariff on polysilicon imports, a critical raw material used in semiconductors and solar panels. The move, confirmed by the White House on Friday, is designed to counter China's near-monopoly in the material's global supply chain.

Polysilicon is the foundation of solar cells and an essential input for advanced electronics. China accounts for over 80% of the world's polysilicon production, giving Beijing significant leverage in two industries Washington considers vital to its economic and national security.

Price Floors and Strategic Goals

Alongside the tariff, the administration has introduced price floors on imported polysilicon. These measures are intended to stabilise domestic production and make it harder for Chinese suppliers to undercut American manufacturers.

Officials say the action is part of a broader strategy to reshore critical supply chains. The semiconductor industry, already reeling from earlier export controls, will now face higher input costs for a material that has few viable substitutes.

Solar Industry Braces for Impact

US solar developers are likely to feel the pinch first. Polysilicon accounts for a significant share of solar panel costs, and a 15% tariff could raise project expenses at a time when the industry is already grappling with supply chain disruptions.

Some analysts argue the tariff may accelerate domestic polysilicon capacity, which has been slow to scale. However, building new plants takes years, and in the interim, American manufacturers will have to absorb higher costs or pass them on to consumers.

China's Response Remains Unclear

Beijing has not yet issued an official response, but past trade spats suggest retaliation is likely. Chinese authorities have previously targeted US agricultural exports and rare earth minerals in similar disputes.

The tariff marks the latest escalation in a tech war that has reshaped global trade patterns. Washington has already restricted advanced chip exports to China and imposed curbs on semiconductor equipment sales, and this move extends that pressure to upstream materials.

What to Watch

Market watchers will be monitoring whether the tariff triggers a spike in global polysilicon prices and how quickly US domestic producers can ramp up output. The industry is also awaiting clarity on whether exemptions will be granted for certain grades of polysilicon used in niche applications.

For now, the administration has signalled that this is just one plank in a larger strategy to secure America's technological edge. Further trade actions targeting other critical minerals cannot be ruled out in the coming months.
