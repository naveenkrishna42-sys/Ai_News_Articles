Ashish Yadav's U20 Worlds silver: From dry ponds to Neeraj Chopra's trail

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ashish Yadav secured silver in men's javelin at the World Athletics U20 Championships.
  • His journey began in dry ponds with a wooden javelin, mirroring Neeraj Chopra's early struggles.
  • The silver comes exactly 10 years after Chopra's historic U20 Worlds gold in 2016.

Ashish Yadav's silver medal at the World Athletics U20 Championships is not just a personal milestone. It is a decade-old echo of Neeraj Chopra's golden moment, and a testament to a journey that began in the most unassuming of settings—dry ponds and a wooden javelin.

The 19-year-old from a small village in Uttar Pradesh finished second in the men's javelin event, becoming the first Indian since Chopra to stand on the U20 Worlds podium. The achievement, confirmed by Athletics Federation of India officials, places him firmly in the spotlight as the next big hope in Indian javelin.

From barren fields to the world stage

Ashish's early training was far removed from the polished tracks of international stadiums. With no access to proper equipment or facilities, he practised by throwing a wooden javelin into dry ponds, a makeshift arrangement that demanded raw strength and relentless repetition. His coach, who spotted him at a local district meet, recalls the young athlete's obsession with technique even then.

That foundation of grit has now translated into a medal at the global level. The U20 Worlds silver, won in the Peruvian capital Lima, places Ashish among the top junior throwers in the world, with a throw that would have challenged the best in his age group.

In Chopra's footsteps

The timing carries symbolic weight. Exactly ten years after Neeraj Chopra's gold at the 2016 U20 Worlds in Bydgoszcz, Ashish has replicated that feat—albeit with silver—on the same stage. Chopra's journey from a similar rural background to Olympic gold has been a template for young Indian throwers, and Ashish has openly drawn inspiration from it.

His performance in Lima is being read as a sign that India's javelin pipeline is producing talent beyond the celebrated names. With Chopra now mentoring younger athletes informally, Ashish's emergence adds depth to a discipline that has become India's most consistent medal source in global athletics.

More Indian success at the championships

Ashish was not the only Indian to shine at the U20 Worlds. Pooja Singh qualified for the high jump final, keeping the country's hopes alive in another field event. The results from Lima come on a day when Indian sports saw multiple developments, including Ashmita Chaliha's progress to the Korea Masters final in badminton.

For Ashish, the immediate focus will be on transitioning to the senior circuit, where the competition is steeper and the distances required are significantly higher. His senior debut is expected later this year, with the national championships providing the first real test.

What to watch next

Ashish's silver is a starting point, not a finish line. The next few months will reveal whether he can push his personal best beyond the 80-metre mark, a threshold that separates junior promise from senior reality. With the 2026 Asian Games on the horizon and Chopra's legacy as a benchmark, all eyes will be on how this young thrower builds on his Lima breakthrough.
