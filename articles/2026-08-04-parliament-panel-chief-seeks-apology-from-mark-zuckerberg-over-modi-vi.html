Parliament panel chief seeks apology from Mark Zuckerberg over Modi video row

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Parliamentary panel chief seeks apology from Mark Zuckerberg
  • Centre summons Meta's public policy head over PM Modi video row
  • Meta's global team to meet officials on August 5-6 regarding CSAM and AI concerns

Panel chief's demand

The head of a parliamentary committee has escalated the row over a video featuring Prime Minister Narendra Modi, demanding a personal apology from Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg. The demand comes amid growing friction between the Indian government and the social media giant over content moderation practices.

The committee chief's stance reflects a hardening position on the part of lawmakers. They argue that the removal of the PM's video was not just an error but a deliberate act that undermines India's digital sovereignty.

Centre summons Meta again

In a related development, the Centre has once again summoned Meta's Public Policy head. This is the second time the executive has been called before officials in connection with the incident, according to sources familiar with the matter.

The summons underscores the government's insistence on accountability. Meta's global team is also expected to appear before officials on August 5-6, where issues beyond the video row, including child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and artificial intelligence-related concerns, will be discussed.

Apology not enough, says panel

The House panel has made it clear that a simple apology may not suffice. Reports indicate that the panel has discussed foreign links and content control mechanisms, suggesting a deeper probe into how Meta operates in India.

Lawmakers are particularly interested in understanding the decision-making process that led to the video's removal. The panel's scrutiny appears to extend beyond this single incident, touching on broader questions of platform accountability and regulatory oversight.

Broader implications

The confrontation between the Indian government and Meta is part of a larger global debate on tech regulation. India, with its vast user base, has been tightening rules for social media platforms, and this episode may accelerate that process.

Officials have not yet confirmed what specific action might follow the upcoming meetings. However, the repeated summons and the demand for Zuckerberg's apology signal that the government is not willing to let the matter rest quietly.

As the August 5-6 meetings approach, all eyes will be on Meta's response. The company has not publicly commented on the panel chief's demand so far.

What to watch

The coming days will reveal whether Meta will offer an unconditional apology or contest the government's position. The outcome of these discussions could set a precedent for how foreign tech firms engage with Indian regulators on content moderation and platform governance.
