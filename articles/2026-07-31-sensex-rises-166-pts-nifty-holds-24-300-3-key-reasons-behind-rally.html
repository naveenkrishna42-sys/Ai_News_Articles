Sensex rises 166 pts, Nifty holds 24,300; 3 key reasons behind rally

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex climbed 166 points, Nifty settled above 24,300
  • IT and banking stocks led the gains
  • Positive global cues and easing crude oil prices supported sentiment

Mumbai: The Indian equity markets ended the trading session on a positive note on Friday, with the Sensex rising 166 points and the Nifty comfortably holding above the 24,300 mark. The benchmark indices extended their winning streak, driven by a mix of domestic and global factors that kept investor sentiment buoyant throughout the day.

The BSE Sensex closed at 79,724.11, up 166 points, while the NSE Nifty settled at 24,313.45, gaining 0.24 per cent. The broader market also showed resilience, with midcap and smallcap indices trading in the green, reflecting broad-based buying interest.

IT stocks lead the charge

One of the primary drivers of the market's upward move was the strong performance of information technology (IT) stocks. Major IT firms, including Infosys, TCS, and HCL Technologies, saw robust buying interest, contributing significantly to the Sensex's gains. The sector has been in focus amid expectations of steady demand from the US and European markets, which remain key revenue generators for Indian IT companies.

Analysts noted that the IT pack's resilience, despite global headwinds, has instilled confidence among investors. The sector's ability to sustain margins and maintain growth momentum has made it a preferred choice for institutional investors looking for stability.

Banking and financial stocks add support

Banking and financial stocks also played a crucial role in lifting the market. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India were among the top gainers on the Sensex, with the Nifty Bank index rising 0.3 per cent. The sector's strength was attributed to expectations of improved credit growth and stable asset quality, which have been key themes in recent quarterly earnings.

Private sector lenders, in particular, saw healthy buying interest, with HDFC Bank contributing the most to the Sensex's gains. The banking index's performance helped offset some of the losses in other sectors, ensuring the overall market remained in positive territory.

Global cues and crude oil prices support sentiment

Positive global cues also aided the domestic market's upward movement. Asian markets traded mixed, but European indices opened higher, providing a supportive backdrop. US futures also pointed to a firm opening, which boosted investor confidence in the Indian market.

Additionally, easing crude oil prices provided relief to the market. A decline in global crude prices reduces the import bill for India, which is a major importer of oil. This, in turn, helps improve the country's fiscal position and supports the rupee. The softer crude prices also eased concerns about inflationary pressures, which could influence the Reserve Bank of India's policy stance in the coming months.

Market breadth and sectoral performance

The market breadth was positive, with more than 2,000 stocks advancing on the BSE, while around 1,500 declined. Among sectoral indices, IT, banking, and oil & gas emerged as the top gainers, while pharma and FMCG indices witnessed some profit booking.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) were net buyers in the cash market, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) also supported the market. This combined buying interest underscores the positive sentiment prevailing among both domestic and foreign investors.

What to watch next

Market participants will now closely monitor the upcoming US Federal Reserve meeting and its commentary on interest rates, which could influence global capital flows. Domestically, the progress of the monsoon and its impact on inflation will also be key factors to watch in the coming weeks.

As the earnings season progresses, stock-specific action is likely to continue, with investors focusing on companies that deliver strong results and provide clear guidance. The market's ability to sustain its upward momentum will depend on a combination of global cues, domestic economic data, and corporate earnings performance.
