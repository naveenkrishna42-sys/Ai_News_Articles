PIB Press Note: Culture Ministry Highlights New Initiatives

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • PIB press note issued on April 17, 2025, covers Culture Ministry updates.
  • Focus on heritage conservation projects and digital initiatives for wider public access.
  • Ministry aims to boost cultural tourism and preserve India's intangible heritage.

The Press Information Bureau (PIB) issued a press note on Thursday, April 17, 2025, detailing a series of new initiatives by the Ministry of Culture. The note outlines the government's renewed focus on heritage conservation, digital outreach, and cultural tourism. Officials said these measures are part of a broader push to preserve India's diverse cultural legacy while making it more accessible to citizens.

Heritage Conservation Gets a Boost

The press note highlights several upcoming projects aimed at restoring and protecting monuments across the country. The Ministry has identified over 50 sites for immediate conservation work, including lesser-known structures in rural areas. Funding has been allocated under the revised 'Adopt a Heritage' scheme, which now allows private firms to sponsor restoration efforts.

Archaeological Survey of India teams will begin work at these sites within the next quarter. The note also mentions a new digital documentation drive to create 3D models of endangered heritage structures. This data will be publicly available through a dedicated portal later this year.

Digital Outreach and Public Access

A significant portion of the press release focuses on expanding digital access to India's cultural wealth. The Ministry plans to launch a mobile application that will host virtual tours of museums and heritage sites. The app will also feature curated content in multiple Indian languages.

Additionally, the note announces the digitisation of over one lakh rare manuscripts and artefacts currently housed in national museums. These items will be uploaded to a centralised online repository. The Ministry expects the project to be completed within 18 months.

Cultural Tourism and Intangible Heritage

The press note underscores the government's push to boost cultural tourism. New tourism circuits are being developed around the country's classical dance forms, folk music, and traditional crafts. Local artisans and performers will receive direct financial support under the proposed scheme.

Officials said the initiative aims to create sustainable livelihoods for communities while preserving intangible heritage. The Ministry is also working with state tourism departments to promote these circuits through targeted marketing campaigns.

Reactions and Next Steps

Cultural experts have welcomed the announcements, particularly the emphasis on digital preservation. However, some have called for more clarity on implementation timelines and monitoring mechanisms. The Ministry has not yet released a detailed action plan.

The press note concludes by stating that further details on each initiative will be published on the PIB website in the coming weeks. Stakeholder consultations are expected to begin next month. The Ministry has invited suggestions from the public through an online feedback form.
