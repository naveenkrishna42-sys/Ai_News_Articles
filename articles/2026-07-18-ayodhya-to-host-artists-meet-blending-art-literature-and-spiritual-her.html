Ayodhya to host artists’ meet blending art, literature and spiritual heritage

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Three-day ‘Kala Sangam’ event from June 20 to 22 at Ram Katha Park.
  • Over 200 artists, writers and spiritual leaders expected to participate.
  • Includes painting exhibitions, poetry readings and discussions on Ramayana art.

Ayodhya gears up for a cultural confluence

Ayodhya is set to host a three-day artists’ meet titled ‘Kala Sangam’ from June 20 to 22, 2026. The event will bring together painters, poets, writers and spiritual leaders to celebrate the city’s artistic and spiritual legacy.

Organised by the Uttar Pradesh Culture Department in collaboration with the Ayodhya Development Authority, the meet will be held at Ram Katha Park. Officials said the event aims to position Ayodhya as a centre for creative expression beyond its religious significance.

What to expect at Kala Sangam

More than 200 artists from across the country are expected to participate. The programme includes live painting sessions, poetry recitations, and panel discussions on the influence of the Ramayana on Indian art forms.

A dedicated exhibition will showcase contemporary interpretations of scenes from the Ramayana. There will also be workshops on traditional art forms such as Madhubani and Pichwai, giving local artists a platform to interact with masters from these schools.

“This is not just an event, but a movement to revive the cultural soul of Ayodhya,” a senior culture department official said. The official added that the meet will also feature a literary session on the works of Tulsidas and other poets linked to the city.

Spiritual leaders to join discussions

Several spiritual leaders and scholars have confirmed their participation. They will lead talks on the role of art in deepening spiritual experience and preserving heritage. The organisers hope these conversations will attract younger audiences to traditional art forms.

Local artisans and craftspeople will also get a chance to display their work. The event is free and open to the public, with special slots for school and college students. Authorities have made arrangements for parking and security along the venue.

The city has seen a surge in tourism and infrastructure development since the Ram Temple consecration in 2024. The culture department views this meet as a step to diversify Ayodhya’s appeal beyond pilgrimage.

Looking ahead

If successful, the Kala Sangam could become an annual feature on Ayodhya’s calendar. The organisers are already in talks with other state culture departments to include artists from across India in future editions. For now, the focus remains on making this first edition a memorable celebration of the city’s creative and spiritual heartbeat.
