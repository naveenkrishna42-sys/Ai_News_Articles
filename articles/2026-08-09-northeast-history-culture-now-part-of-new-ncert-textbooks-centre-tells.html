Northeast history, culture now part of new NCERT textbooks, Centre tells Parliament

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Union Education Ministry confirms inclusion of Northeast history, culture, and heritage in new NCERT textbooks.
  • The move aims to provide comprehensive regional representation and address previous gaps.
  • Response came in Lok Sabha, highlighting the government's commitment to holistic education.

New NCERT books to cover Northeast India's rich past

The Centre has informed Parliament that the history, culture, and heritage of Northeast India have been incorporated into the latest NCERT textbooks. In a written reply to the Lok Sabha, the Union Education Ministry detailed how the revised syllabus now reflects the region's significant contributions to the national narrative.

This marks a notable shift in how regional histories are represented in school curricula. For years, educators and scholars have pointed out that mainstream textbooks often glossed over the Northeast, leaving students with a limited understanding of the region's diverse traditions and historical milestones.

Addressing long-standing gaps in representation

The ministry's response comes after sustained demands from parliamentarians and civil society groups. They argued that the absence of detailed Northeast content in textbooks created a cultural disconnect, especially for students outside the region.

Officials have not yet disclosed the specific chapters or subjects where the new material has been added. However, the ministry's statement indicates that the changes are part of a broader exercise to make the curriculum more inclusive and representative of all states.

A step towards holistic education

The integration of Northeast history and culture is seen as a step towards fostering national unity. By learning about the region's unique traditions, festivals, and historical figures, students across India can develop a more rounded perspective of the country's composite heritage.

Education experts have welcomed the move but also stressed the need for accurate and nuanced content. They caution that mere inclusion is not enough; the material must be researched thoroughly and presented in a way that resonates with young learners.

What this means for students and teachers

For teachers, the revised textbooks will require updated training and resources to effectively deliver the new content. The NCERT is expected to roll out orientation programmes for educators in the coming months, though no official date has been announced.

Students, particularly those in the Northeast, are likely to feel a greater sense of recognition and pride. The inclusion of their regional history in mainstream textbooks validates their cultural identity within the larger Indian framework.

The government's response signals a continued effort to revisit syllabi with an eye on regional balance. Whether this extends to other underrepresented areas remains to be seen, but the precedent is now set.

As the new academic session approaches, schools will be watching closely to see how the updated books are received. The larger test lies in implementation—ensuring that the content reaches classrooms and sparks genuine curiosity about the Northeast's vibrant heritage.
