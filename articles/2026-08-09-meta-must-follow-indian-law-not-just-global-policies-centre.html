Meta must follow Indian law, not just global policies: Centre

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Centre says Meta must adhere to Indian laws, not just global policies
  • Government examining if Meta's recommendation system fits intermediary status
  • Meta apologised for restricting PM Modi's post; anti-BJP content takedowns under scanner

New Delhi: The Indian government has told Meta Platforms Inc. that it must operate within the framework of Indian law, and not merely rely on its global content moderation policies. The message was conveyed during ongoing discussions between the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) and the social media giant, which owns Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp.

The development comes amid heightened scrutiny of how Meta handles content on its platforms in India, particularly around political speech and algorithmic curation.

Recommendation system under review

Officials in the government are examining whether Meta's recommendation system — the algorithm that decides what posts, reels, and videos appear in a user's feed — fits within the legal definition of an 'intermediary' under Indian law. The distinction is crucial: intermediaries enjoy safe harbour protections, meaning they are not liable for user-generated content, provided they follow due diligence obligations.

If the recommendation system is deemed to be actively curating content rather than passively hosting it, Meta could lose that protection. That would expose the company to legal liability for content that its algorithms amplify, including misinformation and harmful material.

The government's position, as articulated in the discussions, is that any entity operating in India must comply with the country's laws, including the Information Technology Act and the recently enacted Digital India Act framework. Global policies, however well-intentioned, cannot override local statutory requirements.

Apology over PM Modi's post

The talks come just days after Meta apologised to the Indian government for temporarily restricting a post by Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The restriction was reportedly triggered by a technical glitch in Meta's automated moderation systems, but it raised questions about how the company applies its policies in different jurisdictions.

In parallel, reports have emerged of a significant increase in takedowns of anti-BJP content across Meta's platforms in recent weeks. Digital rights activists and opposition supporters have alleged that the company is disproportionately removing posts critical of the ruling party, even as it apologises for restricting the Prime Minister's own content.

Meta, for its part, has maintained that its content moderation practices are consistent and apolitical, and that any takedowns are based on violations of its community standards. The company has not commented on the specific allegations of bias.

Deepfakes and child sexual abuse material

The government has also pressed Meta on two other fronts: the proliferation of deepfake content and the spread of child sexual abuse material (CSAM) on its platforms. Both issues have been flagged as priority concerns by MeitY in recent months.

Officials have sought detailed information on Meta's proactive measures to detect and remove such content, including the use of artificial intelligence and human moderators. The government has emphasised that the onus is on platforms to ensure their systems are robust enough to prevent the dissemination of illegal content.

Industry observers note that India is one of Meta's largest markets, with over 500 million users across its apps. Any regulatory action against the company could have significant implications for its operations globally, given the precedent it might set.

The discussions are part of a broader push by the Indian government to assert greater control over digital platforms, which it says is necessary to ensure user safety and national security. Tech companies, however, have warned that overly prescriptive regulations could stifle innovation and free expression online.

What to watch

As the government's examination of Meta's recommendation system continues, the key question is whether the Centre will amend the intermediary guidelines to explicitly cover algorithmic curation. A decision is expected in the coming months, and it could reshape how all social media platforms operate in India.
