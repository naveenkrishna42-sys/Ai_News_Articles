Sensex drops 400 points, Nifty below 24,600; Bajaj Finance drags

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex slipped over 400 points; Nifty fell below 24,600.
  • TCS emerged as a top gainer, supporting the indices.
  • Bajaj Finance dropped 4.5%, becoming a major drag.
  • Market breadth was negative amid broad-based selling.

Indian equity benchmarks opened the session on a shaky note on Friday, with the Sensex tumbling over 400 points and the Nifty sliding below the 24,600 mark. Selling pressure was broad-based, though select IT and defensive stocks offered some cushion.

The 30-share BSE Sensex was trading at around 80,900 levels, down 0.5% from the previous close. The broader NSE Nifty hovered near 24,580, as investors trimmed positions across banking, financials, and auto counters.

Bajaj Finance drags financials

Bajaj Finance was the biggest laggard on the Nifty, falling as much as 4.5% in early deals. The stock's sharp decline weighed on the entire financial services pack, with peers like HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank also trading in the red.

Traders cited profit booking after a recent run-up in the non-banking financial company's shares. The stock had gained nearly 12% over the past month, making it vulnerable to a pullback.

TCS leads gainers

On the other end, Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) emerged as the top gainer on the benchmark indices, rising over 2% in a weak market. The IT major's resilience provided some support to the Nifty, which otherwise faced intense selling pressure.

Other IT stocks, including Infosys and HCL Technologies, also traded higher, tracking positive global cues for the sector. Analysts said the defensive nature of IT stocks is attracting inflows amid volatility.

Market breadth remains negative

The overall market breadth was firmly negative, with more than two stocks declining for every one advancing on the BSE. Midcap and smallcap indices fell in line with the benchmarks, as risk appetite waned.

Auto stocks were among the worst hit, with Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra losing over 1% each. Metal and energy shares also traded lower, mirroring a cautious tone across global markets.

What's driving the selloff?

Global cues were subdued, with Asian markets trading mixed after overnight losses on Wall Street. Investors are also awaiting key US inflation data due later this month, which could influence the Federal Reserve's rate path.

Domestically, concerns over elevated valuations and a slowdown in corporate earnings growth have prompted some investors to book profits at higher levels. Foreign institutional investors have been net sellers in recent sessions, adding to the pressure.

Market participants are now watching the weekly options expiry for any sharp moves. The Nifty is expected to find support around the 24,500 level, while resistance is seen near 24,800.

In the coming sessions, the focus will shift to quarterly earnings from major companies and global macroeconomic data. Any surprise on the inflation front could trigger a sharper correction, analysts caution.
