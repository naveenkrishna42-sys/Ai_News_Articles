Iran's Guards Say Two Oil Tankers Hit Mines, Explode in Strait of Hormuz

📅 2026-07-18 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran's Revolutionary Guards say two oil tankers exploded after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • The United States has denied the claim, calling it false.
  • The incident comes amid escalating US-Iran tensions, with reports of Iran blocking tankers and fresh strikes.

Explosions in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has reported that two oil tankers exploded after striking mines in the Strait of Hormuz. The claim, carried by Iran's Tasnim news agency, said the IRGC navy was involved in the incident and that one of the vessels was a Thai-flagged tanker.

The explosions mark a sharp escalation in the strategic waterway, through which about a fifth of the world's oil passes. The IRGC did not immediately provide details on casualties or the condition of the crews.

US Rejects Iran's Account

The United States swiftly dismissed the Iranian narrative. A US official was quoted as saying, 'This is false,' without elaborating. The denial sets up a direct clash of accounts between Tehran and Washington, each accusing the other of destabilising the region.

The US Navy's Fifth Fleet, based in Bahrain, has not yet issued a statement. The Strait of Hormuz has been a flashpoint for weeks, with the US blaming Iran for a series of attacks on commercial shipping.

Broader Conflict Heats Up

The tanker explosions come amid a wider confrontation. Reports indicate that Iran has attempted to block four tankers in the strait, with the US intervening to assist them. Two tankers were reportedly on fire after the latest incident, though independent verification is lacking.

Fresh strikes and military threats have been reported from both sides. The region is bracing for further confrontation as diplomatic channels remain strained. The Hindustan Times reported a 'US-Iran war' dynamic, with the strait turning into a live theatre of operations.

What Happens Next

Global shipping companies are likely to reroute vessels away from the strait, pushing up insurance premiums and oil prices. The UN Security Council may hold an emergency session. The next 24 hours will be critical in determining whether this is a one-off incident or the start of a sustained campaign. All eyes are on Washington and Tehran for their next moves.
