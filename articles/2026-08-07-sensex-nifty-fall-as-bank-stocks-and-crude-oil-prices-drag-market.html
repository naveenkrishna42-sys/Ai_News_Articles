Sensex, Nifty Fall as Bank Stocks and Crude Oil Prices Drag Market

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty ended lower as banking shares came under pressure.
  • Rising crude oil prices added to investor concerns over inflation and fiscal deficit.
  • Analysts say market sentiment remains cautious amid global and domestic headwinds.

Indian equity benchmarks Sensex and Nifty ended lower on Friday, dragged by losses in banking stocks and persistent concerns over elevated crude oil prices. The sell-off was broad-based, with investors turning cautious amid a mix of domestic and global headwinds.

Banking stocks lead the decline

The banking sector was the biggest drag on the indices, with major lenders witnessing selling pressure. Concerns over margin pressures and a potential rise in bad loans kept investors on edge, prompting profit-booking in recent winners.

Private and public sector banks both ended in the red, contributing significantly to the benchmark losses. The Nifty Bank index fell sharply, reflecting the weak sentiment across the financial segment.

Crude oil prices add to worries

Elevated crude oil prices continued to weigh on market mood. India imports about 85% of its oil requirements, so any sustained rise in global prices raises concerns about inflation and the country's current account deficit.

Higher oil prices also put pressure on the rupee, which in turn affects foreign institutional investor flows. Traders said the combination of rising input costs and rupee weakness could hurt corporate margins in the coming quarters.

Global cues remain mixed

Global markets offered little support, with Asian peers trading mixed and European indices opening on a cautious note. The US Federal Reserve's stance on interest rates, along with geopolitical uncertainties, kept investors from taking aggressive positions.

Back home, volatility was evident across midcap and smallcap stocks, which have been outperforming the largecaps in recent sessions. Analysts said the current correction is a healthy pause after a strong run, but caution is warranted given the uncertain macro environment.

What investors are watching

Market participants are now focusing on the trajectory of crude oil prices and any further cues on domestic inflation. The upcoming monthly retail inflation data and quarterly earnings from key companies are likely to guide the market direction in the near term.

Technical analysts noted that the Nifty has immediate support around its 50-day moving average, and a break below that level could trigger further selling. On the upside, sustained buying is needed to cross recent record highs.

In the near term, the market is expected to remain range-bound with a negative bias, unless there is a significant drop in crude prices or a fresh round of positive domestic data.

Investors are advised to keep a close watch on global oil supply developments and the rupee's movement, as these will be key determinants of market sentiment in the sessions ahead.
