Saidapet bus shelter 'inaccessible' for disabled, say activists

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A bus shelter under construction near Saidapet court has drawn criticism from disabled persons' groups.
  • Activists say the design lacks ramps, tactile paving, and other accessibility features.
  • Officials have not yet responded to the concerns raised.
  • The shelter is part of a broader city infrastructure project.

A bus shelter under construction near the Saidapet court in Chennai has come under fire from disability rights activists, who say it will be inaccessible to persons with disabilities. The shelter, part of a city-wide infrastructure upgrade, has been designed without basic accessibility features, according to those who rely on public transport daily.

Activists point out that the shelter lacks ramps, tactile paving, and designated space for wheelchair users. The raised platform, meant to ease boarding, has become a barrier in itself, they argue. Those who need to use the shelter will find it difficult, if not impossible, to access it safely.

Design flaws cited

The location near the Saidapet court is busy, with heavy footfall from litigants, lawyers, and visitors. Many of them are elderly or have mobility issues. The shelter, instead of aiding them, is likely to add to their struggles.

The absence of tactile paving, which guides visually impaired persons, is a particular concern. Without it, navigating the area becomes hazardous. The activists have also noted that the shelter's height and placement do not conform to universal design principles.

Activists demand changes

Local disability rights groups have written to the authorities, asking for modifications to the design. They want the shelter to be rebuilt to meet accessibility standards, even if it means delaying the project. The demand is not new, but the response has been slow, they say.

Some activists have suggested that the shelter be relocated to a spot where it can be built with proper access. Others have called for an audit of all upcoming shelters in the city to prevent similar issues. The focus, they stress, should be on making public infrastructure inclusive from the start.

Officials yet to respond

Officials have not yet confirmed whether they will make any changes to the current design. The civic body responsible for the construction has not issued a statement on the matter. This silence has only added to the frustration of those affected.

In the past, similar complaints have led to redesigns, but only after prolonged campaigns. The activists are hoping for a quicker resolution this time. They are also planning to approach the disability commissioner if their concerns are not addressed soon.

What happens next

The shelter is still under construction, leaving a narrow window for modifications. Whether the authorities will act remains to be seen. For now, the disabled community in Chennai is watching closely, hoping that their voices will not be ignored again.
