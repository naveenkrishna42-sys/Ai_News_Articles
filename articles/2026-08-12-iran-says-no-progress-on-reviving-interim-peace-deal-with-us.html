Iran says no progress on reviving interim peace deal with US

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran says no progress on reviving interim peace deal with the US.
  • A 60-day ceasefire extension deadline is approaching on August 17.
  • Pakistan says mediation file remains open and period can be extended.

Stalemate persists

Iran has said there has been no progress on reviving an interim peace deal with the United States, casting doubt on the future of the fragile ceasefire between the two nations. The statement comes just days before the current 60-day truce is set to expire on August 17.

A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran and Washington have not discussed extending the ceasefire. This directly contradicts earlier signals from other regional players that negotiations might be underway.

Pakistan keeps mediation alive

Pakistan, which has been acting as a mediator between the two sides, said it has not closed the mediation file. A Pakistani Foreign Ministry spokesperson indicated that the 60-day period outlined in the memorandum of understanding can be extended if both parties agree.

However, with Iran reporting no discussions on the matter, the likelihood of a smooth extension appears slim. The lack of dialogue between Washington and Tehran raises concerns about what happens after the deadline passes.

What the ceasefire means

The interim deal, brokered with Pakistan's help, was seen as a rare diplomatic breakthrough between the US and Iran. It was designed to de-escalate tensions that had been building for months over Tehran's nuclear programme and regional activities.

The 60-day ceasefire provided a window for both sides to step back from the brink. But without progress on a more permanent arrangement, the truce's expiration could lead to renewed hostilities.

Mixed signals from all sides

While Pakistan insists the door remains open, Iran's public stance suggests otherwise. The senior Iranian official's remarks to Reuters indicate that no formal or informal talks have taken place regarding the extension.

US officials have not yet commented on the latest Iranian statement. Washington's silence leaves room for interpretation — either it is waiting for the right moment, or it has already moved on from the interim arrangement.

Regional implications

The standoff does not exist in a vacuum. Countries across West Asia are watching closely, as any collapse of the ceasefire could trigger a wider regional crisis. Pakistan's continued involvement suggests that diplomatic channels, while strained, are not entirely shut.

The coming days will be critical. If no extension is agreed upon by August 17, the interim peace deal may effectively lapse, forcing both sides back to square one.

What to watch

All eyes are now on the August 17 deadline. Whether Pakistan can coax both sides back to the table, or whether Washington breaks its silence, will determine if the ceasefire survives. For now, the clock is ticking.
