CBSE paper leak: Student protests target PM Modi over exam integrity

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Students across India protest against CBSE paper leak, directing anger at PM Modi.
  • Demonstrators demand systemic reforms in examination processes and accountability.
  • Government yet to announce concrete measures to prevent future leaks.

Protests spread across cities

Student demonstrations erupted in multiple Indian cities this week following the leak of CBSE examination papers. What began as scattered complaints on social media quickly turned into coordinated protests, with students holding placards and shouting slogans. The anger, however, has taken an unexpected turn — much of it is directed at Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

Protesters argue that the repeated leaks reflect a systemic failure in governance. "We are not just angry about one paper," said a student at a rally in Delhi, speaking on condition of anonymity. "This shows the system does not care about our future." Similar scenes unfolded in Mumbai, Kolkata, and Bengaluru, where students gathered outside local education offices.

Leak triggers trust deficit

The Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) has not yet issued a detailed statement on the scope of the leak or how it occurred. Officials have not confirmed the number of papers affected, but reports suggest at least one major examination was compromised. The lack of clarity has only deepened the trust deficit among students and parents.

For many, the incident is not isolated. Over the past few years, several state board exams have faced similar breaches, raising questions about the robustness of India's examination infrastructure. "Every year we hear of leaks, but nothing changes," said a parent outside a protest site in Pune. "Our children's hard work is being devalued."

Political overtones intensify

The protests have acquired a political edge, with some demonstrators directly blaming PM Modi for the situation. Chants of "Shame on Modi" were heard at several rallies, reflecting a broader dissatisfaction among the youth. Political analysts note that while the CBSE is an autonomous body, its functioning falls under the central government's purview, making the Prime Minister a natural target.

Opposition parties have seized on the moment, with leaders demanding a thorough investigation and the resignation of the Education Minister. However, the government has yet to respond formally to these demands. A senior official, speaking to reporters, said the matter is being taken "very seriously," but offered no specifics.

Students demand concrete action

The protesters' demands are clear: a transparent probe into the leak, strict action against those responsible, and a revamp of the examination process. Many are also calling for the introduction of digital question paper delivery to reduce human interference. "We need a system that is leak-proof," said a student leader in Chennai. "Otherwise, this will happen again."

CBSE has announced that it will conduct an internal inquiry, but students remain skeptical. In the absence of immediate measures, protests are expected to continue. Some student groups have planned a nationwide strike next week, urging peers to skip classes until their demands are met.

What happens next

The coming days will be crucial. If the government announces a high-level investigation and concrete reforms, tensions may ease. But if the response is seen as inadequate, the protests could gain further momentum, potentially embarrassing the administration ahead of key state elections. For now, students remain in the streets, determined to make their voices heard.
