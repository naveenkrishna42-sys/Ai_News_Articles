Two ships with 22 Indians hijacked by pirates off Yemen, Somalia

Hijacking reported in high-risk waters

Two vessels carrying 22 Indian nationals have been hijacked by armed pirates in waters near Yemen and Somalia, according to initial reports. The incidents took place amid growing concerns over a spike in Somali piracy, which has seen a notable uptick in recent days.

The ships were reportedly seized in the Arabian Sea and Gulf of Aden region, a corridor long considered high-risk for maritime traffic. Indian maritime agencies and the Ministry of External Affairs have not yet issued a detailed statement, but officials are believed to be tracking the situation.

Resurgence of piracy in the region

Reports from international media indicate that the hijackings are part of a broader pattern. At least two hijackings have occurred within four days, signalling what some analysts describe as the biggest resurgence of Somali piracy in a decade.

The renewed activity comes against the backdrop of the ongoing West Asia conflict, with some reports suggesting that pirates have received support from Houthi elements. This has complicated naval responses and raised the stakes for commercial shipping in the region.

One vessel reportedly carrying Turkish weapons

According to The Times of India, one of the hijacked cargo ships was carrying Turkish weapons off Somalia's coast. The exact nature of the cargo and its destination have not been independently verified by Indian authorities.

The presence of military equipment on board could escalate the seriousness of the incident, potentially drawing in multiple nations for a coordinated response. However, no official confirmation has been made regarding the cargo or the status of the crew.

Indian crew safety is the immediate concern

With 22 Indian nationals on board the two vessels, the safety of the crew remains the primary worry for families and officials alike. Past incidents of piracy in these waters have often involved prolonged negotiations and occasional military intervention.

Indian naval assets have been deployed in the region in recent years for anti-piracy patrols, though their current positioning near the hijack sites is unclear. The government is expected to provide an update once verified information is available.

Authorities have not yet confirmed whether any contact has been established with the hijackers or if ransom demands have been made. The situation remains fluid, and naval forces from multiple countries are on alert.

What to watch next

Over the coming days, the focus will be on any official statements from the Indian government and international naval coalitions operating in the region. The response to these hijackings could set a precedent for how shipping security is managed in the Red Sea and Arabian Sea corridors amid ongoing geopolitical tensions.
