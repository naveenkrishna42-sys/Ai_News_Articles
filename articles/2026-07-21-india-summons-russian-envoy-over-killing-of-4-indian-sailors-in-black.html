India Summons Russian Envoy Over Killing of 4 Indian Sailors in Black Sea

📅 2026-07-21 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • India summoned a senior Russian diplomat to protest the killing of four Indian sailors in the Black Sea near Odessa.
  • The sailors were aboard a foreign-flagged vessel that was struck by a missile during an attack near the Ukrainian port.
  • New Delhi has called for an immediate investigation and the safe repatriation of the remains of the deceased.
  • Russia has acknowledged the incident but said its military operations in Ukraine will continue.
  • Families of the victims, including one from Kasaragod, Kerala, have appealed for the quick return of the bodies.

New Delhi summoned a senior Russian diplomat on Tuesday to lodge a strong protest over the killing of four Indian sailors in a missile strike near the Ukrainian port of Odessa. The Ministry of External Affairs conveyed India’s deep anguish and called for a thorough investigation into the incident.

The sailors were part of the crew of a foreign-flagged vessel that came under attack in the Black Sea. Reports from multiple sources, including The Moscow Times and The Hindu, confirmed the deaths occurred during a missile strike near Odessa, a key flashpoint in the ongoing war between Russia and Ukraine.

Diplomatic Response and Demand for Action

India’s decision to summon the Russian envoy underscores the seriousness with which New Delhi views the loss of its citizens. The MEA stated that it expects the Russian government to ensure the safety of Indian nationals and to cooperate fully in establishing the circumstances of the attack.

“We have demanded a complete and transparent investigation into the missile strike that killed four Indian seafarers,” an official source said. “We have also asked for the immediate repatriation of the remains of the deceased to their families.”

Russia has acknowledged the incident but has not changed its military posture. In a statement reported by NDTV, Moscow said it is in touch with India over the deaths but indicated that its strikes in Ukraine would continue.

Families in Grief, Appeal for Bodies

Back in India, the families of the victims are in mourning. In Kasaragod, Kerala, the family of one of the deceased sailors has publicly appealed for the speedy repatriation of the body. The Times of India reported that relatives are struggling with the uncertainty and the delay in getting the remains back for funeral rites.

Local community leaders have also called on the central government to expedite the process. “We want our son back. We need closure,” a family member told reporters, fighting back tears.

A Broader Pattern of Risk

The incident highlights the persistent danger faced by merchant navy personnel operating in war zones. The Black Sea has been a volatile area since the Russian invasion of Ukraine began. Several commercial vessels have been caught in crossfire, and Indian sailors, who form a significant part of the global shipping workforce, have been among the victims.

India has repeatedly raised the issue of safety for its seafarers in international waters, but Tuesday’s summons marks an escalation in diplomatic engagement. The government is now under pressure not only to secure a probe but also to ensure that future Indian crews are warned away from high-risk maritime zones.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact ownership or flag of the vessel that was hit. Further details are expected once the investigation gets underway.

New Delhi will now wait for Moscow’s formal response. The families of the four sailors, meanwhile, are hoping for a swift resolution that allows them to perform last rites and begin the process of grieving.
