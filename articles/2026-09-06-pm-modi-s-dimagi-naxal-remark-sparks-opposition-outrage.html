PM Modi's SRCC address triggers political backlash

Prime Minister Narendra Modi's interaction with students at Shri Ram College of Commerce centenary celebrations on 6 September 2026 has triggered a sharp political row. The remarks referencing 'dimagi naxal' and 'naraaz phoopa' were made during a youth pitch in Delhi. Opposition parties and the Centre for Justice and Peace have condemned the language as an attempt to demonise an entire generation.

The event was framed by the Prime Minister's Office as an informal metro journey to SRCC and back, with glimpses of conversations shared online. The political context of the address was quickly highlighted by critics who said a college centenary was used for partisan messaging.

Remarks at SRCC centenary and metro outreach

Youth pitch and 'dimagi naxal' reference

PM Modi addressed students at the top Delhi college and contrasted India's condition in 2013 with the present. He spoke on growth numbers and said the Opposition could not digest the country's progress. The reference to 'dimagi naxals' was made amid the youth pitch, drawing comparisons to past political rhetoric against dissent.

The 'naraaz phoopa' remark was cited in reporting as part of the same interaction. The terms were presented as descriptors for a section of disgruntled youth, and were quickly amplified across media coverage of the SRCC centenary.

Metro journey narrative

The Prime Minister shared glimpses of conversations during his metro journey to SRCC and back. The outreach was positioned as accessibility to students, but critics argued the setting was used to deliver a political message. The college centenary provided a high-visibility platform with national media coverage.

Opposition and civil society response

Congress and Kharge criticism

Congress President Mallikarjun Kharge said PM Modi used the student interaction as a political campaign. The party argued that an academic centenary should not be converted into an electoral platform. The criticism focused on the timing and tone of the remarks in a college setting.

Opposition commentary also centred on the claim that the Prime Minister was targeting young voices of dissent. The pushback highlighted concerns about stigmatising students who question government policy.

CJP fumes over generational demonisation

The Centre for Justice and Peace responded strongly to the language used at SRCC. The organisation described the remarks as demonising an entire generation. Civil society groups warned that labelling students as 'dimagi naxal' could chill campus debate.

Media reporting from Telegraph India and NDTV framed the episode as a clash between government youth outreach and opposition concerns over political messaging in educational spaces.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • SRCC centenary provided a national stage for direct youth engagement by the Prime Minister.
  • Terms 'dimagi naxal' and 'naraaz phoopa' have been used previously in political discourse to question oppositional youth.
  • Criticism is focused on perceived politicisation of a college event rather than the growth narrative itself.
  • Civil society and Opposition argue the remarks risk alienating students ahead of key electoral cycles.
