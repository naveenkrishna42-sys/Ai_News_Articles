Sensex Gains Momentum, Nifty Cautious After RBI Policy; Key Takeaways

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex gained momentum while Nifty remained cautious post-RBI policy.
  • RBI policy decision influenced market sentiment and sectoral performance.
  • Investors advised to watch key support and resistance levels.
  • Market breadth and global cues likely to drive near-term trend.

Market Opens Mixed After RBI Announcement

Indian equity benchmarks opened on a mixed note on Wednesday, a day after the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy decision. The Sensex picked up early momentum, climbing steadily in the first hour of trade, while the Nifty 50 moved sideways, reflecting a cautious undertone among investors.

The divergence between the two indices pointed to selective buying, with heavyweight banking and financial stocks lending support to the Sensex. Meanwhile, profit-taking in select IT and FMCG counters kept the Nifty in check.

RBI Policy: What Changed for Markets

The RBI's policy statement, delivered on Tuesday, did not spring any major surprises. However, the tone on inflation and liquidity management was closely parsed by market participants. The central bank's stance on maintaining adequate liquidity while keeping an eye on price pressures was seen as balanced.

According to analysts, the policy offered no immediate triggers for a sharp rally, but it also removed any near-term uncertainty. This, they said, allowed the market to focus on stock-specific fundamentals rather than macro headlines.

Sectoral Moves: Banks Lead, IT Drags

Banking stocks, particularly private sector lenders, were among the top contributors to the Sensex's gains. Expectations of stable policy rates and better credit growth prospects supported the sector. On the other hand, information technology stocks faced mild selling pressure, possibly due to concerns over global demand and currency fluctuations.

Auto and pharma stocks traded mixed, while midcap and smallcap indices showed resilience, indicating that broader market participation remained intact. Market breadth was slightly positive in early trade, with more advances than declines on the BSE.

Key Levels and What to Watch

For the Nifty, the immediate support is seen around its recent swing low, while resistance lies near its all-time high zone. A decisive break above this level could invite fresh buying, but a failure to hold support may trigger further consolidation.

Investors are also keeping an eye on global cues, including US Federal Reserve commentary and crude oil prices. A stable global environment, coupled with domestic earnings momentum, could provide the next directional push for the market.

Foreign institutional investors have been net buyers in recent sessions, though their activity post-policy will be closely monitored. Domestic institutional flows have also remained supportive, cushioning any downside.

What Investors Should Do Now

Market experts suggest that investors should not chase momentum blindly. Instead, they recommend focusing on quality stocks with strong earnings visibility and reasonable valuations. Given the mixed signals, a stock-specific approach may be more rewarding than index-level bets.

For traders, maintaining a stop-loss discipline is crucial, as volatility could pick up in the coming sessions. The market is likely to take cues from upcoming macroeconomic data and quarterly earnings updates.

As the session progresses, the Nifty's ability to hold above its key moving averages will be a critical indicator of near-term strength. A close above the 24,800 mark could signal renewed optimism, while a drop below 24,500 may invite fresh selling.

The Road Ahead

With the RBI policy behind, the market's focus now shifts to global central bank actions and domestic earnings. The next few sessions are likely to be data-driven, and any surprise on inflation or industrial production could alter the trajectory.

Investors would do well to stay nimble and review their portfolios in light of the evolving macro environment. The medium-term outlook remains constructive, but near-term volatility should not be underestimated.
