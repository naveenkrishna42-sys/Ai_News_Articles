Mohammad Nawaz suspended for anti-doping violation post T20 World Cup

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mohammad Nawaz failed a doping test conducted during the 2024 T20 World Cup.
  • The Pakistan all-rounder has been handed a provisional suspension pending further investigation.
  • Details of the banned substance and the length of the ban have not yet been disclosed.

Provisional suspension imposed

Pakistan all-rounder Mohammad Nawaz has been sanctioned for an anti-doping rule violation. The test was conducted during the ICC Men's T20 World Cup in June 2024. The International Cricket Council (ICC) confirmed the development on Friday.

Nawaz has been handed a provisional suspension. This means he cannot participate in any cricket activity until the case is resolved. The ICC's independent tribunal will now review the evidence.

What the test revealed

The specific substance detected in Nawaz's sample has not been disclosed. Officials have not yet confirmed whether the violation was intentional or due to a contaminated supplement. The all-rounder has not issued a public statement so far.

Under ICC anti-doping rules, a player can face a ban of up to four years if the violation is proven to be intentional. A lesser sanction applies if the player can demonstrate no significant fault or negligence.

Impact on Nawaz's career

Mohammad Nawaz, 31, has played 42 ODIs and 49 T20Is for Pakistan. He was part of the squad that reached the T20 World Cup Super 8 stage in 2024. His last international appearance was in a T20I against New Zealand in April 2025.

The suspension comes at a time when Pakistan is preparing for upcoming bilateral series. Nawaz's absence will leave a gap in the spin-bowling all-rounder department. The Pakistan Cricket Board has not commented on the matter yet.

Anti-doping context

Cricket has seen a rise in anti-doping cases globally in recent years. The ICC conducts both in-competition and out-of-competition tests. Players are required to declare all medications and supplements on their doping control forms.

Nawaz's case is the first high-profile doping violation from Pakistan since 2020. That year, fast bowler Mohammad Irfan was banned for six months for failing to provide his whereabouts for out-of-competition testing.

The ICC's anti-doping code is aligned with the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) standards. Sanctions are mandatory even if the player claims ignorance about the substance.

What happens next: Nawaz can request a hearing before the tribunal. He may also ask for analysis of his B-sample. A final decision on the ban length is expected within the next few weeks.
