Background: A Bollywood Icon Turns Global Parent

Veteran choreographer and director Farah Khan recently faced a rare moment of vulnerability on social media. Her triplets—Ayaan, Zara and Rohan—are set to begin undergraduate studies in the United States, each at a different university. The public farewell, captured in a series of candid videos, sparked widespread conversation about the realities of sending multiple children abroad.

The Three Destinations: Campus Profiles and Academic Paths

1. University of California, Berkeley – Ayaan (Engineering)

Berkeley offers a rigorous engineering curriculum, renowned faculty, and a vibrant Bay‑Area tech ecosystem. The campus’s emphasis on research aligns with Ayaan’s ambition to join a startup after graduation.

2. New York University – Zara (Film & Media)

NYU’s Tisch School places Zara at the heart of America’s entertainment capital. Proximity to studios and networking events provides a practical edge for a budding filmmaker.

3. University of Texas at Austin – Rohan (Business)

UT Austin’s McCombs School is known for its strong finance and entrepreneurship tracks, offering Rohan access to a growing Texas business scene.

Financial & Logistical Realities for the Triplet Trio

Coordinating three separate admissions cycles, visa applications, and housing contracts amplifies costs and administrative load. Parents often underestimate the cumulative expense of tuition, living allowances, health insurance, and travel.

Emotional Impact and Public Resonance

Farah Khan’s on‑camera goodbye—“If you thought moving one kid to college was hard, try doing it three times”—resonated with Indian parents navigating similar dilemmas. The candid moment highlighted the psychological strain of separation, especially when children are scattered across continents.

  • Pros: Access to world‑class education, diverse cultural exposure, potential for higher earnings.
  • Cons: Elevated financial burden, complex logistics, emotional stress for both parents and children.

The episode also sparked debate on the growing trend of Indian families sending multiple children abroad simultaneously, raising questions about long‑term financial sustainability and cultural assimilation.

Key Takeaways for Prospective Global Parents

1. Conduct a detailed cost‑benefit analysis for each institution, not just tuition.
2. Prepare a unified visa strategy to avoid timing conflicts.
3. Prioritize mental health resources for students transitioning to new environments.

Farah Khan’s experience serves as a case study in balancing ambition with practicality, reminding families that the journey to a global education is as much an emotional marathon as it is an academic sprint.
