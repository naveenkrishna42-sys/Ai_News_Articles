Vijay sworn in as Tamil Nadu chief minister after 2026 election win

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vijay was sworn in as Tamil Nadu chief minister on Saturday after his party swept the 2026 assembly elections.
  • The actor-turned-politician's victory marks a significant shift in the state's political landscape, ending decades of dominance by major parties.
  • Vijay's campaign focused on anti-corruption and development, resonating with a wide cross-section of voters.

Tamil Nadu witnessed a historic political shift on Saturday as actor-turned-politician Vijay took oath as the state's chief minister. The swearing-in ceremony, held at Chennai's Rajarathinam Stadium, drew thousands of supporters and marked the culmination of a fiercely contested 2026 assembly election.

Decisive mandate for Vijay's party

Vijay's party, Tamizhaga Vetri Kazhagam (TVK), secured a clear majority in the 234-member assembly, winning over 140 seats. The result ended the alternating rule of the DMK and AIADMK that has defined Tamil Nadu politics for decades.

The election commission reported a voter turnout of nearly 72%. Political analysts say Vijay's campaign struck a chord with young voters and urban middle-class families tired of corruption and dynastic politics.

From silver screen to state leadership

Vijay, 51, is one of Tamil cinema's biggest stars with a fan base that cuts across age groups. He announced his entry into electoral politics in early 2024, forming TVK after months of speculation.

His transition from actor to chief minister mirrors the path taken by earlier film stars in Tamil Nadu, including M.G. Ramachandran and Jayalalithaa. Unlike them, however, Vijay did not align with any established party, building his own organisation from scratch.

Campaign promises and priorities

During the campaign, Vijay promised a corruption-free administration and focused on job creation, education reform, and improving public healthcare. He also pledged to revive the state's industrial growth, which has lagged behind neighbouring states in recent years.

His rallies drew massive crowds, and his speeches — often delivered without notes — were widely shared on social media. Critics, however, questioned his lack of administrative experience and his party's thin organisational presence in rural areas.

The swearing-in ceremony was attended by leaders from several opposition parties, including Congress and the Left. The DMK and AIADMK stayed away, with their leaders congratulating Vijay in separate statements.

What lies ahead for the new government

Vijay inherits a state with an economy of roughly Rs 20 lakh crore, but facing challenges including high unemployment among graduates, fiscal stress from welfare spending, and water disputes with neighbouring Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh.

His cabinet, sworn in alongside him, includes a mix of former bureaucrats, academics, and first-time legislators. None of the ministers have previous experience running a government department.

The new chief minister has indicated he will present a full budget within 60 days, focusing on infrastructure and job creation. Political observers will be watching how his government handles the transition from campaign rhetoric to governance.

The coming weeks will test whether Vijay can translate his electoral mandate into effective administration, as Tamil Nadu enters uncharted political territory under a leader whose only prior public office was on screen.
