National Green Tribunal grapples with crumbling infrastructure in Chennai

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chennai's NGT benches suffer from leaking roofs, electrical faults, and rodent infestations.
  • Staff and lawyers report safety hazards and disruptions to court proceedings.
  • Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for repairs or renovation.

A court in distress

Chennai's National Green Tribunal (NGT) bench, an institution tasked with protecting the environment, is itself battling serious decay within its own walls. The building, located on the city's suburban fringes, shows visible signs of neglect that have been mounting for months.

Leaking roofs, faulty electrical wiring, and rodent infestations have become routine. Court staff and lawyers say the dilapidation now affects daily operations.

Infrastructure at breaking point

The tribunal's main hearing room has a persistent leak. Monsoon rains force proceedings to be paused as water drips onto desks and case files. Lawyers have complained about the risk of short circuits from exposed wiring near the dais.

Rodents have gnawed through documents stored in record rooms. Staff say the problem has worsened since last year. “We have to keep files inside steel cupboards,” a court employee told reporters, asking not to be named. “Plastic and cardboard are useless now.”

Toilets remain non-functional for weeks at a stretch. Employees and visitors often have to walk to a nearby government building to use washrooms.

Safety and hygiene concerns

The crumbling infrastructure poses safety risks. Lawyers argue that the conditions violate basic workplace safety norms and affect the dignity of the court. “We are here to enforce environmental laws, but we can't even enforce basic maintenance here,” a senior advocate said.

Stagnant water from leaks has led to breeding of mosquitoes. Staff have reported cases of dengue among colleagues. The tribunal's own registry has sent multiple requests for repairs to the parent ministry, but little has changed.

Some courtrooms have been temporarily shifted to alternative rooms, but the space is inadequate. Overcrowding has become common.

No timeline for repairs

Officials from the National Green Tribunal's administrative wing have acknowledged the issues. They say a detailed assessment is underway. However, no timeline has been announced for major repairs or renovation.

The Principal Bench in New Delhi has been briefed about the situation. Sources say a decision on relocating the Chennai bench to a rented facility may be considered if the building cannot be fixed quickly.

What is clear is that the decay has been allowed to fester for too long. A court that hears cases on pollution, waste management, and forest conservation cannot afford to ignore its own environmental health.

The coming months will show whether the tribunal's administration can move from acknowledgment to action. For now, lawyers, staff, and litigants wait — under leaky roofs and flickering lights — for repairs to begin.
