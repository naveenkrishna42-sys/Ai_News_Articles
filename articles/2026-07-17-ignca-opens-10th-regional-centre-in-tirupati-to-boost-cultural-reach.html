IGNCA Opens 10th Regional Centre in Tirupati to Boost Cultural Reach

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • IGNCA's tenth regional centre opened in Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh.
  • The centre aims to strengthen cultural outreach and research in southern India.
  • It will focus on preserving and promoting local traditions, art, and heritage.

The Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts (IGNCA) has opened its tenth regional centre in Tirupati, Andhra Pradesh. The new facility marks a significant step in the organisation's efforts to expand its cultural footprint across India.

IGNCA, an autonomous institution under the Ministry of Culture, already operates regional centres in cities like Varanasi, Bengaluru, and Guwahati. The Tirupati centre becomes the latest addition to this network.

Focus on Southern Heritage

The centre will concentrate on documenting and preserving the rich cultural traditions of southern India. Officials said it will serve as a hub for research on local art forms, manuscripts, and intangible heritage.

Tirupati, known for its ancient temples and vibrant arts scene, offers a strategic location for such work. The centre will collaborate with local universities and cultural organisations.

Inauguration and Key Figures

The inauguration ceremony took place at the regional centre premises. Union Minister for Culture and Tourism, Gajendra Singh Shekhawat, was present for the event. He highlighted the government's commitment to decentralising cultural activities.

IGNCA Chairman Dr. Sachchidanand Joshi also attended. He said the centre will soon launch specific projects focusing on the region's oral traditions, dance forms, and classical music.

Expanding the Network

With this inauguration, IGNCA now has ten regional centres nationwide. The organisation plans to add more in the coming years, particularly in states with rich but under-documented cultural histories.

Each centre operates with a degree of autonomy, tailoring its programmes to local needs. The Tirupati centre will follow this model, initially focusing on archival work and public outreach.

The centre will also host exhibitions, workshops, and lectures. These events are expected to draw participation from artists, scholars, and the public.

Challenges Ahead

While the expansion is welcome, experts point to challenges. Funding and staffing remain concerns for regional centres. IGNCA officials have not yet detailed the budget or personnel allocation for the new unit.

The centre will need to attract local talent and build partnerships quickly. Its success will depend on sustained engagement with the community.

IGNCA has a track record of such work. Its Bengaluru centre, for example, has documented over 5,000 folk songs from Karnataka. Similar projects are expected from the Tirupati centre.

What to watch: The centre's first major project, likely to be announced in the coming weeks, will set the tone for its work. Observers will also look for signs of collaboration with the nearby Sri Venkateswara University.
