Millions removed from voter rolls ahead of key state election in India

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • More than 2 crore voters removed from Assam's electoral rolls ahead of state assembly elections.
  • Government officials say the deletions are part of a drive to remove duplicate and ineligible names.
  • Opposition parties allege a politically motivated purge targeting minority communities.

Mass deletions from electoral rolls spark controversy

Over 2 crore voters have been removed from the electoral rolls in Assam ahead of the state's assembly elections, triggering a political firestorm. The state government says it is 'purifying' the roll by eliminating duplicate entries and ineligible names.

Opposition parties, however, see the move as a targeted purge aimed at disenfranchising minority populations, particularly Bengali-speaking Muslims. The deletions have affected millions in a state where elections are often decided by narrow margins.

Government defends the drive

Chief Election Commissioner Rajiv Kumar said the deletions follow an exhaustive verification process. 'We are not targeting any community. Our only aim is to ensure a clean and accurate electoral roll,' he told reporters in Guwahati. Officials say over 80 lakh names were identified as duplicates, while others belonged to deceased persons and those who have moved out of the state.

The National Register of Citizens (NRC) update in Assam has already left over 19 lakh people without citizenship documentation. Critics argue the voter roll purge is an extension of that process, used selectively against specific groups.

Opposition cries foul

Congress leader Rahul Gandhi called the deletions a 'blatant assault on democracy.' Speaking in Delhi, he said, 'You cannot simply delete millions of voters months before an election and call it purification. This is a political weapon.'

Local activist groups have filed petitions in the Gauhati High Court, seeking a stay on the deletions. Many affected voters claim they were not given notice or a chance to appeal. 'I have voted in every election since 1991. Suddenly my name is gone. No one told me why,' said Abdul Karim, a resident of Barpeta district.

Potential impact on election outcome

Assam has 126 assembly seats and votes in two phases starting next month. In the 2021 elections, the BJP-led coalition won 75 seats. Analysts say the removal of millions of voters could shift the balance in at least a dozen constituencies, especially in the lower Assam districts with large Muslim populations.

Political strategist Neerja Chowdhury noted that 'who is on the list and who is off it can decide the winner in a tight race. This is not a technical exercise — it has deep political implications.'

What to watch

The Supreme Court is expected to hear a challenge to the deletions next week. The Election Commission has said it will set up special camps for affected voters to file claims. Whether those camps are accessible and effective in the run-up to the polls remains to be seen.
