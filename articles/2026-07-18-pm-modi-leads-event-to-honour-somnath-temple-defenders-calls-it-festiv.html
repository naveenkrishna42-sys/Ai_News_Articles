PM Modi leads event to honour Somnath Temple defenders, calls it festival of India's existence

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi led an event honouring the defenders of Somnath Temple.
  • He described the event as a festival of India's existence.
  • The ceremony underscored the temple's role in Indian history and resilience.

Event at Somnath Temple

Prime Minister Narendra Modi led a ceremony on Sunday at the Somnath Temple in Gujarat, honouring those who defended the temple over centuries. He described the gathering as a "festival of India's existence," linking the temple's story to the nation's enduring spirit.

The event drew a large crowd of devotees and local leaders. Officials said it was organised to recognise the sacrifices of those who protected the temple from repeated invasions. Somnath, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, has been rebuilt multiple times after being destroyed by foreign invaders.

PM's remarks on national identity

In his address, Modi said the temple's survival symbolises India's unbroken civilisational thread. He did not name any specific historical figures but referred broadly to "countless unknown warriors" who laid down their lives for the shrine.

The Prime Minister stressed that such events are not merely religious but reaffirm the country's cultural roots. "Somnath is not just a temple; it is the identity of India's soul," he told the gathering. The audience responded with chants of "Jai Somnath."

Modi also linked the temple's resilience to India's present-day challenges. He urged citizens to draw inspiration from the defenders' courage to protect the nation's heritage and values.

Historical context of Somnath

The Somnath Temple has a recorded history of being looted and destroyed multiple times, most notably by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1024 AD. It was rebuilt by various Hindu rulers, including the Solanki king Bhima I and later by the Maratha ruler Ahilyabai Holkar in the 18th century.

After independence, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel spearheaded the temple's reconstruction, and it was completed in 1951. The current structure reflects the Chalukya style of architecture. The temple trust has since organised annual events to commemorate its history.

Political and cultural significance

The event comes amid ongoing debates about India's historical narratives and the role of religious sites in national identity. Critics have accused the government of using such ceremonies for political mileage, but supporters see them as necessary to honour neglected heritage.

Opposition leaders did not attend the event. Some questioned the timing, suggesting it was aimed at rallying the Hindu voter base ahead of state elections later this year. The ruling BJP dismissed these claims, stating the programme was purely cultural and religious.

Local priests performed special rituals, and a guard of honour was presented by the Gujarat Police. The temple was decorated with flowers and lights for the occasion.

What happens next

The temple trust has announced plans to digitise the history of Somnath's defenders and create a museum on the premises. The government is expected to allocate funds for the project in the upcoming budget. The event is likely to be repeated annually, with invitations extended to descendants of historical defenders.
