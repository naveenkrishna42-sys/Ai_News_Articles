Salman Khan's Ganpati Aarti Mistake: What Really Happened

On the final day of Ganeshotsav, Salman Khan was filmed performing the aarti at the visarjan ceremony held at his brother Sohail Khan's residence. In the now-viral clip, the superstar rotates the aarti thali in an anti-clockwise direction—a ritual error known as ulti aarti. His sister, Arpita Khan Sharma, gently intervenes, guiding his hand to rotate the thali clockwise. Salman smiles and follows her correction, and the moment has since been shared widely across social media.

The video, originally reported by Hindustan Times and later picked up by NDTV, Rediff, and Moneycontrol, has amassed millions of views. The family's Ganpati celebrations are an annual fixture in Mumbai's celebrity calendar, but this candid exchange offered a rare, unpolished glimpse into the Khan household.

Why the Direction of Aarti Matters: A Cultural Explainer

In Hindu ritual practice, the direction of the aarti thali carries symbolic weight. A clockwise rotation—moving from the deity's left to right—is considered auspicious and is standard for daily worship and festivals. An anti-clockwise rotation is traditionally reserved for specific tantric rites or, in some regional traditions, for warding off negative energies. Performing ulti aarti during a celebratory occasion like Ganpati visarjan is therefore seen as a ritual oversight rather than a deliberate act.

For devotees, the correction is not about superstition but about adherence to vidhi (procedure). Families often rely on elders or knowledgeable members to maintain these protocols, which explains why Arpita's intervention was swift and accepted without argument.
