Nirmal Purja Killed in Pakistan Avalanche, Climbing Company Confirms

📅 2026-08-02 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nirmal Purja's death was confirmed by his climbing company after an avalanche on Broad Peak.
  • His body and three others were recovered days after the incident.
  • Purja held the record for climbing all 14 eight-thousanders in just over six months.

New Delhi: Nirmal Purja, the celebrated Nepali-British mountaineer who conquered all 14 of the world's highest peaks in a record-breaking 189 days, has died in an avalanche on Broad Peak in Pakistan. His climbing company confirmed the news on Saturday, ending days of desperate hope among the global climbing community.

The avalanche struck the 8,051-metre peak, which lies in the Karakoram range near the disputed territory of Gilgit-Baltistan, earlier this week. Purja's body, along with those of three other climbers, was recovered from the mountain on Friday, three days after the fatal incident.

A Final Dream Cut Short

Purja, known to the world as 'Nimsdai', had set his sights on Broad Peak as part of a new expedition. Friends and fellow climbers noted that after his historic feat on all 14 eight-thousanders in 2019, he was chasing one more dream on the mountain that had eluded him in previous attempts.

The 42-year-old had built a formidable reputation for pushing human limits. His 2019 project, 'Possible', saw him summit Everest, K2, and other giants in a single season, a feat that was earlier deemed impossible. He also led a daring rescue mission on K2 in 2021, saving a climber stranded at the bottleneck.

Three-Day Wait Ends in Grief

Rescue teams, including Pakistani army aviation and local high-altitude porters, had battled adverse weather to reach the avalanche site. The recovery operation was delayed by treacherous conditions, leaving family and supporters in anguish. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact circumstances of the avalanche.

Purja's company, Elite Exped, which he founded to guide climbers on the world's highest mountains, issued a brief statement expressing profound sorrow. "It is with heavy hearts that we confirm the loss of our founder and leader, Nirmal Purja," the statement read. "He died doing what he loved most, in the mountains that were his home."

Legacy Beyond Summits

Born in the Dhaulagiri region of Nepal, Purja served in the British Army's Gurkha regiment and the Special Boat Service before turning to mountaineering full-time. His feats brought him international fame and a devoted following on social media, where he documented every climb with raw honesty.

Many in the mountaineering world remember him not just for speed, but for his audacity and resilience. Colleagues often described him as a man who refused to accept limitations, whether it was thin air or the weight of expectation. His records, including the fastest ascent of all 14 peaks, are yet to be officially surpassed.

Mourning and Questions

Tributes have poured in from across the globe, with fellow climbers, politicians, and sports personalities praising his courage. Nepal's government has yet to issue a formal statement, but officials in Kathmandu have expressed condolences informally. The recovery of the three other climbers' bodies has been confirmed, but their identities have not been released pending family notification.

Broad Peak, the 12th highest mountain in the world, is known for its technical difficulty and unpredictable weather. It has claimed several lives over the years, and this incident is a stark reminder of the risks that remain even for the most experienced mountaineers.

The bodies are expected to be flown to Islamabad for formal procedures before being repatriated. As the climbing community grieves, attention will turn to the broader question of safety on Pakistan's peaks, which have seen a surge in expeditions in recent years.

What happens next will likely involve a detailed investigation into the avalanche's causes and renewed calls for better forecasting and rescue infrastructure in the region. For now, the mountains stand silent, and a legend's journey has come to an abrupt halt.
