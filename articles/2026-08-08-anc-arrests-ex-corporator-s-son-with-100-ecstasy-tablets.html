ANC arrests ex-corporator's son with 100 ecstasy tablets

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The accused is the son of a former corporator.
  • 100 ecstasy tablets were seized by the Anti-Narcotics Cell.
  • Further investigation is underway.

The Anti-Narcotics Cell (ANC) of Mumbai Police has arrested the son of a former corporator after recovering 100 ecstasy tablets from his possession. The arrest was made on Thursday evening, following a tip-off received by the ANC's special squad. The accused has been identified as the 28-year-old son of a former corporator from the Mumbai region, though officials have not yet confirmed his name.

Details of the operation

Acting on a specific input, a team from the ANC laid a trap near a suburban locality and intercepted the accused. During a search, the officers found 100 ecstasy tablets, a synthetic drug popular in party circuits, hidden in a bag he was carrying. The estimated street value of the seized tablets is yet to be determined, but preliminary reports suggest it runs into several lakh rupees.

The accused was immediately taken into custody and booked under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act. He was produced before a local court on Friday, which remanded him to police custody for further questioning. The ANC is now probing the source of the drugs and whether the accused was a consumer or a supplier.

Background of the accused

The former corporator, whose son has been arrested, is a known political figure in the city. While the ANC has not commented on the family's political connections, sources indicate that the accused had no prior criminal record. Neighbours described him as a quiet individual, though some admitted to seeing him frequenting high-end parties in the city.

Police are also examining his phone records and financial transactions to trace his network. This arrest comes at a time when the Mumbai Police have intensified crackdowns on drug peddling, especially in the western suburbs, which have seen a rise in synthetic drug seizures in recent months.

Investigation and next steps

The ANC is likely to conduct further searches at the accused's residence and question his associates. Officials have not ruled out the possibility of a larger racket operating behind the scene. The court has allowed the police to keep the accused in custody for three days to facilitate the probe.

In the meantime, the former corporator has not issued any public statement. It remains unclear whether he will seek legal help for his son or distance himself from the incident.

This case adds to the growing list of drug-related arrests in Mumbai, where authorities have been under pressure to curb the availability of party drugs. The police have urged citizens to report any suspicious activity related to narcotics.

Going forward, the focus will be on unravelling the supply chain and identifying the source of the ecstasy tablets. The outcome of the probe could have wider implications for the city's anti-drug drive.
