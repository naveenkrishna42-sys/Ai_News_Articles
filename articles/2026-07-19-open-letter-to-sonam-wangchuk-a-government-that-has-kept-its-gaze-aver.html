Open Letter to Sonam Wangchuk: A Government That Has Kept its Gaze Averted Will Not See

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sonam Wangchuk's fast draws attention to systemic flaws in India's education system.
  • The government faces criticism for ignoring deeper issues in the NEET exam and beyond.
  • Calls for accountability and reform grow amid ongoing student protests.

The Letter That Speaks for a Movement

An open letter addressed to Sonam Wangchuk has struck a chord across India. Published by TheWire.in on Saturday, it accuses the government of deliberately averting its gaze from the country's educational crisis. The letter is not just about one man's protest. It is a mirror held up to a system that has, for years, refused to see what is broken.

Wangchuk, known for his work in the cold deserts of Ladakh and his environmental activism, is now at the centre of a debate on exam reform. The letter describes him not as a protester, but as an educator pulled away from his work. It argues that the government's silence on the NEET exam crisis is a symptom of a larger failure.

Beyond the Fast: What the Government Misses

The open letter goes beyond political reshuffles. It calls for a renewal of education in India. The writer suggests that the government has focused on managing optics while ignoring the root causes of student unrest. The NEET exam, a gateway for medical aspirants, has been plagued by allegations of paper leaks and procedural lapses.

Parsa Venkateshwar Rao Jr, writing in Deccan Chronicle, argues that the real problems of NEET go deeper than dealing with Wangchuk's fast. The fast is a symptom, not the disease. The disease is a system that prioritises exam scores over learning, and administrative convenience over fairness.

Vikalp Sangam and the Demand for Accountability

Vikalp Sangam, a collective of civil society groups, has demanded accountability for the exam crisis. In a statement reported by Countercurrents, the group backed the student protests and called for a thorough investigation. They argue that the government cannot simply dismiss Wangchuk's demands as the actions of a single activist.

The group's statement reads like a challenge to the establishment. It insists that the crisis is not about one man or one exam. It is about the failure of a system that has lost the trust of millions of students and parents. The demand is clear: accountability, transparency, and reform.

The Government's Averted Gaze

The open letter's core accusation is that the government has kept its gaze averted. It has chosen not to see the anger simmering among students, the flaws in the examination process, or the broader decay of the education system. The letter warns that a government that refuses to look will eventually be forced to see.

This is not a new argument. Critics have long pointed to the disconnect between policy and ground reality. The NEET crisis is just the latest flashpoint. But the letter frames it as a moral failure, not just an administrative one. It asks Wangchuk to keep fighting, because the government's blindness cannot last forever.

What Comes Next

Sonam Wangchuk's fast has entered its second week. Student groups in several states have held solidarity protests. The government has not yet responded to the open letter or the demands from Vikalp Sangam. But the pressure is building. The question now is whether the government will finally turn its gaze towards the crisis, or continue to look away.
