ACB raids TGTRANSCO engineer properties in DA case

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • ACB conducted raids on properties of TGTRANSCO deputy engineer
  • Case pertains to alleged disproportionate assets
  • Raids underway at multiple locations

The Telangana Anti-Corruption Bureau (ACB) on Wednesday conducted raids on multiple properties linked to a deputy engineer of the Telangana State Power Generation Corporation Limited (TGTRANSCO). The searches are part of a disproportionate assets case registered against the official.

Raids at multiple locations

ACB teams fanned out to several locations, including the engineer's official residence and other properties believed to be owned or controlled by him. Officials said the searches were ongoing and details were being documented.

The agency is acting on a complaint that alleged the engineer had amassed assets far beyond his known sources of income. The exact number of locations raided has not been officially confirmed.

Case details emerge

The disproportionate assets case was registered under the Prevention of Corruption Act. The ACB is examining property documents, bank records, and other financial papers seized during the raids.

Sources indicated that the engineer's assets include land parcels, residential buildings, and bank deposits that appear disproportionate to his salary and legal income over the years. Investigators are verifying the ownership and source of funds for each asset.

Previous ACB action in power sector

This is not the first time the ACB has targeted officials in the power sector. The agency has conducted similar raids in the past against employees of TGTRANSCO and other state utilities. Such actions are part of a broader crackdown on corruption in government departments.

The engineer has not been arrested yet. The ACB said it will file a detailed report after completing the searches and analyzing the evidence collected.

The raids come amid heightened vigilance by the state's anti-corruption watchdog. Officials said the investigation will follow due process and any evidence of disproportionate assets will be presented in court.

The ACB has urged the public to share information about corrupt practices by government employees. The agency has set up helplines for whistleblowers to report suspicious activities.

As the searches continue, all eyes are on the outcome of the raids and whether they lead to further action against the engineer. The case is expected to be monitored closely by transparency activists and the general public.
