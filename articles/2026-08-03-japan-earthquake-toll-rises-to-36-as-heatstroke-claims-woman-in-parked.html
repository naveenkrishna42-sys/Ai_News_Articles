Japan earthquake toll rises to 36 as heatstroke claims woman in parked car

📅 2026-08-03 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Death toll from Japan earthquake rises to 36
  • Woman dies of heat stroke in parked car during evacuation
  • Quake halts auto and chip plants, disrupting supply chains

Heatstroke death amid quake aftermath

The death toll from the powerful earthquake that struck Japan has climbed to 36, with officials confirming that a woman died of heat stroke while sheltering in a parked car. The quake, which hit at around 9:47pm local time, registered a magnitude of 6.8 on the Richter scale.

The woman's death highlights the secondary dangers that often follow major earthquakes, as survivors face extreme summer temperatures while their homes are damaged or without power. Authorities have not yet released her identity or the exact location where she was found.

Widespread destruction in Kumamoto

The Kumamoto region bore the brunt of the quake, with intensities of 5-minus recorded there. Neighbouring areas, including Fukuoka Chikugo, Kumamoto Aso, and Kumamoto Amakusa-Ashikita, experienced lower intensities of 3.

Videos circulating on social media show a shopping mall in Japan exploding after the earthquake struck, adding to the chaos. The blast is believed to have been caused by a gas leak, though officials have not yet confirmed the cause.

Supply chain disruptions

The earthquake has halted operations at several auto and chip manufacturing plants, testing the resilience of global supply chains. Japan is a major producer of automotive components and semiconductors, and any prolonged shutdown could have ripple effects worldwide.

Companies have not yet announced when production will resume, and engineers are assessing the structural integrity of the affected facilities. The full economic impact is still unclear.

Evacuation and relief efforts

Rescue teams are working through the rubble in Kumamoto, searching for survivors and clearing debris. Temporary shelters have been set up for displaced residents, but the scorching heat is complicating relief efforts.

Medical officials are urging people to stay hydrated and avoid prolonged exposure to heat, especially those living in cars or temporary housing. The government has not yet declared a state of emergency, but local authorities are coordinating with national agencies.

What to watch

As the death toll continues to rise, the focus will shift to the recovery phase. Officials are expected to provide more details on the number of injured and the extent of structural damage in the coming days.

The disruption to manufacturing could also draw attention from global markets, with investors watching for any prolonged shutdown of key plants. For now, the immediate priority remains saving lives and preventing further casualties from the summer heat.
