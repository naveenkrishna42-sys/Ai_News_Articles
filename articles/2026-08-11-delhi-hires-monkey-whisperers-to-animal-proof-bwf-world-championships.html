Delhi hires monkey whisperers to animal-proof BWF World Championships

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Monkey whisperers, or langur mimics, hired to deter monkeys from venues
  • Non-toxic gel and double-door entries installed at stadiums
  • Imported machines brought in to ensure hassle-free World Championships

New Delhi is leaving no stone unturned—or tree branch unguarded—as it prepares to host the BWF World Championships. Among the more unusual measures: a team of monkey whisperers who mimic langur calls to keep stray monkeys away from the venues.

The capital's monkey population has long been a nuisance at sporting events, and organisers are taking no chances. The Sports Authority of India (SAI) and the Badminton Association of India (BAI) are coordinating efforts to ensure the tournament runs without disruption—both on and off the court.

Langur mimics on the payroll

Monkey whisperers, trained to imitate the alarm calls of langurs—natural predators of the smaller rhesus macaques—have been deployed around the stadiums. The tactic exploits the monkeys' instinctive fear of langurs, keeping them at a safe distance without harming them.

"You can laugh, but you have to love it," said shuttler PV Sindhu, reacting to the presence of langur mimics at the venues. The Olympic medallist's endorsement has drawn attention to the quirky yet practical solution.

Beyond monkeying around

The animal-proofing plan extends beyond vocal decoys. Imported machines have been brought in to emit ultrasonic frequencies that deter monkeys, while non-toxic gel has been applied to surfaces where the animals tend to climb. Double-door entries have been installed at key access points to prevent monkeys from slipping into playing arenas.

The measures are part of a broader effort to ensure a hassle-free experience for players, officials and spectators. Delhi's notorious monkey menace has disrupted events in the past, and organisers are determined to avoid any such embarrassment on the world stage.

SAI and BAI leave nothing to chance

SAI and BAI have been working round the clock, coordinating logistics, security and now wildlife management. The agencies have also roped in local municipal bodies to address the issue of stray monkeys in the vicinity of the venues.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total cost of these measures, but sources suggest it runs into several lakh rupees. The investment is seen as necessary to project a polished image of Delhi as a host city.

For players like Sindhu, the presence of langur mimics is a light-hearted touch in an otherwise high-pressure tournament. But for the organisers, it's a serious operation.

What to watch

As the championships get underway, all eyes will be on how the animal-proofing measures hold up under real match-day conditions. If the langur mimics prove effective, Delhi's approach could become a template for other cities grappling with similar challenges.

The BWF World Championships are set to test not just the players' mettle, but also the city's ability to keep its wild residents in check.
