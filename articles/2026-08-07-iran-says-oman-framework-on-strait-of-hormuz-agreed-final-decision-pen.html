Iran says Oman framework on Strait of Hormuz agreed; final decision pending

📅 2026-08-07 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iranian official says framework agreed with Oman on Strait of Hormuz
  • Iranian parliamentary committee reviewing draft bill to bar US, Israeli ships
  • US President Trump says Hormuz talks 'moving along'
  • Reports suggest reopening plan involves no fees or tolls

Framework with Oman announced

An Iranian official said on Friday that a framework for the Strait of Hormuz has been agreed with Oman, adding that a final decision must still be made. The announcement comes amid heightened tensions in the region, with the strait at the centre of a bitter standoff between Tehran and Washington.

The official, speaking on condition of anonymity, did not specify the terms of the agreement. But the statement marks the first public confirmation of a structured plan to manage one of the world's most critical oil chokepoints.

Parliamentary push to bar US, Israeli ships

Separately, the Iranian parliamentary committee is reviewing a draft bill that would bar US and Israeli ships from transiting the strait, according to the semi-official Fars news agency. The move signals that hardliners in Tehran are pressing for a tougher line even as diplomatic channels remain open.

If passed, the bill could complicate any deal with Oman and raise the risk of direct confrontation in the waterway. Analysts note that the strait carries roughly a fifth of global oil consumption, making any disruption a serious concern for world markets.

Trump says talks 'moving along'

US President Donald Trump, meanwhile, said that Hormuz talks were 'moving along', even as Iran mulled the ban. His remarks, reported by Bloomberg, suggested Washington is cautiously optimistic about a negotiated outcome.

The White House has not released details of the discussions. But the president's tone appeared to soften from earlier threats to use military force if Iran blocked the strait.

How the Iran-Oman deal might work

The Times of India reported that the proposed reopening of the Strait of Hormuz under an Iran-Oman arrangement would involve 'no fees or tolls'. The plan appears designed to reassure shipping firms and insurers, who have faced soaring risk premiums since the conflict escalated.

Under the reported framework, Oman would act as a neutral guarantor, helping to manage traffic and verify cargoes. The absence of tolls could be a key concession to international shippers, though questions remain about enforcement and security.

The Houthi movement in Yemen has stepped up attacks in recent days, according to The Economist, adding another layer of complexity to any agreement. The rebels, backed by Iran, have targeted vessels in the Red Sea and the Arabian Sea, raising fears of a wider maritime conflict.

What to watch

The coming days will be critical. Iran's parliament is expected to debate the draft bill, and any final decision on the Oman framework will hinge on Tehran's political calculus. International shipping firms are watching closely, as are oil markets, which have already priced in a risk premium.

Whether the framework can survive the hardliners' push for restrictions on American and Israeli ships remains uncertain. The world will be looking to Muscat and Tehran for clarity, and to Washington for a response.
