Michigan F-16 Crash: Pilot Ejected, Jet Goes Up in Flames

Incident Overview

On 16 September 2026, a Texas Air National Guard F‑16C Block 50 entered an uncontrolled spin over Blair Township, Michigan. During its final approach to Morison Field, the aircraft collided with shallow ground on County Road 633 and ignited, creating a high‑profile crater. The pilot, 2nd Lt. Alex Gordon, activated the seat‑belts‑to‑tether pivot, flipped into the ejection seat and left the aircraft at 12 knot altitude

Investigation Findings

The Air Force Accident Investigation Board (AFAIB) concluded that the loss of control stemmed from a combination of pilot‑induced oscillation and a previously undetected fault in the aircraft’s flight‑control computer.

Human Factors

Investigators noted that 2nd Lt. Alex Gordon, while experienced in basic flight maneuvers, had limited exposure to high‑angle‑of‑attack recoveries in the F‑16C Block 50.

  • Recent simulator training emphasized low‑speed handling but omitted deep‑stall scenarios.
  • Stress indicators from cockpit voice recordings showed elevated heart rate prior to the spin entry.
  • The ejection sequence was executed correctly; the seat‑belt‑to‑tether pivot functioned as designed.

Technical Factors

Post‑flight diagnostics revealed a transient glitch in the digital flight‑control system (DFCS) that caused an uncommanded rudder deflection of approximately 5°.

  • The glitch occurred during a rapid throttle reduction, coinciding with the aircraft’s transition from powered flight to glide.
  • Software version 3.2.1 of the DFCS contained a known race condition that had been patched in later releases, but the aircraft had not yet received the update.
  • Structural inspection of the wreckage showed no pre‑existing fatigue cracks; the crater depth was consistent with a shallow‑impact, high‑energy burn.

Takeaways

  • Human performance factors, particularly limited exposure to deep‑stall recovery, played a significant role in the mishap.
  • A latent software defect in the DFCS can precipitate uncommanded control inputs under specific throttle‑reduction profiles.
  • Timely application of software patches and updated training curricula are critical mitigations for similar platforms.
  • The F‑16C Block 50 remains a cost‑effective multirole fighter, with a flyaway price under $30 million and operational costs comparable to contemporaneous fourth‑generation aircraft.
  • Investments in ejection‑seat reliability and post‑impact survivability systems continue to yield high crew‑survival rates, as demonstrated in this incident.
