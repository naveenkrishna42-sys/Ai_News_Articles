NEET Paper Leak: 180 Students Detained in Chennai; CPM Criticises Police Action

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Around 180 students were detained during a protest in Chennai over the NEET paper leak.
  • The CPM has criticised the Tamil Nadu government for its handling of the protest.
  • The NEET paper leak has sparked widespread outrage among students and political parties.

Mass Detentions at Chennai Protest

Chennai witnessed a significant show of student dissent on Thursday as around 180 protesters were detained by police. The students were demonstrating against the recent leak of the NEET examination paper, a scandal that has roiled the state.

Police officials confirmed the detentions, stating that the protest had turned unlawful. The demonstration took place near the state secretariat, drawing a heavy police presence.

CPM Condemns Government Response

The Communist Party of India (Marxist) has strongly criticised the Tamil Nadu government's response to the protest. Party leaders accused the police of an overreaction and called the detentions an attack on democratic rights.

In a statement, the CPM demanded the immediate release of all detained students. The party also questioned the government's commitment to a transparent investigation into the NEET paper leak.

Background of the NEET Scandal

The NEET paper leak has sent shockwaves through the state, with thousands of medical aspirants feeling cheated. The leak, which came to light earlier this week, has led to widespread calls for a re-examination.

Students have been holding sporadic protests across Tamil Nadu, demanding strict action against those responsible. The state government has promised a high-level inquiry into the matter.

Political Fallout

The incident has quickly become a political flashpoint. Opposition parties, including the CPM, have seized on the issue to attack the ruling DMK-led government.

Critics argue that the government's handling of the leak has been inadequate. They point to the detention of students as an attempt to suppress dissent rather than address the core issue of examination integrity.

What Happens Next

The detained students are expected to be produced before a magistrate later today. Meanwhile, the investigation into the NEET paper leak continues, with the state government under mounting pressure to ensure a fair process.

All eyes are now on the government's next move, as student protests show no signs of abating. The coming days will likely determine whether the administration can restore confidence among the state's medical aspirants.
