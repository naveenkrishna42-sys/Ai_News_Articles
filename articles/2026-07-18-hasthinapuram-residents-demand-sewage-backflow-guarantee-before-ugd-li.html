Hasthinapuram residents demand sewage backflow guarantee before UGD link-up

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hasthinapuram residents are hesitant to opt for UGD connections without a guarantee against sewage backflow.
  • Past incidents of sewage flooding during rains have raised concerns about the new drainage system.
  • Local authorities are yet to provide written assurance to address the residents' demands.

Residents of Hasthinapuram in Chennai have sought a written assurance from the local authorities that connecting to the underground drainage (UGD) system will not lead to sewage backflow into their homes. The demand comes as the civic body pushes for universal adoption of the newly laid drainage network in the locality.

Many homeowners remain wary of signing up for the connections, citing past experiences where heavy rains caused sewage to overflow from manholes and enter residential properties. The fear is that the new system, while designed to improve sanitation, could replicate or worsen the problem if not properly managed.

Recurring sewage flooding

Hasthinapuram, a densely populated neighbourhood in southern Chennai, has faced chronic sewage flooding during the monsoon season. Residents recall instances where stormwater mixed with untreated waste, creating health hazards and damaging household belongings.

"We have seen what happens when the drains get clogged. Even a moderate shower can turn the streets into a sewer. We need a clear guarantee that the UGD connection will not bring that into our houses," said a local resident who did not wish to be named.

Conditional opt-in

Several families have indicated they will only connect to the UGD system if the authorities provide a written commitment to maintain the network regularly and ensure backflow prevention mechanisms are in place. The demand has slowed the rollout of connections in the area.

Municipal officials have held meetings with resident welfare associations but have not yet furnished the requested assurance. They maintain that the new UGD system is built to modern specifications and includes measures to prevent backflow, but residents remain unconvinced without a formal document.

Broader context of UGD expansion

The Chennai Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board, commonly known as Metrowater, has been expanding the UGD network across the city to improve sanitation and reduce groundwater contamination. Hasthinapuram is among the latest areas to be covered under this initiative.

However, similar trust deficits have emerged in other neighbourhoods where past infrastructure projects failed to deliver promised results. Residents are now demanding accountability and transparency before committing to long-term utility connections.

Officials from the local ward office have stated that they are considering the residents' request and will respond after technical evaluations. They have urged patience, noting that the new system has passed all safety checks.

What lies ahead

The standoff between residents and authorities in Hasthinapuram highlights a common challenge in urban infrastructure upgrades: winning public confidence. The outcome of this demand could set a precedent for how other localities approach similar transitions.

Metrowater is expected to hold another round of consultations with residents in the coming weeks. A resolution will likely depend on whether the board can offer a binding commitment on maintenance and backflow prevention, or whether residents will have to rely on verbal assurances.
