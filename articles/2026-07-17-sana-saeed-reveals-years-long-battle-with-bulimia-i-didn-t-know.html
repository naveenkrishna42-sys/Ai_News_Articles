Sana Saeed Reveals Years-Long Battle With Bulimia: 'I Didn't Know'

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sana Saeed revealed she has been battling bulimia, an eating disorder characterised by binge eating and purging.
  • The actress said she did not know she had the condition for years, as she had never heard about it growing up.
  • Saeed urged others to seek help and break the stigma around mental health and eating disorders.

Child star Sana Saeed, best remembered as little Anjali in the 1998 blockbuster Kuch Kuch Hota Hai, has opened up about a silent health struggle that went undiagnosed for years. In a candid video shared on social media, the 37-year-old actress revealed she has been battling bulimia nervosa — a serious eating disorder.

“I never heard about it growing up,” Saeed said in the clip, her voice steady but emotional. “For years, I didn’t even know I had it.” The confession has sparked conversations around mental health and body image in Bollywood, an industry known for its punishing beauty standards.

The Disorder She Didn't Recognise

Bulimia nervosa is characterised by cycles of binge eating followed by compensatory behaviours like self-induced vomiting, excessive exercise, or laxative misuse. According to the National Institute of Mental Health, it affects roughly 1-2% of women in India, though experts believe the actual number is higher due to underreporting.

Saeed explained that she had no vocabulary for what she was experiencing. “I thought it was normal. I thought everyone felt this way after eating. But I was wrong,” she said. The actress admitted that the disorder went unchecked because she never associated her symptoms with an illness.

A Career in the Spotlight

Saeed shot to fame as a child actor in Shah Rukh Khan and Kajol's iconic film. She later returned to the big screen in Karan Johar's Student of the Year (2012), playing a supporting role. Over the years, she has maintained a low profile, occasionally posting about fitness and lifestyle on Instagram.

In her video, Saeed did not specify when the disorder began or how long she suffered before seeking help. But she stressed the importance of early intervention. “If I had known the signs, I would have reached out sooner. I want others to know they are not alone,” she said.

Breaking the Stigma

Mental health professionals have long warned that eating disorders are underdiagnosed in India. Cultural taboos, lack of awareness, and limited access to specialised care often keep sufferers silent. Saeed's openness marks a rare moment of vulnerability from a Bollywood insider.

“It takes a lot of courage to speak about something so personal,” said Dr. Meera Sharma, a Mumbai-based clinical psychologist who works with eating disorder patients. “When a public figure shares their story, it can help normalise seeking help.” Saeed ended her message with a plea: “Please talk to someone. It’s okay to not be okay.”

What happens next? Saeed has not announced any specific recovery plan or treatment, but she has urged her followers to educate themselves about bulimia and its warning signs. For now, her confession stands as a reminder that the battle with body image often starts quietly — and sometimes, the first step is simply naming it.
