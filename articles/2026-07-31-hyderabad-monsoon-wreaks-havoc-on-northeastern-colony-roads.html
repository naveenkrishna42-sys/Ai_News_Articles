Hyderabad Monsoon Wreaks Havoc on Northeastern Colony Roads

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Monsoon rains have severely damaged roads in Hyderabad's northeastern colonies.
  • Residents face daily commuting challenges due to multiple potholes.
  • Local authorities yet to announce a timeline for repairs.

Rains Expose Crumbling Infrastructure

The southwest monsoon has been relentless over Hyderabad this July, and its impact is now starkly visible on the roads of the city's northeastern colonies. Several arterial and interior roads in areas such as Alwal, Safilguda, and Malkajgiri are now riddled with potholes, making commutes a bumpy and hazardous affair for residents.

Waterlogging during heavy spells has weakened the asphalt layer, and the constant movement of heavy vehicles has worsened the damage. What were once smooth stretches are now cratered surfaces, forcing two-wheelers to weave dangerously and cars to crawl at a snail's pace.

Commuters Bear the Brunt

Daily commuters in these colonies are bearing the brunt of the poor road conditions. Auto-rickshaw drivers and cab operators report longer travel times and increased vehicle maintenance costs. School buses and office goers, too, are struggling to navigate the pockmarked roads, especially during peak hours.

Residents have lodged complaints with the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) and local corporators, but no concrete action has been taken so far. Many are questioning the quality of road restoration work carried out before the monsoon, which appears to have failed under the seasonal onslaught.

Officials Yet to Respond

Officials from the GHMC's engineering wing have not yet confirmed any specific timeline for repairing these roads. The monsoon is expected to continue for several more weeks, which could further delay any patchwork or resurfacing efforts. Meanwhile, residents are left to fend for themselves, navigating a labyrinth of potholes every single day.

Telangana Today first reported the issue, highlighting the urgent need for intervention. The civic body has in the past cited lack of funds and the continuous rain as obstacles, but residents argue that preventive maintenance could have mitigated the damage.

A Recurring Annual Problem

This is not a new phenomenon. Every year, the monsoon exposes the fragility of Hyderabad's road network, particularly in the peripheral and northeastern zones. The problem is compounded by rapid urbanisation and the laying of utility cables and pipelines, which often leave trenches that are poorly reinstated.

Road safety experts note that potholes are not just an inconvenience but a serious safety hazard, especially for two-wheeler riders who risk fatal accidents. The lack of proper signage or barricades around the damaged stretches adds to the danger, particularly at night.

What Lies Ahead

As the monsoon shows no signs of abating, the immediate outlook remains grim. The GHMC is expected to undertake repair works once the rains subside, but no official announcement has been made. Residents and local activists are now demanding a more durable, long-term solution rather than temporary patchwork that washes away with the next downpour.

The coming weeks will be crucial, as any further delay could deepen the crisis. All eyes are now on the civic authorities to see if they will act swiftly to restore normalcy to these beleaguered neighbourhoods.
