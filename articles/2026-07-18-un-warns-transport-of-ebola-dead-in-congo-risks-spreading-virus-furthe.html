UN Warns Transport of Ebola Dead in Congo Risks Spreading Virus Further

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • UN agency warns transport of Ebola victims is spreading the virus within Congo.
  • Cases have topped 2,100 as the outbreak worsens.
  • Medics in DR Congo strike over unpaid salaries, complicating response.
  • Growing violence in the region is hampering humanitarian efforts.

Deadly Cargo: How Body Transport Fuels Ebola's Reach

The United Nations has issued a stark warning: the movement of dead bodies within the Democratic Republic of Congo is actively spreading the Ebola virus. The agency says unsafe transport and burial practices are turning victims into vectors of the disease.

When a person dies of Ebola, their remains remain highly infectious. Moving them across districts without proper precautions is a direct route for the virus to find new hosts. The UN has stressed that this is a major, yet preventable, driver of the current outbreak.

Ebola Cases Surge Past 2,100 as Health System Buckles

The outbreak has now recorded over 2,100 cases, according to the World Health Organization. The WHO has warned that the virus is finding new footholds, spreading faster than containment teams can keep up.

The sheer volume of dead is overwhelming local burial teams. In some areas, families are bypassing official protocols, transporting loved ones home for traditional rites. This is exactly the behaviour the UN says must stop.

Medics Strike Over Unpaid Wages, Paralyzing Response

Compounding the crisis, frontline health workers have gone on strike in parts of DR Congo. The reason: months of unpaid salaries. They have downed tools at a time when every hour counts.

Without these medics, treatment centres are understaffed. Contact tracing has slowed. The UN is now scrambling to negotiate with authorities to release funds, but the damage to morale and momentum is already visible.

Rising Violence Forces UN to Rethink Strategy

Humanitarians are also facing a surge in attacks. Armed groups operating in eastern Congo are targeting health facilities and convoys. The UN has had to step up its own security measures just to deliver basic aid.

Several response teams have been forced to suspend operations in dangerous zones. This creates a vacuum where the virus can spread unchecked. The UN is now calling for a ceasefire in these areas to allow medics to work safely.

What Happens Next

The coming weeks will be critical. The UN is racing to train local teams in safe burial techniques and to secure safe passage for humanitarian convoys. Without a halt to the violence and a resolution to the strike, the outbreak is likely to push past all previous records.
