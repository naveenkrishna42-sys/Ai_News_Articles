Sunny Deol: Dharmendra backed Ghayal after every producer rejected it

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sunny Deol said Dharmendra supported Ghayal after all producers rejected it
  • The actor recalled this while promoting his upcoming film Batwara 1947
  • He said his father was proud that he stood by his conviction in the project

Mumbai: Sunny Deol has opened up about the struggles behind his 1990 blockbuster Ghayal, revealing that his father Dharmendra was the only one who backed the film after every producer turned it down. The actor made the disclosure while promoting his upcoming film Batwara 1947.

Speaking at a promotional event, Deol recalled how the action drama, which later became a landmark in his career, initially found no takers in the industry. "Every producer rejected it," he said, adding that it was Dharmendra who stepped in with unwavering support.

Father's faith in the project

Deol said his father believed in the script when no one else did. "He was proud that I stood by my conviction," the actor recounted, emphasising that Dharmendra's backing went beyond just financial support. It was a vote of confidence in his son's judgment as an actor.

The veteran star, known for his intense action roles, said the experience taught him the value of patience and self-belief. Ghayal went on to become one of the biggest hits of his career, cementing his position as a leading action hero in Hindi cinema.

Recreating Ghatak magic in Bihar

The revelation comes amid a busy promotional season for Deol, who recently recreated his iconic dialogue from Ghatak during a fan meet in Bihar. The moment, captured on video, sent fans into a frenzy as the actor delivered the famous lines with the same intensity that made the 1996 film a cult favourite.

Deol's connect with his audience remains strong, with fans across generations turning up to see him. The Bihar event was part of a wider outreach as he gears up for the release of Batwara 1947, a film that deals with the Partition era.

Actor on controversy and cinema

In a separate interaction, Deol addressed the perception that his films court controversy. "We don't make films to create controversy," he said, clarifying that his choices are driven by strong scripts and compelling narratives rather than deliberate provocation.

The statement comes as Batwara 1947 prepares for release, with the film expected to tackle sensitive historical themes. Deol said the team approached the subject with care, focusing on human stories rather than political commentary.

His remarks on controversy also drew attention to his recent work, including the 2023 hit Gadar 2, which revived his box-office dominance. That film, a sequel to the 2001 blockbuster, reaffirmed his status as a mass entertainer.

What to watch for

As Batwara 1947 heads to theatres, audiences will be keen to see how Deol balances historical gravitas with his signature action style. The film's trailer has already generated significant buzz, and early buzz suggests it could be another strong outing for the actor.

With promotions in full swing, Deol's candid anecdotes about his early struggles are likely to keep resonating with fans, offering a glimpse into the journey of one of Hindi cinema's most enduring stars.
