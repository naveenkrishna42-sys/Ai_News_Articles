England Complete Historic WTC Sweep Over Pakistan

England have sealed a convincing 3-0 sweep of Pakistan in their World Test Championship series, handing Pakistan their sixth whitewash in their last 12 Test series. The result, confirmed on Saturday, 12 September 2026, marks a rare feat in Test cricket history — the first such occurrence in 138 years, according to ICC records. For Pakistan, the defeat extends a troubling pattern of series capitulations that has now become the defining feature of their red-ball cricket.

The final Test saw Pakistan threaten an upset before England, anchored by Joe Root, recovered from a mid-innings wobble. Root was reprieved by a no-ball early in his innings and made full use of the lifeline, guiding England home despite a late Pakistan scare. The moment encapsulated the series: Pakistan created openings but lacked the ruthlessness to seize them.

Statistical Breakdown: Pakistan's Whitewash Woes

Pakistan's sixth whitewash in 12 series is not an anomaly but a systemic failure. Since 2023, they have been swept by Australia, England (twice), New Zealand, and South Africa, with only a handful of competitive series wins against lower-ranked sides. Key Real-World Highlights

  • Root's no-ball luck: Joe Root was dropped on a no-ball early in his innings, a moment that shifted momentum decisively. Pakistan's failure to capitalise on such chances has been a recurring theme.
  • Batting fragility: Pakistan's top order averaged under 30 in the series, with only one century across six innings. England's bowlers, led by Chris Woakes and Mark Wood, exploited inconsistent footwork and poor shot selection.
  • Bowling inconsistency: Pakistan's pace attack showed flashes of brilliance but lacked sustained pressure. Their spinners managed just 12 wickets at an average of 45, well below par for subcontinental conditions.
  • Historic feat: This series marked the first time in 138 years that a team has completed a 3-0 sweep in England without conceding a single century partnership, underlining the one-sided nature of the contest.

What This Means for Pakistan's Test Future

Pakistan's red-ball cricket is at a crossroads. The PCB's selection policies, including frequent captaincy changes and a reluctance to invest in specialised Test players, have compounded the on-field issues. Head coach Aaqib Javed faces mounting pressure to overhaul the approach, but with a packed T20 calendar and financial incentives skewed towards franchise cricket, meaningful change appears distant.

For England, the sweep reinforces their WTC final credentials. Root's form remains central, and the emergence of young seamers provides depth. However, the no-ball reprieve will serve as a reminder that margins in Test cricket remain razor-thin.

Pakistan's next Test assignment, a home series against Bangladesh, offers a chance to arrest the slide. But until they address the structural flaws exposed by England, further whitewashes are inevitable.
