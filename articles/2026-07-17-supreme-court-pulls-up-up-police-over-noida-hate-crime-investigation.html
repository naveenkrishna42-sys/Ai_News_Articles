Supreme Court Pulls Up UP Police Over Noida Hate Crime Investigation

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court pulls up UP Police over Noida hate crime probe.
  • Court questions delays and lapses in investigation.
  • Case involves allegations of hate crime targeting a specific community.
  • Police told to file a fresh status report within a week.

SC Flags Noida Hate Crime Probe

The Supreme Court of India on Wednesday pulled up the Uttar Pradesh Police over its investigation into a hate crime incident in Noida. The bench, while hearing a petition, expressed strong displeasure over the slow pace and alleged lapses in the probe.

Justices noted that the police appeared to be dragging their feet on key aspects of the case. The court questioned why crucial evidence had not been collected and why witnesses had not been examined promptly.

Court Questions Police Conduct

“What is happening? Why is the investigation moving at a snail’s pace?” the bench asked the state’s counsel. The judges pointed out that hate crimes require swift and transparent action to maintain public confidence in the law.

The court also raised concerns about the handling of the complaint. It noted that the first information report appeared to have been lodged after considerable delay, undermining the credibility of the probe.

Case Background

The case pertains to an alleged hate crime in Noida, where members of a particular community were targeted. Petitioners had approached the apex court seeking a fair investigation and protection for the victims.

They alleged that the local police were not cooperating and that the accused were being shielded. The court took serious note of these allegations and demanded a detailed response from the state government.

One-Week Deadline

The Supreme Court directed the Uttar Pradesh Police to file a fresh status report within a week. The report must detail the steps taken so far and the timeline for completing the investigation.

The bench also warned against any attempt to derail the probe. It said that if the court found the response unsatisfactory, it would consider transferring the case to another agency, such as the Central Bureau of Investigation.

What happens next: The court will take up the matter again after the status report is submitted, and could order a CBI probe if the police fail to show progress.
