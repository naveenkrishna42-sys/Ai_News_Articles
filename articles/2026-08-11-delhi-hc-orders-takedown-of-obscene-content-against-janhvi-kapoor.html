Delhi HC Orders Takedown of Obscene Content Against Janhvi Kapoor

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Court orders takedown of obscene and AI-generated content against Janhvi Kapoor from 5,000 internet pages.
  • Judges caution against blanket bans on fan pages, citing free speech protections.
  • Ruling underscores need for 'sanity' in personality-rights cases involving celebrities.

The Delhi High Court on Tuesday ruled in favour of actress Janhvi Kapoor, directing the removal of obscene and morphed content from approximately 5,000 internet pages. The plea covered allegedly pornographic material, AI-generated deepfakes, and other fake content that had been circulating online, causing distress to the actor.

While the court acknowledged the seriousness of the abuse, it also raised questions about the broader implications for free speech, particularly in cases involving celebrity fan pages. The bench reportedly remarked that celebrities cannot expect to be immune from criticism or satire, and that banning fan pages outright would be akin to living in a cave.

Court's Balancing Act

The High Court's order is significant because it attempts to strike a balance between protecting an individual's right to dignity and preserving the fundamental right to free expression. The judges noted that while obscene and defamatory content cannot be allowed to persist, not every critical or even unflattering post qualifies as illegal.

The court called for "sanity" in personality-rights cases, suggesting that celebrities, by virtue of their public standing, must tolerate a certain degree of public scrutiny. However, this tolerance does not extend to content that is explicitly pornographic or fabricated using artificial intelligence to demean the person.

Takedown Mechanics and Scope

According to the order, the takedown will apply to content hosted on various platforms, including social media websites, video streaming sites, and other online repositories. The court directed authorities to identify and remove the offending material, which includes deepfake videos and morphed images that misrepresented the actress.

The scale of the operation—covering 5,000 pages—highlights the pervasive nature of such abuse, which has become a growing concern for public figures in India. Legal experts note that while existing laws address obscenity and defamation, the rapid rise of AI-generated content poses new challenges for enforcement.

Free Speech Concerns Raised

The High Court's remarks on fan pages have sparked debate among legal circles. The bench reportedly questioned whether banning fan pages, which often celebrate a celebrity's work, would be a proportionate response to the problem. Such pages are generally non-commercial and may contain critical commentary, which is protected under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution.

Observers say the court is wary of setting a precedent where celebrities can silence dissent by invoking personality rights. The ruling appears to suggest that takedown orders must be narrowly tailored to target only unlawful content, rather than sweeping legitimate speech.

What This Means for Celebrities

For Janhvi Kapoor, the immediate relief is the removal of offensive material that has caused her personal and professional harm. The actress had approached the court seeking urgent intervention, citing the psychological toll of having her image manipulated for malicious purposes.

The decision also sends a message to other public figures who have faced similar abuse: the courts are willing to act, but not at the cost of fundamental freedoms. It remains to be seen how platforms will implement the takedown and whether they will develop mechanisms to prevent re-uploading of the same content.

Going forward, the focus will be on how the court's balancing test is applied in future cases, especially those involving AI-generated content. As technology evolves, the judiciary may need to craft more specific guidelines to address the unique challenges posed by deepfakes and other synthetic media.
