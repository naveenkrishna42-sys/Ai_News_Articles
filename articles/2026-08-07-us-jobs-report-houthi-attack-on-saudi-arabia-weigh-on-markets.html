US jobs report, Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia weigh on markets

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • S&P 500 futures little changed ahead of US nonfarm payrolls report
  • Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia pushes crude oil prices higher
  • Dollar holds steady, markets cautious ahead of key data

Global markets opened Friday in a cautious mood, with S&P 500 futures barely moving as investors braced for the latest US jobs report. At the same time, crude oil prices edged up after a Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia, adding a fresh layer of uncertainty to an already tense trading day.

Futures steady, all eyes on nonfarm payrolls

US stock futures were little changed in early trading, reflecting the market's wait-and-see stance. The focus is squarely on the nonfarm payrolls data due later in the day, which is expected to offer clues on the health of the world's largest economy.

Traders are looking for signs of cooling in the labour market, which could influence the Federal Reserve's next policy move. A stronger-than-expected reading might reignite fears of prolonged high interest rates, while a weak print could revive hopes for a cut.

According to Investing.com, the S&P 500 futures remained flat, indicating that investors are reluctant to take big positions before the release. The dollar held steady against major currencies, reflecting similar caution across asset classes.

Oil gains after Houthi attack

Crude oil prices wobbled but ultimately moved higher following reports of a Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia. The incident, which targeted energy infrastructure, raised concerns about supply disruptions in one of the world's key producing regions.

Brent and WTI futures both saw modest gains, though the move was tempered by broader demand worries. Analysts note that while the attack has not yet caused any reported output loss, the geopolitical risk premium is back on the table.

The attack comes at a delicate time for oil markets, which have been grappling with oversupply concerns and patchy demand from major economies like China. Any sustained escalation could push prices higher, but so far the market's reaction has been contained.

Stocks muted, dollar steady

European and Asian equities also showed little direction, tracking the cautious tone set by US futures. The dollar index remained rangebound, with traders hesitant to bet on any single outcome from the jobs report.

Bloomberg's markets wrap described the session as "muted," with crude oil the only notable mover. The combination of a key data release and a geopolitical flashpoint has left many investors on the sidelines.

For Indian markets, the global cues suggest a flat to slightly negative opening. The Sensex and Nifty are likely to take their cue from the jobs data and oil price movements, which have a direct bearing on domestic inflation and fiscal health.

What traders should watch

The US jobs report is due at 8:30 AM ET, and any surprise could trigger sharp moves across equities, bonds, and currencies. A hot number could push the dollar higher and weigh on emerging market assets, including the rupee.

On the oil front, developments in the Red Sea and the broader Middle East will be closely monitored. The Houthi attack has not been officially confirmed by Saudi authorities, and details remain sketchy, but the market is clearly on edge.

Investors are also keeping an eye on any potential supply response from OPEC+, which has been managing output levels to support prices. A sustained rally in crude could complicate the inflation picture globally, making the central bank's job harder.

As the day progresses, the interplay between the jobs report and oil prices will likely dictate market direction. For now, patience seems to be the prevailing strategy.
