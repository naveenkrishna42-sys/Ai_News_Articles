EU Fines Google $1 Billion Over Play Store, Search Practices

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • EU fines Google $1 billion for favoring its own services in search and Play Store
  • Google ordered to share search data and open Android to rival AI companies
  • This is the latest in a series of antitrust actions by the EU against Google

Record Fine for Antitrust Violations

The European Union has imposed a $1 billion fine on Google for violating competition laws by prioritizing its own services in search results and on the Play Store. The penalty, announced on Thursday, marks one of the largest antitrust fines in the bloc's history.

The European Commission found that Google abused its dominant position by giving preferential treatment to its own shopping, travel, and other services over those of competitors. The company was also accused of restricting app developers on the Play Store from using alternative payment systems.

Google Ordered to Change Practices

Beyond the financial penalty, the EU has ordered Google to take significant steps to level the playing field. The company must now share its search data with rival search engines and open its Android operating system to competing AI companies.

This order is aimed at fostering competition in the digital market, which the EU says has been stifled by Google's practices. The commission has given Google a timeline to implement these changes, though specific details have not been disclosed.

Reactions and Implications

Google has stated that it disagrees with the EU's findings and plans to appeal the decision. In a statement, the company said its practices are designed to improve user experience and that it will continue to defend its position.

The fine is part of a broader crackdown by the EU on big tech companies. The bloc has previously fined Google billions of euros in other antitrust cases, including a €4.34 billion penalty in 2018 for Android-related violations.

Impact on the Tech Industry

The decision could have ripple effects across the tech industry, as it sets a precedent for how dominant platforms can operate. Other companies like Apple and Amazon are also under scrutiny for similar practices.

Industry analysts say the ruling may force Google to fundamentally change its business model in Europe, potentially affecting its revenue from search and app store operations. The company's shares saw a slight dip in after-hours trading following the announcement.

Next Steps for Google

Google has two months to pay the fine and must submit a compliance plan to the EU within 90 days. The company is expected to challenge the order in the European Court of Justice, a process that could take years.

The EU has made it clear that it will continue to monitor Google's compliance closely. Failure to adhere to the order could result in additional penalties of up to 5% of Google's daily global turnover.

As the legal battle unfolds, the tech world will watch how Google adapts to these new rules, and whether other regulators follow the EU's lead in curbing Big Tech's dominance.
