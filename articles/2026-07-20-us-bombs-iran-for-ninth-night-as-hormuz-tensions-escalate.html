US bombs Iran for ninth night as Hormuz tensions escalate

📅 2026-07-20 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US has bombed Iran for the ninth consecutive night.
  • Houthis have imposed a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea.
  • Iran's president says the country is in a 'full-scale war'.
  • Iran has hit out at Gulf neighbours as US strikes continue.
  • The conflict now involves multiple fronts, including sea routes.

The United States has carried out a ninth consecutive night of bombing raids on Iran, as tensions in the strategic Strait of Hormuz reach a new peak. The strikes, now in their second week, mark one of the most sustained aerial campaigns in the region in decades.

Reports from the ground indicate that targets have included military installations, radar sites, and missile batteries along Iran's southern coast. The US military has not released a detailed assessment of the damage, but officials have described the operations as 'precision strikes' aimed at degrading Iran's ability to threaten shipping in the Persian Gulf.

Houthis open a new front at sea

The conflict took a dramatic turn on Monday when Yemen's Houthi movement announced a naval blockade against Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea. The group, which is backed by Iran, said it would target all vessels heading to Saudi ports in retaliation for the US strikes on Iran.

The blockade threatens one of the world's busiest shipping lanes. The Red Sea route is critical for oil and goods moving between Europe, Asia, and the Middle East. Saudi Arabia has not yet confirmed the full extent of the disruption, but global oil prices have already spiked on the news.

This development effectively opens a second front in what was initially a US-Iran confrontation. The Houthis have long possessed anti-ship missiles, and their ability to enforce a blockade, even partially, could strangle Saudi oil exports and draw Riyadh deeper into the conflict.

Iran's president declares 'full-scale war'

Iranian President Ibrahim Raisi addressed the nation on Sunday, stating that the country is now in a 'full-scale war' with the United States. His remarks came after the US expanded its bombing campaign to include targets in central and northern Iran, moving beyond the initial coastal focus.

Raisi accused Gulf Arab states of colluding with Washington. 'Our neighbours have opened their airspace and bases to the enemy,' he said. 'They will pay a price for this betrayal.' The comment signals a potential shift in Iran's strategy, possibly targeting US allies in the region who are hosting American forces.

Gulf neighbours under pressure

Iran has specifically hit out at the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain, both of which host US naval and air bases. Tehran has warned that these countries will not be safe from retaliation if they continue to support American operations.

For now, the Gulf states have remained publicly aligned with the US, but there are signs of unease. The blockade in the Red Sea directly threatens Saudi Arabia, while the UAE is vulnerable to Iranian missiles. Diplomatic backchannels are reportedly active, though no breakthroughs have been announced.

The US has not indicated when the bombing campaign might end. Military analysts suggest that Washington is trying to force Iran to the negotiating table, but the expanded strikes and the new naval front suggest the conflict is widening rather than narrowing.

What happens next will depend on whether the Houthi blockade holds and whether Iran retaliates directly against Gulf states. For now, the region waits for the tenth night.
