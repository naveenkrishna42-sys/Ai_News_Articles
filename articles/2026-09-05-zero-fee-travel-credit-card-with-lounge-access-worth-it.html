Zero Joining Fee, But Read the Fine Print Carefully

Zero-fee travel credit cards have become the loudest pitch in India’s premium card market. Banks use the absence of a joining or annual fee to lure first-time cardholders, but the real economics sit in the reward caps, forex markups, and quarterly spend hurdles. A card advertising unlimited lounge access and 10% rewards on UPI transactions looks generous on the surface. The terms underneath tell a more cautious story for frequent flyers.

Most zero-fee travel cards waive the annual fee for life, yet they levy a 3.5% foreign currency markup on international spends. A traveller spending Rs. 4 lakh overseas will quietly absorb Rs. 14,000 in markup charges, which can erase the value of lounge visits and reward points combined.

Unlimited Lounge Access: The Real Conditions

Unlimited domestic lounge access is rarely unconditional. Banks typically require a minimum quarterly spend threshold ranging between Rs. 20,000 and Rs. 50,000. International lounge access, where available, is capped at 2 to 6 visits per year and tied to premium visa variants like Visa Signature or Mastercard World.

For a regular domestic traveller taking four to six flights annually, the lounge benefit alone can justify the card. For someone flying once a year, the reward structure and fuel surcharge waiver deliver more tangible savings than lounge access ever will.

10% UPI Rewards: Genuine Cashback or Marketing Hook?

The 10% UPI reward is the headline grabber, but it carries a strict monthly cap. Most banks limit the accelerated reward to Rs. 500 or Rs. 1,000 per month, reverting to a base 1% rate after that. For a user spending Rs. 15,000 monthly on UPI, the effective return drops from a tempting 10% to a realistic 3.3%.

This makes the card highly rewarding for grocery and bill payments under Rs. 10,000 monthly, but indifferent for users who route large purchases through UPI expecting premium returns.

Who Should Apply and Who Should Skip

The card fits a specific buyer profile: a frequent domestic flyer spending Rs. 50,000 to Rs. 1 lakh per quarter, paying utility and grocery bills via UPI, and avoiding overseas travel to dodge the forex markup. For this user, the lifetime zero fee converts into an effective 2% to 4% return across spending categories.

International travellers, high-net-worth users with existing premium cards, and buyers seeking high hotel or air mile conversions should look at fee-based travel cards with structured reward ecosystems. A Rs. 10,000 annual fee unlocks tiered benefits that a zero-fee card structurally cannot match.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Lifetime zero fee eliminates the break-even anxiety for low-spend users.
  • Quarterly spend threshold for lounge access is the single biggest deal-breaker for casual cardholders.
  • 3.5% forex markup makes international spending the weakest feature of this card.
  • UPI reward cap drastically reduces the advertised 10% return for high-volume users.
  • Welcome benefit voucher usually expires within 60 to 90 days of card issuance.
  • Reward point expiry ranges from 2 to 3 years, which demands active redemption cycles.
