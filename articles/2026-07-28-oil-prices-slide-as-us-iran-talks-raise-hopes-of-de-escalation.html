Oil prices slide as US-Iran talks raise hopes of de-escalation

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil prices dropped 6% amid US-Iran talks for de-escalation
  • Stocks drift as Mideast tensions cool, crude hits one-week low
  • Strait of Hormuz remains a key uncertainty for future supply

Oil prices tumble as diplomatic talks gain pace

Crude oil prices extended their slide on Tuesday, falling to a one-week low as fresh negotiations between the United States and Iran raised hopes of a lasting de-escalation in the Middle East. The talks, confirmed by former President Donald Trump, are aimed at ending the prolonged conflict that has rattled global energy markets for months.

Brent crude futures dropped over 6% in early trading, while West Texas Intermediate followed a similar trajectory. The decline marks the sharpest single-day fall in weeks, driven by growing optimism that supply disruptions linked to the conflict may soon ease.

Market sentiment shifts amid cooling tensions

Stock markets on Wall Street and across Asia drifted lower but remained relatively stable, as the focus shifted from geopolitical risk to economic fundamentals. Analysts noted that the drop in oil prices provided a tailwind for consumer-facing sectors, though broader uncertainty persisted.

The Times of India reported that crude prices fell to a one-week low, reflecting a broader reassessment of supply risks. Investors are now pricing in a higher probability of a diplomatic resolution, which would remove a key premium that had been baked into oil prices since the conflict escalated.

Strait of Hormuz remains the wild card

Despite the positive signals from the negotiating table, the Strait of Hormuz remains a critical flashpoint. The waterway, through which about 20% of the world's oil passes, has been a focal point of tensions throughout the conflict. Any disruption there could reverse the current price slide in hours.

OilPrice.com analysts warned that while talks are encouraging, the situation on the ground is still volatile. Iran has previously threatened to close the strait in response to military actions, and no concrete agreement has been reached on freedom of navigation.

What happens next

The coming days will be crucial. If US-Iran talks yield a framework for a ceasefire or broader peace deal, oil prices could fall further, potentially testing pre-conflict levels. However, any breakdown in negotiations or new military incidents could quickly send prices soaring again. Markets are watching Tehran and Washington closely for the next signal.
