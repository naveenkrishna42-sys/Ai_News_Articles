RBI Cancels 59 NBFC Licences, Accepts Surrender by 4, Restores Goli Finance

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI cancelled registration of 59 NBFCs for regulatory non-compliance
  • Four NBFCs' surrender requests accepted; Goli Finance licence restored
  • Actions part of RBI's ongoing scrutiny of non-banking financial sector

Regulatory Crackdown Intensifies

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has cancelled the registration of 59 non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) for failing to meet regulatory requirements. In a separate move, the central bank accepted the surrender of registration by four other entities, while restoring the licence of Goli Finance.

The cancellations and surrenders come as part of the RBI's ongoing efforts to clean up the financial sector and ensure compliance with its norms.

Why Registrations Were Cancelled

According to the RBI, the 59 NBFCs had their registrations cancelled because they were found to be non-compliant with the regulatory framework. The central bank did not specify individual reasons for each cancellation, but such actions typically follow violations of prudential norms, failure to submit returns, or engaging in activities beyond the scope of their licences.

The names of the affected companies have been published on the RBI's website. The cancellations take effect immediately, and the entities are barred from carrying on the business of a non-banking financial institution.

Surrenders Accepted

In addition to the cancellations, the RBI accepted the surrender of registration by four NBFCs. This means these companies voluntarily chose to wind down their NBFC operations, possibly due to business restructuring or inability to comply with the regulatory burden.

The RBI's acceptance of surrender is a routine process that allows entities to exit the sector in an orderly manner, provided they have no outstanding liabilities or legal issues.

Goli Finance Gets Licence Back

In a contrasting development, the RBI restored the certificate of registration of Goli Finance. The company had previously faced regulatory action, but after a review, the central bank decided to reinstate its licence.

The restoration suggests that Goli Finance addressed the issues that led to the earlier cancellation, demonstrating that the RBI is willing to give entities a second chance if they show compliance.

Impact on the Sector

The latest actions are part of a broader trend of regulatory tightening in the NBFC sector. Over the past year, the RBI has been proactive in weeding out non-serious or non-compliant players, aiming to protect depositors and maintain financial stability.

Industry experts note that while the cancellations may seem harsh, they are necessary to maintain the integrity of the financial system. Smaller NBFCs often struggle with compliance, and the RBI's strict stance serves as a warning to others to adhere to the rules.

The move also underscores the importance of timely regulatory filings and adherence to capital adequacy norms. Companies that fail to meet these standards risk losing their licences, which can have severe implications for their business operations.

What to Watch

Going forward, market participants will be watching whether the RBI continues its crackdown on non-compliant NBFCs, and whether more entities will face similar actions. The restoration of Goli Finance's licence also raises questions about the criteria for reinstatement, and whether other cancelled entities could seek similar relief.

The RBI's actions are expected to encourage greater compliance among NBFCs, and the sector could see further consolidation as smaller players either exit or merge with larger entities.
