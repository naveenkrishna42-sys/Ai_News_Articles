Sensex Sheds 180 Points as Hormuz Tensions Rattle Markets

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex declined 180 points in early trade amid Hormuz deadlock.
  • Nifty 50 slipped below key levels, tracking global cues.
  • Crude oil prices and foreign fund flows remain key watch points.

Markets Open Lower as Geopolitical Risks Weigh

Indian equity benchmarks opened in the red on Wednesday, with the BSE Sensex shedding about 180 points in early trade. The slide came as investors tracked an escalating deadlock in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments.

The Nifty 50 too struggled, hovering near the 24,400 level after opening weak. Volatility was visible across sectors, with IT stocks bearing the brunt of the selling pressure.

Hormuz Tensions Spook Global Sentiment

The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of global oil consumption passes, has emerged as the key overhang for risk assets. Any disruption in the waterway could push crude prices sharply higher, reigniting inflationary concerns worldwide.

Brent crude futures have already firmed up in response to the standoff. For India, a net importer of oil, a sustained spike in crude prices would widen the current account deficit and put pressure on the rupee.

IT Stocks Lead Declines, Broader Market Mixed

Software exporters were among the top losers in early trade, tracking weakness in US tech peers. The Nifty IT index slipped over a percent, dragging the benchmarks lower.

Meanwhile, select banking and auto stocks showed some resilience, though buying remained cautious. Midcap and smallcap indices traded mixed, reflecting a lack of clear directional conviction.

Key Factors to Watch Through the Session

Traders are keeping a close eye on crude oil prices, which remain sensitive to any fresh headlines from Hormuz. A sustained climb above recent highs could trigger further selling in domestic equities.

Foreign institutional investor flows will also be monitored. After several sessions of net selling, any reversal in FII participation could provide support to the market.

On the domestic front, the rupee's movement against the dollar and bond yields will be watched for cues on inflationary expectations.

What Lies Ahead

With the Hormuz situation still fluid, market direction is likely to remain hostage to geopolitical developments. Any signs of de-escalation could trigger a sharp rebound, while further deterioration may push benchmarks lower.

Investors are advised to stay nimble and avoid building aggressive positions until clarity emerges.
