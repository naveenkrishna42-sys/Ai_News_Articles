School Students in Hyderabad Learn About Dangers of Tobacco Use

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • School students in Hyderabad were educated on the perils of tobacco use in a special awareness drive.
  • The initiative highlighted health risks like cancer and financial strain caused by tobacco addiction.
  • Students were encouraged to spread awareness among family and friends to create a tobacco-free environment.

New Awareness Drive Targets Young Minds

A health awareness campaign recently took place in several schools across Hyderabad, focusing on the dangers of tobacco consumption. The initiative aimed to educate students about the severe health and financial consequences of tobacco use, including smoking and chewing tobacco products.

Organised by local health authorities in collaboration with educational institutions, the drive used interactive sessions and visual aids to explain how tobacco leads to diseases such as oral cancer, lung cancer, and heart problems. Officials stressed that early education is critical in preventing addiction among teenagers.

Students Pledge to Stay Away

During the programme, students were shown real-life examples of how tobacco damages the body and drains family finances. Many participants took a pledge to never start using tobacco and to discourage others from doing so.

Teachers reported that the sessions were well-received, with students asking questions about the long-term effects of passive smoking and the addictive nature of nicotine. The school management plans to make such workshops a regular feature.

Message Reaches Families Through Children

A key aspect of the campaign was urging students to act as messengers at home. Children were asked to talk to their parents and relatives about the risks of tobacco, effectively turning the classroom lesson into a community outreach effort.

Health experts believe that peer and family influence is a powerful tool in reducing tobacco use. By targeting school-going children, the programme hopes to create a ripple effect that discourages adults from smoking or chewing tobacco in front of their children.

Focus on Prevention Over Cure

The initiative aligns with national health goals to reduce tobacco consumption, particularly among youth. Data shows that most adult smokers start before the age of 18, making school-level intervention essential.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total number of students reached, but early reports suggest participation from at least a dozen schools in the city. Similar drives are being planned for other districts in Telangana.

Moving forward, authorities aim to integrate tobacco awareness into the regular school curriculum, ensuring that every new batch of students receives this critical education. The focus remains on prevention as the most effective strategy against tobacco-related diseases.
