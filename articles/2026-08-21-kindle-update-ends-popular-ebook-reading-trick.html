Amazon's Kindle update disables popular ebook reading trick

Amazon has rolled out a new Kindle update that disables a widely used method for reading ebooks at a slower pace. The update, which was pushed out to Kindle users and Libby users, aims to toughen DRM and improve the overall user experience.

The update aims to toughen DRM and improve user experience

The Kindle update, which was first reported by Boing Boing, disables a popular ebook reading trick that allowed users to read ebooks at a slower pace. The trick involved using a third-party app to adjust the font size and line spacing, which in turn slowed down the reading speed. Amazon has now locked down this loophole, which has been a favorite among Kindle users.

The update also includes hardened DRM, which is expected to make it more difficult for users to bypass the digital rights management system. This change is likely to be welcomed by publishers and authors, who have long been concerned about the ease with which ebooks can be copied and shared.

The change affects Kindle users and Libby users

The Kindle update affects both Kindle users and Libby users. Libby is a free app that allows users to borrow ebooks and audiobooks from their local library. The update, which was reported by Android Police and Android Authority, aims to make the Libby app more secure and user-friendly.

However, the update has not been without its controversies. Some users have expressed concern that the update will make it more difficult to access ebooks, especially for those with visual impairments. The update also disables a popular ebook reading trick that has been used by many Kindle users.

Kindle Oasis receives its first update in a year

The Kindle Oasis, Amazon's premium e-reader, has also received its first update in a year. The update, which was reported by AndroidPure, includes several bug fixes and performance improvements. The update also includes hardened DRM, which is expected to make it more difficult for users to bypass the digital rights management system.

The update is likely to be welcomed by Kindle Oasis users, who have been waiting for an update for some time. The update also includes several new features, such as improved battery life and faster page turns.

What happens next?

The Kindle update has sparked a debate about the future of ebooks and digital rights management. As more and more users turn to ebooks for their reading needs, publishers and authors are under increasing pressure to protect their intellectual property. The Kindle update is likely to be just the beginning of a broader conversation about the future of ebooks and DRM.
