Banks Reported Fraud of Rs 1.42 Lakh Crore in 5 Years: Finance Ministry

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Total fraud reported by banks and financial institutions in last 5 years stands at Rs 1.42 lakh crore.
  • Finance Ministry disclosed the data in a written reply in Parliament.
  • Fraud cases include loan frauds, card-related frauds, and digital banking frauds.

Massive Fraud Figures Revealed in Parliament

The Finance Ministry has informed Parliament that banks and financial institutions across India reported fraud involving a staggering Rs 1.42 lakh crore over the last five years. The data was shared in a written reply to a question in the Lok Sabha.

According to the ministry's submission, the amount covers frauds detected and reported by scheduled commercial banks as well as select financial institutions between 2021-22 and 2025-26. The numbers underscore the growing challenge of financial malfeasance in the banking sector.

Breakdown of Fraud Types

The reply did not detail a full year-wise split but indicated that loan frauds accounted for a significant portion of the total amount. Card-related frauds, digital banking frauds, and chequefrauds also contributed to the tally.

Regulatory agencies, including the Reserve Bank of India and the Central Bureau of Investigation, have been tasked with tracking and prosecuting such cases. The ministry noted that banks have been asked to strengthen their internal controls and reporting mechanisms.

Recovery and Action Taken

On the question of recovery, the Finance Ministry said banks have managed to recover a fraction of the total fraud amount. Officials did not provide exact recovery figures in the reply, but sources indicate the recovery rate remains low across most public sector banks.

The ministry also stated that thousands of bank employees and external parties have been booked in connection with these frauds. Many cases are pending trial in various courts, while others are under investigation by law enforcement agencies.

Impact on Banking Sector and Customers

The staggering figure of Rs 1.42 lakh crore has raised concerns among banking experts and consumer rights groups. They argue that such large-scale frauds erode public trust in the financial system and put pressure on bank profitability.

Banking unions have called for stricter oversight and faster prosecution of fraudsters. They also demand better training for bank staff to detect and prevent fraud at an early stage.

The Finance Ministry has assured Parliament that the government is committed to tackling financial fraud through a multi-pronged approach, including technology upgrades and legal reforms.

What Happens Next

The government is expected to introduce stricter norms for loan disbursal and digital transactions in the coming months. Banks have been directed to submit quarterly reports on fraud detection and recovery to the RBI. The public will watch closely whether these measures can bring down the alarming trend of banking fraud.
