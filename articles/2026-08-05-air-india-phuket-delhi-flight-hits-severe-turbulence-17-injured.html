Air India Phuket-Delhi Flight Hits Severe Turbulence, 17 Injured

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Air India flight AI2379 encountered severe turbulence, dropping 300 feet mid-air.
  • 17 people injured, including 13 passengers and 4 crew members.
  • Passengers described chaotic scenes and fear for their lives during the incident.
  • Air India said it is providing assistance to affected individuals; a probe has been launched.

An Air India flight from Phuket to Delhi turned into a nightmare for passengers on Tuesday after the aircraft encountered severe turbulence, plunging 300 feet in a matter of seconds. The sudden drop left 17 people injured—13 passengers and four crew members—and triggered a formal investigation into the incident.

Passengers Recount Terrifying Moments

Passengers aboard flight AI2379 described scenes of chaos and sheer terror as the Airbus lost altitude without warning. One passenger, still visibly shaken after landing, said, "We thought we would die." Another called the experience "a rebirth for all of us," capturing the collective relief felt by those on board when the aircraft finally stabilised.

The cabin, which had been calm moments before, became a blur of screams and flying objects. Unsecured items, meal trays, and personal belongings were tossed around as the plane lurched downward. Several passengers who were not wearing seatbelts were thrown from their seats, leading to injuries ranging from minor bruises to more serious trauma.

Air India Responds, Assistance Underway

In an official statement, Air India confirmed the injuries and said it was providing medical assistance to those affected. "13 passengers and 4 crew members have been injured. We are providing assistance to individuals," the airline said, adding that the aircraft landed safely at Delhi airport and emergency medical teams were on standby to receive the injured.

The airline did not specify the nature or severity of the injuries, but officials on the ground indicated that some passengers were taken to nearby hospitals for further evaluation. Air India has also arranged for counselling and support services for passengers who experienced trauma during the flight.

Probe Launched Into Sudden Altitude Loss

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has ordered a probe into the incident, focusing on why the aircraft lost 300 feet so abruptly. Preliminary reports suggest the turbulence was likely clear-air turbulence, which is notoriously difficult to predict and can strike without warning. However, investigators will examine flight data, weather reports, and crew actions to determine the exact sequence of events.

Aviation experts note that while turbulence-related injuries are not uncommon, a drop of this magnitude is serious and warrants a thorough investigation. The incident also raises questions about seatbelt usage during flights, as most of the injured were reportedly not wearing seatbelts at the time of the drop.

Safety Protocols Under Scrutiny

The incident has reignited discussions about in-flight safety protocols, particularly during meal service and cabin crew movement. The crew members who were injured were likely on their feet when the turbulence hit, a scenario that airlines train for but cannot always prevent.

Air India has said it will cooperate fully with the investigation and review its operational procedures. The airline also urged passengers to keep seatbelts fastened whenever seated, even when the seatbelt sign is off, as a precautionary measure.

For now, the focus remains on the recovery of the injured and understanding what went wrong. As investigators sift through data, the aviation community will be watching closely for any systemic issues that need addressing.

The probe is expected to release its findings in the coming weeks, and Air India has promised to implement any recommended safety measures immediately. For the passengers of AI2379, the memory of that terrifying plunge is unlikely to fade soon, but for now, they are safe on the ground.
