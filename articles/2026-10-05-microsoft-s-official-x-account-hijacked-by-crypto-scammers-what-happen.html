Microsoft’s Official X Account Hijacked by Crypto Scammers – What Happened and How to Stay Safe

  • The hijack led to tens of thousands of impressions before the account was secured, highlighting risks even for verified brand profiles.

Incident Overview

On Friday, 2 October 2026, the official Microsoft account on X (formerly Twitter) was taken over by unknown actors who posted a series of messages advertising a fraudulent cryptocurrency giveaway. The posts claimed that users could send a small amount of Bitcoin or Ethereum to a specified wallet address and receive a multiplied return, a classic advance‑fee scam tactic. The account, verified with the blue checkmark and boasting more than 20 million followers, remained under the attackers’ control for approximately two hours before Microsoft’s security team regained access.

How the Hijack Occurred

SecurityWeek reported that the breach likely stemmed from compromised credentials associated with a third‑party social‑media management tool authorized to post on Microsoft’s behalf. Attackers may have obtained session tokens or passwords through phishing or credential‑stuffing attacks targeting employees or contractors with access to the account. Once inside, the scammers removed legitimate posts and replaced them with the deceptive giveaway content, leveraging the account’s credibility to reach a broad audience quickly.

Impact on Users and Platform

Although the fraudulent tweets were removed swiftly, analytics from X indicate that the scam posts garnered tens of thousands of impressions within the short window they were live. Several users reported clicking the links and attempting to send funds, though the exact number of victims and any financial loss have not been publicly disclosed by Microsoft or law enforcement. The incident underscored that even high‑profile, verified accounts are not immune to credential‑based takeovers, and that the perceived trust associated with the blue check can be exploited for rapid scam dissemination.

Microsoft’s Response and Security Measures

Microsoft’s internal security team confirmed the breach shortly after the anomalous posts appeared. They initiated an immediate password reset, revoked all third‑party application tokens associated with the X account, and enforced multi‑factor authentication (MFA) for the accounts managing the profile. A post‑mortem review was launched to identify how the attackers gained initial access, with findings expected to inform tighter controls over social‑media management tools and employee credential hygiene.

Steps Taken to Secure the Account

  • Reset of the primary password and all associated service accounts.
  • Revocation of OAuth tokens and API keys for any external scheduling or analytics platforms.
  • Enforcement of MFA and conditional access policies for admin logins.
  • Increased monitoring for anomalous login locations and activity spikes.
  • Communication to followers advising them to disregard the fraudulent posts and report any similar scams.

What Users Should Do

Individuals who encountered the scam posts should treat any request to send cryptocurrency to an unknown address as highly suspicious. Legitimate companies, including Microsoft, do not conduct giveaways that require users to transfer funds first. Users are advised to:

  • Verify the authenticity of announcements through official channels such as the company’s website or verified press releases.
  • Enable MFA on all personal and professional accounts, especially those linked to social‑media management.
  • Regularly review authorized applications and revoke any that are no longer needed or appear unfamiliar.
  • Report suspicious posts directly to the platform using the built‑in reporting tools.

Best Practices to Avoid Crypto Scams

Beyond this specific incident, the broader crypto‑scam ecosystem relies on urgency, impersonation, and the promise of easy returns. Staying skeptical of unsolicited offers, double‑checking wallet addresses, and using hardware wallets for large holdings can reduce risk. Educating colleagues and friends about common tactics — such as fake giveaways, impersonated support accounts, and phishing links — helps create a more resilient community against these evolving threats.
