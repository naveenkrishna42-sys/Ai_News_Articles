CWG 2026: Lovepreet wins silver in weightlifting, Seema takes discus bronze

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Lovepreet Singh wins silver medal in weightlifting at CWG 2026
  • Seema Kaliramna takes bronze in discus throw, taking India's tally to 17 medals
  • Neeraj Chopra books spot in javelin throw final on Day 8

Lovepreet's grit pays off

India's medal rush at the Commonwealth Games 2026 continued on Day 8 as Lovepreet Singh delivered a fighting performance to clinch silver in weightlifting. The lifter, competing in the men's 109kg category, produced a series of steady lifts under pressure to secure the runner-up spot.

Lovepreet's effort was marked by composure in the clean and jerk, where he overcame early jitters to post a total that pushed him ahead of the competition. The gold went to Liti of New Zealand, who edged out the Indian with a commanding display of strength and technique.

This is Lovepreet's first Commonwealth Games medal, and it adds to a growing weightlifting contingent that has consistently delivered for India at the multi-sport event. His silver follows a string of podium finishes by Indian lifters in Birmingham and now in this edition.

Seema shines in discus throw

Later in the day, Seema Kaliramna added a bronze in the women's discus throw, taking India's overall medal tally to 17. Seema, who has been a consistent performer on the international circuit, produced a best throw that was enough to secure third place.

The discus final saw a tight contest, with the top three separated by just a few metres. Seema's opening rounds set the tone, and she maintained her position through the later attempts despite stiff competition from Commonwealth rivals.

Her bronze is significant not just for the medal count but for the depth it signals in Indian athletics. With multiple throwers now competing at the highest level, the country's track and field programme is showing clear signs of progress.

Neeraj books javelin final spot

In another highlight, Neeraj Chopra confirmed his place in the men's javelin throw final with a confident qualification round. The Olympic champion, who is competing at his second Commonwealth Games, did not need to push beyond his early throws to seal a top-eight finish.

Neeraj's performance in qualification was measured, with his opening throw comfortably crossing the automatic qualifying mark. Two other Indian athletes also made it to the final, raising hopes of a strong showing in the medal round.

The javelin final is expected to be one of the most watched events of the Games, with Neeraj facing stiff competition from athletes from Grenada, Pakistan and England. His form in the lead-up has been solid, and the Indian camp will be optimistic about a podium finish.

India's day of mixed fortunes

While the medal winners dominated the headlines, Day 8 also had its share of close calls. In hockey, the Indian men's team secured a spot in the semifinals, while the women's team suffered a narrow defeat in their group-stage match.

Boxing saw two Indian pugilists advance to the quarterfinals, while badminton doubles pairs continued their winning run. However, there were disappointments in shooting, where a couple of finalists missed out on medals by narrow margins.

With the Games entering its final stretch, India's total of 17 medals includes five golds, six silvers and six bronzes. The athletics track events, along with boxing and wrestling, are expected to add more to the tally in the coming days.

Looking ahead

All eyes will now be on the javelin final, where Neeraj Chopra will aim to add a Commonwealth gold to his collection. The Indian contingent will also be watching the boxing ring and the wrestling mat, where several medal hopefuls are scheduled to compete.

With three days of competition remaining, India's target of surpassing its previous best overseas haul remains within reach. The athletes have shown resilience throughout the Games, and the final push could well define their legacy here.
