Cyclosporiasis Spreads in US: Steps to Protect Your Family

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cyclosporiasis cases are increasing across multiple U.S. states.
  • The parasitic infection spreads through contaminated food and water.
  • Simple hygiene and food-safety measures can significantly reduce risk.

What Is Cyclosporiasis and Why Is It Spreading?

Health officials are reporting a rise in cyclosporiasis cases across the United States. This intestinal infection, caused by the microscopic parasite Cyclospora cayetanensis, leads to prolonged diarrhea, cramping, and fatigue. Outbreaks typically spike during warmer months, often linked to imported fresh produce.

The current spread has put families on alert, especially those who consume raw fruits and vegetables. While most healthy individuals recover without treatment, the illness can be severe for young children, the elderly, or those with weakened immune systems.

How the Parasite Reaches Your Plate

Cyclospora is not spread directly from person to person. Instead, people ingest the parasite through contaminated food or water. Fresh herbs, berries, and leafy greens have been common culprits in past outbreaks, particularly when grown in regions with poor sanitation.

The parasite can survive on produce even after washing, as it clings to surfaces in a dormant form. This makes prevention tricky, but not impossible, with the right handling techniques.

Symptoms to Watch For

Infection symptoms usually appear about a week after exposure. They include watery diarrhea, loss of appetite, weight loss, bloating, and stomach cramps. Some people also experience nausea, vomiting, or low-grade fever.

Because symptoms mimic other gastrointestinal illnesses, cyclosporiasis is often underdiagnosed. If diarrhea persists beyond a few days, especially after travel or eating imported produce, seeking medical advice is crucial.

Practical Prevention Steps for Your Kitchen

Thoroughly washing produce under running water is your first line of defense—even if you plan to peel it. Scrubbing firm-skinned fruits and vegetables with a brush can dislodge any lurking parasites. Avoid using the same cutting board for raw meats and fresh produce without washing it in between.

When dining out, choose cooked dishes over raw salads in high-risk settings. If you drink untreated water from streams or wells, boil it first. These small habits significantly cut the odds of infection.

What to Do If You Suspect Infection

If you or a family member develop prolonged diarrhea, consult a doctor promptly. A simple stool test can confirm cyclosporiasis, and antibiotics are available to treat it. Stay hydrated with oral rehydration solutions, as dehydration is the primary complication.

Rest and proper nutrition help recovery, but do not self-medicate with anti-diarrheal drugs without medical guidance. They can prolong the infection in some cases.

The Road Ahead

As investigations continue into the source of the current outbreak, health agencies are tracking cases to identify contaminated batches. For now, vigilance in the kitchen and prompt medical attention remain the best safeguards. Expect updates from the CDC and FDA as they trace the outbreak and issue further guidance.
