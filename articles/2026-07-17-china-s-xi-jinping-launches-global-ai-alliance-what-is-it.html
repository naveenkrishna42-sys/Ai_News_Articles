China's Xi Jinping launches global AI alliance: What is it?

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Xi Jinping launched the 'Global AI Alliance' at the World AI Conference in Shanghai.
  • The alliance aims to promote international cooperation in AI development and governance.
  • Xi contrasted China's approach with US-led efforts, calling AI a 'symphony of global collaboration'.

Xi Jinping announces new global AI initiative

Chinese President Xi Jinping has launched a new international artificial intelligence alliance, positioning Beijing as a leader in shaping global AI governance. The announcement was made at the World AI Conference (WAIC) in Shanghai on Friday.

The initiative, called the 'Global AI Alliance', aims to foster international cooperation in AI development and standards. Xi described it as an effort to ensure AI benefits all nations, not just a select few.

‘A symphony, not a solo performance’

In his keynote address, Xi stressed that AI should not be a zero-sum game. 'AI should not be a solo performance, but a symphony of global collaboration,' he said, according to state media.

The Chinese leader's remarks come amid intensifying competition with the United States in the AI sector. Xi's rhetoric directly challenged the American approach, which has focused on US-led safety agencies and export controls on advanced chips.

Countering US-led AI governance

The alliance is seen as a direct response to recent moves by Washington. Earlier this month, Google AI CEO Demis Hassabis pushed for a US-led international AI safety agency, arguing that 'AI systems must be subject to rigorous oversight.'

Beijing has consistently opposed what it calls 'tech decoupling' by the US. The Global AI Alliance is expected to include countries from the Global South, including many that are part of China's Belt and Road Initiative. Details on membership criteria and governance structure have not yet been released.

China opens its AI gains to the world

State broadcaster CGTN reported that the alliance would facilitate sharing of AI research, datasets, and computing resources. 'China opens up AI gains to the world,' the network said, framing the initiative as a public good.

Chinese tech giants like Baidu, Alibaba, and Tencent are expected to play key roles. The alliance may also include academic institutions and think tanks from partner countries. However, Western nations, particularly the US and its allies, are unlikely to join given the current geopolitical tensions.

Skepticism from the West

Analysts in Washington have expressed caution, noting that China's AI push could serve to export its model of state-led surveillance and social control. The US National Security Council has not yet commented on the announcement.

European officials, speaking on condition of anonymity, said they would study the proposal but remain wary of data security risks. 'We need to see the fine print,' one diplomat told TIVRA News.

What happens next

The Global AI Alliance is set to hold its first working group meeting in Beijing later this year. Member nations will begin drafting shared principles on AI safety, ethics, and transparency. Whether the alliance can attract major economies beyond China's immediate sphere of influence remains the key question.
