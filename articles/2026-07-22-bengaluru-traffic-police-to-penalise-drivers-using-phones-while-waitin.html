Bengaluru traffic police to penalise drivers using phones while waiting at signals

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Drivers caught using phones at traffic signals will face fines under the Motor Vehicles Act.
  • The move aims to curb distracted driving, a leading cause of accidents in Bengaluru.
  • Police will deploy additional personnel and CCTV monitoring for enforcement.

New traffic rule targets phone use at signals

Bengaluru's traffic police have announced a stricter crackdown on drivers using mobile phones while behind the wheel, including when waiting at traffic signals. The move comes as part of a broader effort to reduce accidents caused by distracted driving.

Officials say that many drivers mistakenly believe it is safe to use phones at red lights. However, police argue that this habit leads to delayed reactions when the signal turns green, often causing rear-end collisions and confusion at intersections.

Fine and enforcement details

Under the Motor Vehicles Act, using a handheld mobile phone while driving attracts a fine of Rs 5,000 for a first offence. Repeat offenders could face higher penalties or even suspension of their driving licence.

Traffic police will deploy additional personnel at key junctions and use CCTV cameras to catch violators. The enforcement will be particularly strict during peak hours and at accident-prone locations across the city.

Accident data shows need for action

According to police records, distracted driving—primarily due to mobile phone use—has contributed to a significant number of road accidents in Bengaluru over the past year. The exact figures have not been released, but officials indicate the trend is alarming.

“We have seen an increase in cases where drivers are not paying full attention to the road,” a senior traffic police officer said. “This rule is not about collecting fines; it is about saving lives.”

Public reaction and advisories

The announcement has drawn mixed reactions from Bengaluru's commuters. Some have welcomed the move, pointing out that phone use at signals often leads to delays and near-misses. Others have expressed concerns about the practicality of enforcement, especially in heavy traffic.

Police have advised drivers to pull over to a safe spot if they need to use their phones. Hands-free devices are permitted under the law, but officers caution that even these can be distracting if not used responsibly.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the new enforcement will be extended to other parts of Karnataka. For now, the focus remains on Bengaluru's roads, where the problem is most acute.
