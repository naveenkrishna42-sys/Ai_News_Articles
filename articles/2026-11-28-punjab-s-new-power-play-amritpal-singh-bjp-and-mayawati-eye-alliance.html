Punjab's New Power Play: Amritpal Singh, BJP, and Mayawati Eye Alliance

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-28 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Breakaway Akali Dal seeks a larger seat share than the traditional SAD.
  • BJP balances its national agenda with regional sensitivities in Punjab.
  • Mayawati's BSP could become a kingmaker, influencing Dalit vote consolidation.

Background: A Fragmented Mandate in Punjab

Punjab’s 117‑member Legislative Assembly is set for a decisive election in early 2027. The last poll saw the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP) win a commanding majority, while the traditional SAD‑BJP alliance fell to a distant third. Discontent within the Shiromani Akali Dal led Amritpal Singh to form a breakaway faction, SAD‑B, that now commands a distinct voter base, especially in rural Malwa.

Against this backdrop, both national and regional parties are scrambling to rebuild a viable opposition. The stakes are high: a cohesive coalition could prevent AAP’s continued dominance and restore a more balanced power structure.

Key Players and Their Stakes

1. Amritpal Singh’s Breakaway Akali Dal (SAD‑B)

SAD‑B, under Amritpal Singh, commands roughly 12‑15% of the vote share in its strongholds, translating to an estimated 15‑18 seats based on past trends. The faction demands a minimum of 30 seats in any seat‑sharing formula, arguing that its grassroots network can swing swing constituencies.

Singh’s political narrative blends traditional Sikh concerns with a youthful, development‑focused agenda, attracting first‑time voters disillusioned with the old guard.

2. Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)

The BJP, despite its limited footprint in Punjab (about 5% vote share in 2022), views an alliance as essential to retain relevance. Its primary objective is to secure a foothold in the state’s industrial districts while safeguarding its national agenda of economic reforms.

In negotiations, the BJP has offered a modest 10‑seat allocation, hoping to leverage its central government resources to win over the urban electorate.

3. Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP) – Mayawati’s Potential Entry

Mayawati’s BSP, traditionally strong among Dalit voters, recorded a 7% vote share in the last Punjab poll. While the party has not formally entered talks, senior BSP strategists have hinted at a “conditional partnership” if the coalition can guarantee at least 8 seats for Dalit‑dominant constituencies.

A BSP inclusion could reshape the caste calculus, making the alliance more palatable to marginalized communities.

The remaining 64 seats would be contested by AAP, Congress, and other minor outfits. The alliance’s success hinges on whether the combined 53 seats can secure a plurality against AAP’s projected 55‑60 seats.

Implications for Voters and Governance

For the electorate, the emerging coalition promises a clearer alternative to AAP’s single‑party rule. Voters in Malwa may benefit from a stronger Akali voice, while Dalit communities could see amplified representation if the BSP joins.

However, the alliance also raises concerns about policy coherence. The BJP’s market‑oriented agenda may clash with SAD‑B’s agrarian priorities, and BSP’s social justice platform could demand concessions on land‑reform bills.

Should the coalition win, Punjab could witness a coalition government that must negotiate internal policy differences, potentially leading to slower legislative action but broader consensus‑building.

Pros & Cons of the Emerging Coalition

  • Pros: Consolidates anti‑AAP votes; expands Dalit and Sikh representation; leverages BJP’s central resources for development projects.
  • Cons: Ideological mismatches may cause internal friction; seat‑sharing disputes could alienate core supporters; reliance on BSP’s entry adds uncertainty.
