4 Indian Sailors Killed in Missile Attack on Cargo Ship Near Odesa

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Four Indian sailors died, one is critical after a missile hit their ship near Odesa.
  • The attack targeted a cargo vessel leaving Ukraine's Black Sea port, officials said.
  • MEA condemned the strike and is coordinating with Ukraine for repatriation.

Four Indian nationals were killed and one is in critical condition after a Russian missile struck a cargo ship near the port of Odesa in Ukraine, the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) confirmed on Monday.

The attack occurred as the vessel was departing the Black Sea port, a key hub for grain exports. Officials said the strike is part of a recent intensification of Russian attacks on Odesa's maritime infrastructure.

What happened at the Odesa port

The missile hit the cargo ship shortly after it left the harbour. Five Indian crew members were on board. Four died on the spot. The fifth sailor was severely wounded and rushed to a local hospital in Odesa, where doctors describe his condition as critical.

Ukraine's port authorities said the vessel was carrying commercial cargo. They have not disclosed the ship's flag or ownership. The Indian embassy in Kyiv is in touch with Ukrainian officials to verify the details.

Russian military spokespersons have not commented on the specific strike. However, Moscow has repeatedly targeted Odesa's port facilities since it pulled out of the Black Sea Grain Initiative in 2023.

India's response

The MEA issued a statement expressing deep shock and sorrow over the deaths. Officials said the Indian ambassador to Ukraine is personally monitoring the situation. The embassy is working to facilitate the repatriation of the bodies and provide consular assistance to the injured sailor's family.

New Delhi has not directly named Russia in its official statement but called for the safety of all merchant sailors in conflict zones. India has maintained a neutral position on the Ukraine war, urging dialogue and diplomacy.

This is not the first time Indians have been caught in the crossfire of the Black Sea conflict. In 2024, a Ukrainian missile struck a cargo ship in the same region, injuring two Indian crew members.

Why Odesa is under renewed attack

Russia has stepped up missile and drone strikes on Odesa in recent weeks, targeting port infrastructure, grain storage, and ships. Military analysts say Moscow aims to cripple Ukraine's export capacity and pressure Kyiv into concessions.

The Port of Odesa remains critical for Ukraine's economy. It handles millions of tonnes of grain, sunflower oil, and other goods every month. Despite the risks, merchant vessels continue to operate through a narrow corridor established by Ukraine's navy.

Shipping industry bodies have called for safe passage guarantees. They warn that indiscriminate attacks on civilian vessels violate international maritime law. The United Nations has not yet issued a statement on Monday's strike.

Indian maritime unions are demanding that the government secure better protections for Indian seafarers working in warzones. Over 20,000 Indian sailors are employed on international cargo ships, many transiting the Black Sea.

What happens next will depend on the injured sailor's recovery and the diplomatic fallout. The Indian embassy in Kyiv is expected to release a more detailed report in the coming hours. Families of the deceased are being notified.
