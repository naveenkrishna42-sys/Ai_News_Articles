Bengaluru puts its own spin on global beauty trends

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru's beauty consumers blend global trends with local needs
  • Independent brands are driving the shift toward sustainable, inclusive products
  • Experts say the city's tech-savvy population fuels rapid trend adoption

A city that remixes the beauty rulebook

Bengaluru's beauty aisles look nothing like they did five years ago. Global trends such as Korean skincare, glass skin, and bold 'baddie' makeup now sit alongside traditional ingredients like turmeric and sandalwood. But this isn't a simple transplant of international styles. The city's consumers are actively editing, blending, and reinterpreting these trends to suit their own preferences, lifestyles, and climate.

What emerges is a distinct Bengaluru aesthetic — one that is experimental yet practical, global yet rooted. Local salons and independent brands are leading this charge, offering products and services that would be familiar to a Seoul or New York customer, but with a local twist.

Local brands, global playbook

Independent beauty labels in the city are cashing in on this hybrid demand. They pair international formulations with locally sourced botanicals, often highlighting sustainability and transparency. Packaging is minimal, ingredient lists are clean, and many products are vegan or cruelty-free — features that appeal to Bengaluru's environmentally conscious shoppers.

These brands are also rethinking inclusivity. Shade ranges now cater to deeper Indian skin tones, and marketing campaigns feature models who look like the city's diverse population. This is a marked shift from the uniform beauty standards that dominated Indian advertising for decades.

Tech-savvy consumers drive change

Bengaluru's reputation as India's tech hub plays a role in how quickly trends catch on. Consumers here research products online, compare reviews, and follow international influencers before making a purchase. This digital fluency means trends from TikTok or Instagram reach Bengaluru within days, not months.

However, adoption is not blind. The same consumers are quick to reject products that don't work in Bengaluru's humid climate or that clash with their daily routines. As one local beauty entrepreneur put it, the city's approach is 'try everything, keep what works'.

The 'good, bad and baddie' spectrum

The title of a recent trend report sums up the city's attitude: 'good, bad and baddie'. The 'good' refers to clean, sustainable beauty. The 'bad' is the playful indulgence in bold, high-impact makeup. And the 'baddie' is the confident, unapologetic aesthetic popularised by social media.

All three coexist in Bengaluru. A single consumer might use a minimalist skincare routine in the morning, then experiment with graphic eyeliner for an evening out. This fluidity is what sets the city apart from more conservative markets.

What to watch next

As global beauty giants expand into India, they are taking notes from Bengaluru's indie labels. Expect more multinational brands to launch localised product lines, and watch for Bengaluru-based brands to scale nationally. The city's beauty story is still being written, but its signature mix of innovation and practicality is likely to influence the rest of India for years to come.
