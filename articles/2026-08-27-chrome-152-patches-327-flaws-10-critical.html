Chrome 152 Patches 327 Flaws, 10 Critical

📅 2026-08-27 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chrome 152 fixes 327 vulnerabilities, including 10 critical bugs.
  • The update addresses security flaws in Chrome's WebRTC, PDF viewer, and other components.
  • Google's security team patched several zero-day vulnerabilities, some of which were actively exploited.

Google Rolls Out Chrome 152 With Major Security Update

Google has released Chrome 152, the latest version of its popular web browser, which includes fixes for 327 security vulnerabilities. Among these, 10 are classified as critical, highlighting the ongoing efforts to secure the browser from potential threats.

Addressing Critical Vulnerabilities

The update addresses a range of security issues, including flaws in Chrome's WebRTC (Web Real-Time Communication) component, which could have been exploited to compromise user privacy. Additionally, vulnerabilities in the browser's PDF viewer and other components have been patched to prevent potential exploits.

Zero-Day Vulnerabilities Patched

Google's security team has patched several zero-day vulnerabilities, some of which were actively exploited in the wild. Zero-day vulnerabilities are particularly dangerous because they are unknown to the software vendor and often exploited before a fix is available. By addressing these issues promptly, Google has taken a proactive stance to protect its users.

Broader Implications for Browser Security

The release of Chrome 152 underscores the importance of regular updates in maintaining browser security. With cyber threats evolving rapidly, it is crucial for users to keep their browsers up to date to protect against the latest exploits. This update serves as a reminder for users to enable automatic updates or manually check for updates to ensure their browsers are secure.

User Recommendations

Users are advised to update their browsers to the latest version to benefit from the security enhancements. Additionally, it is recommended to use strong, unique passwords and enable two-factor authentication to add an extra layer of security. Regularly reviewing privacy settings and clearing browsing data can also help mitigate risks associated with potential vulnerabilities.
