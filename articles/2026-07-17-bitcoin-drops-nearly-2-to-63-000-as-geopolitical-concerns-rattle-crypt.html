Bitcoin Drops Nearly 2% to $63,000 as Geopolitical Concerns Rattle Crypto Markets

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin slid nearly 2% to $63,000, its lowest level in weeks.
  • Geopolitical tensions, particularly in Eastern Europe and the Middle East, are driving risk aversion.
  • Altcoins including Ethereum and Solana also declined, mirroring Bitcoin's losses.

Bitcoin dropped nearly 2% to $63,000 on Friday, extending its recent slide as escalating geopolitical tensions pushed investors away from risky assets. The world's largest cryptocurrency has now lost over 8% in the past week, with traders pointing to uncertainty stemming from conflicts in Eastern Europe and the Middle East.

The decline came amid a broader sell-off in digital assets. Ethereum fell 1.5% to $3,400, while Solana slipped 2.2% to $145. The total cryptocurrency market cap shrank by roughly $30 billion over the past 24 hours, according to data from CoinGecko.

Geopolitical Jitters Dominate Sentiment

Investors have been on edge after reports of fresh military posturing near the Ukraine-Russia border and renewed hostilities between Israel and Hezbollah. These developments have prompted a flight to safe-haven assets like gold and US Treasuries, leaving cryptocurrencies vulnerable.

Bitcoin's correlation with traditional risk assets such as equities has been evident this week. The S&P 500 also dipped 0.3% on Friday, while gold climbed 0.8% to $2,450 per ounce.

“Crypto markets are not immune to macro shocks,” said a Mumbai-based analyst, speaking on condition of anonymity. “When global uncertainty spikes, traders tend to liquidate volatile positions first.”

Liquidations and Leverage

The sell-off triggered a wave of forced selling. Data from Coinglass showed over $200 million in long positions were liquidated across exchanges in the last 24 hours. Bitcoin accounted for nearly $80 million of that figure.

Open interest in Bitcoin futures also dropped by 3%, suggesting that leveraged traders are unwinding bets. The funding rate for perpetual contracts turned negative for the first time this month, indicating bearish sentiment among short-term speculators.

Despite the pressure, some analysts view the correction as a healthy pullback. “Bitcoin was overbought after its rally to $70,000 earlier this month. A reset is normal,” said a Delhi-based fund manager.

Regulatory and Macro Headwinds

The geopolitical unrest comes alongside regulatory uncertainty. India's crypto tax regime, which imposes a 30% levy on gains, continues to dampen retail participation. Market participants are also watching the US Federal Reserve's next move. Minutes from the Fed's June meeting, released earlier this week, signalled that rate cuts may not come until late 2026, keeping borrowing costs high.

High interest rates typically reduce appetite for speculative assets like cryptocurrencies. Bitcoin's drop to $63,000 has put the $60,000 support level in focus. A break below that could trigger further declines, traders warn.

On-chain data shows that long-term holders have not rushed to sell. The number of Bitcoin wallets holding over 1 BTC has remained steady at around 1.1 million, suggesting that panic is not yet spreading among seasoned investors.

What Lies Ahead

Next week, all eyes will be on the Federal Reserve's interest rate decision and any diplomatic breakthroughs in conflict zones. A dovish Fed or de-escalation could revive risk appetite, but for now, the mood remains cautious.

Bitcoin's ability to hold above $62,000 in the coming days will likely determine whether the correction deepens or stabilises. Traders are advised to watch for volatility spikes around key economic data releases.
