Sonu Nigam reflects on Jaan-E-Mann's failure, praises cast performances

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sonu Nigam calls Jaan-E-Mann a good film that failed at the box office.
  • He says sometimes 'faltu' films become big hits while quality cinema is ignored.
  • Nigam praises the acting of Salman Khan, Akshay Kumar, and Preity Zinta in the film.

Singer Sonu Nigam has opened up about the commercial failure of the 2006 film Jaan-E-Mann, expressing his disappointment over how a well-made movie was ignored by audiences. In a recent conversation, Nigam reflected on the film's box office debacle and did not hold back in his assessment of the industry's unpredictable nature.

The film, directed by Shirish Kunder, starred Salman Khan, Akshay Kumar, and Preity Zinta in lead roles. Despite its ensemble cast and a soundtrack that became popular, Jaan-E-Mann failed to perform well commercially. Nigam, who lent his voice to several songs in the film, has now shared his thoughts on why it did not resonate with viewers at the time.

'Such a good film fails'

Nigam expressed his bewilderment at the film's reception, saying that sometimes 'faltu' (worthless) films go on to become big hits while a genuinely good film fails to find an audience. He did not name any specific films that succeeded, but his comments point to a broader trend in Bollywood where box office outcomes often defy logic.

The singer's remarks come nearly two decades after the film's release, yet his frustration appears undiminished. He noted that the film had all the elements of a successful entertainer, but the audience stayed away, leaving the team baffled.

Praise for the cast

Nigam was particularly effusive in his praise for the lead actors. He said, "Salman Khan, Akshay Kumar, Preity Zinta ne kitni acchi acting ki thi" (Salman, Akshay, and Preity acted so well). His words serve as a reminder of the performances that were part of the film, which he believes were overshadowed by its commercial failure.

The film's music, composed by Kunder himself, was among its highlights, with songs like 'Jaan-E-Mann' and 'Saudebazi' becoming chartbusters. Nigam's renditions were widely appreciated, but the film's poor box office performance meant that the music's success did not translate into ticket sales.

The unpredictability of Bollywood

Nigam's comments highlight the inherent unpredictability of the film industry, where a film's success is never guaranteed despite its pedigree. Jaan-E-Mann had a strong production team, a popular cast, and a memorable soundtrack, yet it could not overcome the audience's indifference.

This is not the first time a well-crafted film has failed at the box office, and Nigam's candid remarks add to the ongoing conversation about what makes a film succeed or fail. His observation that 'faltu' films often become hits while good films are ignored is a sentiment shared by many in the industry, though rarely expressed so bluntly.

What lies ahead

As Nigam continues to reflect on the film's legacy, his comments may reignite interest in Jaan-E-Mann among fans who missed it in theaters. The film has since gained a cult following on streaming platforms, and its music remains a favourite among listeners. Whether this renewed attention will change its fortunes remains to be seen, but for Nigam, the film's place in his heart is secure.

For now, the singer's words serve as a tribute to a film that, in his view, deserved better than what it got. His candid assessment is a reminder that in Bollywood, success is often a matter of timing, luck, and audience mood—factors that no amount of talent can guarantee.
