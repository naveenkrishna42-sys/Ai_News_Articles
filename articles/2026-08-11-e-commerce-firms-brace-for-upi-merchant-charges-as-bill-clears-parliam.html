E-commerce firms brace for UPI merchant charges as bill clears Parliament

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Taxation Laws Amendment Bill has been passed by Parliament, potentially paving the way for UPI merchant charges.
  • Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has asserted that UPI transactions will remain free for users.
  • E-commerce companies are preparing for the financial impact of possible MDR (Merchant Discount Rate) on UPI payments.

New Bill Clears Parliament, Sparks Cost Concerns

The passage of the Taxation Laws Amendment Bill in Parliament has set the stage for a significant shift in India's digital payments ecosystem. E-commerce firms, which have long relied on the zero-cost model of UPI transactions, are now bracing for the possibility of merchant charges. The bill, which amends several provisions of the Income-tax Act, is seen as a precursor to introducing a Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on UPI payments.

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman has moved to allay fears, stating that UPI transactions will continue to remain free for users. However, the ambiguity around merchant pricing has left industry stakeholders on edge. The government's stance suggests that while consumers may not be directly charged, businesses could bear the brunt of new fees.

What the Bill Means for UPI's Zero-Cost Model

For years, UPI has been celebrated as a public good, with zero MDR for merchants and no charges for users. This model has been instrumental in driving the country's digital payment revolution, with billions of transactions processed monthly. The new legislation, however, could alter this equation by allowing payment service providers to levy charges on merchants.

The bill's passage has drawn sharp reactions from various quarters. The Indian Express, in an editorial, noted that the new Bill puts free UPI under threat and deserves debate in Parliament and discussion with citizens. Critics argue that any cost burden on merchants would eventually trickle down to consumers, undermining the very ethos of financial inclusion that UPI was built upon.

E-commerce Sector on Edge

E-commerce companies, which operate on thin margins, are particularly vulnerable to any additional transaction costs. Industry insiders say that an MDR of even 0.5% to 1% could significantly impact profitability, especially for small and medium-sized sellers who rely heavily on UPI for daily settlements.

The timing of the bill is also noteworthy. It comes at a time when digital payments are at an all-time high, with UPI clocking record volumes. Any disruption in the cost structure could have ripple effects across the economy, affecting everything from small kirana stores to large online marketplaces.

Government's Assurance vs. Industry Skepticism

Sitharaman's assurance that UPI transactions will remain free for users has been met with cautious optimism. However, industry experts point out that the devil lies in the details. The bill's provisions could allow for a tiered pricing structure, where certain transaction types or amounts attract fees, even if the basic service remains free.

The Telegraph India has also weighed in, highlighting the need for a nuanced discussion on the issue. The publication argues that while the government's intent to rationalize costs is understandable, it must not come at the expense of the country's digital payment momentum.

What Lies Ahead

As the bill awaits presidential assent, stakeholders are watching closely for the next steps. The Reserve Bank of India and the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) are expected to provide clarity on the implementation timeline and the exact nature of charges, if any.

For now, e-commerce firms are advised to prepare for multiple scenarios, including a phased rollout of MDR. The government's final guidelines will determine whether UPI remains the people's payment system or becomes another revenue stream for banks and payment companies.

In the coming weeks, all eyes will be on the finance ministry and the RBI to see how they navigate this delicate balance between fiscal prudence and digital inclusivity.
