RBI relaxes PSL rules for NRI deposit-backed loans

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has eased the priority sector lending (PSL) calculation for banks on loans that are backed by non-resident deposits. The decision, announced on Friday, is expected to provide relief to lenders who have been grappling with the complexities of classifying such advances under the priority sector head.

Priority sector lending mandates that banks allocate a certain portion of their net bank credit to sectors like agriculture, micro and small enterprises, and education. The new relaxation specifically addresses loans where the funding originates from non-resident Indian (NRI) deposits, a segment that has seen steady growth in recent years.

What the change means for banks

Under the revised norms, banks will be able to calculate their PSL targets more flexibly when the underlying funds come from NRI deposits. This is likely to reduce the burden of maintaining separate records and aligning these loans with the stringent PSL guidelines that were earlier applicable.

Bankers say the move could free up capital and improve liquidity, allowing them to deploy more resources toward productive sectors. It also aligns with the RBI's broader objective of simplifying compliance while ensuring that credit reaches underserved areas.

Context and implications

The relaxation comes at a time when banks are increasingly relying on non-resident deposits to shore up their dollar and rupee resources. NRI deposits have historically been a stable source of funding, and the central bank's latest step is seen as an attempt to encourage their usage without distorting the priority sector framework.

Analysts note that the change may particularly benefit smaller banks and cooperative lenders, which often struggle to meet PSL targets due to their limited branch networks. By easing the calculation for these loans, the RBI is effectively giving them more headroom to comply without compromising on their lending obligations.

What to watch

The RBI is expected to issue detailed guidelines in the coming weeks, clarifying the exact methodology and any transitional provisions. Banks will be watching closely to see how the revised norms interact with existing PSL certificates and reporting requirements.

For now, the move signals a pragmatic approach by the central bank—one that balances regulatory discipline with the practical realities of banking operations. How quickly lenders adapt to the new calculation will determine the real impact on credit flows in the months ahead.
