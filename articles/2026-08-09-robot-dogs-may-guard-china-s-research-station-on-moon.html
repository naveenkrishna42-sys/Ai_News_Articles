Robot dogs may guard China's research station on Moon

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Robot dogs could be used for patrol and maintenance at China's lunar station
  • The quadruped robots are being developed for extreme lunar conditions
  • China plans to establish a research station on the Moon by 2035

China is exploring the use of quadruped robots, commonly known as robot dogs, to patrol and maintain its planned research station on the Moon. The development signals a shift toward autonomous systems for long-term lunar operations.

The robots are being designed to navigate the Moon's rugged terrain, which includes craters, loose regolith, and steep slopes. Unlike wheeled rovers, these legged machines can adapt to uneven surfaces and could access areas that are otherwise difficult to reach.

Role of robot dogs on the lunar surface

According to reports, the robot dogs would primarily be tasked with security and inspection duties. They could monitor the station's exterior, check for damage from micrometeorites or temperature fluctuations, and assist in routine maintenance.

The robots may also be used to carry small payloads, such as tools or scientific instruments, between different modules of the station. This would reduce the need for astronauts to perform extravehicular activities, which carry significant risks.

Adapting to extreme lunar conditions

The Moon presents a harsh environment, with temperatures ranging from over 100°C in sunlight to below -150°C in shadow. The robot dogs would need to withstand these extremes, as well as the vacuum of space and the abrasive nature of lunar dust.

Chinese researchers are reportedly working on specialised materials and mechanisms to protect the robots' joints and sensors. The robots would also need to operate with minimal human intervention, given the communication delay between Earth and the Moon.

Part of China's broader lunar ambitions

China has outlined plans to build an international lunar research station (ILRS) in the coming years. The station is expected to be a hub for scientific experiments, resource utilisation, and possibly a staging point for deeper space missions.

The use of robot dogs fits into a wider trend of deploying autonomous systems in space. Several space agencies, including NASA, have experimented with similar concepts, but China appears to be advancing quickly in this specific application.

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for when the robot dogs might be deployed. However, reports suggest that prototypes could be tested on Earth in lunar-like environments before any actual mission.

What to watch

The next few years will be critical, as China is expected to launch several robotic missions to the Moon to test key technologies. The success of these missions will determine whether robot dogs become a regular feature of the lunar station.
