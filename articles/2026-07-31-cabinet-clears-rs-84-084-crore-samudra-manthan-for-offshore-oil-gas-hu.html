Cabinet clears Rs 84,084 crore 'Samudra Manthan' for offshore oil, gas hunt

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cabinet approves Rs 84,084 crore 'Samudra Manthan' scheme
  • Scheme aims to boost offshore oil and gas exploration and production
  • Focus on deepwater and ultra-deepwater drilling in Indian basins

The Union Cabinet on Friday gave its nod to a Rs 84,084-crore scheme named 'Samudra Manthan' aimed at accelerating oil and gas exploration and production in India's offshore basins. The approval comes at a time when the country is looking to cut its dependence on imported crude and ramp up domestic output from challenging deepwater and ultra-deepwater reservoirs.

The scheme, cleared in a meeting chaired by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, is expected to open up new frontiers in the country's eastern and western offshore regions. Officials said the initiative will provide fiscal incentives and infrastructure support to explorers, including state-run ONGC and Oil India, as well as private players willing to take on the risks of deep-sea drilling.

What 'Samudra Manthan' entails

Under the programme, the government will offer a package of incentives covering seismic surveys, drilling operations, and production enhancement activities in difficult terrains. The scheme is designed to make otherwise uneconomical deepwater fields viable by subsidising part of the exploration cost and offering a more flexible revenue-sharing model.

Industry trackers say the move could help unlock significant reserves that have remained untapped due to high technical complexity and capital intensity. The name 'Samudra Manthan'—drawn from the mythological churning of the ocean—signals the government's intent to extract value from the deep seas, much like the legend's pursuit of treasures.

Why the push for offshore output

India currently imports nearly 85% of its crude oil requirements, leaving the economy vulnerable to global price shocks. Domestic production has been stagnant for years, with ageing fields and a lack of new discoveries compounding the problem. The new scheme is part of a broader strategy to reverse that trend and achieve greater energy self-sufficiency.

Exploration in deepwater and ultra-deepwater zones has historically been slow in India, with only a handful of commercial discoveries. The government hopes that by de-risking early-stage exploration, it can attract both domestic and foreign companies with the technical know-how to operate in such demanding environments.

Industry response and expectations

Reactions from the oil and gas sector have been cautiously optimistic. Executives say the scheme's success will hinge on swift implementation and clear guidelines on how the incentives will be disbursed. Some have also called for faster environmental clearances and better data sharing from existing seismic surveys to reduce upfront costs.

Analysts note that the Rs 84,084 crore outlay is substantial, though much of it will be spent over several years. The programme is likely to be rolled out in phases, with priority given to blocks that show the most promise in terms of reserve potential. ONGC is already operating in several deepwater fields, including the Krishna-Godavari basin, and could be an early beneficiary.

Broader energy context

The cabinet approval comes amid a global push for cleaner energy, but India has made it clear that oil and gas will remain central to its energy mix for decades. The government has simultaneously been promoting renewable energy and green hydrogen, but officials argue that domestic hydrocarbon production is essential for energy security during the transition period.

Given the long gestation period for offshore projects, the first tangible results from 'Samudra Manthan' are not expected before the turn of the decade. The scheme, however, sends a strong signal to investors that India is serious about developing its offshore resources.

As the programme takes shape, the focus will be on how quickly the government can auction exploration blocks and whether global majors show interest. The coming months will also reveal the fiscal details and how the scheme interacts with existing production-sharing contracts. For now, the approval itself marks a clear shift towards a more aggressive exploration push in India's maritime zones.
