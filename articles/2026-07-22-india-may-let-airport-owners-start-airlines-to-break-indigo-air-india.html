India May Let Airport Owners Start Airlines to Break IndiGo-Air India Duopoly

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government weighs allowing airport owners to enter airline business to boost competition.
  • Reforms aim to challenge the dominant IndiGo-Air India duopoly in Indian aviation.
  • Jeet Adani indicates the airline business is not currently attractive for new entrants.

The Indian government is examining a significant shift in aviation policy. Sources indicate the Centre is considering relaxing cross-ownership norms that currently bar airport operators from running airlines. The move is part of a broader set of reforms designed to lower entry barriers and break the tight grip of the IndiGo-Air India duopoly on the domestic market.

Reforms to Spur New Entrants

For years, the aviation sector has been dominated by two major players. IndiGo and Air India together control over 80% of domestic air traffic. Smaller carriers struggle to survive. The government believes allowing infrastructure players, such as airport developers, to also operate airlines could inject fresh capital and operational expertise into the market.

The proposed reforms would amend existing rules that prevent vertical integration. An airport owner currently cannot hold a significant stake in an airline that operates from that airport. The logic was to prevent monopolistic practices. Now, the thinking has shifted. Policymakers argue that such restrictions are stifling competition and keeping new airlines from taking off.

Jeet Adani's Cautionary Note

Not everyone is convinced the move will trigger a rush of new airlines. Jeet Adani, who oversees the Adani Group's airport business, recently stated that the airline business does not make the cut for his company. His remarks highlight a key challenge: the industry is capital-intensive and notoriously low-margin. Simply clearing the regulatory path may not be enough to attract serious investment.

The Adani Group is one of the largest airport operators in the country, managing seven airports. If the rules change, it would be a natural candidate to launch an airline. Jeet Adani's public skepticism, however, suggests the group sees more value in running airports than flying planes. This reservation could be shared by other infrastructure firms.

Wider Sweep of Aviation Reforms

The potential change in cross-ownership rules is just one part of a larger reform agenda. The government is also looking at easing the process for obtaining an airline license. Current norms require a carrier to have a minimum fleet of five aircraft and a track record of two years before it can fly internationally. These requirements may be relaxed.

Another area of focus is the leasing framework. Officials are exploring ways to make it cheaper and easier for new airlines to acquire aircraft. The goal is to reduce the upfront capital burden. If these measures are implemented together, they could create a more hospitable environment for startups.

The Ministry of Civil Aviation has not issued an official statement on the timeline for these reforms. Industry insiders expect a draft policy paper to be circulated for public comment within the next quarter.

What This Means for Flyers

For passengers, more competition typically means lower fares and better service. The current duopoly has led to concerns about pricing power and reduced choices, especially on trunk routes. A new airline backed by an airport operator could offer seamless connectivity and integrated services, potentially reducing travel hassle.

However, analysts warn that the aviation business remains risky. High fuel costs and intense price wars have buried many carriers in the past. The success of any new entrant will depend on its business model, not just the regulatory freedom to enter the market.

The government's next move will be closely watched by the industry. A formal proposal is expected in the coming weeks. The real test will be whether the reforms can translate paper permissions into actual planes in the sky.
