RBI Governor Malhotra on which banks will lead in AI era

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra addressed banks on AI adoption, stressing data quality and tech investment as key differentiators.
  • He emphasized that AI should complement, not replace, human judgment in banking decisions.
  • The central bank is working on a framework to ensure responsible AI use in the financial sector.

MUMBAI: RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra on Tuesday laid out a clear picture of which Indian banks are best positioned to thrive in the artificial intelligence era. Speaking at a banking conference, he said the winners will be those that invest heavily in data infrastructure and build in-house AI capabilities, rather than merely adopting off-the-shelf tools.

Malhotra's remarks come at a time when Indian lenders are racing to deploy AI for everything from customer service chatbots to credit underwriting. The central bank, he noted, is watching these developments closely—not to stifle innovation, but to ensure stability and fairness.

Data is the new differentiator

The Governor argued that a bank's success in the AI era will hinge on the quality and governance of its data. "Banks that have cleaned up their data and invested in modern storage and processing systems will have a significant advantage," he said. Those still grappling with legacy systems and fragmented records will struggle to extract meaningful insights.

He also stressed that technology investment alone is not enough. Organisational culture, talent retention, and a willingness to experiment will separate leaders from laggards. Smaller banks, he added, could still compete by partnering with fintechs or adopting cloud-based solutions that lower entry barriers.

AI to augment, not replace, human judgment

A key theme of Malhotra's address was the need for a balanced approach. While AI can speed up processes and reduce errors, it cannot replicate the nuanced judgment required in complex lending decisions or customer grievances. "AI should be a tool for the banker, not the other way around," he said, urging lenders to keep humans in the loop.

He warned against over-reliance on algorithmic models, which can perpetuate biases if trained on flawed historical data. The RBI, he said, expects banks to have robust oversight mechanisms to detect and correct such biases before they harm customers.

Regulatory framework in the works

Malhotra revealed that the Reserve Bank is developing a comprehensive framework for responsible AI adoption in the financial sector. This framework will likely cover aspects like model risk management, transparency, and consumer protection. He did not give a timeline, but indicated that consultations with industry stakeholders would begin soon.

The Governor also touched on cybersecurity, calling it the "foundation" on which AI trust must be built. Banks that cannot secure their AI systems, he said, will face not only financial losses but also reputational damage that could erode customer confidence.

What this means for the banking sector

For India's banking industry, the message is clear: the AI race is not just about buying the newest software. It is about building a solid data backbone, nurturing talent, and ensuring ethical safeguards. Public sector banks, which have historically lagged in technology adoption, will need to accelerate their digital transformation efforts if they wish to remain competitive.

Private lenders with deep pockets and a track record of tech innovation appear better positioned. Yet, as Malhotra noted, agility and a clear strategy can trump sheer size. The coming months will likely see more banks announcing AI partnerships and internal capability-building exercises.

All eyes now turn to the RBI's upcoming framework, which is expected to set the ground rules for how AI can be used responsibly across the sector. Banks that align early with these guidelines may find themselves with a competitive edge in the long run.
