RBI Allows Urban Co-op Banks to Fund Multi-Unit Projects

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Urban cooperative banks can now finance multi-unit residential and commercial projects.
  • Move aims to increase credit flow to housing and real estate sectors.
  • Banks must comply with existing exposure and prudential norms.
  • Decision follows recommendations from a committee on urban cooperative banks.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has cleared the way for urban cooperative banks (UCBs) to fund multi-unit real estate projects. The decision, announced on Friday, marks a significant shift in the lending framework for these banks.

Until now, UCBs were largely restricted to financing single-unit housing or small-scale construction. The new directive allows them to extend credit for multi-unit residential and commercial projects, a move that could unlock fresh capital for the real estate sector.

Expanding the lending scope

The RBI's circular states that UCBs can now finance multi-unit projects, provided they adhere to the existing exposure norms and prudential guidelines. Banks must ensure that the projects meet all statutory approvals and that the borrowers have a clear title to the land.

This expansion is expected to benefit both developers and homebuyers. Developers in smaller cities and towns, where UCBs have a strong presence, may find it easier to access credit. Homebuyers could see more project launches as funding becomes available.

Why the change matters

Urban cooperative banks have traditionally played a key role in financing housing and small businesses. However, their lending was capped by regulatory restrictions that limited their ability to fund larger projects.

The RBI's decision comes after recommendations from a committee on urban cooperative banks, which argued that the restrictions were holding back the sector. The central bank has now accepted those recommendations, allowing UCBs to compete more effectively with commercial banks in the real estate lending space.

For the real estate sector, which has faced a liquidity crunch in recent years, this move could provide a much-needed boost. The National Real Estate Development Council (NAREDCO) has welcomed the decision, calling it a step in the right direction.

What remains unchanged

While the lending scope has widened, UCBs must still follow the existing rules on single-borrower exposure, group exposure, and capital adequacy. The RBI has made it clear that these norms will not be relaxed.

Banks will also have to conduct thorough due diligence on projects before sanctioning loans. The central bank has warned against any laxity in assessing project viability and borrower creditworthiness.

Analysts say that the move could increase credit flow to the housing sector, but it also carries risks. UCBs have a smaller capital base compared to commercial banks, and any spike in defaults could strain their balance sheets.

The road ahead

The RBI has directed UCBs to put in place robust risk management systems before venturing into multi-unit project financing. The central bank will monitor the implementation of the new guidelines closely.

Industry observers expect the first wave of funding to focus on mid-sized projects in tier-2 and tier-3 cities, where UCBs have a strong network. The full impact of the decision will become clearer as banks begin to roll out the new lending products in the coming months.
