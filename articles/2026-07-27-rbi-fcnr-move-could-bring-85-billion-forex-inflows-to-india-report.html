RBI FCNR move could bring $85 billion forex inflows to India: Report

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's FCNR policy change expected to attract up to $85 billion in forex inflows.
  • The move aims to bolster India's foreign exchange reserves and stabilise the rupee.
  • Report suggests inflows could come from NRIs and foreign banks over the next few months.

RBI's FCNR policy change sparks forex inflow expectations

A new report suggests India could see foreign exchange inflows of up to $85 billion following the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) latest move on Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposits. The Economic Times report, published Monday, highlights the potential boost to the country's forex reserves.

The RBI recently eased norms for FCNR deposits, allowing banks to offer more attractive rates to non-resident Indians (NRIs). This is expected to encourage a surge in dollar deposits, providing a much-needed cushion for the rupee.

How the FCNR mechanism works

FCNR deposits are foreign-currency accounts held by NRIs in Indian banks. The deposits are denominated in foreign currencies like the US dollar, pound sterling, or euro. Banks can use these funds to meet their foreign currency liabilities without converting them into rupees.

By tweaking the interest rate caps on these deposits, the RBI has made them more competitive. The report notes that this could trigger a wave of repatriation of funds by NRIs, particularly from Gulf countries, the US, and the UK.

Impact on forex reserves and rupee stability

India's forex reserves have faced pressure in recent months due to global uncertainties and trade imbalances. An inflow of $85 billion would significantly strengthen the reserves, currently hovering around $600 billion. This would give the RBI greater firepower to intervene in currency markets and prevent sharp rupee depreciation.

The rupee has been volatile against the dollar, with the central bank occasionally stepping in to smooth out fluctuations. The FCNR move is seen as a pre-emptive measure to attract stable, long-term dollar inflows without resorting to aggressive market interventions.

What banks and NRIs can expect

Indian banks are likely to launch special FCNR deposit schemes in the coming weeks, offering higher returns than before. NRIs may find these attractive compared to similar products abroad, especially in a low-interest-rate global environment.

The report, however, cautions that actual inflows may vary depending on global interest rate trends and geopolitical factors. Banks will need to manage the cost of these deposits carefully, as higher rates could squeeze their margins.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact timeline for the inflows, but market participants expect the first wave of deposits to arrive within the next quarter.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on the RBI's next monetary policy meeting and any further tweaks to the FCNR framework. If inflows materialise as projected, India's forex reserves could cross new milestones by the end of the financial year.
