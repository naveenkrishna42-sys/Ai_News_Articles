SEBI probes Aug 3-4 trades during closing auction for manipulation

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • SEBI is investigating trades from August 3-4 during the new Closing Auction Session (CAS) for potential market manipulation.
  • The probe follows a sharp market reaction and criticism from retail traders, some of whom have called for a boycott of the new mechanism.
  • SEBI has defended CAS, saying it will improve as participation increases, while analysts remain divided on its impact.
  • Market participants are awaiting clarity on whether the regulator will tweak the session rules after the investigation.

The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) has initiated a probe into trades executed during the Closing Auction Session (CAS) on August 3 and 4, to determine whether any market manipulation occurred during the newly introduced mechanism. The move comes after a volatile two-day period that saw sharp swings in stock prices during the final minutes of trading, unnerving investors and triggering demands for a rethink of the system.

The investigation marks the first formal regulatory response to the turbulence that accompanied the rollout of CAS, which replaced the traditional closing price mechanism. SEBI has not yet disclosed the scope of the probe or the specific trades under scrutiny, but officials are believed to be examining unusual price movements and order patterns during the closing window.

What is the Closing Auction Session?

CAS is a new mechanism introduced by SEBI to determine the closing price of stocks through an auction process, rather than the earlier method of averaging trades in the last 15 minutes. The session is designed to reduce manipulation and improve price discovery at the close of trading.

However, the transition has been rocky. On August 3 and 4, the market witnessed erratic swings in the final minutes, with some stocks moving sharply in either direction. Retail traders, who have been vocal on social media, have expressed frustration, with some calling for a boycott of the new system altogether.

Retail traders cry foul

The backlash from retail investors has been swift and loud. Many have alleged that the auction window is being exploited by large institutional players, who can place orders at the last moment and influence closing prices unfairly. Some have taken to online platforms to urge fellow traders to refrain from participating in CAS, arguing that it favours high-frequency traders and market makers.

SEBI, for its part, has defended the mechanism, asserting that CAS is here to stay. The regulator has expressed confidence that the session will improve as participation rises and market participants become more familiar with its functioning. In a statement, SEBI indicated that initial teething issues are not unusual for new systems and that it remains committed to the long-term integrity of the process.

Market impact and analyst views

The introduction of CAS has also led to a broader debate among analysts and market watchers. Some argue that the mechanism adds unnecessary complexity and volatility at a time when global markets are already grappling with uncertainty. Others believe that the auction system is a step forward in aligning Indian markets with global best practices, provided it is refined over time.

The 20-minute window that rattled Dalal Street on August 3 — when the Nifty and Sensex reversed course sharply — has become a talking point across financial circles. Business dailies have dedicated extensive coverage to the episode, with headlines questioning what changed at market close and whether the system needs urgent recalibration.

While the markets have since stabilised, the after-effects of the CAS rollout continue to linger. Nifty and Sensex are expected to open flat on Monday, as traders adopt a cautious stance ahead of any regulatory announcements. The investigation by SEBI, once concluded, could lead to either a clean chit for the system or a series of corrective measures.

What lies ahead

As SEBI's probe unfolds, market participants will be watching closely for any interim orders or changes to the CAS framework. The regulator has not set a timeline for completing the investigation, but its findings could shape the future of closing price discovery in Indian equities.

For now, the message from the regulator is clear: CAS is not going away. Whether it evolves into a trusted mechanism or remains a source of friction, the coming weeks will likely provide answers. Investors, meanwhile, are advised to stay informed and exercise caution as the system finds its footing.
