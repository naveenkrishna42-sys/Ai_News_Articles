CJP launches 'Kya Bolti Public' campaign to tackle unemployment

📅 2026-08-07 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • CJP announces nationwide youth outreach campaign 'Kya Bolti Public'
  • Campaign will focus on four pillars, with unemployment as a central issue
  • Abhijeet Dipke takes charge as convener; Vijay Mallangi named south India in-charge

New campaign, new priorities

The Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) has shifted its attention to unemployment, unveiling a nationwide youth outreach initiative called 'Kya Bolti Public'. The campaign, announced on Thursday, aims to turn joblessness into a defining national issue ahead of upcoming elections.

Party leaders said the outreach will span multiple states, with a focus on engaging young voters directly. The announcement marks a strategic pivot for the fledgling party, which has so far struggled to break through in a crowded political space.

Four pillars, one central issue

CJP's agenda for the campaign rests on four pillars, though party insiders suggest unemployment will be the glue holding them together. The pillars are expected to cover economic revival, skill development, local employment generation, and support for small businesses.

Abhijeet Dipke, who has taken charge as the party's national convener, said the campaign would use public feedback to shape policy proposals. 'We want to hear what the youth are saying,' Dipke told reporters, adding that the party would take its message to colleges, industrial belts, and rural job fairs.

Leadership line-up announced

Alongside Dipke's appointment, the CJP has unveiled a full leadership structure. Vijay Mallangi has been named south India in-charge and national media lead, a role that puts him at the forefront of the party's communication strategy.

Other posts have been distributed across state units, though the party has not yet detailed every appointment. The move signals an attempt to build organisational depth after months of informal, social-media-driven activism.

Unemployment becomes a political flashpoint

The CJP's pivot comes as joblessness remains a top concern for voters across India, particularly among the youth. Official data has shown persistently high unemployment rates, but the party's decision to anchor its campaign on the issue could intensify pressure on established rivals.

Political analysts note that smaller parties often struggle to own a national narrative. However, the CJP's provocative branding—the party's name references a commonly used political insult—has already earned it attention, if not yet electoral success.

The 'Kya Bolti Public' campaign will begin with a series of town-hall style meetings in major cities, followed by door-to-door outreach in semi-urban and rural areas. Party workers have been instructed to collect local employment data and grievances during these visits.

What to watch

The coming weeks will test whether the CJP can translate buzz into on-ground traction. With the campaign's first phase set to roll out shortly, all eyes will be on how many young Indians show up—and whether the party can turn their frustration into votes.
