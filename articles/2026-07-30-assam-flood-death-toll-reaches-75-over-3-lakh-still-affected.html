Assam Flood Death Toll Reaches 75, Over 3 Lakh Still Affected

📅 2026-07-30 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Death toll in Assam floods rises to 75, over 3 lakh people still affected across multiple districts.
  • Rescue operations ongoing with boats and medical teams; survivors face challenges of damaged homes and mud.
  • CM Himanta Biswa Sarma notes waters receding but people unable to return due to extensive damage.
  • Actor Alia Bhatt appeals for public support, calling the annual crisis one that still catches the state off guard.

The death toll from the devastating floods in Assam has climbed to 75, with over three lakh people still affected across the state. Rescue and relief operations remain in full swing as authorities struggle to reach marooned communities.

The floods, which have submerged vast stretches of land, have displaced thousands. Many survivors in the worst-hit districts, including Sivasagar, recount harrowing escapes but now face a long road to recovery.

Rescue Efforts and Medical Support

Teams are using rescue boats to evacuate those trapped in remote villages. A medical lifeline of over 300 doctors is now heading towards Assam's marooned areas, according to reports. The medical teams are expected to set up temporary health camps to treat waterborne diseases and other flood-related illnesses.

State authorities have been coordinating with the army and paramilitary forces. However, access to some areas remains a challenge due to the extent of waterlogging and damaged infrastructure.

Survivors Face Long Recovery in Sivasagar

In Sivasagar, survivors spoke of their narrow escapes as floodwaters rose rapidly. Many lost their livestock and belongings. With water levels now slowly receding, the immediate threat has reduced, but a new crisis has emerged.

Homes are filled with mud and debris. The damage is so severe that residents cannot return even though the floodwaters have retreated. The recovery process is expected to take weeks, if not months, for the most affected families.

CM Sarma's Assessment

Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma acknowledged the scale of the disaster. He noted that while the waters are receding, the damage to housing and infrastructure means many cannot go back home. The state government has announced compensation for the families of the deceased.

Officials have not yet confirmed the final number of displaced persons, but the toll on livelihoods is already evident. Farmlands remain submerged, raising concerns about food security in the coming months.

Alia Bhatt Appeals for Relief Support

Actor Alia Bhatt has lent her voice to the relief efforts, urging people to support Assam flood relief. She described the annual crisis as one that 'still catches us off guard', highlighting the recurring nature of the disaster.

Her appeal comes as various NGOs and citizen groups mobilise resources. Donations of food, medicine, and clothing are being collected in multiple cities.

As the waters gradually recede, the focus will now shift to rehabilitation and rebuilding. Authorities are assessing the full extent of the damage, with the next few weeks critical for the affected families to return to some semblance of normalcy.
