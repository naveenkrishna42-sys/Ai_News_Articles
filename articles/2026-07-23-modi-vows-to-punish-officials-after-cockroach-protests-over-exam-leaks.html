Modi vows to punish officials after 'cockroach' protests over exam leaks

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi announced strict punishment for officials involved in exam paper leaks.
  • Fast-track courts will be set up to handle leak cases.
  • Protests, called 'cockroach' protests by some, have erupted nationwide.
  • Assam CM stresses that student interests are paramount.
  • Response to fast-track courts announcement has been mixed.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has vowed to punish officials responsible for recurring exam paper leaks, announcing the creation of fast-track courts to handle such cases. The pledge comes amid growing student unrest, with protests dubbed 'cockroach' protests spreading across several states.

Speaking at a public event, Modi said that those found guilty of leaking exam papers would face severe consequences. The government, he stated, would not tolerate any compromise on the integrity of the examination system.

Fast-track courts and mixed reactions

The announcement of fast-track courts specifically for paper leak cases has drawn a mixed response. While some see it as a necessary step to ensure swift justice, others question whether it will be enough to address the root causes of the problem.

Critics argue that setting up fast-track courts alone cannot prevent future leaks. They point to a lack of transparency in the examination process and weak enforcement of existing laws as key issues that need to be tackled.

Student protests and the 'cockroach' label

Students have been staging protests in multiple cities, demanding accountability. The protests have been referred to as 'cockroach' protests by some, a term that has gained traction on social media, though its exact origin remains unclear.

The demonstrations have seen students clash with police in some areas. In several instances, authorities have used batons and water cannons to disperse crowds. Officials have not yet confirmed the number of arrests or injuries.

Assam CM speaks out

The Chief Minister of Assam has asserted that the interests of students are paramount for the government. He stated that those responsible for paper leaks will not be spared, echoing the Prime Minister's tough stance.

“The government is committed to ensuring a fair and transparent examination system,” the CM said. “We will take all necessary steps to protect the future of our students.” His comments come as Assam has witnessed some of the most vocal protests.

Scope of the problem

Exam paper leaks have been a persistent issue in India, affecting major competitive exams for government jobs and professional courses. These leaks often derail the academic and career plans of lakhs of students who prepare for months.

The recent spate of leaks has triggered widespread anger. Students and parents alike have demanded systemic changes, including better security protocols and stricter penalties for offenders. The government has not yet released a detailed plan on how the fast-track courts will function.

The coming weeks will show whether the fast-track courts announcement can quell the protests. The government faces the challenge of restoring faith in the examination system while managing public discontent.
