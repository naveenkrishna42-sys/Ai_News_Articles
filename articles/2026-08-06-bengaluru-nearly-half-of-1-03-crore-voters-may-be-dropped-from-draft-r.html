Bengaluru: Nearly half of 1.03 crore voters may be dropped from draft roll

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Draft roll shows significant voter deletions in Bengaluru, potentially affecting nearly 50% of 1.03 crore electors.
  • Officials have not yet confirmed the exact reasons, with claims and objections window open for affected voters.
  • Final electoral roll will be published after review, with outreach planned to address discrepancies.

Large-scale deletions in draft roll

Bengaluru's draft electoral roll has raised concern after it emerged that nearly half of the city's 1.03 crore registered voters could be dropped from the list. The preliminary document, prepared by the Election Commission, shows significant deletions, a move that has sparked worry among political parties and citizens alike.

According to officials familiar with the process, the draft roll reflects routine housekeeping measures, including removal of duplicate entries, deceased persons, and those who have shifted residence. However, the sheer scale of the proposed deletions has surprised many, given that Bengaluru is one of India's fastest-growing urban centres with a high migrant population.

What the numbers indicate

The city's electoral roll currently lists approximately 1.03 crore voters. If the draft roll is finalised as is, nearly 50 per cent of these names could be struck off. This would mark one of the largest such exercises in recent memory for any Indian metropolis.

Election officials, however, have not yet confirmed the exact breakdown of deletions or the reasons behind each category. They have emphasised that the draft is not the final list, and that a formal claims and objections window is now open for voters to correct errors or restore their names.

Process and next steps

Voters whose names appear missing can file claims in the prescribed format at their respective polling stations or through the Election Commission's online portal. The authorities have urged citizens to check the draft roll promptly and submit objections before the deadline.

The commission has also announced plans for special camps and outreach drives in the coming weeks to assist residents, particularly those in IT corridors and peripheral areas where population mobility is high. Political observers note that such large-scale deletions, if carried through, could alter the electoral arithmetic in several assembly constituencies.

Concerns and clarifications

Civil society groups have questioned whether the process has been thorough enough, especially in localities with dense rental housing and frequent address changes. They argue that many genuine voters may lose their franchise due to administrative lapses rather than actual ineligibility.

Officials, for their part, maintain that the exercise is aimed at cleansing the roll and ensuring accuracy. They have pointed to standard procedures that allow for corrections and re-inclusion, and have assured that no eligible voter will be disenfranchised without due process.

The final electoral roll is expected to be published after the review period, following which polling arrangements will be tightened for upcoming elections. For now, the onus is on Bengaluru's citizens to verify their names and act quickly if they find themselves missing from the list.

As the claims window progresses, all eyes will be on how many of the deleted names are restored and whether the final roll reflects a more accurate picture of the city's electorate.
