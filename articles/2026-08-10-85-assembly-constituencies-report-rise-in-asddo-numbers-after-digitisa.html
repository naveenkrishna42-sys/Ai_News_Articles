85 Assembly constituencies report rise in ASDDO numbers after digitisation

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 85 Assembly constituencies record higher ASDDO numbers
  • Rise attributed to 100% digitisation of records
  • Officials yet to disclose full data or reasons

Digitisation drive linked to higher ASDDO counts

At least 85 Assembly constituencies in the state have reported a rise in the number of ASDDOs — Assistant Statistical Data and Documentation Officers — after the completion of a 100% digitisation drive. The development has put the spotlight on how technology-led governance is reshaping administrative workflows at the grassroots.

The increase in ASDDO numbers comes weeks after the government completed a full-scale digitisation of revenue and statistical records across all constituencies. Officials familiar with the matter said the jump is likely a result of better data capture and more accurate documentation, rather than a sudden addition of staff on the ground.

What the data shows

While the exact figures have not been made public yet, sources indicate that the rise is spread unevenly across districts. Some constituencies recorded a marginal increase, while others saw a more substantial jump. The total number of constituencies affected is at least 85, out of the 224 in the state.

Statistical officers are typically tasked with collecting, compiling, and maintaining data related to agriculture, land records, and local schemes. With digitisation, many manual processes have been replaced by digital entries, which may have led to a reclassification or re-mapping of existing posts.

Officials remain cautious

Senior officials have not yet confirmed the exact reasons for the increase. A senior officer, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the department is still analysing the data to ascertain whether the rise reflects a genuine need for more officers or is a technical artefact of the new system.

“The digitisation process has brought in more transparency, but it has also created new challenges in understanding what the numbers mean,” the officer said. The government has not issued an official statement so far, and it remains unclear whether the increase will lead to additional recruitment or a redistribution of existing staff.

Impact on administration

The rise in ASDDO numbers could have implications for administrative efficiency and budget allocation. More officers on paper may mean higher administrative costs, but it could also improve the quality of data available for policymaking. Local bodies in some constituencies have welcomed the development, hoping it will speed up the processing of schemes and reduce delays in beneficiary identification.

However, some experts have cautioned against reading too much into the numbers without a clear methodology. “If the digitisation has led to double-counting or misclassification, the rise may not be real,” said a former bureaucrat, who did not wish to be named.

What to watch next

In the coming weeks, the state government is expected to release a detailed report explaining the reasons behind the increase and its implications. Officials have indicated that a review meeting has been scheduled to discuss the findings. Whether this leads to a formal policy change or simply a clarification remains to be seen.
