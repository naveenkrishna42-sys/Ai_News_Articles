JNU cancels Umar Khalid book discussion over 'non-disclosure' of details

📅 2026-08-09 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • JNU cancels auditorium booking for Umar Khalid book discussion, cites 'non-disclosure of full facts'
  • JNUSU slams administration for the cancellation, calls it an attack on academic freedom
  • ABVP claims credit for the cancellation, while The Indian Express headline highlights Khalid's imprisonment
  • Event was to discuss Khalid's book, with his father expected to participate

The Jawaharlal Nehru University (JNU) administration has cancelled the booking of an auditorium for a discussion on a book by Umar Khalid, a former research scholar of the university who is currently in jail. The administration cited 'non-disclosure of full facts' regarding the programme as the reason for the cancellation. The decision has sparked a fresh row on the campus, with the JNU Students' Union (JNUSU) accusing the administration of buckling under political pressure.

What the administration said

According to officials, the booking for the auditorium was made without disclosing complete information about the nature of the event. The administration said that the programme details were not fully furnished at the time of the request, which necessitated the cancellation. However, the university has not elaborated on what specific facts were withheld or how the event differed from the initial description.

This is not the first time such a cancellation has happened. In the past, events involving controversial speakers or topics have been stalled or shifted at the last minute, often triggering protests from student groups. The university's official statement did not name any individual or organisation, but it emphasised that all events must adhere to the prescribed guidelines.

Students' response and ABVP's stance

The JNUSU has strongly condemned the move, calling it an infringement on academic freedom. In a statement, the union said that the cancellation sets a dangerous precedent, where discussions on scholarly works are being suppressed. The union has demanded that the university withdraw its decision and allow the event to proceed as scheduled.

On the other side, the Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP), the students' wing of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh, has claimed credit for the cancellation. The ABVP said it had alerted the administration about the event, arguing that holding such a discussion would disturb peace on the campus. The group has been vocal in its opposition to any programme associated with Khalid, who is facing charges under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act.

The book and the larger context

Umar Khalid, once a PhD scholar at JNU, was arrested in 2020 in connection with a Delhi riots conspiracy case. He remains in custody, and his father has been speaking on his behalf. The book in question is said to be a collection of writings or reflections, though its title and publisher have not been confirmed in the reports. The event was expected to feature his father, who has been a prominent voice demanding his son's release.

The cancellation comes amid a broader debate on free speech in Indian universities. While some see such decisions as necessary for maintaining order, others view them as curbs on dissent. The Indian Express, in its report, used the headline, 'His words are free, my son is not', quoting the sentiments of Khalid's father. This encapsulates the emotional charge around the issue.

What to watch next

The JNUSU has said it will continue to press for the event to be rescheduled. Whether the university will reconsider its decision or stand firm remains to be seen. With the political overtones, this controversy is likely to stay in the news, especially as student elections approach. For now, the campus remains divided, and the debate over the limits of academic freedom in India continues.
