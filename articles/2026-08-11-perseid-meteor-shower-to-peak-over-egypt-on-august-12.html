Perseid Meteor Shower to Peak Over Egypt on August 12

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Perseid meteor shower peaks on the night of August 12-13, visible across Egypt.
  • NASA recommends observing from dark areas, away from city lights, after midnight.
  • The shower is known for bright, fast meteors, with up to 100 visible per hour under ideal conditions.

Skywatchers in Egypt are in for a treat tomorrow, August 12, as the annual Perseid meteor shower reaches its peak. This celestial display, one of the most anticipated of the year, promises a dazzling show of shooting stars streaking across the night sky.

What Makes the Perseids Special?

The Perseids are caused by debris left behind by the comet Swift-Tuttle. As Earth passes through this trail of dust and ice, particles burn up in the atmosphere, creating bright streaks of light. The shower is known for its speed and brilliance, with meteors often leaving glowing trails that linger for a moment.

Under ideal conditions, observers can spot up to 100 meteors per hour, though this number drops significantly in urban areas due to light pollution. Even so, the Perseids rarely disappoint, offering a reliable show for amateur astronomers and casual stargazers alike.

How to Watch in Egypt

NASA advises that the best viewing time is after midnight, when the sky is darkest and the radiant point—the constellation Perseus—is high above the horizon. In Egypt, this means heading outdoors in the early hours of August 13, well before dawn.

To get the most out of the experience, find a location far from city lights. Desert areas, coastal spots, or any open space with a clear, unobstructed view of the sky will work. No special equipment is needed; the naked eye is actually the best tool, as binoculars or telescopes restrict the field of view.

Tips for a Better Viewing Experience

  • Let your eyes adjust to the darkness for at least 20 minutes.
  • Lie flat on your back and look up, taking in as much sky as possible.
  • Avoid looking at your phone, as the bright screen will ruin your night vision.
  • Bring a blanket or reclining chair for comfort.

A Global Spectacle

Egypt is not the only place where skygazers will be looking up. The Perseids are a northern hemisphere phenomenon, visible across the globe. In Nova Scotia, Canada, observers will have a rare double feature: the meteor shower coinciding with a partial solar eclipse earlier in the day. Elsewhere, astronomy enthusiasts from the United States to Europe will be heading outside for the same reason.

This year's peak is particularly well-timed, as the Moon will be in its waning crescent phase, meaning it won't interfere with meteor visibility. That's a boon for viewers, as a bright moon can wash out fainter meteors.

What to Expect

Meteors will appear to radiate from the constellation Perseus, but they can show up anywhere in the sky. Patience is key; some nights, there may be lulls between bright streaks, but the overall display is usually worth the wait.

For those in Egypt, the best hours are from around 2 a.m. to dawn. The weather forecast suggests clear skies in most regions, though coastal areas may see some humidity. As always, check local conditions before heading out.

After the peak, the shower will continue at a reduced rate for several more days, so there's still a chance to catch some stragglers if you miss the main event. But for the full experience, tomorrow night is the one to mark on your calendar.

With the Moon cooperating and skies expected to be clear, this could be one of the finest Perseid displays in recent years. So set your alarms, find a dark spot, and look up—the universe is putting on a show.
