Bengaluru civic bodies hunt for land to store abandoned vehicles

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Civic bodies in Bengaluru are actively identifying land parcels to store abandoned vehicles.
  • Abandoned vehicles have become a major nuisance, clogging roads and public spaces.
  • The move aims to streamline enforcement and clear city streets.
  • Multiple wards and zones are being surveyed for suitable locations.

Bengaluru's civic corporations are moving to tackle a persistent urban headache: abandoned vehicles. Officials have started scouting for land to create designated impound lots. The goal is to clear roads, footpaths and public spaces of rusting cars, motorcycles and autorickshaws that have been left to decay.

Why the sudden drive?

Abandoned vehicles have become a common sight across the city. They block traffic, attract garbage and become breeding grounds for mosquitoes. Residents have complained for years, but enforcement has been patchy. The lack of storage space often meant towed vehicles ended up in temporary lots that quickly overflowed.

Now, multiple wards and zones are being surveyed. Officials from the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) and other local bodies are zeroing in on potential sites. The search includes vacant government land and unused plots that can be fenced and secured.

Ground-level challenges

Identifying land is only the first step. Officials must ensure the sites are accessible for tow trucks and away from residential clusters. Environmental clearance and land-use permissions will also be required. Some locations have already been shortlisted, but final approvals are pending.

The corporations are also working on a standard operating procedure. This includes notifying owners, auctioning unclaimed vehicles and scrapping those beyond repair. The aim is to create a streamlined process that doesn't end with vehicles simply being moved from one spot to another.

A long-standing problem

Bengaluru's vehicle population has exploded over the past decade. With it, the number of vehicles abandoned after accidents, mechanical failure or owners leaving the city has also risen. Many are stripped for parts and left as metal skeletons.

Previous drives have yielded mixed results. Without permanent storage, towed vehicles often ended up in private yards or remained on the roadside. The new initiative hopes to break that cycle by creating dedicated, legal storage yards.

What residents can expect

Once the land is acquired, the corporations plan to issue public notices. Residents can report abandoned vehicles through helplines or ward offices. The response time for removal is expected to improve significantly.

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for the first lot to become operational. But the process of identifying land and securing approvals is underway. For now, the city waits to see if this will finally clear the streets of its rusting eyesores.

The next step will be finalising the chosen sites and beginning the tender process for setting up the impound yards. If successful, the model could be replicated in other cities facing similar issues.
