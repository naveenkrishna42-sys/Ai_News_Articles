Newly married man's body recovered from Budgam stream after overnight search

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Body of 28-year-old Aijaz Ahmad Rather recovered from a stream in Budgam after an overnight search.
  • Rather was married three months ago and went missing during flash floods in Beerwah.
  • Jammu and Kashmir continues to grapple with severe weather, infrastructure damage, and highway closures.

Overnight search ends in tragedy

Rescue teams in Budgam district recovered the body of a 28-year-old man from a rain-swollen stream on Wednesday morning, ending an overnight search that had gripped the local community. The deceased has been identified as Aijaz Ahmad Rather, a resident of Beerwah.

Rather went missing on Tuesday evening after he was swept away by strong currents. Heavy rainfall had triggered flash floods across several parts of central Kashmir, turning streams into deadly torrents.

Married just three months ago

Neighbours described Rather as a quiet man who had married only three months ago. The news of his death has plunged his family into grief, with relatives gathering at his home in Beerwah as the body was brought back for last rites.

Local officials confirmed that search operations, which involved police and State Disaster Response Force personnel, continued through the night. The body was finally spotted wedged between rocks nearly two kilometres downstream from where he was last seen.

Third day of weather fury

Rather's death is the second reported fatality in the Beerwah area during the ongoing spell of extreme weather. Another person died on Monday as flash floods damaged homes, shops, and agricultural land.

Jammu and Kashmir is now in its third consecutive day of torrential rains. Civilian infrastructure has taken a heavy blow, with roads washed away, power lines down, and several villages cut off. A terrifying video circulating on social media shows a shopping complex being slowly swallowed by raging floodwaters near the Kashmir valley, highlighting the scale of the disaster.

Highway closed, travel disrupted

The Jammu–Srinagar national highway, the region's only all-weather road link, remained closed on Wednesday. Landslides at multiple points, including stretches near Ramban and Banihal, have made the route impassable. Hundreds of vehicles, including those carrying essential supplies, are stranded on either side.

Authorities have advised people to avoid non-essential travel. The meteorological department has warned that more heavy rain is expected over the next 48 hours, with a risk of further flooding and landslides.

What to watch

Rescue and relief operations are underway, but with more rain forecast, the risk of additional casualties and damage remains high. The administration is racing to clear the highway and restore connectivity before the next spell of heavy weather hits.
