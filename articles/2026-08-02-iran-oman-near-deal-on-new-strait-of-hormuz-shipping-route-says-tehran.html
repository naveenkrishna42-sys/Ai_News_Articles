Iran, Oman near deal on new Strait of Hormuz shipping route, says Tehran

📅 2026-08-02 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran says negotiations with Oman on a new Strait of Hormuz shipping route are in the final stages.
  • The development comes after the US suspended an attack plan in the region.
  • Tehran has rejected claims of a reopening deal, saying no agreement has been reached yet.

Talks in final stages

Iran has said that negotiations with Oman on a new shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz are in their final stages. The announcement was made by Tehran on Sunday, signalling a potential breakthrough in a waterway that carries about a fifth of global oil consumption.

Officials in Tehran said the proposed route would respect the sovereignty of both nations. The exact contours of the deal have not been disclosed, but the strait has been a flashpoint for regional tensions in recent months.

US attack plan suspended

The development comes weeks after the United States suspended its attack plan in the region. That decision appears to have opened diplomatic space for Iran and Oman to push their discussions forward.

Tehran's latest statement follows a flurry of conflicting reports. Earlier, there were claims that a deal had already been reached to reopen the strait. Iran has dismissed those assertions.

No agreement yet, says Iran

“There is no agreement,” an Iranian official said, rejecting suggestions that a reopening deal had been finalised. The clarification came after some international media reports suggested a breakthrough had already been achieved.

Iran's insistence on the final-stage status of talks suggests that while progress has been made, key details remain under negotiation. Neither Oman nor Iran has offered a timeline for when the route might become operational.

Strategic implications

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow chokepoint between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. Any disruption to shipping there has immediate ripple effects on global energy prices and supply chains.

A new route, if agreed upon, could offer an alternative passage that eases congestion and reduces the risk of confrontation. But analysts caution that the deal's success will depend on how it is implemented and whether both sides can maintain trust.

Iran has framed the talks as a matter of regional cooperation. Oman, which has historically played a mediator's role in the Gulf, is seen as a credible partner for such an arrangement.

Observers will be watching whether the US, which has significant naval presence in the region, responds to the proposed route. Washington has not yet commented on the latest statement from Tehran.

What to watch next

Negotiators are expected to hold further discussions in the coming weeks. The key question is whether the new route will be operational before the next peak shipping season, and how global markets react to the news.
