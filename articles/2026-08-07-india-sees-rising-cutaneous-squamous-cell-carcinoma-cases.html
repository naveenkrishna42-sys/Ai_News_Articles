India Sees Rising Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma Cases

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Incidence of cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma is increasing year-on-year in India.
  • Experts attribute the rise to cumulative sun exposure and lack of awareness.
  • Early detection is critical; dermatologists urge regular skin checks.
  • Preventive measures include sunscreen use and protective clothing.

Steady Climb in Skin Cancer Cases

New data from the Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) indicates that cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma (cSCC) rates have continued their upward trajectory across the country. The latest figures, released this week, show a consistent year-on-year increase, with dermatologists describing the trend as 'concerning but not unexpected.'

The rise is particularly noticeable in urban centres, where lifestyle factors and occupational sun exposure play a significant role. While cSCC remains less common than basal cell carcinoma, its incidence is growing at a faster pace, according to the report.

Why Are Numbers Going Up?

Experts point to cumulative ultraviolet (UV) radiation exposure as the primary driver. 'People are spending more time outdoors without adequate protection, and the thinning ozone layer is making things worse,' said Dr. Meera Krishnan, a dermatologist at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), Delhi.

Another factor is the lack of awareness. Many patients ignore early signs—persistent scaly patches, non-healing sores, or raised growths—until the disease advances. By the time they seek medical help, the cancer often requires more aggressive treatment.

Who Is Most at Risk?

The data reveals that fair-skinned individuals, outdoor workers such as farmers and construction labourers, and those over 50 are most vulnerable. However, younger adults are also showing up in clinics with suspicious lesions, a trend that worries specialists.

Immunosuppressed patients, including organ transplant recipients, face a markedly higher risk. For them, cSCC can behave more aggressively, necessitating closer monitoring and prompt intervention.

Early Detection Saves Lives

Dermatologists stress that cSCC is highly treatable when caught early. Surgical excision remains the gold standard, with cure rates exceeding 90% for localised tumours. Advanced cases may require radiotherapy or systemic therapies, but outcomes are better with prompt diagnosis.

'We urge everyone to perform monthly self-examinations and visit a dermatologist annually,' Dr. Krishnan added. 'Look for new growths, changes in existing moles, or any sore that doesn't heal within two weeks.'

Prevention Is Key

Public health campaigns are increasingly focusing on prevention. Simple measures—using broad-spectrum sunscreen, wearing wide-brimmed hats, and seeking shade during peak sun hours (10 a.m. to 4 p.m.)—can significantly reduce risk. Yet compliance remains low, especially among men who often dismiss sunscreen as unnecessary.

The ICMR report recommends integrating skin cancer awareness into primary healthcare. Training ASHA workers and rural health practitioners to spot early warning signs could help bridge the gap in remote areas.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether national screening programmes will be introduced, but discussions are underway at the Ministry of Health.

What to Watch

With summer approaching, dermatology departments across India expect a surge in consultations. The coming months will reveal whether increased awareness translates into earlier detection. For now, the message is clear: protect your skin, and if in doubt, get it checked.
