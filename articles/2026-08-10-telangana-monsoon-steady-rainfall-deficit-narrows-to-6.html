Telangana monsoon steady; rainfall deficit narrows to 6%

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cumulative rainfall in Telangana is 6% below normal for the season so far.
  • Recent steady spells have helped narrow the deficit from earlier peaks.
  • Weather officials continue to monitor district-wise distribution.

Telangana's monsoon has held its ground this season, with cumulative rainfall now trailing the long-period average by just 6 per cent. The latest data from the India Meteorological Department (IMD) shows the state has received near-normal showers, a marked improvement from the erratic start in June.

As of the latest update, the state's rain gauge readings stand close to the expected benchmark, giving a measure of relief to farmers and water managers alike. The deficit, which had widened in the first half of July, has narrowed considerably following a series of steady wet spells over the past fortnight.

District-wise distribution remains uneven

While the state-wide figure is reassuring, the distribution across districts tells a more varied story. Some northern and eastern districts have recorded surplus rainfall, while a few southern pockets continue to lag behind their normal quotas.

Officials from the State Disaster Management Authority have been tracking these variations closely. They point out that even a narrow overall deficit can mask localised dry spells, which could affect sowing patterns and reservoir levels in specific mandals.

Agriculture department sources say the recent rains have supported the ongoing kharif operations. Paddy, cotton, and soybean crops, which cover large swathes of the state, are reported to be in satisfactory condition in most areas.

Reservoirs and irrigation get a boost

The monsoon's steady performance has also reflected in the state's water storage positions. Major projects, including those on the Godavari and Krishna basins, have seen inflows pick up, easing concerns over drinking water and irrigation supplies for the coming months.

Farmers in the tail-end areas of these projects, who often bear the brunt of a weak monsoon, have reason to be cautiously optimistic. However, officials caution that the season is far from over, and the next few weeks will be critical in determining the final outcome.

Forecast and outlook for the week

The IMD's forecast for the next few days indicates that the southwest monsoon will remain active over Telangana. Scattered to fairly widespread rainfall is expected across many districts, with isolated heavy spells possible in the northern parts.

This comes as a relief after the brief lull in the third week of July, which had raised fears of a prolonged dry patch. The revival has been attributed to a favourable monsoon trough position and an active easterly wave over the Bay of Bengal.

Weather officials have advised farmers to maintain drainage in low-lying fields, as excess water in some areas could harm young crops. They have also urged the public to stay alert to updates, especially in districts where heavy rain warnings are in place.

What to watch next

With the monsoon's second half just beginning, the focus now shifts to whether the remaining weeks can erase the deficit entirely. A normal or above-normal September could still tip the seasonal totals into surplus, benefiting the rabi sowing season as well.

Meteorologists will be watching the development of any new weather systems over the Bay of Bengal, which often drive late-season rainfall. For now, Telangana's monsoon story remains one of steady resilience, with the narrow deficit a sign of a season that has largely delivered on its promise.
