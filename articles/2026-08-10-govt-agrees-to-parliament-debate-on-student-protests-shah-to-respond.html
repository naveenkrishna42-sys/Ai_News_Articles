Govt agrees to Parliament debate on student protests; Shah to respond

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government agrees to discuss student protests in Parliament
  • Amit Shah will respond to the debate, says Kiren Rijiju
  • Opposition had demanded statement on Delhi Police action against protesters

New Delhi: The government on Monday signalled its readiness to hold a discussion in Parliament on the ongoing student protests, with Union minister Kiren Rijiju announcing that Home Minister Amit Shah will reply to the debate. The statement came on Day 16 of the Monsoon Session, amid heavy sloganeering by Opposition members who have been pressing for a detailed response on the police action against student demonstrators.

Rijiju's appeal amid din

As Lok Sabha witnessed repeated disruptions, Rijiju appealed to the Opposition, saying the government was willing to discuss the issue and urged members not to "run away" from the debate. His remarks were aimed at breaking the logjam that has stalled proceedings for several days over the handling of student protests in the national capital.

The minister's intervention follows sustained pressure from Opposition parties, who have accused the government of avoiding a parliamentary discussion on the matter. The protests, which have drawn thousands of students to the streets, have become a flashpoint in the ongoing session.

Opposition's demand for Shah's statement

Congress leader K C Venugopal has written to the Lok Sabha Speaker, demanding that Amit Shah make a statement on the police action against students. The letter underscores the Opposition's insistence that the Home Minister personally address the House, rather than a junior minister or a written reply.

Venugopal's move reflects a coordinated strategy by Opposition parties to keep the spotlight on the government's handling of the protests. They have argued that the use of force against students requires a direct explanation from the Home Minister, who oversees the Delhi Police.

What the debate could cover

The proposed discussion is expected to cover the circumstances leading to the protests, the nature of the police response, and the government's broader approach to student unrest. Analysts say the debate could also touch upon the legal and administrative framework governing protests in the capital.

While the government has not yet specified a timeline for the discussion, Rijiju's statement suggests a willingness to engage. However, the path to a smooth debate remains uncertain, given the charged atmosphere in the House and the Opposition's demand for a categorical assurance on Shah's participation.

Stalled session, unresolved tensions

The Monsoon Session has been repeatedly disrupted over the issue, with both sides trading blame for the impasse. The government has maintained that it is open to dialogue, while the Opposition has accused it of stonewalling. Monday's development marks a potential shift, but the actual conduct of the debate will depend on the Speaker's ability to restore order.

With the session entering its final stretch, the coming days will be crucial. If the debate takes place as indicated, it could provide a rare moment of substantive parliamentary engagement on a contentious issue that has polarised political opinion.

Observers will be watching whether the government's offer translates into a full-fledged discussion or remains a tactical move to deflect criticism. The ball is now in the Speaker's court to ensure the House functions and the debate sees the light of day.
