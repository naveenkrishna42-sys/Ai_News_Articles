RBI limits FCNR(B) swap deals to one day a week, slowing dollar transfers

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI restricts banks to one FCNR(B) swap day per week, down from daily access.
  • The move slows dollar transfers to RBI, affecting liquidity and forex reserves management.
  • Bankers say the weekly window could strain short-term funding and hedging strategies.

RBI Tightens FCNR(B) Swap Window

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has restricted banks to a single day each week for FCNR(B) swap transactions, a move that is delaying the transfer of dollars to the central bank. The change, effective immediately, replaces the previous daily access to the swap window, according to a Business Standard report.

Under the new arrangement, banks can only execute FCNR(B) swaps on a designated day, which bankers say is disrupting their ability to manage foreign currency liabilities efficiently. The FCNR(B) scheme allows banks to accept deposits in foreign currency from non-resident Indians, and the swap facility enables them to convert these into rupees while hedging exchange rate risks.

Why the Weekly Cap Matters

The swap window is a key tool for banks to square their dollar positions with the RBI. By limiting access to one day, the central bank is effectively slowing the pace at which dollars flow into its reserves from these transactions. This could have implications for the RBI's management of the rupee's exchange rate, as it reduces the frequency of intervention in the forex market.

Bankers familiar with the matter said the weekly window creates a bottleneck. "Earlier, we could match our FCNR(B) inflows with the swap window daily, but now we have to wait, which delays our dollar transfers and affects our treasury operations," a senior treasury official at a private bank told Business Standard on condition of anonymity.

Impact on Banks and NRI Deposits

The restriction may also influence banks' appetite for fresh FCNR(B) deposits. With only one swap day per week, banks might become cautious about taking on new foreign currency deposits, as they face a lag in converting them into rupees. This could slow the growth of NRI deposits, which have been a stable source of funding for Indian banks.

Some bankers argue that the move is aimed at curbing speculative flows or managing the central bank's own balance sheet, but the RBI has not issued an official statement explaining the rationale. Market participants are left to interpret the change as a liquidity management measure, possibly to align with broader monetary policy objectives.

Market Reaction and Adjustments

The forex market has shown limited immediate reaction, but traders are bracing for potential volatility on the designated swap days. The weekly window could lead to clustering of swap transactions, causing temporary spikes in dollar-rupee rates.

Banks are now exploring alternative hedging mechanisms, such as using onshore and offshore derivatives markets, to bridge the gap. However, these routes are often costlier and less efficient than the RBI's swap window, which offers a direct and stable channel.

The RBI's decision comes amid evolving global economic conditions, where central banks are fine-tuning their forex operations. For Indian banks, the immediate challenge is to adapt their treasury calendars to the new weekly rhythm without disrupting customer services.

What to Watch

All eyes will now be on the RBI's next move — whether this is a temporary measure or a precursor to a broader policy shift. Banks will also monitor the impact on NRI deposit flows in the coming weeks, as the first few swap days under the new regime will test the system's resilience.
