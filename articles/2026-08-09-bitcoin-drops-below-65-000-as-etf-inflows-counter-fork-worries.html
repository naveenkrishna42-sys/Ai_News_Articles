Bitcoin Drops Below $65,000 as ETF Inflows Counter Fork Worries

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin slipped below $65,000, extending recent losses.
  • Spot ETF inflows are helping cushion the downside.
  • Fork-related uncertainty remains a key overhang.
  • Market watchers expect choppy trading in the short term.

Bitcoin slipped below the $65,000 mark on Sunday, extending a recent pullback that has rattled crypto traders. The decline comes even as inflows into spot exchange-traded funds (ETFs) continue to provide a cushion, partially offsetting fears tied to a possible network fork.

The world's largest cryptocurrency changed hands at around $64,800 in early Asian trading, down roughly 2% over the past 24 hours. The move marks the first sustained break below the psychologically important level in nearly three weeks.

ETF inflows steady the ship

Despite the price weakness, data from multiple fund issuers shows steady net inflows into spot Bitcoin ETFs. Investors appear to be treating the dip as a buying opportunity, with daily inflows averaging over $150 million this week.

"The ETF bid is real," said one Mumbai-based crypto fund manager. "Institutions are using these dips to add exposure, which is why the sell-off hasn't been more violent."

Analysts note that ETF inflows have historically acted as a stabilising force, absorbing selling pressure from short-term holders.

Fork fears linger

The overhang, however, remains the possibility of a contentious network upgrade. A faction of developers has proposed a hard fork to alter Bitcoin's consensus rules, raising concerns about chain splits and asset duplication.

In past forks, prices have often swung wildly as miners and exchanges position themselves. Some traders are buying put options to hedge against a sharp drop if the fork materialises.

"The market is pricing in uncertainty, not catastrophe," said a Singapore-based options trader. "But you can't ignore the tail risk."

Mixed signals for the week ahead

Technical indicators present a mixed picture. Bitcoin's 50-day moving average sits just below current prices, offering potential support. On the upside, the $68,000 resistance level has held firm since late July.

Open interest in Bitcoin futures has climbed 8% over the past two days, suggesting fresh positioning rather than outright liquidation. Funding rates remain slightly positive, indicating that leveraged longs are still paying a premium.

Macro factors also loom. The US dollar index inched higher on Friday, and Treasury yields remain elevated, both of which typically weigh on risk assets like crypto.

What to watch

Investors will closely monitor ETF flow data on Monday and any statements from the fork's proponents. A clear timeline for the upgrade could trigger sharp moves in either direction.

For now, the market appears to be in a wait-and-see mode, with Bitcoin caught between institutional buying and lingering technical uncertainty. The next few sessions could set the tone for the rest of August.
