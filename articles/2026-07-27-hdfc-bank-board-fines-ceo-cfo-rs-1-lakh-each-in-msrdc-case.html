HDFC Bank board fines CEO, CFO Rs 1 lakh each in MSRDC case

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • HDFC Bank board imposed a Rs 1 lakh penalty on CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan and CFO Srinivasan Vaidyanathan.
  • The penalty follows an internal review into a deposit pricing arrangement with the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation (MSRDC).
  • The board said it found no evidence of improper motive but noted certain procedural lapses.

Board action on top executives

The board of HDFC Bank has imposed a Rs 1 lakh fine on its Managing Director and CEO Sashidhar Jagdishan and Chief Financial Officer Srinivasan Vaidyanathan. The penalty is linked to a review of the bank's dealings with the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation (MSRDC).

The development was confirmed by the bank in a regulatory filing. The board concluded its internal review into a specific deposit pricing matter involving MSRDC and decided to take action against the two senior executives.

No improper motive found

According to the bank's statement, the board did not find any evidence of improper motive or intentional wrongdoing by Jagdishan or Vaidyanathan. The internal committee set up to examine the matter determined that the executives acted in what they believed was the bank's best interest.

However, the board noted that certain procedural lapses and gaps in oversight were identified during the review. These shortcomings prompted the decision to impose the financial penalty as a measure of accountability.

Background of the MSRDC case

The case revolves around a deposit arrangement between HDFC Bank and MSRDC. The specifics of the pricing and the terms of the deposit had come under scrutiny, leading to the internal investigation. The bank has not disclosed the exact details of the transaction that triggered the review.

Regulatory and compliance issues in the banking sector have been under heightened focus in recent years. This case adds to the list of instances where top management has been held responsible for lapses in internal processes.

Reaction and market impact

Market participants viewed the development as a measured response from the board. The relatively small fine of Rs 1 lakh is seen more as a symbolic gesture of accountability rather than a punitive measure reflecting serious misconduct.

Shares of HDFC Bank traded in a narrow range following the news. Analysts said the clarity that the board found no improper motive could help contain any negative sentiment around the stock.

The bank's statement reiterated its commitment to high standards of governance and compliance. It said the management has taken steps to strengthen internal controls and prevent recurrence of such lapses.

What comes next

The matter is now considered closed by the bank's board. Regulatory authorities have not indicated any separate investigation at this stage. The bank will continue to operate under the current leadership as it focuses on business growth and compliance improvements.
