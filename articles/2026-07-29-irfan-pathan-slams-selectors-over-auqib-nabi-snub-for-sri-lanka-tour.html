Irfan Pathan slams selectors over Auqib Nabi snub for Sri Lanka tour

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Irfan Pathan questioned the selection rationale after Auqib Nabi was omitted from India's Test squad for Sri Lanka.
  • Nabi, a left-arm spinner from Jammu and Kashmir, has been in outstanding form in domestic cricket.
  • The former all-rounder's comments have sparked a wider debate about transparency in Indian cricket selection.
  • Pathan specifically said he 'didn't understand the selection process' regarding Nabi's exclusion.
  • The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) announced the squad earlier this week.

Pathan questions selection logic

Former India all-rounder Irfan Pathan has publicly criticised the national selection committee over the omission of Jammu and Kashmir spinner Auqib Nabi from India's Test squad for the upcoming tour of Sri Lanka. In a strongly worded statement, Pathan said he was baffled by the decision. 'I didn't understand the selection process,' Pathan said, as reported by Hindustan Times.

The BCCI announced the squad earlier this week. While several players earned recalls, Nabi's name was conspicuously absent. The left-arm orthodox bowler has been a consistent performer in domestic cricket, particularly in the Ranji Trophy, where he emerged as the leading wicket-taker last season.

A breakout domestic season

Nabi's performances for Jammu and Kashmir have been hard to ignore. He picked up 45 wickets in the Ranji Trophy 2025-26 season, often single-handedly dismantling batting line-ups on pitches that offered little assistance. His control and ability to extract turn made him a talking point among cricket analysts.

Despite these numbers, the selectors opted for other spin-bowling options for the Sri Lanka tour. The squad includes Ravichandran Ashwin, Ravindra Jadeja, and Axar Patel. Pathan argued that Nabi deserved more than just a place in the standby list. 'When a bowler takes 45 wickets in a season, you expect him to at least be in the squad. Not even a mention in the standby list is disappointing,' he added.

Wider concerns about selection transparency

Pathan's critique has revived discussions about how the national selection committee evaluates talent from non-traditional cricketing regions. Jammu and Kashmir has produced several promising players in recent years, but many argue that the pathway to the Indian team remains unclear for those outside the major cricketing hubs.

Auqib Nabi's journey is particularly notable because he did not come through the IPL route. Unlike many current India players who were fast-tracked after franchise league performances, Nabi ground it out in first-class cricket for years. The Telegraph India highlighted this in a profile, noting that at 33, Nabi's India call-up was not via the IPL 'but a different route'—the gruelling domestic circuit.

The selection panel, led by former India spinner Ajit Agarkar, has not responded to Pathan's comments. The BCCI typically does not comment on selection decisions publicly.

What happens next

India's Test series in Sri Lanka is set to begin in August. Nabi, meanwhile, will continue to play domestic cricket and hope that his consistent performances eventually force the selectors' hand. With the selectors under scrutiny, any dip in form from the chosen spinners could open the door for the J&K bowler later in the season.
