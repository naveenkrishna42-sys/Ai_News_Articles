Sensex tumbles 350 pts, Nifty slips below 24,500 as oil prices spike

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 350 points, Nifty dropped below 24,500 in early trading
  • Spike in crude oil prices, concerns over Strait of Hormuz weigh on sentiment
  • Weak global cues and rising input costs add to market pressure

Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply lower on Tuesday, with the Sensex tumbling 350 points and the Nifty sliding below the 24,500 mark, as a spike in crude oil prices and renewed global uncertainties rattled investor sentiment.

The sell-off was broad-based, with banking, auto, and energy stocks bearing the brunt. Market participants pointed to rising oil prices as a key trigger, stoking fears of higher inflation and a potential drag on corporate margins.

Oil prices and the Strait of Hormuz factor

Crude oil prices have climbed sharply, partly on concerns over potential disruptions to supply through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy shipments. Any escalation in the region tends to spook markets, and Tuesday's decline reflected that anxiety.

India, being a major oil importer, is particularly sensitive to crude price movements. A sustained rise in oil prices could widen the current account deficit and put pressure on the rupee, making imported goods costlier and squeezing fiscal headroom.

Weak global cues add to pressure

Sentiment was also dampened by weak cues from global markets. Asian peers traded mixed, while overnight losses on Wall Street over growth concerns added to the cautious mood. Foreign institutional investors have been selective in their buying, and any spike in risk aversion tends to trigger outflows from emerging markets like India.

Domestic investors, meanwhile, are keeping a close watch on the monsoon progress and the upcoming US Federal Reserve policy signals, both of which could influence the trajectory of interest rates and capital flows.

What's dragging the market down

Beyond oil, several factors are weighing on the indices. Elevated valuations in certain pockets, mixed quarterly earnings, and lingering concerns over global growth have made investors jittery. The fear of sticky inflation, partly fueled by higher energy costs, could delay rate cuts by central banks, including the Reserve Bank of India.

Auto and aviation stocks were among the hardest hit, as higher fuel costs directly impact their operating expenses. Metal and oil marketing companies also saw selling pressure, though a rise in crude prices typically benefits upstream oil explorers.

Broader market and sectoral impact

The broader market indices also mirrored the decline, with midcap and smallcap stocks facing significant erosion. Analysts said volatility is likely to persist in the near term, especially if crude prices remain elevated or geopolitical tensions escalate further.

Market breadth was negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. Defensive sectors like IT and FMCG offered some support, but that was not enough to offset the selling in cyclical and rate-sensitive stocks.

Investor sentiment has turned cautious, and many are choosing to sit on cash rather than chase prices at current levels. The coming sessions will hinge on oil price movement, global cues, and any fresh domestic triggers.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on crude oil inventory data and any diplomatic moves around the Strait of Hormuz. A moderation in oil prices could provide some relief to the markets, but until then, traders are likely to brace for more volatility.
