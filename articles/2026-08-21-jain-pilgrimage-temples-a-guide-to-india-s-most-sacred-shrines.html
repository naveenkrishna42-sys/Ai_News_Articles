Jain Pilgrimage Temples: A Guide to India's Most Sacred Shrines

📅 2026-08-21 📂 Sacred Places Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mount Shikharji in Giridih is the most sacred Jain pilgrimage site, believed to be where Lord Mahavira attained Kevala Gnana
  • Palitana temples in Gujarat form the largest Jain temple complex in the world with over 863 shrines on Mount Shikhar
  • Dilwara Temples at Mount Abu are celebrated for unmatched white marble carvings dating to the 11th-13th centuries
  • Shravanbelagola in Karnataka hosts the 57-foot Gomateshwara monolith statue, a landmark of Jain art and devotion
  • Ranakpur Jain Temple in Rajasthan features 1,444 uniquely carved marble pillars and is free to visit year-round

Mahavira Jayanti and the Sacred Landscape of Jainism

The recent Mahavira Jayanti celebrations on April 10, 2025, drew pilgrims to Jain temples across India, but these sites deserve attention year-round. Lord Mahavira, the 24th Tirthankara, was born in Kundalagrama near Vaishali in Bihar. His teachings — rooted in ahimsa (non-violence), anekanta (multiperspectivism), and aparigraha (non-attachment) — shaped one of India's oldest living religions. These temples are not merely heritage structures; they are active places of worship where monks, nuns, and lay devotees perform puja, observe fasts, and undertake paryushan.

Mount Shikharji (Giridih, Jharkhand)

The most sacred site in Jainism, Mount Shikharji — often confused with Shikharji in Pavagadh, Gujarat — rises in the Giridih district of Jharkhand. According to tradition, this is where the 24th Tirthankara attained Kevala Gnana (omniscience) after 12 years of severe austerity. The mountain is covered with numerous temples and shrines, some dating to the 18th and 19th centuries. Pilgrims climb approximately 6,000 stone steps to reach the summit, a journey many undertake barefoot in strict observance of ahimsa, careful not to harm even an insect. The Bhumigrah (earth-touching) idol of Mahavira here is considered among the most revered in the faith.

Palitana Temples (Bhavnagar, Gujarat)

The hill of Shikharji at Palitana in Gujarat holds what is arguably the world's largest temple complex dedicated to a single religion. Over 863 temples, spread across the summit of the mountain, were built between the 11th and 16th centuries by successive monarchs and wealthy patrons. The main temples belong to the Shvetambar sect. Chaumukhi (four-faced) Temple, Siddhijainath, and Kumarpal Solanki Temple are among the most visited. The complex is entered through a gate after a steep climb, and strict vegetarian and non-alcohol rules apply. Pilgrims often stay overnight in the town of Palitana at the mountain's base, ascending at dawn.

Dilwara Temples (Mount Abu, Rajasthan)

The Dilwara group of temples at Mount Abu is widely regarded as a masterpiece of world architecture. Built between the 11th and 13th centuries, these white marble shrines belong to the Svetambar sect and are famed for intricacy that rivals the finest Gothic cathedrals. The Vimal Vasahi temple, dedicated to the first Tirthankara Lord Rishabhdev, took 14 years and enormous wealth to complete. The Hall of 360 pillars inside the Luna Vasahi temple is particularly striking, with each pillar carved to a different design. Photography is prohibited inside the temples — a rule strictly enforced to protect the marble from moisture and handling.

Ranakpur Jain Temple (Rajsamand, Rajasthan)

Built in 1439 CE by a wealthy merchant named Dharna Shah under the patronage of the Mewar kingdom, the Chaumukha Temple at Ranakpur is one of the most beautifully maintained Jain shrines in India. Its 29 halls are supported by 1,444 marble pillars, each carved with a unique motif. The temple is dedicated to Lord Adinath, the first Tirthankara. Unlike many pilgrimage sites, Ranakpur is open to all visitors free of charge throughout the year. The structure itself is a lesson in symmetry — the central shrine is surrounded by subsidiary shrines in a grid-like layout that reflects the Jain principle of cosmic order.

Shravanbelagola (Hassan, Karnataka)

While not a temple in the conventional sense, the Bahubali statue at Shravanbelagola is one of the most significant Jain sites in South India. The 57-foot monolithic statue of Lord Gomateshwara (Bahubali) stands atop the Indragiri hill, carved around 978–993 CE by the minister Chamundaraya. Every 12 years, the Mahamastakabhisheka ceremony anoints the statue with thousands of pots of sacred water, milk, and sandalwood paste. The site attracts both Svetambar and Digambar Jain worshippers. The smaller Chamundaraya Basti at the hill's base features outstanding 10th-century carvings.

Practical Information for Visiting

Jain pilgrimage sites are best visited between October and March to avoid the intense summer heat that makes mountain climbs at Mount Shikharji and Palitana hazardous. Most temples require visitors to remove footwear before entering and to dress conservatively — full sleeves and long trousers or skirts are standard. During Paryushan Parva (usually September) and Mahavira Jayanti, temples are significantly more crowded, and accommodation near major sites books out quickly. Mount Abu and Ranakpur are connected by road and lie roughly 80 km apart, making them an ideal two-site combination. The nearest major airport for Mount Abu, Ranakpur, and Palitana is Udaipur; for Shravanbelagola, it is Bangalore; and for Mount Shikharji, the nearest railhead is Giridih. Shravanbelagola has a direct railway station, while Giridih Junction connects to the Delhi-Howrah main line. Always confirm local rules regarding photography and food before visiting — many sites prohibit onion, garlic, and non-vegetarian food within temple premises, and some require advance registration for darshan during festivals.
