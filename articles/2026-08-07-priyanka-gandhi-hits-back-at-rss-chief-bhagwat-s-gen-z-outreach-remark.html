Priyanka Gandhi hits back at RSS chief Bhagwat’s Gen Z outreach remarks

📅 2026-08-07 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Priyanka Gandhi Vadra criticised RSS chief Mohan Bhagwat's remarks on engaging with Gen Z.
  • She asserted that young Indians are capable of forming their own opinions.
  • The exchange comes amid broader political debate over youth outreach and protests.

Congress leader Priyanka Gandhi Vadra on Friday launched a sharp riposte against Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS) chief Mohan Bhagwat, after he spoke about the need for the organisation to better connect with Gen Z. Her pointed remark — “They don’t need a certificate from you” — cut through the usual political courtesies and signalled a more confrontational stance from the Opposition ahead of upcoming state elections.

Bhagwat, during a recent interaction, had reportedly acknowledged that the RSS was “late” in engaging with the younger generation. His comments, which were intended as a self-critical assessment, quickly became fodder for political rivals who accused the Sangh of trying to rebrand itself among youth fatigued by unemployment and social polarisation.

Priyanka’s sharp counter

Speaking to reporters, Priyanka Gandhi dismissed the RSS chief’s outreach as unnecessary. “The youth of this country are not waiting for anyone’s approval. They know what they stand for — their aspirations, their rights, their future. They don’t need a certificate from you, nor from anyone else,” she said, in an apparent direct challenge to Bhagwat.

Her remarks were widely circulated on social media, with opposition supporters hailing them as a timely rebuke. Congress leaders have consistently attacked the RSS-BJP combine over what they describe as an authoritarian drift, and Priyanka’s latest comments fit into that broader narrative of resistance.

Political context and reactions

The exchange comes at a politically charged moment. The RSS has been trying to expand its footprint among urban youth, who have shown signs of disillusionment with traditional politics. Bhagwat’s admission of a “late start” was seen by many as an attempt to correct course, but critics argue that the Sangh’s ideology remains fundamentally at odds with the aspirations of young India.

Meanwhile, other political leaders have weighed in. In a separate development, National Conference leader Omar Abdullah condemned the government’s action against NEET protesters, saying that “protest against the government is not anti-national.” His remarks add to the growing chorus of opposition voices questioning the Centre’s handling of student unrest.

Maharashtra political twist

In Maharashtra, the political temperature rose further as Raj Thackeray, chief of the Maharashtra Navnirman Sena (MNS), claimed that the RSS was “late” in interacting with Gen Z. His statement was interpreted by many as a veiled criticism of the BJP’s youth outreach, which has struggled to match the connect that regional satraps like Thackeray enjoy with local youth.

Political analyst Nilesh Dipke, quoted in a Rediff report, went a step further, suggesting that the BJP should heed the RSS chief’s advice on Gen Z. “The RSS chief is acting as a godfather to the BJP. If the BJP ignores his counsel on youth, it does so at its own peril,” Dipke said.

Youth, protests, and the road ahead

The exchange between Priyanka Gandhi and the RSS chief underscores a deeper battle for the hearts and minds of India’s young voters, who make up a significant portion of the electorate. With unemployment and inflation dominating public discourse, both the ruling dispensation and the Opposition are scrambling to present themselves as the true champions of youth interests.

For the Congress, Priyanka’s aggressive posture marks a deliberate shift from the party’s earlier hesitancy in taking on the RSS directly. Her message is simple: the youth will judge the government on its actions, not on the certificate of any ideological organisation.

As the political season heats up, all eyes will be on how the RSS and BJP respond to this frontal challenge. The coming months are likely to witness more such exchanges, with Gen Z emerging as the pivotal constituency in the electoral arithmetic.
