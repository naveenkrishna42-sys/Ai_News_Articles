Hyderabad: One-way traffic around KBR Park from Aug 18

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • One-way traffic system around KBR Park starts August 18
  • Move aims to reduce congestion and improve traffic flow
  • Commuters advised to follow new routes and signage

Hyderabad commuters will need to plan their routes differently from August 18, as the city police are set to introduce a one-way traffic system around KBR Park. The decision comes after months of congestion complaints in the area, which sees heavy footfall from morning walkers and office-goers alike.

The new arrangement will affect all roads encircling the park, a popular green lung in the heart of the city. Officials hope the move will streamline vehicle movement and cut down on the daily snarls that have become a routine for residents and visitors.

Why the change now

The area around KBR Park has long struggled with narrow roads and high traffic volume, especially during peak hours. With the park drawing hundreds of visitors daily, the mix of pedestrian and vehicular traffic has often led to bottlenecks.

Traffic police have been studying the pattern for weeks, and the one-way system is seen as a low-cost, high-impact solution. Similar measures in other parts of Hyderabad have shown measurable improvement in travel times, officials noted.

What commuters should expect

From August 18, vehicles will only be allowed to move in a single direction along the roads flanking the park. This means some familiar turns and shortcuts will no longer be available, and drivers will need to follow the new signage.

Police have said they will deploy additional personnel at key junctions during the first few days to guide motorists. Digital boards and social media alerts will also be used to spread the word before the rollout.

Residents living in the immediate vicinity have been urged to cooperate, as the change could initially feel inconvenient. However, authorities believe the long-term benefits will outweigh the short-term disruption.

Alternate routes and parking

While the one-way system is expected to keep traffic moving, some diversions are unavoidable. Commuters heading towards Jubilee Hills or Banjara Hills from the park side may need to take slightly longer detours.

Parking near the gates is likely to become more regulated, with designated zones for two-wheelers and cars. Walkers entering the park from multiple points may also see new entry and exit arrangements.

Police have requested the public to remain patient during the transition phase. Adjustments, they said, will be made based on ground-level feedback in the first week of implementation.

Looking ahead

The success of this initiative could pave the way for similar traffic reforms in other congested parts of Hyderabad. As the city grows, such incremental changes are expected to become more common.

Commuters are advised to check official traffic updates before heading out on August 18. The police will review the system after a fortnight and may tweak timings or routes if needed.
