New “Ramayana” poster campaign puts Kaikeyi and Manthara in the spotlight

Fresh promotional posters for the upcoming “Ramayana” have been released, with NDTV and other outlets highlighting two major casting announcements: Lara Dutta as Queen Kaikeyi and Sheeba Chadha as Manthara. The makers’ decision to foreground these characters early signals a campaign built around dramatic conflict—Kaikeyi’s political influence and Manthara’s scheming counsel.

For viewers, posters are often the first “data point” for how a mythological retelling will look and feel: costume silhouettes, facial expression style, and the visual tone assigned to each character. Here, the promotional material is designed to make Kaikeyi and Manthara instantly recognizable, even before audiences see full scenes.

Why Kaikeyi and Manthara matter in Ramayana storytelling

In the traditional narrative, Kaikeyi is not merely a villain figure; she is a queen whose claims and timing reshape events. Manthara, meanwhile, functions as a catalyst—her presence drives the emotional tempo and plot turns that lead to Rama’s exile.

When poster makers choose which characters to lead, they also hint at narrative emphasis. By featuring Kaikeyi and Manthara prominently in the promotional cycle, the “Ramayana” team appears to be treating the stakes around power, persuasion, and loyalty as central to the viewing experience.

Buyer’s view: what this kind of poster release tells audiences

As an entertainment buyer guide, the most practical way to interpret poster drops is to connect promotion signals with what audiences tend to respond to: casting alignment, character consistency, and recall value. Lara Dutta and Sheeba Chadha bring established screen identities—so the risk and reward for the makers shifts from “can they act?” to “does the production’s visual language match audience expectations?”

These posters also shape audience planning: people deciding whether to watch at launch, follow episodic marketing, or rewatch mythological classics will use promo imagery to gauge tonal direction. If the look leans toward theatrical grandeur, viewers who prefer spectacle may commit faster; if it leans toward realism, audiences seeking grounded drama may be more likely to show up early.

What to watch next: promo cadence before Diwali 2026

Multiple outlets in the snippet mention that part of the release schedule is tied to Diwali 2026, with posters released in advance as part of a broader campaign. That timing matters commercially because Indian festive windows typically pull high “first-week” attention—trailers, character posters, and ensemble reveals become part of a countdown strategy.

For buyers of entertainment content—whether that’s subscriptions, streaming bundles, or event viewing plans—the key is how the makers build momentum. A sustained sequence of character-focused posters can reduce uncertainty for audiences and improve conversion from casual interest to watch-now behavior.
