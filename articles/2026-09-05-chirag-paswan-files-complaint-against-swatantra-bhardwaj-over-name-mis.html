Chirag Paswan Files Complaint Against Swatantra Bhardwaj Over Name Misuse

Key points
  • Chirag Paswan filed a formal police complaint against social media personality Swatantra Bhardwaj
  • The complaint alleges misuse of Paswan's name in online activities and possible impersonation
  • Bhardwaj is already facing multiple cases including alleged hate speech targeting a minor
  • A Delhi court remanded Bhardwaj to one-day police custody following his arrest

Background: The Dispute Between Paswan and Bhardwaj

Union Minister Chirag Paswan has formally lodged a police complaint against social media personality Swatantra Bhardwaj, alleging that Bhardwaj misused the minister's name for personal or professional gain. The complaint adds a new legal front to the already extensive troubles facing Bhardwaj, who was taken into custody following multiple allegations ranging from assault to the publication of hate content.

Paswan's representatives stated that the minister had no prior association with Bhardwaj's online activities. The complaint specifically seeks to establish whether Bhardwaj projected an official connection to the Minister or his political office to influence followers, secure engagements, or amplify his social media reach.

Legal Status of Swatantra Bhardwaj

Bhardwaj is currently entangled in at least three distinct investigations. A Delhi court recently remanded him to one-day police custody, a decision that drew sharp criticism from the counsel representing the complainant in a separate case.

Key Cases and Procedural Questions

  • Assault complaint: Filed following an alleged physical altercation, currently under active investigation.
  • Hate speech probe: Triggered by online posts deemed offensive, with a minor identified as a complainant.
  • Name misuse allegation: The fresh complaint filed by Chirag Paswan, alleging unauthorised use of his name.

The minor's counsel publicly termed the one-day custody order "very strange," questioning the procedural basis for such a brief remand when broader allegations of hate speech remain under examination. Live Law reported that a lawyer associated with the Citizens for Justice and Peace (CJP) also raised procedural concerns about the remand order.

Public Profile and Online Activity of Bhardwaj

Investigative reports have reconstructed Bhardwaj's digital footprint. According to Telegraph India, Bhardwaj maintained an active online presence featuring government invitations and photographs with politicians, content that several outlets suggest may have created an impression of official proximity. NDTV framed his trajectory as a progression from assault allegations to podcast appearances to hate-oriented posts.

For political figures and their offices, the case underscores the reputational exposure created when external actors claim association with elected representatives. Paswan's office moved quickly to formally deny any connection, a standard response when political capital faces potential dilution.

Implications for Politicians and Digital Influencers

The case highlights how political branding can be exploited without consent. Influencers who post photographs with elected officials or reference political figures in their content face heightened scrutiny when those figures later seek to clarify their position publicly.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Political offices increasingly monitor social media for unauthorised name references.
  • Short police remands are drawing judicial and procedural scrutiny from defence counsel.
  • Digital evidence, including archived posts and metadata, is becoming central to hate speech cases.
  • Reputational disassociation through formal complaints has emerged as the standard response from political figures.

What Happens Next

Investigators are expected to examine Bhardwaj's digital records, archived posts, and any communications suggesting he implied endorsement from Paswan or his political office. The one-day remand window limits immediate custodial interrogation, meaning investigators will depend heavily on device forensics and witness statements.

Paswan's legal team has indicated it will cooperate with investigators while reserving the right to pursue civil remedies for reputational harm. Bhardwaj's defence counsel is likely to argue that no direct impersonation occurred, distinguishing the allegations from traditional identity fraud.
