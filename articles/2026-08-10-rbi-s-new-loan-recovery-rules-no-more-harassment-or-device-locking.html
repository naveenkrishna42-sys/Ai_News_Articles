RBI's New Loan Recovery Rules: No More Harassment or Device Locking

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI prohibits lenders from locking borrowers' devices or using coercive recovery tactics.
  • New rules mandate transparent and fair loan recovery processes for all regulated entities.
  • Borrowers can now report violations to the RBI's ombudsman for faster redressal.

New Safeguards for Borrowers

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has introduced a fresh set of rules to govern loan recovery practices, aiming to protect borrowers from harassment and high-handed tactics. The new guidelines explicitly ban lenders from locking a borrower's mobile device or using abusive language during collection calls.

This move comes amid rising complaints from consumers who faced aggressive recovery methods, including threats, public shaming, and unauthorized access to personal devices. The RBI's directive applies to all commercial banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), and digital lending apps regulated by the central bank.

What the Rules Say

Under the new framework, lenders must ensure that recovery agents are trained to communicate respectfully and professionally. The RBI has barred any form of physical or verbal intimidation, and has made it mandatory for recovery calls to be made only during designated hours, avoiding late nights and early mornings.

Device locking—a practice where lenders remotely disable a borrower's smartphone or access its data to pressurize repayment—has been categorically prohibited. This practice had become a major concern, especially among users of small-ticket digital loans, who often reported their phones being bricked without prior notice.

Lenders are also required to share clear and itemized statements of dues, including interest and penalties, so that borrowers are not kept in the dark about their outstanding amounts. Any dispute raised by the borrower must be acknowledged and resolved within a stipulated timeframe.

Grievance Redressal Strengthened

The RBI has strengthened the ombudsman mechanism to handle complaints related to recovery malpractices. Borrowers who face harassment or violation of the new norms can approach the RBI's integrated ombudsman scheme, which now covers all regulated entities.

In cases where lenders are found to have violated the guidelines, they could face penalties, including monetary fines and restrictions on business operations. The RBI has made it clear that compliance will be strictly monitored through periodic audits and customer feedback.

Industry experts say the rules could lead to a short-term spike in compliance costs for lenders, but will ultimately improve customer trust and reduce litigation. For borrowers, the key takeaway is that they now have a stronger legal shield against coercive recovery tactics.

What Borrowers Should Do

  • Keep records of all communication with lenders, including call logs and messages.
  • Report any instance of device locking or harassment to the RBI ombudsman portal.
  • Verify the identity of recovery agents and ensure they are authorized by the lender.
  • Maintain a clear paper trail of all repayments and loan statements.

Looking Ahead

The RBI's new rules are expected to be enforced in phases, with full implementation likely by the next financial year. Borrowers and lenders alike will be watching how the central bank handles early violations, as this will set the tone for a more accountable lending ecosystem.
