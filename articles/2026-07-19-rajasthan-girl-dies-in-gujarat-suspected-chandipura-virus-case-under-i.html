Rajasthan girl dies in Gujarat, suspected Chandipura virus case under investigation

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • A 9-year-old girl from Rajasthan died at a hospital in Gujarat on Friday.
  • Doctors suspect Chandipura virus based on clinical symptoms, but lab confirmation is pending.
  • Gujarat and Rajasthan health departments have initiated surveillance and contact tracing.
  • Chandipura virus is a rare, mosquito-borne pathogen with high fatality in children.
  • Officials urge preventive measures as monsoon conditions favor vector breeding.

Child’s death raises alarm

A nine-year-old girl from Rajasthan died at a hospital in Gujarat on Friday, with doctors suspecting Chandipura virus infection. The child, a resident of a village near the Rajasthan-Gujarat border, was admitted to a government facility in Banaskantha district with high fever and neurological symptoms.

Health officials said the girl’s condition deteriorated rapidly within 24 hours of admission. She was put on ventilator support but could not be revived. Confirmatory test results from the National Institute of Virology in Pune are awaited.

What is Chandipura virus?

Chandipura virus is a rare, mosquito-borne pathogen belonging to the Rhabdoviridae family. It primarily affects children and can cause acute encephalitis syndrome, leading to rapid neurological decline and death. The virus has a high case fatality rate, often exceeding 50 percent in outbreak settings.

First identified in 1965 in Maharashtra, the virus has caused sporadic outbreaks in central and western India. The last major outbreak in Gujarat was in 2003-04, when over 300 children died. Since then, sporadic cases have been reported, but large-scale outbreaks have not occurred.

Health authorities swing into action

Gujarat’s health department has formed a rapid response team to investigate the case. Surveillance has been intensified in the girl’s home village and surrounding areas in both Gujarat and Rajasthan. Health teams are conducting door-to-door fever surveys and collecting samples from suspected cases.

“We are not taking any chances. All necessary steps for vector control and awareness are being implemented,” a senior health official in Banaskantha said. The Rajasthan health department has also been alerted and is carrying out similar measures in its border districts.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the girl had any travel history to known outbreak areas. Her family members are being monitored for symptoms, and sample collection from close contacts is underway.

Prevention and public response

Chandipura virus spreads through the bite of infected sandflies and mosquitoes. Preventive measures include using mosquito nets, repellents, and wearing full-sleeved clothing. Health authorities are urging people, especially in rural and peri-urban areas, to seek immediate medical attention if a child develops high fever with convulsions or altered consciousness.

Local hospitals have been put on alert. Medical staff have been trained to identify symptoms early and initiate supportive care. The virus has no specific vaccine or antiviral treatment; management relies on intensive care and symptomatic relief.

News of the death has caused anxiety in the border region. Some parents in the affected village have stopped sending children to schools, though authorities say there is no evidence of community transmission so far. The district education department has been asked to monitor absenteeism and report any fever cases.

The coming week will be critical. If confirmatory tests return positive for Chandipura virus, it could mark the first confirmed case of the year in India. Health departments in both states are expected to scale up surveillance and vector control operations accordingly.
