Google pulls AI image tool from Earth after policy violations

📅 2026-08-01 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Alphabet withdrew the AI image generation feature from Google Earth within 24 hours of launch.
  • Users shared screenshots of manipulated locations, prompting policy violation warnings.
  • The tool, dubbed 'Nano Banana', was meant to transform any place into stylized imagery.

Quick retreat after launch

Alphabet has rolled back the new AI-powered image generation feature in Google Earth, barely a day after it went live. The company confirmed the withdrawal in a statement, citing policy violations and growing concerns over misinformation risks.

The feature, which the tech giant had branded as "Nano Banana" in a blog post, allowed users to transform any location into stylised, AI-generated visuals. But within hours of its release, social media was flooded with screenshots of altered landmarks and places, some of which appeared misleading or doctored.

Google acknowledged the backlash in a terse update, noting: "We have also seen people sharing screenshots." The company did not elaborate on the exact nature of the violations, but external observers have pointed to the potential for the tool to fabricate realistic-looking but false imagery of real places.

Misinformation risks flagged early

Critics had warned about the dangers even before the launch. The BBC reported that the tool carried significant misinformation risks, while The Atlantic went further, saying Google had "ruined one of its most important tools" by attaching AI generation to a platform trusted for accurate geographic data.

Google Earth has long been a reference point for journalists, researchers, and disaster-response teams. The ability to generate photorealistic or heavily stylised images of any coordinate could blur the line between actual satellite imagery and AI fabrications, a concern that several experts highlighted in the hours after the feature appeared.

The company has not disclosed how many users accessed the feature in the short window it was live, nor has it confirmed whether any specific images triggered the policy review. Officials have not yet confirmed whether the rollback is permanent or temporary.

Policy framework under strain

This incident marks the latest in a series of AI-related policy stumbles for Alphabet. The company has been racing to integrate generative AI across its product suite — from search to maps — but has repeatedly faced criticism over transparency and safety guardrails.

In this case, the violation appears to stem from the tool's ability to generate content that could be mistaken for authentic satellite views, potentially misleading users about the actual state of a location. The Times of India reported that the feature was pulled less than 24 hours after launch, a remarkably short lifespan for a product Google had promoted on its official blog.

Google's statement did not specify which policies were breached, but it is widely believed that the company's own synthetic content guidelines — which require clear labelling of AI-generated imagery — were not adequately enforced within the feature.

What happens next

Alphabet has not announced a revised timeline for reintroducing the feature. The company is likely to overhaul the tool's safeguards, possibly adding visible watermarks, stricter prompt filters, and a review mechanism before any future rollout.

For now, Google Earth users will have to rely on the conventional satellite and street-view imagery that has been its staple for nearly two decades. Industry watchers will be closely monitoring whether Google issues a detailed post-mortem or quietly buries the experiment, as it has done with other failed AI products in the past.
