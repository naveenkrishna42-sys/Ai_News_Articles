Sensex Surges 373 Points, Nifty Flat as Banks and Reliance Rally

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex climbed 373 points on Thursday, while Nifty ended flat.
  • Bank stocks and Reliance Industries were the primary drivers of the rally.
  • Market breadth remained mixed, with select sectors under pressure.
  • Investors now await global cues and upcoming earnings for direction.

Indian equity benchmarks ended on a mixed note on Thursday, with the Sensex closing 373 points higher while the Nifty remained virtually unchanged. The divergence was driven by strong buying in banking stocks and heavyweight Reliance Industries, which offset weakness in other sectors.

The BSE Sensex settled at its intraday high, propelled by a rally in private lenders and the oil-to-telecom conglomerate. Meanwhile, the NSE Nifty 50 ended flat, as gains in financials were largely neutralised by selling in IT and FMCG counters.

Bank Stocks Lead the Charge

Public and private sector bank stocks were the day's standout performers, with investors piling into lenders amid expectations of improving asset quality and steady credit growth. The Nifty Bank index climbed over a percent, providing the bulk of the upward push to the Sensex.

Heavyweights such as HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India contributed significantly to the index gains. Analysts said the sustained interest in banking names reflects a broader confidence in the sector's earnings resilience despite a slowing economy.

Reliance Industries Extends Gains

Reliance Industries emerged as another major contributor, continuing its recent upward momentum. The stock rose nearly 2 percent, supported by reports of strong retail and telecom subscriber additions. The company's energy business also remained steady, offering a cushion against global crude price volatility.

Index heavyweight Reliance accounts for a significant weightage in the Sensex, and its rally helped the 30-share index outperform the broader Nifty. Market participants noted that the stock's strength is acting as a counterweight to weakness in export-oriented sectors.

Mixed Market Breadth

Despite the Sensex's gains, market breadth remained negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. IT stocks, including Infosys and TCS, came under selling pressure as investors booked profits after a recent run-up. FMCG majors like Hindustan Unilever also traded lower on concerns over rural demand.

Midcap and smallcap indices ended slightly lower, reflecting cautious sentiment among retail investors. The rupee held steady against the dollar, while bond yields remained range-bound, indicating that traders were not yet ready to take aggressive positions.

Foreign institutional investors have been net buyers over the past few sessions, but domestic institutions have been selling, creating a tug-of-war that has kept the market range-bound at higher levels.

Broader Cues and Global Sentiment

Global cues were largely stable, with Asian markets closing mixed and European indices opening on a positive note. Investors are now awaiting key US inflation data due later this week, which could influence the Federal Reserve's rate path. Any surprise in the data may trigger volatility in emerging markets, including India.

On the domestic front, quarterly earnings from a few mid-tier companies are expected in the coming days, which could provide fresh direction. The market's focus will also remain on the progress of the monsoon and its impact on the rural economy.

Overall, Thursday's session highlighted the selective nature of the current rally, with sectoral rotation playing a key role. While banking and energy stocks lifted the Sensex, the Nifty's flat close suggests that investors remain wary of chasing the market at elevated valuations.

Looking ahead, traders will closely monitor the US inflation print and any domestic policy announcements. The near-term trend is likely to remain stock-specific, with earnings and global cues dictating the market's next move.
