Survey: 62% of women’s hostels in Hyderabad flunk safety standards

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Survey by Telangana State Commission for Women evaluated 200 hostels across Hyderabad.
  • 62% of hostels scored poorly on safety parameters like lighting, CCTV, and emergency exits.
  • Commission to issue notices to violators and recommend stricter enforcement of hostel regulations.

Poor safety in 124 out of 200 hostels

A fresh survey by the Telangana State Commission for Women has revealed that 62% of women’s hostels in Hyderabad fall short on basic safety standards. The commission inspected 200 hostels across the city — including areas like Ameerpet, Kukatpally, and Himayatnagar — and found that 124 of them had poor safety infrastructure.

Inspectors noted that several hostels lacked proper lighting in common areas and corridors. Many did not have functional CCTV cameras at entry points or emergency exits. In some cases, fire extinguishers were either missing or had expired.

“We had reports from parents and students about unsafe conditions. This survey confirms that a large number of hostels are not following even the minimum safety norms,” said a commission official who spoke on condition of anonymity.

CCTV, lighting, and emergency exits are major gaps

The survey looked at 10 key parameters, including CCTV coverage, fire safety equipment, lighting, emergency exits, and the availability of a warden on-site. Over 70% of the hostels that failed scored poorly on at least three of these parameters.

In over 40 hostels, inspectors found that the warden was not present during the inspection. In some cases, the warden’s room was located far from the main entrance, making it difficult to monitor visitors. The commission also flagged instances where male staff had unrestricted access to living areas.

“A hostel in Ameerpet had no CCTV at the main gate, and the warden was not on duty after 10 pm. That is a serious safety lapse,” the official added.

Commission to issue notices, recommend stricter rules

The Telangana State Commission for Women will now issue show-cause notices to all 124 hostels that failed the survey. They have been given 15 days to submit a compliance report with corrective measures.

The commission is also expected to recommend to the state government that hostel registration rules be tightened. This includes mandatory CCTV installation, regular fire safety audits, and surprise inspections at least once every three months.

“We want to ensure that no hostel operates without meeting these basic standards. If they fail to comply, we will recommend cancellation of their license,” the commission official said.

Hostel owners’ associations have said they will cooperate with the commission. However, some have pointed out that many hostels are small operations with limited budgets, making it difficult to install expensive safety equipment.

“We are willing to upgrade, but the government should provide some subsidy or tax break for safety equipment,” said Ramesh Kumar, a hostel owner in Kukatpally.

The commission is expected to submit its final report to the state government within a month.
