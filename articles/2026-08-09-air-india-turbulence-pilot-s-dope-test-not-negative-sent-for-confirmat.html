Air India Turbulence Pilot's Dope Test Not Negative, Sent for Confirmatory Check

📅 2026-08-09 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Screening test for Air India captain after turbulence-hit Phuket-Delhi flight is 'not negative'.
  • Sample sent for confirmatory test; final result awaited.
  • Captain suspended; incident under investigation by DGCA and airline.

The government has confirmed that the captain of the Air India flight that hit severe turbulence on its Phuket-Delhi route has returned a screening dope test result that is 'not negative'. The sample has now been sent for a confirmatory test, officials said, as the aviation regulator and the airline continue to probe the incident.

The development comes days after the Boeing 787 encountered turbulence that left several passengers injured and the cockpit crew scrambling to stabilise the aircraft. The captain was taken off flying duties shortly after the incident, with Air India stating that it was cooperating fully with the investigation.

What 'Not Negative' Means

In standard drug-testing procedures, a screening result that is not negative indicates the presence of a substance that requires further laboratory analysis. The confirmatory test, typically done using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry, is more precise and rules out false positives from over-the-counter medication or other benign causes.

Officials have not yet disclosed which psychoactive substance or class of substances was flagged in the preliminary screening. The captain's identity has not been officially released, though some media reports have suggested he has been suspended pending the outcome.

Sequence of Events

The flight, operating as AI 331, was en route from Phuket to Delhi on the night of the incident when it encountered sudden, severe turbulence. Passengers described a terrifying few minutes as the aircraft dropped altitude, with loose items flying and several people thrown from their seats. At least a dozen passengers sustained injuries, some requiring hospitalisation upon landing in Delhi.

Investigators have been examining whether the captain's medical fitness or any substance use could have contributed to the handling of the situation. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has ordered a detailed probe, and the airline has set up its own internal review.

Airline and Regulator Response

Air India, in a brief statement, said it is extending full cooperation to the DGCA and that the pilot has been removed from rostering until the investigation is complete. The airline did not comment on the test result, citing the ongoing process.

The DGCA has also initiated a review of the crew's fatigue management and pre-flight medical checks. While the confirmatory result is pending, the captain remains on leave, and the co-pilot has also been interviewed as part of the inquiry.

Broader Concerns

This episode has raised questions about pilot wellness and the adequacy of random drug testing in Indian aviation. Under current rules, pilots are subject to periodic and random screenings, but the process has often been criticised for its limited scope. The DGCA has not indicated whether it plans to revise these protocols, though the incident may prompt a closer look.

Passengers on the flight have been offered counselling and compensation, with the airline saying it is in touch with those affected. Some have taken to social media to demand greater transparency about the incident.

What Happens Next

The confirmatory test result is expected within a few days, and it will be decisive in determining the captain's fate. If the result is positive, he could face suspension of his licence and disciplinary action. If negative, the matter may be closed, though the turbulence response itself remains under scrutiny. The DGCA's full report is likely to be made public only after the test outcome is known.

For now, the aviation community is watching closely, as the case could set a precedent for how India handles substance-related incidents involving flight crew.
