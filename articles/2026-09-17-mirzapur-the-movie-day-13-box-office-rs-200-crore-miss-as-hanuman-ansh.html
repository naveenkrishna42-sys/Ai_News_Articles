Mirzapur The Movie Box Office Day 13: The Rs 200 Crore Question

Mirzapur The Movie has completed 13 days in theatres, and the headline number is now a matter of arithmetic rather than hype. Pankaj Tripathi's return as Kaleen Bhaiya has delivered a strong but front-loaded run, with the film still short of the Rs 200 crore mark in India even as it crosses the milestone globally. The gap is not a collapse — it is the predictable result of a big opener losing screens to a newer, cheaper, faster-moving competitor.

The film's trajectory mirrors what happened with several 2026 Hindi releases: a huge opening weekend, a solid first week, then a sharp weekday drop once a mass-market title arrives. Hanuman Ansh is that title, and its hold on single-screen and tier-2 multiplex audiences is the single biggest reason Mirzapur The Movie is now collecting in single digits on weekdays.

Why Hanuman Ansh Is Winning the Mass Circuit

1. Ticket Pricing and Repeat Value

Hanuman Ansh is priced lower, runs shorter, and plays to families and devotional audiences who buy in groups. Mirzapur The Movie is a two-hour-forty-minute gangland drama with adult content — a harder sell for the 4 pm and 7 pm family slots. Exhibitors follow footfalls, not brand loyalty.

2. Screen Allocation Economics

In the Hindi belt, a screen showing Hanuman Ansh at 70 percent occupancy out-earns a Mirzapur show at 35 percent, even with a higher ticket price. Distributors have therefore surrendered prime single-screen slots, which directly caps Mirzapur's weekday upside.

3. The Fanbase Ceiling

The series has a large but finite core audience. Most of that audience watched in week one. Without a fresh hook — a new song, a festival holiday, or a strong word-of-mouth surge — the film cannot pull back casual viewers who are now choosing the newer release.

Real-World Buyer and Exhibitor Takeaways

  • Profit is locked: At a reported Rs 130-150 crore cost, the film has already recovered its investment from India theatrical alone, before digital, satellite, and music rights.
  • Rs 200 crore is symbolic, not survival: Missing the mark affects perception and star-power narratives, not the film's financial viability.
  • Exhibitors should pivot shows: Weekday morning and afternoon Mirzapur screenings are underperforming; shifting those to Hanuman Ansh and retaining Mirzapur for evening and night shows maximises per-screen revenue.
  • Streaming buyers gain leverage: A film that stalls below a round number weakens the producer's negotiating position for an early OTT window.
  • Franchise risk: A third instalment will now be pitched on a lower opening projection, which changes the budget and casting math.

What to Watch Over the Next Seven Days

The second weekend is the last real test. If Mirzapur The Movie holds above Rs 8-10 crore across Saturday and Sunday, it can still finish near Rs 215-225 crore in India. If it drops below Rs 6 crore, the film likely settles in the Rs 200-210 crore band and surrenders the narrative to Hanuman Ansh.

For audiences, the practical advice is simple: evening shows are still widely available and often discounted on weekday deals. For the trade, the lesson is familiar — a strong franchise opening cannot survive a weaker second week against a mass-appeal competitor with cheaper tickets and broader family appeal.
