Bengaluru youth panel demands transparency for stronger democracy

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru youth panel emphasises transparency as key to democratic participation.
  • Young citizens seek accountability and accessible governance mechanisms.
  • Panel discussions highlight need for civic education and digital engagement.

A panel discussion in Bengaluru on strengthening democratic participation has thrown up a clear message from the city's youth: they want transparency. The event, organised by local civil society groups, brought together young professionals, students, and activists to debate how to make democracy more meaningful for the next generation.

Transparency as the bedrock

The central demand from participants was straightforward. They want governments and institutions to be more open about decision-making. Without transparency, they argued, trust in democratic processes erodes.

"We are tired of opaque systems," one participant said. The panel noted that young Indians are increasingly looking for accountability. They want to know how their taxes are spent, why policies are made, and who benefits.

The discussion highlighted that transparency is not just about access to information. It is about creating feedback loops where citizens can question and influence decisions.

Accountability and civic engagement

Accountability was the second major theme. The panel stressed that young people are willing to participate, but only if they see results. Empty promises and delayed responses push them away from formal political processes.

Civic education emerged as a critical gap. Many participants noted that schools and colleges do not teach how to engage with local governance. "We learn about the Constitution but not how to file an RTI or attend a ward committee meeting," a student said.

The panel recommended that civic bodies invest in digital tools to make participation easier. Online platforms for public consultations and real-time tracking of complaints were cited as examples.

Digital democracy and inclusion

The role of technology in democratic participation was a recurring point. Young people are digital natives, and they expect governments to meet them where they are. Apps, portals, and social media can bridge the gap between citizens and the state.

However, the panel warned about digital exclusion. Not everyone has equal access to the internet or digital literacy. Any move towards digital democracy must ensure it does not leave behind marginalised communities.

Participants also called for more inclusive spaces. They want political parties and institutions to actively recruit young voices, especially from underrepresented backgrounds.

What the panel recommended

The panel put forward a set of actionable suggestions. These include mandatory public disclosure of local body decisions, simplified procedures for filing grievances, and regular youth dialogues with elected representatives.

They also proposed that schools introduce practical civics, teaching students how to participate in democracy beyond just voting. Internships with local government bodies could be another way to build interest and trust.

Several participants stressed that the burden is not just on the government. Civil society and media have a role in explaining how democracy works and why participation matters.

The discussion concluded with a sense of cautious optimism. Young Bengalureans are not apathetic, the panel noted. They are waiting for systems to become worthy of their engagement.

What happens next will depend on whether political and administrative leaders take these demands seriously. The city's civic bodies are expected to review the panel's recommendations in the coming months.
