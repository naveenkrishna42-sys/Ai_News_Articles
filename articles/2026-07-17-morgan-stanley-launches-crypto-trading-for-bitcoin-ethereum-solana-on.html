Morgan Stanley Launches Crypto Trading for Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana on E*Trade

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Morgan Stanley enables Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana trading on its E*Trade platform.
  • The move signals growing institutional acceptance of digital assets.
  • Clients can trade these cryptocurrencies alongside traditional securities.

Wall Street Giant Adds Crypto to E*Trade

Morgan Stanley has quietly opened the doors to cryptocurrency trading for clients of its E*Trade brokerage. The bank now allows users to buy, sell, and hold Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana directly through the platform.

The move brings the three largest digital assets by market cap into a mainstream brokerage that serves millions of retail investors. E*Trade, acquired by Morgan Stanley in 2020 for $13 billion, has long been a staple for stock and options traders.

What This Means for Retail Investors

E*Trade users can now trade crypto alongside equities, ETFs, and mutual funds in a single account. No separate wallet or exchange login is needed. The integration appears seamless — trades settle in the same interface investors already use.

This convenience could pull in skeptics who found crypto exchanges too complicated or risky. Morgan Stanley handles custody and compliance, reducing the burden on individual traders.

Selected Assets Signal Strategy

The choice of Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana is telling. Bitcoin remains the flagship store of value. Ethereum powers the largest smart-contract ecosystem. Solana brings speed and lower fees.

By offering only these three, Morgan Stanley avoids the chaos of thousands of altcoins. The bank is betting on established networks with real-world use, not memecoins or speculative tokens.

Institutional Adoption Accelerates

Morgan Stanley is not the first big bank to offer crypto, but its scale matters. With over $6 trillion in client assets, even a small percentage flowing into digital assets represents billions of dollars.

Other major brokerages like Fidelity and Robinhood already offer crypto. But Morgan Stanley's entry carries the weight of a traditional Wall Street powerhouse. It signals to conservative investors that crypto is no longer fringe.

Regulatory Clarity Helps

The launch comes as US regulators provide clearer rules for digital assets. The SEC's approval of spot Bitcoin ETFs in January 2024 paved the way. Banks now have a compliance framework to work within.

Morgan Stanley likely spent months ensuring its offering meets anti-money laundering and know-your-customer requirements. That due diligence protects both the bank and its clients.

What to Watch Next

E*Trade's crypto trading is rolling out to eligible clients now. The big question is whether Morgan Stanley will expand the offering to its wealth management arm, which advises ultra-high-net-worth individuals.

If so, expect more institutional money to flow into Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana. The line between traditional finance and crypto just got a little thinner.
