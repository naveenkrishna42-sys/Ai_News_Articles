SoBo tunnel project dumping concrete waste into drains, says report

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Construction debris from the SoBo tunnel project is being discharged into local drainage systems.
  • The practice has raised concerns about blockages and environmental damage.
  • Authorities have not yet commented on the reported violations.

A major infrastructure project in South Mumbai's tony SoBo locality is under scrutiny after a Mumbai Mirror report alleged that construction waste, including concrete, is being dumped directly into local drains.

The tunnel project, part of the city's ongoing efforts to ease traffic congestion, has been accused of illegal disposal of debris. The report, published on Sunday, claims that slurry and solid concrete remnants are flowing into the drainage network, potentially causing blockages and environmental harm.

What the report says

According to the news report, residents near the construction site have complained about the practice for weeks. Photographs and videos shared with the publication show grey, concrete-laden water entering roadside drains.

Environmental activists point out that such dumping can choke the drainage system, leading to waterlogging during monsoon. The debris can also contaminate local water bodies and harm aquatic life.

The Mumbai Mirror report did not name the contractor or the specific agency overseeing the tunnel work. It said that the project involves building a tunnel to connect key areas in South Mumbai.

No official response yet

As of now, neither the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) nor the project contractor has issued a statement on the allegations. Officials are yet to confirm if any inspection has been carried out.

This is not the first time a large infrastructure project in Mumbai has faced flak for waste mismanagement. Similar complaints have been raised during the construction of the Coastal Road and the Mumbai Metro.

The BMC's solid waste management rules mandate that construction debris must be disposed of at designated sites. Violators can face fines and legal action.

What residents are saying

Local residents, who spoke to the newspaper on condition of anonymity, said the dumping has been going on for over a month. They claimed that repeated complaints to the BMC and the traffic police have yielded no result.

“We see concrete slurry flowing into the drain every day. During the rains, the water level rises and the drain overflows. It’s a health hazard,” one resident told Mumbai Mirror.

Another resident said that the road outside their building has developed cracks, possibly due to heavy machinery movement and improper drainage.

What the rules say

Under the Maharashtra Non-Biodegradable Garbage (Control) Act and BMC's own by-laws, construction waste must be segregated and disposed of at designated landfills. Dumping in stormwater drains is a punishable offence.

However, enforcement remains a challenge. Environmental groups have repeatedly called for stricter monitoring of large projects, especially those in congested urban areas.

The BMC has the authority to impose a fine of up to Rs 5 lakh on contractors found violating waste disposal norms. Repeat offenders can face cancellation of their contract.

It remains to be seen whether the civic body will take action in this case. With the monsoon already active in Mumbai, any delay could worsen the situation for residents living near the tunnel project.
