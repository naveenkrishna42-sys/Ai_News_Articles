New Blood Marker Shows Promise for Ulcerative Colitis Monitoring

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Serum REG3α levels found to correlate with ulcerative colitis disease activity.
  • Blood test could reduce need for frequent invasive colonoscopies.
  • Study published in EMJ suggests REG3α as a reliable biomarker.
  • More research needed before clinical adoption.

Blood Test Could Simplify Ulcerative Colitis Monitoring

Doctors may soon have a new tool to track ulcerative colitis without relying solely on invasive procedures. A recent study highlights serum REG3α as a potential biomarker for disease activity.

Ulcerative colitis is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease that causes inflammation and ulcers in the colon. Patients often undergo repeated colonoscopies to assess disease severity, a process that is uncomfortable and carries risks.

What the Study Found

Researchers measured REG3α levels in blood samples from patients with ulcerative colitis. They found a strong correlation between elevated REG3α levels and active disease.

The marker was particularly effective at distinguishing between active and inactive disease states. This could help clinicians make faster, more accurate treatment decisions.

Non-Invasive Alternative

Current monitoring relies on symptoms, stool tests, and colonoscopy with biopsy. Each method has limitations. Symptoms can be subjective, and colonoscopy is invasive.

A simple blood draw for REG3α could offer a convenient, repeatable measure. Patients would face fewer procedures, and doctors could track disease activity more frequently.

How REG3α Works

REG3α is a protein produced by intestinal cells in response to inflammation. Its levels rise when the gut lining is damaged, making it a direct indicator of disease activity.

The study, published in EMJ, adds to a growing body of evidence that this biomarker could be clinically useful. However, larger trials are still needed to confirm its reliability across diverse patient groups.

What This Means for Patients

If validated, the test could become part of routine care. Patients with ulcerative colitis would benefit from less frequent colonoscopies and more timely adjustments to their treatment.

Doctors would gain objective data to guide therapy. This could lead to better disease control and fewer flare-ups.

Experts caution that REG3α is not yet ready for widespread use. Standardisation of test methods and cost-effectiveness studies remain necessary.

Researchers are now planning larger multicentre trials. If these confirm the initial findings, a blood test for ulcerative colitis activity could reach clinics within a few years.
