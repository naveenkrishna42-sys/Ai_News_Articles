Delhi government drops all cases against CJP protesters, with one condition

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • No legal action will be taken against CJP protesters in Delhi.
  • All arrests linked to the protests will be reviewed and dropped.
  • The amnesty does not apply to protesters with a history of criminal offences.

Government withdraws cases

The Delhi government has decided to drop all legal proceedings against protesters who participated in the Common Joint Platform (CJP) agitation. The order, issued on Thursday, covers over 2,800 cases registered during the demonstrations.

Officials confirmed that no fresh cases will be filed. All arrests made in connection with the stir will be reviewed and subsequently closed.

One key condition

The blanket amnesty comes with a single caveat. Protesters who have a known criminal background will not be covered under this order.

Those with pending criminal cases or a proven history of offences will continue to face legal scrutiny. The government has made it clear that the relief is meant for first-time or peaceful protesters only.

What this means for those affected

For the majority of protesters, this means they can return to their normal lives without fear of arrest or prosecution. Over 2,800 individuals who were booked for various charges under the Indian Penal Code and other laws will now have their cases closed.

The police have been directed to issue closure reports in all such matters at the earliest. Court cases that were pending will be withdrawn.

Police and legal implications

The order has significant operational implications for the Delhi Police. They must now identify and segregate cases involving individuals with criminal antecedents from the rest.

Legal experts say the decision could set a precedent for how future protests are handled. The government appears to be drawing a line between peaceful dissent and criminal conduct.

Background of the protests

The CJP stir had seen large-scale demonstrations across the national capital. Protesters demanded action on a range of issues, including education and employment reforms.

The agitation had turned confrontational at several points, leading to the registration of numerous FIRs. The government's decision now effectively closes that chapter for most participants.

What happens next depends on how quickly the police can process the review and issue closure reports. Those excluded from the amnesty will continue to face legal proceedings as per law.
