Vodafone Idea Q1 loss narrows 43% on higher per-user revenue

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Net loss narrowed 43% year-on-year to Rs 3,754 crore in the June quarter.
  • Revenue rose 6% to Rs 11,689 crore, aided by higher average revenue per user.
  • Company added subscribers for the first time since 2018, a key turnaround signal.
  • Analysts see tariff hikes and 5G rollout as critical for sustaining momentum.

Vodafone Idea, India's third-largest telecom operator, has reported a sharper-than-expected narrowing of its quarterly net loss, helped by improved per-user revenue and a rare uptick in subscriber numbers. The company posted a net loss of Rs 3,754 crore for the April-June quarter, a 43% reduction from the same period last year, when it had lost Rs 6,609 crore.

Revenue from operations climbed 6% year-on-year to Rs 11,689 crore, driven largely by a rise in average revenue per user (ARPU) — a key metric for telecom firms. The result marks a modest but meaningful step in the company's long struggle to recover ground against rivals Reliance Jio and Bharti Airtel.

First subscriber gain since 2018

The most striking development in the quarter was a net addition of subscribers — the first time Vodafone Idea has expanded its customer base since 2018. For years, the operator bled users, losing tens of millions to competitors with cheaper data plans and wider 4G coverage. The reversal, though small, signals that recent investments in network capacity and targeted tariffs may be starting to pay off.

Executives have not yet detailed the exact number of subscribers added, but the shift in direction was confirmed in the company's earnings statement. Analysts caution that one quarter does not make a trend, but they acknowledge the psychological importance of ending a seven-year losing streak.

ARPU rise drives margin recovery

ARPU, which measures the average revenue earned from each user per month, rose to a healthier level during the quarter, though the company has not disclosed the precise figure. The increase reflects a combination of tariff hikes implemented last year and a better mix of postpaid and higher-usage prepaid customers.

Operating margins also improved as the company continued to rationalise costs, including lower marketing spends and streamlined tower operations. The loss reduction was sharper than market expectations, with most brokerages having pencilled in a net loss of over Rs 4,000 crore for the quarter.

Debt and 5G remain the big tests

Despite the encouraging numbers, Vodafone Idea's balance sheet remains under heavy pressure. The company carries a substantial debt burden, including spectrum liabilities and bank loans, and has been in prolonged discussions with lenders over repayment schedules. A portion of the quarterly loss is attributed to interest costs on these borrowings.

On the 5G front, the operator has been slower than its rivals to launch services. Jio and Airtel have already rolled out 5G across major cities, while Vodafone Idea is still in the early stages of deployment, focusing on select urban clusters. The company's ability to expand 5G coverage without stretching its finances further will be crucial in retaining the subscribers it has just started winning back.

What to watch

Investors will now look to the coming quarters for signs that the subscriber addition is sustainable and that ARPU growth continues, possibly through another round of tariff revisions. The company's next earnings call is expected to shed light on its 5G rollout timeline and any progress on fresh funding, which remains essential to compete effectively.

The telecom sector as a whole is watching closely. If Vodafone Idea can maintain this trajectory, it could stabilise a market that has largely consolidated around two dominant players. For now, the loss reduction offers a glimmer of hope, but the road to sustained profitability remains steep.
