Arth Culture Fest returns to Delhi from 24-26 February

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Arth- A Culture Fest will be held in Delhi from 24-26 February 2023.
  • The festival aims to celebrate India's rich heritage and culture.
  • It will feature performances, exhibitions, and workshops.

New Delhi: A three-day festival celebrating India's cultural heritage, Arth- A Culture Fest, is set to take place in the capital from 24 to 26 February 2023. Organisers have announced the event as a platform to showcase the country's diverse traditions through art, music, dance, and crafts.

What the festival offers

The festival will feature live performances by folk and classical artists, exhibitions of traditional crafts, and interactive workshops for visitors. Attendees can expect a curated experience that highlights regional art forms and cultural practices from across India.

Organisers have not yet disclosed the full lineup of performers or the specific venue. They have indicated that the event aims to attract both locals and tourists interested in immersive cultural experiences.

Focus on heritage

Arth- A Culture Fest is designed to create a space where traditional art meets contemporary audiences. The organisers have emphasised that the festival will include demonstrations of age-old crafts, storytelling sessions, and culinary offerings that represent India's regional cuisines.

This edition of the festival comes at a time when cultural events are regaining momentum after pandemic-related disruptions. Organisers hope the festival will contribute to reviving interest in India's intangible cultural heritage.

Details for visitors

The festival will be open to the public from 24 February to 26 February 2023. Specific timings and entry fees have not been announced yet. Organisers have advised checking official channels for updates.

Those planning to attend can expect a family-friendly environment with activities suitable for all age groups. The event is expected to draw art enthusiasts, students, and families looking for a weekend outing.

Broader context

Cultural festivals in India have seen a steady rise in participation over the past decade. Events like Arth- A Culture Fest serve as platforms for artisans and performers who often struggle to find mainstream audiences. The festival also aligns with government efforts to promote India's soft power through cultural diplomacy.

While the festival is still a week away, early interest from cultural organisations and social media communities suggests a strong turnout. Organisers have not yet confirmed any sponsorship or partnership details.

What remains to be seen is how the festival will differentiate itself from the many cultural events that dot Delhi's calendar. The organisers have promised a focus on authenticity and grassroots participation, which could set it apart.
