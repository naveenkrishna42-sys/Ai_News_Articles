Miners Add $1.78 Billion Selling Pressure to Bitcoin Market

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin miners have added $1.78 billion in selling pressure since June.
  • The sell-off is linked to rising operational costs and post-halving revenue squeeze.
  • Market analysts are divided on whether miner selling will cap bitcoin's upside.

Miners' Selling Spree

Bitcoin miners, often a quiet force in the market, have been quietly offloading their holdings. Since June, they have added $1.78 billion in selling pressure to the world's largest cryptocurrency, according to fresh data analyzed by CoinDesk.

This is not a single dramatic dump but a steady stream of sales, accumulating into a significant figure. For context, $1.78 billion is roughly equivalent to the market cap of a mid-sized listed company, and in the crypto world, it is enough to move prices.

Why Are Miners Selling?

The primary driver is economics. Bitcoin mining is an energy-intensive business, and operational costs have climbed. The recent halving event, which cut block rewards in half, has squeezed miner margins further. To cover electricity bills and upgrade hardware, miners have little choice but to sell a portion of their mined coins.

This is a classic pattern: when miners sell, they add supply to the market. If demand does not keep pace, prices can stagnate or fall. The $1.78 billion figure represents a notable overhang that traders are now watching closely.

Market Impact and Analyst Views

So far, the market has absorbed this selling pressure without a major crash, but the effect is visible. Bitcoin's price has struggled to break decisively above recent resistance levels, and some analysts attribute this to miner supply.

Others argue that the impact is overstated. They point out that institutional inflows and spot ETF demand have been robust enough to offset miner sales. The net effect, they say, is a tug-of-war between old-school miners and new-wave institutional buyers.

What to Watch Next

The key question is whether miner selling will persist. If bitcoin's price rallies, miners may hold back, expecting higher prices later. If it drops, they might accelerate sales to cover costs, creating a feedback loop.

Investors should keep an eye on miner inventory data and hash rate trends in the coming weeks. A slowdown in miner outflows could signal a bullish shift, while continued selling may keep a lid on bitcoin's upside.
