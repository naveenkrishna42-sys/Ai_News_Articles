India, Uzbekistan ink $5bn uranium deal, elevate ties

  • PM Modi receives Uzbekistan's highest civilian honour

India-Uzbekistan Uranium Deal: A Strategic Partnership

India and Uzbekistan have signed a long-term uranium supply deal during PM Narendra Modi's visit to Tashkent, marking a significant step in their strategic partnership. The deal, valued at $5 billion, is set to provide India with a reliable source of uranium, a critical component for its nuclear energy program.

Key Details of the Deal

The deal, inked on August 30, 2026, is expected to see Uzbekistan supply uranium to India over the next decade. The exact terms of the agreement, including the quantity of uranium and the payment schedule, remain undisclosed. However, the deal is seen as a significant boost for India's nuclear energy ambitions, which aim to meet 25% of the country's electricity needs through nuclear power by 2030.

PM Modi's Visit: Elevating Ties

PM Modi's visit to Uzbekistan was not just about the uranium deal. It was also an opportunity to elevate the India-Uzbekistan relationship to a 'comprehensive strategic partnership'. Modi was conferred with Uzbekistan's prestigious ‘Oliy Darajali Do'stlik’ Order, the country's highest civilian honour. This honour is a testament to the strong ties between the two countries and the trust they have built over the years.

Broader Implications

The deal is not just a bilateral agreement but also a part of India's broader strategy to secure energy resources in Central Asia. Uzbekistan, with its vast uranium reserves, is seen as a key player in this region. The deal is expected to strengthen India's position in the region and enhance its energy security.

Expert Opinion

Dr. Arjun Nair, a senior fellow at the Observer Research Foundation, believes that the deal is a significant step in India's energy security strategy. He says, 'This deal is a testament to India's growing influence in Central Asia and its commitment to securing energy resources. However, it is crucial for India to diversify its energy sources to mitigate risks.'

Dr. Nair also warns that the deal could lead to geopolitical tensions if other countries perceive this as India encroaching on their interests. He advises India to approach such deals with caution and ensure that they do not undermine regional stability.
