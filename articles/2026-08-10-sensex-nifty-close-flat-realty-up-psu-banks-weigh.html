Sensex, Nifty close flat; realty up, PSU banks weigh

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty closed flat after a rangebound session
  • Realty sector emerged as the top gainer
  • PSU bank stocks lagged, dragging the market

Indian equity benchmarks ended Monday's session on a flat note, as investors chose to stay on the sidelines amid a lack of fresh triggers. The Sensex and the Nifty oscillated within a narrow band through the day, ultimately closing with marginal changes. The mood was cautious, with market participants awaiting the next round of domestic and global cues.

Rangebound trade keeps investors guessing

The session was characterised by low volatility, with the indices failing to establish a clear direction. Buying interest was selective, keeping the broader market in check. Analysts noted that the absence of any major macroeconomic data or corporate earnings due in the near term left little reason for aggressive positioning.

Volumes were moderate, and the advance-decline ratio remained balanced. The resilience of the market, however, was evident as it held onto its recent gains despite the lack of momentum. Traders appeared to be waiting for a breakout, either on the upside or the downside, before committing fresh capital.

Realty stocks shine bright

Amid the otherwise dull session, the realty sector emerged as the clear outperformer. Stocks of property developers and related firms rallied, driven by expectations of sustained demand in the housing market. The sector's strength provided some support to the headline indices, even as other heavyweight sectors struggled.

The positive sentiment in realty was attributed to ongoing urbanisation trends and steady interest in residential properties. Investors seemed to be betting on continued growth in the sector, which has been a consistent performer in recent months. The rally in realty stocks helped offset some of the weakness seen elsewhere.

PSU banks play the laggard

On the other end of the spectrum, public sector undertaking (PSU) banks were the main drag on the market. Shares of state-owned lenders declined, pulling down the banking and financial services indices. The weakness in PSU banks was notable, as private sector banks managed to hold their ground.

The selling pressure in PSU banks was attributed to profit-taking after a recent run-up, as well as concerns over asset quality and margin pressures. Investors appeared to be rotating out of these stocks and into safer havens or sectors with more visible growth prospects. The decline in PSU banks weighed on the overall market sentiment, preventing any meaningful upside.

Broader market and sectoral trends

Apart from realty and PSU banks, other sectors showed mixed trends. IT and FMCG stocks were relatively stable, while metal and energy shares saw some volatility. The broader market, represented by midcap and smallcap indices, also ended flat, mirroring the lack of direction in the benchmarks.

Foreign institutional investors remained net buyers, albeit at a slower pace, while domestic institutional investors were seen as net sellers. The flow dynamics suggested a cautious approach from all participants, with no single group willing to take a decisive stance. The rupee traded in a narrow range against the dollar, adding to the overall sense of stability.

What to watch next

With no major domestic triggers on the horizon, market direction will likely be dictated by global cues, including US Federal Reserve policy signals and crude oil prices. Any surprise on the geopolitical front or in inflation data could inject volatility into the session.

Traders will also keep an eye on the upcoming monthly derivatives expiry, which could bring some short-term movement. The coming days are expected to remain rangebound unless a significant catalyst emerges. Investors would do well to stay nimble and watch for any shift in momentum, as the market appears to be coiling for its next big move.
