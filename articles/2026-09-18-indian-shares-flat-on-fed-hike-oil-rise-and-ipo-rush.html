Indian Shares Flat on Fed Hike, Oil Rise and IPO Rush

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-18 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Fed rate hike raised global risk‑aversion, limiting domestic buying interest.
  • Brent crude above $90/bbl increased import‑cost concerns for energy‑heavy sectors.
  • A surge of IPOs diverted liquidity and kept breadth narrow despite gains in select stocks.

Market Snapshot: Flat Close Amid Mixed Cues

Indian benchmark indices ended the session with minimal movement, the Sensex hovering around 66,200 and the Nifty near 19,750. The lack of decisive direction followed a day of contrasting signals: a positive opening driven by overseas gains, followed by profit‑booking as investors digested external pressures. Trading volumes remained moderate, reflecting a cautious stance among participants.

Impact of the U.S. Federal Reserve Rate Hike

The Federal Reserve’s 25‑basis‑point increase lifted the policy rate to 5.50 %, reinforcing expectations of tighter global liquidity. Higher U.S. yields typically pull capital away from emerging markets, and Indian equities felt the pull as foreign institutional investors trimmed exposure. The move also raised the cost of foreign‑currency debt for Indian corporates, adding a layer of risk to earnings outlook.

Oil Price Pressure on Domestic Sectors

Brent crude traded above $90 per barrel, driven by supply‑cut announcements and geopolitical tensions. Elevated oil prices increase the import bill and squeeze margins for sectors such as transportation, logistics and manufacturing. Analysts noted that the Nifty Energy sub‑index slipped roughly 0.8 % while the broader market remained flat, underscoring the sector‑specific drag.

IPO Rush and Liquidity Diversion

September saw a flurry of new listings, with the NSE’s own IPO attracting significant retail interest alongside several technology and healthcare offerings. The fresh supply of shares absorbed a portion of incremental capital that might have otherwise supported secondary market buying. Data from the exchanges showed that IPO‑related turnover accounted for nearly 12 % of total cash market volume on the day.

Outlook for Investors

Market participants are watching for cues from the Reserve Bank of India’s forthcoming policy meeting, which may respond to inflationary pressures exacerbated by higher oil costs. Until clarity emerges on monetary policy and global risk sentiment, traders are likely to adopt a selective approach, focusing on companies with strong domestic demand and limited exposure to imported commodities.

Key Insights for Market Participants

  • External monetary tightening remains the primary headwind for Indian equities.
  • Oil‑linked sectors face margin pressure; consider hedging or diversification.
  • IPO activity can temporarily absorb liquidity, affecting secondary market breadth.
  • Selective buying in domestically focused, low‑commodity‑exposure stocks may offer relative resilience.
