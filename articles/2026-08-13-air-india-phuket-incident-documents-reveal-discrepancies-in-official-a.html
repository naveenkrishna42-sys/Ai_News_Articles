Air India Phuket Incident: Documents Reveal Discrepancies in Official Account

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Internal documents reportedly show Air India described technical issues on the Phuket-Delhi flight as 'turbulence', raising questions about disclosure.
  • The Union government has asked Air India to 'take responsibility' and directed the DGCA to strengthen its dope-testing protocols for pilots.
  • The pilot in question has reportedly tested positive for marijuana but claims he was on prescribed medication for sleep issues; further lab results are awaited.

New Delhi: Internal documents reviewed by NDTV suggest that Air India may have downplayed the severity of multiple technical malfunctions on a Phuket-Delhi flight, describing them as mere 'turbulence' in official communications. The disclosure has prompted the Union government to ask the airline to 'take responsibility' and has directed the Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) to improve its dope-check procedures for flight crews.

The incident, which occurred earlier this month, has snowballed into a multi-layered controversy involving operational safety, pilot health protocols, and corporate transparency. While Air India has updated the Centre on its internal probe, a full report on the Phuket-Delhi incident is still awaited, according to the Ministry of Aviation.

The 'Turbulence' Discrepancy

Documents accessed by NDTV reportedly indicate that Air India's initial internal communications referenced 'turbulence' as the cause of the disruption during the Phuket-Delhi leg. However, a closer examination of the flight's technical logs suggests the crew may have dealt with more than just rough weather — including possible pressurisation irregularities and other system warnings that would warrant a formal technical failure disclosure.

Aviation experts point out that the distinction matters. Turbulence is a routine weather phenomenon that pilots handle without escalation. A technical failure, on the other hand, triggers mandatory reporting protocols under DGCA regulations, and any delay or mischaracterisation could constitute a compliance breach. The documents, if authenticated, would add weight to allegations that the airline sought to minimise the incident's gravity.

Air India has not yet issued a detailed rebuttal to these specific document findings. The airline's earlier statement to the government acknowledged the incident but maintained that safety protocols were followed throughout the flight.

Pilot's Medical and Drug Test Controversy

Complicating the narrative is the revelation that the flight captain tested positive for marijuana in a preliminary screening. The pilot, who has not been named publicly, has claimed he was suffering from sleep issues and was on prescribed medication that may have influenced the test result.

The Hindustan Times reports that the pilot's defence hinges on a doctor's prescription for a sleep aid that, he argues, could have caused a false positive. The Aviation Ministry has stated that further laboratory tests are awaited, and until those results are known, the pilot's status remains suspended pending a full inquiry.

The DGCA's dope-testing regime for pilots has come under scrutiny. The government's directive to improve dope checks suggests an acknowledgment that the current system may have gaps — either in frequency, methodology, or the handling of prescription medications that could mask or mimic prohibited substances.

Government's Response and Regulatory Pressure

The Times of India reports that the government has asked Air India to 'take responsibility' for the incident, a phrase that carries weight in bureaucratic circles. It signals that the Centre is not satisfied with the airline's initial handling and wants a more proactive admission of any lapses.

Simultaneously, the DGCA has been told to tighten its oversight. This could mean more frequent random drug tests, better training for medical examiners, and clearer guidelines on what constitutes a valid prescription defence. The regulator's credibility is on the line, given that this is not the first time a pilot's medical fitness has been questioned.

For Air India, which is in the midst of a high-profile privatisation-era turnaround, this incident is a reputational setback. The airline has been positioning itself as a world-class carrier, and any suggestion that it might have papered over technical failures or laxly vetted its pilots undermines that pitch.

What the Documents Actually Say

While the full text of the documents has not been made public, the available excerpts reportedly show that the word 'turbulence' was used in at least two separate internal communications regarding the flight. Technical logs, however, list multiple system alerts that are not consistent with a simple turbulence encounter.

It is unclear whether the use of 'turbulence' was a genuine error by a junior staffer or a deliberate attempt to simplify a complex situation for higher management. Either way, the discrepancy has eroded trust. The Telegraph India notes that the airline has updated the Centre on its probe, but a complete report is still pending, leaving room for further revelations.

NDTV's original reporting indicates that the documents were obtained from a source within the airline's operations wing. Their authenticity has not been independently verified by other outlets, but the government's swift response suggests they have been taken seriously at the highest levels.

Regulatory Implications

The DGCA's directive to improve dope checks is a direct consequence of this case. If the pilot's preliminary positive test is confirmed by the lab, it would mark the first such instance involving a commercial airline captain on a scheduled international route in recent memory.

If the test is invalidated by the prescription defence, it will still raise questions about how airlines track crew medication and whether pilots are fit to fly while on sleep aids. Both scenarios are problematic for the industry.

For passengers, the takeaway is unsettling: a routine flight from Phuket to Delhi may have involved more than just a bumpy ride, and the communication around it was less than transparent.

What to Watch

The next few weeks will be critical. The final lab test results for the pilot are expected shortly, and the DGCA's full inquiry report will determine whether Air India faces penalties for any reporting irregularities. Watch for the Ministry of Aviation's response to the DGCA's findings, and whether any senior Air India officials face action.

Also, keep an eye on the DGCA's revised dope-testing guidelines — the speed and stringency of those changes will signal how seriously the regulator takes this lapse.
