Houthis fire ballistic missiles at Yemen's al-Makha and Marib

📅 2026-08-11 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Houthi rebels launched ballistic missile attacks on Yemen's al-Makha and Marib
  • Renewed attacks target key port city, raising war risk to highest since 2022 truce
  • Escalation follows Houthi-Saudi clashes and regional tensions
  • UN Security Council issued press statement on Yemen on August 7, 2026

Yemen's Houthi rebels have launched ballistic missile attacks on the strategic port city of al-Makha and the central province of Marib, according to reports from the region. The strikes mark a significant escalation in the long-running conflict, which had largely stalled since a fragile truce was brokered in 2022.

The attacks, which targeted key military and civilian infrastructure, have heightened fears of a return to full-scale warfare. Al-Makha, a vital Red Sea port, has been a focal point of the conflict due to its strategic importance for shipping and aid deliveries. Marib, meanwhile, remains the last northern stronghold of the internationally recognised government.

Renewed Assault on Key Port City

The Iranian-backed Houthi rebels have intensified their campaign against al-Makha, which serves as a critical entry point for humanitarian supplies. Witnesses reported several explosions in and around the port area, though officials have not yet confirmed the extent of damage or casualties.

The renewed assault on al-Makha is seen as a deliberate attempt to disrupt supply lines and exert pressure on the government and its allies. The port city has changed hands multiple times since the conflict began, making it a symbolic and strategic prize for both sides.

Marib Under Fire

In Marib, the missile strikes targeted areas near the city, which hosts a large population of internally displaced persons. The province has been a battleground for years, with Houthi forces repeatedly attempting to seize control of its oil and gas resources.

Residents reported hearing loud blasts, but details remain scarce as communication lines in the region are often disrupted during such attacks. The Houthis have not yet claimed responsibility, but their history of using ballistic missiles and drones suggests a coordinated operation.

War Risk at Highest Since 2022 Truce

Analysts are describing the current situation as the highest war risk Yemen has faced since the 2022 truce. The escalation comes amid heightened tensions between Houthi forces and Saudi-led coalition troops, with clashes reported along multiple frontlines in recent weeks.

The breakdown of the truce, which had brought a relative lull in fighting, has raised concerns about a broader regional conflict. The involvement of external powers, including Iran and Saudi Arabia, continues to complicate efforts to find a political solution.

The United Nations Security Council issued a press statement on Yemen on August 7, 2026, expressing concern over the deteriorating situation. The statement called for restraint and urged all parties to return to dialogue, but no concrete actions have been announced yet.

Regional and International Reactions

The attacks have drawn condemnation from several governments, though responses have been muted so far. India Today reported on Iran's supreme leader appointing a Guards general as military head, a move that could signal deeper Iranian involvement in the region's conflicts.

Meanwhile, France 24 noted the Security Council's press statement, which highlighted the need for de-escalation. However, with no ceasefire in sight, the international community faces a challenging path forward in addressing the Yemeni crisis.

The Houthi-Saudi clashes, which have intensified over the past month, are seen as a precursor to the current missile attacks. These confrontations have already claimed numerous lives and displaced thousands more, compounding the humanitarian crisis in Yemen.

Humanitarian Concerns

The renewed violence threatens to worsen an already dire humanitarian situation. Millions of Yemenis rely on aid for survival, and attacks on ports like al-Makha could severely hamper relief efforts. Humanitarian agencies have repeatedly called for safe corridors to deliver essential supplies.

The displacement of civilians, particularly in Marib, remains a pressing concern. The province already shelters over a million displaced people, and further fighting could trigger another wave of internal displacement.

As the conflict enters a more volatile phase, the international community is watching closely. The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether the parties can be brought back to the negotiating table or whether Yemen will slide further into chaos.

Observers note that the Houthis' ability to sustain such missile campaigns depends on external support. The recent appointment of a Guards general as Iran's military head could indicate a more assertive Iranian posture in the region, potentially prolonging the conflict.

For now, the people of al-Makha and Marib remain in the crosshairs, bracing for what may come next. The path to peace, already fraught with obstacles, appears more elusive than ever.

What happens next depends largely on the response of the Saudi-led coalition and the international community. If the current momentum towards escalation is not reversed, Yemen could face its deadliest period in years.
