RBI Deputy Governor Calls for Faster MSME Loans and Cash‑Flow Credit Expansion

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-28 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI aims to lift MSME loan share to 15% of bank portfolios by 2027.
  • Cash‑flow‑based credit could fund up to 70% of loan value, easing collateral demands.
  • Banks must tighten underwriting while expanding digital assessment tools.

Policy Context and RBI's Mandate

The Reserve Bank of India’s Deputy Governor, Mr. Swaminathan J., reiterated the central bank’s 2026 roadmap to deepen MSME financing. In a speech to the Indian Bankers Association, he warned that the sector’s credit gap remains above ₹1.5 trillion despite a 2025‑26 credit growth of 12%.

RBI’s Financial Stability Report 2026 cites MSMEs as contributing roughly 30% of GDP and employing over 1.1 crore workers. Yet, only 20‑30% of the free‑trade‑agreement benefits are being tapped, underscoring the need for cheaper, faster credit.

Cash‑Flow‑Based Lending: Mechanics and Benchmarks

Cash‑flow‑based credit evaluates a borrower’s recurring revenue streams rather than physical assets. Under RBI’s new guidelines, banks may fund up to 70% of a loan on cash‑flow metrics, with the remaining 30% covered by limited collateral or guarantees.

Key performance indicators include:

  • Average monthly turnover growth >5% YoY
  • Operating cash‑flow margin >15%
  • Debt‑service‑coverage ratio (DSCR) ≥1.25

Digital platforms such as the RBI’s MSME Credit Hub will aggregate bank statements, GST filings and ERP data to generate a real‑time credit score.

Implications for MSME Borrowers

For MSMEs, the shift promises three tangible benefits. First, reduced reliance on land or equipment as security cuts processing time from 45‑60 days to under 15 days. Second, interest rates are expected to align with the repo‑linked base, currently 6.75%, rather than the historic 12‑14% premium for collateral‑heavy loans.

Third, the new framework encourages tier‑2 lenders and fintechs to enter the market, expanding the pool of credit sources. However, firms must maintain clean digital records; gaps in GST returns or irregular cash‑flow can trigger higher spreads.

Bank Response and Market Impact

Major Indian banks have begun pilot projects. State Bank of India reported a 22% rise in MSME loan approvals in Q2‑2026 after integrating cash‑flow analytics. Similarly, HDFC Bank’s fintech arm, FinX, launched a sandbox for real‑time cash‑flow verification, reporting an average turnaround of 9 days.

Risk‑adjusted return on capital (RAROC) for cash‑flow loans is projected at 8‑9%, comparable to traditional term loans but with lower provisioning needs. Analysts at Motilal Oswal note that banks could see a 0.5‑1.0% uplift in net interest margin if the target MSME share of total loan book (15% by 2027) is met.

Key Real‑World Highlights

  • Target Share: Increase MSME loan portfolio to 15% of total bank assets by FY27.
  • Funding Ratio: Up to 70% of loan value may be granted on cash‑flow evidence alone.
  • Interest Rate Anchor: Base rate linked to RBI repo (6.75% as of Oct 2026).
  • Processing Speed: Expected reduction to 10‑15 days for qualified borrowers.
  • Risk Metrics: Minimum DSCR of 1.25 and turnover growth >5% YoY.
