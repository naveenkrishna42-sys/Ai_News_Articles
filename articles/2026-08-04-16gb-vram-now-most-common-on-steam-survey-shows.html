16GB VRAM Now Most Common on Steam, Survey Shows

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • 16GB VRAM has overtaken 8GB as the most common GPU memory on Steam
  • 8-core CPUs are now the standard for a majority of Steam users
  • Survey reflects a broader industry shift toward higher-end PC hardware
  • Despite the milestone, a significant chunk of gamers still run 8GB or less

The latest Steam Hardware Survey for July 2026 has confirmed a long-anticipated shift in the PC gaming ecosystem: 16GB of VRAM is now the most common graphics memory configuration among players on the platform. For years, 8GB was the baseline for mainstream gaming graphics cards, but that era has clearly ended.

The data, collected from millions of Steam users worldwide, shows that GPUs with 16GB of video memory have now surpassed all other capacities. This marks a decisive moment for the industry, as game developers increasingly target higher resolutions and texture-heavy assets that demand more memory.

A Slow but Steady Transition

The move toward 16GB has been building for several quarters. Early adopters and enthusiasts began upgrading when next-generation consoles pushed memory requirements upward, and the trend has now reached the mainstream. The survey indicates that a majority of active Steam users are playing with 16GB or more, a figure that seemed unlikely just a couple of years ago.

However, the shift is not universal. A notable portion of the Steam user base still runs 8GB cards, and some older systems with even less memory remain in use. This means developers cannot yet ignore lower-spec hardware entirely, even as the high-end segment grows.

8-Core CPUs Also Go Mainstream

Alongside the VRAM milestone, the July survey highlights another significant trend: 8-core processors have become the norm. CPUs with eight physical cores now account for the largest share of Steam systems, overtaking quad-core and six-core chips that previously dominated the charts.

This aligns with the broader PC market, where AMD and Intel have pushed core counts upward across their product stacks. Budget and mid-range processors now routinely offer eight cores, making them an attractive option for gamers who multitask while playing or stream their sessions.

What This Means for Gamers

For players, the rise of 16GB VRAM and 8-core CPUs means better performance in modern titles, particularly at 1440p and 4K resolutions. Games with high-resolution textures, ray tracing, and complex open worlds stand to benefit the most from these hardware advancements.

It also signals that the days of 8GB as a recommended specification are numbered. Upcoming game releases are likely to list 16GB as a recommended or even minimum requirement, especially as developers optimise for the latest console hardware, which shares similar memory pools.

The survey also reflects a broader trend of PC gamers holding onto their hardware longer. Instead of upgrading every generation, many are investing in higher-spec components that can remain viable for several years, which explains the steady but not explosive growth of 16GB adoption.

The Road Ahead

As the next generation of graphics cards approaches, the 16GB mark will likely become the new entry point. Budget cards are expected to ship with at least this much memory, while higher-end models may push toward 24GB or more. The Steam survey will be a key indicator of how quickly these newer capacities gain traction.

For now, the data paints a clear picture: PC gaming hardware is moving upward, and the baseline for a capable gaming rig has risen. Developers and hardware makers will be watching the next few surveys closely to see if the trend accelerates or plateaus.

With the holiday season and new game releases on the horizon, the next Steam Hardware Survey could show whether 16GB becomes the undisputed standard or if a new capacity benchmark emerges. Either way, the days of 8GB dominance are firmly in the rearview mirror.
