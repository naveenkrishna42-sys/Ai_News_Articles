Shambhu Border Sealed as Farmers Head to Delhi for Protest

📅 2026-07-21 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shambhu border point sealed by Haryana police to block farmers' march to Delhi
  • Farmers protesting against proposed India-U.S. trade deal, fearing impact on agriculture
  • Protesters turned back after assurances from officials; rally planned at Jantar Mantar

The Shambhu border point on the Punjab-Haryana boundary was sealed on Tuesday morning as thousands of farmers attempted to march towards Delhi to protest against a proposed India-U.S. trade deal. Security personnel deployed barricades and tear gas shells to disperse the crowd. The protest, organised by several farmer unions, aims to pressure the government against signing an agreement they claim will harm Indian agriculture.

Protest Over Unseen Trade Deal

Farmers from Punjab began gathering at the border point early Tuesday, carrying banners and shouting slogans against the proposed trade pact. The deal, still under negotiation, has not been signed by India. Yet, unions argue that its provisions — including lower tariffs on American agricultural imports — could flood the Indian market with subsidised foreign produce.

"We are protesting against a deal that hasn't even been signed," said a farmer leader on the ground. "But the direction is clear. If it goes through, our livelihoods will be destroyed." The rally was planned to culminate at Jantar Mantar in Delhi, a common protest venue.

Security Response and Blockade

Haryana police sealed the Shambhu border early in the morning, citing law and order concerns. Heavy vehicles and concrete barriers were placed on the highway. Officers said they were acting on intelligence that a large number of protesters could disrupt traffic and public order.

"We have sealed the border to prevent any untoward incident," a senior Haryana police official told reporters. "Farmers have been stopped and asked to return. We are in touch with their leaders." The U.S. Embassy in New Delhi issued a demonstration alert on July 17, warning American citizens to avoid the area.

Assurances Lead to Retreat

After hours of standoff, farmer leaders agreed to turn back following assurances from district officials. The exact nature of the assurance remains unclear, but union representatives said they were promised a meeting with senior government officials in the coming days.

"We have decided to return for now," one leader announced to the crowd. "But our struggle is not over. We will come back if the government does not listen." The protest on Tuesday was the latest in a series of such marches, highlighting growing unrest among farming communities.

What the Trade Deal Means

The proposed India-U.S. trade deal has been under negotiation for months. It aims to reduce tariffs and increase market access for goods. Farmer unions, however, fear that agricultural imports from the U.S. — including dairy, pulses, and edible oils — will undercut local produce.

Indian farmers already face challenges from low incomes and rising input costs. The protest reflects deeper anxieties about globalisation and its impact on small-scale agriculture. The government has not yet commented on the specifics of the deal.

What happens next depends on the promised meeting between farmer leaders and officials. If no resolution emerges, unions have warned of a larger march on Delhi in the coming weeks.
