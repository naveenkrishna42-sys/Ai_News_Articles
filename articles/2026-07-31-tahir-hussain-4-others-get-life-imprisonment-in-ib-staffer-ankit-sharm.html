Tahir Hussain, 4 Others Get Life Imprisonment in IB Staffer Ankit Sharma Murder Case

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tahir Hussain and four others sentenced to life imprisonment
  • Case relates to murder of Intelligence Bureau staffer Ankit Sharma during 2020 Delhi riots
  • Victim's brother said the convicts should have received the death penalty

Court Pronounces Sentence in 2020 Riots Murder Case

A Delhi court on Friday sentenced former Aam Aadmi Party councillor Tahir Hussain and four others to life imprisonment in the murder case of Intelligence Bureau staffer Ankit Sharma. The violence took place during the northeast Delhi riots in February 2020.

The court found the accused guilty of killing Sharma, whose body was dragged through the streets during the communal clashes. The quantum of punishment was pronounced after the prosecution argued for the harshest penalty under law.

Victim's Family Demands Death Penalty

Ankit Sharma's brother, reacting to the verdict, said the convicts "should have got death penalty." He expressed disappointment that the court stopped short of capital punishment, given the brutal nature of the crime.

Sharma, who worked with the Intelligence Bureau, had gone missing during the riots. His body was recovered from a drain in the Chand Bagh area, bearing multiple stab wounds. The family has maintained that the murder was premeditated and linked to the mob violence that engulfed the region.

Prosecution's Case and Evidence

The prosecution relied on eyewitness testimony, mobile phone records, and forensic evidence to establish the role of Hussain and the other accused in the killing. It argued that the victim was targeted because of his affiliation with the intelligence agency.

During the trial, the defence claimed that the accused were falsely implicated and that there was no direct evidence linking them to the murder. The court, however, found the prosecution's case credible and convicted all five accused.

Background of the 2020 Delhi Riots

The northeast Delhi riots, which erupted in February 2020, resulted in over 50 deaths and hundreds of injuries. The violence was triggered by clashes between supporters and opponents of the Citizenship Amendment Act. Several cases related to the riots are still pending in courts.

Tahir Hussain, a former municipal councillor, was also an accused in other riot-related cases, including the murder of Delhi Police constable Ratan Lal. He had been in judicial custody since March 2020.

Legal Implications and Reactions

Legal experts note that life imprisonment in India typically means imprisonment for the remainder of the convict's natural life, though remission is possible. The court's decision sends a strong signal that those involved in targeted killings during communal violence will face severe consequences.

Meanwhile, human rights groups have called for a fair trial in all riot-related cases, urging authorities to ensure that justice is delivered without bias. The verdict has also reignited debates about accountability for the 2020 riots.

Political reactions have been divided, with some leaders demanding stricter punishment and others questioning the judicial process. The convicts have the option to appeal against the sentence in a higher court.

As the legal process moves forward, all eyes will be on the appellate courts to see if the sentence is upheld or modified. The case remains a crucial benchmark for how India's judiciary handles communal violence cases.
