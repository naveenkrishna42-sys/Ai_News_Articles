Fold phone wars: Samsung battles Chinese rivals as best models reviewed

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 faces competition from Chinese copycats and the rumored foldable iPhone
  • Samsung is pushing AI features across three new foldables before Apple enters the market
  • Common complaints about foldable phones include hinge issues and screen durability, though hinges have improved
  • The real problem with foldable phones is said to be something beyond the hinge mechanism

The foldable phone market is heating up like never before. Samsung, the pioneer in this space, is now fighting off a wave of Chinese competitors while also keeping an eye on Apple's rumored entry. The battle lines are drawn, and consumers are reaping the benefits.

Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 vs Chinese rivals

Samsung's Galaxy Z Fold 8 is the company's latest flagship foldable. It arrives as Chinese brands like Huawei, Xiaomi, and Oppo have launched their own devices with similar form factors. These rivals often undercut Samsung on price while offering comparable or even superior specifications in some areas.

Reviews indicate that the Galaxy Z Fold 8 refines the formula but doesn't reinvent it. The device features a larger cover display, improved cameras, and a more durable hinge. However, Chinese competitors have brought their own innovations, such as thinner designs and faster charging. The question many buyers are asking is whether Samsung's brand trust and software support justify the premium price.

Chinese copycats are getting closer

Chinese manufacturers have moved beyond mere imitation. Devices like the Huawei Mate X series and Oppo Find N series now offer unique hinge mechanisms that reduce crease visibility. Some even feature self-developed bending glass that rivals Samsung's Ultra Thin Glass.

These phones are often sold at prices significantly lower than Samsung's offerings. In markets like India and Southeast Asia, this price difference matters. Samsung is responding by bundling aggressive trade-in offers and extended warranties to retain customers.

Apple's foldable iPhone looms large

The biggest wild card in the foldable phone war is Apple. Rumors of a foldable iPhone have circulated for years, but recent reports suggest the device could launch as early as next year. Some analysts believe the foldable iPhone could knock the Galaxy Z Fold 8 out of contention even before Samsung's next premiere.

Apple's entry would bring its massive ecosystem and loyal user base. If Apple delivers a polished foldable experience, it could redefine the market just as the original iPhone did. Samsung is not waiting idly. The company is pushing AI features hard, introducing three new foldables before Apple even enters the market.

AI is Samsung's new weapon

Samsung is betting big on artificial intelligence to differentiate its foldables. The Galaxy Z Fold 8 comes with Galaxy AI, a suite of on-device and cloud-based tools. These include real-time translation during calls, smart photo editing that can remove objects, and an AI assistant that learns user habits.

Chinese rivals are also investing in AI, but Samsung claims its deep integration with Google's Gemini model gives it an edge. The company hopes that AI features will become a reason to buy a Samsung foldable instead of a cheaper alternative.

The real problem with foldable phones

Despite years of development, foldable phones still face user complaints. Hinge issues and screen durability have been top concerns, but manufacturers claim they have largely solved these problems. The real problem, according to some experts, is something else entirely: software optimization.

Many apps still don't adapt well to foldable screens. Split-screen multitasking can be clunky, and some apps simply stretch or crop content instead of using the extra space intelligently. Until developers fully embrace foldable displays, even the best hardware will feel incomplete.

Other common complaints include battery life and the thickness of folded devices. While Chinese brands have made strides in reducing thickness, Samsung's foldables remain relatively bulky. Camera quality also trails behind traditional flagship phones in some cases.

The foldable phone war is far from over. Samsung is fighting to defend its lead, Chinese brands are nipping at its heels, and Apple is waiting in the wings. The next year will determine whether foldables become mainstream or remain a niche for early adopters. What to watch: How Apple's entry reshapes pricing and software standards across the industry.
