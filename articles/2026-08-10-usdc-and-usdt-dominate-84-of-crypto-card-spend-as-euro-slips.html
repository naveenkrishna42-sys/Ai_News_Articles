USDC and USDT Dominate 84% of Crypto Card Spend as Euro Slips

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • USDC and USDT together account for 84% of all crypto-linked card spending globally.
  • The euro's share of crypto card transactions has declined, reflecting weaker demand for euro-backed stablecoins.
  • The trend underscores the growing dominance of dollar-pegged stablecoins in everyday digital payments.

The Dollar-Pegged Duo Tightens Its Grip

Two dollar-backed stablecoins now account for a staggering 84% of all crypto card spending worldwide, according to fresh data. USDC and USDT, the industry's largest stablecoins, have consolidated their lead as the euro's presence in crypto payments fades.

The figures, released over the weekend, show a decisive shift. Just a year ago, the pair controlled roughly three-quarters of the market. Now, nearly every crypto-linked card transaction—whether for coffee, groceries, or online subscriptions—flows through one of these two tokens.

Why the Euro Is Losing Ground

The euro's retreat is notable but not sudden. Euro-backed stablecoins, such as EURT and EURC, have struggled to gain traction outside select European markets. Liquidity remains thin, and merchant adoption lags far behind dollar-pegged alternatives.

Analysts point to network effects. USDT and USDC are accepted on virtually every major exchange and payment gateway. Euro stablecoins, by contrast, often require extra conversion steps, adding friction that card issuers and users are unwilling to tolerate.

Regulatory uncertainty in the European Union has also played a role. While the bloc's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) framework aims to provide clarity, its phased rollout has left some issuers hesitant to push euro-pegged products aggressively.

Card Spend: The New Battleground

Crypto cards—debit and credit cards that let users spend digital assets directly—have become a crucial bridge between blockchain and everyday commerce. Visa and Mastercard have both expanded crypto card programmes, and the stablecoin share of that spending has become a key metric.

The data suggests that users are not just holding stablecoins as a store of value. They are actively spending them. This marks a maturation of the market, where crypto moves beyond speculation into routine transactions.

For card issuers, the implications are clear. Supporting USDT and USDC is no longer optional; it is table stakes. The euro's decline, meanwhile, may force European fintechs to reconsider their stablecoin strategies.

What This Means for Merchants and Users

For merchants, the dominance of dollar-pegged stablecoins simplifies integration. Instead of juggling multiple settlement currencies, they can accept one or two tokens and reduce conversion costs. For users, it means faster, cheaper cross-border payments, especially in regions with unstable local currencies.

But the concentration also carries risks. A single point of failure—whether a technical glitch or a regulatory crackdown on one of the two issuers—could disrupt a significant slice of crypto-enabled commerce. Regulators worldwide are watching closely, and some have already signalled intent to scrutinise stablecoin reserves more tightly.

The Road Ahead

As the euro's share continues to shrink, the question is whether any challenger can emerge. Central bank digital currencies, or CBDCs, remain a distant prospect for most major economies, and private-sector alternatives have yet to gain meaningful momentum.

For now, the dollar's stablecoin duopoly looks unassailable. The next few quarters will show whether European regulators and issuers can mount a credible response—or whether the retreat becomes a rout.
