RBI draft curbs may hit NBFC flexi loans: Morgan Stanley

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's draft norms on revolving credit facilities could impact flexi loans offered by NBFCs.
  • Morgan Stanley flags potential tightening of credit availability and higher costs for borrowers.
  • The draft rules are part of RBI's broader efforts to curb risky lending practices.

The Reserve Bank of India's draft restrictions on revolving credit could squeeze the flexi loan market offered by non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), according to a note from Morgan Stanley. The global brokerage warned that the proposed norms, aimed at curbing unchecked lending, may force NBFCs to recalibrate their product structures and pricing.

What the draft says

The RBI's draft framework, released last month, seeks to impose tighter limits on revolving credit lines, including those linked to credit cards and overdraft facilities. Under the proposed rules, lenders would need to reassess how they calculate credit limits and manage undrawn portions of such facilities. For NBFCs, which have aggressively marketed flexi loans—where borrowers can draw, repay, and redraw funds within a sanctioned limit—the changes could be significant.

Morgan Stanley's analysts noted that flexi loans, a popular tool for small businesses and individual borrowers, rely heavily on revolving credit structures. The draft norms could prompt NBFCs to either reduce such offerings or pass on higher costs to customers, potentially dampening demand.

Impact on borrowers and lenders

For borrowers, the curbs may translate into stricter eligibility criteria and reduced flexibility in managing cash flows. Many small entrepreneurs use flexi loans to bridge working capital gaps, and any tightening could force them to seek costlier alternatives. For lenders, the draft rules could increase compliance burdens and alter risk-weight calculations, impacting profitability.

Morgan Stanley highlighted that NBFCs, which have grown their flexi loan books rapidly in recent years, are particularly exposed. The brokerage suggested that while the intent of the RBI is to prevent over-leveraging, the implementation could have unintended consequences for credit growth in the informal and semi-formal sectors.

Industry response and next steps

The RBI has invited stakeholder comments on the draft, and industry bodies are expected to push back on certain provisions, arguing that flexi loans serve a critical role in financial inclusion. Some NBFC executives have privately expressed concerns that the norms could stifle innovation in credit delivery, though they acknowledged the need for prudent regulation.

Analysts are watching whether the final rules will include carve-outs for smaller-ticket loans or for NBFCs with lower risk profiles. The central bank has not yet set a timeline for finalising the framework, but market participants anticipate clarity in the coming months.

As the consultation process unfolds, lenders are likely to hold back on launching new flexi loan products. The immediate focus will be on how the RBI balances its oversight objectives with the credit needs of a growing economy.
