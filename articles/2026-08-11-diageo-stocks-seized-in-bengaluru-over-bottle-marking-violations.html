Diageo stocks seized in Bengaluru over bottle marking violations

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Inspectors seized around 18,000 liquor boxes from a Diageo facility in Bengaluru.
  • The action follows alleged violations of food safety norms related to bottle markings.
  • Diageo, owner of Johnnie Walker and Smirnoff, is cooperating with authorities.

In a significant regulatory action, food safety inspectors in Bengaluru have seized thousands of bottles of whisky and vodka manufactured by Diageo India, the local arm of the global spirits giant. The crackdown, which took place earlier this week, resulted in the confiscation of approximately 18,000 liquor boxes from the company's facility in the city.

The seizure is linked to alleged violations of food safety norms concerning bottle markings. Officials familiar with the matter said the markings did not conform to prescribed standards, though the specific nature of the discrepancy has not been publicly detailed.

What prompted the crackdown

The operation was carried out by the Karnataka food safety department following a tip-off. Inspectors descended on the Diageo premises and found what they described as irregularities in the labelling and marking of the products.

The value of the seized stock is estimated to be around US$1.6 million, according to industry sources. The products include some of Diageo's most recognised brands, such as Johnnie Walker whisky and Smirnoff vodka, which have a strong presence in the Indian market.

A senior official from the food safety department said the action was taken under the Food Safety and Standards Act, which mandates strict compliance on packaging and labelling. The official declined to comment on whether the issue posed any health risk, stating that the investigation was ongoing.

Diageo's response

Diageo India has acknowledged the development and stated that it is cooperating fully with the authorities. In a brief statement, the company said it was reviewing the matter and would take appropriate corrective measures.

The company has not yet commented on the specific allegations or the potential financial impact of the seizure. Legal experts suggest that if violations are proven, Diageo could face fines or further regulatory action, though the immediate concern is the fate of the seized inventory.

This is not the first time a major spirits manufacturer has come under scrutiny in India for labelling issues. The country's food safety regulations are stringent, and companies often face challenges in aligning global packaging standards with local requirements.

Market implications

Diageo is one of the largest spirits companies operating in India, with a portfolio that includes popular brands like Antiquity Blue and McDowell's. The company has been focusing on premiumisation in the Indian market, and any regulatory setback could affect its operations in the region.

Industry analysts note that while the seizure represents a logistical and reputational hiccup, it is unlikely to have a lasting impact on Diageo's overall business in India unless further violations are uncovered. The company's shares have remained stable, indicating that investors are treating the incident as a minor issue for now.

The Bengaluru facility is a key production hub for Diageo's domestic and export operations. Any disruption in its functioning could lead to supply chain delays, although the company has not indicated any such impact so far.

What happens next

The food safety department is expected to submit a detailed report to the state government in the coming days. Based on the findings, authorities may issue a formal notice to Diageo or initiate legal proceedings.

Diageo has a history of resolving regulatory matters through dialogue, and industry watchers expect the company to work towards a swift settlement. The seized stock will remain in custody until the investigation concludes.

Observers will be watching how the Karnataka government handles this case, as it could set a precedent for how food safety norms are enforced against large multinational corporations in India. For now, the focus remains on ensuring compliance and safeguarding consumer interests.
