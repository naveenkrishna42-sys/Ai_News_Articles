Iran says Oman framework agreed for Strait of Hormuz, US ships in crosshairs

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iranian official says a framework for the Strait of Hormuz has been agreed with Oman.
  • Iranian parliamentary committee is reviewing a draft bill to bar US and Israeli ships from the strait.
  • The move is seen as an attempt to push US influence out of the Gulf region.

Framework with Oman announced

An Iranian official has said a framework for the Strait of Hormuz has been agreed with Oman. The announcement came amid heightened tensions in the Gulf, with Tehran seeking to reshape the region's maritime security dynamics. The details of the framework have not been fully disclosed, but it is expected to cover navigation protocols and regional cooperation.

The official did not specify the timeline for implementation or the exact terms. Analysts note that any agreement with Oman could carry weight, given Muscat's history of acting as a mediator in the region.

Parliamentary move against US and Israeli ships

Meanwhile, Iran's parliamentary committee is reviewing a draft bill that would bar US and Israeli ships from passing through the strait. The state-linked Fars news agency reported the development, confirming that the bill is under active consideration. If enacted, it would mark a significant escalation in Iran's standoff with Washington and its allies.

The draft bill has been framed as a response to what Tehran describes as provocative US naval presence in the Gulf. However, experts caution that implementing such a ban could trigger a direct confrontation, given that the strait is a critical conduit for global oil shipments.

Potential impact on oil markets

The Strait of Hormuz handles a substantial share of the world's oil trade, making any threat to its security a flashpoint for global markets. A formal ban on US and Israeli vessels would likely draw an immediate response from the US Navy, which has patrolled the waters for decades. Shipping companies and insurers are already factoring in the risks, with war-risk premiums expected to rise if tensions escalate further.

Iran has previously threatened to close the strait but has never followed through, largely due to its dependence on the waterway for its own exports. This time, however, the parliamentary push suggests a more systematic approach.

Regional and international reaction

Oman's role as a potential intermediary has been welcomed by some regional players, but the broader Gulf states have remained cautious. Saudi Arabia and the UAE have not yet commented publicly on the framework. Washington has not issued an official response to the Iranian assertion, but US officials have historically maintained that freedom of navigation in the strait is a red line.

Israeli officials have also stayed silent, though any move to block its ships would likely be seen as a direct provocation. The international community is watching closely, with the UN and maritime bodies urging restraint.

What to watch next

The immediate focus will be on whether the Iranian parliament passes the draft bill and whether the Oman framework translates into concrete actions. Observers will also look for signals from Washington on how it plans to respond to Tehran's growing assertiveness in the Gulf.

As talks continue, the risk of miscalculation remains high. The coming weeks could determine whether this is a diplomatic opening or the prelude to a deeper crisis.
