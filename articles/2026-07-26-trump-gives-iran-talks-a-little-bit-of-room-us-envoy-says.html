Trump gives Iran talks ‘a little bit of room,’ US envoy says

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US envoy says Trump is allowing ‘a little bit of room’ for Iran talks
  • American airstrikes paused for second night after advisers raise concerns
  • Iran says it will halt retaliatory strikes as long as US bombing pause holds
  • Trump reportedly advised to halt bombing to de-escalate tensions

The United States ambassador to the United Nations has said President Donald Trump is giving negotiations with Iran “a little bit of room,” signalling a possible diplomatic opening after days of escalating military exchanges.

The remark, reported by multiple international outlets, comes as American airstrikes on Iranian positions have been paused for a second consecutive night. The Trump administration has not formally announced a ceasefire, but the halt in bombing appears deliberate.

Advisers urge restraint

According to The New York Times, Trump held off on a major war escalation after several of his senior advisers raised concerns about the risks of a broader conflict. The White House was reportedly advised to halt the bombing campaign to create space for talks.

The Guardian quoted the US ambassador as saying the pause was intended to allow negotiations to proceed. “The president is giving talks a little bit of room,” she said.

Tehran’s conditional response

Iran has responded by saying it will stop its own retaliatory strikes as long as the American bombing pause holds. That condition, reported by Reuters, suggests Tehran is willing to test the diplomatic channel but remains wary of US intentions.

The exact nature of any proposed talks remains unclear. Neither Washington nor Tehran has confirmed a venue, agenda or timeline for negotiations. Officials have not yet confirmed whether any backchannel communication has taken place.

What led to the pause

The US military had been conducting strikes against Iranian-linked targets for several days, following attacks on American personnel and assets in the region. The operations were described as defensive, but they drew sharp criticism from some allies and lawmakers who warned of an uncontrolled spiral.

Trump’s decision to pause the bombing, even temporarily, represents a shift from his earlier posture. The BBC reported that the US ambassador explicitly linked the halt to creating an opening for diplomacy.

“A little bit of room” is not a formal ceasefire or a detailed peace plan. It is a narrow window, and both sides are watching each other closely. Iran has shown it can match escalation, but it has also signalled it prefers de-escalation — on its own terms.

The coming days will test whether both sides can turn a pause into a process. No direct talks have been announced, but the silence of the bombs is, for now, the loudest signal of all.
