Karnataka bans sale of gutka, pan masala with tobacco for one year

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • One-year ban on sale and distribution of gutka, pan masala, and tobacco/nicotine products in Karnataka.
  • Order issued under food safety regulations; aims to reduce oral cancer and addiction.
  • Enforcement begins immediately; violators face penalties under the Food Safety and Standards Act.

Karnataka imposes year-long ban on tobacco products

The Karnataka government has banned the sale, distribution, and storage of gutka, pan masala, and all tobacco or nicotine-containing products for one year. The order, issued under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, takes effect immediately across the state.

Officials said the decision follows rising concerns over oral cancer and addiction, particularly among youth. The ban covers all forms of chewable tobacco, flavoured pan masala with nicotine, and similar products sold in shops, online platforms, or through vending machines.

What exactly is banned

The prohibition applies to gutka, pan masala, and any product containing tobacco or nicotine as ingredients. This includes sachets, pouches, and loose retail sales. Wholesale distributors and retailers have been directed to halt supplies and clear existing stock within a stipulated timeframe.

Health department sources said the ban is not a complete prohibition on all pan masala—only those with tobacco or nicotine. Plain pan masala without these substances remains legal, but manufacturers must ensure compliance with labelling rules.

Why the state acted now

Karnataka has witnessed a steady rise in tobacco-related ailments, with hospitals reporting more cases of oral submucous fibrosis—a precancerous condition—among young adults. The state's cancer registry data, though not yet public, reportedly shows a worrying trend, prompting the health department to push for this administrative measure.

The ban also aligns with the中央 government's broader tobacco control efforts under the National Tobacco Control Programme. Karnataka joins several other states that have imposed similar temporary bans, though enforcement has often been patchy.

Enforcement and penalties

Food safety officers and police have been authorised to conduct raids on shops, godowns, and transport vehicles. Violators face fines up to Rs 3 lakh and imprisonment under the food safety law, depending on the severity of the offence.

Officials admitted that enforcement will be challenging given the widespread availability of these products. They have urged citizens to report violations through the state's helpline, and district administrations have been told to form special squads for surprise checks.

Reactions and next steps

Public health experts have welcomed the move, calling it a "bold step" but cautioned that sustained awareness campaigns are needed to prevent a shift to other tobacco forms like bidi or cigarettes. The tobacco industry is likely to challenge the order in court, citing lack of legislative backing.

The ban will remain in force for one year, after which the government will review its impact. Officials said the decision on extension will depend on compliance data and health outcomes during this period.

For now, retailers across Karnataka have begun pulling these products from their shelves. The coming weeks will test the state's resolve to enforce the ban strictly, and whether public health takes precedence over commercial interests.
