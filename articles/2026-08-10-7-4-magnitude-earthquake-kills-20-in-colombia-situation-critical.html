7.4 Magnitude Earthquake Kills 20 in Colombia, Situation Critical

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 20 people killed after 7.4 magnitude earthquake jolts Colombia
  • Buildings collapse, triggering panic among residents in affected areas
  • Earthquake also felt in neighbouring Ecuador, prompting evacuations
  • Authorities describe situation as 'critical' as rescue efforts continue

A devastating 7.4 magnitude earthquake struck Colombia on Monday, killing at least 20 people and causing widespread destruction. The quake sent tremors across the region, with buildings collapsing and terrified residents rushing into the streets.

Authorities have described the situation as "critical," though a full assessment of the damage is still underway. Rescue teams are working through debris as aftershocks continue to rattle the affected areas.

Buildings Collapse, Residents Panic

Visuals emerging from the quake zone show crumpled structures and rubble-lined streets. The force of the tremor was strong enough to bring down buildings, trapping people inside and forcing many others to flee without belongings.

Panic gripped residents as the ground shook violently. In several towns, people gathered in open spaces, too afraid to return indoors. Witness accounts describe chaotic scenes, with dust clouds rising from collapsed structures and emergency sirens filling the air.

Ecuador Also Feels the Tremor

The earthquake's reach extended beyond Colombia's borders. In neighbouring Ecuador, the tremors prompted immediate evacuations as people poured out of offices and apartment blocks. No casualties have been reported there so far, but authorities remain on alert.

Seismologists have noted that the quake's magnitude places it among the strongest to hit the region in recent years. The epicentre's location, combined with building vulnerabilities in older structures, likely contributed to the severity of the damage.

Situation 'Critical', Rescue Operations Underway

Colombian officials have mobilised emergency response units to the worst-hit zones. Medical teams are on standby, and temporary shelters are being set up for those displaced by the quake. The military has been deployed to assist with search-and-rescue operations.

Communication lines in some areas remain disrupted, hampering relief efforts. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of injured, but reports suggest it could be significant. Aftershocks, some of them strong, have complicated the work of first responders.

Residents Spend Night Outdoors

With buildings deemed unsafe, many residents spent the night in the open. Parks and parking lots turned into makeshift camps as families huddled together, wary of returning home. The psychological toll is evident, with children and the elderly particularly affected.

Local authorities are conducting door-to-door checks in affected neighbourhoods to ensure no one is left trapped under debris. The full scale of the destruction is expected to become clearer in the coming hours as rescue teams reach isolated communities.

A Region Accustomed to Tremors

Colombia sits in a seismically active zone, and earthquakes are not uncommon. However, Monday's quake stands out for its intensity and the level of destruction it has caused. The last major quake of this scale in the region prompted significant rebuilding efforts, and experts are already calling for stricter enforcement of building codes.

For now, the focus remains on saving lives. International aid offers have begun to pour in, with neighbouring countries pledging support. The Colombian government has appealed for calm and urged citizens to follow safety protocols as aftershocks continue.

As rescue operations stretch into the night, the coming days will be critical. Authorities are expected to release updated casualty figures and damage assessments soon. The priority, officials say, is to account for the missing and provide relief to thousands left homeless by the disaster.
