Drone Strike Hits US-Owned LNG Tanker at Egypt's Damietta Port, Triggering Explosion

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • A drone strike hit a US-owned LNG tanker at Egypt's Damietta Port, causing an explosion.
  • A security firm called it a major escalation in the Middle East.
  • Egypt confirmed a fire at the port but did not mention a drone attack.
  • The incident raises tensions in a region already on edge.

A drone strike triggered an explosion on a US-owned liquefied natural gas (LNG) tanker at Egypt's Damietta Port on Wednesday, according to multiple reports. Security firms described the attack as a major escalation in the Middle East, though Egyptian authorities have so far confirmed only a fire at the port without attributing it to a drone.

The tanker, which was anchored at the facility, was hit in the late afternoon. Witnesses reported a loud blast followed by flames rising from the vessel. Emergency crews were deployed to contain the fire, but the full extent of damage remains unclear.

Security Firms Point to Drone Attack

Private security firms monitoring maritime traffic in the region said the explosion was caused by a drone strike. They noted that the vessel is US-owned, making it a potential target amid heightened tensions in the Middle East. The attack comes weeks after similar incidents involving commercial ships near strategic waterways.

One security analyst, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the strike could mark a dangerous shift. "Targeting an LNG tanker at a port is a significant step. It directly threatens energy infrastructure and could disrupt global supply chains," the analyst told Reuters.

Egypt's Cautious Response

Egyptian officials confirmed that a fire broke out at Damietta Port but stopped short of linking it to a drone attack. In a brief statement, the port authority said the blaze was under control and that there were no immediate reports of casualties. The omission has raised questions about whether Cairo is downplaying the incident to avoid inflaming regional tensions.

The Times of Israel noted that Egypt's statement made no mention of a drone, despite multiple international outlets reporting the attack. This discrepancy has led to speculation that Egypt may be treading carefully to avoid being drawn into a broader conflict.

Broader Middle East Tensions

The attack comes at a volatile time. The Middle East has seen a series of drone and missile strikes on commercial and military assets in recent months, with Iran-backed groups often blamed. The US has bolstered its naval presence in the region, but such incidents continue to test deterrence.

Al Jazeera reported that the fire erupted on ships at the port, though it did not specify if more than one vessel was affected. The LNG tanker, being US-owned, is likely seen as a symbol of American economic interests in the region. Analysts fear that if confirmed as a deliberate attack, it could provoke a strong response from Washington.

What Happens Next

Investigations are ongoing. Egyptian authorities are expected to release more details once the fire is fully extinguished and the site is secure. The US State Department has not issued an official comment yet, but the incident is likely to dominate diplomatic discussions in the coming days. The key question remains: who launched the drone, and will this spark a new cycle of retaliation in the Middle East?
