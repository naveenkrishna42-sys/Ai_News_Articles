Tunnel discovery at Golconda Fort sparks archaeological interest

📅 2026-08-21 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • A tunnel structure was unearthed near the Qutb Shahi tombs complex.
  • The site falls under the jurisdiction of the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI).
  • History enthusiasts and local authorities are awaiting a formal assessment.

A New Discovery at a Historic Landmark

A recent discovery near the 500-year-old Golconda Fort has captured the attention of history enthusiasts and local authorities alike. A structure, identified as a tunnel, was unearthed in the vicinity of the Qutb Shahi tombs complex, a site known for its deep historical roots and architectural significance.

The discovery has prompted immediate interest across Hyderabad. While local accounts of secret passages have circulated for generations, the emergence of a physical structure near the protected tombs provides a concrete basis for further investigation.

ASI to Conduct Formal Assessment

The Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) has been alerted to the find. Officials are expected to survey the site to determine the tunnel's origins, purpose, and structural integrity. Given the sensitive nature of the Qutb Shahi heritage site, the area is currently being monitored to prevent unauthorized access or damage to the findings.

The ASI has not yet released a timeline for its full examination. Experts will likely focus on mapping the tunnel's path and checking for connections to the main fort complex or other subterranean networks historically rumoured to exist in the region.

The Lore of Secret Passages

Hyderabad’s heritage sites are frequently the subject of speculation regarding hidden subterranean routes. The Golconda Fort, serving as the capital of the Qutb Shahi dynasty, was strategically built to withstand long sieges. Historical accounts often suggest that such forts utilized escape tunnels or ventilation shafts to maintain communication lines during wartime.

  • The site is located near the Qutb Shahi tombs.
  • The Golconda Fort dates back roughly 500 years.
  • Archaeological teams are preparing for a site visit.

Whether this specific discovery is a major military passage or a smaller drainage or utility conduit remains to be seen. Previous explorations of the fort have occasionally revealed brickwork and masonry that suggest a highly sophisticated engineering approach by the builders of the era.

Preservation and Future Study

The primary concern for heritage conservationists is the preservation of the surrounding soil and masonry. Any excavation or inspection must be performed without compromising the stability of the nearby tombs. The ASI is expected to follow strict protocols to ensure that the heritage status of the site is upheld throughout the investigation.

Official reports from the inspection are expected in the coming weeks, which will provide clarity on whether the tunnel will be open for further study or secured for long-term conservation.
