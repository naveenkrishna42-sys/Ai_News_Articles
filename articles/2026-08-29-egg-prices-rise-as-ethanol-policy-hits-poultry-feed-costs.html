Egg prices rise as ethanol policy hits poultry feed costs

📅 2026-08-29 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • E20 fuel policy is increasing ethanol demand, driving maize prices up
  • Higher feed costs are forcing poultry farmers to raise egg prices
  • Egg prices have jumped 40%, impacting consumer breakfast budgets

Ethanol demand surges, maize prices skyrocket

The Indian government's push for E20 ethanol-blended fuel has created a perfect storm for egg prices. Ethanol production requires vast amounts of maize, and the sudden surge in demand has sent maize prices soaring. According to ChiniMandi, maize prices have risen by 25% in the past year, largely due to the ethanol policy.

Poultry farmers face double whammy

Poultry farmers are feeling the pinch on two fronts. Not only are they dealing with higher maize prices, but soya prices have also surged, another key component of poultry feed. The Times of India reports that soya prices have risen by 30% in the past year. This double whammy is forcing farmers to increase egg prices to maintain profitability.

Egg prices jump 40%

Consumers are paying the price for these rising feed costs. Egg prices have jumped by 40% in the past year, according to BusinessLine. This is a significant increase that is directly impacting consumer breakfast budgets. The Economic Times notes that the rise in egg prices is part of a broader trend of increasing food prices in India.

Broader economic implications

The rise in egg prices is not an isolated incident. It is part of a broader trend of increasing food prices in India. The government's push for ethanol-blended fuel is a key driver of this trend. The policy is aimed at reducing India's dependence on fossil fuels, but it is having unintended consequences for food prices and consumer budgets.
