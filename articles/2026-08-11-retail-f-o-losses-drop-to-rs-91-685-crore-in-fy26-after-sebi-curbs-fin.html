Retail F&O losses drop to Rs 91,685 crore in FY26 after SEBI curbs: FinMin

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Retail losses in equity F&O segment declined to Rs 91,685 crore in FY26, an 18% fall year-on-year.
  • The Finance Ministry attributed the reduction to SEBI's regulatory curbs on derivatives trading.
  • The data underscores the continued high-risk nature of retail participation in equity derivatives.
  • SEBI's measures aimed at curbing speculative trading appear to have had a tangible impact on retail losses.

New Delhi: Retail investors in India's equity derivatives market lost Rs 91,685 crore in the financial year 2025-26, a sharp 18% decline from the previous year, according to data cited by the Finance Ministry. The reduction has been credited to a slew of regulatory measures introduced by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI).

The figures, reported by multiple financial news outlets, translate to roughly $9.6 billion in losses. While the quantum remains staggering, the year-on-year drop signals that tighter norms may be starting to cool speculative fervour in the futures and options (F&O) segment.

What drove the decline?

SEBI's interventions over the past year included increasing the minimum contract size, raising margin requirements, and curbing the number of weekly expiries. These steps were aimed squarely at reducing the churn of high-frequency, speculative trades that have been bleeding retail wallets.

Market participants note that the fall in losses is a direct consequence of reduced trading activity. The regulatory push has made it costlier and more cumbersome to take speculative positions, prompting a segment of retail traders to exit the market or scale down their exposure.

A persistent problem

Despite the improvement, the scale of losses underscores a persistent challenge. Industry estimates have long suggested that over 90% of retail F&O traders lose money, with a majority of them trading in options. The FY26 numbers, while lower, still paint a grim picture for household savings.

The Finance Ministry's acknowledgment of the data serves as a reminder that the problem is far from resolved. Policymakers have repeatedly flagged the risk of retail investors treating derivatives as a quick-money avenue, often at the cost of long-term financial security.

SEBI's tightening grip

SEBI's regulatory tightening has been one of the most aggressive in recent years. The market regulator has justified these measures as necessary to protect small investors from systemic risks and excessive leverage. Brokers and exchanges, however, have voiced concerns about the impact on trading volumes and market liquidity.

The latest data will likely bolster SEBI's stance. Officials have not yet commented on whether further curbs are planned, but the regulator is expected to continue monitoring the segment closely.

For now, the trend is encouraging for those advocating stricter oversight. But the sheer size of the losses indicates that regulatory fixes alone may not be enough. Financial literacy and investor education remain critical components of the solution, experts argue.

What to watch

All eyes will now be on the next edition of SEBI's consultation papers and any follow-up measures. The coming months will also reveal whether the decline in losses is a durable trend or a temporary blip driven by market conditions.
