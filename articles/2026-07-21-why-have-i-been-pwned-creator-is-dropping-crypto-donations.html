Why Have I Been Pwned Creator Is Dropping Crypto Donations

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Troy Hunt, founder of Have I Been Pwned, has stopped accepting cryptocurrency donations.
  • He cited regulatory compliance and tax reporting burdens as the primary reasons.
  • The decision reflects growing friction between privacy-focused tools and crypto financial systems.

Security Expert Walks Away From Crypto Donations

Troy Hunt, the Australian security researcher who runs the data breach notification service Have I Been Pwned, has decided to stop accepting cryptocurrency donations. Hunt announced the change on his blog, calling the compliance burden an “impossible requirement” for a small operation like his.

The service, which lets users check if their email addresses or passwords have been leaked in data breaches, relied partly on crypto donations from supporters. Hunt said the decision was not about the value of the donations but about the growing regulatory friction around digital assets.

Tax Reporting and Compliance Headaches

Hunt pointed to the complexity of reporting crypto transactions for tax purposes in Australia. He explained that every donation, regardless of size, triggered a taxable event that required careful tracking and calculation of capital gains or losses.

“The administrative overhead is simply not worth it,” Hunt wrote. “For a project that runs on a shoestring budget, spending hours on crypto tax paperwork doesn't make sense.” He added that the regulatory environment has become more stringent over the past year, with exchanges and wallet providers demanding more identity verification and reporting.

No Political Statement, Just Practical Reality

Hunt was careful to note that his decision should not be read as a criticism of cryptocurrency or its underlying technology. He remains a supporter of blockchain and privacy tools but acknowledged that the current regulatory framework creates friction for small-scale operators.

“I'm not anti-crypto. I'm anti-paperwork,” he said. The move reflects a broader trend where individual developers and small projects are reconsidering their relationship with digital assets as governments worldwide tighten oversight.

What Happens to Existing Donations

Hunt confirmed that all crypto donations received before the cutoff date have been converted to fiat currency. He will not be returning any funds but has stopped accepting new crypto contributions entirely.

The Have I Been Pwned service remains free and continues to accept donations via traditional methods like PayPal and bank transfers. Hunt said he hopes the change will let him focus on improving the site rather than wrestling with tax forms.

What to Watch

As more countries introduce crypto reporting rules, other small projects and individual creators may follow Hunt's lead. The tension between decentralised finance and centralised regulation is unlikely to ease anytime soon. For now, Have I Been Pwned users can still support the service — just not with Bitcoin or Ethereum.
