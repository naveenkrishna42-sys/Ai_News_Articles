Xi anchors Russia ties ahead of trilateral summit

Chinese President Xi Jinping has described relations with Russia as a ‘source of stability’ ahead of a planned trilateral meeting with US President Donald Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of the APEC summit, according to reports by The Hindu and Sputnik News. Xi’s remarks underscore Beijing’s deepening strategic alignment with Moscow, a partnership increasingly viewed as a counterweight to Western influence and a cornerstone of a multipolar world order.

Detailed Analysis: The Shifting Sands of Geopolitics

President Xi’s declaration comes at a pivotal moment in international relations, characterized by escalating trade tensions between the US and China, ongoing US sanctions against Russia, and a broader recalibration of global power dynamics. The trilateral meeting, if it proceeds, represents a rare direct engagement between the leaders of the world's three most powerful nations, each with complex and often adversarial relationships with the others.

China-Russia Strategic Alignment: A 'Source of Stability'

The phrase "source of stability" is particularly telling. For Beijing and Moscow, their bilateral relationship serves multiple strategic purposes:

  • Counterbalancing US Hegemony: Both nations advocate for a multipolar international system, seeking to dilute what they perceive as American unilateralism. Their cooperation on international platforms like the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) and within the BRICS framework reflects this shared vision.
  • Economic Synergy: Russia is a major energy supplier to China, while China’s Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) offers opportunities for infrastructure development across Eurasia, potentially integrating with Russia’s Eurasian Economic Union (EAEU). This economic partnership provides resilience against external pressures.
  • Security Cooperation: Joint military exercises, intelligence sharing, and arms deals between China and Russia have become more frequent and sophisticated, enhancing their collective defense capabilities and signaling a united front on regional security issues.
  • Shared Ideological Ground: Both countries emphasize state sovereignty, non-interference in internal affairs, and a more controlled approach to internet governance, contrasting with Western liberal democratic norms.

For Xi, anchoring ties with Russia provides a strategic advantage going into any discussions with President Trump, allowing China to negotiate from a position of enhanced geopolitical leverage rather than isolation.

The APEC Trilateral Summit: High Stakes Diplomacy

The prospect of a trilateral meeting among Xi, Trump, and Putin carries immense significance. Each leader arrives with distinct objectives:

  • Donald Trump: Likely seeking to address trade imbalances with China, discuss denuclearization with North Korea (where both China and Russia play roles), and potentially explore avenues to de-escalate tensions with Russia while maintaining pressure on its geopolitical actions. Trump's transactional approach could see him attempting to play one power against the other.
  • Xi Jinping: Aiming to stabilize the US-China trade relationship, assert China's growing influence in Asia-Pacific, and demonstrate the strength of the China-Russia partnership as a bulwark against US pressure.
  • Vladimir Putin: Focused on easing international sanctions, reasserting Russia's role as a global power broker, and demonstrating that Russia is not isolated despite Western efforts. His presence alongside Xi reinforces the Sino-Russian axis.

The summit could be a crucible for testing the limits of cooperation and competition among these global titans, with potential implications for trade, security, and the future of international governance.
