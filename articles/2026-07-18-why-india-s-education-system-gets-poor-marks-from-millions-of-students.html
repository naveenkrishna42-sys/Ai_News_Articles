Why India’s education system gets poor marks from millions of students

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's education system faces criticism for intense exam pressure and rote learning.
  • Outdated curricula and lack of skills training are key concerns for students.
  • Unequal access to quality education remains a major challenge across states and income groups.

India's education system, one of the largest in the world, is failing to impress the very people it is meant to serve. A recent report by the Financial Times has highlighted growing discontent among millions of students who say the system is outdated, stressful, and unequal. The findings come as the country grapples with a demographic dividend that could turn into a liability if young people are not adequately prepared for the future.

Rote learning and exam pressure top complaints

Students across India consistently rank exam-centric learning as their biggest grievance. The system rewards memorisation over understanding, leaving little room for creativity or critical thinking. The annual board exams, particularly for Classes 10 and 12, are high-stakes affairs that determine college admissions and career paths.

This pressure cooker environment takes a toll on mental health. Reports of student anxiety and even suicides during exam season are not uncommon. Many students told the FT that they feel their potential is reduced to a single exam score, which they believe is an unfair and incomplete measure of their abilities.

Curricula out of sync with modern needs

Another major point of criticism is the outdated curriculum. While the National Education Policy 2020 promised reforms, implementation has been slow and uneven. Subjects like coding, financial literacy, and environmental science remain elective or poorly integrated in many schools.

Vocational training is often seen as a second-class option rather than a viable career path. This disconnect between what is taught and what the job market demands leaves many graduates underemployed. The FT report notes that a significant number of engineering graduates, for instance, lack the skills required for entry-level tech jobs.

Unequal access deepens the divide

The quality of education varies wildly between states and between urban and rural areas. Private schools in cities often offer better infrastructure, trained teachers, and English-medium instruction. In contrast, government schools in rural India struggle with basic amenities like functional toilets, electricity, and adequate classrooms.

This inequality reinforces existing social and economic divides. Students from poorer families, especially in villages, are far less likely to access quality higher education or competitive coaching. The report underscores that while India produces millions of graduates each year, a large fraction are not job-ready, limiting their ability to break the cycle of poverty.

The digital divide

The pandemic-induced shift to online learning exposed a stark digital divide. While affluent students adapted to Zoom classes and online resources, millions from low-income households were left out due to lack of devices or internet access. Even today, many schools have not fully addressed this gap, leaving a generation of students behind.

Government initiatives like DIKSHA and SWAYAM offer digital resources, but awareness and accessibility remain low. The FT report notes that without targeted investment in digital infrastructure in rural areas, the gap will only widen.

What happens next

The Indian government has acknowledged these shortcomings and is pushing for reforms under the National Education Policy. But translating policy into practice on the ground will require massive funding, teacher training, and political will. As millions of young Indians enter the workforce each year, the pressure to modernise the education system is only going to intensify. All eyes will be on how the next budget allocates resources for education and whether states can deliver on the promise of a more equitable and skill-based system.
