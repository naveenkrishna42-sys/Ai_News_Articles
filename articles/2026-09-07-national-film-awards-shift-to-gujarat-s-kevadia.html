Historic Shift of the National Film Awards

The National Film Awards will be held in Kevadia, Gujarat, for the first time in 72 years, ending a long‑standing tradition of hosting the ceremony in Delhi. According to Deccan Herald, the decision reflects a strategic push to decentralise premier cultural events across India. The ceremony is scheduled for 22 September 2026, coinciding with the peak tourist season around the Statue of Unity.

Industry insiders note that the move aligns with the government’s broader narrative of “One India, One Culture.” The shift also offers a platform to showcase Gujarat’s emerging arts infrastructure, potentially inspiring regional filmmakers to seek recognition beyond the capital. This change could redefine the prestige equation for award contenders, who may now weigh venue accessibility against artistic merit.

Kevadia Venue: Capacity and Logistics

The Statue of Unity site provides an outdoor amphitheatre designed for up to 5,000 seated guests and an additional 10,000 standing attendees. The venue’s existing security apparatus, built for mass tourism, will be repurposed for the red‑carpet event, reducing the need for temporary infrastructure. However, the remote location in Narmada district introduces longer travel times for participants from southern and western states.

Road connectivity from major film hubs—Mumbai (≈350 km), Bangalore (≈800 km), and Chennai (≈950 km)—remains the primary concern. The Gujarat state government has pledged to deploy dedicated shuttle services, yet the reliance on road transport may affect scheduling and crew availability. Organisers must balance the symbolic value of the venue against the practical challenges of on‑time delivery.

Financial Implications for Stakeholders

Preliminary budget estimates place the event cost at ₹12‑15 crore, a modest increase compared to Delhi’s ₹10 crore outlay. The additional expense is attributed to upgraded lighting, acoustic systems, and security upgrades required for an outdoor setting. Film producers, who often sponsor segments, will need to allocate contingency funds for transportation and accommodation of cast and crew.

From a commercial perspective, the elevated visibility of the Statue of Unity can justify the premium. Sponsors gain exposure to a captive audience of domestic and international tourists, while the region’s hospitality sector anticipates a surge in bookings. However, the return on investment hinges on effective media coverage and live‑stream viewership, which must match or exceed previous editions.

Pros & Cons: Key Real‑World Highlights

  • Pros: Symbolic boost for Gujarat’s cultural profile; tourism revenue spike; state‑of‑the‑art outdoor infrastructure reduces need for temporary structures.
  • Cons: Longer travel logistics for participants; weather‑dependent outdoor setup; higher per‑attendee cost due to remote location.
  • Industry Insight: Filmmakers may need to budget extra for crew travel and accommodation, but the unique backdrop could enhance promotional material.
  • Economic Impact: Local hospitality, catering, and transport sectors expect a 30‑40% occupancy rise during the event week.

Industry Outlook and Strategic Considerations

The relocation of the National Film Awards to Kevadia sets a precedent for future cultural gatherings outside the capital. Organisers will need to refine risk management protocols, especially regarding climate contingencies and logistics coordination. For filmmakers, the decision underscores the importance of aligning award strategies with venue accessibility and budget constraints.

Stakeholders across the entertainment ecosystem—from producers to sponsors—should evaluate the long‑term benefits of associating with a high‑visibility, geographically distinct event. If managed effectively, the move could become a template for inclusive cultural programming that balances national prestige with regional development.
