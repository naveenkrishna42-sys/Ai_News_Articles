Why US paused bombing of Iran: Peace talks gain momentum

📅 2026-07-27 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US paused bombing of Iran for a third consecutive night to create space for peace negotiations.
  • The halt comes amid internal concerns from US advisers over the risks of a major war escalation.
  • Both sides have paused fighting to allow diplomatic channels to function, according to the US ambassador.

The United States has paused its bombing campaign against Iran for a third consecutive night, a move that appears to be driven by a combination of diplomatic necessity and internal caution within the Trump administration.

The halt in strikes, which follows days of intense bombardment of Iranian positions near the strategic Strait of Hormuz, marks a sharp departure from earlier threats of a major military escalation. US officials have indicated the pause is intended to give peace talks a chance to gain traction.

Peace talks take centre stage

The US ambassador to the region confirmed on Monday that both Washington and Tehran have paused offensive operations to allow negotiations to proceed. The ambassador described the pause as a deliberate effort to create space for diplomatic engagement.

According to reports, the talks are being facilitated through backchannel communications, with both sides expressing a willingness to explore a negotiated settlement. The exact location and format of the discussions have not been disclosed.

The development comes after the US launched what many analysts described as a limited but punishing aerial campaign against Iranian military infrastructure along the Gulf coast. The strikes were aimed at degrading Iran's ability to disrupt global oil shipments through the Hormuz strait.

Internal concerns over escalation

The New York Times reported that President Donald Trump held off on a major war escalation after several key advisers raised concerns about the long-term consequences of a full-scale conflict with Iran. The internal debate within the administration reportedly centred on the risks of being drawn into a protracted war in the Middle East.

Some advisers argued that a broader campaign could destabilise the region further and trigger retaliatory attacks on US allies and oil infrastructure. Others warned that the economic fallout from a sustained conflict could hurt Trump's domestic standing ahead of the next election cycle.

The decision to pause strikes suggests that, for now, the cautionary voices have prevailed over those advocating for a more aggressive posture.

What the pause means for the region

The lull in fighting has brought a measure of relief to global markets, which had been rattled by the prospect of a full-blown war between the two nations. Oil prices, which spiked sharply during the initial strikes, have stabilised in recent days.

On the ground in the Gulf region, the pause has allowed civilians in coastal areas to emerge from shelters and assess damage. Humanitarian agencies have begun preliminary assessments of the impact of the strikes, though detailed casualty figures remain unconfirmed.

Both the US and Iran have maintained a cautious tone in their official statements, avoiding inflammatory rhetoric that could derail the nascent talks.

It remains unclear how long the pause will hold, or whether the negotiations will yield a substantive agreement. The coming days will test whether both sides are genuinely prepared to step back from the brink, or whether the current calm is merely a tactical breather before the next round of escalation.
