Fact Check Confirms Delhi Police Used Nail-Studded Batons at Jantar Mantar

📅 2026-07-23 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Fact-checking organisations confirmed Delhi police used batons with nails during protests at Jantar Mantar.
  • Reports also document use of pellet guns and shock batons, causing severe injuries.
  • Delhi police have denied the allegations, calling video evidence 'fake'.
  • The incident has revived debate over use of controversial crowd-control weapons.

New Delhi: Multiple fact-checking organisations and news outlets have independently verified that Delhi Police personnel used batons embedded with nails against protesters at Jantar Mantar. The finding contradicts official denials from the police force, which had dismissed similar video evidence as manipulated.

The verification comes amid a wider controversy over the force's crowd-control methods, with reports also documenting the use of pellet guns and electric shock batons during the same protest.

Nail-studded batons confirmed in multiple checks

Newslaundry, in its fact-check, confirmed that the batons used by police at the protest site had nails protruding from them. The visual evidence, widely circulated on social media, was analysed frame-by-frame to establish its authenticity.

BOOM, another fact-checking outlet, reported that despite the clear video evidence, Delhi Police officials had called the footage 'fake' and denied any brutality. The organisation noted that the injuries visible on protesters were consistent with punctures from nail-studded objects.

Pellet guns and shock batons also used

The Hindu reported that pellet guns and shock batons were deployed against protesters at Jantar Mantar. Medical records cited by the publication showed severe pellet-like injuries on at least one youth protester.

TheWire.in documented cases of protesters suffering from injuries consistent with pellet gun fire. Police officials, however, have denied using pellet guns, claiming only standard crowd-control measures were employed.

The News Minute reported that these allegations have revived a long-standing debate over the use of controversial crowd-control weapons. Pellet guns, in particular, have been criticised for causing irreversible eye damage and blindness.

Police deny all allegations

Despite the mounting evidence from independent fact-checkers and media organisations, Delhi Police has maintained that its personnel acted within the law. In official statements, the force has dismissed the videos showing nail-studded batons as doctored or misleading.

Police officials have not yet commented on the specific findings of the fact-checks. They have also not confirmed whether any internal inquiry has been initiated into the use of such weapons.

The protests at Jantar Mantar were part of a larger demonstration, though the exact number of protesters and the range of injuries sustained remain unconfirmed by official sources.

What to watch next

The Delhi Police is expected to face further questions as more fact-checks and medical reports emerge. Civil rights groups are likely to demand a formal investigation into the use of nail-studded batons and pellet guns, potentially leading to court petitions or a review of crowd-control protocols.
