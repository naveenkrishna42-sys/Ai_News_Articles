Telangana Residents Stage Novel Protests Over Bad Roads

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Residents across Telangana are using creative protests to highlight poor road conditions.
  • Protests include planting saplings in potholes and staging mock funeral processions.
  • Local authorities have yet to respond, with no official timeline for repairs announced.

Hyderabad, Aug 9: Tired of waiting for authorities to fix pothole-riddled roads, residents across Telangana have turned to protest with a twist. Instead of banners and slogans, they are planting gardens in craters, holding mock funerals, and even painting artworks around gaping holes. The message is simple: the roads are killing us, literally.

From the outskirts of Hyderabad to district towns, the frustration has boiled over. Monsoon rains have worsened the situation, turning already damaged stretches into hazardous zones. Commuters report daily accidents, two-wheeler riders bearing the brunt of sudden pits hidden under water.

Gardens in Potholes

In one suburb, residents filled a deep pothole with soil and planted marigold saplings, complete with a small fence. They even installed a board that reads, "This is our garden now." The stunt went viral on social media, inspiring similar acts elsewhere.

Another group painted the edges of potholes with bright yellow stripes, turning them into improvised art installations. "If the municipality won't mark them, we will," said a local shopkeeper who helped organise the effort. The goal, he said, is to force officials to notice what they have ignored for months.

Mock Funerals and Roadblocks

In a village in Nizamabad district, residents staged a mock funeral procession, complete with a coffin draped in black, to symbolise the death of safe travel. They marched to the local panchayat office, demanding immediate repair work. Videos of the procession have been shared widely, drawing sympathy and laughter in equal measure.

Elsewhere, protesters have used old tires and wooden planks to create makeshift barricades, blocking traffic until an engineer arrives. Some have even placed chairs and tables in the middle of the road, offering chai to passing motorists while explaining their plight. These protests remain peaceful, but the anger underneath is real.

Authorities Yet to Respond

Officials have not yet issued a formal statement on the protests. While some local corporators have promised to escalate the issue, no timeline has been given for repairs. The state's road maintenance budget has been under strain, sources say, though this has not been officially confirmed.

Residents argue that they have filed multiple complaints through helplines and apps, but responses have been slow or nonexistent. "We are not against the government," said a protester in Sangareddy. "We just want roads that don't kill us."

What's Next

With the monsoon expected to continue for several more weeks, the problem is likely to worsen. Activists are planning a coordinated day of action across affected districts, though details remain sketchy. For now, the pothole gardens and painted craters serve as a colourful, defiant reminder that citizens will not be ignored.
