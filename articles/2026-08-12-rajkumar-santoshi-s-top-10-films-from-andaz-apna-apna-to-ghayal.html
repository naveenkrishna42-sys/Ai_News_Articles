Rajkumar Santoshi's Top 10 Films: From Andaz Apna Apna to Ghayal

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rajkumar Santoshi's filmography spans comedy, action, and drama.
  • Ghayal and Andaz Apna Apna are among his most iconic works.
  • The list includes critical and commercial successes from the 90s and 2000s.

A Director Who Defied Genres

Rajkumar Santoshi has never been easy to pin down. Over three decades, he has moved from slapstick comedy to hard-hitting action, from romantic drama to social commentary. His filmography is a masterclass in versatility, and Rediff's recent ranking of his top 10 films is a reminder of just how many classics he has delivered.

Santoshi burst onto the scene in the early 1990s with Ghayal, a film that redefined the action genre in Hindi cinema. It was raw, angry, and deeply rooted in the realities of urban India. The film won the National Film Award for Best Popular Film Providing Wholesome Entertainment, and it remains a benchmark for revenge dramas.

The Cult of Andaz Apna Apna

If Ghayal showcased Santoshi's serious side, Andaz Apna Apna (1994) revealed his comic genius. The film, starring Aamir Khan and Salman Khan, was initially a box-office disappointment. Over the years, however, it has achieved cult status, with dialogues and scenes now part of Indian pop culture folklore.

What makes Andaz Apna Apna so beloved is its sheer absurdity. The plot is a tangle of mistaken identities, petty rivalries, and a villain who can't help but announce his own plans. Santoshi's direction allowed the chaos to feel effortless, and the result is a film that fans quote endlessly, even three decades later.

From Ghayal to Ghatak: Action With a Conscience

Santoshi returned to action with Ghatak (1996), starring Sunny Deol. The film was another commercial success, but it was more than just punches and explosions. It told the story of a man who stands up against oppression, and its climax remains one of the most powerful in Hindi cinema.

Even in his lighter films, Santoshi never lost sight of moral clarity. In China Gate (1998), he assembled an ensemble cast for a heist drama inspired by The Magnificent Seven. The film was ambitious, if uneven, but it showed his willingness to experiment with scale and narrative structure.

The Lajja and Legend Connection

With Lajja (2001), Santoshi took a sharp turn into social drama. The film, featuring an ensemble of actresses including Madhuri Dixit and Manisha Koirala, tackled issues of gender inequality and violence against women. It was not a commercial hit, but it demonstrated Santoshi's commitment to stories that mattered.

His later works, like the Ajay Devgn-starrer Legend (2014), tried to recapture the magic of his earlier successes. While the film had its moments, it could not match the impact of his 90s output. Still, Santoshi's ability to command big stars and big budgets never waned.

The Top 10 List

Rediff's ranking places Ghayal and Andaz Apna Apna at the top, followed by other notable titles. The list includes films like Damini (1993), which gave Meenakshi Seshadri one of her most memorable roles, and the comedy-drama Pukar (2000), starring Anil Kapoor and Madhuri Dixit.

Also featured are the action entertainer The Legend of Bhagat Singh (2002), for which Santoshi won critical acclaim, and the family drama Family (2006). Each film, in its own way, reflects a different shade of Santoshi's storytelling—whether it's the righteous anger of Ghayal or the unabashed silliness of Andaz Apna Apna.

What stands out across his filmography is a strong sense of character. Santoshi's heroes are flawed, his villains are memorable, and his supporting cast is always given room to shine. He has a knack for creating moments that stay with you long after the credits roll.

What's Next for Santoshi?

Rajkumar Santoshi has been relatively quiet in recent years, with his last directorial venture being the 2018 film 2.0, a massive sci-fi spectacle starring Rajinikanth and Akshay Kumar. As of now, there is no official announcement about his next project. Fans will be watching closely to see if he can once again blend entertainment with meaning, as he did in his heyday.
