Central Hall: Modi Government and the Decay in Exams and Education System

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Modi government faces criticism over alleged irregularities in examination systems.
  • Concerns raised about the overall decline in education quality and governance.
  • Calls for accountability and systemic reforms in the education sector.

The State of Examinations Under Scrutiny

The Modi government has come under sharp criticism for what many describe as the rotting core of India's examination and education system. The latest episode of Central Hall, reported by TheWire.in, delves into the deep-seated issues that have plagued the sector, raising questions about governance and accountability.

Allegations of paper leaks, maladministration, and a lack of transparency have become recurring themes. The recent controversies surrounding the NEET-UG and UGC-NET exams have only amplified the concerns, leaving students and parents in distress.

Systemic Failures in Education Governance

Critics argue that the problems are not isolated incidents but symptoms of a larger systemic failure. The education system, once a pillar of India's aspirations, now grapples with outdated curricula, insufficient funding, and a focus on rote learning over critical thinking.

Experts point to the government's handling of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020 as a mixed bag. While the policy aims to overhaul the system, its implementation has been slow and uneven, with many states yet to adopt its key provisions.

Impact on Students and Future Generations

The consequences are stark. Students face immense pressure, with competitive exams becoming gateways to uncertainty rather than opportunity. The reliance on a single high-stakes exam for medical or engineering admissions has led to a cottage industry of coaching centres and, in some cases, fraudulent practices.

Parents and educators alike have called for a more holistic assessment model. The government's response has been largely reactive, focusing on damage control after each controversy rather than proactive reform.

Political and Administrative Accountability

The political leadership, including Prime Minister Narendra Modi, has defended the government's record, citing increased access to education and digital initiatives like DIKSHA. However, the repeated failures in examination management have eroded public trust.

Opposition parties have seized on the issue, demanding a parliamentary inquiry into the exam irregularities. The education ministry, for its part, has promised stricter oversight but has yet to outline a concrete action plan.

The question remains: can the system be fixed from within, or does it require a fundamental reset? The debate continues, with no easy answers in sight.

As the government pushes for 'New India', the rot in the education system serves as a stark reminder that reforms must go beyond rhetoric. The coming months will be crucial, with the next round of examinations looming and public patience wearing thin.
