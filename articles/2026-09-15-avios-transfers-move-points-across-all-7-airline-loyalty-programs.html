One Currency, Seven Programmes

Avios stopped being a single-airline currency years ago. Today it is shared across seven loyalty programmes: British Airways Executive Club, Qatar Airways Privilege Club, Iberia Plus, Aer Lingus AerClub, Vueling Club, LEVEL and the newer IAG Loyalty-linked partners. For members, this means the same Avios can be earned on a Dublin short hop and burned on a Doha-to-Mumbai Qsuite award — but only if you know which programme to hold it in.

The catch is that transfers are not always one-to-one. Each programme sets its own transfer ratio, minimum balance and account-age requirement, and those rules change more often than most travellers check.

How the Transfer Mechanics Actually Work

The three hub programmes

British Airways Executive Club, Qatar Airways Privilege Club and Iberia Plus act as the practical hubs. Linking accounts between these three is the most common route, and transfers in either direction are typically instant and free of a transfer fee. You log in, open the 'Combine My Avios' or 'Transfer Avios' tool, authenticate both accounts and confirm the amount.

Ratio traps and account-age rules

Transfers into Iberia Plus from BA are generally 1:1, but Iberia has historically applied different ratios when moving Avios out to BA. AerClub and Vueling transfers have also carried non-1:1 ratios at various points. Most programmes require both accounts to be open for a minimum period — commonly 30 to 90 days — before a transfer is permitted, and some block transfers if the account is under a minimum balance.

Should You Transfer at All?

  • Pros: Free movement between the three main hubs, instant transfers, and the ability to top up a programme that is short of an award threshold.
  • Pros: Qatar Avios frequently delivers better value per point on premium cabins than BA Avios on the same oneworld partner.
  • Cons: Non-1:1 ratios on AerClub and Vueling can quietly destroy 10 to 30 per cent of your balance.
  • Cons: Transferring resets your exposure to each programme's devaluation schedule, and you cannot move Avios back at the original ratio if rules change.
  • Cons: Some accounts need to be open for months before a transfer is allowed, which rules out last-minute award top-ups.

Practical Playbook for Indian Travellers

If your goal is a Qsuite redemption out of Bengaluru, Mumbai or Delhi, hold your Avios in Qatar Privilege Club. If you are chasing a BA Club World award to London, keep them in Executive Club. Only transfer when you are within striking distance of an award and the ratio is 1:1.

Check the account-age rule before you need the points, not the week you want to fly. And never transfer a large balance into AerClub or Vueling without first confirming the current ratio on the programme's own transfer page — third-party summaries go stale fast.

Used carefully, Avios remains one of the most flexible points currencies available to Indian cardholders. Used carelessly, it is a currency that quietly shrinks every time you move it.
