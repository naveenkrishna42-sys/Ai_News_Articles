Congress demands 25-year delimitation freeze

Congress Leader Demands 25-Year Freeze on Delimitation

Congress leader Mallikarjun Kharge has written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi, demanding a 25-year freeze on the delimitation process. Kharge, the Congress chief, has urged Modi to share the government's delimitation plan and maintain the strength of the Lok Sabha at 543 seats for the next 25 years.

Delimitation: A Delicate Balancing Act

The delimitation process, which involves redrawing parliamentary and assembly constituencies based on the latest census data, is a complex and contentious issue in India. The last delimitation exercise was conducted in 2008, and the next one is expected to be held in 2033. However, the government has been pushing for an early delimitation to align with the upcoming general elections.

Why 25 Years?

Kharge's demand for a 25-year freeze is a significant departure from the government's proposal. The Congress leader argues that a fixed number of Lok Sabha seats will provide stability and predictability to the political landscape. This will also help in maintaining the balance of power between the states and the Centre. However, the government has been advocating for a more flexible approach, which could lead to a change in the number of seats.

Broader Implications

The delimitation process has broader implications for the political landscape in India. A change in the number of seats could lead to a redistribution of political power, which could have a significant impact on the outcome of the general elections. The Congress party, which has been advocating for a fixed number of seats, is likely to benefit from this demand. However, the government's approach could lead to a more fluid political landscape, which could have its own set of challenges.

Expert Opinion

Political analyst Prashant Jha from the Centre for the Study of Developing Societies (CSDS) believes that Kharge's demand is a strategic move by the Congress to gain an advantage in the upcoming elections. Jha argues that a fixed number of seats will provide stability and predictability, which could be beneficial for the Congress party. However, he also warns that the government's approach could lead to a more fluid political landscape, which could be beneficial for the opposition parties.
