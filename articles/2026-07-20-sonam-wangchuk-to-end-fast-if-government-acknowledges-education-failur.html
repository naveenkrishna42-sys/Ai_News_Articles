Sonam Wangchuk to End Fast if Government Acknowledges Education Failures

📅 2026-07-20 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sonam Wangchuk is on a hunger strike in Delhi as part of the 'Chalo Sansad' protest.
  • He has set three conditions to end the fast, including government accountability for education failures.
  • The Delhi High Court has said the government was right in shifting Wangchuk to a hospital.

Climate activist and education reform advocate Sonam Wangchuk has said he is willing to end his ongoing hunger strike in Delhi today, provided the government takes accountability for what he describes as systemic failures in the education system. Speaking to reporters at the protest site, Wangchuk outlined three specific conditions under which he would break his fast.

Three Conditions for Ending the Fast

Wangchuk, who has been on a hunger strike since last week as part of the 'Chalo Sansad' march, made his demands clear. He wants the government to publicly acknowledge the shortcomings in the current education framework, particularly in rural and marginalised communities.

The activist also called for a concrete timeline to implement reforms that address the learning crisis in government schools. His third condition involves a commitment to revise the National Education Policy to make it more inclusive and accessible for all sections of society.

“I will end my fasting today if the government takes accountability for failures in the education system,” Wangchuk was quoted as saying by The Hindu.

Protests and Hospitalisation

The 'Chalo Sansad' protest, led by Wangchuk and several civil society groups, has drawn attention to the state of education in the country. The activist was recently shifted to a hospital by authorities, a move that the Delhi High Court later upheld, stating the government was right in its decision.

Wangchuk has alleged that his freedom of movement, speech, and communication are being restricted. “My freedom of movement, speech, all communication are restricted,” he said, according to a report in The Times of India.

The protest has seen participation from students, teachers, and activists who argue that the education system is failing to equip young Indians with the skills needed for the 21st century.

Government Response and Legal Scrutiny

The government, through its representatives, has not yet issued a formal response to Wangchuk's latest conditions. However, the Delhi High Court's observation that the shifting of Wangchuk to a hospital was justified has added a legal dimension to the standoff.

Sources indicate that the administration is cautious, wary of setting a precedent if it concedes to demands made during a protest. The court's backing of the government's action suggests that authorities are within their rights to ensure the activist's health is not compromised.

Meanwhile, supporters of Wangchuk have gathered at the protest site, with many vowing to continue the agitation until their demands are met. The police have maintained a presence but have not intervened forcefully so far.

What Happens Next

All eyes are now on the government's next move. If it chooses to engage with Wangchuk's conditions, the fast could end today. If not, the protest may escalate, with more activists expected to join the 'Chalo Sansad' march in the coming days.
