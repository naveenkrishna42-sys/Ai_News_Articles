Stock Market Holiday Announcement

The Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) and the National Stock Exchange (NSE) have confirmed a three‑day trading halt beginning Friday, 2 October 2026. The closure coincides with the national observance of Mahatma Gandhi Jayanti, a gazetted holiday across India. No trading sessions will be held on the exchange platforms during this period.

Details of the Three‑Day Break

Date and Reason

The holiday is triggered by Gandhi Jayanti on Friday, 2 October 2026. Since the exchanges are already closed on Saturdays and Sundays, the break extends through the weekend, giving investors a continuous non‑trading window from Friday evening to Monday morning.

Trading Schedule Impact

All equity, currency and derivative segments will observe the halt. The pre‑open session scheduled for Friday morning is cancelled, and the regular post‑close clearing and settlement processes are shifted to the next available trading day, Monday, 5 October 2026.

Implications for Traders and Investors

Settlement and Margin Adjustments

Trades executed on Thursday, 1 October will settle on the usual T+2 basis, landing on Monday, 5 October. Consequently, any intraday positions held overnight into Friday will carry forward margin requirements through the holiday, and brokers will typically block additional leverage on Thursday evening to mitigate risk.

Derivatives and Options Expiry

The monthly expiry for Nifty and Bank Nifty options contracts falls on the last Thursday of the month. In October 2026, the expiry is on Thursday, 30 October, so the early‑month holiday does not affect derivative expiry. However, traders holding weekly options that expire on Friday, 2 October must note that those contracts will be deemed expired at the close of Thursday’s session, with any intrinsic value settled in cash on the following trading day.

Historical Perspective

Similar three‑day pauses have occurred in past years when a national holiday fell on a Friday, such as the Gandhi Jayanti break in 2021 and the Republic Day holiday in 2020. Market analysts note that while the immediate aftermath can see a slight widening of bid‑ask spreads in low‑liquidity stocks, broader indices typically resume their pre‑holiday trend within one or two sessions.

What Market Participants Should Do

  • Review all open positions on Thursday, 1 October and consider reducing leverage to accommodate the extended margin hold.
