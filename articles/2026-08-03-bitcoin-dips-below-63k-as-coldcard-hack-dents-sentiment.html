Bitcoin dips below $63k as Coldcard hack dents sentiment

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin fell below $63,000, reversing earlier gains from U.S.-Iran de-escalation hopes.
  • A security breach at Coldcard, a popular hardware wallet maker, spooked investors.
  • Market analysts say the hack reignited concerns about crypto exchange and wallet security.

Bitcoin slips as Coldcard breach spooks market

Bitcoin edged lower on Monday, dropping below the $63,000 mark, as a security breach at hardware wallet maker Coldcard overshadowed tentative hopes of a thaw in U.S.-Iran relations. The digital asset, which had briefly rallied on geopolitical optimism, gave up those gains as traders refocused on the hack.

The exact nature of the Coldcard breach has not been fully disclosed, but initial reports suggest that the incident compromised user funds or data, triggering a wave of caution across the crypto community. Coldcard, known for its offline storage solutions, is a trusted name among security-conscious investors, making the news particularly jarring.

Geopolitical hopes fade into background

Earlier in the session, reports of potential diplomatic progress between Washington and Tehran had lifted risk sentiment, with Bitcoin briefly testing higher levels. However, the momentum proved short-lived. By mid-morning GMT, the cryptocurrency had reversed course, trading below the psychologically important $63,000 threshold.

Analysts noted that while geopolitical de-escalation typically supports risk assets, the Coldcard incident served as a stark reminder of the sector's vulnerabilities. "The market was already jittery after recent volatility," one trader said. "A hack like this hits at the core of what Bitcoin is supposed to be — a safe haven."

Security concerns weigh on investor confidence

The Coldcard breach is the latest in a string of security incidents that have plagued the crypto industry this year. From exchange hacks to wallet vulnerabilities, the threat of theft remains a persistent overhang. For many retail and institutional investors, hardware wallets are seen as the gold standard for security, so any compromise in that layer sends ripples across the market.

Coldcard has not yet issued a detailed statement on the extent of the damage, and officials have not confirmed whether funds were stolen or if the breach was limited to non-financial data. The lack of clarity has added to the unease, with some traders opting to reduce exposure until more information emerges.

Market reaction and immediate outlook

Bitcoin's slide below $63,000 has put key support levels in focus. If the cryptocurrency fails to reclaim this mark in the coming sessions, analysts say it could test lower ranges, potentially around $60,000. Conversely, a quick recovery would signal that the market views the Coldcard incident as an isolated event rather than a systemic problem.

Volume data shows modest selling pressure, but not panic. This suggests that while sentiment has soured, most investors are waiting for clearer signals before making major moves. The broader crypto market, including major altcoins, has largely followed Bitcoin's lead, with slight declines across the board.

What to watch next

Investors will be closely monitoring Coldcard's official response and any updates on the breach's scope. Additionally, any fresh developments in U.S.-Iran diplomacy could quickly shift focus back to geopolitical factors. For now, the market appears caught between two narratives — one of easing global tensions, and another of persistent security risks. How Bitcoin navigates this tug-of-war in the next 48 hours could set the tone for the week ahead.
