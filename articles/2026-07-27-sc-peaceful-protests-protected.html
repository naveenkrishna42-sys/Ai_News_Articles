SC: Peaceful Protests Protected

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Right to peaceful protest is constitutionally protected
  • Mere agitation cannot justify lathi-charge
  • Supreme Court to hear case on police excesses on July 28

Supreme Court Weighs In On Peaceful Protests

The Supreme Court has stated that the right to peaceful protest is constitutionally protected. Chief Justice Surya Kant made this remark while addressing a case related to a protest at Jantar Mantar. The court emphasized that mere agitation cannot justify the use of force by the police, such as lathi-charges.

Police Action Under Scrutiny

The Supreme Court has taken notice of the police action at Jantar Mantar and has fixed a hearing on the issue of police excesses for July 28. The court's decision comes after a protest against the NEET exam turned violent, leading to allegations of police brutality.

The court's stance is clear: every life matters, and the use of force by the police must be justified. The Supreme Court's remark is a significant one, as it highlights the importance of protecting the right to peaceful protest in a democratic society.

Implications Of The Court's Decision

The Supreme Court's decision is likely to have implications for future protests and demonstrations. It sends a clear message to the police that they must exercise restraint and respect the right to peaceful protest. The court's emphasis on the importance of protecting every life is also a reminder that the use of force by the police must be proportionate and justified.

What's Next

The Supreme Court will hear the case related to police excesses on July 28. The court's decision is expected to provide further clarity on the issue of peaceful protests and police action.
