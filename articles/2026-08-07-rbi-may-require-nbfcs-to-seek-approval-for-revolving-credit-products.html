RBI may require NBFCs to seek approval for revolving credit products

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI plans to mandate prior approval for NBFCs offering revolving credit products.
  • Move targets unsecured lending risks and aims to strengthen consumer protection.
  • Industry expects tighter compliance and possible product restructuring.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is planning to bar non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) from offering revolving credit facilities without obtaining prior regulatory approval. The proposed measure, reported by The Economic Times, is part of the central bank's efforts to tighten oversight of unsecured lending and consumer credit products.

Revolving credit lines, such as credit cards and overdraft facilities, allow borrowers to draw and repay funds repeatedly up to a set limit. While these products are common among banks, their proliferation among NBFCs has raised concerns about risk management and borrower over-leverage.

Why the central bank is stepping in

The RBI has been closely monitoring the rapid growth of unsecured retail lending, particularly in the digital lending space. NBFCs, which often operate with more flexibility than traditional banks, have expanded into revolving credit offerings, sometimes without adequate underwriting standards.

By requiring prior approval, the regulator aims to ensure that only entities with robust systems and clear risk frameworks offer such products. This move is expected to curb potential systemic risks and protect consumers from aggressive lending practices.

What this means for NBFCs

If implemented, the rule would force NBFCs to reassess their product portfolios. Several fintech-focused NBFCs have built their business models around instant credit lines, often linked to prepaid cards or wallets. These entities may need to pause new launches until they secure the necessary clearances.

Existing products are unlikely to be discontinued immediately, but the approval requirement could slow innovation and expansion. Smaller NBFCs, in particular, may find the compliance burden heavy, while larger players with established relationships with the RBI could navigate the process more smoothly.

Industry response and outlook

Industry experts suggest that the move, while stringent, is in line with the RBI's broader regulatory philosophy of proactive supervision. Some NBFCs have reportedly been operating in a grey area, offering revolving features without explicit regulatory clarity.

The central bank has not yet issued an official notification, and details on the approval process remain unclear. It is expected that the RBI will provide a transition period for existing products and a clear framework for new applications.

For now, NBFCs are advised to review their credit product offerings and prepare for heightened scrutiny. The decision is likely to be discussed in upcoming industry forums, and stakeholders will be watching for the final guidelines.

What to watch next

The RBI is expected to release a discussion paper or draft circular in the coming months. The final rules will determine how quickly NBFCs can adapt, and whether the approval process becomes a standard gatekeeper for all non-bank credit products.
