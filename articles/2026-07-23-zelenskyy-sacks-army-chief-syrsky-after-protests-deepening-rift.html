Zelenskyy sacks Army Chief Syrsky after protests, deepening rift

📅 2026-07-23 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • President Zelenskyy dismissed Army Chief Oleksandr Syrsky amid public protests and internal tensions.
  • The ousted defence chief reportedly rejected an alternative role in the government.
  • The crisis has pitted a tech-savvy reformist camp against the old guard military establishment.

Abrupt dismissal amid public unrest

Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has fired his top general, Army Chief Oleksandr Syrsky, after days of street protests and a widening rift inside the war-time government. The decision was announced late Tuesday, though officials have not yet detailed the exact reasons behind the sudden move.

Witnesses in Kyiv reported large crowds near the presidential administration building earlier this week, demanding accountability and a change in military strategy. The protests added pressure on Zelenskyy, who had until now stood by his commander in chief through the grinding war with Russia.

Ousted general refuses olive branch

According to multiple reports, Syrsky was offered a different position within the government but turned it down. The BBC quoted sources saying the ousted defence chief “rejected any other role in Zelenskyy’s government.” The Guardian also confirmed the refusal, describing it as a clear sign of the deep personal and professional breach between the two men.

Syrsky had been a polarising figure. He oversaw the successful Kharkiv counter-offensive in 2022 but later faced criticism over the protracted battle for Bakhmut and a stalled southern campaign. His dismissal marks the most significant military shake-up since the war began.

Tech reformers versus old guard

The crisis has exposed a fundamental struggle inside Ukraine’s leadership. The Associated Press described it as a clash between “a tech innovator and an old guard general.” Zelenskyy, a former comedian turned wartime president, has pushed for faster digitalisation and Western-style command reforms. Syrsky represented the Soviet-trained, hierarchical school of military thinking.

Analysts say the tension had been building for months. The president’s office wanted more aggressive use of drones and data-driven operations, while Syrsky’s team favoured conventional artillery and manpower-heavy tactics. The rift became public after a leaked memo suggested the general had bypassed civilian oversight on a major procurement deal.

What happens next

Zelenskyy now faces the task of appointing a successor who can unite a fractured command structure while maintaining morale on the front lines. The next army chief will have to manage both the war effort and the political fallout from this unprecedented dismissal.

Ukraine’s parliament is expected to debate the reshuffle later this week. For now, the fate of the military’s top post remains uncertain, with several names circulating in Kyiv’s corridors of power. The coming days will show whether Zelenskyy can steady the ship or whether this crisis deepens further.
