Delhi-NCR reels under record August rain; waterlogging hits commute

📅 2026-08-07 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • IMD issued red alert as heavy rain lashed Delhi-NCR on Friday
  • Waterlogging and gridlock crippled the evening commute across the region
  • Delhi recorded wettest first week of August in 15 years
  • Multiple videos show submerged roads and stranded vehicles

Delhi-NCR came to a standstill on Friday as relentless rain triggered the India Meteorological Department's (IMD) red alert, drowning roads and throwing the evening commute into chaos. Videos from across the region showed knee-deep waterlogging, cars abandoned mid-road, and commuters wading through flooded streets. The downpour has made this the wettest first week of August in 15 years, according to weather officials.

Red alert and record rainfall

The IMD issued a red alert for Delhi and surrounding areas, warning of very heavy rain through the day. The city has already surpassed its monthly average rainfall in just the first week of August, a feat not seen since 2011. Meteorologists attribute the intense spell to a convergence of monsoon winds and a cyclonic circulation over northwest India.

By Friday afternoon, several parts of Delhi-NCR reported rainfall exceeding 100 mm in a span of hours. The sudden deluge overwhelmed drainage systems, turning major arterial roads into rivers. Officials said the rain is likely to continue for the next 24 hours, with the alert remaining in force.

Waterlogging and gridlock

Commuters faced hours-long delays as key stretches, including stretches near ITO, Minto Bridge, and parts of Gurugram's Golf Course Road, went under water. Seven videos circulating on social media showed the scale of the crisis—from a bus half-submerged in a underpass to two-wheelers being pushed through waist-deep water. Traffic police deployed additional personnel to manage jams, but movement remained sluggish well into the evening.

The city's public transport system was hit hard. Several Delhi Metro stations reported water seepage, though services continued with delays. Buses were rerouted from flooded roads, leaving many passengers stranded at stops. Autos and cabs were scarce, with many drivers refusing to take routes through submerged areas.

Residents bear the brunt

For residents in low-lying areas, the situation was worse. Colonies in east and central Delhi reported water entering homes, with people using buckets to bail out water. In Noida and Ghaziabad, several underpasses were closed as a precaution. Emergency helplines received hundreds of calls about stuck vehicles and waterlogging.

Local authorities deployed pumps to clear water, but the sheer volume of rain made progress slow. Power outages were reported in parts of the city after rain affected electrical infrastructure. Schools and offices announced early closures or work-from-home arrangements for Friday and Saturday.

What lies ahead

Forecasters predict more rain over the weekend, with the red alert likely to remain for another 48 hours. The IMD has advised people to avoid unnecessary travel and stay away from waterlogged areas. Meanwhile, civic agencies are on high alert to prevent further disruptions.

As the city braces for more downpours, the focus now shifts to how quickly authorities can restore normalcy. With climate experts linking such intense spells to changing monsoon patterns, Delhi's infrastructure will face tougher tests in the coming years.
