Credit Card Rewards Devalued? How to Extract More Value in 2026

Credit cardholders across India are noticing an uncomfortable pattern: the same card, the same spend, but a smaller reward cheque at the end of the month. Issuers have spent 2025 and 2026 quietly reworking reward structures, capping cashback categories, adding redemption fees and raising annual charges. The headline promise on the card's landing page has not changed, but the fine print has. Understanding where the value leaked out is the first step to getting it back.

Why Your Card Feels Less Rewarding

Three structural changes explain most of the erosion. First, category caps: several banks now limit accelerated rewards to Rs 1,000-2,000 per month, down from unlimited earn in earlier versions. Second, redemption friction: statement credit and voucher redemptions increasingly carry processing charges or reduced conversion ratios. Third, milestone thresholds have moved up, so the bonus that once arrived at Rs 2 lakh annual spend now needs Rs 4 lakh or more.

The net effect is that a card advertising "10X rewards" may deliver an effective return of 1.2-1.8 per cent on real-world spending, not the 3-5 per cent implied by marketing. For a household spending Rs 50,000 a month, that difference is worth roughly Rs 12,000-18,000 a year in lost value.

Calculate Your True Effective Reward Rate

Ignore the points. Convert everything to rupees recovered per Rs 100 spent. Take last month's statement, sum the reward points earned, multiply by the redemption value you actually use (not the best-case value), then divide by total spend. If that number is below 1.5 per cent on a card with an annual fee above Rs 1,000, the card is not earning its keep.

Most cardholders carry four to six cards and use them randomly. Concentrating spend on two cards, one for cashback categories and one for travel or fuel, pushes you past milestone thresholds faster and reduces the number of annual fees you pay.

A fuel card on grocery spend, or a travel card on utility bills, earns the base rate and nothing more. Align each card with the category where it pays the highest verified rate.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Effective reward rates have fallen by roughly 30-50 per cent on many popular cashback cards since 2024.
  • Annual fees have risen even where reward rates were cut, worsening the value equation.
  • Concentrating spend on two cards beats spreading it across five for milestone bonuses.
  • Retention offers are underused; a single phone call often beats switching cards.
  • Points held through a devaluation notice lose real value if not redeemed quickly.

The Buyer's Verdict

A credit card remains a useful tool only when its effective return exceeds its total cost, including fees, redemption charges and the opportunity cost of locked-in points. For most salaried cardholders in India, a lifetime-free cashback card paired with one well-chosen travel or fuel card delivers better net value in 2026 than a single premium metal card with shrinking benefits. Review your portfolio once a year, calculate the real numbers, and act before the next devaluation cycle lands.
