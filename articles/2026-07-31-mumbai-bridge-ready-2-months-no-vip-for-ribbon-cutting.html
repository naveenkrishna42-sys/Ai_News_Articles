Mumbai Bridge Ready 2 Months, No VIP For Ribbon-Cutting

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The bridge is complete and ready for use for two months.
  • No VIP has been available to perform the ribbon-cutting ceremony.
  • The delay in inauguration is holding up the bridge's opening to the public.

Bridge Complete, But Inauguration Pending

A newly constructed bridge in Mumbai has been ready for public use for the past two months, yet it remains unopened. The reason, according to officials, is that no VIP has found time to cut the ribbon at the inaugural ceremony. The bridge, which was built to ease traffic congestion in a busy part of the city, stands idle as commuters continue to navigate through older, more crowded routes.

Local municipal authorities have completed all technical checks and safety inspections. The structure is fully functional, with lighting, signage, and approaches all in place. However, the formal inauguration is a prerequisite for opening it to traffic, following standard protocol that requires a political or administrative figure to officiate.

Protocol Over Practicality

The delay highlights a peculiar bureaucratic hurdle where ceremony trumps utility. A senior civic official, speaking on condition of anonymity, admitted that the bridge could have been opened weeks ago but for the insistence on a ceremonial launch. "The work is done, the bridge is safe, but we need a chief guest to declare it open," the official said.

This is not an isolated incident in Mumbai, where infrastructure projects often wait for political calendars to align. In the past, similar delays have occurred with flyovers and pedestrian subways, drawing criticism from residents who see the roads every day but cannot use them.

Commuters Bear the Brunt

For local residents, the sight of a finished bridge being unused is frustrating. Daily commuters in the area continue to face long detours and jams, even as the new structure promises to shave minutes off their travel. A shopkeeper nearby remarked that the bridge's lights were tested weeks ago, and since then, nothing has moved.

Local activists have written to the municipal commissioner, urging that the bridge be opened without further delay. They argue that in a city where traffic is a daily ordeal, every day of waiting is a cost borne by thousands. Some have suggested that the bridge be inaugurated by a schoolchild or a traffic constable if no VIP is available, to break the impasse.

Political Scheduling and Public Interest

The challenge lies in aligning the schedules of senior politicians, who are often busy with multiple engagements. The responsibility for finding a suitable date typically falls on the local ward office, which coordinates with the party leadership. Officials have not yet confirmed a new date for the ceremony, though they insist it is a matter of days, not weeks.

Meanwhile, the city's infrastructure department is keen to showcase the bridge as a sign of progress, especially with state elections expected later this year. A grand inauguration, complete with media coverage and speeches, serves a political purpose beyond the simple act of opening a structure. But for the common Mumbaikar, the ribbon is just a piece of cloth standing between them and a smoother ride.

What Happens Next

Efforts are reportedly underway to finalise a date for the inauguration, possibly within the next week. Civic officials say they are in touch with the chief minister's office to secure a slot. If that fails, the mayor might step in, though no decision has been announced. Until then, the bridge remains a silent monument to the gap between administrative process and public need.
