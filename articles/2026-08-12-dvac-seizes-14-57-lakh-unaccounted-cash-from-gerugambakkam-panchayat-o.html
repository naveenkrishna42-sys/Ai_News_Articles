DVAC seizes ₹14.57 lakh unaccounted cash from Gerugambakkam panchayat office

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • DVAC seized ₹14.57 lakh in unaccounted cash from Gerugambakkam panchayat office.
  • The raid was conducted as part of an anti-corruption operation in Chennai.
  • Investigation is underway to determine the source and those responsible.

CHENNAI: In a significant anti-corruption operation, the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC) has seized ₹14.57 lakh in unaccounted cash from the Gerugambakkam panchayat office on the city's outskirts. The raid, conducted on Wednesday, has sent ripples through the local administration as officials begin tracing the origins of the money.

Raid details emerge

The DVAC team, acting on a tip-off, swooped down on the panchayat office premises in the morning. During a thorough search of the records and strongrooms, they stumbled upon the cash stash, which was not reflected in any official accounts. Sources said the money was found in bundles, hidden in a manner that suggested an attempt to keep it off the books.

Preliminary inquiries indicate that the amount could be linked to irregularities in panchayat fund management, though no formal charges have been filed yet. The DVAC has seized the cash and is scrutinising the office's financial statements for the past few years to piece together the trail.

Investigation underway

Officials have not yet confirmed who might be responsible for the unaccounted cash, and no arrests have been reported so far. The focus now is on identifying the individuals who had access to the funds and understanding why the money was kept outside official channels. Sources close to the investigation said that the panchayat's administrative records are being examined to check for discrepancies.

The DVAC is also looking into whether the cash was meant to be used for any specific purpose, such as paying contractors or influencing decisions in the panchayat. Investigators are likely to summon the panchayat officials for questioning in the coming days.

Wider context of anti-corruption drives

The raid comes amid a broader push by state agencies to crack down on financial irregularities in local bodies across Tamil Nadu. Over the past year, DVAC has conducted several such operations, leading to the recovery of unaccounted funds and the registration of cases against errant officials. This case, however, stands out due to the relatively modest but significant amount found in a panchayat office, which handles limited budgets.

Local residents expressed shock at the development, with many demanding a thorough probe to ensure accountability. The panchayat office in Gerugambakkam, a rapidly developing area, manages funds for infrastructure and welfare schemes, making the seizure a matter of public concern.

What happens next

The DVAC is expected to file a detailed report after completing its preliminary analysis. Depending on the findings, a case may be registered under the Prevention of Corruption Act, which could lead to arrests and disciplinary action. Officials have assured that the investigation will be transparent and that all those found guilty will be dealt with strictly.

For now, the seized cash remains in DVAC custody, and the probe continues to unfold. Residents and observers alike will be watching to see whether this leads to a larger expose of financial mismanagement in the region's panchayats.
