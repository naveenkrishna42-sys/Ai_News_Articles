Afghanistan vs India T20 2026: Series Snapshot

Afghanistan tour India for a T20I series in September 2026, with Delhi's Arun Jaitley Stadium hosting the matches. The series arrives at a busy point in the international calendar, sitting between the tail end of the global T20 cycle and India's Asian Games commitments. For Afghanistan, it is a rare chance to test their rising white-ball core against a top-tier side on neutral-ish turf. For India, it is a workload-management exercise as much as a competitive fixture.

Broadcast rights for the series sit with India's mainstream cricket network, with digital streaming on the corresponding OTT platform. Confirm the exact channel and app listing on match day, as sub-licensing arrangements occasionally shift between series.

Full Match Schedule and Venue Details

The series is scheduled around the second week of September 2026, with Delhi as the single host city. Afghanistan are officially the home side, which is why India appear as the 'away' team in scorecards and toss protocol despite playing in their own country. The arrangement stems from Afghanistan's continued use of Indian venues for home fixtures, a practice that dates back several years.

Why India Play as the 'Away' Team

Afghanistan have hosted 'home' bilateral series in India, the UAE and Oman because of security and logistical constraints at home. The Delhi fixture therefore carries Afghanistan as the home team in the record books, even though the crowd, pitch conditions and travel favour India. Buyers of tickets and broadcast packages should note this does not affect pricing or access, only the official match designation.

Where to Watch: Live Streaming and Telecast

Television coverage runs on the official rights-holding sports network, with the companion OTT app carrying the live stream. Mobile viewers can stream on the broadcaster's app, typically requiring an active subscription; some plans bundle cricket with general entertainment. Regional-language commentary feeds are usually available for major bilateral series.

  • TV: Official sports network channels, standard and HD feeds.
  • Streaming: Broadcaster's OTT app and website, subscription required.
  • Radio and text: Commentary on licensed radio partners; ball-by-ball updates on official cricket sites.
  • Overseas: Rights vary by territory; check local sports broadcasters or the ICC's official listings.

Team Context: Zadran's New Role and India's Dilemmas

Ibrahim Zadran has taken over as Afghanistan's full-time T20I captain and has spoken about enjoying the new responsibility. His leadership will be tested against India's pace attack and spin depth. Afghanistan's batting order, built around Zadran and a clutch of hard-hitting middle-order players, has the firepower to trouble any side on a good Delhi surface.

India's selection headaches are twofold. The opening combination remains unsettled, with multiple contenders for the two slots. Jasprit Bumrah's workload is the bigger strategic question, given the Asian Games follow soon after. Expect India to rotate pacers and give fringe bowlers a run.

Real-World Buyer and Fan Implications

Ticket demand for a Delhi T20I involving India is typically high, and prices on resale platforms spike closer to match day. Fans planning travel should also account for traffic advisories around the BRICS Summit period, which can affect access routes to the stadium. For streaming subscribers, the series is a low-cost addition to existing cricket packages rather than a standalone purchase.

Key Insights

  • Pros: Competitive T20I cricket in a major Indian venue; chance to see Afghanistan's next-gen leadership; convenient for Delhi-based fans.
  • Pros: Strong broadcast and streaming availability across TV and mobile.
  • Cons: India's squad may be rotated, reducing the certainty of full-strength line-ups.
  • Cons: Traffic restrictions around the BRICS Summit could complicate match-day travel.
  • Cons: Single-venue series limits the touring experience for outstation fans.

The series serves as a useful benchmark for both sides. Afghanistan get a high-pressure test of their new captaincy structure, while India get a controlled environment to settle combinations before the Asian Games. For fans, it is straightforward, accessible cricket with real tactical stakes.
