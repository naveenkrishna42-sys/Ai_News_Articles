Chinese chipmaker CXMT becomes mainland’s most valuable listed firm after 500% debut rally

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • CXMT shares surged more than 500% on its Shanghai debut.
  • The chipmaker is now mainland China's most valuable listed firm.
  • Its $9.8 billion IPO is one of the largest globally this year.
  • The rally reflects investor optimism in China's memory chip ambitions.

Shares of Chinese memory chip maker CXMT exploded more than 500% on their first day of trading on the Shanghai Stock Exchange, propelling the company to the top of the mainland market by valuation.

The blockbuster debut cements CXMT as the most valuable listed firm in mainland China, overtaking established giants like Kweichow Moutai and PetroChina. The stock opened at a sharp premium and continued climbing through the session, driven by a wave of retail and institutional buying.

$9.8 Billion IPO Fuels Frenzy

CXMT raised $9.8 billion in its initial public offering, one of the largest listings globally this year. The IPO was heavily oversubscribed, reflecting strong demand for a domestic champion in a sector Beijing has designated as strategic.

The company is the country's leading producer of DRAM memory chips, a critical component in smartphones, servers, and data centers. Its listing comes as the United States tightens export controls on advanced semiconductor technology to China.

China's Memory Chip Ambitions

CXMT's meteoric rise underscores the government's push to reduce reliance on foreign chipmakers. Beijing has poured billions into subsidies and state-backed funds to build a self-sufficient semiconductor ecosystem.

Analysts caution that the valuation may be stretched. The company is still years away from matching the technological output of global leaders like Samsung and SK Hynix. But investors are betting on the long-term narrative of China's technological sovereignty.

Market Cap Surpasses Trillion Yuan Mark

At the closing bell, CXMT's market capitalisation had crossed the one trillion yuan mark. That makes it more valuable than the combined market cap of several other Chinese tech heavyweights on the Shanghai exchange.

The rally also lifted sentiment across the broader semiconductor sector. Several chip-related stocks on the mainland and Hong Kong exchanges posted gains in sympathy with CXMT's debut.

What This Means for Global Chip Race

The listing is a shot in the arm for China's ambitions in the memory chip market, where it has long lagged behind South Korean and U.S. players. CXMT now has a massive war chest to invest in R&D and production capacity.

However, the company faces significant headwinds. It remains under U.S. export restrictions that limit its access to advanced chipmaking equipment. The path to profitability is also uncertain given the capital-intensive nature of the industry.

For now, the market has spoken. CXMT's debut is a powerful symbol of China's determination to build its own semiconductor champions, and the financial markets are rewarding that vision — at least for today.

The real test will come in the quarters ahead, as the company must now deliver on its technology roadmap and prove it can compete on the global stage without relying solely on domestic enthusiasm.
