Arrest in Bengaluru over hidden camera in bathroom

Bengaluru police have arrested a software professional for allegedly filming his landlady inside the bathroom of her own house. The accused, a tenant in the woman's residence, is said to have installed a concealed camera to record her without consent.

The arrest followed a complaint lodged by the landlady after she reportedly discovered the device. Sources said the camera was found tucked away in a spot that could capture the bathroom area discreetly.

How the matter came to light

According to the police, the landlady grew suspicious after noticing something unusual in the bathroom. On closer inspection, she found what appeared to be a small camera, and immediately alerted the authorities.

Officers from the local police station responded promptly, registered a case under relevant sections of the law, and took the accused into custody. He was later produced before a court and remanded to judicial custody.

Legal provisions and investigation

The case has been filed under provisions of the Indian Penal Code relating to voyeurism and trespass, officials said. The accused has not yet been formally charged, and the investigation is ongoing.

Police are also examining the camera and other electronic devices seized from the accused to determine whether any footage was shared or stored elsewhere. No such evidence has been made public so far.

Growing concern over privacy violations

This incident adds to a rising number of cases across Indian cities where hidden cameras have been used to invade personal privacy. Bengaluru, in particular, has seen several such reports in recent years, prompting calls for stricter enforcement.

Legal experts point out that voyeurism is a non-bailable offence under Section 354C of the IPC, which carries a penalty of up to three years in prison. Conviction rates, however, remain low, often due to lack of digital evidence.

What happens next

The court will decide on bail pleas as the investigation proceeds. Meanwhile, police are expected to submit a chargesheet once the forensic analysis of the seized devices is complete.

Observers will be watching how swiftly the case moves through the judicial system, and whether it prompts renewed discourse on tenant-landlord trust and privacy safeguards in the city's rental housing market.
