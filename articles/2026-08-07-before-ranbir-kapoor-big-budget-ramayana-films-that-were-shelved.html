Before Ranbir Kapoor: Big-budget Ramayana films that were shelved

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Multiple big-budget Ramayana films were announced over the years but shelved.
  • Hrithik Roshan and Ajay Devgn were among the stars attached to these projects.
  • Ranbir Kapoor's Ramayana is finally set for release, breaking the trend.

Bollywood has long dreamt of bringing the Ramayana to the big screen in all its epic glory. But for decades, that dream has repeatedly collided with reality. Several ambitious, big-budget adaptations were announced with much fanfare, only to be quietly shelved before a single frame could be shot.

Now, with Ranbir Kapoor set to play Lord Ram in a new mega-production, the industry is watching closely. Will this one finally break the pattern that has stumped so many before him?

A list of grand announcements, little action

The list of shelved Ramayana films reads like a who's who of Bollywood's biggest stars and most daring directors. Hrithik Roshan was attached to one such project, as was Ajay Devgn. Each announcement generated excitement, but none progressed beyond the planning stage.

Reports suggest various reasons for the stalled projects — from creative differences and budget concerns to logistical nightmares. The sheer scale of the epic, with its massive sets, VFX requirements, and star-studded cast, proved too daunting for many production houses.

The Hrithik Roshan connection

Hrithik Roshan's name has been linked to a Ramayana adaptation multiple times over the years. Fans were particularly excited about the prospect of the actor, known for his god-like screen presence, taking on the role of Lord Ram.

But despite the buzz, the project never took off. Insiders say the combination of an elaborate script, a lengthy production schedule, and the pressure of living up to a revered epic created hurdles that ultimately proved insurmountable.

Ajay Devgn's ambitious attempt

Ajay Devgn was another big name attached to a Ramayana film. The actor, who has a track record of backing large-scale projects, reportedly spent considerable time on pre-production.

However, that project too fell by the wayside. While the exact reasons remain unclear, industry observers point to the immense scale of the story — spanning multiple kingdoms, divine characters, and epic battles — as a key challenge. Getting the visual effects right alone would require a budget that few were willing to risk.

Why is the Ramayana so hard to film?

Filmmakers have long grappled with the question of how to depict divine characters without alienating audiences. The Ramayana is not just a story; it is a sacred text for millions. Any creative liberty risks controversy, while a faithful retelling demands enormous resources.

There is also the question of casting. The actor who plays Lord Ram must embody both nobility and strength, a combination that is rare in any generation. Similar challenges apply to Sita, Hanuman, and Ravana. One wrong choice could doom the project before it even begins.

Add to that the technical demands — from grand sets depicting Ayodhya and Lanka to cutting-edge VFX for the war sequences — and it becomes clear why so many have backed away.

Ranbir Kapoor's Ramayana: A new hope

Now, Ranbir Kapoor is stepping into the role that has eluded so many. His film, directed by Nitesh Tiwari, is reportedly one of the most expensive Indian productions ever mounted. The cast includes some of the biggest names in the industry, and the makers have promised a grand visual spectacle.

Early reports suggest that the film has been in production for a considerable time, with a release date already locked. If it succeeds, it could open the floodgates for more mythological epics. If it fails, it might just cement the Ramayana as Bollywood's most elusive prize.

For now, all eyes are on Ranbir Kapoor and his team. The stakes are enormous, but so is the potential reward. After decades of false starts, Indian cinema might finally be ready to tell this epic the way it deserves.

What happens next will be closely watched not just by fans, but by an industry that has learned to be cautious about such ambitious projects.
