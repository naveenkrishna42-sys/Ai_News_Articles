Lok Sabha Debates Anti-Paper Leak Bill; Opposition Questions Deterrence

📅 2026-07-28 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Lok Sabha debates anti-paper leak Bill on Day 7 of Monsoon Session.
  • Opposition MPs argue proposed punishment is not enough deterrence.
  • Bill termed 'admission of failure' by some members.
  • Patna HC sets up 3 fast-track courts for exam offence cases.

Debate on Anti-Paper Leak Bill

The Lok Sabha on Tuesday took up the anti-paper leak Bill for discussion on Day 7 of the Monsoon Session. The legislation aims to curb the rising number of examination paper leaks across the country.

Several Opposition MPs raised concerns about the efficacy of the proposed punishment. They argued that the current provisions do not serve as enough of a deterrent. Some members termed the Bill an 'admission of failure' by the government to prevent such incidents.

Opposition's Stance

Opposition MPs demanded stricter penalties and better implementation mechanisms. They pointed to recent high-profile leaks that disrupted exams for lakhs of students.

The government defended the Bill, stating it is a comprehensive step forward. Officials argued that the legislation includes provisions for speedy trials and stringent punishment for offenders.

Patna HC Fast-Track Courts

In a related development, the Patna High Court has set up three fast-track courts specifically for exam offence cases. This move is expected to expedite legal proceedings in such matters.

The fast-track courts will handle cases related to paper leaks and other malpractices. This decision comes amid growing public outcry over delays in justice for affected students.

What the Bill Proposes

The anti-paper leak Bill 2026 proposes stringent punishment for those involved in leaking exam papers. It also aims to create a robust framework to prevent future incidents.

Provisions include heavy fines and imprisonment for offenders. The Bill also seeks to establish a national database of malpractices to track repeat offenders.

Student Reactions

Students across the country have welcomed the fast-track courts but remain skeptical about the Bill's deterrent value. Many believe that without strict enforcement, the law will have limited impact.

Student unions have demanded harsher punishments and a zero-tolerance policy. They argue that paper leaks undermine the entire education system and affect millions of young lives.

The debate in Lok Sabha is expected to continue over the next few days. The government hopes to pass the Bill before the end of the Monsoon Session.

What remains to be seen is whether the final version of the Bill will incorporate the Opposition's suggestions for stronger deterrence. The coming days will also reveal how effectively the Patna fast-track courts can clear the backlog of exam offence cases.
