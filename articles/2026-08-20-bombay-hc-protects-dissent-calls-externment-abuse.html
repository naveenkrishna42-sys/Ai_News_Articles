Bombay HC Protects Dissent, Calls Externment Abuse

📅 2026-08-20 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bombay High Court (HC) criticized use of externment orders to silence political dissent
  • Justice Prathiba M. Singhi ruled that opposition to government is not a crime
  • Order comes amid concerns over increasing use of externment to stifle critics

The Bombay High Court (HC) has condemned the misuse of externment orders to silence political dissent, emphasizing that opposition to the government is not a criminal act. In a significant ruling, a division bench headed by Justice Prathiba M. Singhi made it clear that externment should not be used to suppress political opposition.

External Order Misuse Condemned

The court was hearing a petition challenging externment orders issued against several activists and political workers in Maharashtra. The orders, which direct individuals to leave a particular area and not return, are typically used to maintain law and order but have been criticized for being used to target political opponents.

Justice Singhi highlighted that externment orders are meant to prevent anticipated crime, not punish for past opposition to the government. She stated, "It is clear that political opposition, even when Critical, cannot and should not be treated as a crime."

Preventive Detention Abuse

The court also addressed the misuse of preventive detention, such as the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, to stifle political dissent. The bench emphasized that such laws are intended for acts of terrorism, not political disagreements.

The court's comments come amid growing concerns over the increasing use of externment orders and preventive detention laws to target political activists and opposition figures.

State's Response

The HC's ruling may impact several ongoing cases, including those against activists arrested under the UAPA. The state government is yet to respond to the court's ruling, but it may have to review its policy on externment orders and preventive detention.

  • The Bombay High Court's ruling is seen as a significant blow to the misuse of externment orders to target political opponents.
  • Activists and opposition parties have welcomed the court's decision, hoping it will deter the use of externment to stifle political dissent.

What happens next will depend on how the government responds to the court's ruling. The state may challenge the ruling, or it may choose to comply, potentially signaling a shift in its approach to political opposition.
