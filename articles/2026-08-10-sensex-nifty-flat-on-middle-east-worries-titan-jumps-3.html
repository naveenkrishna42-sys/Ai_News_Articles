Sensex, Nifty flat on Middle East worries; Titan jumps 3%

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty closed flat, with gains capped by Middle East uncertainties.
  • Titan emerged as a top gainer, jumping 3% during the session.
  • Markets remain cautious as investors track geopolitical developments.
  • Broader market showed mixed trends, with select stocks outperforming.

Muted Close for Indian Equities

Indian benchmark indices ended the trading session on a flat note on Monday, as escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East weighed on investor sentiment. The Sensex and Nifty oscillated within a narrow range through the day, with late-session selling wiping out early gains.

Traders remained cautious, tracking crude oil price movements and any potential supply disruptions from the region. The uncertainty kept fresh buying interest at bay, even as domestic macros showed resilience.

Titan Shines Bright

In an otherwise lacklustre market, Titan Company emerged as the standout performer, surging 3% during the session. The jewellery and watchmaker's rally provided some support to the indices, though it was not enough to lift the broader market into positive territory.

Analysts attributed the stock's strength to sustained investor interest in the consumer discretionary space, with no specific company announcement driving the move. The uptick in Titan helped offset losses in banking and energy heavyweights.

Geopolitical Overhang

The Middle East situation remained the key overhang for global and domestic markets. Investors are closely monitoring diplomatic efforts and any escalation that could impact oil supplies. A spike in crude prices typically raises inflation concerns, which could influence the Reserve Bank of India's rate trajectory.

Foreign institutional investors have been cautious in recent sessions, awaiting clarity on the geopolitical front. Domestic institutions, meanwhile, have been selective, focusing on stocks with strong earnings visibility.

Broader Market Trends

The broader market showed mixed trends, with midcap and smallcap indices trading slightly higher than their largecap peers. Sectoral indices were a mixed bag, with consumer durables and IT stocks gaining, while metal and public sector banks faced selling pressure.

Market breadth was marginally positive, with more stocks advancing than declining on the BSE. However, volumes were subdued, reflecting the cautious mood among participants.

Analysts suggest that until the geopolitical situation stabilises, the market is likely to remain range-bound. Any positive development on the diplomatic front could trigger a sharp rebound, but for now, patience seems to be the watchword.

Looking ahead, investors will watch for cues from global markets and any policy statements from central banks. The coming sessions are expected to be data-driven, with inflation numbers and corporate earnings taking centre stage once the geopolitical fog clears.
