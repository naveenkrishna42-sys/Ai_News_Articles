Evolution of Combined Air Strikes: Ballistics and Jet Propulsion

Russian military doctrine has pivoted toward saturated, multi-layered strike packages designed to overwhelm Ukrainian air defenses. By combining quasi-ballistic Iskander-M missiles, air-launched Kh-47M2 Kinzhal systems, and turbojet-powered loitering munitions such as the Shahed-238 (designated Geran-3), Moscow compresses radar reaction windows to mere minutes. Recent strikes across central Kyiv, including damage sustained by the National Academy of Sciences, demonstrate how these combined salvos complicate layered interception.

Standard piston-engine drones like the Shahed-136 cruise at speeds around 180 km/h, making them vulnerable to mobile air defense teams equipped with heavy machine guns and short-range MANPADS. In contrast, jet-powered variants achieve flight speeds exceeding 500 km/h. This speed profile forces Ukrainian commanders to commit high-end surface-to-air missile (SAM) batteries rather than relying on cost-effective acoustic detection and mobile gun trucks.

The Attrition Calculus Facing Ukrainian Air Defense

The operational logic behind deploying jet-propelled drones alongside hypersonic and ballistic weapons centers on asymmetric resource depletion. A single Patriot PAC-3 interceptor costs between $3.5 million and $4 million (approx. ₹29 crore to ₹33 crore), while an advanced jet drone costs an estimated $100,000 to $200,000. Launching waves of high-speed decoys and jet drones ahead of ballistic strikes forces defensive batteries to expend high-tier interceptors or risk critical command-and-control assets.

Furthermore, ballistic systems follow steep, high-altitude trajectories before descending at terminal velocities between Mach 5 and Mach 7. When paired with low-altitude, radar-absorbing jet drones that follow erratic flight paths, the simultaneous arrivals saturate automated engagement channels in Western-supplied systems like Patriot, IRIS-T SLM, and NASAMS.

Operational Bottlenecks and Tactical Vulnerabilities

  • Shortened Engagement Timelines: Jet-powered UAVs reduce flight time from launch points by two-thirds, shrinking the operational window for electronic warfare triangulation and mobile firing units.
  • Stockpile Depletion: High-rate ballistic volleys force continuous expenditure of Western interceptors at unsustainable rates relative to monthly manufacturing replenishment cycles.
  • Urban Radar Clutter: Low-altitude ingress into dense city centers like Kyiv limits terrestrial radar detection angles, delaying battery lock-on until munitions are within terminal dive phases.
  • Sensor Overload: Simultaneous arrivals of high-mach ballistics and low-observable jet drones split the multi-function radar tracking channels of defending batteries.

The transition to higher-velocity loitering platforms marks a structural shift in regional strike dynamics. As long as ground-based interceptor resupply remains constrained by industrial manufacturing capacity, saturated salvos will continue to exert acute operational pressure on urban and strategic infrastructure.
