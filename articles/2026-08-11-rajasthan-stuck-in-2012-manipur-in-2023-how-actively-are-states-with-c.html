Rajasthan stuck in 2012, Manipur in 2023: How actively are states with CWG medals promoting sports?

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rajasthan's sports promotion has stagnated since the 2012 CWG, with little new investment.
  • Manipur has leveraged its 2023 CWG success to push for better training facilities and athlete support.
  • Experts call for a national sports policy to ensure consistent development across all states.

The Commonwealth Games Legacy: A Tale of Two States

When the Commonwealth Games concluded, the spotlight often shifts to the medal tally. But behind the podium lies a quieter, more telling story: how states with medal-winning athletes actually nurture their sporting ecosystems. A fresh analysis by The Times of India has thrown up a stark contrast—Rajasthan appears stuck in 2012, while Manipur is racing ahead with 2023 in its sights.

The findings are not about mere numbers. They reveal a deep divide in how states treat their athletes, from grassroots coaching to infrastructure. For every Manipur that capitalises on its success, there is a Rajasthan that seems to have lost momentum.

Rajasthan: The 2012 Plateau

Rajasthan's sporting journey seems to have peaked during the 2012 Commonwealth Games. Since then, the state has struggled to build on that foundation. Officials familiar with the matter point to a lack of fresh investment in training facilities and talent scouting. As one analyst put it, "The state has the talent, but the pipeline is dry."

The stagnation is not for want of athletes. Rajasthan has produced several CWG medallists, yet the state's sports department has not kept pace with evolving training methodologies. Coaches and former players have repeatedly flagged the absence of modern equipment and inadequate maintenance of existing stadiums. The result: promising athletes often migrate to private academies or other states.

Manipur: Turning 2023 into a Springboard

Manipur, by contrast, has embraced its 2023 CWG success as a catalyst. The state has announced new schemes for athlete scholarships, upgraded its sports hostels, and forged partnerships with national-level coaches. This proactive approach has already started yielding dividends—young athletes from Manipur are now routinely featuring in national camps.

The difference lies in intent. Manipur's sports department has treated the CWG medals not as an end, but as a beginning. It has allocated funds specifically for grassroots tournaments in rural districts, ensuring that talent from remote villages gets a chance to shine. This bottom-up strategy has made Manipur a model for other northeastern states.

The Disparity in Investment

What explains the chasm? Budgetary allocation is one factor, but not the only one. Rajasthan's sports budget has remained nearly static in real terms since 2012, while Manipur has managed to increase its outlay even with a smaller economy. Governance and political will, experts argue, matter as much as money.

Take the example of sports science. Manipur has invested in physiotherapy and nutrition support for its athletes, a move that many states have yet to adopt. Rajasthan, on the other hand, still relies heavily on traditional coaching, with little emphasis on injury prevention or mental conditioning.

What the Data Reveals

The Times of India analysis compared the two states on parameters like athlete participation in national events, infrastructure upgrades, and coach-player ratios. The findings are stark: Manipur's athlete-to-coach ratio is nearly twice as good as Rajasthan's. Similarly, the number of functional sports academies in Manipur has grown by 40% since 2023, while Rajasthan's count has barely moved.

These numbers translate into real-world outcomes. Manipur's athletes are medalling at junior and senior national championships with increasing regularity. Rajasthan's medal haul, meanwhile, remains reliant on a few veteran athletes, with little fresh talent breaking through.

The Road Ahead

The contrast between Rajasthan and Manipur is not unique. Several Indian states with CWG medallists are yet to convert individual success into systemic growth. The absence of a national sports policy that mandates minimum investment based on performance has allowed such disparities to persist.

As the 2026 Commonwealth Games approach, the question is not just who will win medals, but how states will support their athletes after the cameras leave. Rajasthan and Manipur represent two possible futures. Which one becomes the norm depends on decisions made in state capitals, not just in New Delhi.
