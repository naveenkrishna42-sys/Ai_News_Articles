Lenovo Legion Y700 Infinite AnTuTu score revealed, battery rumor surfaces

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Lenovo has officially shared the AnTuTu benchmark score for its upcoming Legion Y700 Infinite gaming tablet.
  • The score reportedly surpasses rivals like the RedMagic Astra 2, thanks to an overclocked Snapdragon processor.
  • A separate rumor indicates the tablet may pack a 12,000mAh battery, though Lenovo has not confirmed this.
  • The device is expected to launch soon, with more details anticipated at an official event.

A benchmark to beat

Lenovo has lifted the lid on the AnTuTu score for its next gaming tablet, the Legion Y700 Infinite. The figure, shared by the company itself, puts the device ahead of competitors like the RedMagic Astra 2. The number is the result of an overclocked Snapdragon chip, a move that signals Lenovo's intent to dominate the portable gaming segment.

The Legion Y700 series has carved a niche for compact yet powerful gaming slates. The new 'Infinite' moniker suggests a focus on sustained performance. While the exact chip model remains under wraps, the overclocking detail points to a custom tuning job that could give it an edge in sustained loads.

Battery talk

Alongside the official benchmark reveal, a separate rumor has surfaced about the tablet's battery capacity. Unnamed sources suggest the Y700 Infinite could pack a 12,000mAh cell, a significant jump from its predecessor. If true, this would address a common pain point for gaming tablets: battery life under heavy use.

Lenovo has not confirmed the battery figure, and officials have not yet commented on the leak. The company has a history of outfitting its Legion tablets with generous power packs, so the rumor fits the pattern. However, until an official spec sheet drops, the number remains speculative.

Why the AnTuTu score matters

AnTuTu is a widely used benchmark in the Android world, and a high score often translates to marketing bragging rights. The Legion Y700 Infinite's result reportedly beats the RedMagic Astra 2, a direct rival in the niche of small-screen gaming tablets. This is no small feat, given RedMagic's reputation for aggressive performance tuning.

The overclocked Snapdragon inside the Y700 Infinite is likely a variant of Qualcomm's flagship silicon, pushed beyond stock clocks. Such tuning typically requires robust cooling, and Lenovo has previously employed vapor chamber solutions in this line. Whether the Infinite edition adds an external fan or a larger heat spreader remains to be seen.

What this means for gamers

For Indian gamers, tablets like the Legion Y700 Infinite offer a middle ground between a phone and a handheld PC. The compact form factor appeals to those who want a big screen without lugging a laptop. With the AnTuTu score now public, expectations for real-world gaming performance are high.

The battery rumor, if accurate, would make the device a stronger contender for long sessions of titles like BGMI or Call of Duty Mobile. A 12,000mAh cell could easily deliver over 10 hours of mixed use, a compelling pitch for a device aimed at enthusiasts. But until the official launch, gamers will have to temper their excitement with a dose of caution.

The road ahead

Lenovo is expected to formally unveil the Legion Y700 Infinite in the coming weeks, likely alongside regional pricing and availability. The company has not yet announced a launch date for India, but given the popularity of gaming tablets in the market, a global rollout seems likely.

Watch for more details on the chipset, cooling system, and final battery rating. The AnTuTu score has set the bar, and now it's up to the hardware to deliver in the real world.
