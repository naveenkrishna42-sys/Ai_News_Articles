Why Indian crypto exchanges are shutting down one after another

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • High taxes like 30% on crypto gains and 1% TDS have made trading unviable.
  • Banks have cut off services to exchanges, citing RBI guidance.
  • Several major Indian crypto platforms have already halted operations or moved overseas.

India's cryptocurrency industry is facing a severe shakeout. Over the past few months, one exchange after another has either shut down, suspended trading, or announced plans to relocate abroad. The reason is not a sudden loss of user interest but a combination of regulatory and fiscal pressures that have made it nearly impossible to operate within the country.

Tax burden chokes trading volumes

The biggest blow came from the government's 2022 tax policy. A flat 30% tax on all crypto income, along with a 1% tax deducted at source (TDS) on every transaction, has crushed trading volumes. Traders now find it unprofitable to make frequent trades, and volumes on Indian exchanges have plummeted by over 90% since the rules took effect.

Smaller exchanges, which relied on high transaction volumes to survive, have been hit hardest. Even larger platforms like WazirX and CoinDCX have reported steep revenue drops. Without enough trades, the fee-based revenue model collapses, forcing exchanges to cut costs or close entirely.

Banking restrictions add to the squeeze

Beyond taxes, exchanges face a hostile banking environment. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has not formally banned crypto, but its informal pressure on banks to avoid dealing with crypto firms has effectively severed the industry's financial lifeline. Many banks have refused to open accounts for exchanges or have closed existing ones.

This makes it difficult for users to deposit or withdraw rupees. When an exchange cannot process payments smoothly, customers leave. Several platforms have cited banking access as a key reason for shutting down. The industry has repeatedly sought clarity from the RBI, but no formal circular has been issued to reverse the trend.

Regulatory uncertainty and compliance costs

Crypto companies are also grappling with unclear regulations. While the government has brought crypto under tax and anti-money laundering laws, there is still no comprehensive licensing framework. Exchanges operate in a grey zone, unsure about what rules they must follow tomorrow.

Compliance costs have risen sharply. Meeting know-your-customer (KYC) and reporting requirements for every transaction is expensive. Smaller exchanges cannot afford the legal and compliance teams needed to navigate this uncertainty, making them the first to shut down.

Some larger players have opted to move operations to friendlier jurisdictions like Dubai or Singapore. But for local investors, this means losing access to a domestic platform and having to deal with foreign entities whose legal recourse in India is limited.

What this means for Indian investors

For retail investors, the shutdowns have led to panic. Many have found their funds stuck on platforms that have suddenly stopped withdrawals. Others have been forced to sell at a loss to shift money out in time. The lack of a clear exit policy from these exchanges has deepened distrust.

A few exchanges have promised to return user funds, but the process is often slow and opaque. Investor protection mechanisms are virtually non-existent in the current regulatory vacuum, leaving users with little recourse beyond legal battles that can drag on for years.

The crypto industry in India is now at a crossroads. Unless the government steps in with a clear regulatory framework and banking access, more exchanges are likely to follow the same path. For now, the shutdowns are unlikely to stop anytime soon.
