Operation Safed Sagar Review: A War Drama That Puts Strategy Over Spectacle

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • The film centres on the Indian Air Force's role in the Kargil conflict, a rarely explored angle.
  • Siddharth and Jimmy Shergill deliver restrained performances, steering clear of melodrama.
  • The production employed 4,000 people daily and reportedly earned Rs 215 crore.
  • Critics note the film initially critiques lazy patriotism but eventually leans into familiar tropes.

A Different Kind of War Film

Most war dramas chase adrenaline. Operation Safed Sagar, now streaming on Netflix, chooses a quieter path. Directed with a steady hand, the film follows the Indian Air Force's Operation Safed Sagar during the 1999 Kargil conflict. It is a story of precision, patience, and the men who execute missions miles above the battlefield.

Siddharth and Jimmy Shergill lead the cast with measured intensity. There is no chest-thumping, no hyperbolic dialogue. Instead, the film leans on the mechanics of aerial warfare, the tension in the cockpit, and the weight of decisions made in seconds.

Strategy Over Spectacle

The screenplay, as noted by critics, hints at fatigue with lazy jingoism. Early scenes almost mock the loud patriotism that has become a staple of Indian war films. The characters question orders, debate collateral damage, and wrestle with the ethics of air strikes. This restraint is the film's sharpest weapon.

Yet, the review from The Wire points out a familiar arc: the film eventually succumbs to the very tropes it initially questions. The second half leans into emotional crescendos that feel borrowed from earlier war dramas. It is not a fatal flaw, but it dulls the edge the opening promises.

A Massive Undertaking

The scale of the production is worth noting. According to ThePrint, Operation Safed Sagar employed 4,000 people daily. That logistical heft translates on screen—the aerial sequences are crisp, the control rooms feel lived-in, and the period detail is meticulous. The film reportedly earned Rs 215 crore, a strong showing for a war drama with a non-traditional focus.

Darjeeling veteran T.K. Singha, who assisted the team, helped bring authenticity to the IAF's role. This collaboration shows in the technical accuracy of the missions depicted, a detail that will resonate with viewers familiar with the conflict.

Performances That Ground the Film

Siddharth, as the lead, carries the film without resorting to theatrical bravado. His restraint is matched by Jimmy Shergill, whose quiet authority anchors several key sequences. Dia Mirza, in a supporting role, adds a layer of emotional stakes without overshadowing the central narrative.

The film's strength lies in its ability to make the audience feel the stillness before a sortie, the hum of the radar, the brief crackle of communication. It is a war drama that trusts its audience to understand the gravity without being told to feel it.

Operation Safed Sagar is not a perfect film. Its second-half drift into convention is noticeable, and some subplots feel underdeveloped. But as a portrait of the IAF's contribution to the Kargil victory, it is a rare and welcome addition to Indian cinema.

As the film settles into its OTT run, the conversation will likely shift to how it redefines the war genre. Whether it sparks more films about the air force's unsung missions remains to be seen. For now, it stands as a reminder that heroism on screen does not always need noise.
