Delhi HC Orders Takedown of Obscene Content, Unauthorised Merchandise on Khushi Kapoor

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi HC orders takedown of obscene content and unauthorised merchandise featuring Khushi Kapoor
  • Court reportedly questions the plea's attempt to restrain fan pages
  • Case covers allegedly pornographic, AI-generated and fake content across thousands of pages

The Delhi High Court has directed the removal of obscene content and unauthorised merchandise involving actress Khushi Kapoor, in a ruling that underscores the growing legal scrutiny over digital personality rights in India. The order, passed on Wednesday, comes in response to a plea filed by the actress seeking protection of her name, image, and likeness from misuse across the internet.

According to reports, the court's directive covers a wide range of material, including allegedly pornographic, AI-generated, and fake content that has been circulating on various platforms. The actress's legal team argued that such content not only violates her privacy but also causes irreparable harm to her professional reputation and personal dignity.

Court Questions Fan Page Restraint

While the court agreed to the takedown of obscene material, it reportedly raised concerns over the plea's attempt to restrain fan pages. The bench questioned whether such a broad restriction could impinge on the legitimate expression of fans, who often create and share content as a form of admiration.

This distinction is significant, as it suggests the court is mindful of balancing personality rights with the freedom of expression. The judges appeared to draw a line between content that is clearly harmful or defamatory, and content that may be benign or even supportive, albeit unauthorised.

Scope of the Order

The order is understood to apply to thousands of pages and links, though the exact number has not been officially confirmed. Reports indicate that the plea sought action against platforms hosting such content, including social media sites and other online forums.

This case is part of a broader trend in Indian jurisprudence, where courts have increasingly recognised the right to control one's digital identity. Earlier rulings, including those involving other celebrities, have established that unauthorised use of a person's image for commercial gain or malicious purposes can be restrained.

Legal Implications

Legal experts note that the ruling could set a precedent for how courts handle personality rights in the digital age. The challenge lies in enforcing such orders effectively, given the borderless nature of the internet and the ease with which content can be reposted or altered.

The court's questioning of the fan page aspect also signals that future pleas may need to be more narrowly tailored. This could push celebrities and their legal teams to focus on clearly harmful content rather than attempting blanket bans on all unauthorised usage.

Khushi Kapoor, who made her acting debut in recent years, has not publicly commented on the matter. Her legal representatives have not yet issued a statement beyond the court proceedings.

The High Court's order is expected to be complied with by the relevant platforms, though the timeline for implementation remains unclear. It is also likely that the case will continue, as the court may issue further directions on the scope of personality rights in subsequent hearings.

Observers will be watching how this ruling influences other pending cases involving celebrities and their digital likenesses. The balance between protecting individual dignity and allowing free expression online remains a contentious issue, and this judgment adds a nuanced layer to that debate.
