Indian Shares Slip as RBI Pause, Inflation Worries Offset Early Gains

Key points
  • Markets pared early gains after RBI signaled a cautious pause, dampening rate-cut hopes.
  • September CPI data reinforced inflation concerns, pressuring consumption and rate-sensitive stocks.
  • Nifty 50 and Sensex closed marginally lower, reflecting profit-booking and global cues.

RBI’s Pause Weighs on Rate-Cut Bets

Indian equity benchmarks gave up their opening highs on Monday as investors reassessed the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI) latest monetary policy stance. The central bank’s decision to hold the repo rate at 6.50% for the third consecutive review, coupled with a shift toward a neutral monetary stance, dented near-term rate-cut expectations that had buoyed markets earlier in the session.

Inflation Data Keeps Pressure Intact

The setback came a day after the National Statistical Office (NSO) reported headline CPI inflation at 5.6% year-on-year for September, marginally above the RBI’s 4% tolerance band. While food prices cooled slightly, core inflation remained elevated at 5.2%, reinforcing concerns that the disinflation trajectory may be slower than anticipated.

Banking and FMCG Stocks Drag

Heavyweight banking and fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) stocks led the decline, as rising bond yields and persistent input costs eroded investor confidence in margin expansion. Private-sector lenders saw profit-booking after recent rallies, while FMCG majors faced pressure from sticky vegetable prices and weak rural demand signals.

Global Cues Add to Volatility

Global markets were mixed, with the MSCI Emerging Markets index slipping 0.5% amid renewed caution over U.S. fiscal policy and tech valuations. This weighed on domestic sentiment, particularly among foreign institutional investors (FIIs), who remained net sellers for the third straight session.

  • Pros: RBI’s measured approach supports currency stability and anchors long-term growth expectations.
  • Cons: Delay in rate cuts may defer recovery in credit demand and auto sales.
  • Key Insight: Near-term market direction will hinge on upcoming GDP data and festive-season demand trends.

Investors Eye Q2 GDP and Festive Demand

Looks ahead, market participants are likely to focus on the Q2 gross domestic product (GDP) estimate, scheduled for release later this week. A rebound in consumption during the October-December festive quarter could provide a near-term floor for equities, especially in retail and consumer durables.

Analysts suggest that while liquidity remains ample and systemic risk low, near-term volatility may persist until inflation printings align more clearly with the RBI’s 4% target. For now, the market appears caught between valuing stability and pricing in a delayed rate-cut cycle.
