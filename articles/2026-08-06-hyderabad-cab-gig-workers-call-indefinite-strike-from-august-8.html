Hyderabad cab, gig workers call indefinite strike from August 8

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Indefinite strike called from August 8 in Hyderabad
  • Cab drivers and gig workers cite pay and security concerns
  • Services across ride-hailing and delivery platforms likely affected

Hyderabad's cab drivers and gig workers are set to launch an indefinite strike from August 8, a move that could disrupt ride-hailing and food delivery services across the city. The decision comes after weeks of failed negotiations with platform companies over wages, incentives, and working conditions.

The protesting workers, who operate across major ride-hailing and delivery platforms, have demanded a transparent fare structure and better earnings. They argue that rising fuel prices and inflation have made their current pay unsustainable, pushing many into financial distress.

Core Demands of the Agitation

The workers are seeking a revision in base fares and a reduction in the commission deducted by aggregator apps. Many drivers claim that after paying fuel costs, vehicle maintenance, and platform fees, their net income has fallen sharply over the past year.

Apart from pay, the agitators are also pressing for social security benefits, including health insurance and accident coverage. A section of drivers has been demanding that platforms recognise them as workers with formal rights, rather than treating them as independent contractors.

Impact on Daily Commuters

Hyderabad, a city heavily dependent on app-based cabs and auto-rickshaws for daily commuting, could face significant inconvenience if the strike materialises. Thousands of office-goers, students, and airport travellers rely on these services, and a prolonged stoppage may force many to switch to other modes of transport.

Food and grocery delivery apps are also likely to be affected, as a large number of gig workers in the city are engaged in last-mile logistics. The strike is expected to hit operations across multiple neighbourhoods, from the IT corridors of HITEC City to the residential zones in the old city.

Negotiations Break Down

Talks between worker unions and platform representatives reportedly failed in the days leading up to the announcement. The unions have accused companies of not taking their demands seriously, while platform officials have not yet issued a detailed response to the strike call.

This is not the first time Hyderabad has witnessed such a protest. Earlier agitations in the city over similar issues had resulted in partial disruptions, with services resuming after temporary agreements. However, the indefinite nature of the current strike signals a harder stance from the workers.

What Lies Ahead

As August 8 approaches, all eyes are on whether the state government will intervene to broker a settlement. Officials have not yet confirmed any mediation efforts, but past instances suggest that administrative pressure often leads to a temporary truce.

The coming days will reveal whether the platform companies are willing to reconsider their payout structures or if the strike will extend beyond the initial days, leaving Hyderabad's commuters to navigate the city without their usual ride options.
