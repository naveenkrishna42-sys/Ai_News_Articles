TNPDCL orders re-inspection of 3.70 lakh power bills in Chennai

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • TNPDCL CMD orders re-inspection of 3.70 lakh electricity bills in Chennai.
  • Move follows complaints from consumers over billing discrepancies.
  • Officials to verify meter readings and bill calculations, correct errors.

Re-verification ordered after billing complaints

The Chairman and Managing Director of Tamil Nadu Power Distribution Corporation Limited (TNPDCL) has directed officials to re-inspect 3.70 lakh electricity bills issued across Chennai. The order comes in the wake of multiple complaints from consumers about incorrect billing, including inflated charges and erroneous meter readings.

Senior officials in the distribution company have been asked to conduct a thorough review of the bills, covering both domestic and commercial connections. The re-inspection will involve cross-checking meter readings, verifying consumption patterns, and confirming that tariffs have been applied correctly.

Sources said the CMD's directive is aimed at addressing consumer grievances promptly and restoring trust in the billing system. The exercise is expected to cover all zones within the Chennai region where discrepancies have been reported.

Scope of the re-inspection

The re-inspection will cover bills generated over the past few billing cycles, with a focus on those where consumers have raised objections. Officials have been told to prioritise cases involving unusually high bills, sudden spikes in consumption, or mismatches between meter readings and the amounts charged.

Each bill will be examined on a case-by-case basis, and field staff have been instructed to physically verify meters wherever necessary. The process is expected to take a few weeks, depending on the volume of bills and the availability of field teams.

Consumers who believe their bills are incorrect can also approach TNPDCL's customer care centres or use the online portal to register complaints. The corporation has assured that all genuine errors will be rectified, and adjustments will be made in subsequent bills.

Context and previous issues

Chennai has seen periodic complaints about electricity billing, with some consumers reporting bills that are several times higher than their usual consumption. In some cases, the issue has been traced to faulty meters or incorrect data entry during the billing process.

Earlier this year, TNPDCL had introduced a new billing software, which officials said would improve accuracy. However, the transition has not been smooth, and there have been instances of bills being generated with outdated or incorrect readings.

The current directive is seen as a proactive step by the CMD to address these issues before they escalate. Officials have been told to submit a detailed report on the findings of the re-inspection, along with recommendations to prevent such errors in the future.

What consumers should do

Consumers who have not yet received their bills or have questions about them are advised to check their latest bills carefully. If they notice any anomaly, they should file a complaint through the official TNPDCL app or website, or visit their nearest customer service centre.

TNPDCL has also urged consumers to ensure that their contact details and meter numbers are up to date in the corporation's records, as this can help in faster resolution of issues.

Officials have clarified that the re-inspection does not mean all 3.70 lakh bills are wrong. It is a precautionary measure to identify and correct errors, if any, and to reassure consumers that the corporation is accountable.

Next steps

The re-inspection is expected to be completed within a month, after which TNPDCL will release a summary of the findings. The corporation is also likely to issue guidelines to billing staff to avoid similar discrepancies in the future.

Consumers are advised to keep their bills and payment receipts handy while the review is underway. The outcome of this exercise will determine whether any systemic changes are needed in the billing process, and how TNPDCL plans to address the concerns raised by Chennai's electricity users.
