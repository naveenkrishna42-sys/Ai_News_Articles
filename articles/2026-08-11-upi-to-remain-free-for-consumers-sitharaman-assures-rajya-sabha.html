UPI to remain free for consumers, Sitharaman assures Rajya Sabha

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sitharaman assures UPI remains free for consumers
  • Small traders exempted from proposed MDR
  • Taxation Laws Amendment Bill passed by Parliament

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman on Monday assured the Rajya Sabha that Unified Payments Interface (UPI) transactions will continue to be free for consumers, putting to rest speculation that a levy could be introduced on the popular digital payment system.

The assurance came during a discussion on the Taxation Laws Amendment Bill, which Parliament has cleared. The legislation enables the government to frame rules around Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on digital transactions, a move that had triggered concerns about the future cost of UPI payments.

What the Bill does

The Bill, as passed, provides a legal framework for imposing MDR — the fee paid by merchants to banks for processing digital payments. However, Sitharaman was categorical that this charge would not fall on individual users of UPI.

“UPI will remain free for consumers,” she told the Upper House, adding that the proposed MDR would only apply to larger merchants. Small traders, she clarified, will be kept out of the ambit of this charge, a relief for the millions of kirana stores and street vendors who have adopted UPI for daily collections.

Exemption for small traders

The exact turnover threshold for classifying a merchant as “small” has not been officially spelled out yet, but the government’s intent is clear: protect the micro-entrepreneur while possibly charging bigger businesses that benefit disproportionately from digital payments.

This is a significant policy signal. UPI has become the backbone of India’s retail payments, with over a billion transactions processed each month. Any cost imposed on merchants, even if not passed on to consumers, could have altered adoption patterns among smaller businesses.

Why the reversal talk

The debate over UPI charges is not new. Banks and payment aggregators have long argued that MDR is necessary to sustain the infrastructure, pointing to the costs of maintaining the network. The government, however, has historically resisted any levy, fearing it would slow the digital payments push.

The Bill’s passage had led to speculation that a policy reversal was imminent. Live Law and other legal trackers noted that the legislation “paves the way for possible UPI charges,” which is why Sitharaman’s clarification on Monday carried weight.

The finance minister’s statement does not rule out an MDR on larger merchants, but it does draw a clear line: the consumer’s UPI experience remains free of direct charges.

What this means for users and businesses

For the average user, nothing changes. Sending money to a friend, paying a vegetable vendor, or settling a utility bill via UPI will continue to cost nothing. For larger businesses, however, the landscape could shift once the government frames the rules under the new law.

Industry watchers will now look for the notification that defines the merchant categories. That document, when it comes, will determine how much of the MDR burden falls on big retailers, e-commerce platforms, and corporate entities.

The government’s balancing act is evident: keep the masses happy, keep the digital economy humming, but also ensure the payments ecosystem remains financially viable. Monday’s assurance addresses the first two concerns, but the third remains a work in progress.

The next step is the framing of rules under the amended Act, and the definition of “small trader” will be the key detail to watch. Until then, consumers can transact without worry, and small merchants can breathe easy.
