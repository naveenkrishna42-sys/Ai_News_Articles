CJI's Exclusion From Poll Panel Selection Questioned by Supreme Court

📅 2026-07-31 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court asks why Chief Justice of India is excluded from the panel that selects Election Commissioners.
  • Union government tells the court that the Prime Minister's role should be trusted in the appointment process.
  • The constitutionality of the 2023 law governing the appointment of CEC and ECs is being challenged.
  • A Constitution Bench is hearing arguments on the selection process and the need for judicial oversight.

New Delhi: The Supreme Court on Thursday pressed the Union government on why the Chief Justice of India (CJI) has been left out of the panel that picks the Chief Election Commissioner and Election Commissioners. The exchange came during a hearing on a batch of petitions challenging the constitutional validity of the 2023 law that governs these appointments.

A Constitution Bench, led by the Chief Justice of India, asked the government why the selection committee, which under the new law comprises the Prime Minister, a Union Cabinet Minister, and the Leader of Opposition, does not include the CJI. The court's query was direct: what is the logic in excluding the head of the judiciary from a process that decides who will oversee the country's elections?

Government's Defence: Trust the Prime Minister

The Union government, represented by its top law officer, responded by arguing that the Prime Minister cannot be presumed to act against democratic principles. The government said the appointment process under the 2023 Act is fair and that the PM's role is in line with constitutional conventions.

"Can't presume the Prime Minister will act against democracy," the government told the bench, adding that the executive's discretion in such appointments has been a settled practice. The Attorney General is expected to elaborate on this stance as the hearing progresses.

Why the CJI's Presence Matters

The petitioners argue that the absence of the CJI from the selection panel undermines the independence of the Election Commission. They contend that a body responsible for conducting free and fair elections must be insulated from executive influence. The earlier system, which was struck down by the Supreme Court in 2023, had a similar composition but was deemed flawed.

The court's repeated questions suggest a discomfort with the current arrangement. "Why exclude the Chief Justice when the office of the Election Commissioner demands the highest standards of impartiality?" the bench remarked, hinting that the government's rationale may not pass constitutional muster.

The 2023 Law Under Scrutiny

The Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Act, 2023, replaced the old system where the government appointed the CEC and ECs. The new law set up a three-member committee, but notably left out the judiciary.

Opposition parties and civil society groups have challenged the Act, saying it concentrates power in the executive. The Supreme Court's intervention is seen as a critical moment in defining how independent watchdogs are constituted in India.

What the Bench Is Examining

The Constitution Bench is examining whether the 2023 law violates the basic structure doctrine and the principle of free and fair elections. The court has also questioned whether the Leader of Opposition's inclusion is meaningful when the post is not always recognised in Parliament.

During the hearing, the bench also asked whether the government's defence rests on a presumption of good faith. The court noted that constitutional offices require checks and balances, not just trust in individuals.

The hearing is underway, and the government is expected to file additional submissions. The bench has indicated it will pronounce its verdict after hearing all parties.

This case could reshape how India appoints its election guardians. A ruling that mandates CJI's inclusion would restore the pre-2023 arrangement, which had judicial oversight. The court's final decision, expected in the coming weeks, will be watched closely by political parties and constitutional experts alike.
