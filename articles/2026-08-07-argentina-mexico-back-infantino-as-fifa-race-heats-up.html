Argentina, Mexico back Infantino as FIFA race heats up

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Argentina and Mexico have publicly backed FIFA president Gianni Infantino.
  • African nations had earlier voted unanimously in his favour through CAF.
  • UEFA has rejected FIFA's apology and continues to threaten a boycott.
  • The divide deepens football's ongoing governance crisis.

The global football community is increasingly split as Argentina and Mexico have joined African nations in backing FIFA president Gianni Infantino. The moves come amid rising pressure from European federations, including Norway's explicit call for Infantino to step down, and UEFA's refusal to accept FIFA's recent apology.

Latin American support solidifies

Argentina and Mexico, two of the most influential football nations in the Americas, have now lined up behind Infantino. Their support adds significant weight to the FIFA president's position, especially as he faces challenges from European quarters.

The backing from these Latin American powerhouses is seen as a counterbalance to the growing dissent in Europe. While neither country has issued an official statement detailing their reasoning, their alignment signals a broader geopolitical split within the sport's governing bodies.

Africa's unanimous vote

Earlier, the Confederation of African Football (CAF) unanimously voted in favour of Infantino, providing a solid block of support from the continent. This coordinated backing from Africa and now Latin America suggests a coordinated strategy to shore up the president's position ahead of potential challenges.

African football officials have praised Infantino's engagement with the continent, including increased representation and investment in grassroots programmes. However, critics argue that such support may be influenced by FIFA's financial distributions to member associations.

Europe's growing resistance

European football's governing body, UEFA, has rejected FIFA's apology and continues to threaten a boycott. The apology, which was widely seen as an attempt to defuse tensions, has done little to assuage European concerns over Infantino's leadership style and governance reforms.

Norway has been particularly vocal, with its football federation calling for Infantino to resign. The Norwegian stance reflects a broader dissatisfaction among some European nations with the direction FIFA has taken under Infantino's tenure.

A deepening civil war

The Guardian has described the situation as football's 'civil war', with the sport's governance increasingly polarised. On one side, Infantino enjoys support from Africa and parts of the Americas; on the other, European federations are pushing for accountability and change.

The rift threatens to disrupt international competitions and could have implications for upcoming FIFA events. While the immediate focus is on Infantino's leadership, the underlying issues of transparency, governance, and power distribution remain unresolved.

What to watch

As the battle lines harden, all eyes will be on the next FIFA Council meeting, where member associations may formally challenge Infantino's position. European federations are expected to press for an extraordinary congress, though whether they can muster the necessary support remains uncertain.
