Why the Sony WH-CH720N Still Matters in the Budget ANC Segment

The Sony WH-CH720N arrived as a mid-range follow-up to the popular WH-CH710N, and it borrows heavily from Sony's flagship playbook. It uses the same V1 integrated processor found in the WH-1000XM5, which is the core reason its noise cancellation punches well above its price bracket. For buyers who cannot justify spending $350–$400 on a flagship, the CH720N is designed to deliver the "good enough" version of Sony's premium experience — lighter weight, solid ANC, and long battery life — without the premium materials or advanced codec support.

Design, Comfort, and Build

At roughly 192 grams, the WH-CH720N is one of the lightest over-ear headphones Sony has produced in this class. The plastic construction keeps weight down but does feel less premium than the WH-1000XM5's soft-touch finish. The headband uses a simple sliding mechanism rather than the stepless adjusters on flagship models, which is a minor annoyance when fine-tuning fit. Clamping force is moderate, and the oval earcups with soft synthetic leather padding stay comfortable through multi-hour sessions. There is no folding hinge, so the earcups swivel flat rather than collapsing — the included pouch is functional but bulky compared to a hard case.

Controls and Connectivity

Physical buttons handle playback, volume, track skipping, power, and ANC/Ambient Sound toggling. There is no touch panel here, which some users actually prefer for reliability. Bluetooth 5.2 is on board with multipoint pairing, letting you connect to a laptop and phone simultaneously — a feature that was rare at this price when the headset launched. Codec support covers SBC and AAC only; there is no LDAC or aptX, so Android users who care about hi-res wireless audio should look elsewhere. Wired listening is possible via a 3.5mm cable (included), and the USB-C port is for charging only — no USB audio.

Noise Cancellation and Sound Quality

The V1 processor drives dual noise-sensing microphones to cancel ambient noise, with a dedicated feed-forward mic for wind reduction. In practice, the CH720N handles low-frequency drone — airplane cabins, bus engines, HVAC hum — impressively well for the price. It struggles more with human speech and higher-frequency chatter, which flagships like the WH-1000XM5 suppress more effectively. The 30mm dynamic drivers deliver a warm, bass-forward tuning with a slight boost around 100–200Hz and a gentle treble roll-off. That signature suits pop, hip-hop, and podcasts but can make acoustic and classical tracks sound slightly veiled. The Sony Headphones Connect app provides an EQ with presets and a custom 5-band or 10-band adjuster, plus the useful "Find Your Equalizer" feature.

Battery Life and Charging

Sony rates the WH-CH720N at 35 hours with ANC on, which is accurate under moderate volume. Turning ANC off extends runtime toward 50 hours. A 10-minute quick charge yields roughly 4.5 hours of playback, and a full charge takes about 3.5 hours over USB-C. There is no wireless charging.

How It Compares on Paper

The CH720N's standout advantages are its low weight, Sony's mature app ecosystem, and the V1-powered ANC that outperforms most sub-$150 rivals at cutting steady low-frequency noise. Its weaknesses are equally clear: no LDAC, no USB audio, no folding design, and a build that feels its price. The Soundcore Q45 beats it on paper for battery and codec support, but real-world ANC tuning and app polish generally favor Sony.

Who Should Buy the Sony WH-CH720N

  • Commuters and frequent flyers who want effective low-frequency noise cancellation at a budget price.
  • Multi-device users who value Bluetooth multipoint for switching between phone and laptop.
  • Podcast and streaming listeners who prefer a warm, bass-forward tuning and long battery life.
  • Users who dislike touch controls and want dependable physical buttons.

Who Should Look Elsewhere

  • Android audiophiles who need LDAC or aptX for higher-bitrate wireless streaming.
  • Critical listeners who want a neutral, detailed sound signature out of the box.
  • Travelers needing a compact hard case, since the CH720N does not fold.
  • Buyers wanting premium materials and a more rigid, flagship-grade build.

Final Verdict

The Sony WH-CH720N is not a flagship killer, and it is not trying to be. It is a carefully positioned budget ANC headphone that inherits just enough of Sony's premium DNA — the V1 processor, the Headphones Connect app, multipoint — to be a genuinely compelling option under $150. Its noise cancellation is strong where it matters most, its battery life is excellent, and it is comfortable enough to wear all day. The compromises are real but predictable at this price: AAC-only wireless, a plastic build, and no folding hinge.

If your priorities are effective ANC, light weight, and long battery life rather than hi-res codecs or premium materials, the WH-CH720N remains one of the smartest values in its class. If you need LDAC, a foldable design, or reference-grade tuning, you should either step up to the WH-1000XM5 or cross-shop the Soundcore Q45 and JBL Tune 770NC depending on which trade-off you can live with.

Key Takeaways

  • The WH-CH720N uses Sony's V1 processor, giving it class-leading ANC for its price.
  • Bluetooth 5.2 with multipoint is a standout feature at this price point.
  • Battery life of roughly 35 hours with ANC on is excellent for the segment.
  • No LDAC or aptX limits its appeal for Android hi-res audio fans.
  • At 192 g it is lighter than most rivals, but the build is entirely plastic.
