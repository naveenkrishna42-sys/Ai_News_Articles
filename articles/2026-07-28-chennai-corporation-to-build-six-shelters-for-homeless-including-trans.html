Chennai Corporation to build six shelters for homeless, including transit hubs

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Six new facilities will include transit shelters and support centres for the homeless.
  • Shelters will offer medical aid, skill training, and job placement services.
  • Project aims to provide temporary accommodation and long-term rehabilitation.
  • Locations include Anna Nagar, T. Nagar, and areas near railway stations.

New shelters for the homeless in Chennai

The Greater Chennai Corporation has announced plans to develop six new facilities for the homeless population across the city. These will include transit shelters and comprehensive support centres aimed at providing both temporary accommodation and long-term rehabilitation.

Officials said the project is part of a broader effort to address the needs of the urban homeless, who often lack access to basic amenities and social services. The facilities are expected to be operational within the next 12 to 18 months.

Transit shelters and support services

The six facilities will be located in different parts of the city, including Anna Nagar, T. Nagar, and areas near major railway stations. Each shelter will have dormitory-style accommodation, washrooms, and common areas.

Beyond just a roof, the centres will also offer medical aid, counselling, skill training, and job placement services. The idea is to help the homeless stabilise their lives and eventually transition to independent living.

Corporation officials said the design of the shelters will focus on safety and dignity, with separate sections for men, women, and families. They will be managed by non-governmental organisations with experience in homeless outreach.

Rehabilitation and job support

One of the key features of the new facilities is the emphasis on rehabilitation. Each centre will have a dedicated team to help residents find employment, access government schemes, and reconnect with family members.

A senior official from the Corporation's social welfare department said that many homeless individuals are skilled workers who have fallen on hard times. The job support component will aim to match them with employers in construction, housekeeping, and other sectors.

The transit shelters, in particular, are designed for those who need temporary accommodation while they secure work or housing. Residents will be allowed to stay for up to three months, with extensions possible in special cases.

Funding and timeline

The project will be funded through the city's budget allocation for social welfare, with additional support from the state government. The Corporation has not yet disclosed the total estimated cost.

Construction is expected to begin in the next quarter, with the first batch of shelters ready by mid-2026. Officials said they will also refurbish some existing night shelters as part of the initiative.

The Corporation is currently finalising the locations and will conduct a survey to identify the most vulnerable homeless populations in each area. This data will help prioritise which facilities are built first.

Chennai has an estimated 8,000 to 10,000 homeless individuals, according to a 2024 survey by the Tamil Nadu Slum Clearance Board. The new shelters will add around 500 beds to the city's existing capacity of 1,200 beds across 15 night shelters.

The Corporation will also set up a helpline and mobile outreach teams to connect homeless persons to the new facilities. Officials plan to review the project's impact after the first year before deciding on further expansion.
