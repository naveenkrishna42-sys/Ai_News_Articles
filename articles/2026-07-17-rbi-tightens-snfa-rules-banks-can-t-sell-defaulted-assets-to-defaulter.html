RBI tightens SNFA rules: Banks can't sell defaulted assets to defaulters

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Banks cannot sell stressed assets to the original borrower who defaulted.
  • New rules apply to sales under the Swamitva National Framework for Asset resolution.
  • Move aims to prevent circular transactions and improve recovery discipline.

The Reserve Bank of India has tightened the rules for banks selling stressed assets under the Swamitva National Framework for Asset resolution (SNFA). The central bank has now barred lenders from selling such assets to the same borrower who originally defaulted on the loan.

The new directive aims to close a loophole that allowed defaulters to buy back their own debts at a discount. This practice often defeated the purpose of asset recovery and encouraged moral hazard.

What the new rule says

Under the updated SNFA guidelines, banks cannot sell a stressed asset to the borrower who defaulted, or to any entity connected to that borrower. This includes relatives, business associates, or companies where the defaulter holds a significant stake.

The RBI has clarified that the restriction applies to all sales conducted through the SNFA platform. The framework was originally designed to bring transparency and efficiency to the resolution of bad loans.

Why the RBI stepped in

Bankers and regulators had noticed a pattern where defaulters used proxy entities to repurchase their own loans. These repurchases were often done at a fraction of the original loan value, leaving banks with significant losses.

By barring such sales, the RBI hopes to ensure that asset resolution actually results in recovery for lenders. The move also discourages borrowers from deliberately defaulting with the intention of buying back assets cheaply.

The central bank has not yet specified the effective date of the new rule. Banks are expected to receive detailed operational guidelines shortly.

Impact on banks and borrowers

For banks, the new rule means they must now conduct deeper due diligence on buyers of stressed assets. Lenders will need to verify that the purchaser is not linked to the original defaulter.

This could slow down the resolution process in the short term. However, the RBI believes the long-term benefits of improved recovery rates will outweigh the initial compliance burden.

For defaulting borrowers, the option of quietly buying back their own loans is now off the table. They will have to negotiate directly with lenders or face the possibility of losing assets to third-party buyers.

What analysts are saying

Banking analysts have broadly welcomed the move. Many had flagged the conflict of interest inherent in allowing defaulters to repurchase their own debt.

“It was a perverse incentive,” one analyst said. “The new rule aligns the SNFA framework with the original intent of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code.”

The RBI has been steadily tightening norms around asset resolution. This latest step is part of a broader push to clean up bank balance sheets and reduce the stock of non-performing assets.

The central bank will monitor compliance through its supervisory mechanisms. Banks found violating the new rule could face penalties.

What to watch: Banks will now need to update their internal policies and sale documentation to comply with the new restrictions. The RBI is expected to release a detailed circular in the coming weeks, clarifying the scope of the rule and any transitional arrangements.
