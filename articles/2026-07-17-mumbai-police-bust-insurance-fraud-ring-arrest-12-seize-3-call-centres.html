Mumbai Police Bust Insurance Fraud Ring, Arrest 12, Seize 3 Call Centres

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • 12 individuals arrested in connection with an insurance fraud scheme.
  • Three call centres operating in Mumbai were raided and closed.
  • Fraudulent calls were made to policyholders, extracting Rs 17 lakh.
  • Police are investigating if the accused had ties to larger criminal networks.

The Mumbai Police have arrested 12 people and shut down three call centres in a coordinated operation that uncovered a Rs 17 lakh insurance fraud. The accused are believed to have posed as insurance agents and duped policyholders over the phone.

How the Scam Worked

According to officials, the call centres were operating out of rented premises in the city. The suspects would call policyholders, claiming to be from legitimate insurance companies. They offered lucrative policy upgrades or bonuses, asking victims to pay a small fee to unlock the benefits.

The fraudsters used high-pressure sales tactics and spoofed phone numbers to appear authentic. Victims, believing they were dealing with their own insurers, transferred money. The total amount defrauded has been pegged at Rs 17 lakh, though police suspect the figure could rise as more complaints come in.

The Raids and Arrests

The police acted on a tip-off after a victim filed a complaint. Teams from the cyber crime wing conducted simultaneous raids at three locations. They seized computers, mobile phones, and documents used to track potential targets.

All 12 accused have been booked under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code and the Information Technology Act. They are currently in police custody and being questioned about their modus operandi. Investigators are trying to trace if the call centres were part of a larger syndicate operating across multiple states.

Modus Operandi Revealed

Preliminary investigations reveal that the accused maintained detailed databases of insurance policyholders. They would call people who had recently renewed their policies or claimed a settlement. The fraudsters exploited this information to build trust quickly.

The calls were made in a professional manner, often using scripts. Victims were told that their policies were due for a special upgrade that required a one-time payment. Once the money was transferred, the numbers became unreachable.

Police Advisory for Citizens

The Mumbai Police have urged citizens to be cautious of unsolicited calls regarding insurance or financial schemes. They advise people to never share personal details or make payments over the phone without verifying the caller's identity.

Officials recommend contacting the insurance company directly using the official customer care number if such a call is received. Complaints can also be lodged on the cyber crime portal.

The investigation is ongoing. Police are examining the seized devices for evidence of other potential victims or larger financial trails.

What happens next: The accused will be produced in court for remand. Police are also expected to issue a public notice asking other victims to come forward and file complaints.
