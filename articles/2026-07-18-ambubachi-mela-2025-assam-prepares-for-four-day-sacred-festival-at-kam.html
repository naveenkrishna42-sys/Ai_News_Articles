Ambubachi Mela 2025: Assam Prepares for Four-Day Sacred Festival at Kamakhya Temple

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ambubachi Mela 2025 will be observed for four days in June at Kamakhya Temple in Guwahati.
  • The festival marks the annual menstruation period of Goddess Kamakhya, symbolising fertility and power.
  • Key rituals include temple closure for three days, followed by reopening with special offerings and a large fair.
  • Devotees from across India, including sadhus and tantric practitioners, gather for the event.
  • The mela is deeply rooted in Assamese culture and the Shakti tradition of Hinduism.

Dates and Timings for Ambubachi Mela 2025

Ambubachi Mela 2025 is set to begin on the evening of June 22 and will conclude on June 26, officials have confirmed. The four-day festival is one of the most significant religious gatherings in Assam, drawing lakhs of devotees to the Kamakhya Temple atop the Nilachal Hill in Guwahati.

The temple will remain closed for three days starting June 22, reopening on June 26 after the performance of essential rituals. The exact timings of the temple closure and reopening depend on astrological calculations determined by the temple priests.

Rituals and Core Beliefs

The Ambubachi Mela marks the annual menstruation period of Goddess Kamakhya, a central deity in the Shakti tradition of Hinduism. During this time, the goddess is believed to be in a state of seclusion, and the temple doors are shut to devotees.

No regular worship is performed. Instead, the temple premises are cleaned and sanctified. On the fourth day, the temple reopens with special pujas, and prasad — including red cloth and sacred water — is distributed among devotees. The prasad is considered highly auspicious, especially for women seeking fertility or blessings.

Devotees observe a strict fast during the three days, refraining from cooking or eating at home. Many take a holy dip in the Brahmaputra River before visiting the temple after its reopening.

Cultural Significance and the Gathering of Ascetics

Ambubachi Mela is not just a religious event — it is a deep cultural marker for Assam and the broader Hindu tradition. The festival symbolises the earth's fertility, the power of the feminine divine, and the cyclical nature of life. It is one of the few major Hindu festivals where menstruation is openly celebrated as a sacred and powerful phenomenon.

The mela attracts a large number of sadhus, tantric practitioners, and ascetics from across India. They set up camps near the temple and engage in meditation, discussions, and rituals. The presence of these holy men adds a distinct spiritual energy to the gathering, making it a unique confluence of devotion and mysticism.

The fair that accompanies the mela is a major attraction. Hundreds of stalls selling religious items, local handicrafts, and food line the pathways leading to the temple. The Assam government makes extensive arrangements for security, sanitation, and crowd management during this period.

What Happens Next

With the temple reopening on June 26, the main day of the mela will see the largest influx of devotees. The government has advised devotees to plan their travel and stay in advance. The Ambubachi Mela remains a powerful testament to the living traditions of Shakti worship in India, blending ancient rituals with a massive, modern-day congregation of faith.
