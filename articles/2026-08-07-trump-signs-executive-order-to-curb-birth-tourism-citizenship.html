Trump Signs Executive Order to Curb 'Birth Tourism' Citizenship

📅 2026-08-07 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump signs executive order to curb 'birth tourism' citizenship
  • The move follows a Supreme Court setback for the administration
  • Critics argue the order violates the 14th Amendment
  • The order directs federal agencies to deny visas to birth tourists
  • Legal challenges are expected from civil rights groups

Executive Order Targets 'Birth Tourism'

President Donald Trump has signed an executive order aimed at curbing what his administration calls 'birth tourism' — the practice of foreign nationals traveling to the United States to give birth and secure automatic citizenship for their children. The order directs federal agencies to deny visa applications from individuals suspected of intending to give birth on US soil.

The move comes despite a significant legal setback from the US Supreme Court, which earlier this year declined to hear a case that could have redefined the 14th Amendment's citizenship clause. The amendment, ratified in 1868, grants citizenship to all persons born or naturalized in the United States.

What the Order Does

The executive order instructs the State Department to tighten visa screening procedures, particularly for pregnant women from countries with high rates of birth tourism. It also mandates the Department of Homeland Security to scrutinize existing visa holders who may give birth during their stay.

Officials argue the measure is necessary to close loopholes in immigration law and reduce the burden on US hospitals, which often bear the cost of uninsured births. However, the administration has not yet confirmed how many births annually are attributable to birth tourism, with estimates ranging widely from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands.

Legal Hurdles Ahead

Civil rights groups and constitutional scholars have already signaled they will challenge the order in court. They argue that birthright citizenship is enshrined in the 14th Amendment and cannot be overturned by executive action alone.

The Supreme Court's earlier refusal to hear a related case suggests the justices are wary of revisiting settled precedent. Legal experts say any attempt to deny citizenship to children born in the US would likely face an uphill battle in federal courts.

Impact on Indian Nationals

For Indian families, the order could have significant implications. India is among the top source countries for birth tourism, with many families traveling to the US to secure citizenship for their newborns, a practice that is legal but increasingly scrutinized.

The order's visa restrictions may affect pregnant Indian women seeking tourist visas, though the administration has not specified how it will identify potential birth tourists without discriminatory profiling. Indian diplomatic missions have not yet issued a formal response.

Political Reactions

Supporters of the order, including anti-immigration groups, have praised the move as a step toward enforcing immigration laws. They argue that birthright citizenship encourages illegal immigration and exploits a loophole in the system.

Opponents, including Democratic lawmakers, have condemned the order as unconstitutional and a distraction from broader immigration reform. Some have called for legislation to codify birthright citizenship, a move that would require a two-thirds majority in both houses of Congress.

As legal battles loom, the order's implementation will likely be paused pending judicial review. The administration has said it is prepared to defend the measure, but constitutional experts remain skeptical.

What Happens Next

The order is expected to face an immediate injunction from lower courts, setting the stage for a prolonged legal fight. All eyes will be on the Supreme Court, which may eventually be forced to clarify the scope of the 14th Amendment in the modern immigration context.

For now, the executive order remains a symbolic victory for Trump's base, but its practical impact will depend on the courts. Observers should watch for the first legal challenge, which is likely to be filed within days.
