Israel Rejects Trump's Gaza Plan, Demands Hamas Disarm First

📅 2026-08-09 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Israel has formally rejected former US President Trump's 15-point Gaza peace plan.
  • Prime Minister Netanyahu stated there will be no pullout of Israeli forces until Hamas is fully disarmed.
  • The rejection underscores a hardening Israeli position amid stalled ceasefire negotiations.

Netanyahu's Firm Stand Against US Proposal

Israel has formally rejected the 15-point Gaza peace plan put forward by former US President Donald Trump, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu making clear that no withdrawal of Israeli forces will occur until Hamas is disarmed. The announcement, reported across multiple international outlets, signals a significant rift between Jerusalem and Washington over the future of the Gaza Strip.

Speaking to reporters, Netanyahu struck a defiant tone, asserting, "We know how to stand up to American ideas." The prime minister's remarks reflect a broader Israeli consensus that security guarantees must precede any political arrangement, a position that has gained traction among hardline members of his coalition.

Details of the Rejected Plan

The Trump plan, which has been circulating in diplomatic circles for weeks, reportedly includes a phased Israeli withdrawal, a temporary governance framework, and internationally backed reconstruction efforts. However, Israeli officials have privately expressed concerns that the plan does not adequately address the disarmament of Hamas, the militant group that has controlled Gaza since 2007.

According to sources familiar with the negotiations, the plan's 15 points were designed to create a gradual de-escalation, with confidence-building measures on both sides. Yet, for Israel, the absence of a binding mechanism to ensure Hamas's demilitarization rendered the proposal untenable.

Divergence in US-Israel Relations

The rejection marks a notable shift in the dynamics between the two allies. While the Trump administration had previously enjoyed close ties with Netanyahu's government, this episode highlights a growing divergence over how to handle the Gaza crisis. The White House has not yet issued an official response, but diplomatic observers suggest that the rebuff could strain ongoing mediation efforts led by other regional actors.

Analysts note that Israel's stance is likely to complicate the already fragile ceasefire talks. With Hamas refusing to disarm and Israel demanding it as a precondition, the path to a lasting truce appears increasingly narrow. Regional powers, including Egypt and Qatar, have been quietly pushing for a compromise, but so far, neither side has shown flexibility.

Humanitarian and Political Stakes

The standoff carries profound humanitarian implications for the enclave's 2.3 million residents, who have endured repeated cycles of violence and a crippling blockade. Reconstruction efforts, which require international funding and coordination, remain stalled without a political breakthrough. Aid agencies have warned that the situation is untenable, but their appeals have yet to translate into tangible progress.

Politically, Netanyahu's hardline position is seen as a bid to shore up support among his right-wing base, especially as domestic pressure mounts over the handling of the conflict. Meanwhile, the opposition has accused him of missing an opportunity to de-escalate, though such criticisms have done little to alter the government's course.

What Comes Next

With the US plan now off the table, attention is turning to alternative frameworks, including a possible UN-backed initiative or renewed shuttle diplomacy by regional mediators. Israeli officials have indicated they remain open to discussions, but only if the disarmament issue is addressed upfront.

For now, the Gaza ceasefire hangs in a delicate balance, with both sides digging in. The coming weeks will be critical in determining whether a revised proposal can bridge the gap or whether the region slides back into confrontation.
