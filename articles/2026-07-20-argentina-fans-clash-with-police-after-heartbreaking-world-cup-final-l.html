Argentina Fans Clash With Police After Heartbreaking World Cup Final Loss to Spain

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Massive fan gatherings in Buenos Aires turned violent after Argentina's World Cup final loss to Spain.
  • Police deployed tear gas and water cannons to disperse crowds, leading to scuffles and injuries.
  • Two fans died of heart attacks during the match, officials confirmed.
  • Despite the loss, many supporters gathered to salute the team's campaign, with chants for Lionel Messi.

Buenos Aires erupted in chaos late Sunday night after Argentina fell to Spain in a tense FIFA World Cup 2026 final. What began as a somber gathering of fans hoping to console their team quickly spiraled into violent confrontations with police.

Thousands had poured onto the streets of the capital, many still draped in the blue-and-white stripes, their faces streaked with tears. But as the reality of the 2-1 defeat sank in, frustration boiled over in several neighborhoods.

Police Take Major Step to Control Crowds

Riot police moved in swiftly after groups of fans began overturning vehicles and setting trash bins on fire near the Obelisco, the traditional site of celebrations. Officers fired tear gas canisters and used water cannons to break up the crowds, leading to running battles that lasted well past midnight.

Local authorities have not yet confirmed the number of arrests or injuries from the clashes. However, reports from the scene describe chaotic scenes as families with children fled the tear gas clouds.

Two Fatal Heart Attacks Reported

Amid the emotional turmoil, health officials reported that two male fans, aged 34 and 52, died of heart attacks while watching the final match. Both were rushed to nearby hospitals but could not be revived. The news added a somber note to an already tense night.

These were not isolated incidents. Emergency services across the country reported a spike in calls related to cardiac issues and panic attacks during and after the match.

Fans Still Flood Streets to Salute the Team

Despite the violence, a different sort of gathering took shape in other parts of the city. Thousands of fans marched peacefully to the Obelisco, not to riot, but to applaud the team's campaign.

Chants of "Messi, Messi, Messi" rang out as fans waved flags and lit flares in a show of gratitude. "Messi took us to the top of the world," one supporter told reporters. "We lost tonight, but this team gave us everything."

The Buenos Aires Times described the scene as a flood of emotion, with fans packing the streets to salute the squad despite the final defeat. Social media was awash with videos of crowds singing the national anthem long after the final whistle.

Scuffles Erupt as Police Disperse Gatherings

However, even these peaceful gatherings were not entirely spared. Police moved in to disperse large crowds in several districts, citing concerns over public safety and the potential for further unrest. Scuffles broke out as some fans refused to leave, leading to brief but tense standoffs.

Witnesses reported that the police action was heavy-handed in some cases, pushing back elderly fans and families. Authorities have defended the operation, stating that they were forced to act to prevent the situation from escalating further.

What happens next is uncertain. The Argentine Football Association has called for calm, while the government is expected to review the police response. For now, a nation that came so close to glory is left to mourn not just a lost trophy, but the lives that were lost along the way.
