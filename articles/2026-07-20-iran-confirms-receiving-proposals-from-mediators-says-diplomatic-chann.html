Iran Confirms Receiving Proposals From Mediators, Says Diplomatic Channel Active

📅 2026-07-20 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran confirms proposals received from mediators, diplomatic channel still active
  • Iran says US talks could be pursued based on national interests
  • Oil prices steady as hopes of renewed negotiations offset Houthi threats

Tehran has confirmed that it has received proposals from mediators and that the diplomatic channel with the United States remains active, state-linked media reported on Monday. The announcement comes as regional tensions simmer over Iran's nuclear programme and attacks on shipping in the Red Sea.

Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson said the country is in touch with interlocutors and will weigh any proposal based on its national interests. The statement did not disclose the content of the proposals or name the mediators.

Oil Markets React to Diplomatic Signals

Global oil prices erased earlier gains after Iran signalled it could pursue talks with the US under the right conditions. Benchmark crude had risen on fears of supply disruptions from the Strait of Hormuz, but the diplomatic overture eased some of that anxiety.

Analysts said the market is caught between two forces — the threat of Houthi attacks on Red Sea shipping and the possibility of a renewed US-Iran negotiation track. Brent crude steadied near $82 a barrel in afternoon trade.

The Houthis, an Iran-aligned group in Yemen, have launched multiple strikes on commercial vessels in recent weeks, disrupting a key global trade route. Iran has denied direct involvement but has voiced support for the group.

Iran's Dual Strategy: Diplomacy and War

Iranian officials have stated that Tehran will pursue both diplomacy and military means to advance its interests. This dual approach has been consistent since the collapse of the 2015 nuclear deal and the reimposition of US sanctions.

Reports from Israeli media suggest that Iran is suspending some commitments under an interim agreement with the US, even as the two sides exchange attacks through proxies. The exact nature of those commitments remains unclear.

Officials in Washington have not commented on the latest Iranian statements. The US administration has repeatedly said it prefers a diplomatic resolution but will not hesitate to use force to protect its assets and allies.

Nuclear Deal Uncertainties

The broader context of Iran's announcement is the stalled negotiation over reviving the Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA). Talks in Vienna have been on hold for months, with both sides blaming each other for the lack of progress.

Iran has enriched uranium to levels close to weapons-grade, according to the International Atomic Energy Agency. Tehran insists its programme is peaceful but has refused to roll back enrichment until all sanctions are lifted.

The US has imposed fresh sanctions on Iranian entities and individuals in recent weeks, citing human rights abuses and support for militant groups. Iran has responded by accelerating its nuclear activities and expanding its missile programme.

What Happens Next

Diplomatic efforts are expected to continue in the coming weeks, with Gulf Arab states and European powers likely to play mediating roles. The path to a deal remains narrow, but Monday's statement from Tehran suggests the door is not yet closed.

Markets and governments will be watching closely for any sign of a breakthrough — or a breakdown — that could reshape the region's security and energy landscape.
