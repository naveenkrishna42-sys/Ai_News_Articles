Parliament clears taxation bill; FM assures UPI stays free, MDR framework pending

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Parliament clears the taxation bill amid discussions on digital payments.
  • FM Sitharaman assures UPI transactions will remain free for users.
  • No framework on Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) has been announced yet.

Taxation Bill Passed in Parliament

Parliament has cleared the taxation bill, marking a significant legislative step. The bill's passage comes after deliberations that touched on various aspects of the economy, including digital payments.

Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman, responding to concerns raised during the debate, addressed the future of UPI transactions. She stated unequivocally that UPI will remain free for users.

UPI Charges Ruled Out for Now

The Finance Minister's assurance puts to rest speculation about a possible fee on UPI transactions. For millions of Indians who rely on UPI for daily payments, this comes as a relief.

However, Sitharaman did not announce any framework for Merchant Discount Rate (MDR). MDR is the fee paid by merchants to banks and payment processors for accepting digital payments.

No MDR Framework Yet

The absence of an MDR framework means the issue remains unresolved for now. Merchants and payment companies have been waiting for clarity on this front.

Industry observers had expected the bill to address MDR, given its impact on the digital payments ecosystem. The government's silence on this matter suggests further deliberation is needed.

What This Means for Digital Payments

For end-users, the status quo continues—digital payments via UPI will not incur additional costs. This aligns with the government's push for a cashless economy.

For banks and payment service providers, the lack of MDR clarity could affect their revenue models. They have often cited the need for a sustainable fee structure to support the infrastructure.

Context of the Taxation Bill

The taxation bill is part of broader efforts to streamline India's tax regime. Its passage in Parliament reflects a consensus on certain fiscal measures, though specifics of the bill were not detailed in the discussions.

The debate also touched on the costs end-users face when making digital payments. Lawmakers raised questions about transparency and affordability in the digital payments space.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether a separate policy on MDR is in the works. The finance ministry has not issued a detailed statement beyond the FM's remarks.

What to Watch Next

All eyes will now be on the government's next move regarding MDR. A formal framework could reshape the economics of digital payments in India.

Stakeholders, including merchants and fintech firms, are likely to seek clarity in the coming weeks. For now, UPI users can continue transacting without worrying about charges.
