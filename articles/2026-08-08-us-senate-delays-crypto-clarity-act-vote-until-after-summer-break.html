US Senate delays crypto Clarity Act vote until after summer break

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Senate postpones Clarity Act vote until after August recess
  • Senate Majority Leader John Thune confirms the delay
  • Crypto lobby groups express opposition to the bill's provisions

Senate defers action on crypto bill

The US Senate will not hold a vote on the Clarity Act, a proposed law aimed at regulating digital assets, before its summer break. Senate Majority Leader John Thune confirmed the delay, pushing the vote to after the August recess. The decision ends weeks of speculation over whether the chamber would act before lawmakers leave Washington.

The Clarity Act has been a focal point for the crypto industry, which has lobbied heavily for clearer rules. But the bill has also drawn criticism from some quarters, including portions of the crypto lobby itself, who argue the current draft does not go far enough in addressing industry concerns.

What the Clarity Act proposes

The legislation seeks to define which federal agency has authority over digital assets, a question that has dogged the market for years. It would also set out rules for when a cryptocurrency is considered a security versus a commodity. Industry players have long complained that the lack of clarity hampers innovation and pushes business overseas.

However, the bill's approach has not satisfied everyone. Some crypto advocates believe it grants too much power to existing regulators, while others worry it could impose burdensome compliance costs on smaller projects. The delay gives both sides more time to press their case with senators.

Lobbying intensifies before recess

In the run-up to the recess, lobbying activity around the bill intensified. Reports indicate that crypto firms and trade groups have been meeting with lawmakers to shape the final text. The CoinDesk opinion piece titled "The Crypto Lobby Objects on the Clarity Act" highlights the industry's unease with certain provisions, even as it supports the overarching goal of regulatory clarity.

Thune's confirmation, first reported by Politico, ends any chance of a quick vote. The Senate will now take up the bill when it returns in September, leaving the crypto market in a state of regulatory limbo for at least another month.

Market reaction muted for now

Bitcoin and other major cryptocurrencies showed little immediate reaction to the news, with traders seemingly having priced in the delay. Analysts note that the broader market has been more focused on macroeconomic factors, including interest rate expectations and inflation data. Still, the postponement means uncertainty around US crypto regulation will persist into the autumn.

The delay also gives opponents of the bill more time to mobilise. Cryptocurrency exchange Binance and other major players have been vocal in their criticism of certain provisions, arguing that the law as drafted could stifle innovation. Their lobbying efforts are expected to intensify over the coming weeks.

What happens next

When the Senate returns after Labour Day, the Clarity Act will be among the priority items on the legislative calendar. Thune has not indicated whether amendments are planned, but the lobbying push suggests changes are likely. The outcome of the vote could set the tone for US crypto policy for years to come.

Observers will be watching whether the bill's sponsors can secure enough support to pass it, or whether further delays and revisions lie ahead. For now, the industry must wait a while longer for the regulatory clarity it has been demanding.
