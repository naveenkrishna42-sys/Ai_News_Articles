8 must-visit pilgrimage sites in India

  • Practical visiting information for each site

Introduction

India is a land of diverse spiritual traditions, with pilgrimage sites that cater to followers of Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism, and Sikhism. This article explores eight must-visit pilgrimage sites, each with unique cultural and historical significance.

Hindu Pilgrimage Sites

1. Varanasi

Varanasi, one of the oldest living cities in the world, is a major pilgrimage site for Hindus. The city is believed to be the spiritual capital of India, and its ghats (riverfront steps) are where millions of Hindus perform cremations and rituals. The Kashi Vishwanath Temple, dedicated to Lord Shiva, is the most famous attraction in Varanasi.

2. Rameswaram

Located on the southern tip of India, Rameswaram is an important pilgrimage site for Hindus. The city is associated with Lord Rama, and the Rameswaram Temple, dedicated to Lord Rama, is the most famous attraction. The temple is built on a small island connected to the mainland by a causeway, and it is believed to be one of the 12 Jyotirlingas, or divine abodes of Lord Shiva.

3. Dwarka

Dwarka is an ancient city on the western coast of India, and it is one of the seven sacred cities of Hinduism. The city is associated with Lord Krishna, and the Dwarkadhish Temple, dedicated to Lord Krishna, is the most famous attraction. The temple is built on a small island, and it is believed to be one of the 12 Jyotirlingas.

Jain Pilgrimage Sites

4. Mount Abu

Mount Abu is a hill station in the Aravalli Range, and it is an important pilgrimage site for Jains. The city is home to the Dilwara Temples, a group of five Hindu temples that were later converted into Jain temples. The temples are known for their intricate marble carvings and stunning architecture.

5. Shravanabelagola

Shravanabelagola is a town in Karnataka, and it is an important pilgrimage site for Jains. The town is home to the Keshava Temple, a 7th-century temple dedicated to Lord Gomateshvara, the 23rd Tirthankara of Jainism. The temple is known for its massive monolithic statue of Lord Gomateshvara, which is carved out of a single rock.

Buddhist Pilgrimage Sites

6. Sarnath

Sarnath is a town in Uttar Pradesh, and it is an important pilgrimage site for Buddhists. The town is associated with the First Sermon of the Buddha, and the Dhamek Stupa, a large stupa built in the 5th century BCE, is the most famous attraction. The stupa is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and it is one of the most important Buddhist pilgrimage sites in India.

Sikh Pilgrimage Sites

7. Amritsar

Amritsar is a city in Punjab, and it is an important pilgrimage site for Sikhs. The city is home to the Golden Temple, a major gurudwara (Sikh place of worship) that is one of the holiest sites in Sikhism. The temple is built around a sacred tank, and it is believed to be the resting place of the fifth Guru of the Sikhs, Guru Arjan Dev.

8. Anandpur Sahib

Anandpur Sahib is a town in Punjab, and it is an important pilgrimage site for Sikhs. The town is home to the Anandpur Sahib Gurudwara, a major gurudwara that is associated with the sixth Guru of the Sikhs, Guru Hargobind. The gurudwara is built on the site of a battle between the Sikhs and the Mughals, and it is a symbol of Sikh resistance and resilience.

Practical Visiting Information

India's pilgrimage sites are open year-round, with the best time to visit being from October to March, when the weather is pleasant and comfortable. The nearest major airports are Indira Gandhi International Airport in Delhi, Chennai International Airport, and Bengaluru International Airport. Visitors can also reach the sites by train, with many pilgrimage sites having their own railway stations.

Nearby attractions worth combining into the same trip include the Taj Mahal in Agra, the backwaters of Kerala, and the hill stations of Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand.
