NASA’s Perseverance rover finds signs of giant asteroid impact on ancient Mars

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Perseverance rover discovered layered rock structures indicating a cataclysmic asteroid impact on ancient Mars.
  • The impact is estimated to have occurred around 4 billion years ago, comparable in scale to the Chicxulub event on Earth.
  • The findings were reported by BBC Sky at Night Magazine and other outlets, with NASA confirming the discovery.

Mars’ ancient scar revealed

NASA’s Perseverance rover has stumbled upon evidence of a colossal asteroid impact on Mars that took place roughly 4 billion years ago. Scientists say the event was as powerful as the one that wiped out the dinosaurs on Earth.

The discovery came as the rover examined layered rock structures near the Jezero Crater. These formations, described as honeycomb-like in pattern, point to a massive撞击 that reshaped the Martian surface.

What the rover found

Perseverance’s cameras captured images of giant, layered rock structures that geologists believe were formed by the heat and pressure of an asteroid strike. The honeycomb patterns, initially spotted by the Curiosity rover in a different region, have now been confirmed by Perseverance’s instruments.

NASA scientists have not yet confirmed the exact size of the asteroid, but early estimates suggest it could have been several kilometres wide. The impact would have carved a crater hundreds of kilometres across, though erosion and volcanic activity have since obscured much of the evidence.

Comparing Earth and Mars

The Chicxulub impact 66 million years ago ended the reign of dinosaurs on Earth. On Mars, this ancient collision happened when the planet was still young and potentially habitable. The timing — roughly 4 billion years ago — coincides with the Late Heavy Bombardment, a period when the inner solar system was pelted by asteroids.

Unlike Earth, Mars lacks plate tectonics, which means its ancient impact craters are better preserved. The honeycomb structures found by Perseverance are likely the remains of shocked rock, melted by the extreme temperatures of the impact.

What this means for Martian history

This discovery adds weight to the theory that Mars was once a much more dynamic world. The impact may have triggered volcanic activity or even hydrothermal systems, creating environments that could have supported microbial life.

Perseverance is currently collecting rock samples from the area. NASA plans to bring these samples back to Earth in the 2030s for detailed analysis. Scientists hope to find traces of ancient organic molecules or even fossilised microbes.

The rover’s findings were first reported by BBC Sky at Night Magazine and later confirmed by multiple outlets including NDTV and Space.com. NASA’s official website posted images of the Broom Point region, where the honeycomb patterns were most visible.

Looking ahead

NASA will now direct Perseverance to conduct a more detailed survey of the impact site. The rover’s SHERLOC and PIXL instruments will scan the rocks for chemical signatures. If the impact did create a habitable niche, the evidence could be right beneath the rover’s wheels.
