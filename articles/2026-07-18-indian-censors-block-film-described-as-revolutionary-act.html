Indian censors block film described as 'revolutionary act'

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • CBFC denied certification to the film, blocking its theatrical release.
  • The film's makers and supporters view the ban as a form of censorship.
  • The case has reignited debates over artistic freedom and censorship in India.

CBFC blocks controversial film

The Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC) has refused to certify a film that critics and supporters alike have described as a 'revolutionary act to watch'. The decision effectively prevents the movie from being screened in theatres across India.

The film, whose title has not been officially disclosed, has been at the centre of a growing controversy. Its makers argue that the CBFC's move is an act of censorship that stifles creative expression.

The 'revolutionary act' label

Supporters of the film have rallied around the phrase 'a revolutionary act to watch it', coined by a prominent critic. The line has become a rallying cry for those who believe the film challenges what they call outdated norms.

The CBFC, however, has not publicly detailed the specific objections that led to the denial. Sources close to the board suggest that certain scenes and themes were deemed controversial under the Cinematograph Act, 1952.

Makers explore legal options

The film's producers are now considering legal recourse. They plan to appeal the CBFC's decision before the Film Certification Appellate Tribunal (FCAT).

Legal experts say the case could set a precedent for how the board handles politically or socially sensitive content. 'This is not just about one film,' a lawyer familiar with the matter said. 'It tests the limits of what can be shown on screen.'

Debate over censorship reignites

The controversy has sparked fresh discussions about censorship in India. Filmmakers, artists and activists have taken to social media to voice their support for the movie.

Many point to a history of CBFC cuts and bans, from 'Bandit Queen' to 'Padmaavat'. They argue that the board's approach often lags behind changing social mores.

What happens next

The FCAT hearing is expected in the coming weeks. Until then, the film remains in limbo, unable to reach audiences in theatres.

The outcome will be closely watched by the film industry and those who advocate for fewer restrictions on artistic expression. For now, the question remains whether the film will ever see the light of day in Indian cinemas.
