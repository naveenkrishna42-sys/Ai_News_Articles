Hindustan Copper OFS: Govt plans deeper stake cut

📅 2026-08-26 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government to sell additional 3% stake in Hindustan Copper via OFS
  • Total stake sale could reach 6%
  • OFS opens at 10% discount to retail investors
  • Analysts doubt strong retail investor interest in the OFS

Government Plans Deeper Stake Cut in Hindustan Copper

The government is set to sell an additional 3% stake in Hindustan Copper Limited (HCL) via an Offer for Sale (OFS), pushing the total stake sale to 6%. This move comes after the government already sold 3% stake in the company earlier this year. The OFS opens at a 10% discount to retail investors, offering shares at ₹1,760 each, down from the ₹1,950 issue price.

Market Reaction to OFS

Hindustan Copper shares have seen their biggest fall in five months, with the stock price dropping by over 5% on the news. This decline is likely due to the government's decision to open the OFS at a discount, which may not be attractive to retail investors. Analysts have noted that the OFS may not be a big draw for retail investors, as the discount offered is relatively small and the total stake sale is limited.

Broader Implications for Hindustan Copper

The stake sale by the government is part of the company's strategic plan to reduce its stake and focus on core operations. Hindustan Copper is a key player in the copper industry in India, and the government's stake sale is expected to have a significant impact on the company's financial health. The reduction in government stake will also pave the way for more private investment in the company, which could lead to further growth and development.

Analyst Perspectives on OFS

Analysts have mixed views on the OFS. While some believe that the government's decision to open the OFS at a discount is a strategic move to attract more investors, others are skeptical. They argue that the discount offered is not substantial enough to attract significant interest from retail investors. Additionally, the limited stake sale could mean that the government may not be able to raise the funds it needs to support the company's operations.
