Bitcoin Drops Below $63K as AI Slump and Middle East Worries Hit Markets

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin (BTC) price slipped under $63,000 on Friday.
  • AI-related stock weakness and Middle East tensions are weighing on risk assets.
  • Tech stocks also declined, reflecting broader market caution.

Bitcoin slid below $63,000 on Friday, extending a recent pullback as a double dose of negative sentiment — fatigue around artificial intelligence stocks and escalating tensions in the Middle East — dragged down both cryptocurrency and equity markets.

The world's largest digital asset was trading near $62,800 in Asian afternoon hours, down roughly 3% in the past 24 hours. The move mirrored a broader sell-off in technology shares, with the Nasdaq Composite falling more than 1% in Thursday's US session.

AI Fatigue Hits Risk Appetite

Investors have grown wary of the high valuations in AI-related stocks after a string of earnings reports failed to impress. Companies that had previously ridden the AI wave to record highs are now facing scrutiny over profitability and return on investment.

This caution has spilled over into crypto markets, where traders often treat Bitcoin as a high-beta tech proxy. The correlation between Bitcoin and tech stocks has remained elevated in 2026, making the asset vulnerable to any shift in risk sentiment.

CoinDesk data shows that Bitcoin has now erased most of the gains made earlier this month, when it briefly touched $68,000. The current level is a far cry from the all-time highs above $73,000 seen in early 2024.

Middle East Tensions Add to Pressure

Geopolitical risks are compounding the sell-off. Fresh flare-ups in the Middle East have pushed oil prices higher and sent investors scrambling for safe-haven assets like gold and US Treasuries.

Gold, often seen as a hedge against uncertainty, edged up 0.4% to $2,340 an ounce. Meanwhile, Bitcoin — sometimes touted as digital gold — failed to attract the same safe-haven flows, highlighting its continued sensitivity to broader risk-off moves.

Analysts point out that crypto markets tend to react negatively to sudden geopolitical shocks, especially when they threaten global supply chains or energy prices.

Tech Stocks Join the Slide

The sell-off wasn't limited to crypto. Major tech names including Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta Platforms all closed lower on Thursday. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped nearly 2%, reflecting deepening concerns over demand for AI chips.

Some market participants believe the AI trade has become overcrowded. A correction, they argue, was overdue. The knock-on effect on Bitcoin underscores how interconnected digital assets have become with traditional financial markets.

Despite the downturn, trading volumes for Bitcoin remain healthy. Data from CoinMarketCap shows daily turnover of around $25 billion, suggesting that the pullback is being met with active buying and selling rather than panic.

Regulatory developments in the US and Europe continue to move slowly, with no major policy announcements expected in the near term. That leaves market direction largely in the hands of macroeconomic and geopolitical factors.

What to watch next: All eyes are on the upcoming US jobs report and Federal Reserve commentary for clues on interest rate policy. Any hint of a dovish pivot could reignite risk appetite and lift Bitcoin back above $63,000.
