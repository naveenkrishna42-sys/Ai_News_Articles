Tribal body seeks stronger safeguards for Girijana rights in Telangana

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Girijana Aikya Vedika has demanded stronger safeguards for tribal rights
  • The demand comes amid concerns over land, forest and welfare issues
  • The organisation has urged the Telangana government to act

Hyderabad: The Girijana Aikya Vedika, a tribal rights organisation, has called on the Telangana government to put in place stronger safeguards for the rights of Girijana (tribal) communities. The demand was made on Monday, with the group pressing for clearer legal and administrative protections.

The Vedika's appeal comes at a time when tribal groups across the state have voiced concerns over land alienation, forest rights and the implementation of welfare schemes. The organisation argues that existing safeguards, though present on paper, often fail to translate into real protection on the ground.

Core demands of the Vedika

The organisation has sought a review of current policies governing tribal lands and forest resources. It has specifically asked for stricter enforcement of laws that prevent transfer of tribal land to non-tribals, a recurring issue in several districts.

Besides land rights, the Vedika has flagged gaps in the delivery of education, healthcare and livelihood support to remote tribal hamlets. Leaders of the organisation say that without robust safeguards, development programmes often bypass the very communities they are meant to serve.

Concerns over forest rights

Another key area of concern is the implementation of the Forest Rights Act. The Vedika has pointed out that many tribal families are yet to receive individual or community forest rights certificates, despite being eligible. This, it says, leaves them vulnerable to eviction and loss of livelihoods.

The organisation has also urged the state to set up a monitoring mechanism to track the status of tribal rights cases. It believes that periodic audits and public reporting could help close the gap between policy and practice.

Government response awaited

Officials in the tribal welfare department have not yet issued a formal response to the Vedika's demands. Sources indicate that the government is likely to examine the representations and may hold consultations with tribal representatives in the coming weeks.

The Girijana Aikya Vedika has said it will continue to press its case through both dialogue and, if needed, public demonstrations. It has asked for a time-bound action plan from the state government.

What to watch

All eyes will now be on the Telangana government's next move. Whether it agrees to a comprehensive review of tribal safeguards, or offers only piecemeal concessions, could set the tone for tribal-state relations in the months ahead. The Vedika has indicated it will escalate its campaign if no tangible progress is made by the end of the year.
