Google Ads Relaxes Business Name Rules: What Advertisers Need to Know

Overview of the Updated Business Name Policy

Google Ads has announced a modification to its business name requirements for a subset of advertisers operating in specific countries. Previously, the platform mandated that the displayed business name exactly match the legal entity name on file with Google. The update now permits the use of a trading name, "doing business as" (DBA), or a commonly recognized brand variation, provided the advertiser can verify the connection through acceptable documentation.

What Changed and Why

The adjustment responds to feedback from local businesses that operate under a brand distinct from their registered legal name, especially in markets where DBAs are common. Google states the goal is to reduce ad disapprovals caused by naming mismatches while maintaining transparency for users. The change does not affect the core policy that prohibits misleading or deceptive representation.

Policy AspectPrevious RuleUpdated Rule
Business Name RequirementMust match legal entity name exactlyMay match legal entity name, registered DBA, or verified trading name
Verification MethodLegal registration document onlyLegal registration OR official DBA/trade name certificate
Geographic ScopeAll regionsSelected regions where DBA usage is prevalent (initial rollout)

Practical Implications for Campaign Performance

Click‑Through Rate and Quality Score Effects

Allowing a brand‑recognizable name can increase ad relevance for users who search by the trading name rather than the legal name. Early data from pilot participants indicates a modest uplift in click‑through rate (CTR) ranging from 3% to 8% in sectors such as retail and hospitality. Because Google’s Quality Score incorporates expected CTR, advertisers may see a corresponding improvement, potentially lowering cost‑per‑click (CPC) by a similar margin.

Cost‑Per‑Acquisition Considerations

When the displayed name aligns with the landing page’s branding, users experience greater trust, which can reduce bounce rates and improve conversion efficiency. Advertisers in the test group reported a cost‑per‑acquisition (CPA) reduction of approximately 5% after updating ad copy to reflect the verified DBA. These gains depend on maintaining consistency across ad assets, extensions, and the destination URL.

Compliance Checks and Risk Mitigation

Verification Steps and Potential Penalties

Advertisers must submit documentation proving the relationship between the legal entity and the chosen trading name. Acceptable files include government‑issued DBA certificates, trademark registrations, or utility bills showing the trade name. Failure to provide valid proof results in disapproval of the affected ads and may trigger a policy warning. Repeated violations could lead to account suspension, underscoring the need for accurate record‑keeping.

Monitoring and Ongoing Vigilance

Google Ads will surface policy notifications in the account’s "Policy Center" whenever a business name edit is submitted. Advertisers should enable email alerts for policy changes and review the naming field during routine ad audits. Keeping a centralized log of approved names and associated documentation simplifies future updates and reduces the risk of accidental non‑compliance.

Strategic Recommendations for Advertisers

Leveraging Flexibility Without Violating Guidelines

First, confirm eligibility in your account’s policy settings; the update is not yet global. Second, ensure the trading name used in ads matches exactly the name on the verification document, including punctuation and spacing. Third, align the ad’s headline, description, and sitelink extensions with the landing page’s branding to reinforce consistency. Finally, measure performance metrics before and after the change to quantify any impact on CTR, CPC, and CPA.

Pros and Cons of the Policy Shift

  • Pros: Greater branding flexibility, potential CTR and Quality Score improvements, reduced ad disapprovals for DBAs.
  • Cons: Added verification workload, risk of policy warnings if documentation is incomplete, limited geographic availability at launch.
