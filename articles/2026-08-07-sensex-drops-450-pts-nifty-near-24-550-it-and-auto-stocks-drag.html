Sensex drops 450 pts, Nifty near 24,550; IT and auto stocks drag

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell nearly 450 points, Nifty near 24,550
  • IT and auto stocks led the decline
  • Market breadth negative; investors await global cues

Market opens weak, extends losses through the day

Indian equity benchmarks ended Friday's session deep in the red, with the BSE Sensex tumbling nearly 450 points and the Nifty hovering near the 24,550 mark. The sell-off was broad-based, but IT and auto stocks bore the brunt, pulling the indices down from the day's opening.

Traders said weak global cues and profit-booking after recent gains weighed on sentiment. The Nifty, which had been hovering around 24,800 in the previous session, lost ground steadily through the afternoon as selling pressure intensified.

IT, auto stocks lead the slide

Information technology stocks were among the worst hit, with heavyweights like Infosys and TCS trading lower. Auto stocks also struggled, with Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra facing selling pressure. Analysts attributed the decline to concerns over slowing demand and valuation concerns after a strong run-up in these sectors.

Meanwhile, midcap and smallcap indices also traded lower, though the losses were relatively contained compared to the largecaps. Sectoral indices on the NSE showed most sectors in the red, with only a few defensive pockets like FMCG showing some resilience.

What's driving the market today

Global cues were largely negative, with Asian markets trading mixed and European indices opening lower. Investors were also cautious ahead of key US jobs data due later in the day, which could influence the Federal Reserve's rate trajectory.

Domestically, concerns over the monsoon's progress and its impact on rural demand added to the cautious mood. However, some analysts noted that the correction was overdue after the recent rally, and the medium-term outlook remains constructive.

Broader market and investor sentiment

The market breadth was negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. Foreign institutional investors were net sellers in the cash market, while domestic institutions were buyers, providing some support to the indices.

Volatility index India VIX rose marginally, indicating some nervousness among traders. Options data suggested that the Nifty is likely to face resistance around the 24,800-25,000 zone, while support is seen at 24,400-24,500.

Looking ahead

Investors will now watch the upcoming earnings season and any further cues from global central banks. The market's direction in the near term will depend on how these factors unfold, along with the progress of the monsoon and any policy announcements from the government.

As the session ended, traders were bracing for more volatility in the coming days, with the Nifty's ability to hold above 24,500 seen as crucial for short-term sentiment.
