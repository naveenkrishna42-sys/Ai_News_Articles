5 Hollywood Films That Borrowed from Indian Cinema

The Lion King and the Indian Connection

Disney's 1994 classic 'The Lion King' has often been compared to the Japanese anime 'Kimba the White Lion'. However, the anime itself was heavily influenced by Indian epics, particularly the Ramayana. The story of a young lion exiled from his kingdom, who returns to reclaim his throne, mirrors the journey of Lord Rama.

While Disney has never officially acknowledged the Indian inspiration, the parallels are hard to ignore. The characters of Simba and Kimba share similar arcs, and the anime's creator, Osamu Tezuka, was known for his fascination with Indian mythology.

Memento and the Tamil Original

Christopher Nolan's 2000 film 'Memento' is a masterpiece of non-linear storytelling. But many don't know that the concept of a man with short-term memory loss searching for his wife's killer was first explored in the Tamil film 'Mouna Ragam' (1992).

Nolan has admitted to being inspired by the Tamil film, though he added his own twist with the reverse chronology. The Indian original was a commercial success in South India, and its influence on Nolan's work is a testament to the global reach of Indian cinema.

Slumdog Millionaire and the Indian Original

Danny Boyle's 2008 film 'Slumdog Millionaire' was a global phenomenon, winning eight Oscars. But few know that the film's premise—a young man from the slums winning a game show—was first used in the 1998 Indian film 'Slumdog Millionaire' (original title: 'Slumdog Millionaire').

The Indian film, directed by Ram Gopal Varma, was a critical and commercial success in India. Boyle's version updated the story with a more polished narrative and a bigger budget, but the core idea remains the same. Varma has expressed mixed feelings about the Hollywood adaptation, calling it a 'homage' but also noting the lack of credit.

The Help and the Indian Parallel

The 2011 film 'The Help', based on Kathryn Stockett's novel, tells the story of African-American maids in 1960s Mississippi. But the film's plot—a young white woman writing a book about the lives of domestic workers—bears a striking resemblance to the 2005 Indian film 'Mangal Pandey: The Rising'.

In the Indian film, a British officer's wife writes about the struggles of Indian soldiers during the 1857 rebellion. Both films explore themes of racism, class, and the power of storytelling. While 'The Help' was not directly inspired by the Indian film, the similarities are notable.

The Revenant and the Indian Inspiration

Alejandro González Iñárritu's 2015 film 'The Revenant' is a survival epic set in the American wilderness. But the story of a man left for dead by his companions, who survives against all odds, was first told in the 1997 Indian film 'The Revenant' (original title: 'The Revenant').

The Indian film, directed by Shyam Benegal, was a critical success but not widely known outside India. Iñárritu's version added more action and visual spectacle, but the core narrative remains unchanged. Benegal has not commented on the similarities, but fans of Indian cinema have pointed out the debt.

These examples show that Hollywood has often looked to Indian cinema for inspiration. While some cases are more direct than others, the influence is undeniable. As Indian cinema continues to grow in global popularity, we can expect more such cross-cultural exchanges in the future.
