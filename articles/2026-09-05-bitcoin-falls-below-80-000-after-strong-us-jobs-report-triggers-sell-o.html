Bitcoin Falls Below $80,000 After Strong US Jobs Report Triggers Sell-Off

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-05 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • US non-farm payrolls likely beat consensus, pushing bond yields higher and pressuring risk assets like Bitcoin.
  • Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows as institutional traders rotated into strengthened US dollar positions.
  • Technical rejection at the $80,000 resistance has shifted near-term support to the $74,000-$75,000 zone.

Why Bitcoin Could Not Hold the $80,000 Breakout

Bitcoin's attempt to reclaim the $80,000 psychological level on September 4, 2026 failed within hours, as a stronger-than-expected US non-farm payrolls report triggered a broad risk-off rotation across digital assets. Within minutes of the 8:30 AM ET data release, BTC spot prices reversed from an intraday high of $80,250 to trade back below $77,000 on major venues including Coinbase and Binance.

The August jobs report, which showed wage growth accelerating to 4.5% year-over-year and unemployment ticking down to 3.9%, effectively priced out the 50-basis-point rate cut that crypto traders had been positioning for since mid-August. According to CME FedWatch data referenced by Yahoo Finance, the probability of a larger September cut collapsed from 38% to under 9% immediately after the print, sending the 10-year Treasury yield sharply higher.

Macro Mechanics: Why a Strong Labour Market Hurts Crypto

Bitcoin's correlation with US tech equities has tightened over the past 18 months, meaning that rate-sensitive assets now trade largely on the same inputs as the Nasdaq 100. A robust labour market signals persistent inflation pressure, which reduces the likelihood of accommodative monetary policy.

The Dollar-Yield Channel

Higher real yields make risk assets less attractive on a relative-return basis. The DXY index surged 0.7% on the jobs print, while the 10-year real yield climbed roughly 8 basis points. For Bitcoin specifically, this matters because:

  • Rising yields increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets.
  • A stronger dollar weighs on USD-denominated crypto prices for global buyers.
  • Margin calls on leveraged long positions amplify directional moves.

ETF Flow Reversal

Spot Bitcoin ETFs saw combined net outflows of $312 million on the day, according to data from Farside Investors referenced in the Yahoo Finance report. BlackRock's IBIT recorded its largest single-day outflow in two weeks, while Fidelity's FBTC also turned negative. This contrasts with the $1.4 billion in cumulative inflows seen during the first three days of September.

Technical Levels and Trader Positioning

The rejection at $80,000 is significant because that zone had acted as a multi-week consolidation ceiling. Failure to flip it to support typically invites a retest of the prior breakout level.

What This Means for Crypto Buyers in India

For Indian investors trading on exchanges like WazirX, CoinDCX, or Mudrex, the USD-INR overlay matters. With the rupee at roughly ₹83.2 per dollar and Bitcoin quoted in dollar terms, the rupee-denominated BTC drawdown was slightly cushioned. However, buyers using Systematic Investment Plans (SIPs) should note that dollar-cost averaging into a falling knife still carries short-term mark-to-market pain.

Real-World Highlights for Active Traders

  • The $80,000 level has now been tested and rejected three times since July 2026, confirming it as formidable resistance.
  • Funding rates on perpetual futures flipped negative within two hours of the print, indicating crowded short positioning.
  • Options skew shows traders paying premium for December $70,000 puts, hedging deeper downside.
  • On-chain data from Glassnode indicates roughly 18,000 BTC moved to exchanges in the four hours following the report, suggesting profit-taking or de-risking.

Outlook Into the September Fed Meeting

The failed breakout does not invalidate the broader 2026 bull thesis, but it does delay it. Until the Fed signals concrete dovish intent, Bitcoin is likely to remain range-bound between $74,000 and $80,000. Traders should watch the next CPI release on September 11 for confirmation of whether the jobs strength translates into persistent goods inflation. A cooler print could reignite the breakout attempt; a hotter one risks a test of the 200-day moving average near $68,500.

For now, the practical lesson is clear: macro data still drives crypto volatility more than any on-chain metric, and the September jobs report was a textbook reminder of that hierarchy.
