Rooney Calls World Cup 2026 Final Half-Time Show 'Crap', Goes Viral

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Wayne Rooney called the World Cup 2026 final half-time show 'crap'.
  • The show featured BTS, Shakira, Justin Bieber, and Madonna.
  • Rooney's comments have gone viral on social media.
  • The match was between Spain and Argentina.
  • Rooney's verdict contrasts with official praise for the show.

Rooney's Blunt Verdict

Wayne Rooney has delivered a characteristically blunt verdict on the half-time show for the FIFA World Cup 2026 final, calling it 'crap'. The former England captain's comments have gone viral after the show, which featured a lineup of global pop stars including BTS, Shakira, Justin Bieber, and Madonna.

Rooney, a pundit for a UK broadcaster during the tournament, did not mince his words. 'It was crap,' he said, according to reports from The Times of India. The clip of his reaction has been shared widely on social media, drawing a mix of agreement and outrage from fans.

The Star-Studded Show

The half-time spectacle was a highlight of the 2026 World Cup final between Spain and Argentina. FIFA had promoted the show as the biggest in the tournament's history, featuring acts that spanned generations and genres. BTS, the K-pop sensation, performed a medley of their hits, while Shakira brought her signature Latin energy to the stadium.

Justin Bieber and Madonna also took the stage, with Madonna's appearance being particularly hyped as a historic moment. Euronews reported that the show 'shook up' the final, while ESPN described it as a 'stunning closing ceremony and halftime show'. FIFA itself hailed the event as a milestone.

Contrasting Reactions

Rooney's harsh review stands in stark contrast to the official and fan reactions. Many viewers on social media praised the production value and the sheer star power. However, a significant number of football fans agreed with Rooney, arguing that the show was too long and detracted from the match itself.

The debate highlights a long-running tension between the sport and its entertainment. For every fan who enjoys the spectacle, there is another who wants the focus to remain on the pitch. Rooney's comments have become a flashpoint in that argument.

What Happens Next

FIFA has not responded to Rooney's critique. The football world now turns its attention to the next World Cup, with the 2030 edition set to be co-hosted by Uruguay, Argentina, and Paraguay. Whether the halftime show will be scaled back or expanded remains to be seen.
