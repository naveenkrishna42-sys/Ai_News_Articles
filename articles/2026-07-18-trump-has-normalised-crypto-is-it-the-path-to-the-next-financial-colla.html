Trump Has Normalised Crypto. Is It the Path to the Next Financial Collapse?

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump’s pro-crypto stance has legitimised volatile digital assets as mainstream investments.
  • Critics warn of speculative bubbles, fraud, and potential contagion to the broader financial system.
  • Regulatory gaps remain as crypto markets surpass $3 trillion in value.
  • Porter draws parallels to past financial crashes driven by deregulation and hype.

Former President Donald Trump has turned what was once a fringe libertarian experiment into a mainstream financial phenomenon. But according to Eduardo Porter, writing in The Guardian, this embrace of cryptocurrency may be setting the stage for the next great financial collapse.

Trump’s pivot from crypto sceptic to champion has been dramatic. During his 2024 campaign, he accepted donations in Bitcoin and promised to make the US “the crypto capital of the planet.” Since returning to office, his administration has pushed friendly regulations, appointed pro-crypto officials, and even floated the idea of a national Bitcoin reserve.

The Mainstreaming of Risk

For years, crypto was seen as a niche playground for tech nerds and speculators. That perception has now vanished. Major banks, pension funds, and even local governments are piling into digital assets. In India, too, crypto trading volumes have surged, with millions of retail investors entering the market through apps and exchanges.

But Porter warns that normalisation does not mean safety. The same volatility that made crypto exciting now threatens to spill over into the broader economy. “When a speculative asset becomes this big and this intertwined with the financial system, the crash isn’t just a personal loss — it becomes a public crisis,” he writes.

Lessons from History

The parallels to past bubbles are hard to ignore. In 2008, complex mortgage-backed securities were hailed as innovations before they imploded. In the 1920s, margin lending fuelled a stock market mania that ended in the Great Depression. Crypto, Porter argues, shares the same DNA: opaque instruments, extreme leverage, and a belief that “this time is different.”

Already, signs of strain are visible. Stablecoin collapses, exchange bankruptcies, and massive fraud cases have wiped out billions. Yet the market keeps climbing, now valued at over $3 trillion globally. Regulators in India and elsewhere are scrambling to catch up, but the pace of innovation outruns the rulebooks.

What Happens When the Music Stops?

The core danger, according to Porter, is contagion. Unlike previous crypto crashes that remained largely within the crypto ecosystem, the current market is deeply linked to traditional finance. Major banks are issuing crypto loans; retirement funds hold digital tokens; and corporations keep crypto on their balance sheets.

“If the crypto market crashes today, it will take down banks, pensioners, and even government balance sheets with it,” Porter warns. He compares it to the subprime mortgage crisis, where losses in a seemingly small corner of finance triggered a global meltdown.

Regulatory Gaps Remain Wide

Despite growing alarm, meaningful regulation has been slow. The Trump administration has favoured a light-touch approach, arguing that innovation should not be stifled. Critics say this is a recipe for disaster. In India, the government has oscillated between bans and taxation, leaving investors in a grey zone.

Porter points out that the very features advocates celebrate — decentralisation, anonymity, lack of oversight — are the ones that make crypto prone to abuse. “You cannot have a stable financial system built on instruments designed to evade control,” he writes.

Whether the next crash is imminent or years away remains uncertain. What is clear is that the guardrails are not in place. Investors, regulators, and ordinary citizens are now riding a wave that few fully understand. And as history repeatedly shows, when the music stops, the fallout rarely stays contained.

What to watch: The upcoming G20 summit may push for global crypto standards, but until then, the market remains a high-stakes gamble with no safety net.
