Iran releases undated video of Mojtaba Khamenei amid health rumours

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Undated video of Mojtaba Khamenei released by Iranian media
  • Comes after Israeli reports of his death or serious illness
  • Video does not specify when or where it was recorded
  • Analysts see it as an attempt to counter growing speculation

Iranian state media has released an undated video of Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, in an apparent effort to push back against Israeli reports claiming he is dead or seriously ill. The footage, aired across multiple outlets, shows the leader in an unspecified setting, but officials have not confirmed when or where it was recorded.

The move comes amid heightened tensions between Tehran and Tel Aviv, with the two nations locked in a bitter confrontation. Israeli media have in recent days circulated claims that Khamenei had either died or was incapacitated, prompting a flurry of speculation across global news networks.

What the video shows — and what it doesn't

The released clip is brief and lacks any timestamp or contextual markers. It appears to show Khamenei in a routine setting, but the absence of a date has raised eyebrows among regional analysts. Without a verifiable time frame, the footage fails to conclusively rebut the death or illness rumours.

Iranian officials have not issued a formal statement explaining why the video was released, nor have they provided details about the leader's current health status. State media simply presented the visuals as "recent," a term that leaves ample room for interpretation.

Why the timing matters

This is not the first time Tehran has resorted to releasing visuals of its leadership to counter adverse narratives. But the current context is unusually charged. Israeli officials have openly discussed the possibility of targeting Iran's top leadership, and any sign of vulnerability in Tehran is seized upon by adversaries.

The undated nature of the video is itself a tell. If Khamenei were in robust health, analysts argue, Iranian media would likely have shown him in a dated, public setting — such as a meeting with officials or a religious ceremony. The choice to air an ambiguous clip suggests the leadership is attempting to manage perceptions without offering concrete proof.

Regional and international reaction

Reactions from the region have been cautious. Arab media outlets have covered the video with a degree of scepticism, noting the lack of verification. International news agencies, including those in the US and Europe, have highlighted the absence of a date, framing the release as a propaganda move rather than a definitive update.

In Israel, officials have not officially commented on the video. However, defence correspondents have pointed out that Tehran's response to the rumours — however vague — indicates that the reports have rattled the Iranian establishment enough to warrant a public rebuttal.

What lies beneath the surface

The episode underscores a broader information war between Iran and Israel, where visuals, statements, and even silences are weaponised. For Tehran, projecting stability at the top is crucial, especially amid ongoing domestic unrest and international sanctions. Any perceived weakness could embolden both external foes and internal dissent.

For now, the video has achieved its immediate goal: it has given Iranian media something to broadcast and supporters something to share. But it has not silenced the sceptics. The lack of a clear, dated appearance leaves the door open for further speculation, and Israeli outlets are already questioning why Tehran did not simply show Khamenei in a live or timestamped setting.

As the standoff continues, the world will be watching for the next piece of evidence — a dated photo, a public appearance, or an official statement — that could settle the question of the Supreme Leader's health. Until then, the ambiguity is likely to persist, feeding both rumours and counter-rumours in equal measure.
