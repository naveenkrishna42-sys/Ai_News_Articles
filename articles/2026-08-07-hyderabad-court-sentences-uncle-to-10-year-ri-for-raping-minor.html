Hyderabad court sentences uncle to 10-year RI for raping minor

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • The accused, the victim's uncle, was convicted under relevant sections of the POCSO Act and IPC.
  • The court also imposed a fine on the convict, with the amount to be paid as compensation to the victim.
  • The victim, a minor, had narrated her ordeal to her mother, leading to the FIR and subsequent trial.

In a case that underscores the horror of abuse within families, a Hyderabad court on Friday sentenced a man to 10 years of rigorous imprisonment for raping his minor niece. The convict, a relative of the victim, was found guilty under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act and relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code.

The court, presided over by the Special Judge for POCSO cases, also imposed a fine on the convict. The amount will be paid as compensation to the survivor, who was a child at the time of the offence. The verdict brings a measure of closure to a case that had dragged on for several years.

Crime and complaint

According to the prosecution, the accused sexually assaulted the girl multiple times over a period, exploiting the trust that comes with being a close family member. The victim, who was a minor when the incidents took place, eventually gathered the courage to tell her mother about the ordeal.

The mother filed a complaint with the local police, leading to the registration of an FIR. The accused was arrested and subsequently tried in the special court, which fast-tracks cases involving child sexual abuse.

Trial and testimony

During the trial, the prosecution examined several witnesses, including the victim, whose testimony was crucial in establishing the guilt of the accused. The court relied on the victim's consistent narration of events, corroborated by medical evidence and other circumstantial details.

The defence argued for acquittal, claiming the case was a fabrication. However, the judge found the evidence compelling enough to convict, noting that the victim's account bore the ring of truth and that the trauma of testifying against a family member was evident.

Sentence and compensation

The court sentenced the convict to 10 years of rigorous imprisonment, along with a fine. The fine amount, once realised, will be transferred to the victim as compensation, a provision under the POCSO Act that aims to support survivors in their recovery.

Legal experts say the verdict sends a strong signal that such crimes, even when committed by those in positions of trust, will be dealt with sternly. The case also highlights the need for families to be vigilant and for children to feel safe in reporting abuse.

Broader context

This case is a stark reminder of the prevalence of child sexual abuse in India, where a significant number of offenders are known to the victims. The POCSO Act, enacted in 2012, provides for stringent punishments and a child-friendly trial process, but implementation remains a challenge.

Activists argue that awareness and education are key to preventing such crimes, and that survivors must be assured of support, not stigma. The court's order, which includes compensation, is a step in that direction.

As the convict begins his prison term, the focus now shifts to the victim's rehabilitation. The court has directed the District Legal Services Authority to ensure that the compensation is disbursed promptly, aiding the minor's family in accessing counselling and other support services.

The case will be monitored by child welfare agencies to ensure the survivor's well-being in the coming months, as she continues to rebuild her life.
