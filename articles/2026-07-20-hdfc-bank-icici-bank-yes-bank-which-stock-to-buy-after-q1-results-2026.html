HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Yes Bank: Which stock to buy after Q1 results 2026?

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • HDFC Bank reported a modest 5% profit rise to Rs 19,060 crore, with narrowing margins.
  • ICICI Bank and Yes Bank also released Q1 numbers, with varying credit growth and asset quality trends.
  • Analysts cite robust credit growth, clean balance sheets, and FCNR inflows as positive sector drivers.
  • Stock recommendations differ based on valuation comfort and earnings trajectory.

Q1 results and market reaction

India's private banking sector saw a mixed bag of results in the first quarter of FY26. HDFC Bank reported a modest 5% year-on-year rise in net profit to Rs 19,060 crore. The earnings call revealed narrowing margins, which tempered market enthusiasm.

ICICI Bank and Yes Bank also announced their quarterly numbers. While ICICI continued to show steady growth, Yes Bank's performance was closely watched for signs of a sustained turnaround. The broader banking sector outlook remains bright, driven by robust credit demand and improving asset quality.

What analysts are saying

Several brokerages have upgraded their stance on select private banks. The key talking points from earnings calls include clean balance sheets, higher provisioning buffers, and strong FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) deposit inflows that are supporting liquidity.

Analysts at leading domestic and foreign brokerages have highlighted that HDFC Bank's margin compression could be a near-term headwind. However, its strong retail franchise and low NPAs offer a safety net. ICICI Bank is seen as a balanced play with consistent performance across segments.

Yes Bank, on the other hand, is viewed as a high-risk, high-reward bet. The bank's management has focused on recovering bad loans and rebuilding the corporate book, but the stock remains volatile.

Valuation and earnings trajectory

HDFC Bank trades at a premium to its peers, but analysts argue that the premium is justified given its market leadership in mortgages and retail deposits. ICICI Bank offers relative value with a lower price-to-earnings multiple and a strong return on assets.

Yes Bank's valuation is heavily discounted, reflecting the market's scepticism about its ability to sustain growth. The bank's Q1 numbers showed some improvement in net interest income, but the cost of deposits remains elevated.

FCNR inflows have provided a cushion for the banking system, but the sustainability of these flows depends on global interest rate differentials. A stable rupee and high domestic rates have attracted foreign deposits, benefiting banks with strong NRI franchises.

Which stock to buy?

For long-term investors, HDFC Bank remains a core holding despite near-term margin concerns. Its deposit franchise and loan book quality are unmatched. ICICI Bank is a strong candidate for those seeking growth at a reasonable price.

Yes Bank is only for aggressive investors with a high risk appetite. The stock could deliver outsized gains if the turnaround gains traction, but the downside risk is equally significant.

Most analysts recommend a selective approach. Diversifying across HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank may offer the best risk-reward balance, while Yes Bank should be treated as a tactical bet with strict stop-losses.

The next few quarters will be crucial. Investors should watch for margin trends, credit cost movements, and any regulatory changes that could impact the sector.
