Nominate a teacher who made a difference in your life

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Hindu is accepting nominations for teachers who have made a difference in students' lives.
  • Nominations are open to readers in Chennai, with the last date being August 31.
  • The initiative aims to honour educators who go beyond the call of duty.

CHENNAI: A teacher can change a life with a single word, a moment of patience, or an act of kindness that stays with a student for decades. The Hindu is now inviting readers across Chennai to nominate such educators—those who have gone beyond the blackboard to make a real difference in their classrooms and communities.

The initiative, launched this week, seeks to honour teachers who have inspired, mentored, or supported students in ways that often go unnoticed. Whether it is a school teacher who stayed back after hours to help a struggling child, a college professor who encouraged a hesitant student to pursue a dream, or a tutor who believed in a young person when no one else did, the call is open to all.

Readers can send in their nominations with a brief description of how the teacher impacted their life. The last date for entries is August 31. The Hindu will feature selected stories of these remarkable educators in a special series, celebrating their contributions to education and society.

Why this matters

Teachers in India often work under enormous pressure—large class sizes, limited resources, and immense societal expectations. Yet, many still manage to create safe, supportive spaces where students can grow. This nomination drive is a small way to recognise that effort.

Over the years, such public acknowledgements have helped highlight the quiet, consistent work of educators who rarely seek attention. For students and former students, the act of nominating a teacher can also be a deeply personal one—a chance to say thank you, in a public way.

How to nominate

Nominations can be sent through The Hindu's website or via email, as specified in the call. The organisers have asked nominators to include the teacher's name, the institution they are associated with, and a brief account of why they deserve recognition.

There is no restriction on the subject taught or the level of education. Teachers from schools, colleges, and even informal learning spaces are eligible.

A tradition of honouring educators

The Hindu has a long history of engaging with the education sector in Tamil Nadu, from covering board exam results to reporting on policy changes. This initiative builds on that tradition, focusing on the human side of teaching.

Last year, a similar call in another city drew hundreds of responses, with many readers describing teachers who had helped them through personal crises, encouraged them to take up unfamiliar subjects, or simply listened when no one else would. Organisers hope Chennai's response will be equally heartfelt.

What happens next

Once the nominations close, a panel will review the entries and select a set of stories for publication. The selected teachers will be featured in a special series, and all nominators will be notified of the outcome.

For those who have been meaning to acknowledge a teacher who changed their path, this is a straightforward opportunity. The deadline is August 31, and every story has the potential to remind us why teaching remains one of the most powerful professions.
