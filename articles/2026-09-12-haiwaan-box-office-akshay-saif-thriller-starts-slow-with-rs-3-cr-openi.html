Haiwaan Box Office: Akshay-Saif Thriller Starts Slow With Rs 3 Cr Opening

Key points
  • Haiwaan opened to a modest Rs 3 crore, significantly below trade expectations for an Akshay-Saif collaboration.
  • Strong competition from 'Hanuman Ansh' and the 'Mirzapur' film adaptation hampered the screen count and footfall.
  • Critical reception is polarized, with Akshay Kumar’s villainous turn receiving praise despite script weaknesses.

The Commercial Underperformance of Haiwaan on Day 1

The cinematic reunion of Akshay Kumar and Saif Ali Khan under the direction of veteran filmmaker Priyadarshan was expected to ignite the domestic box office. However, 'Haiwaan' recorded a tepid opening, collecting an estimated Rs 3 crore on its first Friday. This figure marks a significant departure from the historic highs usually associated with this duo, reflecting a shift in audience appetite and the impact of a highly congested release window.

The film, positioned as a psychological thriller with high-octane action, failed to capture the morning and afternoon shows, with occupancy hovering around 12-15% in major metros. While secondary markets in North India showed slightly better traction due to Akshay Kumar’s star power, the numbers were insufficient to offset the slow start in the South and West zones. Trade analysts suggest that the niche nature of the thriller genre, coupled with a lack of chart-busting music, contributed to the low initial turnout.

Market Cannibalization: The Hanuman Ansh and Mirzapur Factor

One of the primary drivers behind the sluggish start for Haiwaan is the intense competition. The film shared the marquee with the mythological juggernaut 'Hanuman Ansh' and the long-awaited film adaptation of the 'Mirzapur' series. 'Hanuman Ansh' has reportedly secured the lion's share of single-screen audiences in Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities, leaving Haiwaan to fight for premium multiplex slots.

Furthermore, the 'Mirzapur' film has leveraged its existing digital fanbase to dominate early ticket bookings. For Haiwaan, this resulted in a lower screen count than initially projected by the distributors. In a market where high-concept thrillers require strong word-of-mouth to grow, entering a three-way clash has proven to be a high-risk gamble that has not immediately paid off in terms of gross collections.

Critical Reception: A Divided Audience Sentiment

The reception for Haiwaan is split between two distinct camps. Professional critics have largely criticized the screenplay, with many describing it as a 'defanged' thriller that lacks the characteristic Priyadarshan sharpness. Critics argue that the narrative, while ambitious, fails to sustain the tension required for a film of this genre. Saif Ali Khan’s performance as the protagonist has been described as earnest but hampered by a predictable script.

Conversely, the audience reaction to Akshay Kumar’s portrayal of the antagonist has been overwhelmingly positive. Viewers have noted that Kumar’s transition into a darker, villainous role provides the film with its few moments of genuine intensity. This 'villain vs. hero' dynamic is currently the primary selling point for the film as it heads into the crucial Saturday and Sunday slots. The survival of the film at the box office now depends entirely on whether this positive buzz regarding Kumar's performance can translate into higher evening show occupancy.

Key Real-World Highlights: Pros and Cons

  • Pro: Villainous Masterclass – Akshay Kumar’s performance as the antagonist is being hailed as a career-best in the gray-shade category.
  • Pro: Production Quality – High-end cinematography and sound design provide a premium theatrical experience.
  • Con: Weak Screenplay – The narrative pacing is inconsistent, leading to a loss of momentum in the second half.
  • Con: Direct Competition – Facing two major franchise-led films has severely limited the film's commercial breathing room.

For investors and distributors, the focus now shifts to the 'Monday Test.' Given the Rs 120 crore estimated budget, Haiwaan requires an exponential jump over the weekend to remain commercially viable. Without a significant surge, the film risks being labeled a 'disaster on arrival,' despite its high-profile casting and veteran direction.
