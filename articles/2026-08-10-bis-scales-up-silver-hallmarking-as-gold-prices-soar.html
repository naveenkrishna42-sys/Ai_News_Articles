BIS scales up silver hallmarking as gold prices soar

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • BIS to expand silver hallmarking and testing infrastructure nationwide.
  • Rising gold prices have shifted consumer demand to silver jewellery and artefacts.
  • Move aims to build consumer confidence and curb adulteration in silver products.

Silver takes centrestage as gold gets costlier

With gold prices scaling new highs, Indian consumers are increasingly turning to silver — and the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) is responding by scaling up its hallmarking and testing infrastructure. The national standards body is set to expand its lab network and testing capacity to handle the surge in demand for certified silver products.

The shift is not just a passing trend. Industry watchers say the affordability of silver, compared to gold, has made it an attractive alternative for both everyday buyers and investors. Jewellers across the country have reported a noticeable uptick in silver purchases, from traditional ornaments to modern artefacts.

Why hallmarking matters

Hallmarking is a guarantee of purity. For silver, BIS certification ensures that the metal meets the required fineness standards, protecting buyers from adulteration and substandard products. As demand grows, so does the risk of unethical practices creeping into the market.

By ramping up its testing facilities, BIS aims to stay ahead of that curve. The expansion will include new labs in key cities and increased capacity at existing centres, reducing turnaround times for testing and certification.

Consumer trust at the core

Officials familiar with the development say the move is designed to instil confidence among buyers who are new to silver. Many first-time purchasers may not be aware of the nuances of silver purity, making certification all the more critical.

The BIS has been pushing for wider awareness about hallmarking, and this expansion is expected to complement those efforts. Jewellers too are being encouraged to register for BIS certification and display the hallmark logo prominently.

Industry response

The jewellery trade has welcomed the initiative. Retailers point out that certified silver products command better trust and often fetch a premium. With the festival season approaching, demand is expected to climb further, making the timing of the BIS move significant.

Some industry bodies have also urged BIS to consider streamlining the certification process for small artisans and workshops, who may find the current procedures cumbersome. The standards body has not yet commented on these suggestions.

What lies ahead

The silver market in India is poised for steady growth, and the BIS expansion signals a long-term commitment to quality assurance. Over the coming months, more labs are expected to come online, and the certification process is likely to become faster and more accessible.

For consumers, the takeaway is simple: as silver becomes a mainstream choice, hallmarking will play a pivotal role in ensuring they get what they pay for. The coming quarters will show how quickly the infrastructure catches up with demand.
