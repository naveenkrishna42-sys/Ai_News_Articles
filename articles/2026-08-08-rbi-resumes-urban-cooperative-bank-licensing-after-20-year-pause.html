RBI resumes urban cooperative bank licensing after 20-year pause

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has decided to resume licensing of urban cooperative banks after a gap of 20 years
  • The move is expected to expand banking services in underserved urban and semi-urban areas
  • Applicants will need to meet revised capital and governance norms set by the central bank

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has decided to resume the licensing process for urban cooperative banks (UCBs) after a two-decade-long pause. The announcement, made on Wednesday, marks a significant shift in the central bank's approach towards the cooperative banking sector, which has been under regulatory scrutiny for years.

The decision comes as part of the RBI's broader effort to strengthen financial inclusion and expand the reach of banking services in urban and semi-urban areas. The central bank had last issued fresh licences to UCBs in the early 2000s, after which the process was halted amid concerns over governance and financial stability in the sector.

What this means for the cooperative sector

The resumption of licensing is expected to open the doors for new urban cooperative banks to enter the market. These institutions primarily serve small businesses, traders, and low-income households in cities and towns, often filling gaps left by larger commercial banks.

Industry experts believe the move could spur competition and improve access to credit in underserved pockets. However, the RBI is likely to impose stricter eligibility criteria, including higher capital requirements and stronger governance norms, to ensure that new entrants are financially sound from day one.

Background of the licensing freeze

The licensing freeze was originally imposed following a series of failures and frauds in the cooperative banking space. Several UCBs faced liquidity crises, and depositors in some instances lost their savings, prompting the central bank to adopt a cautious stance.

Over the past two decades, the RBI has focused on consolidating and supervising existing UCBs rather than expanding their numbers. That approach has yielded mixed results — while some banks have stabilised, others continue to struggle with weak capital buffers and poor asset quality.

Regulatory framework and next steps

The RBI is expected to release detailed guidelines for prospective applicants in the coming weeks. These will likely cover minimum paid-up capital, promoter contribution, board composition, and risk management frameworks.

Banks that receive licences will be subject to regular supervisory reviews and will need to comply with the same prudential norms that apply to other regulated entities. The central bank has also indicated that it will prioritise applications from regions with limited banking infrastructure.

While the announcement has been welcomed by cooperative sector leaders, some analysts caution that the success of the new licensing regime will depend on how rigorously the RBI enforces compliance. The central bank has not yet specified a timeline for issuing the first new licences.

What to watch

All eyes will now be on the detailed circular from the RBI, which is expected to clarify the capital thresholds and application process. The first wave of new licences, once issued, could reshape the competitive dynamics in India's urban banking space.
