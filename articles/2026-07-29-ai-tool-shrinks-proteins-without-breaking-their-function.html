AI Tool Shrinks Proteins Without Breaking Their Function

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • AI can shrink proteins while preserving their core function, acting like a 'molecular shrink ray'.
  • Researchers combined AI design with lab evolution to create enzymes that outperform natural ones.
  • The approach speeds up protein engineering for applications in drugs, biofuels, and materials.
  • AI-redesigned starting points help enzymes evolve beyond what nature alone can achieve.

AI as a Molecular Shrink Ray

Scientists have developed an artificial intelligence tool that can redesign proteins to be smaller while keeping them fully functional. The breakthrough, described as a 'molecular shrink ray', allows researchers to trim away unnecessary parts of a protein without breaking what it does.

Proteins are the workhorses of biology. They catalyse reactions, send signals, and build structures. But many contain large, redundant regions that evolution has never bothered to prune. This AI finds the essential core and rebuilds the rest.

Better Starting Points for Evolution

The technique works by generating compact protein 'starting points' that are then fine-tuned in the lab using directed evolution. In a study published in Nature, teams showed that AI-redesigned enzymes could be evolved to perform beyond natural limits.

Traditional evolution starts with a natural protein and mutates it. But natural proteins are often bulky and inefficient. AI-generated starting points are leaner, giving evolution a head start and a higher ceiling.

Enzymes That Go Beyond Nature

In one experiment, researchers used the approach to engineer an enzyme that could break down a tough plastic polymer. The redesigned enzyme was not only smaller but also more active at higher temperatures than any natural version.

Another test involved a biosensor protein. The AI-shrunk version retained its ability to detect a specific molecule while being easier to manufacture and more stable in solution.

These results were reported across multiple outlets, including Phys.org and News-Medical, highlighting a consistent pattern: AI design plus lab evolution beats either method alone.

Implications for Medicine and Industry

Smaller proteins have big advantages. They are cheaper to produce, penetrate tissues more easily, and are less likely to trigger immune responses. That makes them attractive for drug delivery, diagnostic tests, and industrial catalysts.

For example, an AI-shrunk antibody fragment could be loaded with a chemotherapy drug and target tumours more precisely than a full-sized antibody. Or a compact enzyme could be embedded in a washing powder to remove stains at lower temperatures, saving energy.

The research team has made the AI tool available to other scientists. They expect it to accelerate protein engineering across dozens of fields, from biofuels to biomaterials.

What Comes Next

The team is now testing whether the same approach works on larger protein complexes and on proteins that interact with DNA. If it does, the 'molecular shrink ray' could become a standard tool in every synthetic biology lab, turning bulky natural designs into sleek, high-performance machines.
