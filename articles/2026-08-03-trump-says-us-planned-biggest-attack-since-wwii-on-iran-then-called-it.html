Trump says US planned biggest attack since WWII on Iran, then called it off

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump disclosed the planned attack during a recent address, citing last-minute cancellation.
  • He accused Iran of 'duplicity' in denying talks, saying blockade will continue until 'deal or total surrender'.
  • Analysts suggest the reversal signals a shift in leverage in the ongoing Middle East crisis.

In a startling disclosure, former US President Donald Trump has revealed that the United States had prepared what he described as the 'biggest attack since World War II' against Iran, only to call it off at the last moment. The remarks, made during a recent address, add a dramatic new layer to the already tense standoff between Washington and Tehran.

Trump did not specify the exact nature or scale of the planned assault, nor the precise timeline, but his comments have reignited speculation about how close the two nations came to a full-blown military confrontation. He framed the decision to abort as a calculated move, though he stopped short of detailing the reasoning behind the last-minute reversal.

Accusations of 'Duplicity' and a Hardline Stance

Alongside the revelation, Trump launched a blistering attack on Iran's leadership, accusing them of what he called 'unbelievably duplicitous' behaviour in denying any willingness to engage in negotiations. His language was blunt, describing the situation as a choice between a 'deal or total surrender'.

He made it clear that the US naval blockade in the region would remain in force until Tehran agrees to terms. The former president's rhetoric underscores a policy of maximum pressure, a hallmark of his previous administration, now seemingly revived in his current political campaign.

Mixed Signals on Negotiations

Despite the bellicose tone, there are conflicting signals emerging from both sides. Reports suggest that Trump has also indicated that new negotiations with Iran could be starting, though details remain murky. Iranian officials have publicly denied any such talks, adding to the confusion and heightening diplomatic uncertainty.

This mixed messaging has left observers puzzled. On one hand, the threat of massive military action looms large; on the other, there is a hint of openness to dialogue. The contradiction may be a deliberate strategy, but it also reflects the volatile nature of US-Iran relations, which have swung between brinkmanship and tentative diplomacy for decades.

Who Holds the Upper Hand?

International analysts are now debating what Trump's admission reveals about the balance of power in the region. Some argue that the decision to call off the attack signals a recognition of Iran's resilience, suggesting Tehran may have the upper hand in this standoff. Others contend it was a tactical pause, not a sign of weakness, aimed at preserving options.

The New York Times, in its analysis, posited that Trump's reversal on escalation indicates a shift in leverage, with Iran appearing more confident in its position. This perspective is bolstered by Iran's steadfast refusal to capitulate under pressure, despite facing severe economic sanctions and a crippling blockade.

Regional and Global Implications

The stakes are immense. A military strike of the magnitude Trump described could have triggered a catastrophic regional war, drawing in multiple actors and potentially disrupting global oil supplies. The fact that it was called off does little to ease the underlying tensions, as the blockade continues to strangle Iran's economy.

For ordinary citizens in the region, the threat of conflict remains a daily reality. The US blockade has already had severe humanitarian consequences, and any escalation would only worsen the plight of millions. The international community watches with bated breath as both sides navigate this precarious tightrope.

As the situation evolves, the world will be watching closely. Whether this latest revelation is a prelude to renewed diplomacy or a precursor to further escalation remains an open question. What is clear is that the US-Iran standoff is far from resolved, and the coming weeks will be crucial in determining the path forward.
