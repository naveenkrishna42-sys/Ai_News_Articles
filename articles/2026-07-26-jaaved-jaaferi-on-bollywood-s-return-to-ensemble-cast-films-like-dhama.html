Jaaved Jaaferi on Bollywood's return to ensemble cast films like 'Dhamaal 4'

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Jaaved Jaaferi attributes the comeback of ensemble films to audience craving collective star power.
  • He points to 'Dhamaal 4' and 'Welcome' franchise as examples of this trend.
  • The actor believes such films offer a 'complete entertainment package' with multiple popular faces.

The Return of the Multi-Starrer

Jaaved Jaaferi has a simple explanation for why Bollywood is reviving the big ensemble cast film. The actor, known for his comic timing in films like 'Dhamaal' and 'Welcome', says audiences are tired of watching a single hero carry an entire movie.

Speaking to The Times of India, Jaaferi pointed to the success of upcoming projects such as 'Dhamaal 4' and the next instalment of the 'Welcome' franchise. He believes these films are bringing back a flavour that had gone missing from Hindi cinema.

Why Audiences Want Groups, Not Just One Star

Jaaferi argues that the solo hero film has run its course for now. Viewers want a variety of faces and comic styles in one frame. 'When you have multiple stars, the energy on screen multiplies,' he said.

The actor added that ensemble casts allow for multiple sub-plots and character arcs. This gives the audience more to latch onto, especially in the comedy genre where timing and chemistry between actors matter most.

The Economics of the Ensemble

Producers are also taking note. A film with four or five known faces can pull in different fan bases. This reduces the risk of a single star's box office failure dragging down the entire project. Jaaferi noted that this model worked wonders in the 2000s with films like 'Dhamaal' and 'Golmaal'.

'The audience wants to see their favourite actors together. It is a complete entertainment package,' he said. The strategy appears to be paying off, with trade analysts predicting strong opening numbers for 'Dhamaal 4'.

Nostalgia Meets Fresh Energy

Jaaferi also credited nostalgia as a driving force. Many of the actors in these films have been in the industry for decades. Their reunion on screen triggers fond memories for older viewers while introducing younger audiences to their style of comedy.

'We are not just making sequels. We are updating the formula with new jokes and situations,' he clarified. The challenge, he added, is to keep the script tight so that no actor feels underutilised.

Bollywood's shift back to ensemble films is not limited to comedies. Action and family dramas are also experimenting with larger casts. However, Jaaferi believes the comedy genre benefits the most because laughter is contagious when shared by a group.

With 'Dhamaal 4' and 'Welcome' sequel in production, the trend is unlikely to fade soon. Jaaferi is already shooting for both projects and says the sets are buzzing with collaborative energy.

What remains to be seen is whether these films can match the cult status of their predecessors. The first 'Dhamaal' released in 2007 and remains a favourite on streaming platforms. The new instalments will have to deliver fresh laughs without losing the original's spirit.
