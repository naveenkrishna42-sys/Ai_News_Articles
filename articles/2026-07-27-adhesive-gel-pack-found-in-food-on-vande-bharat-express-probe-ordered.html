Adhesive gel pack found in food on Vande Bharat Express, probe ordered

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A passenger found an adhesive gel pack in biryani served on the Mysuru-Chennai Vande Bharat Express.
  • The incident occurred on Saturday evening during the train's journey.
  • The Railway Ministry has ordered a high-level inquiry into the matter.
  • Catering services on the train have been suspended pending investigation.

A passenger on board the Mysuru-Chennai Vande Bharat Express made a disturbing discovery on Saturday evening — an adhesive gel pack mixed in with the biryani served during the journey. The incident has prompted the Railway Ministry to order a high-level inquiry.

What happened on the train

The passenger, who was travelling in an executive coach, noticed the foreign object while eating. He immediately alerted the train staff. The gel pack, typically used in industrial or household adhesives, was found embedded in the rice.

Other passengers in the same coach reported similar concerns about the quality of the food. The train's catering staff collected the evidence and handed it over to railway officials at Chennai Central station.

Railways responds swiftly

South Western Railway officials confirmed the incident late Saturday night. A spokesperson said the matter was being taken "very seriously." The catering contract for the Mysuru-Chennai Vande Bharat Express has been suspended with immediate effect.

The Railway Ministry has ordered a probe by the Commercial Department. The investigation will look into how the foreign object entered the food and whether standard operating procedures were followed at the kitchen.

Previous incidents raise questions

This is not the first time food quality on premium trains has come under scrutiny. In recent months, passengers on multiple Vande Bharat Express routes have complained about stale food, insects, and even plastic pieces in meals.

Railway officials said the caterer in this case is a private vendor. The company has been asked to submit a detailed explanation. The Railways has also threatened to blacklist the vendor if negligence is proven.

What happens next

The passenger who made the discovery has been asked to provide a written statement. Railway Protection Force (RPF) personnel are also reviewing CCTV footage from the pantry car to trace the source of the contamination.

A final report from the inquiry committee is expected within a week. Meanwhile, the Railways is exploring temporary arrangements to ensure food service continues on the route without disruption.

The incident has once again highlighted the need for stricter quality checks in railway catering. For passengers, the safety of what is served on board remains a basic, non-negotiable expectation.
