Lok Sabha Speaker recognises merger of 6 UBT rebel MPs with Shinde Sena

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla has approved the merger of six Shiv Sena (UBT) rebel MPs with the Eknath Shinde-led Shiv Sena faction.
  • The decision is a significant political setback for former Maharashtra Chief Minister Uddhav Thackeray.
  • The Shiv Sena (UBT) has termed the merger illegal and said it will challenge the Speaker's order.

Speaker approves merger of rebel MPs

Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla on Saturday recognised the merger of six Shiv Sena (UBT) rebel Members of Parliament with the Eknath Shinde-led Shiv Sena faction. The decision marks a fresh political blow to Uddhav Thackeray, whose party continues to grapple with defections since the 2022 split.

The Speaker's approval, conveyed through a formal order, paves the way for these MPs to officially function as part of the Shinde camp within the Lok Sabha. The six MPs had earlier crossed over to the Shinde-led faction, citing dissatisfaction with Thackeray's leadership.

Political implications for Uddhav Thackeray

For Thackeray, the merger is more than a procedural matter. It effectively reduces his party's strength in the Lower House and strengthens rival Eknath Shinde's claim over the original Shiv Sena's legacy and organisational structure.

The move comes months after the Election Commission of India recognised the Shinde faction as the real Shiv Sena, allotting it the party's traditional bow-and-arrow symbol. Saturday's decision by the Speaker further consolidates Shinde's position as the dominant faction in the eyes of parliamentary authorities.

Political analysts see this as a pattern. The Speaker also reportedly gave a nod to the merger of rebel TMC members with the NCPI, indicating a broader willingness to recognise floor-level realignments.

UBT Sena cries foul, vows legal challenge

The Shiv Sena (UBT) has not taken the decision lying down. Party leaders termed the merger illegal and accused the Speaker of acting in a partisan manner. They argued that the six MPs had not followed proper procedure under the anti-defection law.

“This is a violation of the Tenth Schedule. We will challenge this order in the Supreme Court,” a senior UBT leader said. The party claims that the merger was not backed by a formal resolution from the party's authorised decision-making body.

Legal experts point out that the Speaker's discretion in recognising mergers under the anti-defection law is broad but not absolute. The matter could now head to the courts for adjudication.

What the anti-defection law says

Under the Tenth Schedule of the Constitution, a merger of a political party is considered valid if at least two-thirds of its legislators agree to it. The Speaker's role is to verify whether this threshold has been met.

In this case, the six MPs from the UBT camp joined the Shinde faction, which already has a majority of Shiv Sena MPs. The Speaker's recognition effectively endorses that the merger meets the legal requirement. However, the UBT camp disputes the numbers and the process.

What happens next

The UBT Sena is expected to file a petition in the Supreme Court in the coming days. The legal battle will test the limits of the Speaker's powers and the interpretation of the anti-defection law in cases of factional splits. For now, the Shinde camp has gained a clear numerical advantage in Parliament, while Thackeray's faction faces an uphill fight on both political and legal fronts.
