Shashi Tharoor's Son Wants 'Boomers' Banned on WhatsApp, Gets Witty Reply

📅 2026-07-26 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ishaan Tharoor made a lighthearted post calling for a ban on baby boomers on WhatsApp.
  • Congress MP Shashi Tharoor replied with a clever retort, referencing his son's generation.
  • The exchange was widely shared on social media, drawing laughter and commentary.

Congress MP Shashi Tharoor and his son Ishaan engaged in a public exchange on social media that has since gone viral. The younger Tharoor posted a tongue-in-cheek demand to ban baby boomers from WhatsApp, prompting a sharp and witty comeback from his father.

The Post That Started It

Ishaan Tharoor, who is often active on social media, shared a post that read, "Can we please ban boomers on WhatsApp." The remark was clearly intended as a joke, targeting the older generation's perceived habits on the messaging platform.

Despite the playful tone, the post quickly gained traction. It struck a chord with many younger users who have shared similar frustrations about family group chats and forwarded messages. The comment section soon filled with amused reactions.

Shashi Tharoor's Reply

Shashi Tharoor, known for his eloquence and sharp wit, did not let the opportunity pass. He replied to his son's post with a message that was both a retort and a lesson in generational dynamics. "Perhaps we can also ban 'snapping' by those who can't even afford a camera," the Congress MP wrote.

The reply was a clever jab at the younger generation's habit of using Snapchat, an app heavily reliant on smartphone cameras. Tharoor's response highlighted the irony in criticising one platform's user habits while ignoring similar quirks in another.

Digital Divide in a Family Chat

The exchange between father and son quickly became a talking point online. Users praised Tharoor's ability to turn the tables with humour. Many noted that the lighthearted banter reflected a broader, relatable tension between generations in the digital age.

Neither Tharoor nor Ishaan have commented further on the matter. The post and reply remain on social media for public viewing.

Viral and Relatable

The back-and-forth was widely shared across platforms, with many users tagging their own parents or children. Memes and screenshots of the exchange circulated, cementing its place as a moment of online levity. The incident underscored how even public figures engage in the same family dynamics as ordinary Indians.

Observers pointed out that the exchange was harmless fun. It did not involve any political statement or controversy, a rarity for the Tharoor family who are often in the news for political reasons.

What happens next is uncertain. The viral moment may fade, but it has already provided a brief, humorous respite from the usual news cycle. For now, the Tharoors have given social media a talking point that bridges the generational gap with a laugh.
