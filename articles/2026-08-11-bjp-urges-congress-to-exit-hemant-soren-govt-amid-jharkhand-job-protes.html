BJP urges Congress to exit Hemant Soren govt amid Jharkhand job protests

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP has formally asked Congress to withdraw support to the Hemant Soren-led government in Jharkhand.
  • Rahul Gandhi slammed the use of force against protesters demanding government job recruitments.
  • CM Hemant Soren appealed to students in an open letter, urging them not to fall for political traps.

Political pressure mounts on Soren government

The Bharatiya Janata Party has escalated its offensive against the Jharkhand government, formally asking the Congress to pull out of the ruling coalition. The demand comes as protests by government job aspirants intensify across the state, with the BJP accusing the Hemant Soren-led administration of failing to address recruitment irregularities.

In a sharp rebuke, Congress leader Rahul Gandhi condemned the police action against demonstrators, calling it an attack on the rights of young people. His remarks have added a fresh layer of friction between the two alliance partners, who have so far maintained a united front in the state.

The BJP's move is being seen as an attempt to isolate the JMM-Congress combine ahead of the assembly elections, though the Congress has not yet responded officially to the demand.

What the protesters want

The agitation, which has been simmering for weeks, centres on allegations of paper leaks and irregularities in the state's teacher and police recruitment exams. Thousands of aspirants have taken to the streets, demanding a re-examination and a transparent selection process.

Protesters say their future has been held hostage by a flawed system. "We have spent years preparing, only to be told the exams were compromised," one demonstrator said, reflecting the frustration that has boiled over in cities like Ranchi and Jamshedpur.

The state government has denied any wrongdoing, but has promised to investigate the matter. It has also set up a committee to look into the demands, though protesters remain sceptical of its sincerity.

Soren's open letter to students

In a bid to de-escalate tensions, Chief Minister Hemant Soren released an open letter to the protesting students. He urged them not to fall into what he called a "political trap", insisting that his government was committed to fair recruitment.

"Your anger is justified, but do not let it be hijacked by those who have never cared about your future," Soren wrote, in an apparent reference to the BJP. He promised action against those responsible for any irregularities within a fixed timeframe.

The letter, however, has done little to calm the protesters, who have vowed to continue their stir until their demands are met. Several opposition leaders, including Rahul Gandhi, have extended support to the students, further complicating the political calculus.

Alliance under strain

The BJP's demand has put the Congress in a difficult spot. While Rahul Gandhi's criticism of the police action aligns with the protesters' cause, it also contradicts the Congress's continued support to the Soren government. Party insiders suggest that a split may be imminent, though no formal decision has been announced.

Political analysts point out that the Congress is caught between its national stance on law and order and its regional alliance compulsions. The Jharkhand unit of the party has so far remained silent, awaiting a directive from New Delhi.

Meanwhile, the BJP has sought to capitalise on the unrest, organising its own protests in support of the aspirants. The party has also demanded a Central Bureau of Investigation probe into the recruitment irregularities, a move the state government has rejected.

What lies ahead

As the standoff continues, all eyes are on the Congress's next move. The party's response to the BJP's demand, and Rahul Gandhi's sustained criticism, could determine the fate of the coalition. Negotiations are expected to take place within the week, though neither side has confirmed any meeting.

For the thousands of job aspirants, the political drama is a sideshow. Their demand is simple — a fair shot at government employment. Whether the state government can deliver that, amid the political crossfire, remains the key question.
