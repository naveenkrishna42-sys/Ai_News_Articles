Road tenders hit as state yet to revise bitumen rates post price hike

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitumen price rise prompts need for revised rates.
  • Government delay stalls road work tenders in Chennai.
  • Contractors await official notification to proceed.

Chennai's road infrastructure projects are facing an unexpected hurdle as the state government has yet to release revised bitumen rates following a recent increase in the commodity's price. This delay has stalled the tendering process for several road works across the city, leaving contractors and civic officials in a state of limbo.

The price of bitumen, a key material in road construction, has risen sharply in recent weeks. However, the government's failure to update the scheduled rates has created a discrepancy between current market costs and the figures contractors are expected to work with. Without the revised rates, the tender process cannot proceed, as bids would be based on outdated financial parameters.

What's Holding Up the Tenders

According to sources within the civic body, multiple road repair and resurfacing projects are ready to be floated. But the absence of the updated bitumen rates has forced officials to put them on hold. A senior engineer from the Chennai Corporation, speaking on condition of anonymity, said, "We are waiting for the government order. Once the revised rates are published, we can immediately move forward with the tenders."

The delay is not just a procedural issue. It has a cascading effect on project timelines, especially with the northeast monsoon expected in a few months. Road works that should have been completed before the rains are now at risk of being pushed into the next dry season.

Contractors Caught in the Middle

Contractors, who have already purchased materials or mobilised equipment, are feeling the pinch. Many have expressed frustration over the uncertainty, as they are unable to quote accurate prices without the official rate revision. "We can't bid based on old rates. If we do, we'll incur losses," said a representative of a prominent construction firm involved in city road projects.

The issue also raises questions about the state's procurement efficiency. While bitumen prices fluctuate globally, state governments typically revise their rates periodically to reflect market realities. The current lag suggests a breakdown in this routine process, possibly due to administrative bottlenecks.

Impact on Chennai's Road Network

Chennai has a significant backlog of road maintenance work, with many arterial stretches showing signs of wear. The delay in tenders means these roads will continue to deteriorate, leading to more potholes and safety hazards for commuters. Residents in several suburbs have already complained about poor road conditions, and the prolonged delay is unlikely to improve the situation.

Moreover, the stalled tenders could affect the state's overall infrastructure spending for this fiscal year. If projects are not awarded soon, the allocated funds may lapse, further slowing down development.

What Officials Say

Officials in the state highways department have acknowledged the issue but have not provided a clear timeline for the release of the revised rates. They maintain that the process is underway and that the rates will be finalised soon. However, repeated queries have yielded no concrete assurances, leaving stakeholders guessing.

The last revision of bitumen rates was done several months ago, and the recent price surge has made the old rates obsolete. The difference is substantial enough that proceeding with tenders would either discourage bidders or lead to inflated quotes to cover risks, neither of which serves the public interest.

The Road Ahead

Moving forward, experts suggest that the government should adopt a more dynamic system for updating material rates, perhaps on a quarterly basis, to avoid such logjams. Until then, Chennai's road projects will remain hostage to the whims of the market and the pace of bureaucracy.

All eyes are now on the state secretariat, where the file is believed to be pending approval. If the revised rates are not published in the coming days, the city may have to brace for another season of neglected roads and delayed infrastructure works.

The coming weeks will be crucial. A swift resolution could restore momentum to the tendering process, while further delays might force the civic body to consider interim measures, such as emergency repair contracts, to address the most critical stretches.
