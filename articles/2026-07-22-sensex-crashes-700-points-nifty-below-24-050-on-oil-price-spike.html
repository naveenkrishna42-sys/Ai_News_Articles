Sensex Crashes 700 Points, Nifty Below 24,050 On Oil Price Spike

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex slumped over 700 points intraday on Wednesday, eventually closing 238 points lower at 77,470.
  • Nifty 50 slipped below 24,050 during the session, closing at 24,187, down 50 points.
  • Rising crude oil prices driven by escalating US-Iran tensions were the primary trigger for the selloff.
  • PSU bank stocks were among the worst hit, dragging the broader market down.

Markets in the Red

Indian stock markets witnessed a brutal selloff on Wednesday, with the BSE Sensex plunging over 700 points during intraday trading. The broader Nifty 50 index also fell sharply, slipping below the 24,050 mark before staging a partial recovery.

The bloodbath was driven by a sudden spike in global crude oil prices, triggered by escalating tensions between the United States and Iran. Fears of supply disruptions hit energy markets, sending shockwaves through equity markets worldwide.

Oil Shock Hits Dalal Street

Brent crude surged past $85 a barrel on Wednesday morning, marking its highest level in weeks. Traders said the jump was sparked by reports of increased US naval deployments in the Persian Gulf and a warning from Tehran about potential retaliation against any aggression.

India, being a major oil importer, is acutely sensitive to such price spikes. Higher crude costs widen the trade deficit, stoke inflation, and pressure corporate margins. This combination spooked Dalal Street investors, who rushed to cut exposure.

Sectoral Wreckage

The selloff was broad-based, but PSU bank stocks took the hardest hit. The Nifty PSU Bank index tumbled over 3% in early trade, dragging the benchmarks lower. Metal and auto stocks also came under pressure.

Defensive sectors like IT and pharma fared slightly better, but could not prevent the indices from posting their worst single-day fall in over a month. Foreign portfolio investors were net sellers, adding to the domestic jitters.

Recovery Attempt Fades

By the afternoon session, the markets attempted a mild recovery. The Sensex cut losses from over 700 points to around 500 points, but fresh selling emerged in the final hour. The index eventually closed at 77,470.11, down 238.41 points.

The Nifty 50 ended at 24,187.70, a loss of 50.80 points from the previous close. Trading volumes were above average, indicating genuine selling pressure rather than just algorithmic noise.

What Triggered the Panic?

Analysts pointed to three factors behind Wednesday's crash: the US-Iran standoff, profit-booking after recent highs, and weak cues from Asian peers. The Sensex had been hovering near all-time highs, making it vulnerable to such shocks.

The geopolitical risk remains hard to price. Any further escalation could push oil prices beyond $90, which would force the Reserve Bank of India to keep interest rates higher for longer. That prospect spooked bond markets too, with the 10-year yield inching up.

What to Watch

All eyes are now on diplomatic channels between Washington and Tehran. Any sign of de-escalation could spark a sharp rebound. In the absence of that, the markets may remain volatile, with traders closely tracking oil futures and the rupee's movement against the dollar.
