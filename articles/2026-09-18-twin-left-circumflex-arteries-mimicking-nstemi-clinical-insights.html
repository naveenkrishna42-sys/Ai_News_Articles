Twin Left Circumflex Arteries Mimicking NSTEMI: Clinical Insights

✍️ By TIVRA News Science & Health Desk 📅 2026-09-18 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Twin left circumflex arteries are a rare anatomic variant that can obscure ischemic patterns on ECG.
  • Coronary angiography remains the gold standard for definitive diagnosis and risk stratification.
  • Timely revascularisation guided by lesion complexity improves outcomes in this subset.
  • Awareness of the variant prevents misinterpretation of NSTEMI and unnecessary interventions.

Introduction

Dual (twin) left circumflex arteries (LCx) represent an uncommon coronary anomaly where the left circumflex bifurcates into two parallel trunks before supplying the lateral wall. Though often asymptomatic, this variant can mimic acute coronary syndrome on electrocardiography and biomarkers, leading to diagnostic uncertainty. Understanding its angiographic appearance is essential for accurate risk assessment and appropriate management.

Anatomical Overview of the Left Circumflex Artery

The normal left circumflex artery arises from the left main coronary artery and runs in the atrioventricular groove, giving off obtuse marginal branches that lateralize the left ventricle. In the twin LCx configuration, the trunk splits early into two roughly equal-sized channels that run side‑by‑side, each giving off its own set of marginal branches. This duplication does not alter total myocardial perfusion but creates a distinctive parallel‑tube appearance on angiography.

Clinical Presentation and Diagnostic Challenges

Patients with twin LCx may present with chest pain, elevated high‑sensitivity troponin, and ST‑segment depressions suggestive of non‑ST‑elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI). The parallel vessels can obscure flow‑related defects, causing false‑negative stress tests or misleading ECG changes. Clinicians must maintain a high index of suspicion when ischemic signs appear discordant with risk factor burden.

Imaging Findings and Interpretation

Invasive coronary angiography is the definitive tool. Key angiographic hallmarks include: (1) early bifurcation of the LCx trunk within 5 mm of the left main, (2) two contiguous conduits of similar diameter coursing along the groove, and (3) separate obtuse marginal branches arising from each channel. Intravascular ultrasound or optical coherence tomography can further confirm true luminal duplication versus a prominent side branch.

Management Strategies and Outcomes

Management follows standard NSTEMI pathways guided by lesion severity. If significant stenosis (>50 %) is identified in either twin channel, percutaneous coronary intervention with drug‑eluting stenting is performed, targeting the culprit lesion. In cases of diffuse disease, coronary artery bypass grafting using the left internal mammary artery to the lateral wall remains effective. Follow‑up studies show comparable mortality to typical NSTEMI when revascularisation is timely, underscoring that the anatomic variant itself does not worsen prognosis when correctly addressed.

  • Twin left circumflex arteries are a benign variant but can masquerade as NSTEMI, necessitating careful angiographic correlation.
  • Early identification via angiography prevents unnecessary antiplatelet intensification or premature cath lab activation.
  • Revascularisation outcomes mirror those of conventional NSTEMI when the anatomic peculiarity is accounted for.
  • Educating emergency and cardiology teams about this variant reduces diagnostic delays and optimises resource use.
  • Future research should focus on non‑invasive imaging markers (e.g., CT angiography) to detect the variant prior to invasive testing.
