What the Warning Covers

SlowMist's chief information security officer has flagged an underground iPhone remote-control tool that sellers claim can extract crypto wallet data from infected devices. The alert follows separate iVerify reporting on 'FomoPeek,' described as an exploit delivered through an App Store app, which complicates the long-held assumption that iOS is safe simply because users install only App Store software.

Neither SlowMist nor iVerify has published a verified victim count, and the tool's theft claims have not been confirmed by independent labs. Buyers and holders should treat the capability claims as a serious risk indicator, not proven fact, and should not pay ransoms or 'recovery fees' to anyone referencing this tool.

How the Threat Reaches Devices

App Store Front, Underground Back End

The reported pattern is a two-stage risk: a seemingly ordinary app passes review, then gains dangerous access after installation through social engineering or abused entitlements. Once a user grants permissions, attackers can pivot to remote-control functionality, screen capture, or keylogging.

Why Wallet Data Is the Prize

Phone-based wallets often keep seed phrases in notes apps, browsers, or hot-wallet software. A remote-access tool that captures screens, keystrokes, or clipboard content can drain funds in seconds, faster than a user can revoke access or freeze an account.

Buyer and Holder Playbook

Move long-term holdings to a hardware wallet, typically $80 to $250, which keeps seed phrases offline and signs transactions on-device. That spend is trivial next to a single drained wallet, and it neutralizes most phone compromise scenarios.

Revoke token approvals and dApp sessions you no longer use, and audit them monthly. Enable Lockdown Mode if you are a high-value target. Treat screen-recording, accessibility, and third-party keyboard permissions as red flags, never type seed phrases on a phone, and store backups on steel. When buying security tools, demand independent audits and open-source firmware, and ignore anyone promising guaranteed fund recovery.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pro: iOS review still blocks most malware; the exposure window is narrow but real.
  • Con: 'App Store only' is no longer a complete safety guarantee on its own.
  • Pro: Hardware wallets blunt this threat even if the phone itself is compromised.
  • Con: Unverified claims mean panic-selling or overpaying for 'anti-spy' tools is unwarranted.
  • Key insight: Clipboard, screen, and keyboard abuse, not OS
