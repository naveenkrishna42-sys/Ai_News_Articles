US missile stocks depleted by Iran war, raising China conflict concerns

📅 2026-08-04 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Reports indicate the US has used 'virtually all' of its long-range precision missiles during the Iran war.
  • Depleted stocks raise concerns about US military readiness for a potential conflict with China.
  • Analysts and officials are questioning the sustainability of US weapons production and strategic reserves.

The United States has expended 'virtually all' of its long-range precision missiles during the ongoing war with Iran, according to multiple reports. This depletion has sparked fresh concerns about America's ability to deter or fight a potential conflict with China, a scenario that military planners have long prioritised.

Reuters, citing unnamed sources, reported that the US military's stockpiles of these critical munitions have been drastically drawn down. The Atlantic, in a separate analysis, questioned why the US is running so low on weapons, pointing to the intensity of the Iran campaign and the limited production capacity of defence contractors.

A War of Attrition on Missiles

The conflict with Iran, which began earlier this year, has proven to be a high-intensity engagement. The US has relied heavily on precision-guided munitions to strike Iranian targets, including missile batteries, nuclear facilities, and command centres. This sustained use has outpaced the Pentagon's expectations and the industry's ability to replenish supplies in real time.

According to India Today, the Trump administration's decision to escalate military action against Iran has left the US short of critical long-range missiles. The report highlighted that the Pentagon's initial stockpile estimates were based on shorter, more limited strikes, not the prolonged campaign that has unfolded.

Implications for the Indo-Pacific

The timing of this depletion is particularly troubling for US strategists. The Pentagon's own posture has been to shift focus toward the Indo-Pacific, where China presents the primary long-term challenge. A conflict with China would likely require an even larger volume of long-range precision munitions, given the distances involved and the sophistication of Chinese air defences.

Military analysts argue that the current state of US inventories may undermine deterrence. If Beijing perceives that Washington lacks the firepower to sustain a major engagement, it could embolden aggressive actions in the South China Sea or Taiwan Strait. The report from The Indian Express noted that the depletion of America's most powerful missile arsenal has become a central topic in defence circles.

Production Challenges

The core issue, as The Atlantic points out, is not just the number of missiles used but the rate at which they can be replaced. The US defence industrial base has not been geared for high-volume production of precision munitions since the Cold War era. Supply chain bottlenecks, a skilled labour shortage, and the high cost of these weapons have all slowed efforts to ramp up manufacturing.

Defence officials have acknowledged the strain but have not disclosed specific figures on remaining stockpiles. They have, however, emphasised that emergency measures are being taken to accelerate production. Yet, even optimistic timelines suggest it could take years to fully restore inventories.

What to Watch

The coming months will be critical as the Pentagon reviews its war plans and budget requests. Congress is likely to press for increased defence spending on munitions, and the administration may seek to fast-track procurement contracts. Whether the US can rebuild its arsenal before a potential confrontation with China remains an open question that will shape global security dynamics.
