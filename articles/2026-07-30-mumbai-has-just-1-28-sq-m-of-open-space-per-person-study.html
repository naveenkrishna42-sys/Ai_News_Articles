Mumbai Has Just 1.28 Sq.M. of Open Space Per Person: Study

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mumbai's per capita open space is 1.28 sq.m., well under the WHO's 9 sq.m. recommendation.
  • The study was published by Governance Now, analyzing municipal data.
  • Deficit is most acute in densely packed areas like Dharavi and Byculla.
  • Experts link lack of green space to rising heat island effect and health issues.

City's Green Cover Shrinks to Critical Low

Mumbai's residents are left with barely a patch of grass to call their own. A new study by Governance Now reveals that the city has only 1.28 square metres of open space per person. This is a fraction of the 9 square metres recommended by the World Health Organization.

The findings paint a stark picture of urban density. For a city of over 1.2 crore people, the total available open space—parks, gardens, and playgrounds—adds up to just over 1,500 hectares. That number has not kept pace with population growth.

Dharavi and Byculla Hit Hardest

The deficit is not uniform. In congested wards like Dharavi and Byculla, the per capita figure drops to less than 0.5 square metres. Residents in these areas often use streets or railway sidings as makeshift recreation zones.

Researchers used satellite imagery and municipal records to map all designated open spaces. They excluded private compounds and inaccessible areas. The data shows that only 3% of Mumbai's land is under public green cover.

Heat and Health Consequences

Urban planners have long warned about the effects of this scarcity. Without adequate parks, the city suffers from a pronounced heat island effect. Surface temperatures in built-up areas can be 4 to 6 degrees Celsius higher than in greener suburbs.

Public health experts also point to rising cases of respiratory illness and stress-related disorders. Open spaces act as natural air filters and provide room for physical activity. Their absence disproportionately affects children and the elderly.

What the Municipal Corporation Says

The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation has not yet officially responded to the study's findings. Past efforts to increase green cover have been slow. Projects like the Mumbai Coastal Road include park space, but critics say these are not enough to bridge the gap.

Activists have demanded that the city declare a climate emergency and set a target of 2 square metres per person by 2030. They also want stricter enforcement of rules that require developers to set aside land for gardens.

The Governance Now study comes ahead of the state government's annual review of urban development plans. Officials have confirmed that the issue will be discussed in the next town hall meeting. Whether that discussion leads to action remains to be seen.
