NASA-ISRO Radar Mission Preview Set Ahead of NISAR Launch

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • NASA to host a preview of the NISAR mission, a collaborative US-India radar satellite.
  • The satellite will track Earth's surface changes, including ecosystem shifts and natural hazards.
  • Launch preparations are underway, with no confirmed date announced yet.

Joint Mission Takes Center Stage

NASA is set to offer a first detailed look at its upcoming radar satellite mission with India, known as NISAR, ahead of its scheduled launch. The preview will showcase the spacecraft's capabilities and science goals, highlighting a rare partnership between the US and Indian space agencies.

The mission, short for NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar, is designed to observe Earth's land and ice surfaces with unprecedented precision. It will track changes as subtle as a few millimetres, making it a powerful tool for studying everything from shifting tectonic plates to melting glaciers.

What NISAR Will Track

The satellite's twin radar systems will scan nearly all of Earth's land and ice twice every 12 days. This frequent revisit will allow scientists to monitor natural hazards like earthquakes, landslides, and volcanic activity in near real time.

Beyond hazards, NISAR will also measure changes in forests, wetlands, and agricultural lands, offering insights into carbon cycles and ecosystem health. The data will be freely available to researchers worldwide, a key goal for both agencies.

US-India Collaboration

NISAR is one of the largest Earth-observation satellites ever built, developed jointly by NASA and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). The spacecraft integrates two radar systems—one from each agency—operating at different wavelengths to capture complementary views of the surface.

The collaboration has been years in the making, with engineers from both countries working to integrate the complex instruments. ISRO is providing the satellite bus and launch vehicle, while NASA contributed the higher-frequency radar and other key components.

Launch Preparations

The satellite is currently undergoing final testing and integration at ISRO's facilities in India. Officials have not yet confirmed an exact launch date, but the mission is expected to lift off aboard a Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV) from Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh.

Once in orbit, NISAR will operate for at least three years, but its fuel reserves are expected to extend the mission well beyond that. The data it returns will build a continuous record of Earth's changing surface, which scientists say will be invaluable for climate research and disaster response.

Preview Event Details

The upcoming preview at NASA will offer journalists and the public a chance to see the mission up close, with experts explaining its technical innovations and scientific promise. It will also mark a milestone in US-India space ties, which have grown stronger in recent years.

NASA officials have emphasised that NISAR is not just a technological achievement but a practical tool for communities facing environmental risks. By mapping ground movement and water resources, the mission could aid in managing everything from groundwater depletion to flood forecasting.

As the launch nears, all eyes will be on the final preparations. The mission represents a significant step forward in Earth observation, and its success will depend on the seamless integration of two nations' engineering efforts.

Watch for the formal launch date announcement in the coming weeks, as NISAR prepares to begin its watch over a changing planet.
