May 2026 Box Office: Regional Films Karuppu, Drishyam 3, Raja Shivaji Beat Bollywood

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tamil film Karuppu led the May 2026 box office with strong opening weekend numbers.
  • Malayalam sequel Drishyam 3 continued the franchise's record-breaking trend.
  • Marathi historical drama Raja Shivaji emerged as a surprise blockbuster.
  • Bollywood films failed to match the regional giants' collections.

Regional Films Dominate May Box Office

The May 2026 box office has delivered a clear verdict: regional cinema is ruling over Bollywood. Tamil, Malayalam, and Marathi films have not only outperformed Hindi releases but have also set new benchmarks for ticket sales and audience engagement.

Tamil film Karuppu emerged as the top grosser of the month, with a massive opening weekend that drew crowds across Tamil Nadu and beyond. The film's gritty action and strong performances resonated with viewers, making it the month's biggest hit.

Drishyam 3 Continues Franchise Legacy

Malayalam cinema’s Drishyam 3 proved that the franchise's appeal remains undiminished. The third installment in the hit series opened to packed houses in Kerala and key metro cities, breaking several opening day records for a Malayalam film.

The film's gripping narrative and return of the original cast kept audiences hooked, leading to strong word-of-mouth publicity. Trade analysts note that the film’s success underscores the growing appetite for well-crafted regional content across India.

Raja Shivaji: A Marathi Blockbuster

Marathi historical drama Raja Shivaji emerged as the surprise package of May 2026. The film, which chronicles the early life of Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj, struck a chord with audiences in Maharashtra and beyond.

Its authentic storytelling, grand visuals, and powerful performances turned it into a word-of-mouth hit. The film's collections grew steadily week after week, eventually surpassing several big-budget Bollywood releases.

Bollywood Lags Behind

In contrast, Bollywood's big-ticket releases in May 2026 failed to generate the same level of excitement. Despite major marketing campaigns and star power, Hindi films struggled to match the regional giants' box office numbers.

Industry insiders point to a growing disconnect between Bollywood's formulaic offerings and what audiences now expect. Regional films, with their rooted stories and authentic characters, are filling that gap effectively.

Trade experts believe this trend is not a temporary blip but a long-term shift in Indian cinema. The success of Karuppu, Drishyam 3, and Raja Shivaji highlights that quality content, regardless of language, can draw crowds and generate revenue.

What remains to be seen is whether Bollywood will adapt its strategy or continue to lose ground to regional powerhouses in the months ahead.
