Case registered against real estate firm for duping investors in Hyderabad

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • A case has been filed against a real estate firm in Hyderabad for allegedly defrauding investors.
  • Investors claim they paid crores for plots that were never handed over.
  • Police have launched an investigation based on a complaint lodged by the victims.

Hyderabad police have registered a case against a real estate firm for allegedly cheating investors of crores of rupees. The complaint, filed by a group of investors, accuses the company of promising residential plots in a project that never materialised.

The investors said they paid significant sums over the past few years. In return, the firm issued allotment letters but failed to develop the land or transfer ownership.

Investors allege long-running fraud

According to the complaint, the firm collected money from dozens of people between 2021 and 2025. Each investor paid between Rs 5 lakh and Rs 25 lakh for plots in a layout near Shamshabad.

Despite repeated follow-ups, the company kept delaying possession. It cited various reasons, including pending approvals and land disputes.

One of the complainants, a retired government employee, said he invested his life savings. "We trusted their promises. Now we have no money and no land," he said.

Police action and investigation underway

The case has been registered at the Raidurgam police station under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code for cheating, criminal breach of trust, and conspiracy. Officials said they are examining documents, bank records, and the firm's registration details.

Police have not yet made any arrests. They said they will issue notices to the company directors and seek their version of events.

Investigators are also checking if the firm had similar complaints against it in the past. Sources said at least two other groups have approached the police in recent months with similar allegations against the same company.

Regulatory gaps and investor caution

The case highlights a recurring problem in Hyderabad's real estate market. Despite RERA registration being mandatory, many smaller developers operate without full compliance. Investors often rely on verbal assurances or incomplete paperwork.

Legal experts say buyers must verify land titles, project approvals, and developer track records before investing. Many frauds occur when companies offer easy payment plans or heavy discounts to lure customers.

"We see such cases every year. Investors should insist on registered agreements and check the status of the land with the sub-registrar's office," a lawyer familiar with the matter said.

The Telangana Real Estate Regulatory Authority has also issued warnings in the past about unregistered projects. But enforcement remains a challenge.

What happens next: The police will file a charge sheet after completing their investigation. The accused directors could face arrest if evidence of fraud is found. Investors are now waiting for the court to decide on their plea for refunds or compensation.
