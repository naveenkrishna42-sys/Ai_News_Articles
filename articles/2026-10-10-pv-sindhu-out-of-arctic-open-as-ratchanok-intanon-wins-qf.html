While quarter-final defeats can look like single-match outcomes, they often reflect a cluster of tactical issues: how a player handles first contact, whether they convert defence into tempo, and how quickly they recover after losing rally control.

What changed in the quarter-final match-up

Against players like Sindhu, those tactics matter because the difference between a clean attack and a “half-chance” often decides the outcome of the next rally.

For Sindhu, a loss to Intanon also signals a need to manage pace transitions more effectively—particularly when the rally shifts from flat exchanges to sharp, angle-driven rallies. At elite levels, even one or two extended sequences of ineffective returns can compound into a score gap.
