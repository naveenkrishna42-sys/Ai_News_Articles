CJP Protesters Regroup at Jantar Mantar After Crackdown

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Thousands regroup at Jantar Mantar after police used tear gas and lathis to stop a march to Parliament.
  • The 'Chalo Sansad' march was halted by police, leading to injuries and arrests.
  • Media coverage has focused on the crackdown, with images of injured protesters circulating widely.

Protesters Return to Jantar Mantar

Thousands of protesters from the Cockroach Janata Party (CJP) gathered at Jantar Mantar in Delhi on Tuesday. The crowd began assembling in the morning, a day after police used tear gas and lathis to break up their march to Parliament.

The demonstration, called the 'Chalo Sansad' march, had aimed to pressure the government on a range of demands. Videos from Monday's crackdown showed police charging at protesters and firing tear gas shells to disperse the crowd.

Police Action Draws Scrutiny

Images of injured protesters being carried away from the scene were widely shared on social media. News outlets including The Hindu, NDTV, Al Jazeera, and AP News have covered the crackdown, with one report referring to the movement as the 'Cockroach movement'.

A blog by The Hindu noted what it called 'what the cameras missed' during the student protest march in Delhi, hinting at details beyond the widely broadcast visuals. NDTV reported that the movement has halted its marches following the police action.

Movement's Name and Origins

The group, which has adopted the 'cockroach' label, has been holding protests across the city for weeks. The name is reportedly a reclamation of a term used by a political rival to dismiss the activists.

Tuesday's regrouping at Jantar Mantar signals that the movement is not backing down. The site has been a traditional location for protests in the capital, and the crowd was seen holding banners and shouting slogans.

Mixed Reactions to the Crackdown

While police have justified the action as necessary to maintain order, opposition leaders and civil rights groups have condemned the use of force. The crackdown has drawn comparisons to previous instances of police action against protestors in Delhi.

Al Jazeera reported that police used tear gas to stop the march on Parliament. AP News published a photo essay titled 'Lathis, tear gas and injured protesters: The ‘Chalo Sansad’ crackdown in pictures', documenting the violence.

Newslaundry, a media watchdog, also covered the event, contributing to the growing narrative of a heavy-handed response.

What Comes Next

The CJP has not announced its next move, but the large turnout at Jantar Mantar suggests the movement retains significant momentum. Police presence in the area remains high, and authorities are monitoring the situation closely.

The coming days will likely see continued protests and possibly further negotiations between the group and the administration.
