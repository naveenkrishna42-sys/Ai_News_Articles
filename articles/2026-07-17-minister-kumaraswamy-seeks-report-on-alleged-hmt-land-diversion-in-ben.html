Minister Kumaraswamy seeks report on alleged HMT land diversion in Bengaluru

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Minister Kumaraswamy seeks report on alleged land diversion by HMT Ltd.
  • Complaint claims HMT land meant for public use was given to a private developer.
  • Minister has asked for details within a week.
  • HMT land in question is located in Bengaluru's industrial area.
  • Action will be taken based on the report's findings, says minister.

Minister seeks details on HMT land complaint

Union Minister for Heavy Industries and Public Enterprises H D Kumaraswamy has asked Hindustan Machine Tools Limited (HMT) for a detailed report on allegations that land belonging to the public sector undertaking was diverted to a private developer in Bengaluru. The minister's move comes after a complaint was filed claiming that HMT land, originally earmarked for a public project, was instead handed over to a private entity.

Kumaraswamy confirmed on Thursday that he has sought the report from HMT officials. He stated that the company has been given a week to submit a comprehensive explanation. The minister said he would take appropriate action after reviewing the findings.

Allegations of land misuse

The complaint, details of which are still emerging, alleges that a parcel of HMT land in Bengaluru's industrial belt was diverted from its intended use. The land was reportedly meant for a project that would benefit the public or the company itself. Instead, it is alleged to have been leased or sold to a private developer without proper approval.

Sources close to the matter said the complaint was filed by a former employee of HMT. The complainant has alleged that the diversion occurred over the past few years and that the deal lacked transparency. HMT officials have not yet commented on the specific allegations.

Government's stance and next steps

Minister Kumaraswamy has made it clear that any misuse of public sector assets will not be tolerated. He said the government is committed to protecting the interests of public sector undertakings and their employees. The minister's office has not confirmed the exact location of the land or the name of the developer involved.

HMT, once a flagship of Indian manufacturing, has faced financial troubles in the past and has been selling off land parcels to raise funds. However, such sales have often been controversial, with critics alleging a lack of oversight. The current complaint adds to the scrutiny on the company's asset management practices.

The report will be examined by the ministry, and if irregularities are found, action could include a formal inquiry or even legal proceedings. The minister has indicated that he will not hesitate to act if the allegations are proven true.

HMT has not yet issued a statement on the matter. The company is expected to submit its report within the stipulated time frame. The Bengaluru development authority and local civic bodies may also be roped in if the land's zoning or usage rights are found to have been violated.

What to watch: HMT's response in the coming week will be crucial. If the report confirms wrongdoing, the government could order a wider investigation into other land deals by the PSU. The outcome may also affect the company's ongoing restructuring efforts.
