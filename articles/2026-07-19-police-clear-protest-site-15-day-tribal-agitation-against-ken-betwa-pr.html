Police clear protest site, 15-day tribal agitation against Ken-Betwa project ends

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tribal protest against Ken-Betwa project in MP ended after 15 days
  • Police and administration forcibly cleared the agitation site
  • Protesters were involved in 'pyre protests' against the river linking project
  • Congress has alleged irregularities in gram sabha approvals for the project

Protesters removed from site in Chhatarpur

Police and district administration officials in Madhya Pradesh's Chhatarpur district forcibly cleared a protest site on Sunday, bringing a 15-day-long tribal agitation against the Ken-Betwa river linking project to an abrupt end. The protesters had been holding what they termed 'pyre protests' — symbolic funeral pyres — to oppose the project.

Officials moved in early in the morning, removing makeshift structures and dispersing the crowd. The exact number of people detained has not been confirmed by authorities.

Why the Ken-Betwa project faces opposition

The Ken-Betwa link project, a flagship interlinking of rivers initiative, aims to transfer water from the Ken river to the Betwa river. It is designed to irrigate large tracts of farmland in the Bundelkhand region, which frequently faces drought. But the project has faced sustained opposition from tribal communities and environmental activists.

Critics argue that the project will submerge large forest areas, including parts of the Panna Tiger Reserve, and displace thousands of families. The 'pyre protests' were a symbolic gesture to highlight what protesters call the 'death' of their ancestral lands and livelihoods.

Congress raises questions over approvals

The Congress party has alleged irregularities in the gram sabha approvals obtained for the project. Party leaders claim that consent from local village councils was not taken properly, violating the Forest Rights Act and the Panchayats (Extension to Scheduled Areas) Act.

State government officials have denied these allegations, stating that all due processes were followed. The matter is likely to be raised in the upcoming assembly session.

What happens next

With the protest site cleared, the administration is expected to resume preparatory work for the project. However, opposition from tribal groups and political parties is unlikely to fade. Legal challenges and fresh rounds of agitation remain possibilities as affected communities vow to continue their fight through other means.
