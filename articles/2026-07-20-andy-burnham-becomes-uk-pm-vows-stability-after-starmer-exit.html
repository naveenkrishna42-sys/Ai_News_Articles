Andy Burnham becomes UK PM, vows stability after Starmer exit

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Andy Burnham sworn in as UK Prime Minister after Keir Starmer's resignation
  • He is the 7th PM in 10 years, vows to bring stability
  • First policy pledge: ending rough sleeping

Andy Burnham was sworn in as the United Kingdom's Prime Minister on Monday, becoming the seventh person to hold the office in the last ten years. After accepting the King's invitation to form a government, Burnham addressed the nation from outside 10 Downing Street, promising a 'new path for Britain.'

He takes over following the resignation of Keir Starmer, whose tenure was marked by internal party strife and a stagnating economy. Burnham's swift elevation signals the Labour Party's desire for a reset without triggering a general election.

A familiar face with a new mandate

Burnham, the former Mayor of Greater Manchester, is no stranger to national politics. He served as Health Secretary under Gordon Brown and was a prominent voice during the COVID-19 pandemic. His shift from regional leadership to the top job came through an internal party process after Starmer stepped down.

Political observers note that Burnham inherits a divided parliament and a public weary of political instability. 'The British people want competence, not chaos,' Burnham said in his first remarks as PM. 'I intend to deliver that.'

First policy: ending rough sleeping

In a move that surprised few who have followed his career, Burnham made ending rough sleeping his first concrete policy pledge. He announced a cross-departmental task force to be set up within the first week of his tenure.

'No one in the sixth richest country in the world should be forced to sleep on the streets,' Burnham stated. The plan includes emergency housing funding and mental health support for homeless individuals. Critics have questioned the cost, but early reactions from charities have been positive.

The challenge of longevity

Burnham enters office aware of the brutal churn in British politics. Since the Brexit referendum in 2016, the UK has cycled through leaders from David Cameron to Theresa May, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss, Rishi Sunak, and now Starmer. None completed a full five-year term.

His first 100 days will be crucial. The new PM faces a cost-of-living crisis, strained public services, and a restless backbench. He has promised a 'government of service' and has already reached out to opposition MPs for cooperation.

Downing Street sources confirm that Burnham will deliver a full budget plan within 50 days, focusing on housing, healthcare, and green energy. He is expected to retain several key Starmer-era ministers while bringing in fresh faces from his own network.

The next parliamentary session will test whether Burnham can translate his regional popularity into national stability. For now, the Labour Party and the country are watching closely.
