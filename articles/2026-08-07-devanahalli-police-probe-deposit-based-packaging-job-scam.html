Devanahalli police probe deposit-based packaging job scam

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Police in Devanahalli have launched a probe into an alleged packaging job scam involving deposits.
  • The scam reportedly targeted job seekers with promises of packaging work in exchange for upfront deposits.
  • Authorities have not yet confirmed the number of victims or the total amount involved.

Job seekers allege fraud in packaging work scheme

Police in Devanahalli, on the outskirts of Bengaluru, have registered a case and launched an investigation into an alleged deposit-based packaging job scam. The complaint, filed by a group of job seekers, claims that a private agency collected money from them under the pretext of providing packaging work, but later failed to deliver on its promises.

The development comes amid growing concerns over fraudulent employment schemes that exploit the desperation of job hunters. The agency, whose name has not been officially disclosed, is accused of demanding deposits ranging from a few thousand to tens of thousands of rupees per applicant.

How the alleged scam operated

According to preliminary details available, the accused individuals reportedly advertised packaging job openings in and around Devanahalli, promising steady work and decent wages. Interested candidates were asked to pay a refundable deposit to secure their position, a common but legally dubious practice.

After collecting the money, the agency allegedly became unreachable, leaving applicants without jobs or refunds. Some victims have told investigators that they were given appointment letters and asked to report to a warehouse, only to find no such facility existed.

Police sources indicate that the accused may have used fake documents and temporary premises to lend legitimacy to their operation. The exact number of victims and the total amount collected are still being ascertained.

Investigation underway

A case has been registered under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code, including cheating and criminal breach of trust. The Devanahalli police have formed a team to trace the accused, who are believed to have shut down their offices and switched off their phones.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether more than one person is behind the scam, nor have they revealed the identities of the complainants. Investigators are currently examining bank records and call detail records to map the flow of money.

This is not the first time such a scam has surfaced in the region. Similar cases have been reported in other parts of Bengaluru, where fraudulent agencies have preyed on people seeking blue-collar and semi-skilled jobs.

Authorities urge caution

The police have advised job seekers to verify the credentials of any agency before paying deposits. They also encourage victims of such scams to come forward and file complaints, as this helps in building a stronger case.

Legal experts point out that under Indian labour laws, charging a fee for job placements is a grey area, and deposit-based recruitment is often a red flag. However, enforcement remains a challenge, especially when agencies operate informally.

As the investigation progresses, more victims are expected to emerge. The police have appealed to anyone who has been defrauded by the agency to contact the Devanahalli police station.

The case serves as a reminder of the vulnerabilities in the informal job market, where promises of quick employment can sometimes mask fraudulent intent.

What to watch

Over the coming weeks, the focus will be on whether the police manage to apprehend the accused and recover the deposits. The outcome of this probe could also prompt stricter scrutiny of similar job placement schemes in the region.
