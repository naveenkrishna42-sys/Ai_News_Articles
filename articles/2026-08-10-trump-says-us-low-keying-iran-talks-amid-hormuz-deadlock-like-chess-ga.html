Trump says US 'low-keying' Iran talks amid Hormuz deadlock: 'Like chess game'

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump confirms US is de-escalating public rhetoric in talks with Iran
  • Strait of Hormuz remains a flashpoint amid ongoing nuclear tensions
  • President likens diplomatic process to a chess game, expresses optimism
  • No breakthrough announced as both sides remain entrenched on key issues

White House tones down public posturing

US President Donald Trump has said Washington is deliberately "low-keying" its diplomatic engagement with Iran, even as the two countries remain locked in a tense standoff over the Strait of Hormuz. Speaking to reporters, Trump described the ongoing negotiations as a "chess game" that would eventually "work out," signalling a more measured approach after weeks of heightened rhetoric.

The remarks come amid reports that Tehran has raised the stakes in the strategic waterway, through which a significant share of global oil traffic passes. While the President did not detail specific proposals, his language suggested a willingness to let the process unfold without public pressure tactics.

Hormuz remains the central flashpoint

The Strait of Hormuz has emerged as the primary point of friction between Washington and Tehran. Iranian officials have repeatedly threatened to disrupt shipping in the strait in response to US sanctions and military posturing, a move that would have immediate consequences for global energy markets.

Trump's latest comments suggest a tactical shift toward economic pressure rather than further military strikes, a position that some analysts see as an attempt to avoid escalation while maintaining leverage. The President's framing of the standoff as a strategic contest, rather than a crisis, appears designed to reassure markets and allies alike.

Nuclear deal hangs in the balance

Behind the Hormuz tension lies the unresolved question of Iran's nuclear programme. Earlier reports indicated that Trump might be willing to end military action without a comprehensive nuclear agreement, provided certain conditions are met. However, no such conditions have been publicly outlined, and Iranian leaders have shown little inclination to compromise under duress.

The chess analogy is telling: it implies a long game, with multiple moves and counter-moves still to come. But unlike a chess match, the stakes here include the potential for armed conflict and economic disruption across the region.

Regional and global implications

India, like other major importers of oil, watches the situation closely. Any sustained disruption in the Strait of Hormuz would ripple through energy prices, affecting economies from New Delhi to Tokyo. New Delhi has maintained a careful balancing act, calling for de-escalation while preserving its ties with both Washington and Tehran.

So far, there has been no official confirmation of a breakthrough, and diplomats familiar with the talks suggest that both sides remain entrenched. The US administration has not clarified what "low-keying" means in practical terms, leaving room for interpretation.

What to watch next

As the chess game continues, the next few weeks will reveal whether this quieter approach yields tangible progress or merely delays an inevitable confrontation. Observers will be watching for any signs of movement on the nuclear file or a shift in Iran's posture regarding Hormuz. For now, the President's optimism remains untested by events on the ground.
