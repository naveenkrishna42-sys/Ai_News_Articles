Android lock screen flaw lets Gemini bypass PIN, send messages

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Android lock screen bug lets Gemini bypass PIN authentication.
  • The flaw allows sending messages, making calls, and accessing data on a locked phone.
  • Google is rolling out a security update to patch the vulnerability.

Security flaw discovered in Android lock screen

A critical vulnerability has been found in the Android operating system that allows the Gemini AI assistant to bypass the lock screen and send messages without requiring a PIN or biometric authentication. The flaw, reported by multiple security researchers, affects devices running the latest versions of Android.

According to reports from SC Media and other outlets, the bug permits users—or malicious actors with physical access—to interact with Gemini and, through it, perform actions like sending SMS messages, making calls, or accessing certain apps without unlocking the phone.

How the bypass works

The vulnerability leverages the deep integration of Gemini into the Android lock screen interface. In normal operation, the assistant is designed to answer queries and perform limited tasks without compromising security. However, researchers found that specific voice commands or sequences could trick the system into granting broader access.

Once triggered, Gemini can execute commands that typically require the phone to be unlocked, such as composing and sending text messages. The exploit does not require complex tools or special privileges—just physical access to the locked device.

Security analysts have pointed out that the flaw could be exploited in public settings, such as leaving a phone unattended at a café or during a meeting. An attacker could quickly send fraudulent messages or extract sensitive information without leaving a trace.

Google acknowledges the issue, fix underway

Google has acknowledged the vulnerability and confirmed that it is rolling out a security patch. The company did not provide a specific timeline but said the update would be delivered to supported devices via the usual monthly security update channel.

"We are aware of the issue and are deploying a fix to all affected Android devices," a Google spokesperson said. "Users are advised to ensure their devices are set to receive automatic updates and to apply the patch as soon as it becomes available."

While the bug is serious, there have been no confirmed reports of it being actively exploited in the wild. Security experts recommend that users keep their phones physically secure and avoid leaving them unlocked or unattended in public places.

Implications for Android users

This incident highlights the risks associated with integrating powerful AI assistants directly into the operating system's lock screen. As assistants like Gemini gain more capabilities—including the ability to send messages, control smart home devices, and access personal data—the attack surface for potential exploits widens.

Google has not disclosed whether the bug was introduced in a recent update or has been present for longer. The vulnerability appears to affect a wide range of Android devices, including those from Samsung, OnePlus, and Xiaomi, as they all use Google's stock lock screen implementation.

Users are encouraged to install the upcoming security update as soon as it is available and to review their lock screen settings to minimize exposure. Until the patch is applied, disabling Gemini's lock screen access in the assistant settings may provide an additional layer of protection.

The rollout of the fix is expected to begin within the next week. Android users should monitor their device's system update notifications to ensure they receive the patch promptly.
