Three arrested for dangerous bike stunts in Chennai's Vadapalani

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Three men were arrested for performing dangerous bike stunts in Vadapalani.
  • The police seized the motorcycle used in the stunts.
  • The arrests come amid a crackdown on reckless riding in Chennai.

Arrests in Vadapalani

Chennai police arrested three men on Wednesday for performing dangerous bike stunts in Vadapalani, a busy residential and commercial neighbourhood. The arrests followed a tip-off and a swift response by local law enforcement.

The three individuals were taken into custody after witnesses reported seeing them performing wheelies and other risky manoeuvres on public roads. Police have not yet released the names of the accused, but confirmed that the motorcycle used in the stunts has been seized.

Rising concern over stunt riding

Reckless biking has become a growing concern in Chennai, with several incidents reported across the city in recent months. Residents in Vadapalani had earlier complained about youngsters using the wide stretches of road near the junction for impromptu races and stunts, endangering themselves and other road users.

Police have intensified patrols in vulnerable spots, especially during late evening hours when such activities are more common. The latest arrests are part of a broader effort to curb what officials describe as a public safety hazard.

Legal action taken

The three men have been booked under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code and the Motor Vehicles Act, including charges related to rash driving and endangering public safety. They were produced before a magistrate and later released on bail, sources said.

Authorities are also investigating whether the trio had any prior involvement in similar incidents. The seized motorcycle is being examined for any modifications that may have been made to enhance speed or performance illegally.

Public response and safety measures

Local residents have welcomed the police action, with many calling for stricter enforcement and heavier penalties for repeat offenders. Some have suggested installing speed breakers and CCTV cameras at known stunt spots to deter such behaviour.

Traffic police have urged the public to report any instances of dangerous riding immediately. They have also advised parents to monitor their children's use of two-wheelers, stressing that such stunts often lead to severe injuries or fatalities.

In a related advisory, police reminded riders that public roads are not playgrounds, and that violating traffic rules can have serious legal consequences.

What happens next

The investigation is ongoing, and police are expected to file a detailed chargesheet in the coming days. Meanwhile, patrols in Vadapalani and other flagged zones will continue, with officials hinting at the possibility of deploying plainclothes officers to catch offenders in the act.
