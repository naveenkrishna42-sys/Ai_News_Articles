Bombay HC orders removal of deepfake videos targeting Nitin Gadkari

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Court orders immediate takedown of deepfake videos
  • Videos linked to ethanol policy criticism
  • Gadkari approached court seeking relief

The Bombay High Court on Wednesday directed the removal of deepfake videos targeting Union Minister for Road Transport and Highways, Nitin Gadkari, over his ethanol policy. The interim order comes amid concerns that the manipulated clips could stoke public anger and disrupt social harmony.

The court's directive was issued in response to a plea filed by Gadkari, who sought urgent intervention after the videos surfaced online. The clips, created using artificial intelligence, purportedly showed the minister making statements critical of his own policy, a move that could confuse voters and the public at large.

Court's directive

Justice A.S. Chandurkar, presiding over the bench, ordered the removal of the videos from all platforms, including social media and messaging apps. The court also directed the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology to identify the origin of the deepfakes and take appropriate action against those responsible.

Advocate General Birendra Saraf, representing the state, assured the court of full cooperation in tracing the creators. The court emphasised that such content, if left unchecked, could lead to a breakdown of public trust in government communications.

Gadkari's plea

In his petition, Gadkari argued that the deepfakes were designed to tarnish his image and mislead the public about the ethanol policy, which aims to reduce India's dependence on fossil fuels. The minister claimed that the videos were not only defamatory but also posed a threat to his safety, as they could incite anger among farmers and other stakeholders.

The petition highlighted that the deepfakes were crafted with high precision, making it difficult for the average viewer to distinguish them from genuine footage. Gadkari's counsel argued that this was a clear violation of his right to reputation and privacy under Article 21 of the Constitution.

Legal implications

This case is among the first of its kind in India, where a senior political figure has sought judicial intervention specifically against deepfake content. The court's order sets a precedent for handling similar cases involving AI-generated misinformation.

Legal experts note that while existing laws, including the Information Technology Act, provide some recourse, the rapid evolution of deepfake technology poses fresh challenges. The court's proactive stance signals a willingness to adapt legal frameworks to address these emerging threats.

Broader concerns

The incident underscores the growing menace of deepfakes in Indian politics. In recent months, several such videos have surfaced, targeting public figures across the spectrum, raising alarms about the integrity of electoral processes and public discourse.

Authorities have not yet confirmed any arrests, and the investigation is ongoing. The court has scheduled a further hearing in two weeks, during which it will review compliance with its orders and consider more permanent relief.

As the case progresses, the focus will remain on how digital platforms respond to court-mandated takedowns and the effectiveness of existing mechanisms in curbing AI-driven disinformation. The outcome could influence future policy on deepfake regulation in India.
