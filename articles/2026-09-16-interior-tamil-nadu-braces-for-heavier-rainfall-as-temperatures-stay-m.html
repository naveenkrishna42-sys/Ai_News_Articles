Interior Tamil Nadu Braces for Heavier Rainfall as Temperatures Stay Moderate

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-16 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Interior districts are forecast to receive 20‑35% more rainfall than normal, keeping daytime temperatures 2‑4°C below seasonal averages.
  • Enhanced rains improve groundwater recharge and reservoir levels but raise flood risks for low‑lying areas and certain crops.
  • Urban planners must upgrade drainage and issue timely alerts to mitigate disruption while leveraging cooler conditions for public health.

Overview of the Forecast

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) predicts a noticeable intensification of rainfall across Tamil Nadu's interior regions over the next 72 hours. Districts such as Coimbatore, Madurai, Tiruchirappalli, and Salem are expected to receive between 80 and 120 mm of rain, which is 20‑35% above the climatological mean for mid‑September. Simultaneously, maximum temperatures are projected to stay 2‑4 °C below the usual 32‑35 °C range, offering a moderating influence on heat stress.

Meteorological Drivers Behind the Intensify

A persistent low‑pressure trough over the Bay of Bengal is channeling moist south‑westerly winds inland, interacting with the Western Ghats' orographic lift. This setup enhances convective activity over the plateau and interior valleys. Satellite‑derived precipitable water values exceed 45 mm, indicating ample moisture availability for sustained rainfall episodes.

Impact on Agriculture and Water Security

Crop Yields and Soil Moisture

For rain‑fed crops such as millets, pulses, and early‑stage paddy, the added soil moisture can improve germination and reduce irrigation demand. However, water‑logging poses a threat to standing cotton and sugarcane fields in low‑lying tracts, potentially causing root rot and yield losses of 5‑10% if drainage is inadequate.

Groundwater Recharge and Reservoir Levels

Hydrogeological models suggest that the anticipated rainfall could recharge shallow aquifers by 0.8‑1.2 m in the interior hard‑rock zones, benefiting drinking‑water supplies. Major reservoirs like Mettur and Vaigai are projected to gain 15‑20 % of their live storage, easing pressure on downstream irrigation canals.

Urban Infrastructure and Disaster Preparedness

Drainage and Flood Management

City corporations in Coimbatore and Tiruchirappalli have pre‑positioned de‑watering pumps and cleared storm‑water drains to handle peak flows of up to 150 m³/s. Nonetheless, areas with inadequate gradient, such as parts of Madurai’s old town, remain vulnerable to flash flooding, necessitating real‑time monitoring and community alerts.

Temperature Moderation and Public Health

The subdued daytime temperatures lower the risk of heat‑related illnesses, especially for outdoor labourers and elderly residents. Night‑time lows are expected to stay around 22‑24 °C, providing comfortable conditions that may reduce peak electricity demand for cooling by approximately 8‑10 % during the period.

Advisories for Stakeholders

Farmers are advised to delay sowing of water‑sensitive crops until soil moisture stabilises and to utilise early‑warning SMS services from the Tamil Nadu State Disaster Management Authority. Urban residents should avoid travel through flooded low‑lying routes and secure loose objects that could become hazards in strong gusts. Continuous updates from IMD’s Doppler radar network will guide timely interventions.
