Why the 555-Day FD Has Become a Rate Magnet

Fixed deposit investors hunting for yield have a new favourite tenure: 555 days. Several small finance banks and a handful of NBFCs have priced this odd-duration product at up to 7.50% per annum for general citizens, with senior citizens earning an extra 50-75 basis points. The tenure is deliberately engineered — long enough for banks to lock in stable funding, short enough to stay under the two-year mark where rates typically slip.

The Economic Times reported this week that 555-day deposits now rank among the highest-yielding bank products outside of tax-saving and corporate FDs. For a household parking Rs 5 lakh, the difference between a 6.50% large-bank FD and a 7.50% special tenure works out to roughly Rs 5,000 in extra annual interest.

Post-Tax Reality Check for Buyers

The headline number rarely survives taxation. FD interest is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate. An investor in the 30% bracket earning 7.50% effectively nets about 5.25% pre-cess and roughly 5.10% after cess. That is still competitive against a 1-year bank FD at 6.50%, which nets around 4.55% for the same investor.

Senior citizens get a useful cushion here. Section 80TTB allows up to Rs 50,000 of FD interest to be deducted from taxable income, which materially improves the effective yield on a 555-day deposit for retirees. A senior earning 8.25% can realistically retain 6.5-7% post-tax if total interest stays within that deduction window.

Pros and Cons Before You Book

  • Pro: 555-day rates are 50-125 bps higher than comparable 12-24 month deposits at most large banks.
  • Pro: Tenure is short enough to avoid the interest-rate risk of a 5-year lock-in if rates rise further.
  • Pro: Senior citizens get both a higher card rate and the Rs 50,000 Section 80TTB deduction.
  • Con: The highest rates come from small finance banks with weaker credit profiles than SBI or HDFC Bank.
  • Con: Premature withdrawal usually triggers a 0.50-1% penalty, wiping out the rate advantage.
  • Con: Only Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank is insured by DICGC, so large sums need splitting across institutions.

How to Decide If the 555-Day FD Fits Your Portfolio

Treat this product as a mid-duration parking slot, not a long-term retirement instrument. It suits investors who have matured a 1-year FD and want to extend duration slightly without committing to a 3-5 year lock-in. Splitting Rs 15 lakh across three small finance banks keeps each deposit under the DICGC insurance ceiling while capturing the 7.50% card rate.

Compare the post-tax yield against debt mutual funds and arbitrage funds before booking. For investors in the 30% bracket, a 7.50% FD nets roughly 5.10%, which is competitive but not automatically superior to a short-duration debt fund with indexation benefits. The 555-day FD wins on certainty and capital protection, not on absolute return.
