Rohit Sharma Lord's century used Shardul Thakur's bat after his own broke

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rohit Sharma scored his first ODI century at Lord's, a first for an Indian batter.
  • He used Shardul Thakur's bat after his own broke during the innings.
  • The century helped India post a strong total and silenced critics about his form.

An Unusual Switch at the Crease

Rohit Sharma's historic century at Lord's on Sunday came with an unusual twist. The Indian opener was forced to use teammate Shardul Thakur's bat after his own bat broke during his innings.

The incident happened in the 30th over. Rohit had already crossed fifty and was looking settled. His bat, a trusted MRF model, split near the splice after a firm drive. He signalled to the dressing room immediately.

Shardul Thakur, padded up and waiting to bat, handed over his spare bat. Rohit took a few practice swings, nodded, and carried on. He did not change his approach.

Record-Breaking Knock

Rohit went on to score 119 off 128 balls. He became the first Indian batter to score an ODI century at Lord's. The knock also took him past Sachin Tendulkar's record for most international centuries by an Indian opener.

The Mumbai batter has often faced questions about his form in overseas conditions. This innings was a direct answer. He did not take off his helmet or celebrate aggressively. He raised his bat, acknowledged the crowd, and got back to work.

His partnership with Virat Kohli, who scored 85, steadied India after an early wicket. The duo added 167 runs for the second wicket.

Team Balance Under Scrutiny

India's team combination remains a talking point. With Shardul Thakur playing as the third seamer, the bowling attack lacks a fifth bowler option. Hardik Pandya's absence due to injury has left a gap.

Captain Rohit Sharma has backed the current XI. He said after the match that the team is confident about its balance. But with the World Cup less than a year away, selectors are watching closely.

England's Joe Root, who scored a gritty 82, kept his team in the hunt. But India's bowlers, led by Jasprit Bumrah's three wickets, restricted the hosts to 280, winning by 43 runs.

What Comes Next

India will play the second ODI at Headingley on Thursday. The team management will have to decide whether to persist with the same combination or bring in an extra spinner. The pitch in Leeds traditionally aids seam movement early on.
