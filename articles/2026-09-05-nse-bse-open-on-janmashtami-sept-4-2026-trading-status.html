Stock Market Holiday on Janmashtami: September 4, 2026

Indian benchmark exchanges, the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE), will observe a complete trading holiday on Friday, September 4, 2026, to mark Janmashtami. The festival commemorates the birth of Lord Krishna and falls on the eighth day (Ashtami) of the Krishna Paksha in the month of Shravan or Bhadrapada, depending on the regional calendar.

Both exchanges confirmed the closure in their annual holiday circulars released in January 2026. The holiday falls under Section 2 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, which exempts banking and settlement institutions from operational mandates during gazetted regional festivals. For retail and institutional traders, this means no live order routing across any segment for the entire session.

Which Segments Remain Closed?

According to the exchange holiday calendars, the shutdown extends across the full capital market stack, including F&O, currency derivatives, and the wholesale debt segment, which will operate only with a truncated window on certain regional holidays but remains fully shut on Janmashtami. MCX commodity derivatives will also remain closed.

Real-World Implications for Investors and Traders

A full-day holiday carries practical consequences beyond a missed session. With T+1 settlement now standard since January 2023, trades executed on Thursday, September 3, will not be settled until Monday, September 7. Brokerage margin reporting and pledge invocation requests submitted on the holiday will queue for processing on the next working day.

Corporate action watchers should note that record dates falling on September 4 for dividends, bonus issues, or splits will typically shift to the immediately preceding trading day, per SEBI's standard listing obligations. The September 2026 F&O expiry series contracts expiring on Thursday, September 24, remain unaffected by this single-day interruption.

  • Zero intraday liquidity in cash and derivatives segments on September 4, with no pre-open or closing call auction.
  • Currency and interest rate derivative segments fully suspended; no evening or morning reference rate fix on this date.
  • Settlement deferral extends clearing members' exposure window by one calendar day, marginally impacting leveraged overnight positions taken on September 3.
  • Algorithmic and HFT participants must halt all deployed strategies and recalibrate volatility parameters for the September 7 open.
  • No disruption to GIFT-IFSC exchange operations at IFSC Exchange Trading Platform, which follows its own NSE IFSC holiday calendar.

Historical Context and Market Behaviour Around Festival Holidays

Indian markets have recorded elevated overnight gap movements following festival holidays. Historical data from Nifty 50 sessions preceding Janmashtami (2022 to 2025) shows an average absolute move of approximately 0.6 percent in the following session, broadly in line with non-festival post-holiday behaviour. The volatility factor (India VIX) typically contracts by 1 to 2 percent during festival weeks due to reduced participation, then expands at the reopening.

For F&O traders, the absence of Thursday's weekly expiry on this particular date is worth tracking. The NSE weekly NIFTY and BANKNIFTY options expire every Thursday; since September 4 is a Friday, no weekly contracts are at risk on the holiday itself. However, traders with monthly contracts expiring the same week should manage delta exposure before the September 3 close.

What Retail Investors Should Do Before the Holiday

Retail participants using discount brokers like Zerodha, Groww, and Upstox should place all equity delivery and intraday orders before the 3:30 PM IST close on Thursday, September 3. SIP instalments scheduled through auto-debit will process on the next business day. Mutual fund NAVs are declared on working days, so transactions placed on September 4 will receive the September 7 NAV.

Tax-saving deadline watchers should track the approaching September 30 ALGO deadline for FY26 tax-saving investments. ALGO-eligible ELSS and tax-saver funds remain purchasable through AMC websites on holidays, but unit allotment follows the next business day's NAV. No special dispensation or extended window is granted by SEBI for this Janmashtami holiday.
