India's Dilemma at SCO Summit

In a significant display of unity, the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) leaders, including Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin, recently gathered in Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan. However, India's commitment to the fight against terrorism faced scrutiny, as the summit highlighted divergent stances among member nations.

India's Message on Terrorism

The Bishkek Declaration, issued at the end of the summit, called upon all nations to cooperate in fighting terrorism. However, India's insistence on naming Pakistan-based terrorist groups was notably absent, raising questions about its resolve in the global fight against terrorism.

Modi's Meet with Putin

PM Modi's bilateral meeting with President Putin was notable for its warmth, with both leaders expressing commitment to enhancing bilateral ties. However, India's decision not to join Russia's proposed security architecture for the region, due to its concerns about Russian interference in its internal affairs, remains a contentious issue.

Russia and China's Regional Influence

Russia and China's increased engagement in Central Asia, including through SCO, has raised concerns about their growing regional influence. Their presence at the summit served as a counterbalance to Western influence in the region, further complicating India's strategic calculus.

Key Takeaways

  • India's stated commitment to fighting terrorism faced scrutiny at the SCO summit.
  • PM Modi's meeting with President Putin underscored India's desire to maintain strong bilateral ties, despite differences.
  • Russia and China's increased engagement in Central Asia poses challenges to India's regional aspirations.

The SCO summit highlighted the complex nature of the global fight against terrorism, with nations adopting differing stances and taking varied actions. India's commitment to fighting terrorism, while commendable, faces challenges from its own foreign policy and the divergent views of its SCO partners. The future of India's anti-terrorism efforts will depend on its ability to navigate these complex dynamics and maintain its commitment to the cause.
