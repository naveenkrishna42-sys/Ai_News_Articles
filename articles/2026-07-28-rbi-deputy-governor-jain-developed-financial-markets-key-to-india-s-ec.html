RBI Deputy Governor Jain: Developed financial markets key to India's economic goals

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI Deputy Governor Jain stressed the need for developed financial markets for a developed economy.
  • He emphasised the role of market infrastructure, liquidity, and regulatory frameworks.
  • Jain's remarks come amid India's push to become a $5 trillion economy and attract global capital.

Financial markets as a prerequisite

India cannot become a developed economy without first building developed financial markets, Reserve Bank of India Deputy Governor M. Rajeshwar Rao Jain said on Tuesday. Speaking at a conference in Mumbai, Jain argued that deep and liquid financial markets are not just a byproduct of economic growth but a necessary foundation for it.

His comments come at a time when India is positioning itself as a fast-growing major economy and a destination for global capital. The government has set an ambitious target of becoming a $5 trillion economy.

What makes a market 'developed'

Jain outlined several characteristics of developed financial markets. These include a wide range of instruments, robust trading and settlement infrastructure, and a regulatory environment that balances innovation with stability. He stressed that such markets allow efficient price discovery and risk management.

India's equity markets have made significant strides, but the corporate bond market remains relatively shallow. Jain pointed out that a vibrant bond market is critical for financing long-term infrastructure projects, which in turn drives economic development.

The role of regulation and innovation

The deputy governor highlighted the need for continuous regulatory improvements. He noted that the RBI is working on frameworks to deepen the government securities market and encourage more retail participation. At the same time, he cautioned against excessive caution that could stifle innovation.

Jain also touched on the importance of financial inclusion. Developed markets must serve not just large corporations but also small businesses and individuals. Digital public infrastructure like UPI has helped, but more work is needed in areas like insurance and pension products.

Global context and India's path

India's financial markets are more advanced than those of many other emerging economies, but they still lag behind developed nations. Jain said India must learn from global best practices while tailoring solutions to its own context. He warned against trying to skip stages of market development.

The RBI has been gradually liberalising capital account rules and allowing more foreign investment in debt markets. Jain indicated that this process would continue, but only when domestic markets are deep enough to handle potential volatility.

What to watch: The RBI is expected to release a discussion paper on deepening the corporate bond market later this year. Market participants will be watching for concrete steps, such as easing listing norms or introducing new hedging instruments.
