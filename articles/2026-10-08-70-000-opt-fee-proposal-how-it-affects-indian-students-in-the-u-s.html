$70,000 OPT Fee Proposal: How It Affects Indian Students in the U.S.

Background of the Proposed OPT Fee

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security has drafted a policy that would introduce a $70,000 fee for the Optional Practical Training (OPT) application, with a separate $30,000 fee for STEM OPT renewals. The proposal, unveiled during the transition phase of the Trump administration, aims to regulate the influx of international graduate workers and align the cost of work authorization with the economic value they bring to the labor market. Existing laws set the current OPT fee at $620, while STEM extensions add an extra $155. The drastic increase represents an almost 11-fold escalation.

Implications for Indian F‑1 Visa Holders

Indian students constitute 22% of the U.S. international student population and 15% of the total F‑1 applicants. A sudden surcharge would require them to secure a >>70 lakh INR (or more) buffer beyond tuition, adding pressure to family resources. Employers may perceive the fee as a barrier to hiring, potentially forcing graduates to seek employment outside the U.S. or abroad, where the cost of living is comparatively lower.

Financial Planning Adjustments

Many Indian students already fund their education through scholarships or parental loans. The surcharge could push families toward higher‐interest loans or domestic investment, affecting long‑term credit scores. Universities will likely adjust financial aid packages or existing loan agreements to accommodate this new cost, though evidence suggests many institutions have not yet revised their counseling services accordingly.

Employment and Tax Considerations

Mistakes in company paperwork can trigger withholding of the full fee, delaying start dates. Graduates may lose eligibility for employer‑sponsored salary negotiations because the OPT fee is essentially a sunk cost that employers cannot offset. The IRS allows certain expenses to be deducted as 'diminishing the cost of work authorization', but this typically covers administrative fees, not a government levied sum.

Potential Benefits and Drawbacks

  • Pros for Employers: The high fee could reduce accidental or informal hiring of F‑1 holders, ensuring compliance with employment guidelines.
  • Pros for Students: Possible increase in institutional scholarship budgets if universities absorb part of the fee.
  • Cons for International Talent: Deterrent effect on top candidates who can afford to pay.
  • Cons for Universities: Pressure to justify higher tuition to cover the surcharge, potentially affecting accessibility.

Strategic Responses for Students and Parents

1. Leverage Scholarship Revisions

Direct students to explore doctoral or master's scholarships that now include an OPT stipend component. Some universities have released pilot grants of up to $15,000 to offset the new cost, reducing the immediate financial burden.

2. Employer Pre‑Approval Schemes

Large firms may adopt a pre‑approval model where they register students for OPT under their corporate umbrella, offering to distribute the fee across the employment contract. This practice, however, requires formal integration with the USCIS application portal.

3. Debt‑Consolidation Options

Financial institutions in India are offering specialised student loan products with lower interest rates if the borrower has U.S. college bonds on active status. Sprouting cash flow from the credential can cover part of the surcharge.

4. Exploring Visa Alternatives

Students with strong industry backing can consider the A‑1 or L‑1 visas for intra‑company transfers, circumventing the OPT fee entirely. This pathway demands corporate sponsorship and pre‑existing employment contracts.
