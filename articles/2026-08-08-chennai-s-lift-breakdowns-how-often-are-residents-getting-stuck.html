Chennai’s Lift Breakdowns: How Often Are Residents Getting Stuck?

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A recent survey highlights the frequency of elevator breakdowns in Chennai apartments.
  • Residents report being trapped in lifts, raising safety and maintenance concerns.
  • Experts call for stricter enforcement of elevator safety norms and regular checks.

Frequent Breakdowns Alarm Residents

Chennai’s love for high-rise living is colliding with a frustrating reality: malfunctioning lifts. A fresh survey has thrown light on just how often residents find themselves trapped between floors, with many reporting incidents at least once a month.

The findings are a wake-up call for apartment associations and builders, who are now under pressure to address what many call a neglected aspect of urban living.

The Survey Speaks

While exact figures are yet to be officially released, the survey indicates a significant number of respondents have experienced lift failures in the past year. Some residents told The Hindu that breakdowns are not rare, describing instances where they were stuck for over thirty minutes.

One resident, who requested anonymity, said, “It happened twice last month. You feel helpless, especially when you have elderly family members at home.” The problem is particularly acute in older buildings, where maintenance is often deferred.

Why Are Lifts Failing?

Experts point to a mix of factors: lack of regular servicing, aging equipment, and poor-quality installation. Many residential complexes in Chennai do not have annual maintenance contracts, leaving repairs to be done only after a breakdown occurs.

Additionally, rapid urbanisation has led to taller towers, putting greater strain on elevator systems. Safety norms exist, but enforcement is often lax, a point that residents and activists have repeatedly raised.

Safety Concerns on the Rise

Being stuck in a lift is more than an inconvenience; it is a safety hazard. Cases of medical emergencies inside stuck elevators have been reported in the past, though no recent incident has been confirmed in the survey.

Residents are now demanding that the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA) and local corporations step in. They want mandatory periodic inspections and stricter penalties for non-compliance.

What Can Residents Do?

Apartment associations are being advised to document all breakdowns and report them to the authorities. Experts also recommend that buildings conduct emergency drills, so residents know how to use alarm systems and communicate with security.

Some complexes have begun installing backup power systems and better intercoms, but these remain exceptions rather than the rule. The cost of modernising lifts, which can run into several lakh rupees, is a deterrent for many associations.

Call for Stronger Regulation

The survey adds to a growing chorus of voices demanding a dedicated elevator safety law in India. While some states have introduced regulations, Tamil Nadu is yet to have a comprehensive framework.

Officials have not yet commented on the survey or on any proposed changes. However, residents hope that the data will spur action, especially as Chennai’s skyline continues to grow.

For now, the advice remains simple: report every breakdown, keep emergency numbers handy, and do not hesitate to raise the issue at your next association meeting.

As the city waits for official response, the question is whether lift makers and builders will voluntarily improve standards or wait for a tragedy to force change.
