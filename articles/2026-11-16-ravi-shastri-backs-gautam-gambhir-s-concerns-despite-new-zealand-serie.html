Ravi Shastri Backs Gautam Gambhir's Concerns Despite New Zealand Series Win

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-16 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shastri endorsed Gambhir's public concerns even after India sealed the series
  • Matt Henry's six-wicket haul exposed India's fragile batting in helpful conditions
  • Ishant-style pace stocks, middle-order continuity and overseas bench depth are the real gaps
  • The 2027 ODI World Cup cycle makes these fixes urgent, not optional

Why Shastri's backing matters

Former India head coach and current mentoring set-up figure Ravi Shastri has publicly backed Gautam Gambhir's concerns over India's batting, even though the team returned from New Zealand with a series win. The endorsement matters because Shastri rarely critiques a coaching setup openly, and Gambhir had flagged the fragility after wickets fell in clusters against swing and seam.

The result, as reports from The Times of India and others noted, masked a recurring problem: India chased down targets in patches but the top order kept folding under sustained pressure.

What New Zealand exposed

The top-order problem

India's batting failures in New Zealand, as Gambhir himself flagged, centred on the inability to convert starts. Matt Henry's six-for in the decisive game silenced the innings and left the middle order exposed to the second new ball, a pattern that has repeated across tours of England, Australia and now the subcontinental side's tour of New Zealand.

Pitch behaviour and planning

Shreyas Iyer's point, reported by The Hindu, was that the nature of the surface changed from belter to seamer-friendly mid-match. Will Young's comments on New Zealand's plan to level the series, carried by Rediff, underline that opponents now read Indian batting selections better than India reads them.

The buyer's-eye view: what selectors must fix

Think of a squad like a purchase decision. A series win is the sticker price; the underlying build quality is the cost of ownership. India's win rate overseas is strong, but the margin for error is shrinking as pace attacks worldwide get faster and more skilful.

  • Pros: Series won, bowling attack held its nerve, Shastri publicly backing coach signals internal alignment.
  • Cons: Batting collapses, thin overseas pace bench, no settled No. 4, reliance on conditions turning favourable.

What happens next

Gambhir's concerns, endorsed by Shastri, should translate into concrete calls: a settled middle order, a third seamer who can bowl long spells, and clearer overseas selection criteria. With the 2027 ODI World Cup cycle underway, these are urgent fixes, not talking points.
