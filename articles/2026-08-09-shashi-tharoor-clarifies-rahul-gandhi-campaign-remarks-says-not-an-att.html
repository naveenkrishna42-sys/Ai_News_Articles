Shashi Tharoor clarifies Rahul Gandhi campaign remarks, says not an attack on Congress

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shashi Tharoor clarified his comments on Rahul Gandhi's youth outreach campaign were misrepresented.
  • He said the remarks were not an attack on the Congress party or its leadership.
  • Tharoor also commented on CJP protests, noting they didn't resonate as much with students.
  • He called the protests a 'rude wake-up call', saying Gen Z will be largely influential.

Tharoor sets the record straight

Senior Congress leader Shashi Tharoor has moved to clarify his recent comments on Rahul Gandhi's youth outreach initiative, 'Chhatron ki goonj', insisting that his remarks were not aimed at the party. Speaking to media, Tharoor said his statements had been "distorted" and taken out of context, creating a false impression of internal discord.

The clarification comes after a flurry of reports suggested that Tharoor had criticised the campaign's impact among students. The Kerala MP, known for his articulate and often candid assessments, found himself at the centre of a controversy that threatened to overshadow the party's messaging.

What Tharoor actually said

At an event, Tharoor had drawn a comparison between the Congress's student outreach and the protests by the Conference of Journalists and Publishers (CJP). He noted that one of the two initiatives did not "resonate as much" with students. The remark was widely interpreted as a veiled critique of Gandhi's campaign, sparking speculation about rifts within the party.

Tharoor, however, rejected this interpretation outright. "It is being distorted," he said, adding that his observation was a factual statement about student engagement, not a political attack. He stressed that he remains fully supportive of the Congress leadership and its efforts to connect with younger voters.

Media misrepresentation or honest critique?

The controversy highlights the delicate balance Congress leaders must strike when commenting on party matters. Tharoor's history of outspokenness has often put him at odds with the party's official line, but he has consistently denied any intent to undermine the organisation. In this case, he blamed the media for amplifying a minor comment into a major story.

Political observers note that Tharoor's remarks, even if taken at face value, reflect a genuine concern about the party's ability to mobilise students. The CJP protests, which drew significant attention, were seen as a moment of resurgence for opposition politics. Tharoor's comparison, though impolitic, touched on a real question: why did the Congress's own campaign fail to match that energy?

A rude wake-up call

Tharoor did not shy away from addressing the broader implications of the student demonstrations. Describing the CJP protests as a "rude wake-up call", he acknowledged that the youth are increasingly assertive and discerning. "Gen Z will be largely influential," he said, suggesting that political parties must adapt to their expectations or risk becoming irrelevant.

His comments come at a time when the Congress is trying to rebuild its base among young voters, a demographic that has shown signs of drifting towards other alternatives. The party has invested heavily in outreach programmes, including Gandhi's campus tours and interactive sessions, but the returns have been mixed.

Tharoor's clarification may not completely quell the speculation, but it does underscore a simmering tension within the Congress — between those who favour a more vocal, critical approach and those who prefer strict party discipline. Whether this episode will have any lasting impact on the party's internal dynamics remains to be seen.

What happens next

All eyes are now on the Congress's upcoming events and whether the leadership will address the underlying concerns Tharoor raised. For now, the party appears keen to move past the controversy, with Tharoor himself signalling that his focus remains on strengthening the organisation. The episode serves as a reminder that in Indian politics, even a stray remark can ignite a storm — and clarity often comes only after the damage is done.
