Gen Z protesters plan march to Indian Parliament amid security clampdown

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gen Z protesters, part of the 'Chalo Sansad' march, are demanding action on unemployment and education reforms.
  • Delhi Police have imposed Section 144 and tightened security ahead of the Parliament session.
  • The CJP has issued guidelines for the march, urging participants to carry only the national flag.

Security has been tightened across Delhi as thousands of young protesters, led by the Coalition for Justice and Progress (CJP), prepare for a march to Parliament. The group, largely made up of Gen Z activists, has called the demonstration 'Chalo Sansad' in a bid to pressure lawmakers on key issues.

The protest comes amid a fast by activist Wangchuk, who remains hospitalised after a prolonged hunger strike. His condition has added urgency to the demands, which include sweeping changes to the education system and job creation measures.

Why are Gen Z protesters marching to Parliament?

The CJP has framed the march as a direct appeal to elected representatives. Organisers say they want Parliament to debate and pass legislation addressing unemployment, which has hit the youth hardest. Official data shows joblessness among those aged 18-25 has crossed 18% in several states.

Education reform is another core demand. Protesters are calling for a rollback of recent changes to university funding and entrance exam policies, which they argue favour the wealthy. The CJP has described the current system as 'elitist and exclusionary'.

CJP releases guidelines for the march

The CJP has issued a clear set of dos and don'ts for participants. In a statement, the group said: 'Carry the Tiranga, avoid political flags.' The directive is meant to keep the protest focused on national issues rather than party politics.

Organisers have also urged marchers to remain peaceful and to cooperate with police. 'We have no intention of breaking the law. Our fight is with ideas, not with the security forces,' a CJP spokesperson said on Sunday.

Security clampdown and prohibitory orders

Delhi Police have imposed Section 144 of the Criminal Procedure Code in areas around Parliament, banning the assembly of five or more people. Authorities have cited the possibility of unrest and have not granted permission for the march.

Police barricades have been set up at key intersections, and extra personnel have been deployed. 'No permission has been given for any march or rally in central Delhi,' a senior police officer told reporters. The prohibitory orders will remain in place until the end of the Parliament session.

The CJP has alleged a crackdown on peaceful dissent. 'This is a democratic right. We will not be intimidated,' the group said in a social media post. Lawyers for the CJP are expected to challenge the prohibitory orders in court on Monday.

What happens next?

The march is scheduled to begin on Tuesday morning from Jantar Mantar. The CJP has said it will proceed regardless of the ban. All eyes will be on how police respond if protesters attempt to break the cordon.

Wangchuk's health remains a flashpoint. Doctors have said his condition is stable but serious. The outcome of the march and the government's response could determine whether the protest movement gains further momentum or fades.
