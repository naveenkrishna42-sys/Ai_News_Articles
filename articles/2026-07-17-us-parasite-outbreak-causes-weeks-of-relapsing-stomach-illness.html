US Parasite Outbreak Causes Weeks of Relapsing Stomach Illness

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • A US outbreak of the Cyclospora parasite is causing weeks of relapsing stomach illness.
  • Symptoms include severe diarrhea, nausea, and fatigue that can return after initial recovery.
  • Scientists suspect the outbreak is linked to contaminated fresh produce, likely imported.
  • Health officials are urging thorough washing of fruits and vegetables.
  • The illness can last for weeks if untreated and may require specific antibiotics.

A parasite outbreak in the United States is leaving hundreds of people with weeks of relapsing stomach illness, and scientists say contaminated produce is the likely culprit. The culprit is a single-celled parasite called Cyclospora cayetanensis.

Health officials across multiple states are now tracking the cases. The illness is known for its hallmark symptom: watery diarrhea that can go away, then return days later.

What is Cyclospora and How Does It Spread?

Cyclospora is a microscopic parasite that infects the small intestine. It is not spread directly from person to person. Instead, it is usually ingested through contaminated food or water.

In the US, outbreaks are almost always linked to fresh produce. Imported fruits and vegetables—such as raspberries, basil, and lettuce—have been implicated in past flare-ups. The current investigation is focusing on similar sources.

The parasite can survive on produce for weeks. Once ingested, it can take about a week for symptoms to appear. This delay makes it difficult to trace the exact source of contamination.

Relapsing Symptoms Make Diagnosis Tricky

Patients report suffering from explosive, watery diarrhea that can last for days. This is often accompanied by severe stomach cramps, nausea, vomiting, and fatigue.

The relapsing nature of the illness is what sets it apart. Many people feel better after a few days, only to have symptoms return violently a week later. This cycle can continue for several weeks if untreated.

Doctors warn that the infection can lead to dehydration and weight loss in severe cases. People with weakened immune systems are at higher risk for prolonged illness.

How It Is Treated and Prevented

Unlike a bacterial infection, Cyclospora does not respond to common antibiotics. The standard treatment is a specific combination of drugs, usually trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX), which is an oral antibiotic.

Without treatment, the infection can last from a few weeks to over a month. Even with treatment, symptoms may take several days to clear. Health officials stress that anyone with prolonged watery diarrhea should see a doctor.

Prevention hinges on careful food handling. Washing all fresh fruits and vegetables thoroughly under running water is recommended. However, washing alone may not remove all parasites if they are deeply embedded in the produce.

Cooking food kills the parasite. But for raw items like salads and berries, the only advice is to know the source. Health authorities are advising consumers to stay informed about recall alerts.

What to Watch For

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) is coordinating the multi-state investigation. They have not yet confirmed a single supplier or food item. Updates are expected in the coming days.

For now, the advice is simple: if you experience watery diarrhea that goes away and comes back, especially after eating fresh produce, seek medical help. Early diagnosis can prevent weeks of misery.
