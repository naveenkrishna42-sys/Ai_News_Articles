Typhoon Dolphin grounds nearly 950 Shanghai flights, soaks east China

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nearly 950 flights grounded at Shanghai airports due to Typhoon Dolphin
  • More than one million people evacuated across eastern China
  • Red alert issued as storm makes landfall in Zhejiang province

Storm slams eastern coast

Typhoon Dolphin has made landfall in Zhejiang province on China's eastern coast, bringing fierce winds and record rainfall that has paralysed parts of the region. The storm, which arrived on Monday, has forced the evacuation of more than one million people as authorities scramble to limit damage.

Shanghai, the country's financial hub, has been hit especially hard. Nearly 950 flights were grounded at the city's two main airports, stranding thousands of travellers during the peak summer travel season.

Red alert in effect

China's meteorological authority has issued a red alert, the highest level in its four-tier warning system, as Dolphin churns through the densely populated coastal belt. The storm has dumped record rainfall on Shanghai, with streets turning into rivers and subway stations reporting waterlogging in several low-lying areas.

Local officials have shut schools, suspended rail services and halted ferry operations across the affected provinces. Emergency crews are on standby, but the scale of the evacuation—over a million people—underscores how seriously authorities are treating this storm.

Flight disruptions ripple outward

The grounding of nearly 950 flights in Shanghai has had a knock-on effect on air travel across the region. Airlines have cancelled or delayed connections to and from nearby hubs, and passengers are being advised to check with carriers before heading to airports.

For many, the disruption is a familiar ordeal. China's eastern seaboard is no stranger to typhoons, but the intensity of Dolphin has caught even seasoned residents off guard. Social media posts show submerged cars and people wading through knee-deep water in parts of the city.

Evacuations and shelter

More than a million people have been moved to safer ground, many into temporary shelters set up in schools and community centres. Fishermen have been pulled off boats, and construction sites have been secured as the storm's outer bands continue to lash the coast.

Officials have not yet confirmed the full extent of damage, but early reports suggest crop losses in Zhejiang's agricultural heartland could be significant. The province, a major producer of tea and rice, is bearing the brunt of Dolphin's landfall.

The storm is expected to weaken as it moves inland, but forecasters warn that heavy rain could persist for another day or two, raising the risk of flooding in rivers already swollen by summer monsoons.

What to watch

Attention now turns to the recovery effort. As the storm moves inland, authorities will focus on restoring power, clearing debris and assessing the toll on homes and infrastructure. The full scale of the damage may not be known for days, but the priority is clear: keeping people safe as the waters recede.
