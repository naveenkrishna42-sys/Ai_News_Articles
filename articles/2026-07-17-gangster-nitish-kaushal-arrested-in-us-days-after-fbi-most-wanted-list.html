Gangster Nitish Kaushal arrested in US days after FBI most wanted listing

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nitish Kaushal was arrested in the US shortly after the FBI added him to its most wanted list.
  • He is linked to the Bishnoi gang and the Jaggu Bhagwanpuria global crime syndicate.
  • The arrest follows a multi-agency effort by US and Indian law enforcement.

Fugitive nabbed in swift operation

Indian-origin gangster Nitish Kaushal, known by the alias 'Lala', has been arrested in the United States. The capture comes just days after the Federal Bureau of Investigation placed him on its most wanted list.

Kaushal was wanted for his alleged involvement in organised crime networks operating across multiple countries. US law enforcement agencies, acting on a tip-off, tracked him down and took him into custody without incident.

Officials have not yet disclosed the exact location of the arrest or the charges he will face in court.

From Bishnoi associate to FBI target

Kaushal's criminal profile grew significantly in recent years. He was identified as a key operative linked to the Bishnoi gang, which has a vast footprint in India and abroad.

Intelligence reports also connect him to the syndicate run by Jaggu Bhagwanpuria, a notorious figure whose network spans North America, Europe and Southeast Asia. The FBI had flagged Kaushal for his role in cross-border extortion, contract killings and drug trafficking.

His induction into the FBI's most wanted list marked a major escalation. Indian agencies had been sharing intelligence with their US counterparts for months before the warrant was issued.

Multi-agency coordination pays off

The arrest was the result of a coordinated effort between the FBI, the US Marshals Service and Indian law enforcement. Sources indicate that Indian inputs were crucial in narrowing down Kaushal's movements.

Investigators are now examining his digital footprint and financial transactions. They suspect he was using encrypted apps to communicate with gang leaders based in India and Canada.

A senior officer familiar with the case said, "This is a significant blow to the syndicate. Kaushal was a key link between the Bishnoi network and international criminal operations."

What this means for the syndicate

Kaushal's arrest disrupts a critical node in the global supply chain of illicit activities. The Bishnoi gang, once largely confined to northern India, has expanded its reach through associates like Kaushal.

His role allegedly included coordinating logistics for drug shipments and managing extortion rackets targeting Indian businessmen abroad. The FBI had described him as a high-value target.

The development also puts pressure on other fugitives. Agencies on both sides of the Atlantic are now expected to intensify efforts to capture remaining members of the syndicate.

Extradition proceedings are likely to follow. Kaushal could be tried in US federal courts or handed over to Indian authorities, depending on the charges and diplomatic negotiations.

Watch for further announcements from the FBI and India's Ministry of Home Affairs as they piece together the full extent of Kaushal's operations and his network's remaining assets.
