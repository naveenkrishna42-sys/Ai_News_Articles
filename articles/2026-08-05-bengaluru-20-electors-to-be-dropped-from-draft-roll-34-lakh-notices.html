Bengaluru: 20% electors to be dropped from draft roll, 34 lakh notices

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Around 20% of electors in Bengaluru are set to be removed from the draft electoral roll.
  • More than 34 lakh voters will receive notices to confirm their eligibility.
  • The move is part of a special summary revision exercise ahead of upcoming elections.

Bengaluru's electoral roll is set for a major overhaul, with election officials planning to drop approximately 20% of electors from the draft list. The move, part of a special summary revision exercise, will see over 34 lakh people issued notices to confirm their eligibility before their names are struck off.

Why the purge?

The decision follows a comprehensive house-to-house verification drive conducted across the city. Officials found that a significant number of entries were either duplicate, shifted without updating addresses, or belonged to people who have since died.

According to sources, the exercise aims to clean up the electoral database ahead of upcoming polls. The draft roll, once published, will be open for claims and objections for a specified period.

Notices and response window

Over 34 lakh electors will receive show-cause notices, asking them to provide proof of residence and identity. Those who fail to respond within the stipulated deadline may see their names removed from the final list.

Officials have urged citizens to check their names in the draft roll once it is released. They can submit objections online or at designated centres. The process is expected to conclude before the final publication of the electoral roll.

What this means for voters

For many residents, this could mean a sudden loss of voting rights if they do not act promptly. The notices will be delivered via post, SMS, and email, wherever contact details are available.

Election authorities have set up helplines and facilitation counters to assist those who need help understanding the process. They have also clarified that the removal is not permanent — affected persons can apply for inclusion in the next revision cycle.

Context and concerns

The move comes amid broader debates over electoral roll accuracy and inclusion. While officials maintain that the exercise is routine, critics have expressed concern that eligible voters, especially migrants and those in rented homes, might be disproportionately affected.

In past revisions, many such individuals have been dropped due to address mismatches or lack of documentation. The current drive is being watched closely by political parties, who fear that a large-scale deletion could alter the voter composition in key constituencies.

How to check your status

Citizens can check their names on the National Voters' Service Portal (NVSP) or by calling the district election office. The draft roll will also be available at polling stations and local revenue offices for public inspection.

Officials have advised people to keep their voter ID cards and address proof ready while responding to notices.

The final electoral roll is expected to be published after the claims and objections period closes. All eyes are now on how many of the 34 lakh notice recipients will come forward — and how many will be silently struck off.
