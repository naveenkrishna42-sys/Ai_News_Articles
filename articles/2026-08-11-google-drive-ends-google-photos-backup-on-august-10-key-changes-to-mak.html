Google Drive ends Google Photos backup on August 10: Key changes to make

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google Drive will no longer back up Google Photos automatically from August 10.
  • Existing backups remain unaffected, but new photos and videos won't sync to Drive.
  • Users need to adjust settings in Google Photos and Drive to manage storage and backups.

What changes on August 10

Google has confirmed that starting August 10, Google Drive will no longer automatically back up photos and videos from Google Photos. This marks the end of a long-standing integration that allowed users to store their entire photo library in Drive's cloud storage.

The change means any new photos or videos you capture or add to Google Photos after this date will not sync to Google Drive. Your existing files and backups will remain intact, but the automatic pipeline between the two services is being switched off.

Google has not provided a detailed explanation for the move, but it appears to be part of a broader effort to streamline its storage offerings and reduce confusion around duplicate files across services.

Why this matters for users

For many users, especially those who have relied on Google Drive as a secondary backup for their photos, this change could disrupt their workflow. If you are among those who have been using Drive to keep a copy of your Google Photos library, you will need to find an alternative method going forward.

The timing is also significant because Google's storage policies have been tightening. With the end of free unlimited storage for high-quality photos in 2021, many users are already paying for Google One plans. This new change could push some to reconsider how they manage their cloud storage.

For those who use Google Drive for desktop, the backup feature for folders will remain available. However, it will no longer include the automatic photo backup from Google Photos. Users will need to manually export or move their photos if they want them in Drive.

What settings to change before the deadline

To avoid any disruption, users should take a few proactive steps before August 10. First, check your Google Photos settings to see if the backup to Drive is currently enabled. If it is, you can decide whether to rely on Google Photos' own backup or switch to a different service.

Second, if you want to keep a copy of your photos in Drive, you can manually download your Google Photos library and upload it to Drive. This is a one-time process, but it will ensure your photos are stored in both places.

Third, consider enabling the folder backup feature in Google Drive for desktop, which will allow you to back up specific folders on your computer, including those where you store your photo files. This is a viable alternative for users who want to maintain a backup without depending on the automatic sync.

What about existing backups?

Existing backups in Google Drive that contain photos from Google Photos will remain unchanged. You will still be able to access, download, or delete those files as before. The change only affects new additions to Google Photos after the deadline.

Google has not announced any plans to delete existing backups, so users do not need to worry about losing their current data. However, it is always a good practice to maintain your own offline copies, especially for irreplaceable memories.

What to watch next

As the August 10 deadline approaches, Google may update its help pages or notify users via email. It is advisable to keep an eye on official announcements for any last-minute changes or clarifications.

For now, the key is to take control of your photo backup strategy. Whether you choose to rely on Google Photos' standalone backup, switch to a third-party service, or manually manage your files, the important thing is to ensure your photos are safe and accessible.
