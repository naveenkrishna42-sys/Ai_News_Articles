Amid Cauvery dispute, Chennai gets 1 tmcft Krishna water from Andhra

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chennai receives 1 tmcft of Krishna water from Andhra Pradesh under an agreement
  • The supply comes amid heightened tensions over the Cauvery water-sharing dispute
  • Officials say the water will augment Chennai's drinking water storage
  • The transfer is part of an existing inter-state river linking arrangement

Chennai has received one thousand million cubic feet (tmcft) of Krishna river water from Andhra Pradesh, even as the Cauvery water-sharing dispute continues to simmer between Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. The supply, confirmed by state officials on Wednesday, is part of an existing inter-state agreement that allows the transfer of Krishna water to the city for drinking purposes.

Water reaches reservoirs

The water has been released from Andhra Pradesh's Krishna basin and has reached Chennai's major storage reservoirs, including the Poondi reservoir, which is the primary catchment for the city's water supply. Officials from the Chennai Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board (Metro Water) said the inflow will help augment the city's depleting storage levels.

"This is a welcome addition, especially when the city is facing a shortfall due to weak monsoon and the ongoing Cauvery row," a senior Metro Water official said, requesting anonymity. The city's reservoirs had been under stress, with water levels dipping in recent weeks.

Existing pact, not a one-off

The transfer is not a new arrangement but a continuation of a decades-old inter-state river linking project. Under the Krishna Water Supply Project, Andhra Pradesh supplies water to Chennai through a series of canals and pipelines, with the quantum and schedule fixed by a tribunal-appointed committee. This year's release comes at a time when both states are locked in a fresh confrontation over Cauvery water sharing, with Tamil Nadu demanding its due share and Karnataka citing a deficit in its reservoirs.

The timing of the release has raised eyebrows in some quarters, as it comes just days after the Cauvery Supervisory Committee asked Karnataka to release water to Tamil Nadu. However, officials clarified that the Krishna water supply is independent of the Cauvery dispute and follows its own legal framework.

Impact on Chennai's water situation

Chennai's daily water demand is around 830 million litres, and the city has been relying on a mix of surface water, groundwater, and desalination plants. The one tmcft of Krishna water—roughly equivalent to 1,000 million cubic feet—will add significantly to the city's storage, but officials say it is not a permanent solution.

"The city needs sustained inflows, not just occasional releases," said an expert on water management, who did not wish to be named. The monsoon has been erratic, and Chennai's four main reservoirs—Poondi, Cholavaram, Red Hills, and Chembarambakkam—have a combined capacity of about 11 tmcft. Current storage is well below normal for this time of year.

Political and legal overtones

The Cauvery row has been a recurring flashpoint between Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, with protests and legal battles playing out in the Supreme Court and the Cauvery Water Management Authority. The latest round began last month when Tamil Nadu sought an urgent release of water, citing a distress in the delta region. Karnataka has countered, arguing that its own reservoirs are insufficient.

The Krishna water supply from Andhra Pradesh is unlikely to ease the political pressure on the Cauvery issue, but it does provide some practical relief to Chennai's residents. The city has faced severe water crises in the past, most notably in 2019 when the reservoirs ran dry, and this year's situation, while less acute, remains a concern.

What to watch

Officials say more releases are possible if the southwest monsoon picks up in the Krishna basin. The Cauvery Water Management Authority is expected to meet again this month to review Karnataka's compliance. For now, Chennai will take what it can get—one tmcft at a time.
