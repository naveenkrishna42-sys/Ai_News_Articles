Sarvam AI unveils Indic DiarBench for speech recognition in 22 Indian languages

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Indic DiarBench is a joint diarization-ASR benchmark dataset for 22 Indian languages
  • Dataset allocates 2 hours of audio per language for testing
  • Developed by Sarvam AI in collaboration with AI4Bharat
  • Aims to improve speech recognition and speaker attribution in Indian languages

New benchmark targets Indian language speech tech

BENGALURU: Sarvam AI, in collaboration with AI4Bharat, has launched Indic DiarBench, a benchmark dataset designed to test speech recognition and speaker attribution across 22 Indian languages. The dataset allocates two hours of audio per language, providing a standardised evaluation tool for developers and researchers working on speech technologies for the Indian market.

The announcement comes at a time when voice-based interfaces are gaining traction in India, but most speech models are trained primarily on English or a handful of major regional languages. Indic DiarBench aims to fill that gap by covering the full spectrum of scheduled languages, from Hindi and Bengali to lesser-resourced ones like Bodo and Dogri.

What the dataset covers

Indic DiarBench is described as a joint diarization-ASR benchmark. Diarization is the task of identifying who spoke when in an audio recording, while ASR, or automatic speech recognition, converts speech to text. Combining the two allows for a more realistic evaluation of systems that need to handle multi-speaker conversations, such as meeting transcriptions, call centre analytics, and voice assistants.

Each language in the dataset has two hours of labelled audio. That may seem modest compared to English datasets that run into thousands of hours, but for many Indian languages, even this amount of carefully annotated data is a significant step forward.

Why this matters for Indian developers

For Indian startups and researchers, the lack of standardised benchmarks has been a persistent bottleneck. Teams building speech applications often have to create their own test sets, which makes it difficult to compare performance across different models and approaches. Indic DiarBench offers a common yardstick, allowing developers to see how their systems fare on a consistent set of tasks.

The collaboration between Sarvam AI, a Bengaluru-based AI startup focused on Indic language models, and AI4Bharat, a research platform at IIT Madras, brings together industry and academic expertise. Both have been active in the Indic language AI space, with AI4Bharat releasing several open-source datasets and models in the past.

Challenges remain in low-resource languages

While the launch of Indic DiarBench is a positive development, the two-hour allocation per language may not be sufficient for some of the more complex dialects and accents. Officials have not yet clarified whether the dataset will be expanded in the future, nor have they detailed the exact composition of the audio samples.

There are also practical questions about how well the benchmark reflects real-world conditions, where background noise, overlapping speech, and varied recording quality can trip up even the most robust systems. The benchmark appears to be designed with these challenges in mind, but its utility will depend on how closely it matches deployment scenarios.

What lies ahead

Indic DiarBench is now available for researchers and developers to download and test. The next steps will likely involve community feedback, possible expansions to more hours per language, and integration with existing evaluation frameworks. As voice-based technologies become more common in India, benchmarks like this could play a crucial role in ensuring that speech systems work equally well across all 22 official languages, not just the most widely spoken ones.
