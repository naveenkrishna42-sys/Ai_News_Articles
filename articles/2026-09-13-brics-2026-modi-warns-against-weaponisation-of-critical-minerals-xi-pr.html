BRICS 2026: Modi's Warning Against Weaponisation of Critical Minerals

At the BRICS Summit in New Delhi, Prime Minister Narendra Modi issued a stark warning against the "weaponisation" of critical minerals and technology, with Chinese President Xi Jinping by his side. The New Delhi Declaration 2026, the longest in recent years, underscores a collective push for resilient supply chains and strategic autonomy. For Indian buyers, this signals a potential shift in pricing and availability of critical minerals essential for EVs, renewables, and defence.

The declaration, while addressing the Iran war, notably avoids naming the US, Israel, or Iran—a diplomatic balancing act that highlights BRICS' consensus-driven approach. Foreign journalists have hailed it as a breakthrough, but what does it mean for India's tech and manufacturing sectors?

Why Critical Minerals Are the New Geopolitical Weapon

Critical minerals like lithium, cobalt, and rare earths are the backbone of modern technology. China controls over 60% of rare earth mining and 85% of processing, giving it immense leverage. Modi's warning reflects India's vulnerability: the country imports 100% of its lithium and cobalt needs. Weaponisation could disrupt supply chains, spike prices, and stall India's EV ambitions.

India's response includes the National Critical Mineral Mission, targeting 500,000 tonnes of domestic processing capacity by 2030. The government has also acquired mineral assets in Argentina and Australia. For buyers, this means gradual price stabilisation, but short-term volatility persists as global demand surges.

New Delhi Declaration 2026: Key Takeaways

The declaration emphasises cooperation on critical minerals, technology transfer, and supply chain resilience. It calls for a BRICS-led framework to avoid export restrictions and promote fair trade. Notably, it avoids direct criticism of any nation, preserving unity.

For India, the declaration bolsters its stance on strategic autonomy. It also opens doors for joint ventures in mineral processing, potentially reducing costs for Indian manufacturers. However, implementation remains a challenge, as BRICS nations have divergent interests.

Pros and Cons for Indian Buyers

  • Pros: Diversified supply chains could reduce prices of EVs and electronics by 10-15% by 2028; domestic processing creates jobs; strategic autonomy reduces vulnerability to geopolitical shocks.
  • Cons: Short-term price volatility as markets adjust; implementation hurdles due to BRICS internal differences; China's dominance may persist, limiting immediate impact.
  • Key Insight: India's push for self-reliance in critical minerals is a long-term play. Buyers should expect gradual improvements, not overnight changes. The declaration's consensus on Iran without naming nations shows BRICS' diplomatic maturity, but action lags rhetoric.

Pricing Context and Value Justification

Lithium carbonate prices have swung from ₹60,000 per tonne in 2020 to over ₹5 lakh per tonne in 2022, before stabilising around ₹2.5 lakh per tonne in 2026. Cobalt prices hover at ₹35 lakh per tonne. If BRICS cooperation materialises, Indian buyers could see a 10-15% reduction in battery costs by 2028, translating to savings of ₹15,000-20,000 on an average electric car.

However, without concrete agreements, prices remain susceptible to Chinese export policies. India's bilateral deals and domestic exploration are critical. The government's ₹16,300 crore PLI scheme for advanced chemistry cell batteries aims to localise production, but raw material dependency persists.

Strategic Implications for India

Modi's warning is a diplomatic masterstroke, positioning India as a leader in equitable resource access. By aligning with BRICS, India balances its Quad commitments. For buyers, this means increased government focus on securing minerals, potentially leading to stable pricing and improved availability.

Yet, challenges remain: China's stranglehold, environmental concerns in mining, and technology gaps. India must accelerate R&D in recycling and substitute materials. The New Delhi Declaration is a step forward, but its success hinges on actionable follow-through.

In essence, the BRICS Summit 2026 marks a pivotal moment. For Indian consumers and industries, the message is clear: diversification is coming, but patience is required. The weaponisation threat is real, and India's response will shape its economic future.
