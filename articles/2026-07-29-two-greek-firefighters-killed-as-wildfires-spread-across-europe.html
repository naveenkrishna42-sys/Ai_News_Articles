Two Greek Firefighters Killed as Wildfires Spread Across Europe

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two Greek firefighters killed while tackling wildfires in eastern Europe
  • Thousands in Spain allowed to return home as fires stabilize
  • New heatwave warning issued across southern Europe
  • France and Spain see containment but risks remain high

Firefighters Killed in Eastern Europe

Two Greek firefighters have been killed while battling wildfires that are now spreading into eastern Europe, authorities confirmed on Wednesday. The deaths occurred as multiple blazes raged across the region, fueled by extreme heat and dry conditions.

The victims were part of a crew responding to a fast-moving fire near the border with Turkey. Officials have not yet released their names pending family notification. The Greek government expressed condolences but warned that the fire season is far from over.

Thousands Return Home in Spain

In Spain, thousands of evacuees began returning to their homes as several major wildfires showed signs of stabilization. Emergency services said cooler overnight temperatures and lighter winds helped slow the spread of flames in the western provinces.

However, authorities cautioned that the reprieve may be short-lived. A new heatwave is forecast to push temperatures above 40 degrees Celsius in parts of the country later this week, raising fears of fresh outbreaks. Local officials urged residents to remain vigilant and prepare evacuation plans.

France and Spain on Edge

France also reported progress in containing its fires, particularly in the southwestern Gironde region where thousands of hectares have been scorched. Firefighters have managed to prevent the flames from reaching populated areas, but the situation remains tense.

In both countries, the risk of new fires remains high. Meteorologists warn that the heatwave expected over the weekend could undo recent gains. Emergency crews are pre-positioning resources in vulnerable zones, including forested areas near tourist hotspots.

European Heatwave and Climate Concerns

The current crisis is part of a broader pattern of extreme weather across Europe this summer. Scientists have linked the intensity and frequency of these wildfires to climate change, which is making heatwaves more severe and prolonged.

From Portugal to Greece, governments are scrambling to protect lives and property. The European Union has activated its civil protection mechanism, sending firefighting planes and personnel to the worst-hit regions. But experts say the continent must adapt to a future where such disasters become the norm.

What happens next largely depends on the weather. If the forecasted heatwave materializes, containment efforts could be set back significantly. For now, firefighters are bracing for another long week ahead.
