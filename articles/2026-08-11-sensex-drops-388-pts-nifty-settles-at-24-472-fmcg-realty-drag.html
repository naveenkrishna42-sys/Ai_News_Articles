Sensex drops 388 pts, Nifty settles at 24,472; FMCG, realty drag

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 388 points, Nifty settled at 24,472
  • FMCG, realty and cement sectors were major drags
  • Markets closed lower amid mixed global cues

Indian equity benchmarks ended Tuesday's session in the red, with the Sensex slipping 388 points and the Nifty closing at 24,472. The decline was led by losses in FMCG, realty and cement shares, which outweighed gains in select IT and banking counters.

The 30-share BSE Sensex closed at 80,142, down 0.48 per cent from the previous close. The broader NSE Nifty also lost ground, settling 108 points lower at 24,472. Market breadth turned negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on both exchanges.

FMCG stocks under pressure

FMCG majors were the biggest drag on the indices, as investors turned cautious on the sector amid concerns over slowing rural demand and rising input costs. Shares of Hindustan Unilever, Nestle India and Britannia Industries closed with losses, pulling the sector index down by nearly 1.5 per cent.

Traders said the selling was broad-based but concentrated in defensive sectors, suggesting a shift in sentiment. "FMCG stocks had run up sharply in recent weeks, and today's fall is a correction," said a dealer at a domestic brokerage. "The underlying fundamentals haven't changed, but valuations are getting stretched."

Realty and cement shares slide

Realty stocks also witnessed heavy selling, with the BSE Realty index dropping over 2 per cent. DLF, Oberoi Realty and Godrej Properties were among the top losers, as profit-booking intensified after a strong rally in the sector over the past month.

Cement shares followed suit, with UltraTech Cement, Ambuja Cements and Shree Cement all trading lower. Dealers attributed the fall to concerns over rising fuel costs and weak pricing power in the domestic market.

Select IT and banking stocks cushion the fall

Despite the overall weakness, some IT and banking stocks managed to end higher, providing limited support to the indices. Infosys, TCS and HCL Technologies closed with modest gains, while HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank also ended in positive territory.

The rupee, meanwhile, traded in a narrow range against the US dollar, with no major intervention seen from the Reserve Bank of India. Bond yields remained stable, as market participants awaited fresh cues from global central banks.

Global cues and outlook

Global markets were mixed during the day, with Asian peers closing on a subdued note and European indices opening flat. Investors are keeping a close watch on US inflation data due later this week, which could influence the Federal Reserve's rate trajectory.

Domestically, foreign institutional investors have been net sellers in recent sessions, adding to the pressure. Analysts say the market could remain volatile in the near term, with earnings and macroeconomic data driving direction.

Going ahead, traders will monitor the release of US CPI data and any fresh updates on the monsoon session of Parliament. A break below the 24,400 level on the Nifty could trigger further selling, while resistance is seen around 24,600.
