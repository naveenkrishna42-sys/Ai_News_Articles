RBI may hold rates in August as inflation risks linger

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI's Monetary Policy Committee is widely expected to keep the repo rate unchanged in the August review.
  • Persistent inflation risks, especially from food prices, are cited as the main reason for the pause.
  • Analysts expect the central bank to maintain its 'withdrawal of accommodation' stance.

The Reserve Bank of India's rate-setting panel is widely expected to keep the benchmark repo rate unchanged at its August meeting, with inflation risks continuing to cloud the outlook. According to a report by Business Standard, the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) is likely to hold the repo rate at its current level, as price pressures remain a key concern for policymakers.

Inflation remains the key worry

Analysts point out that while retail inflation has moderated from its earlier peaks, the trajectory is still not comfortable enough for the central bank to consider rate cuts. Food prices, in particular, remain vulnerable to weather-related shocks and supply-side disruptions, which could push inflation higher again.

The MPC's primary mandate is to keep consumer price index (CPI) inflation at 4 per cent, within a tolerance band of 2-6 per cent. Recent data has shown inflation hovering above the midpoint, giving the RBI little room to ease policy.

Growth concerns take a back seat

Although economic growth has shown signs of slowing, the central bank is expected to prioritise price stability over supporting growth in the near term. Several economists argue that a premature rate cut could undo the progress made in bringing inflation under control.

Core inflation, which excludes volatile food and fuel prices, has been sticky, indicating that underlying demand pressures persist. This makes the case for a status quo even stronger, according to market participants.

What to watch in the August policy

Besides the rate decision, the MPC's commentary on liquidity and its forward guidance will be closely scrutinised. The central bank has been managing liquidity through variable rate reverse repo operations, and any change in stance could signal the future direction of policy.

Most analysts expect the RBI to maintain its 'withdrawal of accommodation' stance, which it has adopted to ensure that inflation remains aligned with the target. A shift to a neutral stance is seen as unlikely at this juncture.

The August meeting comes at a time when global central banks are also navigating similar challenges, with the US Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank keeping rates elevated to combat inflation. The RBI's decision will be in line with this global trend of cautious monetary policy.

Officials have not yet confirmed any specific date for the policy announcement, but it is typically scheduled in the first week of August.

Market expectations

Bond yields and banking stocks are likely to react to the RBI's decision, with markets already pricing in a pause. The rupee's stability and crude oil prices will also play a role in shaping the central bank's outlook.

For now, the consensus is clear: the RBI will hold rates steady and wait for more clarity on the inflation front before making any move.

Going forward, the focus will shift to the monsoon's progress and its impact on food prices, as well as global commodity price trends. These factors will determine whether the RBI can begin easing policy later this year or if it will need to maintain its hawkish stance for longer.
