FIDC warns NBFCs over RBI's revolving credit ban

📅 2026-09-02 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • FIDC raises concerns over RBI's proposed ban on revolving credit facilities for NBFCs
  • RBI's move could impact working capital and business growth for NBFCs
  • FIDC calls for a balanced approach to address the liquidity crisis without stifling growth

RBI's Proposed Ban on Revolving Credit Facilities

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has proposed a ban on revolving credit facilities for non-banking finance companies (NBFCs), a move that has raised significant concerns among industry stakeholders. The proposed ban, which aims to address the liquidity crisis in the banking sector, could have far-reaching implications for NBFCs, which rely heavily on revolving credit facilities to manage their working capital.

FIDC's Concerns

The Federation of Indian Development Finance Companies (FIDC), a leading industry body representing NBFCs, has flagged its concerns over the RBI's proposed ban. FIDC's President, Mr. Rakesh Sharma, stated that the proposed ban could stifle business growth and impact the working capital of NBFCs. He called for a balanced approach that addresses the liquidity crisis without compromising the growth prospects of NBFCs.

Impact on NBFCs

The proposed ban could have significant implications for NBFCs, which are a crucial part of the Indian financial ecosystem. NBFCs provide a wide range of financial services, including housing finance, corporate finance, and microfinance. A ban on revolving credit facilities could impact their ability to provide these services, leading to a slowdown in business growth and job creation.

RBI's Stance

The RBI has defended its proposed ban, stating that it is necessary to address the liquidity crisis in the banking sector. The central bank has argued that a ban on revolving credit facilities is a temporary measure that will be reviewed after six months. However, industry experts remain skeptical about the effectiveness of this measure and its potential impact on the overall economy.

Way Forward

As the debate continues, it is crucial for all stakeholders to come together and find a balanced solution that addresses the liquidity crisis without stifling growth. The government and the RBI need to engage with industry bodies like FIDC to understand their concerns and find a mutually beneficial solution. In the meantime, NBFCs need to prepare for the potential impact of the proposed ban and explore alternative financing options to manage their working capital.
