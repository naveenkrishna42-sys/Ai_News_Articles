Bangladesh PM Tarique Rahman's India Visit Back On Track After Brief Halt

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Official-level talks for Tarique Rahman's first India visit as Bangladesh PM have restarted after a brief disruption linked to Dhaka's review of 101 bilateral agreements.
  • India has signalled readiness to take 'all necessary actions' to protect its interests, while Dhaka insists the pact review is routine, not hostile.
  • The visit's success hinges on resolving pending issues such as Teesta water sharing, border management, trade imbalances and connectivity projects.

Diplomatic channels between New Delhi and Dhaka have reopened after a short, sharp disruption. Officials have restarted preparatory talks for an India visit by Bangladesh Prime Minister Tarique Rahman, even as Dhaka conducts a wide-ranging review of 101 bilateral agreements signed with India over the past decade. The twin developments — a paused-then-resumed negotiation and a sweeping treaty audit — define the most consequential phase in India-Bangladesh relations since 2024.

Why the Talks Stalled — and Restarted

The disruption was triggered by media reports that Dhaka was reassessing 101 pacts covering trade, transit, energy, defence procurement and river water sharing. New Delhi responded firmly, stating it would take "all necessary actions" to safeguard its interests. Within days, both sides returned to the table, signalling that neither wants a full-blown rupture. For a relationship worth roughly $14 billion in annual bilateral trade, the cost of drift is simply too high.

What Dhaka's 101-Pact Review Actually Means

Bangladesh's interim and subsequent administrations have framed the review as standard due diligence, not a repudiation of past commitments. Several agreements — especially in power purchase, railway connectivity and port access — were fast-tracked under the previous government. Dhaka now wants clearer terms on pricing, sovereign exposure and reciprocity. Indian negotiators, in turn, want continuity guarantees for projects like the Akhaura–Agartala rail link and the Maitree power plant.

The Politics Behind Tarique Rahman's Diplomacy

Tarique Rahman's approach to India differs from his predecessor's warmth. He skipped the BRICS summit hosted in Delhi but is expected at a UN-linked multilateral meet — a calibrated signal that Dhaka will engage India multilaterally rather than bilaterally on its own terms. Domestic pressure from religious-political blocs, including Jamaat-e-Islami, further constrains his room to manoeuvre.

Buyer's-Eye View: What This Means for Business

For Indian exporters, logistics firms and infrastructure contractors, the visit's outcome decides real money. A confirmed pact review could reopen tariff and transit terms, affecting trucking routes through Benapole and Petrapole, garment supply chains and power purchase margins. Companies with Bangladesh exposure should track three deliverables: renewed transit protocols, clarity on LOC-funded projects and any revision to duty-free access for Indian goods.

Pros and Cons of the Current Diplomatic Track

  • Pro: Both governments are still talking, reducing risk of a freeze in trade and transit.
  • Pro: The pact review may produce cleaner, more enforceable agreements with better pricing terms.
  • Pro: A successful visit would stabilise $14 billion in annual trade and cross-border connectivity.
  • Con: Prolonged review uncertainty delays project approvals and investor commitments.
  • Con: Domestic political pressure in Dhaka could force public posturing that slows quiet diplomacy.
  • Con: Unresolved Teesta water sharing remains a perennial flashpoint with no near-term fix.

What to Watch Before the Visit

Three markers will reveal whether the visit proceeds on schedule: a formal joint statement on the pact review's scope, movement on the Teesta framework, and confirmation of dates through official channels. Until then, treat speculative timelines with caution. The restart of talks is a positive signal, but the substance — not the symbolism — will determine whether this diplomatic reset delivers for businesses and citizens on both sides of the border.
