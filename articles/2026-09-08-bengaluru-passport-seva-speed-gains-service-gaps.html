Bengaluru’s Passport Seva: Faster Processing, Lingering Bottlenecks

Bengaluru’s passport seva centres have cut down processing time for applicants, yet service delivery remains uneven across the city. While the number of passports dispatched has risen, citizen complaints about appointment delays and documentation issues persist. The regional passport office (RPO) has introduced digital kiosks and extended counter hours, but ground-level execution still varies.

Speed vs. Accessibility

According to official data, Bengaluru’s three passport seva kendras processed over 1.2 lakh applications in August 2026, marking a 15% rise compared to July. However, booking an appointment remains a hurdle. Slots fill within minutes, especially for tatkal and reissue categories. Applicants report having to retry multiple times before securing a time slot, negating the benefit of faster post-submission processing.

Infrastructure Gaps Behind the Scenes

Despite visible upgrades, back-end infrastructure has not kept pace. The RPO cites a shortage of trained officials, particularly for document verification. Most counters operate with skeleton staff during peak hours, leading to long queues even when appointments are secured. A senior official noted that while digital forms reduce manual errors, physical document checks still require human oversight, slowing final clearance.

Real-World Impact on Applicants

For first-time applicants, the process can take over two weeks if reappearance is required for missing documents. Frequent travellers often opt for tatkal services, which cost ₹1,500 extra and promise 3-day delivery. However, demand for tatkal slots far exceeds supply, creating a secondary queue outside centres. Many rely on local agents charging premiums up to ₹3,000 for slot booking and form assistance.

  • Pros: Faster post-submission processing; extended counter timings; digital kiosks streamline form filling
  • Cons: Appointment slots exhaust quickly; understaffed verification counters; agent dependency increases costs
  • Key Insight: Speed gains are real but limited by front-end access and back-end capacity constraints

What Needs Fixing

Experts suggest expanding appointment availability through mobile-based scheduling and deploying trained volunteers during peak periods. The government has floated tenders for additional staff, but recruitment delays mean improvements may take months. Until then, applicants are advised to apply early, carry complete documentation, and avoid peak hours (10 AM–1 PM) for smoother processing.
