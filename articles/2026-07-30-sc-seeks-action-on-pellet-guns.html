SC Seeks Action On Pellet Guns

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court seeks Delhi Govt's action on pellet gun injured protesters
  • RAF ammunition log from Jantar Mantar protest sought
  • Pellet gun use legal till rules allowing it held unconstitutional

Pellet Gun Use Under Scrutiny

The Supreme Court has asked the Delhi Government to ensure that injured protesters receive proper treatment. This comes amid a plea to ban pellet guns, which have been used to control protests in the past.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of protesters injured by pellet guns, but a study from Kashmir reveals the psychological impact of such wounds, including depression, PTSD, and substance abuse.

Psychological Impact Of Pellet Guns

A study from Kashmir highlights the long-term effects of pellet gun injuries on mental health. The study found that victims often suffer from depression, PTSD, and substance abuse, underscoring the need for adequate medical and psychological support.

Supreme Court's Stand On Pellet Guns

The Supreme Court has stated that pellet guns can be used in exceptional situations, but has sought the RAF ammunition log from the Jantar Mantar protest to review the circumstances under which they were used.

The court has also noted that the use of pellet guns is legal until the rules allowing it are held unconstitutional.

Compensation For Victims

In a positive development, a pellet gun victim from Jammu and Kashmir who lost eyesight in 2016 has received compensation, thanks to an NDTV impact initiative.

While this is a step in the right direction, many more victims are waiting for justice and adequate compensation.

Next Steps

The Supreme Court's directives to the Delhi Government and the RAF are expected to bring some relief to injured protesters. However, the long-term solution lies in addressing the root causes of protests and ensuring that security forces use non-lethal methods to control crowds.

  • Delhi Government to provide medical treatment to injured protesters
  • RAF to submit ammunition log from Jantar Mantar protest
  • Supreme Court to review the use of pellet guns in exceptional situations

The case will continue to be heard in the Supreme Court, with the next hearing expected to shed more light on the government's plans to address the issue of pellet gun use.
