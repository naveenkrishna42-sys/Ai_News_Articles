Railway Board clears CBTC signalling for Namma Metro's Pink, Blue lines

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Railway Board has approved Communication-Based Train Control (CBTC) signalling for Pink and Blue lines.
  • The technology will allow driverless train operations and reduce headway between trains to 90 seconds.
  • Approval is a major step towards commissioning the two corridors, expected to ease congestion in Bengaluru.

Green signal for modern signalling

The Railway Board has cleared the use of Communication-Based Train Control (CBTC) signalling for Namma Metro's upcoming Pink and Blue lines. The approval, granted this week, paves the way for deploying the advanced system on the two corridors that are currently under construction in Bengaluru.

CBTC is a railway signalling system that uses telecommunications to manage train movements, allowing for real-time tracking and precise control. This marks a departure from the conventional fixed-block signalling used on the existing Green and Purple lines, which rely on track circuits to detect trains.

Driverless operations on the horizon

With CBTC in place, the Pink and Blue lines will be equipped to run trains without drivers, a first for the city's metro network. The system enables automatic train operation, which can reduce the gap between trains to as little as 90 seconds during peak hours, significantly boosting capacity.

Bangalore Metro Rail Corporation Limited (BMRCL) had been pushing for the technology to ensure these corridors meet future ridership demands. The Railway Board's nod is seen as a critical step, as it allows BMRCL to proceed with the tendering and installation of the signalling equipment.

What this means for commuters

Once operational, the Pink Line will connect Kalena Agrahara in the south to Nagawara in the east, while the Blue Line will link Central Silk Board to Kempegowda International Airport. Both corridors are expected to ease traffic congestion on some of the city's busiest routes.

The reduced headway will allow more trains to run on the same track, potentially carrying over 40,000 passengers per hour per direction. This is a substantial improvement over the current system, which can handle around 25,000 passengers per hour per direction.

Challenges and next steps

However, implementing CBTC is not without hurdles. The system requires extensive testing and integration with existing infrastructure, and any glitches could delay the launch. BMRCL officials have indicated that they are working on a tight schedule, aiming to open the Pink Line's initial stretch by late 2026.

The Blue Line, which is longer and includes airport connectivity, may take longer. The Railway Board's approval is a significant milestone, but the real test will be in the execution. BMRCL will now need to finalise contracts and ensure that the signalling system is installed and commissioned without hiccups.

As Bengaluru waits for these lines to open, the focus will be on how quickly BMRCL can translate this approval into tangible progress on the ground.
