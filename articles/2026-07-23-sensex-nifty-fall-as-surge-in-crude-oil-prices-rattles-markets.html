Sensex Nifty Fall as Surge in Crude Oil Prices Rattles Markets

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 300 points in early trade, Nifty dropped below 24,400.
  • Rising crude prices raised fears of higher import bills and inflation.
  • Broader market indices also traded in the red, with auto and FMCG stocks hit hard.

Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply lower on Thursday, with the Sensex tumbling over 300 points and the Nifty slipping below the 24,400 mark in early trade. The sell-off was triggered by a surge in global crude oil prices, which fanned fears of higher inflation and a widening trade deficit.

The BSE Sensex was trading at 80,120.45, down 324.12 points, while the NSE Nifty hovered around 24,385.40, losing 98.70 points. Market breadth was negative, with losers outpacing gainers on both exchanges.

Crude Shock Waves

Brent crude futures climbed past $85 a barrel in overnight trading, marking a fresh multi-month high. The spike was attributed to supply concerns following OPEC+ production cuts and rising geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

India, which imports over 85% of its crude oil requirements, is particularly vulnerable to such price shocks. Higher oil prices inflate the country's import bill, worsen the current account deficit, and stoke domestic inflation, weakening investor sentiment.

“The crude surge is a clear negative for India. It puts pressure on the rupee, raises input costs for companies, and could force the RBI to keep interest rates higher for longer,” said a market analyst.

Which Stocks Took the Hit?

Shares of oil-dependent sectors bore the brunt. Automobile stocks, which are sensitive to fuel prices, were among the top losers. Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, and Mahindra & Mahindra all traded in the red. FMCG stocks also declined on fears of rising packaging and transportation costs.

Meanwhile, oil marketing companies such as HPCL, BPCL, and IOC saw mixed trading. While higher crude prices typically hurt their margins, some investors bet on potential government intervention to cushion the blow.

Banking stocks were subdued, with HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank contributing significantly to the Nifty's decline. IT stocks, however, managed to hold ground on the back of a weaker rupee.

Broader Market Feels the Heat

The broader indices mirrored the weakness. The BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices dropped up to 0.7% each. Market volatility, as measured by India VIX, inched higher, indicating increased uncertainty.

Foreign portfolio investors have been net sellers in recent sessions, and the latest crude shock may accelerate their exit. Domestic institutional investors have been providing some support, but the overall mood remains cautious.

Traders are now watching for any commentary from the government or the Reserve Bank of India on steps to manage the impact of rising oil prices. A sharp spike in crude could delay rate cut expectations, which had been building after recent benign inflation data.

The rupee also weakened against the US dollar in early trade, breaching the 83.50 mark, adding another layer of concern for import-heavy industries.

Market participants are likely to keep a close eye on global crude benchmarks and any supply-side announcements from major producers in the coming sessions. A sustained rise in oil prices could trigger a broader correction in Indian equities.
