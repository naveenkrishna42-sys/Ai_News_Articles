Air India Pilot Off Roster After Dope Test Following Turbulence Incident

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pilot removed from roster after preliminary dope test result
  • Sample sent for confirmatory analysis, government confirms
  • Incident follows severe turbulence on Phuket-Delhi flight
  • Social media misinformation around incident flagged by experts

Preliminary Test Not Negative

An Air India pilot who commanded a Phuket-Delhi flight that hit severe turbulence last week has been taken off flying duties after a preliminary dope test returned 'not negative', government and airline sources said on Sunday. The sample has now been sent for a confirmatory analysis, the outcome of which will determine further action.

The development comes days after the flight encountered violent turbulence mid-air, leaving several passengers injured and triggering chaos inside the cabin. The pilot's test result has not been officially declared positive, but the airline has acted swiftly, removing him from the roster pending the second round of testing.

Government Confirms, Airline Silent

A government official, speaking on condition of anonymity, confirmed that the captain's screening test was 'not negative' and that the matter is being handled as per standard protocol. Air India has not issued a formal statement, but sources indicate the pilot has been grounded until the confirmatory report arrives.

This is not a routine case. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) mandates that pilots undergo periodic medical and substance-abuse checks. A failed dope test, if confirmed, could lead to suspension of licence and even termination, depending on the severity and circumstances.

Passengers Recall Harrowing Experience

Passengers aboard the affected flight have described the ordeal as terrifying. One traveller told Times of India, "It was a rebirth for all of us," recalling how the aircraft dropped suddenly, throwing unbelted passengers and crew towards the ceiling. Several people sustained minor injuries, and the flight was diverted as a precaution.

The turbulence incident itself has also sparked a wave of misinformation on social media, with unverified claims about the cause and severity circulating widely. Experts have urged restraint, noting that turbulence is a common aviation phenomenon and that the dope test result is a separate, personnel-specific matter.

What Happens Next

The confirmatory analysis, typically conducted at a government-accredited laboratory, is expected to take a few days. If the result returns positive, the DGCA will likely launch a formal inquiry into the pilot's fitness and the airline's compliance with drug-testing norms.

If it returns negative, the pilot could be reinstated, though the reputational damage to both the individual and the airline may linger. Aviation regulators worldwide treat substance abuse as a zero-tolerance issue, and India has been tightening enforcement in recent years.

For now, Air India faces the dual challenge of managing the fallout from the turbulence incident and the scrutiny around its crew's conduct. The airline has not commented on whether other crew members were tested or if the co-pilot's results have been cleared.

Watch for the confirmatory test outcome and any DGCA action in the coming week. The incident is also likely to prompt renewed debate on pilot fatigue and mental health screening in Indian aviation.
