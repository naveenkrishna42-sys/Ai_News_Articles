Hyderabad to spend ₹1,340 crore on smart street lighting overhaul

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • The project aims to replace existing streetlights with energy-efficient LED fixtures.
  • Smart controls will enable dimming and real-time monitoring of streetlights.
  • The overhaul is expected to reduce the city's power consumption significantly.

The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) has approved a massive street lighting overhaul worth over ₹1,340 crore. The project will replace the city's ageing streetlights with energy-efficient LED fixtures.

Why the overhaul is needed

Hyderabad's street lighting network is decades old. Many poles use outdated sodium vapour lamps that consume more power and provide poor illumination. The new system aims to cut electricity bills and improve road safety.

Officials say the upgrade will also reduce maintenance costs. The current lights often fail, leaving stretches dark and increasing accident risks. The new LEDs are expected to last longer and require fewer replacements.

Smart controls and real-time monitoring

The project includes a centralised control system. This will allow the corporation to dim lights when traffic is low and brighten them during peak hours. Each light can be monitored remotely.

If a light malfunctions, the system will alert maintenance crews instantly. This is a big shift from the current practice of relying on public complaints or manual inspections. The smart system is expected to cut response time from days to hours.

Phased rollout and funding

The work will be carried out in phases across all zones of the city. The first phase will cover major arterial roads and commercial areas. Residential streets will follow.

Funding will come from the GHMC's own budget and possibly from central government schemes for smart city development. The corporation has not yet confirmed the exact financing split.

The project is part of a larger push to modernise Hyderabad's urban infrastructure. The city has already rolled out smart traffic signals and is expanding its surveillance camera network.

Environmental groups have welcomed the move. They point out that LED lights consume up to 60 percent less energy than conventional lamps. This will directly reduce the city's carbon footprint.

Residents will see brighter streets and fewer dark patches. But some have raised concerns about light pollution. The smart controls can dim lights in residential areas during late-night hours to address this.

The GHMC expects the entire upgrade to be completed within three years. Tenders for the first phase are likely to be floated in the coming months.

What to watch: The bidding process and the eventual savings on the city's power bill. If successful, this model could be replicated by other municipal corporations across India.
