MTC buses get unique passwords for route boards to prevent hacking

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • MTC introduces unique passwords for route boards on each bus to prevent unauthorized changes.
  • Move aims to curb hacking incidents that led to wrong route displays and passenger confusion.
  • Passwords will be managed by drivers and depots to ensure real-time accuracy.
  • Step part of broader digital security measures for Chennai's public transport.

New security layer for Chennai's public transport

The Metropolitan Transport Corporation (MTC) in Chennai has rolled out a fresh security measure to protect the electronic route boards on its buses. Each bus will now be assigned a unique password for its route display system, a move aimed at preventing hacking and unauthorized alterations.

Officials said the decision follows instances where route boards were tampered with, leading to confusion among passengers. The unique password system is expected to block external interference and ensure that the displayed route matches the actual journey of the bus.

How the system works

Every MTC bus will have a distinct password linked to its onboard route board. Drivers and depot staff will use these credentials to update or change the display as per the scheduled route. The passwords are expected to be changed periodically to add an extra layer of security.

An MTC official, speaking on condition of anonymity, told The New Indian Express that the move is part of a broader effort to modernize and secure the city's public transport infrastructure. “We are taking all necessary steps to ensure that the information displayed to passengers is accurate and tamper-proof,” the official said.

Why this matters for commuters

For daily commuters in Chennai, the route board is often the first point of information when a bus approaches. Wrong or hacked displays have previously caused passengers to board buses heading to unintended destinations, leading to delays and frustration.

The new password system aims to eliminate such risks by giving control only to authorized personnel. The initiative also aligns with the MTC's larger push towards digital integration, including real-time tracking and electronic ticketing.

Broader digital security efforts

The route board password initiative is not an isolated measure. Over the past year, the MTC has been upgrading its digital systems, including the introduction of GPS-based tracking and automated announcements. The unique password system adds a security layer to these upgrades, ensuring that the hardware and software are not easily compromised.

Transport experts have welcomed the move, noting that public transport systems worldwide are increasingly vulnerable to cyber threats. By adopting simple but effective safeguards like unique passwords, the MTC is setting a precedent for other city transport bodies in India.

Next steps

The MTC is expected to complete the rollout of the unique password system across its entire fleet in the coming weeks. Officials have not yet confirmed whether the passwords will be integrated with the existing depot management software or if a separate system will be maintained.

Passengers can look forward to more reliable and secure information on bus route boards, as the corporation continues to strengthen its digital infrastructure. The focus now shifts to training staff and ensuring smooth implementation across all depots in Chennai.
