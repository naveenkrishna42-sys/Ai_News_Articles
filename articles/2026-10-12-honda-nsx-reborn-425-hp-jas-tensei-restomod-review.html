The NSX Legacy Meets Modern Restomodding

Honda's legendary NSX supercar, discontinued in 2017 after a 30-year production run, has been reborn through Japanese tuning house JAS as the Tensei restomod. This isn't a simple facelift; it's a comprehensive reimagining that strips away the hybrid complexity of the second-generation model to return to the naturally aspirated purity that made the original iconic.

Engine and Performance Specifications

The heart of the Tensei is a 3.5-liter V6 engine producing 425 horsepower at 8,500 RPM, a significant leap from the original NA1's 270 hp. Unlike the turbocharged or hybrid variants, this unit relies purely on high-revving naturally aspirated power delivery, channeling power through a traditional six-speed manual transmission to the rear wheels.

Pininfarina's Design Philosophy

Italian design house Pininfarina collaborated on the exterior redesign, preserving the NSX's signature low-slung profile while incorporating modern aerodynamic elements. The result maintains the essential silhouette that defined the original 1990 launch but adds sharper lines, revised intake systems, and updated lighting clusters that blend retro appeal with contemporary supercar aesthetics.

Interior and Driving Experience

The cabin prioritizes driver engagement with lightweight materials, Alcantara upholstery, and a simplified dashboard layout. Removal of hybrid systems and battery packs reduces curb weight by approximately 150 kg compared to the second-generation NSX, enhancing the power-to-weight ratio and sharpening throttle response through the manual linkage system.

Market Positioning and Value Proposition

Positioned as a premium restomod starting around $250,000, the Tensei targets collectors seeking exclusivity beyond modified originals. While pricing exceeds most NSX variants, it undercuts comparable Italian supercars like the Ferrari Roma while offering superior reliability and lower maintenance costs inherent to Honda engineering.

Real-World Buyer Implications

Potential buyers should consider parts availability given the limited production run and specialized components. The manual transmission appeals to purists but may limit resale appeal compared to automatic alternatives. Insurance costs remain elevated due to the vehicle's performance specifications and exclusivity status.

Competition and Alternatives

The Tensei competes directly with restomodded classics like the Singer DLS 911 and Gunther Werks 993, but distinguishes itself through Japanese engineering precision and naturally aspirated power delivery. Unlike European alternatives emphasizing track-focused performance, JAS has tuned the Tensei for daily driveability while maintaining weekend warrior capabilities.

Technical Considerations for Enthusiasts

Traditionalists will appreciate the return to three-pedal driving in an era dominated by dual-clutch automatics. The engine's redline exceeds most modern V6 applications, requiring experienced driver technique to fully exploit the power band effectively. Cooling system upgrades accommodate sustained high-RPM operation without compromising reliability.

  • Naturally aspirated V6 delivers linear power delivery and high-revving character
  • Restomod pricing exceeds original NSX variants but undercuts European competitors
  • Limited production run creates exclusivity but complicates long-term parts support
