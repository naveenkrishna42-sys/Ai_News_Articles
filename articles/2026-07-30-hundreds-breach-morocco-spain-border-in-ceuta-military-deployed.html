Hundreds breach Morocco-Spain border in Ceuta, military deployed

📅 2026-07-30 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hundreds of migrants swam from Morocco to the Spanish enclave of Ceuta, breaching border barriers.
  • Spanish military has been deployed to the area to manage the situation.
  • Videos of the incident circulating on social media have prompted calls for a national emergency declaration.

Mass border breach at Ceuta

Hundreds of migrants breached the heavily fortified border between Morocco and the Spanish enclave of Ceuta on Thursday, sparking a major security response. Reports describe the scene as 'absolute chaos' as people swam around the sea barriers that separate the two territories.

Spanish authorities confirmed the deployment of military personnel to the enclave, a small but strategic outpost on the North African coast. The Guardia Civil and local police are attempting to regain control of the perimeter.

Swimmers bypass barriers

Videos circulating on social media show large groups of people, including families with young children, swimming in the waters off Ceuta. Many managed to reach the beach on the Spanish side after rounding the border fences that extend into the Mediterranean Sea.

The migrants are believed to have entered the water from the Moroccan side, taking advantage of low tide and calm conditions. Witnesses described a sudden surge of hundreds of people moving towards the Spanish coastline at once.

Military called in

Spain's central government has mobilised army units to assist civil guards in containing the influx. The situation has been described as the most serious border incident in Ceuta in years.

Political voices in Spain have urged the government to declare a national emergency, demanding stricter controls and a coordinated response with Moroccan authorities. The Spanish interior ministry is holding urgent consultations but has not yet commented on the total number of people who entered.

Viral videos fuel calls for action

Footage of the breach has spread rapidly online, fuelling debate over migration policy and border security. Some critics accuse Spanish authorities of being unprepared, while others highlight the desperation driving people to risk the dangerous swim.

Ceuta and its sister enclave Melilla have long been flashpoints for migration into Europe. The enclaves are surrounded by high fences and are patrolled by both Spanish and Moroccan forces.

What happens next

Spanish and Moroccan officials are expected to hold talks in the coming hours to coordinate a response. The number of migrants who remain in Ceuta and their legal status will be key questions in the days ahead.
