Monsoon Session: Houses Adjourned Till 2 pm as Opposition Demands Debate

📅 2026-08-10 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Both Houses of Parliament adjourned till 2 pm amid opposition protests on various issues.
  • Union Home Minister Amit Shah has expressed readiness to discuss queries in Parliament on the Delhi Police crackdown.
  • Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju said Shah will speak on the student protest crackdown, but posed a condition to the opposition.

The Monsoon Session of Parliament witnessed noisy scenes on Monday morning as opposition members trooped into the well of both Houses, forcing adjournments till 2 pm. The protests centred on a range of issues, with the recent Delhi Police crackdown on student protests taking centrestage.

Opposition Demands Discussion

Opposition MPs have been pressing for a detailed discussion on the police action against students, alleging excessive use of force. They have also raised concerns over other unresolved matters, but the student protest issue has become the flashpoint of the session.

Sources indicate that the opposition wants a statement from the government, followed by a debate. The demand has been consistent across party lines, with several leaders calling for accountability and transparency in the handling of the protests.

Shah Ready to Address Parliament

In a significant development, Union Home Minister Amit Shah has conveyed his willingness to answer questions in Parliament regarding the Delhi Police crackdown. This comes amid intense pressure from the opposition, which has accused the government of avoiding a confrontation on the issue.

Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju confirmed that Shah is prepared to speak on the student protest crackdown. However, Rijiju has laid down a condition for the opposition, though the specifics of this condition have not been officially detailed yet.

What Rijiju Said

Speaking to reporters, Rijiju indicated that the government is not shying away from a discussion. He maintained that the Home Minister is ready to clarify the government's position, provided the opposition adheres to certain procedural norms.

The minister's remarks have been met with a mixed response. While some opposition leaders see this as a positive step, others remain sceptical, pointing to the government's earlier silence on the matter.

Allegations of Constitutional Betrayal

Critics have not held back, with some commentators and political figures accusing the Home Minister of betraying constitutional morality by remaining silent on alleged police brutality. Such statements have further heated the political atmosphere within and outside Parliament.

The opposition has cited instances of what it claims were heavy-handed tactics by Delhi Police during the student protests. The government, on its part, has defended the police action as necessary to maintain law and order.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact sequence of events that led to the arrests or injuries, but the issue has clearly struck a chord with civil society and student bodies.

What Happens Next

Parliament will reconvene at 2 pm, and all eyes will be on whether the government and opposition can reach a consensus on the terms of discussion. The adjournment has given both sides a brief window to negotiate behind the scenes.

Observers will be watching if Amit Shah's offer to speak is accepted by the opposition, and whether the session can move to substantive legislative business. The coming hours are crucial for the remainder of the Monsoon Session.
