US official says Trump, Modi will resolve tariff threat over Russian oil

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • White House trade advisor Peter Navarro said Trump and Modi have good working relations
  • Navarro expressed confidence that the 100% tariff threat over Russian oil purchases will be resolved
  • India has not officially responded to the tariff threat yet

Washington: A top White House official has sought to ease concerns over the 100% tariff threat against India, asserting that President Donald Trump and Prime Minister Narendra Modi share a strong working relationship that will help resolve the issue.

Peter Navarro, the White House trade advisor, said the two leaders have "good working relations" and expressed confidence that the matter would be sorted out through dialogue.

What Navarro Said

Speaking to reporters, Navarro said, "Trump, Modi have good working relations; will work it out." His remarks come amid escalating tensions over India's continued purchase of Russian oil, which the US has been trying to curb.

The threat of a 100% tariff on Indian goods has been a point of friction, with New Delhi maintaining that energy security is a sovereign matter. Navarro's comments suggest the administration is open to a negotiated settlement.

The Russian Oil Factor

The tariff threat is directly linked to India's imports of Russian crude, which have continued despite Western sanctions. Washington has repeatedly pressed New Delhi to reduce its dependence on Russian energy, but India has argued that its purchases are necessary for domestic needs.

Analysts believe the US is using the tariff threat as leverage to push India into a tougher stance on Russia. However, Navarro's tone indicates a preference for diplomacy over confrontation.

India's Strategic Autonomy

Experts have warned that India must guard its strategic autonomy amid Washington's broader pressure campaign. The situation has drawn attention to the delicate balance India maintains between its ties with the US and its historical relationship with Russia.

So far, the Indian government has not officially commented on the tariff threat, but officials are believed to be in touch with their American counterparts.

What Happens Next

Both sides are expected to hold further discussions in the coming weeks, possibly on the sidelines of upcoming multilateral meetings. The outcome will likely depend on whether India can offer concessions on Russian oil volumes without compromising its energy needs.

Observers will be watching for signals from both capitals on a possible deal, as the tariff deadline approaches.
