Government Establishes Mediation Council of India

The Central Government has officially established the Mediation Council of India under the Mediation Act, 2023. This move comes after a parliamentary panel urged the government to streamline the mediation process in India. The council, with its headquarters in Delhi, is expected to play a crucial role in promoting mediation as an alternative dispute resolution method.

Background and Context

The establishment of the Mediation Council of India is a significant step towards the government's goal of promoting mediation as a preferred method of dispute resolution. The council will be responsible for regulating and overseeing the mediation process in India. It will also be responsible for setting standards and guidelines for mediation services and for ensuring that mediators are qualified and competent.

Key Features of the Mediation Council of India

The Mediation Council of India will have a three-tier structure, consisting of a National Council, State Councils, and District Councils. The National Council will be the apex body, responsible for setting policies and guidelines for the council. The State Councils will be responsible for overseeing the mediation process in their respective states, while the District Councils will be responsible for ensuring that mediation services are available in their respective districts.

The council will also be responsible for promoting mediation as a method of dispute resolution. It will do this by conducting awareness campaigns, providing training and education for mediators, and by collaborating with other organizations and stakeholders in the dispute resolution field.

Impact and Implications

The establishment of the Mediation Council of India is expected to have a significant impact on the dispute resolution landscape in India. By promoting mediation as a preferred method of dispute resolution, the council is likely to reduce the burden on the courts and the legal system. It will also provide a more accessible and affordable method of dispute resolution for individuals and businesses.

However, the success of the Mediation Council of India will depend on its ability to effectively regulate and oversee the mediation process. It will need to ensure that mediators are qualified and competent, and that mediation services are of a high standard. It will also need to address any potential conflicts of interest and ensure that the mediation process is fair and impartial.
