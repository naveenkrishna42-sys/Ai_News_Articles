Veteran actor Pradeep Rawat of 'Lagaan', 'Ghajini' fame dies at 74

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pradeep Rawat died at 74 after a battle with cancer
  • His manager confirmed the news; funeral is scheduled for Wednesday
  • Yashpal Sharma and others mourned the actor's demise

Veteran actor Pradeep Rawat, known for his roles in films like Lagaan and Ghajini, died on Tuesday. He was 74. His manager confirmed that the actor had been battling cancer, though the specific type and duration of the illness have not been officially disclosed.

Rawat's death has prompted an outpouring of grief from colleagues and fans across the Indian film industry. Actor Yashpal Sharma was among the first to publicly mourn the loss, sharing his condolences on social media. Details of the funeral, which is scheduled for Wednesday, are still emerging.

A career spanning decades and languages

Pradeep Rawat was a familiar face in Hindi, Telugu, Tamil, and Kannada cinema. He could play a menacing villain in one film and a sympathetic character in the next, a versatility that made him a reliable presence in over 100 films.

For many, he is best remembered as the fierce British officer in Ashutosh Gowariker's Lagaan (2001). His portrayal of Captain Andrew Russell, the arrogant and ruthless antagonist, earned him critical acclaim and a wider audience. He also appeared as the father of the protagonist in Aamir Khan's action thriller Ghajini (2008), and as Ashwatthama in the television adaptation of the Mahabharat.

From the villain to the father figure

Rawat's ability to embody both villainy and warmth made him a sought-after character actor. In Ghajini, his role as the adoptive father of the character played by Asin added an emotional layer to the film's narrative. His work in the regional film industries, particularly in Telugu cinema, was equally prolific.

Born in 1951, Rawat began his career in the late 1970s and steadily built a reputation for reliability and craft. He worked with several prominent directors and actors, often in pivotal supporting roles that elevated the films he was part of.

His last years were marked by health struggles, but he continued to be active professionally as long as his health permitted. The news of his death has led to tributes from across the entertainment world, with many recalling his memorable performances and his contribution to Indian cinema.

Industry mourns a respected colleague

Yashpal Sharma, who worked with Rawat in several projects, expressed his sorrow over the loss. "He was a wonderful actor and a kind human being," Sharma was quoted as saying in early reports. "It is a great loss for the industry."

Several other actors and directors have taken to social media to remember Rawat, praising his dedication and the impact he had on their careers. The details of his last rites are yet to be announced, but his manager has confirmed that the funeral will be held on Wednesday.

Rawat is survived by his wife and children. The family has requested privacy during this difficult time, and further details about the funeral arrangements are expected to be shared shortly.

What lies ahead

As the film fraternity comes to terms with the loss, fans are revisiting his iconic performances, particularly in Lagaan and Ghajini. His death marks the end of an era for character actors who defined an entire generation of Indian cinema.

Tributes are expected to continue pouring in over the next few days, and a memorial service is likely to be held in Mumbai, where Rawat had been based for most of his career.
