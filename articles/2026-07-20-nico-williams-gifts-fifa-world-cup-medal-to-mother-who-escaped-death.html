Nico Williams gifts FIFA World Cup medal to mother who 'escaped death'

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nico Williams handed his FIFA World Cup medal to his mother after Spain's win.
  • He said he 'cannot run as fast' as his mother, who 'escaped death'.
  • Williams' parents fled Ghana for Spain 32 years ago, seeking a better life.
  • The footballer set up La Roja's winning goal in the World Cup final.

Medal for mama

Spanish forward Nico Williams turned a World Cup victory into a deeply personal moment when he handed his winner's medal to his mother. The gesture, captured on camera, quickly spread across social media. But the story behind it is older than the tournament itself.

Williams, 23, had just helped Spain lift the FIFA World Cup. In the final, he set up the winning goal. Yet in the post-match chaos, he sought out one person. His mother, who had watched from the stands, received the medal as he embraced her.

'I can't run as fast as my mother'

The Athletic Bilbao winger later explained why the medal meant more in her hands. 'I run very fast, but I can't run as fast as my mother,' he said. 'Because she once escaped death and…' He did not finish the sentence.

Those words resonated far beyond the stadium. For Williams, the medal is not just a reward for his own efforts. It is a symbol of a journey that began 32 years ago, when his parents fled Ghana for Spain in search of safety and opportunity.

A family's escape

Williams' father and mother crossed the Sahara Desert and the Mediterranean Sea to reach Europe. They settled in Pamplona, where Nico and his older brother, Iñaki, were born. Iñaki also plays for Athletic Bilbao and represents Ghana internationally.

The brothers have spoken openly about their parents' sacrifices. Their mother worked multiple jobs to support the family. Their father, who could not find stable work, struggled with the transition. Football became a way out, and a way forward.

Nico Williams broke into the senior Spain squad in 2022. His pace and dribbling made him a key player for La Roja. At the World Cup, he delivered when it mattered most.

Sacrifice and success

Williams' tribute to his mother is not an isolated act. He has regularly credited his family for keeping him grounded. In interviews, he has described his mother as the 'real hero' of his story.

His parents' journey from Ghana to Spain is a familiar immigrant narrative, but one that rarely gets told on a global stage. By giving away his medal, Williams turned a personal debt into a public acknowledgment.

The moment also sparked conversations about the role of family in sport. Many athletes dedicate wins to loved ones. Few hand over the actual prize.

What next

Williams is expected to return to club football with Athletic Bilbao for the new season. His World Cup performance has drawn interest from top European clubs. But for now, the medal stays with his mother.
