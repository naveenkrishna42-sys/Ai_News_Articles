Monsoon Session: Govt offers Amit Shah statement, Oppn insists on debate

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government offers Amit Shah statement in Parliament on students' protest and police action.
  • Opposition rejects the offer, demands a detailed discussion and presses for a statement.
  • Kiren Rijiju challenges Rahul Gandhi to face Amit Shah in Parliament.
  • Congress's Venugopal writes to Lok Sabha Speaker, pressing for Shah's statement.

Parliament Logjam Continues Over Students' Protest

The Centre on Monday offered a statement by Union Home Minister Amit Shah in Parliament on the recent police action against student protesters, but the Opposition remained unrelenting, demanding a structured debate. The standoff has brought the Monsoon Session to a near halt, with both Houses witnessing repeated adjournments.

Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju, addressing the media, said the government was ready to let Shah speak on the issue. He also threw a challenge at Congress leader Rahul Gandhi, asking him not to run away from a face-off with the Home Minister on the floor of the House.

Opposition Demands More Than a Statement

Opposition parties, however, have made it clear that a mere statement will not suffice. They want a full-fledged discussion with a reply from the Home Minister, followed by a vote, if necessary. The demand stems from the alleged use of force against students during protests in several states, an issue that has become a flashpoint in the ongoing session.

Congress leader Abhishek Manu Singhvi and other floor leaders have insisted that the government must not dictate the terms of parliamentary debate. They argue that the sanctity of Parliament lies in the Opposition's right to question the executive, not in the government's convenience.

Rijiju's Challenge to Rahul Gandhi

Rijiju's remarks were pointed. "Don't run away," he said, addressing Gandhi directly, challenging him to engage with Shah on the issue. The minister's tone suggested that the government sees the Opposition's demand as a tactic to avoid a direct confrontation on the floor.

But the Congress has countered that it is the government that has been avoiding a structured discussion. Party leaders point out that the Home Minister's silence on the matter has been conspicuous, and they accuse him of betraying constitutional morality by not addressing the alleged police excesses.

Venugopal's Letter to the Speaker

In a related move, Congress leader K C Venugopal has written to Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla, pressing for Shah's statement on the police action against students. The letter is seen as an attempt to formalise the Opposition's demand and put pressure on the government to relent.

The Speaker's role has come under scrutiny, with the Opposition accusing the Chair of being partisan. The government, on its part, maintains that it is willing to facilitate a statement but not a debate that could derail the legislative agenda.

What Lies Ahead

The impasse is likely to continue unless one side blinks. The government may schedule Shah's statement for later this week, but the Opposition's insistence on a debate remains the sticking point. With the session scheduled to run until mid-September, both sides have time, but the logjam is already delaying key bills.

Observers will be watching whether the Speaker brokered a compromise or whether the government decides to force a vote on a motion, a move that could escalate the confrontation. For now, Parliament's productivity hangs in the balance, and the coming days will determine whether the session can recover or is doomed to be a washout.
