Sensex drops 550 points, Nifty near 24,050 on US-Iran tensions

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex dropped 550 points to settle lower on Tuesday
  • Nifty ended near 24,050, dragged by broad-based selling
  • US-Iran tensions were a key factor behind the market slide
  • Benchmark indices fell sharply as US-Iran tensions rattled markets. Sensex lost 550 pts, Nifty closed near 24,050. Investors turned cautious amid geopolitical uncertainty.

Indian equity markets ended deep in the red on Tuesday as escalating US-Iran tensions spooked global investors. The Sensex tumbled 550 points, while the Nifty closed near the 24,050 mark, marking one of the sharpest single-day falls in recent weeks.

Broad-based selling hits indices

The BSE Sensex closed at 79,280, down 550 points from the previous close. The NSE Nifty settled at 24,052, losing nearly 170 points during the session. All sectoral indices ended in negative territory, with banking, auto, and IT stocks leading the decline.

Market breadth was overwhelmingly negative. On the BSE, around 2,100 stocks declined against just 700 advances. The Nifty Bank index fell over 1.5%, dragged by losses in HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Axis Bank.

Geopolitical uncertainty weighs on sentiment

Investor sentiment took a hit after reports emerged of heightened military posturing between the United States and Iran. Global crude oil prices spiked more than 2% on the news, raising concerns about inflation and its impact on central bank policy.

"The escalation in the Middle East is a clear risk for markets," said a senior analyst at a domestic brokerage. "Higher oil prices can hurt India's fiscal math and keep inflation elevated. That's why we saw selling across the board."

Factors behind the fall

Key triggers for Tuesday's decline included the US-Iran standoff, a sharp rise in Brent crude futures above $78 per barrel, and weakness in other Asian markets. Japan's Nikkei fell 1.2%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 0.8%.

Foreign portfolio investors turned net sellers during the session, offloading shares worth around Rs 1,200 crore, provisional data showed. Domestic institutional buyers provided some support but could not stem the fall.

The rupee also weakened against the US dollar, slipping past the 83.50 mark, adding to the pressure on import-dependent sectors like oil marketing and aviation.

What to watch

Markets will now closely track developments in the Middle East and any official statements from the Indian government on the situation. A sustained rise in crude prices could force the Reserve Bank of India to maintain a hawkish stance, dampening hopes of a rate cut.
