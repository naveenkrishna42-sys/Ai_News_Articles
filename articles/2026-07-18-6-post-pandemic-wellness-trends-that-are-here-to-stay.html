6 Post-Pandemic Wellness Trends That Are Here to Stay

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • Breathwork and cold exposure therapy are rising in popularity.
  • Mushroom-based supplements and adaptogens are mainstream now.
  • Digital detoxes and sleep hygiene are prioritised post-pandemic.

The Shift to Intentional Wellness

The pandemic didn't just change how we work and socialise. It rewired how we think about health. ELLE's latest feature highlights six wellness trends that have moved from fringe to mainstream since 2020.

These aren't passing fads. They reflect a deeper desire for control, resilience and simplicity in a world that felt anything but stable.

Breathwork and Cold Exposure

Breathwork has exploded. Apps, studios and corporate workshops now teach structured breathing techniques. The promise: reduced anxiety, better focus, and physiological calm.

Cold exposure therapy — ice baths, cold showers, cryotherapy — is close behind. Advocates claim it boosts immunity, metabolism and mental toughness. Gyms and wellness centres across Indian metros now offer dedicated cold plunge pools.

Functional Mushrooms and Adaptogens

Mushroom coffee, lion's mane capsules, reishi powders. Adaptogenic herbs like ashwagandha and tulsi have been part of Indian wellness for centuries. But post-pandemic, they've become global stars.

These ingredients are marketed as stress reducers, immune boosters and cognitive enhancers. The science is still emerging, but consumer demand is undeniable. Sales of mushroom supplements have jumped sharply since 2020.

Digital Detox and Sleep Hygiene

Screen fatigue is real. People are consciously stepping away from devices — sometimes for hours, sometimes for days. Retreats that ban phones are booked months in advance.

Sleep hygiene has become a serious practice. Weighted blankets, blue-light blocking glasses, sleep trackers, and strict bedtime routines are now common. Many credit the pandemic for reminding them that rest is not optional.

Biohacking and Wearable Tech

From continuous glucose monitors to Oura rings, wearable tech has moved beyond step counting. Users track heart rate variability, sleep stages, and stress levels in real time.

Biohacking — the practice of using science and tech to optimise the body — is no longer just for Silicon Valley executives. It's reaching Indian professionals who want data-driven insights into their health.

Emotional Fitness and Therapy Culture

Mental health is no longer a taboo topic. Therapy, coaching, and emotional wellness apps have seen massive growth. People are treating emotional fitness like physical fitness — something to work on regularly.

Support groups, journaling communities, and online therapy platforms have normalised conversations about grief, burnout and loneliness. The stigma around seeking help has visibly eroded.

What’s Next for Wellness?

These trends are unlikely to fade. They address real needs — stress management, immune support, mental clarity — that the pandemic made urgent. Expect more integration of traditional Indian practices with modern wellness tech.

Watch for personalised wellness plans, AI-driven health coaches, and a continued focus on mental health as the next wave of this evolution.
