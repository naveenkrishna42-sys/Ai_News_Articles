Can You Drink ORS Daily? Doctor Warns Against Misuse

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • ORS is a medical solution for dehydration, not a daily health drink.
  • Excessive ORS intake can cause sodium overload, dangerous for kidney and hypertension patients.
  • Cipla Health's new campaign aims to educate on correct ORS usage under WHO guidelines.

What Happens When You Drink ORS Every Day?

Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) is a life-saving intervention during diarrhoea and vomiting. But a growing number of people are now consuming it as a daily wellness drink, believing it boosts energy or hydration levels. Doctors are raising red flags.

According to health experts, ORS is designed to replace fluids and electrolytes lost during illness. Drinking it without medical need can lead to an overload of sodium and glucose, which may strain the kidneys and spike blood pressure. "ORS is not a substitute for water or a daily tonic," a senior physician explained.

Who Should Avoid ORS Completely?

Patients with chronic kidney disease, heart conditions, or hypertension must be cautious. The high sodium content in standard ORS packets can worsen fluid retention and elevate blood pressure. "For someone with normal hydration levels, daily ORS intake is unnecessary and potentially harmful," the doctor added.

Individuals on diuretics or with a history of electrolyte imbalances should also avoid ORS unless prescribed. Even healthy adults risk developing hypernatremia—a condition caused by excess sodium—if they consume ORS daily without losing fluids through sweat or illness.

The Correct Way to Use ORS

Health authorities, including the World Health Organization (WHO), recommend ORS only when there is actual fluid loss—such as during acute diarrhoea, vomiting, or extreme heat exposure leading to dehydration. One packet should be mixed with the specified amount of clean water and consumed within 24 hours.

Cipla Health recently launched a campaign titled "W.H.O. hai toh woh ORS hai" to emphasise that ORS must meet WHO standards for it to be effective. The campaign urges people to read labels and not treat ORS as a general health drink.

ORS vs. Coconut Water vs. Energy Drinks

A common question is whether ORS is better than natural alternatives like coconut water or commercial energy drinks. For rehydration after mild exercise or a hot day, coconut water is a safer bet—it has natural electrolytes and less sugar. Energy drinks, however, often contain high caffeine and sugar, which can worsen dehydration.

ORS remains the gold standard for medical dehydration. But for routine hydration, plain water or natural sources are sufficient. The key is to match the rehydration method to the need: sick or severely dehydrated? Use ORS. Thirsty or tired? Stick to water or coconut water.

What to Watch For

As the monsoon season brings a spike in diarrhoeal diseases, state governments have launched stop-diarrhoea drives, distributing ORS packets in affected areas. The focus is on correct usage—not overuse. Health officials advise the public to consult a doctor before making ORS a daily habit, especially for children and the elderly.
