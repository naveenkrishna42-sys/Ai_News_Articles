Infant fostering awareness uneven among Indian women: study

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Study finds infant fostering awareness is uneven among women in India.
  • Disparities exist across regions, education levels, and socioeconomic backgrounds.
  • Experts call for targeted awareness campaigns and better access to information.

Study highlights gaps in infant fostering knowledge

A recent study has found that awareness about infant fostering remains uneven among women in India, with significant disparities across regions, education levels, and socioeconomic backgrounds. The findings underscore the need for targeted interventions to bridge the knowledge gap.

The research, which surveyed women across multiple states, revealed that while some have a clear understanding of the process and benefits of infant fostering, others are largely unaware or hold misconceptions. This inconsistency could impact the uptake of fostering as an alternative for children in need of care.

Regional and demographic disparities

The study noted that women in urban areas and those with higher education levels showed better awareness compared to their rural and less-educated counterparts. This pattern mirrors broader health and social awareness trends seen in other domains.

Socioeconomic factors also played a role, with women from higher-income households more likely to have accurate information. The researchers emphasized that these gaps are not just about access to information but also about cultural attitudes and trust in institutional processes.

Implications for child welfare

Infant fostering is a critical tool for ensuring the well-being of children who cannot be cared for by their biological parents. Uneven awareness among women, who are often the primary caregivers, could slow the progress of child welfare initiatives.

Experts suggest that community-level programs, involving local health workers and grassroots organizations, could help demystify fostering and address misconceptions. Such efforts would need to be tailored to the specific cultural and linguistic contexts of different regions.

Call for targeted campaigns

Public health officials have not yet commented on the study, but child rights advocates are urging the government to launch awareness drives that focus on underserved communities. They argue that information alone is insufficient—it must be accompanied by accessible counseling and support services.

The study's authors recommend incorporating fostering awareness into existing maternal and child health programs, which already have wide reach in rural areas. This could be a cost-effective way to disseminate accurate information.

Moving forward, researchers plan to expand the scope of the study to include more states and to track changes in awareness over time. The hope is that these insights will inform policy and help create a more informed and supportive environment for infant fostering in India.
