Frederick Banting and Charles Best Isolated Human Insulin in 1921

Frederick Banting and Charles Best, two Canadian researchers, made a groundbreaking discovery on July 27, 1921. They successfully isolated human insulin, a hormone crucial for regulating blood sugar levels. This discovery revolutionized the treatment of diabetes, a condition that affects millions of people worldwide.

The Discovery Process

The process involved extracting insulin from cow pancreas. Banting and Best used a method called acid-alkali treatment to purify the insulin. This method involved treating the pancreas extract with acid, followed by alkali, to remove impurities and isolate the insulin.

Significance of the Discovery

The isolation of human insulin marked a significant milestone in medical history. It paved the way for the development of insulin therapy, which has saved countless lives since its inception. Before this discovery, diabetes patients had no effective treatment options.

Legacy of Banting and Best

Banting and Best's discovery earned them the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1923. Their work laid the foundation for further research in endocrinology and diabetes management. Their legacy continues to inspire researchers and medical professionals worldwide.

Future Implications

The isolation of human insulin has led to numerous advancements in diabetes treatment. Researchers are now exploring ways to produce insulin more efficiently and affordably, ensuring that diabetes patients have access to this life-saving hormone.
