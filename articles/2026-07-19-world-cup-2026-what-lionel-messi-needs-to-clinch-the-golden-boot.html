World Cup 2026: What Lionel Messi Needs to Clinch the Golden Boot

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kylian Mbappe currently leads the Golden Boot race with more goals than Lionel Messi.
  • Messi needs a hat-trick or significant goal contribution in the final to surpass Mbappe.
  • The tie-breaker for the Golden Boot is assists, then minutes played, if goals are level.

The Golden Boot Race Comes Down to the Wire

The 2026 FIFA World Cup has delivered a gripping subplot alongside the main event: a battle for the Golden Boot that pits Lionel Messi against Kylian Mbappé. As the tournament enters its final match, the race for top scorer is far from settled.

Mbappé currently holds the lead, but Messi is not far behind. The Argentine captain needs a decisive performance in the final to snatch the award from the French star. The question on everyone's mind is simple: what exactly does Messi need to do?

Goals: The Primary Currency

The Golden Boot is awarded to the player with the most goals. At this stage, Mbappé has a one-goal advantage over Messi. For Messi to win outright, he must outscore Mbappé in the final.

A single goal from Messi would not be enough if Mbappé also scores, as the Frenchman would maintain his lead. The most straightforward path for Messi is a hat-trick. If he scores three and Mbappé scores none, the award is his. Even a brace from Messi would only tie the race, forcing a look at the next tie-breaker.

Assists and Minutes: The Tie-Breaking Formula

If both players finish level on goals, the next criterion is assists. FIFA uses this as the secondary measure to separate top scorers. Currently, Messi has a slight edge in assists, meaning he would win a tie-breaker on that count.

However, if they remain tied on both goals and assists, the award goes to the player who has played fewer minutes. This rule rewards efficiency. Mbappé has played slightly fewer minutes than Messi so far, giving him an advantage in that third tie-breaker. So, a simple tie on goals could still go either way.

What History Tells Us

History offers a rare precedent. At the 2010 World Cup, four players finished tied on five goals: Thomas Müller, David Villa, Wesley Sneijder, and Diego Forlán. Müller won the Golden Boot because he had three assists, the most among them.

This shows that the tie-breakers are not just theoretical. They have decided the award before. If Messi and Mbappé end the final level on goals, the world will be watching the assist column as closely as the scoreline.

The Final Act

The scenario is set for a dramatic conclusion. Messi needs a hat-trick to guarantee the Golden Boot. A brace could force it to assists, where he holds a narrow advantage. Mbappé, meanwhile, knows that any goal he scores makes Messi's task exponentially harder.

All eyes will be on the final. The Golden Boot winner will likely be the man who lifts the trophy, but the individual race adds another layer of tension to an already colossal match.
