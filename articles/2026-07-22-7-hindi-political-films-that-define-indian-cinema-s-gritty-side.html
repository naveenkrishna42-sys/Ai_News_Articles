7 Hindi Political Films That Define Indian Cinema's Gritty Side

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • The list includes classics like Raajneeti, Sarkar, and others that explore political themes.
  • These films are known for their strong narratives and performances.
  • The selection highlights the intersection of cinema and Indian politics.

Indian cinema has often turned its lens on the messy, dramatic world of politics. For viewers who enjoy stories of power, betrayal, and ideology, Hindi films have delivered some memorable entries. A recent list compiled by News18 highlights seven such political films that deserve a spot on any watchlist.

Raajneeti: A Modern Epic

Directed by Prakash Jha, Raajneeti (2010) remains a landmark. It draws loosely from the Mahabharata, placing a family feud at the heart of a political election. The film features an ensemble cast including Nana Patekar, Ajay Devgn, and Ranbir Kapoor.

Its layered plot and strong performances make it a compelling watch. The movie does not shy away from showing the ruthless side of electoral politics.

Sarkar: The Godfather Meets Maharashtra

Ram Gopal Varma's Sarkar (2005) is another essential pick. Inspired by Mario Puzo's The Godfather, the film is set in Mumbai's underworld and political corridors. Amitabh Bachchan plays Subhash Nagre, a powerful patriarch who controls a parallel justice system.

The film's taut direction and Bachchan's towering performance created a cultural touchstone. A sequel followed, but the original remains the most influential.

Other Gems in the List

The list also includes other notable titles. Hazaaron Khwaishein Aisi (2005) captures the political turmoil of the 1970s Emergency. It follows three young people whose lives change during that period.

Madras Cafe (2013) takes a different route, blending political thriller with war journalism. It examines the Sri Lankan civil war and India's involvement. The film stars John Abraham and is directed by Shoojit Sircar.

Shahid (2013) is a biographical drama about lawyer Shahid Azmi, who fought for justice in terror cases. Rajkummar Rao's performance won widespread acclaim.

The list also includes Gulaal (2009) and City of Gold (2010). Gulaal explores student politics and caste dynamics in Rajasthan. City of Gold follows a journalist investigating land scams in Mumbai.

Why These Films Matter

These films are not just entertainment. They reflect the complexities of Indian democracy. They show how power is gained, held, and lost. The stories often mirror real events, offering viewers a glimpse into the machinery of politics.

Directors like Prakash Jha and Ram Gopal Varma have consistently used cinema to comment on governance. Their films often provoke thought and debate.

The list serves as a reminder of Hindi cinema's ability to tackle serious subjects without losing its mass appeal. For anyone looking to understand India's political landscape through art, these seven films are a good starting point.

What to Watch Next

Political cinema in Hindi continues to evolve. Newer films like Newton (2017) and Article 15 (2019) have added fresh perspectives. The selection above, however, remains a solid foundation for fans of the genre.
