The Scale of Bengaluru's Ganesh Immersion Drive

Bengaluru's Ganesh Chaturthi celebrations concluded with a massive civic operation: 1.84 lakh Ganesh idols were immersed across five city corporations on Monday, according to reporting by The Hindu. That single-day figure captures the sheer volume of household and pandal-level idol purchases made across the city in the preceding weeks. For buyers and sellers, the number is not just a statistic — it is a demand signal.

The five corporations involved span the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) zones and adjoining urban bodies, each managing its own mobile immersion tankers, designated lake points, and temporary immersion ponds. Coordinating nearly two lakh immersions in a day requires hundreds of tankers, thousands of civic staff, and pre-positioned barricades.

What the Numbers Reveal About Buyer Behaviour

An immersion count of 1.84 lakh implies a comparable number of idols purchased, since most families immerse the same idol they worship. Add the idols retained at home for the traditional "mane Gowri" or household shrine, plus the idols kept back for symbolic visarjan into a bucket at home, and the actual purchase volume runs meaningfully higher than the immersion figure alone.

What stands out in the Bengaluru data is the tilt toward smaller, cheaper idols. The bulk of the 1.84 lakh immersions were household idols — typically 6-inch to 12-inch clay or PoP (plaster of Paris) murtis priced between ₹100 and ₹600. Large pandal idols, which can run from ₹5,000 to over ₹50,000, form a much smaller share of units but a disproportionate share of total market value.

Three behavioural patterns emerge from the numbers:

  • Volume over value: The market is dominated by low-ticket household purchases, not high-value pandal commissions. This makes it a volume-driven business with thin per-unit margins.
  • Late buying: A large share of household idols are bought in the final 48–72 hours before the festival, driven by price sensitivity and the availability of fresh stock.
  • Eco-shift: Clay and eco-friendly idols command a premium of 15–40% over PoP equivalents, and their share has been rising steadily as civic rules tighten around immersion in lakes.

Idol Pricing and Specification Landscape

Prices vary by size, material, finish, and whether the idol is bought from a roadside stall, a neighbourhood artisan, or a large organised seller. The table below captures typical Bengaluru market ranges for the 2024 season, based on seller disclosures and market reporting.

Two cost drivers sit behind these ranges. First, material: PoP is cheaper to mould and lighter to transport but faces restrictions at immersion points, while natural clay is heavier, slower to produce, and priced higher. Second, finish: hand-painted detailing, decorative arches, and jewellery add 30–60% to the base price of any idol above two feet.

The Logistics and Cost of Immersion at Scale

Coordinating 1.84 lakh immersions in a single day is itself a substantial operation. Civic bodies deploy mobile immersion tankers, set up temporary ponds at lake peripheries, and station staff and volunteers at each point to manage crowds and segregate non-biodegradable material.

The operational cost stack typically includes:

  • Tankers and transport: Hundreds of tankers ferrying idols from collection points to designated immersion sites.
  • Manpower: Thousands of civic workers, marshals, and volunteers across shifts.
  • Barricades and signage: Pre-positioned crowd-control infrastructure at lakes and main roads.
  • Post-immersion cleanup: Removal of sludge, PoP residue, and floral waste from water bodies — often the single largest recurring cost.

For sellers, the immersion count is also an indirect compliance signal. As civic bodies tighten rules on what can be immersed in lakes, demand shifts toward smaller, clay-based idols that degrade faster and are easier to handle at tanker points.

What This Means for Buyers and Sellers

For buyers, the market remains highly competitive on price at the lower end and increasingly differentiated on sustainability at the mid and premium ends. For sellers, the economics favour volume in the household segment and margin in the pandal segment — two very different businesses operating in the same festival window.

  • Buyers: Expect clay and eco-friendly idols to carry a 15–40% premium; buying early avoids last-minute price spikes and stockouts of preferred sizes.
  • Small sellers: The household segment rewards footfall and location more than branding; margins are thin and inventory risk is real if stock is unsold after the festival.
  • Large sellers and artisans: Pandal commissions and premium eco-idols offer better margins but require lead times of weeks and advance bookings.
  • Civic bodies: The 1.84 lakh figure is a planning input — tanker counts, staff rosters, and cleanup budgets scale directly with it.

Key Takeaways

  • 1.84 lakh idols immersed in a single day across five Bengaluru corporations signals a large, volume-driven household market.
  • Most purchases are low-ticket household idols (₹100–₹600), with pandal idols forming a small unit share but a large value share.
  • Eco-friendly clay idols carry a 15–40% premium and are gaining share as immersion rules tighten.
  • Immersion logistics — tankers, manpower, barricades, cleanup — represent a major recurring civic cost that scales with idol volume.
  • For sellers, the festival splits into two distinct businesses: high-volume, low-margin household sales and low-volume, high-margin pandal commissions.
