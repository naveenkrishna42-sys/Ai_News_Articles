RBI Holds Repo Rate, Raises GDP Forecast Amid Iran Crisis

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI keeps policy rates unchanged, no hike or cut
  • GDP forecast raised despite Iran war fallout risks
  • Sensex gains 152 points in volatile session
  • Housing market expects stronger festive demand

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) kept its benchmark repo rate unchanged on Wednesday, choosing stability over action as the Iran crisis casts a long shadow over global markets. The decision, announced after the central bank's bi-monthly monetary policy review, came as no surprise to economists who had widely predicted a status quo.

The central bank, however, raised its GDP growth forecast for the current fiscal year, signalling that domestic fundamentals remain resilient even as geopolitical tensions threaten to disrupt supply chains and push up energy prices.

RBI's Balancing Act

In its policy statement, the RBI acknowledged the mounting inflation risks emanating from the Middle East conflict, but made it clear that supporting growth takes precedence for now. The repo rate—the rate at which the central bank lends to commercial banks—remains unchanged, a move that borrowers and businesses will welcome.

Officials have not yet detailed the exact trajectory of the GDP revision, but market participants interpreted the move as a vote of confidence in the Indian economy's ability to weather external shocks.

The decision reflects a delicate balancing act. On one hand, the RBI must guard against imported inflation, particularly from crude oil prices that have spiked since the Iran crisis began. On the other, it cannot afford to choke off credit to an economy that is still recovering from global headwinds.

Market Reaction: Volatile but Positive

Equity markets reacted with cautious optimism. The Sensex gained 152 points in a volatile session, as investors digested the RBI's stance and weighed the implications of the Iran conflict on global trade.

Volatility is likely to persist, analysts say, until there is clarity on how the Iran situation evolves. The central bank's decision to hold rates provides some near-term comfort, but the geopolitical overhang remains.

Housing Sector Sees a Festive Silver Lining

One sector that is likely to benefit from the status quo is real estate. With the festive season approaching, developers and homebuyers are eyeing stronger demand, aided by stable borrowing costs.

Industry watchers note that unchanged policy rates typically boost sentiment in the housing market, as prospective buyers gain confidence in the affordability of home loans. The coming months could see a pickup in sales, particularly in the affordable and mid-segment categories.

Growth vs Inflation: The Core Tug of War

The RBI's decision underscores a broader dilemma facing central banks worldwide: how to respond to supply-side shocks without derailing growth. In India's case, the choice appears to be a pragmatic one—let growth run while keeping a watchful eye on price pressures.

Inflation risks, however, are not off the table. If crude prices remain elevated or the Iran situation worsens, the RBI may be forced to revisit its stance in subsequent reviews. For now, the message is clear: growth comes first, but vigilance is non-negotiable.

As the festive season begins, all eyes will be on how inflation data evolves and whether the Iran crisis escalates or cools. The RBI's next policy review will be closely watched for any shift in tone, but for now, the central bank has chosen the path of patience.
