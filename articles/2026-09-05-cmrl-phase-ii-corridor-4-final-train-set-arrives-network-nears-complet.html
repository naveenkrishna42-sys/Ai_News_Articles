CMRL Phase II Corridor 4 Nears Fleet Completion

Chennai Metro Rail Limited (CMRL) is on the verge of receiving the last train set for Corridor 4 of its Phase II expansion, closing out one of the most closely watched rolling stock orders in south India. The delivery, expected shortly, will bring the corridor's fleet to its certified operational strength and shift focus to track testing, signalling integration, and safety approvals.

Corridor 4, the 26.1 km Poonamallee–Light House stretch, is among the most strategically significant legs of the Phase II project. It connects emerging western suburbs with the city core and has been identified as a critical link for decongesting the Arcot Road and Kathipara Junction corridors. The arrival of the final train set signals that rolling stock, often a long-lead item in metro procurement, will no longer be a bottleneck.

What the Final Delivery Means for the Fleet

Each train set for Phase II is a three-car configuration, designed for higher passenger throughput compared to the two-car units that operated on parts of the original Phase I network. The wider coach profile, higher acceleration, and regenerative braking systems reflect upgrades over the first-generation fleet. With the last set arriving, CMRL will have the full complement of driverless-ready trains on hand, though operations will initially run in attended mode.

Implications for Daily Commuters and the Real Estate Corridor

For residents of Porur, Valasaravakkam, and Virugambakkam, the fleet completion accelerates the timeline for direct metro access into the central business district. Property analysts have tracked a 12–18% appreciation in residential prices along the corridor's alignment since alignment confirmation, and the proximity of full fleet availability is expected to sustain that momentum.

From a commuter experience standpoint, three-car trains with higher acceleration and regenerative braking translate to shorter journey times and lower energy consumption per passenger kilometre. CMRL's decision to standardise on standard gauge and third-rail power supply ensures compatibility with global maintenance practices, which is relevant for long-term operational costs.

Operational Hurdles Before Commercial Launch

Despite the rolling stock milestone, several gating items remain. The Commissioner of Metro Rail Safety (CMRS) inspection, independent safety certification, and integration testing with the signalling backbone are prerequisites before passenger services begin. Civil construction deadlines for the final station boxes, particularly around the Light House interchange, will also dictate the realistic opening window.

Key Real-World Highlights for Stakeholders

  • Fleet readiness: Last train delivery removes rolling stock as a critical-path risk for Corridor 4 commissioning.
  • Manufacturing footprint: Local production at Alstom's Sricity facility supports domestic value addition and after-sales spares logistics.
  • Commuter impact: Three-car configuration will offer higher peak-hour capacity than legacy two-car Phase I sets.
  • Technology readiness: CBTC enables shorter headways and future driverless migration without new hardware.
  • Cost justification: The Phase II project is sanctioned at ₹63,246 crore, with the elevated Poonamallee–Porur section alone costing roughly ₹1,947 crore, underscoring the need for high ridership utilisation.

Roadmap to Commercial Operations

With the final train arriving, CMRL's immediate focus shifts to dynamic testing on the energised third rail, followed by integrated trial runs covering signalling, platform screen doors where installed, and passenger information systems. The operator has previously indicated that the corridor will be opened in stages, beginning with the Poonamallee–Porur elevated section, which is structurally complete, before extending services toward the Light House terminus.

For daily Chennai commuters, the practical takeaway is straightforward: fleet availability no longer constrains the opening date, but the corridor's commercial debut remains contingent on safety certifications and the completion of last-mile civil works. Until those clearances are secured, the arrival of the final train is best read as a procurement milestone rather than an operational one.
