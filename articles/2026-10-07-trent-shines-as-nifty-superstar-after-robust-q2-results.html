Trent’s Q2 Performance Sparks Nifty Rally

Trent Ltd, the Tata Group‑owned retailer operating Westside, Zara and other brands, posted a strong Q2 FY2026‑27 update that sent its shares up 12% on the Nifty index. The surge came after the company disclosed higher-than-expected same‑store sales growth and a noticeable improvement in operating margins, signalling a turnaround in its core apparel business.

Key Drivers Behind the 12% Surge

The primary catalyst was the reported same‑store sales growth of approximately 14% year‑on‑year, driven by festive season demand and successful product mix changes at Westside. In addition, Trent highlighted a 180‑basis‑point expansion in EBIT margin, attributed to better inventory management and higher full‑price sell‑through. These operational improvements reassured investors that the company is moving past the pandemic‑era sluggishness.

Financial Highlights and Market Reaction

While the exact numbers were not disclosed in the initial wire, Trent’s filing with the BSE indicated that revenue from operations grew in the high‑teens percentage range, and net profit showed a double‑digit increase compared with the same quarter last year. The market responded swiftly, with trading volume spiking to over 2.5 times the 20‑day average as both retail and institutional buyers entered the stock.

Valuation Perspective and Peer Comparison

At the post‑surge price, Trent trades at a price‑to‑earnings multiple of roughly 45x FY27 earnings, a premium to peers such as Avenue Supermarts (DMart) and Titan Company. Analysts justify the premium on the back of Trent’s strong brand portfolio, Tata Group’s operational support, and the defensive nature of discretionary retail in India’s growing middle class. Nevertheless, some caution that any slowdown in consumer spending could pressure valuations.

Pros and Cons for Investors

  • Strong same‑store sales growth and margin expansion signal operational turnaround.
  • Backing from Tata Group provides financial stability and access to sourcing advantages.
  • Diversified brand mix (Westside, Zara, Uterqüe) reduces reliance on a single format.
  • Premium valuation leaves limited upside unless growth accelerates further.
  • Exposure to discretionary spending makes the stock vulnerable to economic downturns.
  • Intense competition from both domestic fast‑fashion players and international e‑commerce platforms.

Outlook and Risks

Looking ahead, Trent’s management has outlined plans to expand store footprint by 8‑10% annually and to deepen its omni‑channel capabilities. The company also aims to improve gross margins through private‑label expansion and better vendor negotiations. Risks remain, including rising cotton and logistics costs, potential slowdown in festive demand, and any adverse shift in consumer preference toward online‑only retailers. Investors should watch quarterly same‑store sales trends and margin trajectory as key indicators of whether the recent rally can be sustained.
