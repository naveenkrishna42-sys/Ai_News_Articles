NASA-ISRO NISAR Satellite Spots Hummingbird-Like Formation in Antarctica

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • NISAR satellite captures unusual geological formation in Antarctica.
  • Formation resembles a hummingbird with wings spread, visible in radar images.
  • Joint NASA-ISRO mission showcases advanced Earth observation capabilities.

A joint India-U.S. satellite has delivered a striking image from Antarctica – a geological formation that looks like a giant hummingbird in flight. The picture, captured by the NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar (NISAR) mission, has drawn attention for its unexpected resemblance to the bird.

The formation appears as a dark, bird-shaped patch against the white expanse of the ice sheet. Wings spread wide, a slender beak pointing forward – the outline is unmistakable. But officials say it is entirely natural, a quirk of ice and rock.

How NISAR Captured the Image

NISAR uses radar imaging to peer through clouds and darkness. This gives it an advantage over optical satellites, which can be blocked by Antarctica's frequent cloud cover.

The hummingbird shape emerged in radar data collected during a routine pass over the continent. Scientists were processing the information when they noticed the pattern. NASA later released an optical-light image for comparison, confirming the feature's location but highlighting how radar reveals details invisible to the naked eye.

What the Formation Actually Is

The formation is not a bird. It is a combination of exposed rock, blue ice, and crevasses arranged by wind and glacial movement. The dark area in the radar image corresponds to rock outcrops and areas of bare ice that reflect radar signals differently than surrounding snow.

Such pareidolia – the human tendency to see familiar shapes in random patterns – is common in satellite imagery. Previous missions have spotted faces, animals, and even letters in landscapes around the world. This one stands out for its clarity and symmetry.

NISAR's Broader Mission

The NISAR satellite, launched in early 2026, is a collaboration between NASA and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). It is designed to monitor Earth's land and ice surfaces with unprecedented precision.

Its primary goals include tracking changes in glaciers and ice sheets, monitoring deforestation, and mapping natural disasters. The hummingbird image, while visually striking, is a byproduct of this serious scientific work. The data will help researchers understand how Antarctica is responding to a warming climate.

The satellite orbits Earth every 12 days, covering the entire planet. Its radar can detect changes as small as a few centimetres in the height of ice or land.

Reactions and Public Interest

The image has spread quickly on social media, with many users expressing amazement. Some have called it a sign of nature's artistry. Scientists, however, caution against reading too much into the shape.

“It is a coincidence of topography and radar physics,” one researcher noted in a NASA blog post. “But it does remind us how beautiful and surprising our planet can be.”

ISRO officials have not commented directly on the viral image, but the mission team has shared the data openly for researchers worldwide.

NISAR will continue scanning Antarctica and other regions for at least three years. As it collects more data, scientists expect to uncover many more such patterns – some beautiful, some useful, all part of the planet's complex story.
