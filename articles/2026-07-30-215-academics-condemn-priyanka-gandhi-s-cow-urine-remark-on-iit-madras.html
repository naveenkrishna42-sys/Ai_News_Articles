215 Academics Condemn Priyanka Gandhi's 'Cow Urine' Remark on IIT Madras Director

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • More than 215 academics from various institutions have signed a statement condemning Priyanka Gandhi's remark.
  • The Congress leader referred to IIT Madras director V Kamakoti as a 'gaumutra expert' during a public address.
  • Kamakoti, known for his traditionalist views, is set to lead reforms at the National Testing Agency (NTA).

More than 215 academics have publicly condemned Congress leader Priyanka Gandhi Vadra for her recent remark targeting IIT Madras director V Kamakoti. The controversy erupted after Gandhi referred to Kamakoti as a 'gaumutra expert' — a phrase many in the academic community have called derogatory and unbecoming of a parliamentarian.

The statement, signed by faculty members from institutions including JNU, DU, and several IITs, accused Gandhi of undermining the dignity of academic leadership. They argued that personal attacks on a scholar's beliefs set a dangerous precedent for public discourse in India.

What Sparked the Row?

Priyanka Gandhi made the remark during a political rally in Wayanad, her Lok Sabha constituency. She was criticising the government's education policies when she singled out Kamakoti, the director of IIT Madras, calling him a 'gaumutra expert' — a reference to his well-known advocacy for cow urine as a traditional remedy.

Kamakoti, a computer scientist by training, has often spoken about the scientific benefits of cow urine and other indigenous practices. His views have made him a polarising figure in academic circles, but supporters say his contributions to technology and education cannot be dismissed.

Who Is V Kamakoti?

V Kamakoti is not your typical IIT director. He failed the Joint Entrance Examination (JEE) as a student and never held a passport until recently. Despite this, he rose to become the director of one of India's premier engineering institutes.

He is now set to reform the National Testing Agency (NTA), the body responsible for conducting major entrance exams in the country. His appointment to lead NTA reforms has been welcomed by some as a fresh perspective, while others remain sceptical of his traditionalist leanings.

Political Fallout

The BJP was quick to seize on the controversy. Party spokespersons accused Gandhi of showing contempt for Indian traditions and academic excellence. Union ministers demanded an apology, calling the remark an insult to the entire IIT community.

Congress leaders defended Gandhi, stating that her comment was aimed at the government's push for pseudoscience in education, not at Kamakoti personally. They pointed out that public figures must be open to scrutiny, especially when their views influence policy.

Academic Community Reacts

The open letter from academics did not take sides in the political debate. Instead, it focused on the tone of the discourse. "Ridiculing a scholar for his beliefs, however unconventional, is not the mark of a mature democracy," the letter read.

Some signatories, speaking anonymously, expressed concern that such remarks could discourage scholars from engaging with traditional knowledge systems. Others felt Gandhi's choice of words was deliberately provocative, aimed at rallying her base rather than advancing a serious debate.

The number of signatories has continued to grow since the letter was first published. Organisers expect more names to be added in the coming days as the conversation spreads across academic networks.

What happens next depends on whether Gandhi responds to the academics' call for a clarification. The controversy has also put the spotlight on Kamakoti's upcoming role at the NTA, with critics and supporters alike watching closely to see how he navigates the political storm.
