Shashi Tharoor clarifies Rahul Gandhi youth event remarks, says not an attack on Congress

📅 2026-08-09 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tharoor clarified his comments on Rahul Gandhi's 'Chhatron ki Goonj' event were not an attack on Congress
  • He said the media misrepresented his remarks, which were about reaching out to young voters
  • Tharoor also noted the party's paper leak protest did not resonate as much as the CJP protests
  • He warned that Gen Z will be the largest voting bloc in the 2029 elections

Congress leader Shashi Tharoor has sought to put an end to speculation over his recent remarks on Rahul Gandhi's youth outreach initiative, 'Chhatron ki Goonj', clarifying that his comments were not aimed at attacking his own party. The clarification comes amid a flurry of media reports interpreting his statements as veiled criticism of the Congress's approach to engaging young voters.

Speaking to reporters, Tharoor said the media had misrepresented his remarks, which he insisted were part of a broader discussion on how political parties, including his own, need to connect with a generation that is increasingly disillusioned with traditional politics. He stressed that his intent was never to undermine the Congress or its leadership, but to highlight the challenges all parties face in reaching out to the youth.

Context of the Remarks

The controversy began when Tharoor made observations about Rahul Gandhi's youth outreach programme, 'Chhatron ki Goonj', which is designed to engage with students and young voters across the country. Some media outlets interpreted his comments as a subtle rebuke of the programme's effectiveness, sparking a debate within political circles.

Tharoor, however, has denied any such intent. He pointed out that his comments were part of a larger conversation about the evolving political landscape and the need for all parties to adapt to the changing demographics of the electorate. He said that any suggestion of a rift within the Congress is unfounded and that he remains committed to the party's goals.

The 'Rude Wake-Up Call'

The clarification comes in the wake of Tharoor's earlier remarks about the protests led by the Congress over the paper leak issue. He had admitted that the party's protest did not resonate as much as the one led by the Central Jail Protest (CJP) group, calling it a 'rude wake-up call'. These comments were also seen by some as a critique of the party's communication strategy.

Tharoor had warned that Gen Z will be the largest voting bloc in the 2029 elections, making it imperative for political parties to understand their concerns and priorities. He said that the Congress needs to check where it failed in connecting with the youth, a statement that was interpreted by many as a direct challenge to the party's current outreach methods.

Party's Response

So far, the Congress leadership has not officially responded to Tharoor's clarification. Party insiders suggest that there is no immediate concern, as Tharoor has consistently been vocal about the need for internal introspection and has often spoken his mind on organisational matters.

Political analysts note that Tharoor's statements reflect a broader unease within the Congress about its ability to attract young voters, especially in the run-up to the 2029 general elections. The party has been trying to rejuvenate its image, with Rahul Gandhi leading several initiatives aimed at engaging with students and first-time voters.

What Lies Ahead

Observers will be watching closely to see whether this episode leads to any further public debate within the Congress or fizzles out as a minor internal squabble. Tharoor has made it clear that his remarks were meant as constructive feedback, not as a personal attack on Gandhi or the party leadership.

As the political calendar moves toward the next general elections, the Congress's ability to harness the energy of young voters will be under increased scrutiny. Whether Tharoor's warnings prompt a strategic rethink or are brushed aside remains to be seen, but the conversation about engaging Gen Z is unlikely to die down anytime soon.
