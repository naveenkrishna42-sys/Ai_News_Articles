Sensex Crashes Over 900 Points, Nifty Slips Below 23,700 on Crude Spike

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex plunged over 900 points in Friday's trade.
  • Nifty fell below the 23,700 mark.
  • Crude oil price surge was a major trigger for the market decline.

Markets in Red as Crude Oil Jitters Spook Investors

Indian equity markets took a severe beating on Friday, with the BSE Sensex plunging over 900 points and the NSE Nifty slipping below the 23,700 level. The broad-based sell-off was triggered by a sharp spike in global crude oil prices, raising fresh concerns about inflation and its impact on the economy.

The benchmark indices opened in the red and continued to slide through the session, as investors rushed to book profits in the face of rising input costs and uncertainty over future interest rate moves by the Reserve Bank of India.

Why Crude Oil Prices Are Driving the Sell-Off

A sudden surge in international crude oil prices was the primary catalyst for Friday's downturn. With India being a major importer of crude, any sustained rise in oil prices directly impacts the country's trade deficit and fuels inflationary pressures.

Higher crude prices also dent corporate profit margins, especially for sectors like aviation, paints, and FMCG, which rely heavily on petroleum-based raw materials. This has led to broad-based selling across these sectors, dragging the market sentiment lower.

Top Gainers and Losers of the Day

Despite the overall negative trend, a few stocks managed to buck the trend and trade in the green. Defensive sectors like IT and pharma saw some selective buying, though gains were limited. On the flip side, banking, auto, and metal stocks were among the biggest losers, with heavyweight index constituents like Reliance Industries and HDFC Bank contributing significantly to the Nifty's fall.

Broader market indices also suffered, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices declining in tandem with the benchmarks, reflecting the widespread risk aversion among traders and institutional investors.

What Investors Should Keep in Mind

Market experts suggest that the current volatility is likely to persist until there is more clarity on the trajectory of crude oil prices and the global interest rate environment. Investors are advised to avoid panic selling and instead focus on quality stocks with strong fundamentals.

Analysts also point out that elevated crude prices could delay any expected rate cuts by the RBI, which would further weigh on market sentiment in the near term. A watchful approach, with a focus on sectors less sensitive to oil price fluctuations, is being recommended.

Looking Ahead

All eyes will now be on the movement of crude oil prices in the coming sessions and any commentary from global central banks that might offer direction. The market's ability to find support near the 23,500 level on the Nifty will be crucial for a potential recovery in the week ahead.
