India calls PoK polls a farce, says reality cannot be hidden

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • India has described the electoral process in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir as a complete farce.
  • The Centre stated that such hollow exercises cannot hide the reality of the situation in the region.
  • Protests and calls for boycott by groups like JAAC highlight growing dissent in PoK.

New Delhi: The Indian government on Tuesday dismissed the ongoing electoral exercise in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK) as a 'complete farce', asserting that such hollow exercises cannot mask the harsh realities on the ground.

The sharp rebuke came amid escalating unrest in the region, where protesters have clashed with security forces, and local groups have openly called for a boycott of the polls, labelling them a 'fraud at gunpoint'.

Centre's blunt assessment

Official sources said the Centre views the so-called elections as a cosmetic exercise designed to lend legitimacy to an illegal occupation. 'These polls are a complete farce. No amount of stage-managed voting can hide the reality of the people's anger and their quest for freedom,' a government spokesperson said.

The statement underscores India's consistent position that PoK is an integral part of Indian territory, illegally occupied by Pakistan. New Delhi has repeatedly urged Islamabad to vacate the occupied areas and end human rights abuses there.

Protests and boycott calls

The situation in PoK has deteriorated in recent weeks, with reports of security forces firing on protesters who refused to disperse. Local civil society groups and political outfits have condemned the crackdown, accusing authorities of suppressing dissent.

The Jammu and Kashmir Awami Action Committee (JAAC), a prominent group in the region, has urged the public to boycott the polls. In a statement, the group described the election as a 'fraud at gunpoint', arguing that any exercise conducted under military occupation lacks credibility.

Protests have erupted in several towns, with demonstrators demanding fundamental rights and an end to what they describe as systematic repression. Witnesses say the situation remains tense, with heavy deployment of security forces in sensitive areas.

India's diplomatic offensive

India has intensified its diplomatic efforts to highlight the plight of people in PoK. At international forums, New Delhi has raised concerns over the deteriorating human rights situation, urging the global community to take note of the ground reality.

The government has also reached out to leaders in the region, reiterating its commitment to the welfare of all Kashmiris, including those living under Pakistani occupation. India has stressed that lasting peace in the subcontinent hinges on Pakistan ending its support for cross-border terrorism and vacating the occupied territories.

Regional implications

The developments in PoK come at a time of heightened regional tensions. Analysts say the unrest and the controversial electoral exercise could further strain relations between India and Pakistan, already at a low over issues of terrorism and ceasefire violations along the Line of Control.

Observers note that the boycott call and protests reflect a growing disillusionment among the local population with Pakistan's governance of the region. However, they caution that the situation could escalate if authorities continue to use force against demonstrators.

The international community's response to the unfolding events in PoK will be closely watched, as will India's next moves to press its case on the global stage.

As the electoral exercise proceeds amid protests, the coming weeks are likely to see intensified diplomatic sparring between New Delhi and Islamabad, with the people of PoK caught in the crossfire.
