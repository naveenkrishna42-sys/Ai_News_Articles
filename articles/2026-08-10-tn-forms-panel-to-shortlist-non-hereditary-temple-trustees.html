TN forms panel to shortlist non-hereditary temple trustees

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Committee constituted by Tamil Nadu government for trustee shortlisting
  • Focus on non-hereditary trustee positions in temples
  • Move aims to streamline appointment process

Panel to Screen Aspirants

The Tamil Nadu government has constituted a committee to shortlist candidates for the posts of non-hereditary trustees in temples across the state. The decision comes as part of an effort to streamline the appointment process for these key positions, which have often been mired in delays and litigation.

The committee will be tasked with screening applications and preparing a panel of suitable candidates. This panel will then be used by the Hindu Religious and Charitable Endowments (HR&CE) department to make final appointments.

Why Non-Hereditary Trustees?

Non-hereditary trustees are appointed by the government to manage temples where no hereditary trustee exists or where the existing management has been found lacking. These individuals play a crucial role in overseeing temple administration, finances, and daily rituals.

In recent years, the appointment of such trustees has faced criticism over allegations of political interference and lack of transparency. The new committee is expected to bring a more structured and merit-based approach to the selection process.

Composition and Process

While the government has not yet disclosed the full composition of the committee, sources suggest it will include senior officials from the HR&CE department and possibly independent experts. The committee is expected to invite applications from eligible candidates and conduct interviews before finalising a shortlist.

Officials have indicated that the process will be completed within a specified timeframe, though no deadline has been officially announced. The move is seen as a step towards ensuring that only qualified and devoted individuals manage temple affairs.

Reactions and Expectations

Devotees and temple management experts have welcomed the initiative, hoping it will reduce arbitrariness in appointments. Some have expressed the need for clear eligibility criteria, including educational qualifications and experience in administration or religious affairs.

However, a few quarters have raised concerns about the potential for bureaucratic delays. They urge the government to ensure the committee works swiftly and transparently, keeping the interests of temples and devotees paramount.

What Next?

The committee is expected to begin its work shortly, with advertisements inviting applications likely to be issued in the coming weeks. The final shortlist will be submitted to the government for approval, after which appointments will be made.

Observers will be watching to see how the new process unfolds and whether it brings the desired efficiency and fairness to temple administration in Tamil Nadu.
