US Fed holds rates steady, three members dissent over inflation concerns

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Federal Reserve holds benchmark interest rate steady
  • Three FOMC members voted for a rate increase, dissenting from the majority
  • Fed cites 'elevated' inflation as reason for holding rates

The US Federal Reserve has decided to keep its benchmark interest rate unchanged, a move that comes amid persistent inflation pressures that remain above the central bank's target. The decision was announced on Wednesday following the conclusion of the Federal Open Market Committee's two-day meeting.

The Fed's statement noted that inflation, while showing some signs of moderation, is still 'elevated.' This language signals that the central bank is not yet confident that price pressures are under control, despite market expectations for a potential cut later this year.

Unusual dissent within the FOMC

The decision to hold rates was not unanimous. Three members of the Federal Open Market Committee voted against the move, instead favouring a quarter-point increase. This marks a significant split within the committee, reflecting deep divisions over the path of monetary policy.

According to reports, this is the first time in recent memory that three officials have publicly dissented in favour of a hike while the majority chose to hold. The dissenting members argued that the economy remains strong enough to absorb higher borrowing costs and that a rate increase is necessary to fully tame inflation.

Chairman Warsh under scrutiny

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh faced immediate questions about his leadership and the committee's credibility. Analysts at Al Jazeera noted that the decision to hold rates, despite the dissent, raises doubts about Warsh's ability to steer the committee toward a coherent policy direction.

The Financial Times and CNBC both reported that the internal disagreement could undermine market confidence in the Fed's commitment to its inflation mandate. Warsh, who has been a vocal advocate for data-dependent policy, now finds himself defending a status quo that a vocal minority considers too cautious.

Economic backdrop and market reaction

The US economy continues to show resilience, with a tight labour market and consumer spending holding up. However, inflation, as measured by the core PCE index, has remained sticky above 3%, well above the Fed's 2% target. This has kept the central bank in a holding pattern since its last rate cut in early 2025.

Wall Street reacted with mixed feelings. Bond yields dipped slightly as traders interpreted the hold as a dovish signal, while the dollar strengthened against major currencies. The S&P 500 index saw modest gains, though the dissent within the Fed tempered investor optimism.

What to watch next

Markets will now turn their attention to the Fed's next meeting in September. With three officials already pushing for a hike, the pressure on Chairman Warsh to deliver a more decisive move will only grow. If inflation does not cool significantly in the coming weeks, the committee's internal rift could widen, forcing a change in policy direction.
