Israel tells Pentagon chief troops will remain in Lebanon, Syria, Gaza security zones

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Israel informed the US Pentagon chief of its decision to keep troops in security zones in Lebanon, Syria, and Gaza.
  • The Trump administration has been pressuring Israel to withdraw forces from Syria and Lebanon.
  • Israel is establishing new permanent military posts in southern Lebanon, drawing comparisons to past conflicts.

Israel has informed the US Pentagon chief that its military will maintain a presence in what it calls 'security zones' in Lebanon, Syria, and Gaza, according to reports. The decision puts Israel on a collision course with the Trump administration, which has been pushing for a withdrawal.

US pressure meets Israeli defiance

President Donald Trump has been pressuring Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to pull forces out of Syria and Lebanon. The White House views the continued deployments as a stumbling block to regional stability.

But Israel's message to the Pentagon was clear. Troops will stay put in these buffer areas, which Israeli officials argue are necessary to prevent attacks by Hezbollah and other militant groups.

The Pentagon chief received the Israeli position during a series of high-level talks. No breakthrough was reported.

New military posts in southern Lebanon

On the ground, Israel is not just staying — it is building. The Israeli army is establishing new permanent military posts in southern Lebanon, according to reports from the region.

This marks a significant escalation from temporary positions. Military analysts say the construction of permanent bases suggests a long-term presence, not a short-term security arrangement.

The move has alarmed local residents. In the occupied zone of Lebanon, Israeli military veterans see the shadow of past wars. Some compare the situation to the 18-year occupation of southern Lebanon that ended in 2000.

Evolving strategy reshaping the region

Israel's evolving strategy is reshaping southern Lebanon. What began as limited incursions after the 2023 war has turned into a sustained military footprint.

The security zones are not confined to Lebanon. Israel also insists on keeping troops in parts of Syria, particularly near the Golan Heights, and in buffer areas inside Gaza.

Each zone serves a different purpose, Israeli officials say. In Lebanon, the aim is to push Hezbollah away from the border. In Syria, it is to prevent Iranian-backed militias from establishing a foothold. In Gaza, it is to prevent Hamas from rebuilding its military infrastructure.

What happens next

The standoff between Washington and Jerusalem is likely to intensify. The Trump administration has limited leverage over Netanyahu, who leads a hardline coalition. Diplomatic channels remain open, but no deadlines have been set. Observers expect Israel to test how far it can push before facing consequences — if any.
