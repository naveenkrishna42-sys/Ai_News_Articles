PM Modi launches 'Nasha Mukt Yuva' campaign for drugs-free India

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi launched the 'Nasha Mukt Yuva' campaign, a 100-week anti-drug initiative.
  • He warned that drugs bring families and careers to a new low.
  • The campaign aligns with India's goal of becoming a developed nation.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Sunday launched the 'Nasha Mukt Yuva' campaign, a 100-week nationwide drive to combat drug abuse, underscoring that a drugs-free youth is crucial for India's journey towards becoming a developed nation. The initiative, announced amid growing concerns over substance abuse among young Indians, aims to mobilise communities, educational institutions, and local bodies to tackle the menace at the grassroots.

Drugs take families, careers to a new low

Speaking at the launch event, Modi delivered a stark warning to the youth, saying, "Drugs can give you a brief high but make your entire life low." He elaborated that substance abuse does not merely affect the individual but drags families and careers to unprecedented lows, eroding the social fabric and economic potential of the nation.

The Prime Minister's remarks come at a time when India is focusing on holistic development, with a vision to become a developed country by 2047. He stressed that the energy and potential of the youth are central to this ambition, and any threat to their well-being is a threat to the nation's future.

A 100-week nationwide mobilisation

The 'Nasha Mukt Yuva' campaign is structured as a 100-week programme, a prolonged and sustained effort rather than a one-off drive. This extended timeline is designed to ensure deeper penetration into villages, towns, and cities, with a focus on prevention, awareness, and rehabilitation.

Authorities are expected to involve schools, colleges, panchayats, and local sports clubs to create a support network for at-risk youth. The campaign also plans to leverage digital platforms to spread its message, making it accessible to a generation that is constantly online.

Global context and past crackdowns

The launch has drawn comparisons to similar anti-drug campaigns across the globe. India's 100-week initiative joins a list of major crackdowns worldwide, from the United States' war on drugs to China's aggressive anti-narcotics operations. However, the Indian approach appears to be more community-centric, focusing on building resilience among youth rather than purely punitive measures.

While details on the campaign's budget and specific implementation strategies are yet to be fully outlined, the Prime Minister's personal endorsement signals a high priority for his administration. The campaign is likely to be monitored closely by state governments, with periodic reviews to assess its impact.

A call to society

Modi also called upon parents, teachers, and community leaders to play an active role in identifying early signs of drug use and guiding young people towards healthier alternatives. He emphasised that the battle against drugs cannot be won by the government alone; it requires a collective societal effort.

The Prime Minister's message was clear: the path to a developed India runs through the discipline and well-being of its youth. As the campaign rolls out over the next two years, its success will depend on how effectively it translates rhetoric into action at the local level.

Observers will be watching the campaign's first few months for tangible outcomes, including the number of awareness sessions held, rehabilitation centres set up, and the participation of educational institutions. The government is expected to release periodic progress reports to keep the momentum alive.
