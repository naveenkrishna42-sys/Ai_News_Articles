Sensex ends flat, Nifty slips: 5 reasons why stock market lost momentum today

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Sensex ended flat and the Nifty slipped, snapping a recent rally.
  • Five key reasons include FII selling, weak global cues, profit-booking, and sector-specific headwinds.
  • Investors are now watching upcoming inflation data and corporate earnings for direction.

Market Loses Steam After Recent Rally

The Indian stock market lost momentum on Thursday, with the Sensex ending flat and the Nifty slipping from its recent highs. The benchmark indices, which had been riding a wave of optimism, failed to hold onto gains as multiple headwinds emerged during the trading session.

The Sensex closed near its previous close, while the Nifty ended slightly lower, snapping a three-day winning streak. Traders and investors were left wondering what drained the momentum from what had been a buoyant market.

Five Factors That Pulled the Market Down

1. FII Selling Returns

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) turned net sellers on the day, pulling out capital from Indian equities. This reversal came after a period of sustained buying, and the selling pressure was visible across large-cap and mid-cap stocks.

FII activity has been a key driver of market direction in recent weeks, and the sudden shift spooked sentiment. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) stepped in to absorb some of the selling, but it was not enough to lift the indices.

2. Weak Global Cues Weigh on Sentiment

Global markets were subdued on Thursday, with Asian and European indices trading in the red. Concerns over slowing economic growth in China and uncertainty around US interest rate policy dampened risk appetite worldwide.

Indian markets often track global trends, and the lack of positive cues from abroad made it difficult for bulls to sustain the rally. The overnight weakness on Wall Street added to the bearish mood.

3. Profit-Booking at Higher Levels

After a sharp run-up in recent sessions, many investors chose to book profits. The Nifty had climbed over 2% in the past week, and traders saw the current levels as an opportunity to lock in gains.

Profit-booking was particularly visible in sectors that had outperformed, such as banking and IT. This selling pressure prevented the indices from moving higher despite initial gains in early trade.

4. Sector-Specific Headwinds

Some sectors faced company-specific or regulatory headwinds that dragged down broader sentiment. The realty and auto indices came under pressure, while metal stocks also saw declines amid weak commodity prices.

Pharma stocks, which had been a bright spot, also witnessed profit-booking. The lack of a clear sectoral leader meant that the market lacked a catalyst to push higher.

5. Caution Ahead of Key Data Releases

Investors turned cautious ahead of upcoming domestic inflation data and corporate earnings reports. The release of monthly retail inflation figures is expected later this week, and any surprise could alter expectations for the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy path.

Additionally, the earnings season is underway, and market participants are waiting to see if companies can deliver on revenue and profit estimates. Uncertainty on both fronts kept buyers on the sidelines.

What to Watch Next

The market's direction in the coming sessions will hinge on global cues, FII flows, and the upcoming inflation print. If inflation comes in lower than expected, it could reignite hopes of a rate cut and push indices higher. On the other hand, any negative surprise may deepen the pullback.

Analysts also advise watching the earnings reports from key sectors like IT and banking, as these will set the tone for the rest of the quarter. For now, the market remains in a wait-and-watch mode, with the Nifty likely to find support at its 50-day moving average.
