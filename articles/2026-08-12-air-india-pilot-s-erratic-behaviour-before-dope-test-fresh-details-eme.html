Air India Pilot's Erratic Behaviour Before Dope Test: Fresh Details Emerge

📅 2026-08-12 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pilot showed erratic behaviour and needed help for dope test after Phuket-Delhi flight.
  • Flight experienced a rare flight-control event before a 300-foot altitude drop.
  • Ministry has demanded a review of dope test protocols following the incident.

Fresh Details on Pilot's Condition

New revelations have emerged about the Air India pilot at the centre of a marijuana controversy, describing behaviour that was erratic and concerning. According to sources, the pilot had to be physically assisted while undergoing a dope test following a Phuket-Delhi flight. The incident has intensified scrutiny on cockpit protocols and substance abuse checks.

The pilot, who has since failed a second dope test, reportedly showed signs of impairment that raised alarms among ground staff. Officials have not yet confirmed the full sequence of events, but the revelations have triggered a broader debate about aviation safety standards.

The Flight Incident

The controversy traces back to a Phuket-Delhi flight that experienced a rare flight-control event, leading to a sudden 300-foot altitude drop. Aviation experts say such events are uncommon and require immediate attention from the crew. The drop, while not catastrophic, has been described as serious enough to warrant a thorough investigation.

Following the incident, the pilot was subjected to a dope test, which reportedly came back positive for marijuana. A second test conducted later also confirmed the presence of the substance, according to The Times of India. The airline has not made an official statement on the test results.

Ministry Steps In

The Ministry of Civil Aviation has now demanded a comprehensive review of dope test protocols across airlines. This move comes after the marijuana row exposed potential gaps in the current testing framework. Aviation Minister Naidu, while meeting Air India CEO Wilson, emphasised that safety remains the topmost priority. "Told time and again, safety in aviation is topmost priority," Naidu said during the meeting.

The ministry's directive is expected to lead to stricter and more frequent testing for pilots and crew. Airlines may also be asked to implement random checks and enhance reporting mechanisms for any behavioural anomalies.

Industry Reaction

Industry observers note that substance abuse among flight crew is a rare but serious concern. The incident has prompted calls for better mental health support and monitoring of pilots. While the pilot has been grounded pending further action, questions remain about how such a lapse went unnoticed.

Air India has yet to comment on the pilot's status or the investigative findings. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) is likely to issue a detailed report after completing its inquiry.

What Happens Next

With the ministry demanding a protocol review, airlines will need to tighten their dope testing procedures in the coming weeks. The DGCA's final report on the flight-control event will be crucial in determining whether systemic changes are needed. For now, the focus remains on ensuring that such incidents do not recur, even as the investigation unfolds.
