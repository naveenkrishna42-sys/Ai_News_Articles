Chennai private college hikes fees, bars defaulters from classes

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The college has raised fees for the current academic year.
  • Students who have not paid the new fees are being stopped from attending classes.
  • Parents and students have expressed concerns over the sudden fee hike and the restriction.
  • The college administration has not yet issued an official statement.

Chennai: A private college in the city has sparked concern among students and parents after it increased its fees for the current academic year and began preventing defaulting students from attending classes. The move, which came into effect this week, has left many families scrambling to arrange funds.

Fee hike and attendance bar

The college, whose name has not been officially confirmed, has reportedly raised tuition fees by a significant margin. Students who have not paid the revised amount are being turned away from classrooms and laboratories, according to multiple students who spoke to The Hindu.

One student, seeking anonymity, said the administration informed them of the fee revision just days before the new term began. "We were told to pay the full amount by the first week, or we wouldn't be allowed to attend classes," the student said. "Many of us are from middle-class families. It's not easy to arrange that much money at short notice."

Parents in a fix

Parents have also expressed frustration, saying the college did not hold any consultation before deciding on the hike. Some have approached the college administration seeking more time, but they claim their requests have been turned down.

"We are not refusing to pay, but we need some breathing space," said a parent who did not wish to be named. "Stopping a student from attending class is a harsh step. It affects their studies and mental peace."

The issue has also raised questions about the broader affordability of private education in Tamil Nadu. With several colleges having increased fees in recent years, parents argue that the burden is becoming unsustainable.

College yet to respond

The college administration has not yet issued a formal statement on the matter. Repeated attempts to reach the principal and other officials were unsuccessful at the time of writing.

Education department officials in Chennai said they have not received any formal complaint yet. However, they indicated that if a complaint is filed, they will look into whether the college has followed the prescribed norms for fee revision.

Under current regulations, private colleges are required to notify the government about fee changes and justify the increase. But enforcement has often been lax, and many colleges continue to set fees unilaterally.

What happens next

Students and parents are now planning to approach the college management with a formal representation. If that fails, they may escalate the matter to the state education department or the Tamil Nadu government's fee regulatory committee.

The coming weeks will show whether the college is willing to negotiate or if the dispute will turn into a larger confrontation. For now, dozens of students are left waiting outside classroom doors, uncertain about their academic future.
