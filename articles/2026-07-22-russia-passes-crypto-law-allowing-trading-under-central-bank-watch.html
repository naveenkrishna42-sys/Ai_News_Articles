Russia Passes Crypto Law Allowing Trading Under Central Bank Watch

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Russia's State Duma passed a law legalizing crypto trading under central bank supervision.
  • The law permits trading of digital currencies classified as 'digital financial assets' but bans their use as payment for goods and services.
  • Trading platforms must be registered with the central bank and comply with AML and KYC regulations.
  • The legislation marks a shift from Russia's earlier restrictive stance on cryptocurrencies.

New Law Passed by State Duma

Russia's State Duma has passed a new law that legalizes cryptocurrency trading under tight oversight from the central bank. The legislation, reported by The Moscow Times, allows trading of digital currencies classified as 'digital financial assets.'

The move marks a significant shift in Russia's approach to cryptocurrencies. The central bank had previously pushed for an outright ban on crypto transactions, citing financial stability risks.

Key Provisions of the Law

The law permits trading but strictly prohibits using cryptocurrencies as payment for goods or services within Russia. All trading platforms must register with the central bank and comply with anti-money laundering and know-your-customer regulations.

These requirements aim to bring crypto trading under the same regulatory framework as traditional financial markets. The central bank retains authority to suspend trading if it detects market manipulation or other risks.

Impact on Investors and Businesses

For individual investors, the law provides a legal pathway to trade cryptocurrencies through registered exchanges. Businesses that want to accept crypto payments must still rely on foreign platforms or face legal hurdles.

Experts say the law could attract more institutional participation in Russia's crypto market. However, the payment ban limits the practical use of digital currencies for everyday transactions.

Shift From Earlier Stance

This legislation represents a departure from the central bank's earlier hardline position. In 2022, the bank proposed a complete ban on crypto issuance and trading, arguing that it threatened financial stability.

Since then, Russia has faced sweeping Western sanctions over its invasion of Ukraine. The government has increasingly explored alternative financial channels, including cryptocurrencies, to facilitate trade with partners like China and India.

Regulatory Framework in Detail

The law categorizes digital currencies into two groups: digital financial assets and digital rights. Only those classified as digital financial assets can be traded on registered platforms.

Platforms must maintain reserves, report transactions to the central bank, and implement systems to prevent fraud. Violations could result in license revocation or fines.

Lawmakers have not yet clarified how existing crypto exchanges operating from abroad will be treated under the new rules. Some analysts expect a transition period for foreign platforms to seek local registration.

What Happens Next

The law now awaits approval from the Federation Council and President Vladimir Putin's signature before it takes effect. Once enacted, the central bank will publish detailed regulations for trading platforms.

Market participants will watch how strictly the central bank enforces the new rules and whether Russia's crypto trading volume increases as a result.
