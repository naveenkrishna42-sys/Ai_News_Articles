Mumbai vendors with smart IDs still face eviction, allege hawkers

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Authorised street vendors in Mumbai say they are being evicted despite possessing smart IDs.
  • Vendors allege civic authorities are not recognising their legal status under the Street Vendors Act.
  • The issue highlights implementation gaps in Mumbai's street vending policy.

Mumbai's authorised street vendors are staring at an ironic predicament: they hold government-issued smart identity cards, yet they claim they are being pushed out of their vending spots by civic authorities. The vendors, who operate under the Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014, say the very document meant to protect them is being ignored on the ground.

Several vendors across the city have reported receiving eviction notices or facing removal drives, even as they display their smart IDs. The situation has sparked frustration among hawkers who believed the IDs would shield them from displacement.

The smart ID promise

Under the central law, municipal bodies are required to designate vending zones and issue identity cards to legitimate street vendors. In Mumbai, the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) has been issuing these smart cards as part of a survey and registration drive.

For many vendors, the card was a ticket to security after years of operating in the shadows. But the reality, they say, is different. "We have the card, yet the authorities treat us like encroachers," said a vendor who requested anonymity.

Eviction drives continue

The vendors allege that eviction drives are being carried out in various parts of the city, often targeting those who have been vending for decades. They argue that the smart IDs are not being recognised by ground-level officials, who continue to view all hawkers as illegal occupants of public space.

This has created a sense of betrayal among the vendor community, which had cooperated with the BMC's survey and registration process in good faith.

Legal and administrative gaps

Experts point to a mismatch between policy and implementation. While the smart ID is a legal document, its enforcement depends on the vigilance of local officials and the clarity of vending zone demarcation.

Many vendors operate in areas that have not yet been formally designated as vending zones, leaving them vulnerable despite holding valid IDs. The BMC has not yet responded to specific allegations of ignoring smart cards during eviction drives.

Vendors demand action

Hawkers' unions have urged the BMC to immediately halt evictions of smart ID holders and to expedite the process of notifying vending zones across the city. They have also called for a grievance redressal mechanism that can act swiftly when a vendor's legal status is questioned.

Without such steps, they warn, the smart ID will remain a piece of plastic with no real power, and the trust of thousands of vendors will be further eroded.

The coming weeks are likely to see increased pressure on the BMC from both vendors and civil society groups to clarify its stance. The question is whether the civic body will enforce its own certification or continue to let ground-level action undermine it.
