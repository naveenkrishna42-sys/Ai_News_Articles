Tharoor: Congress must introspect why paper leak protest fell flat

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tharoor admits Congress's protest on paper leaks lacked the impact of the CJP's campaign
  • He stresses the need to check where the party failed in connecting with students
  • Youth will be the largest voting bloc in 2029, making their concerns crucial

NEW DELHI: Congress MP Shashi Tharoor has conceded that his party's protest over alleged paper leaks did not strike a chord with students the way the Campaign for Justice in Public Examinations (CJP) managed to, prompting a call for internal introspection.

Speaking to reporters, Tharoor acknowledged the disconnect, saying, "We need to check where we failed." The admission comes amid growing scrutiny of the Congress's outreach strategies, especially with the youth electorate set to play a decisive role in future polls.

What the CJP did differently

The CJP, a student-led movement, has managed to galvanise widespread support across states, with protests drawing thousands of young demonstrators. Their demands for transparency in recruitment exams and swift action against leak culprits have resonated far beyond campus walls.

In contrast, the Congress's own agitation on the same issue appeared muted. Tharoor pointed out that the resonance of the CJP's protests should serve as a lesson. "Their ability to mobilise students shows the depth of anger and expectation among the youth," he said.

A 'rude wake-up call'

Tharoor described the development as a "rude wake-up call" for the political class. He noted that young voters are no longer passive spectators but are actively shaping narratives around governance and accountability.

With the 2029 general election on the horizon, the Congress MP stressed that youth will constitute the largest voting bloc. "If we cannot address their concerns credibly, we risk becoming irrelevant to their aspirations," he warned.

Questions over leadership visibility

The remarks come at a time when critics are questioning the visibility of senior Congress leaders, including Rahul Gandhi, in grassroots movements. Political observers have drawn comparisons between the party's handling of the issue and the BJP's response, noting that standards of accountability often differ.

Tharoor did not directly address these comparisons but emphasised the need for consistent engagement. "We cannot pick and choose when to stand with the people," he said, underlining that the party must be present where it matters most.

What lies ahead for Congress

The Congress has historically positioned itself as a party of the masses, but recent protests have exposed gaps in its ability to channel public anger into political capital. Tharoor's candid admission signals a possible recalibration of strategy.

Party insiders suggest that internal reviews are already underway to identify weak spots in communication and on-ground coordination. However, no official roadmap has been announced yet.

As the youth bulge grows, the Congress faces a critical test: translating its legacy into a language that resonates with a generation that demands action over rhetoric. Tharoor's words may well be the first step toward that reckoning.

Observers will watch closely whether the party translates this introspection into tangible outreach programmes ahead of state elections due over the next two years.
