Sensex, Nifty Open Lower as Crude Nears $90; Metals Gain

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty opened lower in early trade
  • Crude oil prices near $90 per barrel weigh on sentiment
  • Metal stocks outperform amid positive global cues

Mumbai: Indian equity benchmarks opened in the red on Wednesday, tracking weak global cues as crude oil prices inched closer to the $90 per barrel mark. The BSE Sensex fell over 200 points in early trade, while the NSE Nifty slipped below its previous close, dragged by energy and banking stocks.

Investors remained cautious ahead of key domestic and global economic data, with rising oil prices stoking concerns about inflation and its impact on corporate margins. However, metal stocks bucked the trend, trading higher on the back of firm global commodity prices and improved demand outlook.

Crude Oil Pressures Sentiment

Brent crude futures hovered near $89.8 per barrel, just a step away from the psychologically significant $90 level. The uptick in oil prices, driven by supply constraints and geopolitical tensions, has raised fears of imported inflation for India, a major crude importer.

Higher crude prices typically widen India's trade deficit and put pressure on the rupee, which in turn affects foreign fund flows. Market analysts said the oil price movement would be a key monitorable for the domestic market in the coming sessions.

Metal Stocks Outperform

In contrast to the broader market weakness, metal stocks gained ground in early trade. Strong demand from China and supply-side disruptions in key producing regions have supported base metal prices globally.

Shares of Hindalco, Tata Steel, and JSW Steel were among the top gainers on the Nifty, rising between 1 percent and 2 percent in early deals. The Nifty Metal index outperformed the benchmark, rising nearly 1 percent.

Banking, Energy Shares Drag

On the losing side, banking and energy stocks led the decline. Heavyweights like Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank traded lower, weighing on the indices. The Nifty Bank index fell over 0.5 percent in early trade.

Auto stocks also came under pressure, with rising input costs and fuel prices threatening to dent demand in the world's fourth-largest auto market. Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra were among the prominent losers.

Broader Market Cautious

The broader market reflected the cautious mood, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices trading flat to marginally lower. Market breadth was slightly negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the previous session, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) provided some buying support. Analysts said the market could remain volatile in the near term until clarity emerges on the oil price trajectory and global interest rates.

Global Cues Mixed

Overnight, US markets ended mixed, with tech stocks showing resilience while energy and industrial shares faced pressure. Asian markets opened lower, with Japan's Nikkei and China's Shanghai Composite trading in the red, tracking weak US futures.

Investors are also awaiting the release of US inflation data later this week, which could influence the Federal Reserve's policy stance. Any surprise in the data could trigger sharp moves in global equities and commodities.

In the currency market, the Indian rupee opened marginally weaker against the US dollar, reflecting the rise in crude prices and persistent outflows. The rupee was trading around the 83.5 mark in early deals.

Looking ahead, traders will keep a close watch on crude oil inventory data from the US and any commentary from OPEC+ officials on supply policy. Domestic cues, including corporate earnings and GST collections, will also guide market direction in the coming days.
