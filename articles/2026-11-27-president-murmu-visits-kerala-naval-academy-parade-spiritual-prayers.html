President Murmu Visits Kerala: Naval Academy Parade, Spiritual Prayers

Introduction

President Droupadi Murmu commenced a two‑day tour of Kerala on 27‑28 November 2026, blending defence diplomacy with cultural outreach. The highlight was the passing‑out parade of newly commissioned officers at the Ezhimala Naval Academy, followed by prayers at Guruvayur Temple and a stop at the birthplace of Adi Shankara. The itinerary underscored the administration’s focus on naval readiness, religious inclusivity and the promotion of Kerala’s heritage on a national stage.

Why Ezhimala Matters

The Ezhimala Naval Academy, situated on the western coast of Kannur, is the Indian Navy’s primary training hub. It has been the venue for the commissioning of more than 6,000 officers since 2007. The annual parade is a ceremonial affirmation of the Navy’s traditions and a public showcase of maritime professionalism. President Murmu’s presence lent political weight to the event and signalled the central government’s continued investment in naval capabilities.

President Murmu’s attendance is more than ceremonial; it signals a strategic emphasis on maritime security in the Indian Ocean Region (IOR). The Navy’s flagship training centre is poised to produce officers equipped for the expanding Indo‑Pacific partnership. The parade’s scale—48 officers, 1,200 enlisted personnel, and 500 civilian staff—highlights the breadth of the training programme. Analysts note that the event coincided with a recent upgrade to the academy’s simulation labs, which now include AI‑driven scenario planning modules.

Murmu’s prayers at the temple were followed by a brief address to devotees, stressing the importance of secular governance. The visit to the birthplace of Adi Shankara—a seminal figure in Advaita Vedanta—underscored a commitment to preserving philosophical heritage. Such engagements reinforce the idea that national leaders can bridge modern statecraft with traditional values.
