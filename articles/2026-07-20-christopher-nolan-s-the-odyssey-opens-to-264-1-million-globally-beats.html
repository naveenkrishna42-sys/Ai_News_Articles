Christopher Nolan's 'The Odyssey' Opens to $264.1 Million Globally, Beats 'Dark Knight Rises'

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Global debut of $264.1 million, including $124 million from the domestic box office.
  • Surpassed 'The Dark Knight Rises' opening weekend record for a Nolan film.
  • The film is the third-biggest domestic debut of the year so far.
  • Scholars have raised questions about the film's historical and mythological accuracy.
  • The Guardian reports Nolan made changes to the original Homeric epic, including elements of sex and violence.

A Record-Breaking Voyage

Christopher Nolan's latest epic, 'The Odyssey,' has sailed into cinemas with a thunderous roar. The film earned a staggering USD 264.1 million globally in its opening weekend. This figure includes a massive USD 124 million from the domestic box office alone.

The debut has officially dethroned Nolan's own 'The Dark Knight Rises' as his biggest opening weekend ever. It now stands as the third-biggest domestic opening of the year, trailing only two other blockbuster releases. The numbers signal a clear appetite for Nolan's audacious vision of Homer's ancient tale.

Nolan's Bold Retelling

Early reviews have been polarising but passionate. The New York Times called it 'Passion in Every Frame,' praising the director's commitment to visual storytelling. However, The Guardian has pointed out that Nolan made significant changes to the source material. The article notes alterations involving sex, beds, and grisly executions — elements that were either invented, altered, or omitted from the original epic.

These creative liberties have not gone unnoticed by academics. The Associated Press reports that scholars are questioning the historical and mythological accuracy of the film. Some argue that Nolan's interpretation strays too far from the spirit of Homer's work, while others see it as a necessary update for modern audiences.

Mixed Reception from Scholars

The debate over authenticity has become a central talking point. Classicists have pointed out that the original 'Odyssey' is a poem about cunning and endurance, not necessarily the action-packed spectacle Nolan has delivered. One scholar told AP that while the film is visually stunning, it risks reducing the epic's nuanced themes to mere spectacle.

Nolan, known for his meticulous research and technical precision, has not yet publicly responded to these critiques. The film's production design and special effects have been widely praised, but the core question remains: can a Hollywood blockbuster faithfully adapt a 2,700-year-old poem?

What Comes Next

With such a strong opening, 'The Odyssey' is poised for a long run in theatres. The film's success could pave the way for more ambitious literary adaptations from major studios. All eyes will now be on its performance in the coming weeks and whether word-of-mouth can sustain the initial hype.
