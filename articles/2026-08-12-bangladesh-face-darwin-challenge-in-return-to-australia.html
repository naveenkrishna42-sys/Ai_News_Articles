Bangladesh face Darwin challenge in return to Australia

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bangladesh play their first Test in Australia since 2017, in Darwin.
  • Josh Hazlewood returns to lead Australia's pace attack, edging out Scott Boland.
  • Najmul Hossain Shanto highlights the importance of adapting to Australian conditions.

A long-awaited return

After a gap of nearly a decade, Bangladesh will step onto Australian soil for a Test match. The venue is Darwin, a city that has its own cricketing quirks and challenges. For the Tigers, it is a return to a place where they have historically struggled, but also a chance to rewrite a narrative.

The last time Bangladesh played a Test in Australia was in 2017, and the results were lopsided. Since then, both teams have evolved. Australia remain a powerhouse at home, but Bangladesh have shown glimpses of resilience in overseas conditions. The upcoming Test, however, presents a unique set of obstacles.

Darwin's unique challenge

Darwin is not a regular Australian venue. The pitch at the Marrara Cricket Ground is known for its pace and bounce, but also for its unpredictability. The heat and humidity in the Top End can be draining, especially for visiting teams not accustomed to such conditions. Bangladesh's players will need to acclimatise quickly if they are to compete.

Historically, subcontinent teams have found it tough in Darwin. The ball comes onto the bat faster than in Dhaka or Chattogram, and the bounce can catch batsmen off guard. Bangladesh's batting lineup, which relies heavily on spin in home conditions, will have to adjust to the pace-friendly track.

Australia's XI takes shape

For Australia, the selection puzzle has been largely resolved. Josh Hazlewood returns to the side after a period on the sidelines, and his inclusion has pushed Scott Boland out of the playing XI. Hazlewood's control and accuracy make him a potent threat on any surface, and he will lead a pace attack that also features Pat Cummins and Mitchell Starc.

Alex Carey retains his spot at No. 6, a decision that had been under scrutiny. The selectors have backed the wicketkeeper-batter to deliver with the bat, even as his recent returns have been modest. Meanwhile, the top order looks settled, with the likes of Usman Khawaja and Steve Smith expected to pile on runs.

Shanto's blueprint for success

Bangladesh captain Najmul Hossain Shanto has identified the key to competing against Australia: discipline with the ball and patience with the bat. In a recent interaction, Shanto stressed the importance of building partnerships and not gifting wickets to a quality attack. He also acknowledged that his bowlers must hit the right lengths consistently to exploit any assistance from the pitch.

The Tigers' spin department, led by Taijul Islam and Mehidy Hasan Miraz, could play a crucial role. If the Darwin pitch offers turn later in the match, Bangladesh might have a window of opportunity. But that is a big if, given Australia's batting depth and their familiarity with home conditions.

What to watch

As the Test approaches, all eyes will be on how Bangladesh's top order handles the new ball. Openers will face a stern test against Hazlewood and Starc, and their ability to see off the early spells could set the tone. For Australia, the focus will be on maintaining intensity and not allowing Bangladesh to settle.

The match is part of a longer series, and both teams will be eager to start on a positive note. Bangladesh, in particular, will see this as an opportunity to prove that they can compete outside the subcontinent. The conditions in Darwin are unforgiving, but they also offer a level playing field for those willing to adapt.

With the first ball just days away, the cricketing world will be watching to see if Bangladesh can unravel the mysteries of Darwin or if Australia's pace battery will prove too hot to handle.
