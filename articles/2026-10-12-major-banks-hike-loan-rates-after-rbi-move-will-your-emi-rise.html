Major Banks Hike Loan Rates After RBI Move: Will Your EMI Rise?

Key points
  • Banks hiked MCLR and repo-linked rates after RBI's October 2026 policy
  • Home loan EMIs may increase by up to 0.4% monthly per crore
  • Auto and personal loan borrowers face immediate rate revisions

RBI Policy Triggers Across-the-Board Rate Hike

Following the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) October 2026 monetary policy committee (MPC) decision to maintain the repo rate at 6.50% while signalling a hawkish stance, major Indian banks including State Bank of India, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and Axis Bank have revised their marginal cost of funds-based lending rates (MCLR) and repo-linked lending rates (RLLR) upward by 10-25 basis points (bps).

This marks the first broad-based rate hike cycle since 2022, driven by persistent retail inflation and sticky core prices. The RBI's action aims to anchor long-term inflation expectations, but the immediate fallout is higher borrowing costs for millions of retail and commercial borrowers.

How Your Home Loan EMI Will Change

Home loan borrowers on floating rates will see their EMIs rise within one to two months of the rate revision. For a standard 20-year home loan of ₹1 crore at an existing rate of 8.50%, a 25 bps increase would push the EMI from approximately ₹97,000 to ₹99,000 per month—a rise of nearly ₹2,000 per month.

Impact on Other Retail Loans

Auto loans and personal loans face even steeper hikes due to their shorter tenures and higher sensitivity to rate changes. Lenders are also tightening internal benchmarks for processing fees and prepayment charges, making early loan closure less attractive than before.

Borrowers should review their loan agreements for reset clauses—most banks reset MCLR every quarter, meaning the full impact may be felt over the next two to three reset cycles.

What Borrowers Can Do Now

Borrowers should immediately contact their lenders to understand the revised rates and calculate the new EMI using online calculators or bank portals. Those nearing the end of their loan tenure may benefit from switching to fixed-rate loans or prepaying before the next reset cycle.

Investors and salaried individuals should also re-evaluate their financial planning, especially if they were considering new loans for property, vehicles, or weddings. Locking in rates early or exploring alternative financing options like credit cooperatives or NBFCs may offer temporary relief, though these often come with stricter terms.

  • Pros: Rate hikes help control inflation and stabilize the economy long-term.
  • Cons: Immediate burden on borrowers; reduced demand for big-ticket purchases like homes and cars.
  • Key Insight: Floating-rate loan holders must brace for 2-3 more hikes if inflation remains above the RBI's 4% target.
  • Action Tip: Prepay or switch to fixed-rate loans before the next MCLR reset to lock in current EMIs.
