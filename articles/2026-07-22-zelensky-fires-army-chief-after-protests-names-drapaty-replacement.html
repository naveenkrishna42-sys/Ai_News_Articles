Zelensky Fires Army Chief After Protests, Names Drapaty Replacement

📅 2026-07-22 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Zelensky dismisses army chief after days of protest in Ukraine
  • Mykhailo Drapaty appointed as new military commander
  • Move signals shift in war strategy and leadership style

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has dismissed his top army commander, ending a tense standoff that followed days of public protests. The decision, announced late Tuesday, brings in a younger general to lead the country's war effort against Russia.

Mykhailo Drapaty, a relatively fresh face in Ukraine's senior military ranks, has been named the new army chief. The change marks a significant shift in Kyiv's approach as the conflict grinds into its fourth year.

Protests That Triggered a Change

For nearly a week, demonstrators had gathered in central Kyiv and other cities. They demanded accountability over battlefield setbacks and criticized what they saw as outdated tactics. The protests grew louder after a series of territorial losses in the eastern Donetsk region.

Zelensky's office had initially stayed silent. But mounting pressure from veterans' groups and families of soldiers forced his hand. The sacking came after a late-night meeting with his top security aides.

"The president believes fresh leadership is essential for the next phase of the war," a senior official told local media, speaking on condition of anonymity.

Who Is Mykhailo Drapaty

Drapaty, 44, previously commanded ground forces in the northern sector. He is known for integrating drone warfare and electronic warfare units into conventional operations. Analysts describe him as a reformist who favors decentralized command.

He replaces a general whose tenure had become synonymous with attritional trench warfare. The outgoing chief had overseen the grueling battle for Bakhmut and the failed counteroffensive last summer. Critics accused him of sticking to Soviet-era tactics that led to heavy casualties.

Drapaty's appointment signals a pivot toward more mobile, technology-driven operations. Western military advisors have long pushed for such changes.

What This Means for the War

The reshuffle comes at a critical juncture. Russian forces have been pressing along several front lines, exploiting what they see as Ukrainian exhaustion. The change in command could either revitalize the military or cause further disruption.

Zelensky has now fired or replaced multiple senior officials since the war began. The defense minister was swapped last year. The head of the security service was changed earlier. Each move reflects the president's growing frustration with the pace of reform.

"This is not just about one general," said a military analyst in Kyiv. "It's about resetting the entire command culture. Drapaty represents a break from the past."

Ukraine's allies have largely refrained from commenting publicly. Privately, U.S. and European officials have expressed cautious support for the change, hoping it will improve coordination on the battlefield.

The coming weeks will test whether the new leadership can stabilize the front lines. Drapaty faces the immediate challenge of halting Russian advances in the Kharkiv region while preparing for a possible winter offensive.
