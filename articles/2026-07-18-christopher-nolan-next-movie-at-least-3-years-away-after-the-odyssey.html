Christopher Nolan: Next movie 'at least' 3 years away after 'The Odyssey'

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Christopher Nolan confirmed a minimum three-year gap before his next movie
  • He described feeling overwhelmed at times while making 'The Odyssey'
  • Nolan emphasized the need for risk-taking in filmmaking

Christopher Nolan has a message for fans hoping for a quick follow-up to his epic 'The Odyssey': be patient. The director confirmed it will be 'at least' three years before his next project hits screens.

The British filmmaker, fresh off the massive success of his adaptation of Homer's ancient Greek poem, opened up about the grueling process of bringing Odysseus's journey to life. In an interview with The Guardian, Nolan admitted there were moments when he questioned his own ambition.

'Bitten off more than I could chew'

'At times I felt I'd bitten off more than I could chew,' Nolan said. The director described sweeping the Oscars and making 'The Odyssey' as a period of intense pressure. The film, which blends myth with historical reality, required a scale of production that tested even his seasoned crew.

Nolan's comments come after pre-release controversies surrounding the film. A report from Telegraph India asked whether those debates ultimately mattered. The box office numbers suggest they did not.

Risk-taking in an era of safe bets

In a separate interview with the BBC, titled 'We need to take risks,' Nolan argued that the film industry must embrace uncertainty. 'The safest thing is often the most dangerous creatively,' he said. His own career has been built on that philosophy, from 'Inception' to 'Interstellar.'

'The Odyssey' is no exception. The film traces Odysseus's journey across ancient maps where myth and geography overlap, as noted by Scroll.in. Nolan insisted on practical effects and location shooting, even when digital alternatives were cheaper.

Life after the epic

Beyond filmmaking, Nolan has found a new companion. He revealed in The Guardian interview that he got a puppy during the making of 'The Odyssey.' The dog, he said, helped him decompress after long days on set.

The director now plans to take a deliberate break. 'At least three years,' he reiterated. Nolan did not reveal what genre or subject he might explore next. He only said he wants to 'recharge and find a story that scares me a little again.'

For now, the film world watches. 'The Odyssey' has cemented Nolan's place as a director who can blend intellectual ambition with mass appeal. But the man behind the camera seems in no rush to repeat the feat.

What to watch: Whether Nolan returns to science fiction, historical drama, or something entirely unexpected. His next move will likely be revealed only when the story demands it — not a moment sooner.
