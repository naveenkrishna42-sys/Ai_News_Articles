New market timings from Monday; F&O trading extended till 3:40 pm

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • New market timings effective from Monday, August 3
  • F&O trading extended till 3:40 pm; equity cash segment closes at 3:30 pm
  • Closing auction introduced for all stocks to prevent manipulation
  • Sebi aims to bring India in line with global market practices

Come Monday, Indian stock markets will operate on a new schedule, with significant changes to the trading day. The equity cash segment will close at 3:30 pm, but derivatives trading—futures and options—will continue till 3:40 pm. This extension, announced by market regulator Sebi, is part of a broader overhaul of the closing price mechanism.

The new timings, effective from August 3, mark the first major shift in market hours in over a decade. Earlier, all segments closed at 3:30 pm. The additional ten minutes for F&O trading is designed to allow smoother convergence between cash and derivatives markets, especially during the closing auction.

Closing auction for all stocks

The most significant change is the introduction of a closing auction for all securities in the cash market. Previously, the closing price was determined based on the volume-weighted average price in the last 30 minutes of trading. This method was often susceptible to manipulation, as traders could place large orders at the fag end of the session to influence the closing price.

Under the new mechanism, a 15-minute closing auction will be held for all stocks, starting at 3:15 pm. During this period, orders are collected but not immediately matched. The final closing price will be determined by the equilibrium price that maximises tradable volume. This is a significant departure from the earlier practice, where only stocks in the F&O segment and top 500 companies by market capitalisation had a closing auction.

How the new schedule works

  • 3:15 pm to 3:25 pm: Order collection for closing auction (no cancellations allowed after 3:20 pm).
  • 3:25 pm to 3:30 pm: Order matching and trade confirmation.
  • 3:30 pm: Cash market closes; closing price published.
  • 3:30 pm to 3:40 pm: F&O trading continues for final price discovery.

For traders, this means the last fifteen minutes of the cash session will see reduced liquidity as orders move to the auction. However, the extension for F&O provides an additional window for hedging and arbitrage opportunities.

Why the change?

Sebi has been pushing for reforms to make the Indian market more transparent and aligned with global standards. The closing price is a critical reference point for mutual funds, index funds, and institutional investors who value their portfolios at the end of the day. A manipulated closing price can distort net asset values (NAVs) and affect millions of investors.

Recent studies, including those by Bloomberg, have highlighted how arbitrageurs exploited the old mechanism. By placing large sell orders in the final minutes, they could push the closing price down, buy at that price, and profit from the subsequent rebound. The new auction mechanism is expected to make such practices difficult, as the price is determined by genuine supply and demand.

Impact on traders and investors

For retail investors, the change may initially seem confusing, but the underlying intent is protective. The closing auction ensures a fairer price discovery, reducing the risk of sudden, artificial swings at the end of the day. For active F&O traders, the extra ten minutes allows better alignment between the cash and derivatives segments, potentially reducing arbitrage risks.

However, some market participants have expressed concerns about operational challenges. Brokers and trading platforms will need to update their systems to handle the extended F&O hours and the new auction process. Sebi has advised all stakeholders to be prepared, and exchanges have conducted mock sessions in the past week to ensure a smooth transition.

Global context

India is not alone in this shift. Markets like the US and Europe have long used closing auctions to determine official closing prices. The move brings Indian exchanges in line with international best practices, which could also make the market more attractive to foreign institutional investors.

The extension of F&O trading till 3:40 pm is a unique feature, though. It provides a buffer for traders to react to the closing cash price before the derivatives market shuts. This could lead to more efficient pricing of futures and options, benefiting all participants.

As the market opens on Monday, all eyes will be on how the new mechanism functions in real-time. Traders and investors are advised to familiarise themselves with the new schedule and adjust their strategies accordingly. The first few sessions may see some volatility as the market adapts.

Going forward, the success of this overhaul will depend on seamless execution and market acceptance. If the closing auction delivers on its promise of reduced manipulation, it could pave the way for further reforms in Indian market microstructure.
