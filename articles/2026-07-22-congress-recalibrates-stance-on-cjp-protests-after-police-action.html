Congress recalibrates stance on CJP protests after police action

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Congress recalibrates its position on youth agitation after police crackdown on CJP protest
  • Shashi Tharoor says students betrayed by system, slams handling of protests
  • Opposition and government spar over NEET debate in Rajya Sabha, leading to repeated adjournments

The Congress party has recalibrated its stance on the ongoing youth-led protests against the Common University Entrance Test (CUET) and paper leaks, after a police crackdown on demonstrators at Jantar Mantar in New Delhi. The shift comes as senior leader Shashi Tharoor publicly criticised the Centre's handling of the agitation, calling it a 'death of democracy'.

The protests, organised by the Confederation of Indian University Students (CIUS) under the banner 'CJP' (Common University Entrance Test, JEE, and other exams), have gained momentum over the past week. Students allege systemic failures in exam conduct, including repeated paper leaks and unfair cancellation policies.

Police action and political fallout

On Sunday evening, Delhi Police removed hundreds of students from Jantar Mantar, using water cannons and batons to disperse the crowd. Several protesters were detained briefly. The government defended the action, citing a Supreme Court order that restricts protests at the venue to specific hours.

The Congress, which had initially expressed solidarity with the students, now appears to be treading cautiously. Party sources indicate a desire to avoid being seen as endorsing lawlessness while still backing the students' grievances. 'We support their right to peaceful protest, but we also respect the rule of law,' a senior Congress leader said, speaking on condition of anonymity.

Tharoor leads the charge

Shashi Tharoor, the Congress MP from Thiruvananthapuram, has emerged as the party's most vocal critic of the government's approach. In a statement on Tuesday, he said, 'Students have been betrayed by the system. Instead of addressing their legitimate concerns, the government is cracking down on them.'

Tharoor also wrote an op-ed in The Indian Express, arguing that 'when the streets burn, Parliament cannot act blind'. He accused the Centre of prioritising political optics over substantive reform in the education sector. The article drew sharp reactions from BJP leaders, who accused Tharoor of encouraging anarchy.

NEET debate stalls Parliament

Meanwhile, the Rajya Sabha witnessed repeated adjournments on Tuesday as the Opposition and government clashed over a proposed debate on the NEET exam controversy. Opposition parties, led by the Congress, demanded a discussion on the paper leaks that have affected lakhs of students across the country.

Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan countered that the government had already taken action, including setting up a high-level committee to review exam security. 'The Opposition is only interested in disruption, not solutions,' he said. The House was adjourned twice before lunch, with no consensus reached.

The Congress has now said it will launch a nationwide campaign, 'Shiksha Bachao, Nyay Do', to mobilise students and parents. Party president Mallikarjun Kharge is expected to address a rally in Delhi later this week.

What to watch: The Congress campaign could test the government's resolve on exam reforms. A larger student turnout in the coming days may force either side to blink.
