Centre Likely to Send FCRA Amendment Bill to Joint Parliamentary Panel

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Centre likely to send FCRA Amendment Bill to JPC for scrutiny
  • Opposition continues to demand complete withdrawal of the Bill
  • Government signals openness to further parliamentary review amid pushback

New Delhi: In a significant shift, the central government has indicated it is open to sending the contentious Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill to a Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) for further scrutiny. The move comes amid sustained opposition pressure, with several parties demanding the Bill be withdrawn entirely.

Government Signals Flexibility

Sources familiar with the development said the government is actively considering referring the Bill to a JPC, a mechanism that allows members from both Houses of Parliament to examine legislation in detail. The signal, though not officially confirmed, marks a departure from the government's earlier stance of pushing the Bill through without such a review.

The Bill, which seeks to amend the Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act, has been a flashpoint in Parliament. Critics argue that the proposed changes could stifle civil society and non-governmental organisations, while the government maintains that the amendments are aimed at ensuring transparency and accountability in the receipt and use of foreign funds.

Opposition Demands Withdrawal

Opposition parties, however, have remained unyielding, insisting that mere referral to a JPC is insufficient. Many have called for the complete withdrawal of the Bill, contending that its provisions are draconian and would severely curtail the functioning of NGOs and voluntary organisations across the country.

Leaders from several parties have argued that the Bill, in its current form, could be misused to target dissent and silence civil society voices. They have pointed to provisions that, in their view, grant excessive powers to the executive without adequate judicial oversight.

What the Bill Proposes

While the full text of the amendments has been circulated, the core changes relate to the regulation of foreign contributions, including stricter reporting requirements and enhanced powers for authorities to scrutinise recipients. The government has defended these provisions as necessary to prevent the misuse of foreign funds for activities detrimental to national interest.

Officials have not yet elaborated on the specific timelines or the composition of the proposed JPC, but sources suggest that a formal announcement could come in the coming days. The referral, if it happens, would likely be made through a motion in either House of Parliament.

Looking Ahead

The coming week is likely to be crucial, as Parliament is expected to take up the Bill for discussion. If the government indeed refers it to a JPC, the Bill's passage would be deferred, allowing for a more prolonged examination of its provisions and potential amendments.

Observers will be watching closely to see whether the government's apparent flexibility translates into a concrete decision, and whether the opposition's demand for outright withdrawal gains further traction. The outcome could set the tone for legislative engagement in the remainder of the session.
