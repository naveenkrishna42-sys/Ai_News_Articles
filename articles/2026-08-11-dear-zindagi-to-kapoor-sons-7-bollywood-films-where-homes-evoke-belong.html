Dear Zindagi to Kapoor & Sons: 7 Bollywood films where homes evoke belonging

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bollywood films often use homes as emotional anchors, reflecting characters' inner lives.
  • Architectural Digest India lists seven films where houses become central to the narrative.
  • Titles include Dear Zindagi, Kapoor & Sons, and others where spaces mirror familial bonds.
  • The article highlights how set design and architecture contribute to storytelling.

In Bollywood, a house is rarely just a structure of bricks and mortar. It is a silent character, a witness to secrets, celebrations, and quiet heartbreaks. From the sprawling ancestral mansions of Kapoor & Sons to the compact, sunlit apartment in Dear Zindagi, homes in Hindi cinema often carry the emotional weight of the story. They are not mere backdrops; they are active participants that shape relationships and mirror the turmoil or peace within the characters.

Architectural Digest India recently spotlighted seven such films where the home transcends its physical form to evoke a deep sense of belonging. The list is a reminder that in Indian storytelling, the walls we live within often define who we become.

The House as a Character

In Shakun Batra's Kapoor & Sons, the crumbling Coonoor bungalow is almost a living relic of the Kapoor family's fractured past. Its peeling paint and creaky staircases are not flaws but metaphors for the family's buried resentments. The house holds together a family that is falling apart, and its eventual sale in the film feels like a loss of identity.

Similarly, Dear Zindagi uses Alia Bhatt's character's rented apartment in Mumbai as a canvas for her emotional journey. The space is temporary, much like her state of mind. Yet it becomes a site of healing, where conversations with her therapist (Shah Rukh Khan) unravel her anxieties. The home here is not about permanence but about finding comfort in the present.

Ancestral Homes and Modern Tensions

The list also includes films like Dil Dhadakne Do, where the cruise ship doubles as a floating family estate, and Piku, where the chaotic Delhi household of Amitabh Bachchan's character is both a burden and a source of love. These homes are not just spaces; they are repositories of generational baggage. The physical clutter in Piku's house mirrors the emotional clutter of the father-daughter relationship.

In Queen, Kangana Ranaut's character leaves her modest Delhi home, but it is her memories of that small, cluttered house that ground her as she travels the world. The home becomes a touchstone for her transformation. The films selected by Architectural Digest India highlight how set design in Bollywood has evolved from mere decoration to a narrative tool that deepens the audience's connection to the characters.

Why This Matters for Indian Cinema

Indian audiences have always had a visceral connection to the idea of home. Whether it is the joint family setups of the 1990s or the urban, isolated apartments of today, homes in films reflect the changing social fabric of the country. The selection by Architectural Digest India underscores a growing appreciation for production design as a storytelling device. Filmmakers are now paying more attention to how a home's architecture—its light, its space, its decay—can convey what dialogues cannot.

This trend is not just about aesthetics. It is about authenticity. A character's home tells you their class, their aspirations, and their emotional state. In Kapoor & Sons, the grandeur of the house contrasts with the emptiness inside the family. In Dear Zindagi, the simplicity of the apartment reflects the protagonist's search for stability. These choices are deliberate and effective.

The Future of Homes in Bollywood

As streaming platforms push for more realistic storytelling, the role of homes in films is likely to grow. Directors are collaborating closely with production designers to create spaces that are not just visually appealing but emotionally resonant. The homes we see on screen are no longer just locations; they are extensions of the characters themselves.

What remains to be seen is how this trend evolves with the rise of virtual production and AI-generated sets. Will the emotional weight of a home survive in a digitally constructed world? For now, the films on Architectural Digest India's list remind us that a home, no matter how simple, can be the most powerful actor in a story.
