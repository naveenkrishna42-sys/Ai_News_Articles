India Rejects Former Japan Minister’s Bullet Train Remarks as ‘At Variance With Facts’

📅 2026-07-18 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's Ministry of External Affairs rejected former Japanese minister's remarks on Shinkansen project delays.
  • The ex-minister had accused India of 'sheer recklessness' in executing the Mumbai-Ahmedabad bullet train project.
  • MEA said the project is progressing well and that the former minister's comments are 'at variance with facts'.

New Delhi: India has firmly rejected remarks made by a former Japanese minister criticising delays in the Mumbai-Ahmedabad bullet train project. The Ministry of External Affairs said the comments were 'at variance with facts' and accused the former minister of making reckless statements.

The former Japanese minister had recently taken to social media to vent his frustration over the slow pace of the project. He called the delays a result of 'sheer recklessness' by Indian authorities. His remarks quickly went viral, drawing sharp reactions from the Indian government.

MEA hits back

In a strongly worded response, the Ministry of External Affairs said the former minister's claims did not reflect the reality on the ground. Officials pointed out that the Mumbai-Ahmedabad High Speed Rail corridor, based on Japan's Shinkansen technology, is making steady progress despite several challenges.

“The remarks are at variance with facts. The project is progressing well, and both sides remain committed to its timely completion,” a spokesperson said. The MEA did not elaborate on specific timelines or stages of the project that have been completed.

Project delays and challenges

The Mumbai-Ahmedabad bullet train project, first announced in 2017, has faced multiple hurdles. Land acquisition delays, the COVID-19 pandemic, and changes in the alignment due to local opposition have pushed back the original 2023 completion deadline.

The project is now expected to be operational by 2030. Japan has extended a soft loan of nearly Rs 88,000 crore for the project, which is being executed by the National High Speed Rail Corporation Limited. Officials say about 30 per cent of the civil work has been completed so far.

What the former minister said

The former Japanese minister, who held a key portfolio during the project's launch, expressed disappointment over the lack of progress. He said the Indian side had failed to honour commitments, leading to cost escalations and wasted opportunities.

His comments, posted on X, were widely shared. Some Japanese media reports suggested that frustration is growing in Tokyo over the slow execution. However, the Indian government has maintained that the relationship remains strong and that the project is a symbol of India-Japan strategic ties.

Diplomatic tightrope

The public spat has put the spotlight on the complex dynamics between New Delhi and Tokyo. While both sides have described the project as a flagship initiative, the delays have occasionally strained diplomatic patience.

Analysts say the former minister's remarks, though blunt, reflect a broader concern in Japan about the viability of large infrastructure projects in India. However, the MEA's swift rebuttal signals that India will not accept criticism from former officials without setting the record straight.

The Mumbai-Ahmedabad High Speed Rail corridor is expected to cut travel time between the two cities from over six hours to about two hours. Once completed, it will be India's first bullet train service.

What to watch: The Indian government is expected to release a detailed progress report on the project in the coming weeks. Whether the former minister's outburst will lead to a formal review by Japan's current administration remains unclear.
