Karnataka to hold HIV camps in colleges over youth infection rise

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • HIV testing and awareness camps to be held in Karnataka colleges
  • Decision follows concerns over rising infection rates among youth
  • Camps aim to provide testing, counselling, and education on prevention

HIV camps planned for college campuses

The Karnataka government has decided to organise HIV testing and awareness camps in colleges across the state. The move comes amid growing concern over a rise in HIV infections among young adults.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact timeline for the rollout. But sources indicate the camps will begin in the coming academic term.

Targeting young adults

Data from recent health surveys shows a worrying trend. More young people are testing positive for HIV, especially in the 18-25 age group. This group is also less likely to seek testing or treatment early.

The camps will offer free, confidential testing. They will also provide counselling and information on prevention methods. The government hopes this will catch infections early and reduce transmission.

Focus on awareness and stigma reduction

Health department officials say the camps are not just about testing. They also aim to break the stigma around HIV. Many young people avoid testing because they fear discrimination.

The camps will include peer educators and trained counsellors. They will talk about safe sex practices and the importance of knowing one's status. The idea is to make HIV a normal part of health conversations on campus.

Statewide rollout expected

The programme is expected to cover both government and private colleges. Districts with higher infection rates will be prioritised. The state has allocated funds for testing kits and awareness materials.

Karnataka's health department is also coordinating with the education department. College principals have been asked to cooperate with the initiative. The government has not disclosed the exact number of camps planned.

What happens next

The success of the camps will depend on student turnout and follow-up care. The government will monitor the data from these camps to see if infection rates start to drop. Officials are also considering regular health screenings as a long-term measure.
