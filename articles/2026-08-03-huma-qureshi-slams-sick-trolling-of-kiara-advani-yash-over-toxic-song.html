Huma Qureshi slams 'sick' trolling of Kiara Advani, Yash over Toxic song

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Huma Qureshi calls trolling of Kiara Advani and Yash 'sick, disgusting'
  • The backlash stems from the song 'Tabaahi' in the film Toxic
  • Sidharth Malhotra's birthday wish for Kiara Advani adds to the discourse

Mumbai: Actor Huma Qureshi has come out strongly in defence of her Toxic co-stars Kiara Advani and Yash, who have been at the receiving end of vicious online abuse following the release of the film's song Tabaahi. In a blunt statement, Qureshi described the trolling as "sick, disgusting", and urged fans to stop targeting the actors.

What sparked the row

The controversy erupted after Tabaahi, a high-energy track from the much-awaited film Toxic, hit streaming platforms. While the song has been trending, a section of social media users began attacking Advani and Yash over their on-screen pairing and the song's lyrics, with some even calling for a boycott.

The abuse escalated to the point where Qureshi, who plays a pivotal role in the film, felt compelled to speak out. Her remarks have since gone viral, drawing both support and criticism.

Qureshi's blunt rebuttal

In her response, Qureshi did not mince words. She called out the anonymous trolls, saying their behaviour reflects poorly on the audience rather than the performers. "This is sick and disgusting," she said, adding that actors work hard to entertain and do not deserve such venom.

She also appealed to fans to focus on the craft rather than spreading hate. Her statement has been widely shared, with many in the industry quietly applauding her for taking a stand.

Industry reactions and the birthday twist

The controversy has also drawn in Sidharth Malhotra, Kiara Advani's husband. On his wife's birthday, Malhotra posted a warm wish on social media, which many interpreted as a subtle dig at the trolls. The post, featuring a picture of the couple, carried a caption that seemed to dismiss the negativity surrounding the film.

While Malhotra did not directly mention Toxic or the trolling, fans were quick to connect the dots. The post has added another layer to the ongoing online debate, with supporters of the couple using it to counter the hate campaign.

The bigger picture

This incident is not an isolated one. Bollywood has seen a spate of such trolling episodes in recent years, often triggered by song releases or casting choices. Experts say the anonymity of social media allows vitriol to spread unchecked, and celebrities are increasingly speaking out against it.

For now, the focus remains on Toxic, which is slated for release later this year. The film, directed by a noted filmmaker, has been in the news for its star cast and bold storytelling.

What happens next

As the debate simmers, the film's team is expected to release more promotional material in the coming weeks. Whether the trolling subsides or intensifies will depend on how the discourse evolves, but Qureshi's stand has made it clear that actors are no longer willing to stay silent.

Observers will be watching to see if other stars from Toxic follow her lead and address the abuse directly.
