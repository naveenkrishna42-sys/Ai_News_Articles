CJP's Saurav Das Defends Noida Woman Booked for Remarks Against PM Modi

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Saurav Das of the Communist Party of India (Marxist–Leninist) Liberation's Central Janwadi Adhikar Manch (CJP) defended the woman.
  • Das claimed close to 100 BJP MPs have criminal cases against them.
  • The FIR against the woman was transferred from Noida to Delhi.
  • The woman was booked for alleged objectionable remarks against PM Modi during a paper leak protest.

New Delhi: The political row over the arrest of a 25-year-old woman in Noida for allegedly making objectionable remarks against Prime Minister Narendra Modi took a fresh turn on Friday. Saurav Das, a leader of the Communist Party of India (Marxist–Leninist) Liberation's Central Janwadi Adhikar Manch (CJP), came out in defence of the woman, questioning why the police were targeting her when a significant number of lawmakers from the ruling party face serious charges.

Das pointed to a stark contrast in how the law is being applied. He said that close to 100 BJP MPs have criminal cases registered against them, yet the focus of the administration appears to be on a young woman who expressed her anger during a protest. His remarks have sharpened the debate around free speech and selective prosecution.

The Incident and the FIR

The woman was booked after a video of her remarks went viral on social media. She was part of a protest related to a paper leak issue, and her comments directed at the Prime Minister were deemed objectionable by the authorities. The case was initially registered as a Zero FIR in Noida, which allows a complaint to be filed at any police station irrespective of jurisdiction.

Subsequently, the FIR was transferred to Delhi, a move that has drawn criticism from civil liberties groups. They argue that the transfer is an attempt to centralise the case and possibly escalate the charges. The woman's identity has not been officially disclosed, and it remains unclear if she has been arrested or merely summoned for questioning.

Das's Counter to the Administration

In his statement, Das did not mince words. He asserted that the police and the political establishment are applying a double standard. "Instead of pursuing cases against powerful figures with criminal backgrounds, they are hounding a 25-year-old for a video," he said. The CJP leader's remarks have found resonance among opposition parties and student organisations, who have called for the immediate withdrawal of the FIR.

The timing of the controversy is notable. It comes amid a series of student-led protests over alleged irregularities in recruitment exams. The woman's remarks were made in the heat of one such demonstration, and her supporters claim her words were taken out of context.

The Political Fallout

The BJP has so far not issued an official response to Das's allegations. However, party spokespersons have in the past defended action against those who insult the Prime Minister, calling it a matter of respect for the highest office. The opposition, on the other hand, has seized the opportunity to highlight the criminal cases pending against some BJP legislators, pointing to data that shows a significant number of MPs across parties have declared criminal antecedents in their election affidavits.

Das's specific figure of "close to 100" has not been independently verified. The Association for Democratic Reforms (ADR), in its past analyses, has noted that a substantial percentage of MPs from various parties face criminal charges, but the exact number for the BJP alone fluctuates across election cycles.

What Happens Next

The Delhi Police will now investigate the case, and the woman is expected to be called for recording her statement. Legal experts suggest that the charges could be diluted if she tenders an apology or if the court finds the remarks were made without malicious intent. For now, the spotlight remains on how the authorities balance public outrage with constitutional safeguards on free expression.

The CJP has announced plans to hold a protest in Delhi next week, demanding the case be dropped. The coming days will reveal whether the administration chooses to escalate the matter or quietly close the file.
