Banks recover 1% in personal guarantor cases; Subhash Chandra case highlights risks

📅 2026-08-29 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Banks recover 1% in personal guarantor cases
  • Subhash Chandra case involves associate companies
  • HDFC Bank plans to move NCLAT against NCLT order in Subhash Chandra case

Banks recover 1% in personal guarantor cases

Banks have recovered 1% of the outstanding personal guarantor cases, according to the latest data from the Reserve Bank of India. This recovery rate is a significant improvement from the previous year, which saw a recovery rate of just 0.5%. The recovery rate is a crucial indicator of the health of the banking sector and the ability of banks to recover their loans.

The recovery rate is influenced by various factors, including the economic conditions, the creditworthiness of the borrowers, and the enforcement mechanisms put in place by the banks. The improvement in the recovery rate suggests that banks are taking a more proactive approach to recovering their loans, and that the enforcement mechanisms are becoming more effective.

Subhash Chandra case highlights risks of corporate debt

The Subhash Chandra case, involving associate companies, has once again brought to the forefront the risks associated with corporate debt. The case involves the settlement of a debt of Rs 6.5 crore, which is a significant sum of money. The settlement involved a 'haircut', which is a reduction in the amount owed, and is a common practice in the settlement of corporate debts.

The Subhash Chandra case is a reminder of the risks associated with corporate debt, and the importance of having robust enforcement mechanisms in place. The case also highlights the importance of having a clear understanding of the terms and conditions of any debt agreement, and the potential risks associated with any 'haircuts' that may be involved.

HDFC Bank plans to move NCLAT against NCLT order in Subhash Chandra case

HDFC Bank has announced that it plans to move the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal (NCLAT) against the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) order in the Subhash Chandra case. The NCLT order had dismissed the bank's appeal, and the bank is now seeking to appeal the decision to the NCLAT.

The move by HDFC Bank is a significant development in the case, and will be closely watched by the legal community. The outcome of the case will have important implications for the enforcement of corporate debts, and the rights of banks to recover their loans.
