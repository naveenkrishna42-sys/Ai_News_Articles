Dr. Reddy's to Bring Qdenga to India by 2027

Dr. Reddy's Laboratories has signed a partnership with Japan's Takeda to commercialise Qdenga (TAK-003), the first dengue vaccine approved for use in India. The rollout is targeted for the first half of 2027, pending final regulatory clearances from the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO).

The deal gives Dr. Reddy's exclusive marketing and distribution rights in India, a market that reports among the highest dengue burdens globally. For Indian buyers — parents, adult travellers, and institutional health programmes — this is the first credible, licensed prevention option after years of reliance on vector control alone.

What Exactly Is Qdenga?

Qdenga is a live-attenuated tetravalent vaccine built on a dengue serotype 2 backbone, engineered to protect against all four dengue virus serotypes (DENV-1 to DENV-4). Unlike Dengvaxia, the earlier Sanofi vaccine that required pre-screening for prior infection, Qdenga is approved for use regardless of prior dengue exposure in eligible age groups.

It is administered as two subcutaneous doses, three months apart. Full protection builds roughly two weeks after the second dose — a schedule buyers must factor into travel or monsoon-season planning.

Real-World Efficacy and Buyer Implications

Takeda's pivotal TIDES trial, spanning multiple countries including endemic regions, reported about 80% efficacy against symptomatic dengue in the 12 months following the second dose, with roughly 90% reduction in hospitalisations. Efficacy tapers over subsequent years, which means booster strategy and revaccination timing will matter for long-term protection.

For Indian households, the practical question is cost-per-protected-season. Dengue hospitalisation in private hospitals routinely runs ₹50,000 to ₹2 lakh per severe case. If a two-dose course lands in the low four-figure rupee range, the economics tilt decisively toward vaccination for children and high-exposure adults.

Pricing, Access, and Market Positioning

Dr. Reddy's has not disclosed India pricing. Global benchmarks offer a rough guide: in Indonesia and parts of Latin America, Qdenga is priced at approximately USD 40–50 per dose in private channels, translating to roughly ₹3,500–4,500 per dose, or ₹7,000–9,000 for the full course before taxes and administration charges.

Indian pricing typically undercuts Western benchmarks. Expect a private-market course in the ₹4,000–8,000 band, with possible inclusion in select corporate and insurance health packages. Government procurement under the Universal Immunisation Programme is unlikely in the first phase given supply constraints.

  • Pros: First licensed dengue vaccine in India; covers all four serotypes; no pre-vaccination screening required; strong hospitalisation-reduction data.
  • Pros: Two-dose schedule is operationally simpler than multi-visit regimens; Dr. Reddy's has deep Indian distribution reach.
  • Cons: Pricing unannounced; efficacy wanes over time, so boosters may be needed.
  • Cons: Initial supply may be limited, prioritising metros and private channels over rural mass rollout.
  • Cons: Live-attenuated vaccine — immunocompromised individuals and pregnant women will need medical clearance.

What Buyers Should Do Now

Do not wait for 2027 to act on dengue prevention. Vector control, repellents, and eliminating stagnant water remain the primary defence until the vaccine is commercially available. Parents of children aged 4 and above — a common eligibility threshold for Qdenga in approved markets — should start conversations with paediatricians about timing and suitability.

Watch for CDSCO's formal approval notification and Dr. Reddy's pricing announcement, expected closer to launch. Corporate wellness and insurance buyers should begin evaluating coverage inclusion now, as dengue claims are a recurring seasonal cost centre.
