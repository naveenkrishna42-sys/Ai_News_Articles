Metrowater MD inspects Chembarambakkam treatment plant

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Metrowater MD visited Chembarambakkam treatment plant
  • Inspection focused on operational efficiency and water supply
  • Officials reviewed monsoon preparedness and maintenance

Inspection at key facility

Chennai Metrowater's Managing Director conducted an inspection of the Chembarambakkam treatment plant on Tuesday. The visit was part of an ongoing review of the city's water infrastructure, officials said.

The plant, one of the major sources of drinking water for Chennai, draws from the Chembarambakkam reservoir. Its treatment capacity and distribution network are critical to the western and southern parts of the city.

Focus on operations and maintenance

During the inspection, the MD reviewed the plant's operational parameters, including water treatment processes, pumping systems, and quality checks. Maintenance schedules and the condition of machinery were also examined.

Officials accompanying the MD briefed him on the current output levels and any pending repairs. The visit comes ahead of the northeast monsoon, when the reservoir typically receives significant inflow.

Pre-monsoon readiness

A senior Metrowater official said the inspection was aimed at ensuring the plant is fully prepared for the monsoon season. 'We are checking all systems to avoid any disruption in supply during the rains,' the official said, without elaborating.

The Chembarambakkam plant has a designed capacity to treat several million litres per day, though exact figures were not disclosed during the visit. Water from the plant is supplied to areas such as Poonamallee, Porur, and parts of Chennai city.

Supply situation in Chennai

Chennai has faced water stress in past summers, and the Metrowater administration has been under pressure to maintain steady supply. The inspection is seen as part of a broader effort to improve reliability.

Residents in the western suburbs have occasionally reported low pressure during peak summer months. Officials said the plant's performance is monitored round the clock, and this visit was a routine but thorough check.

No major faults were reported during the inspection. The MD later held a brief meeting with plant engineers to discuss future upgrades and maintenance protocols.

What to watch

Metrowater is expected to release a detailed action plan for monsoon preparedness in the coming weeks. The focus will be on ensuring the plant operates at full capacity once the reservoir fills up.
