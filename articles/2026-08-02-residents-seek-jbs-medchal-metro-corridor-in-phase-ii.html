Residents seek JBS-Medchal Metro corridor in Phase II

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Residents of JBS-Medchal area demand Metro corridor in Phase II
  • They cite rising traffic congestion and rapid urban growth
  • Officials yet to confirm inclusion in final plans

Residents along the JBS-Medchal stretch have stepped up their demand for a Metro rail corridor under Phase II of Hyderabad's Metro expansion. They argue that the route, which connects the city to the northern suburbs, has become a choke point for daily commuters.

The demand comes as the Telangana government finalises priorities for the next phase of the Metro network. Locals say the corridor has been ignored for too long despite its growing population and traffic load.

Why the corridor matters

The JBS-Medchal road links several residential colonies, IT hubs and industrial zones. It also serves as a key entry point for vehicles coming from neighbouring districts. Commuters say peak-hour travel on this stretch has become unbearable.

One resident, who has been part of the campaign, said the road is clogged with buses, cars and two-wheelers throughout the day. He pointed out that the existing bus services are not enough to handle the volume of passengers.

Real estate growth in areas like Alwal, Kompally and Medchal has added to the pressure. New apartments and commercial complexes have come up rapidly, but public transport infrastructure has not kept pace.

What residents are asking for

Residents have submitted representations to the Hyderabad Metro Rail authorities and local elected representatives. They want the JBS-Medchal line to be included in the Phase II alignment without further delay.

They have also suggested that the corridor be extended to cover emerging nodes like Kandlakoya and beyond. This, they say, would ease congestion on the entire northern belt and improve last-mile connectivity.

Some residents have proposed that the Metro line be integrated with the proposed Regional Ring Road and the existing MMTS network. They believe a multi-modal approach would offer the best value for public money.

Official position unclear

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the JBS-Medchal corridor will find a place in the final Phase II plan. The state government is reportedly still evaluating multiple routes based on feasibility and ridership projections.

A senior official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said that several corridors are under consideration. He added that technical studies are being conducted to assess traffic patterns and financial viability.

Earlier this year, the state government had indicated that Phase II would focus on extending the Metro to the outskirts. However, specific alignments have not been officially announced.

Political pressure builds

Local leaders from across party lines have backed the demand. They have raised the issue in public meetings and through social media campaigns, calling it a matter of regional equity.

Some have argued that the northern corridor has been neglected in successive transport plans. They point to the Metro's existing lines, which largely serve the central and western parts of the city.

Residents say they will continue their agitation until they get a clear commitment. They have hinted at organising larger protests if the demand is not addressed in the upcoming budget.

The final decision on Phase II corridors is expected within the next few months. All eyes will be on the state government's announcement, which will determine whether the JBS-Medchal route finally gets its due.
