Faith Meets Footfall: Spiritual Travel Surges Across India

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Spiritual travel in India is growing at 15-20% annually, outpacing general tourism
  • Domestic pilgrims are flocking to sites like Varanasi, Ayodhya, and Kedarnath
  • State governments are investing in infrastructure to cater to religious tourists

India's ancient temples and holy rivers are witnessing an unprecedented surge in footfall. Spiritual tourism, once a niche segment, is now a driving force in the country's travel industry.

Reports from across states show that pilgrimages and temple visits are not just religious acts—they are becoming the backbone of domestic tourism. The trend is reshaping how Indians travel and how states market themselves.

The Numbers Behind the Boom

Industry data points to a sharp rise. Spiritual travel in India is growing at an annual rate of 15 to 20 percent, significantly faster than general tourism growth. In 2025 alone, the sector attracted over 300 million domestic visits to major religious sites.

Ayodhya, Varanasi, and Kedarnath top the list. Ayodhya, following the Ram Temple consecration, now draws lakhs of visitors every month. Varanasi's ghats remain packed year-round. Kedarnath, despite its high altitude, saw a record number of yatris in the last season.

Why Spiritual Travel Is Rising

Several factors are driving this shift. Improved road and rail connectivity has made remote temples accessible. State governments are investing heavily in facilities—parking, toilets, and accommodation—at pilgrimage spots.

There is also a cultural shift. Younger Indians, even those who rarely visit temples, are now seeking spiritual experiences. They travel not just for darshan but for a sense of peace and belonging. Social media plays a role too: picturesque temple towns are becoming Instagram hotspots.

The rise of domestic tourism is another key factor. With international travel costs high, many Indians are exploring their own country. Religious sites offer a ready-made itinerary, often with low entry barriers.

States Capitalise on the Trend

Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu are leading the charge. Uttarakhand's Char Dham project, which includes improved roads and helipads, has made the pilgrimage safer and faster. Uttar Pradesh has developed a 'Ramayana Circuit' linking key sites.

Madhya Pradesh and Odisha are not far behind. They are promoting ancient temples and tribal rituals as part of their tourism packages. The result is a competitive landscape where every state wants a share of the spiritual tourist's wallet.

Local economies benefit directly. Hotels, eateries, and transport operators near temple towns report steady business. Artisans selling idols, flowers, and offerings see increased demand. The economic ripple effect is significant.

Challenges Amid the Growth

But the boom is not without problems. Overcrowding at sites like Tirupati and Shirdi strains infrastructure. Waste management and water supply often fall short during peak seasons. Pilgrims complain of long queues and inadequate facilities.

Environmental concerns are emerging too. The Char Dham route in Uttarakhand sees heavy vehicular traffic, leading to pollution in ecologically fragile zones. Authorities are now exploring caps on daily visitor numbers.

There are also reports of commercialisation. Some temples have become heavily monetised, with high charges for special darshans and parking. Devotees say the spiritual essence is sometimes lost in the rush of revenue.

What Lies Ahead

The trend shows no signs of slowing. The central government is working on a national framework for spiritual tourism, including standardised amenities and safety protocols. Several states plan to add new circuits linking lesser-known temples.

For now, faith and footfall are walking hand in hand. The challenge will be to manage the crowds without diluting the experience—a test that India's ancient temples have faced for centuries and are now confronting anew.
