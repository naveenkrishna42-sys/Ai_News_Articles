Two held for impersonating minister Aadhav Arjuna to extort Rs 8 crore from MLA

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two people arrested for impersonating minister Aadhav Arjuna
  • They demanded Rs 8 crore from an MLA, police say
  • Investigation underway; more details awaited

Chennai police have arrested two individuals for allegedly impersonating minister Aadhav Arjuna and attempting to extort Rs 8 crore from a Member of the Legislative Assembly (MLA). The arrests were made after the MLA lodged a complaint, officials said on Wednesday.

The accused reportedly contacted the MLA, posing as the minister, and demanded the hefty sum. The matter came to light when the MLA grew suspicious and alerted the authorities. Police acted swiftly, tracking down the suspects and taking them into custody.

How the fraud was carried out

According to preliminary investigation, the duo used phone calls and possibly other means to convince the MLA that they were speaking on behalf of minister Aadhav Arjuna. They claimed the money was needed for some undisclosed purpose, though the exact pretext has not been revealed.

The MLA, whose identity has not been disclosed, did not comply with the demand. Instead, he reported the matter to the police, leading to a trap or a detailed inquiry. Officials have not yet confirmed whether the accused had any prior connection to the MLA or if they targeted him randomly.

Police action and legal process

The two accused have been taken into custody and are being questioned. Police are examining their phone records and other digital evidence to understand the full extent of the fraud. It is not yet clear if they have attempted similar cons against other public representatives.

A case has been registered under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code, including cheating and criminal intimidation. The accused are expected to be produced before a magistrate for remand, though the exact timeline remains unconfirmed.

Impersonation of public figures on the rise

This incident highlights a growing trend of fraudsters impersonating politicians and bureaucrats to extort money. In recent years, several such cases have been reported across the country, with fraudsters using phone calls, fake social media accounts, and even AI-generated voices to deceive victims.

Authorities have repeatedly warned the public, especially those in influential positions, to verify the identity of callers before acting on any demand. In this case, the MLA's alertness likely prevented a significant financial loss.

What happens next

Police are expected to conduct further interrogation to determine if the accused acted alone or as part of a larger syndicate. They may also seek custodial interrogation to uncover potential accomplices and previous victims. The investigation is ongoing, and more details are likely to emerge as the case progresses.
