Mumbai: Cockroach Infestation at Blinkit Unit; Sweets Seized from Reliance Retail

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Civic officials inspected Blinkit's Mumbai unit and found cockroach infestation.
  • Sweets were seized from a Reliance Retail outlet during the same drive.
  • Actions are part of ongoing food safety checks in the city.

Mumbai's food safety machinery swung into action over the weekend, with inspectors flagging hygiene violations at two major retail and quick-commerce players. A Blinkit unit in the city was found to have a cockroach infestation, while a Reliance Retail outlet had sweets seized from its premises.

Blinkit Unit Under Scanner

The inspection at Blinkit's Mumbai warehouse was part of a routine check by the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation's (BMC) food safety department. Officials discovered cockroaches in the storage and handling areas, raising serious concerns about the facility's sanitation standards.

Sources said the unit was immediately served a notice, and corrective measures were mandated. The exact location of the facility and the quantum of stock affected have not been officially disclosed, but the BMC's action underscores a growing scrutiny of quick-commerce operations, which have expanded rapidly across Indian cities.

Sweets Seized From Reliance Retail

In a separate action, inspectors seized sweets from a Reliance Retail outlet in Mumbai. The confiscation was likely due to concerns over quality, expiry, or improper storage, though the specific reasons have not been made public by the authorities yet.

The seized items have been sent for laboratory analysis, and further action will depend on the test results. Reliance Retail has not yet issued a statement regarding the seizure.

Broader Crackdown on Food Safety

These inspections are part of a wider drive by the BMC to tighten enforcement of food safety norms across the city. In recent months, multiple eateries and food storage facilities have been pulled up for violations, ranging from unhygienic premises to the use of banned substances.

For quick-commerce firms like Blinkit, which promise delivery in minutes, maintaining hygiene across a network of dark stores is a logistical challenge. The BMC's actions signal that regulatory bodies are closely watching these operations.

What Happens Next

Both companies are expected to respond to the notices, and the BMC will likely conduct follow-up inspections to verify compliance. If the lab tests on the seized sweets indicate adulteration or contamination, Reliance Retail could face penalties or legal action.

For consumers, these incidents serve as a reminder of the importance of food safety oversight. The coming weeks will reveal whether the BMC intensifies its inspections or if the companies take voluntary corrective steps to restore confidence.
