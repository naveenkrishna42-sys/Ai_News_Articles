Sensex Closes Lower as Global Tensions Drive Sell-Off

The BSE Sensex closed 384 points lower at 76,132 on Monday, marking a second consecutive session of decline amid escalating geopolitical tensions in West Asia and renewed concerns over potential U.S. interest rate hikes. The NSE Nifty 50 also slipped, settling at 23,779, down 118 points or 0.5% from its previous close.

Market breadth remained weak across sectors, with IT, FMCG, and PSU Bank indices leading the downtrend. Oil marketing companies came under pressure too, as crude oil prices edged higher on supply disruption fears linked to Middle East developments.

IT Stocks Drag Markets Amid Rate Worries

The Nifty IT index tumbled over 2%, dragged down by heavyweight names like Infosys and Tech Mahindra, which each lost more than 3%. The sharp fall reflects investor concern that rising U.S. yields could erode the sector's overseas earnings and delay client spending on tech services.

Analysts say the correction in IT stocks is part of a broader repricing across global markets, triggered by sticky inflation data from the U.S., which has increased the probability of another rate hike by the Federal Reserve. Export-oriented sectors remain particularly vulnerable to rate volatility.

Broader Market Sentiment Turns Cautious

Across global markets, benchmark indices in the U.S., Europe, and Asia traded mixed as investors digested fresh commentary from central bank officials. The dollar index held firm near recent highs, keeping emerging market assets under strain.

In India, the rupee slipped slightly against the greenback, closing at 83.42 per dollar, its weakest level in over a week. Bond yields on the 10-year government security rose marginally to 6.61%, reflecting caution among fixed-income traders.

Oil Prices Rise on Supply Fears

Brent crude futures climbed above $93 per barrel, driven by concerns over potential disruptions to shipping routes in the Red Sea and Persian Gulf. India, which imports roughly 85% of its crude needs, faces elevated energy costs that could push up input prices and inflationary pressures.

Rajan Mehta, Senior Analyst at SMC Global, noted that “while domestic earnings remain resilient, external headwinds—especially on interest rates and oil prices—are increasing market volatility. Investors should brace for continued swings until clarity emerges on both fronts.”
