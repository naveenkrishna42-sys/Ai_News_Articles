Sensex drops 265 points, Nifty 70 points down as markets open cautious on July 14

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex opened 265 points lower, Nifty down 70 points on July 14, 2026
  • Markets opened in cautious mode, tracking global weakness and profit booking
  • Broader market indices also saw selling pressure at the open

Cautious Start for Indian Benchmarks

Indian equity markets opened on a cautious note on Tuesday, July 14, 2026, with benchmark indices trading in the red. The BSE Sensex dropped 265 points in early trade, while the NSE Nifty slipped 70 points below the previous close.

Market participants attributed the subdued start to mixed global cues and profit booking at higher levels. The opening bell saw selling pressure across select heavyweights, dragging the indices lower.

According to data from HDFC Sky, the Sensex was trading at 62,450 levels shortly after the opening, while the Nifty hovered around 18,550. The broader market also mirrored the weak sentiment, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices trading marginally lower.

Global Cues Weigh on Sentiment

Asian markets were trading mixed in early hours, with Japan's Nikkei and China's Shanghai Composite showing slight declines. Overnight, US markets ended lower as investors weighed inflation data and corporate earnings, adding to the cautious mood in India.

Foreign institutional investors remained net sellers in the previous session, which also contributed to the negative bias. Analysts said the domestic market is taking a breather after recent gains, as traders book profits ahead of key economic data due later this week.

The rupee opened flat against the US dollar at 82.95, reflecting the cautious sentiment across asset classes.

Sectoral Performance Mixed

Among sectoral indices, IT and banking stocks faced selling pressure in early trade. The Nifty Bank index slipped over 200 points, while the Nifty IT index shed nearly 150 points. On the other hand, pharma and FMCG stocks showed relative resilience, trading with minor gains.

Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, and Infosys were among the top drags on the Sensex, contributing to the index's decline. However, defensive stocks like Sun Pharma and Hindustan Unilever provided some support.

Market breadth was negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. Analysts expect volatility to remain high in the near term as global and domestic cues continue to evolve.

What to Watch Next

Traders will keep a close eye on the US Federal Reserve's policy stance and domestic inflation numbers due later this week. Any surprise in data could trigger sharper moves in the market.

For now, the cautious tone is likely to persist until clearer direction emerges from global markets and institutional flows.
