Senate confirms Trump lawyer Todd Blanche as attorney general

📅 2026-08-08 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Senate voted narrowly along party lines to confirm Todd Blanche
  • Blanche was Trump's personal lawyer and has no prior government experience
  • His confirmation clears the way for a Justice Department aligned with Trump's agenda
  • The vote took place overnight after a prolonged confirmation battle
  • Blanche inherits a department facing scrutiny over political independence

The United States Senate has narrowly confirmed Todd Blanche as attorney general, placing Donald Trump's former personal lawyer at the helm of the Justice Department. The vote, which took place overnight, fell largely along party lines, with Republicans uniting behind the nominee while Democrats opposed his confirmation.

Blanche, who previously represented Trump in his criminal cases, now becomes the top law enforcement officer in the country. His confirmation marks a significant shift for an institution that has traditionally sought to maintain independence from the White House.

A contentious path to confirmation

The confirmation process was fraught from the start. Democrats repeatedly raised concerns about Blanche's lack of government experience and his close personal ties to the president. Several Republican senators, however, dismissed those objections, arguing that Blanche's legal credentials and courtroom record made him fit for the role.

In the end, the tally was close enough to reflect deep divisions within the chamber. The final vote came after a series of procedural hurdles and late-night negotiations, underscoring how polarised the confirmation had become.

A Justice Department reshaped

Blanche steps into a department that has already been transformed during Trump's second term. Senior career officials have been replaced, and several investigations that once targeted the president have been closed. His appointment is widely seen as a consolidation of that shift, aligning the department more closely with the president's agenda.

Critics have warned that Blanche's loyalty to Trump could erode the department's independence. Supporters counter that his mandate is simply to enforce the law as the administration interprets it. Either way, the Justice Department under Blanche is expected to focus its resources on issues that align with the president's priorities, including immigration enforcement and election integrity.

Tax audit immunity remains intact

One immediate consequence of Blanche's confirmation is the continued immunity of Trump's tax audits from public scrutiny. A legal dispute over the release of the president's tax returns has been pending for months, and with Blanche in place, the department is unlikely to pursue the matter further. The AP reported that this immunity remains in effect, a point that has drawn sharp criticism from transparency advocates.

What lies ahead

Blanche's first days in office will be closely watched. He is expected to issue guidance on several sensitive policy areas, including the use of federal prosecutors in local matters and the handling of cases involving the January 6 Capitol riot defendants. His decisions on these issues will signal how aggressively he intends to wield the department's power.

For now, the Senate's narrow verdict leaves little room for error. Blanche will need to navigate a politically charged environment where every move is scrutinised, and where even his own party remains divided on how far the department should go in serving the president.

As he takes the oath of office, observers will be looking for early signals on whether he can balance loyalty with the rule of law. The coming months will test not just Blanche, but the durability of the Justice Department's traditional independence in an era of intense partisanship.
