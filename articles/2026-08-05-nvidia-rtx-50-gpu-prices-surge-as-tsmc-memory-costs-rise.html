Nvidia RTX 50 GPU prices surge as TSMC, memory costs rise

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nvidia RTX 50 GPU prices are increasing due to higher TSMC manufacturing and memory costs.
  • ASUS rejected a pre-price hike order for a GeForce RTX 5090, leading to a $500 increase to $5,100.
  • GIGABYTE has reportedly raised GPU prices by up to 40% in Japan, with further revisions expected in August.

Nvidia's RTX 50 series graphics cards are getting more expensive, and the reasons are piling up. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company's advanced wafer costs have climbed, and memory prices are following the same trajectory. The result is a wave of price hikes hitting retailers and consumers across the globe, with some models seeing increases of up to 40%.

The pressure is most visible in recent order cancellations and revised quotes. One notable case involves a GeForce RTX 5090 order that was cancelled after ASUS rejected the original pricing. The new quote came in at $5,100, a $500 jump from the earlier $4,600 tag. That's not a minor adjustment—it's a clear signal that board partners are no longer absorbing rising input costs.

Why GPU prices are climbing

At the heart of the surge is TSMC's pricing for its most advanced nodes. Nvidia's RTX 50 series relies on these cutting-edge chips, and the foundry has been adjusting its rates to reflect increased capital expenditure and energy costs. Memory is another culprit. High-bandwidth memory and GDDR7 modules, essential for the RTX 50 line-up, have seen sustained price increases over the past year.

These costs don't stay hidden for long. Board partners like ASUS, GIGABYTE, and others typically pass them on to retailers, who in turn adjust their shelves. The August timeframe is proving particularly tricky, as several manufacturers have begun notifying distributors of pending price revisions.

Regional impact and retail uncertainty

Japan is already feeling the pinch. GIGABYTE has reportedly hiked GPU pricing by up to 40% in the region, a move that has caught many buyers off guard. The scale of the increase suggests that the cost pressures are not marginal—they're rewriting the economics of high-end graphics cards.

In Taiwan, the Commercial Development and Foreign Trade Association has announced it will revise order prices for GIGABYTE GPUs starting in August. The exact impact on retail prices remains unclear, but the direction is obvious. Consumers in other markets, including India, are likely to see similar adjustments as global supply chains synchronise.

What this means for buyers

For gamers and professionals eyeing an RTX 5090 or RTX 5080, the window for locking in older prices is closing fast. Retailers across multiple regions have already updated their listings, and further revisions are expected. The $5,100 RTX 5090 order is not an isolated incident; it reflects a broader trend where pre-hike quotes are being invalidated.

It's worth noting that not all models will see identical increases. Entry-level RTX 50 cards may face smaller adjustments, as their memory and cooling requirements are less demanding. But the overall direction is uniform: prices are going up, and they're doing so at a pace that's hard to ignore.

Industry-wide ripple effects

The price hikes are not confined to Nvidia's ecosystem. AMD and Intel, who also rely on TSMC for advanced chips, could face similar pressures. Memory suppliers, including SK Hynix and Samsung, are also benefiting from the tight supply, which means the cost curve is unlikely to flatten anytime soon.

For now, the focus is on the August revisions. Distributors are waiting to see how GIGABYTE and other manufacturers adjust their order prices, and whether retailers can hold the line or must pass on the increases. The next few weeks will be crucial for anyone planning a high-end build.

Watch for official announcements from Nvidia and its board partners in the coming weeks. If the current trend holds, the RTX 50 series could become one of the most expensive mainstream GPU generations in recent memory.
