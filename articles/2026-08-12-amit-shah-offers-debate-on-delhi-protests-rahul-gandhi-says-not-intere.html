Amit Shah Offers Debate on Delhi Protests, Rahul Gandhi Says Not Interested

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Amit Shah says he will reply on the Delhi protests, possibly tomorrow at 3 pm, if a debate is held.
  • Rahul Gandhi rejects the offer, saying he is not interested in a lecture.
  • Lok Sabha adjourned till 2 pm; Rajya Sabha sees continued protests.
  • The monsoon session remains deadlocked over the issue.
  • Shah's condition for the debate is not yet fully clear.

The monsoon session of Parliament remained deadlocked on Wednesday as the Opposition pushed for a discussion on the ongoing Delhi student protests. Union Home Minister Amit Shah said he was ready for a debate, but Congress leader Rahul Gandhi rejected the offer, calling it a potential lecture rather than a genuine dialogue.

Shah's Offer and Condition

Speaking in the Lok Sabha, Amit Shah said he was prepared to answer any question on the protests. He indicated he would reply tomorrow at 3 pm if a debate was held. However, he attached a condition, the specifics of which were not immediately clear.

The Home Minister's remarks came amid persistent clamour by Opposition MPs, who have been demanding a detailed statement on the protests. The protests, which have been ongoing for several days, have become a flashpoint in the ongoing session.

Gandhi's Response

Rahul Gandhi, responding to Shah's offer, said he was not interested in the Home Minister giving him a lecture. The Congress leader's sharp retort underscored the deepening rift between the government and the Opposition on how to address the protests.

The exchange took place in the well of the House, with members from both sides raising their voices. The Opposition has been pressing for a structured debate with a vote, while the government has offered a discussion under specific rules.

Parliament Proceedings Disrupted

The Lok Sabha was adjourned till 2 pm following the heated exchanges. In the Rajya Sabha, protests continued, with Opposition members demanding a statement from the government on the situation in Delhi.

This is the 18th day of the monsoon session, and little legislative business has been transacted due to the stalemate. Several key bills remain pending, and the disruption has raised concerns about the productivity of the session.

What's at Stake

The student protests in Delhi have drawn widespread attention, with demonstrators demanding action on a range of issues. The government has maintained that it is ready to discuss the matter, but the Opposition has insisted on a specific format for the debate.

Observers note that the deadlock reflects a broader pattern of parliamentary logjam, where procedural disagreements often overshadow substantive discussions. Both sides have accused each other of avoiding a genuine dialogue.

Looking Ahead

With Shah indicating a willingness to speak tomorrow, the focus now shifts to whether the Opposition will accept his terms. The adjournment till 2 pm offers a brief window for negotiations between the treasury and opposition benches.

The coming hours will determine if the House can resume normal business or if the stalemate will continue to stall crucial legislation. For now, the corridors of Parliament remain tense, with no immediate resolution in sight.
