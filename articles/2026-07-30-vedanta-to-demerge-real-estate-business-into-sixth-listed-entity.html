Vedanta to demerge real estate business into sixth listed entity

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vedanta to demerge real estate business into Vedanta Property Platforms
  • This will create a sixth listed company under the Vedanta group
  • Q1 profit surged to Rs 5,473 crore amid commodity price tailwinds

Vedanta Ltd has announced yet another corporate restructuring, this time carving out its real estate assets into a separate entity. The company will demerge its surplus real estate business into a new firm called Vedanta Property Platforms, creating a sixth listed company under the group's umbrella.

The announcement comes just months after Vedanta completed the demerger of its four key business units — aluminium, oil and gas, power, and steel — into independent listed entities. The latest move signals that the Anil Agarwal-led conglomerate is pressing ahead with its aggressive simplification and value unlocking strategy.

What the demerger entails

Under the proposed scheme, the real estate division will be hived off and housed in Vedanta Property Platforms. Shareholders of Vedanta Ltd will receive shares in the new entity proportionally, mirroring the structure adopted in the earlier demergers.

The real estate portfolio being transferred includes surplus land parcels and property holdings across Vedanta's operational locations. These are assets that were earlier used for mining or industrial purposes but are now being monetised or developed.

The company has not yet disclosed the valuation of the real estate arm or the exact timeline for the demerger. Such schemes typically require approvals from shareholders, creditors, and the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT).

Strong quarterly performance

Alongside the demerger news, Vedanta reported a sharp jump in its first-quarter profit. Net profit for the April-June period surged to Rs 5,473 crore, driven by higher commodity prices and operational efficiencies across its core businesses.

Revenue from operations also saw a healthy uptick, though the company did not break out segment-level numbers in the initial statement. Analysts tracking the metals and mining sector had expected a strong quarter given the global rally in aluminium and zinc prices.

The profit figure marks a significant recovery from the corresponding quarter last year, when headwinds from input cost inflation and regulatory overhang had weighed on margins.

Why Vedanta is splitting again

Vedanta's demerger spree is aimed at simplifying its complex corporate structure. The group has historically operated as a vertically integrated conglomerate, with multiple businesses housed under one listed entity. Investors and analysts have long argued that this structure hides the true value of individual units.

By creating standalone listed companies for each business, Vedanta hopes to attract sector-specific investors, improve corporate governance, and unlock shareholder value. The real estate demerger follows the same logic — surplus land and property holdings can be better valued and managed as a separate entity.

The company has also indicated that the demerged entities will have independent boards and management teams, allowing each business to pursue its own growth strategy and capital allocation policy.

What it means for shareholders

For existing shareholders, each demerger results in a direct stake in the new entity. Over the past year, Vedanta shareholders have received shares in Vedanta Aluminium, Vedanta Oil & Gas, Vedanta Power, and Vedanta Steel. Now, they will also get shares in Vedanta Property Platforms.

The cumulative effect is a portfolio of six listed stocks instead of one. While this increases paperwork and tracking requirements, it also gives investors the flexibility to hold or sell individual businesses based on performance and outlook.

Market reaction to the announcement was muted, with the stock trading flat in early trade. Some analysts expressed caution, noting that too many demergers in quick succession could lead to administrative complexity and dilute focus.

What to watch next

Vedanta is expected to file the scheme of arrangement with stock exchanges in the coming weeks. The next key milestones are shareholder approval and NCLT clearance. Investors will also watch for any further corporate actions, including potential dividend payouts from the demerged entities.
