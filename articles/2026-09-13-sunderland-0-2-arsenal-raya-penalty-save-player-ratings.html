Sunderland 0-2 Arsenal: Raya's Save Turns a Tense Road Trip into Three Points

Arsenal left the Stadium of Light with a 0-2 win on 13 September 2026, but the scoreline flatters the visitors. David Raya's first-half penalty save kept the game level when Sunderland had the momentum, and Gabriel Martinelli's opener shortly after changed the contest entirely. Bukayo Saka added the second late on as the Gunners moved three points clear at the top of the Premier League table.

For away supporters who have watched Arsenal drop points in exactly these fixtures in recent seasons, this was a different kind of performance: less swagger, more control. Sunderland pressed aggressively, the home crowd stayed in the game until the final ten minutes, and Arsenal had to defend their box with real discipline.

How the Match Was Won

The Penalty Save That Shifted Momentum

Sunderland won a spot-kick midway through the first half after a clumsy challenge in the Arsenal area. Raya guessed correctly, got a strong hand to the ball, and the rebound was cleared. Within minutes, Arsenal broke forward and Martinelli finished a low cross to make it 1-0. Two moments, 121 seconds apart, decided the match.

Saka's Late Strike Ends the Contest

Sunderland pushed bodies forward in the final twenty minutes searching for an equaliser. Arsenal absorbed the pressure and punished them on the counter, with Saka cutting inside from the right and finding the far corner. The goal ended any realistic hope of a Sunderland comeback and gave Arsenal a comfortable margin that the run of play did not fully justify.

Player Ratings: Who Stood Out

Arsenal

  • David Raya (9/10): The penalty save was the defining act of the match. Commanded his area well under sustained aerial pressure.
  • Gabriel Martinelli (8/10): Took his goal clinically and stretched Sunderland's back line repeatedly with runs in behind.
  • Bukayo Saka (8/10): Quiet for long spells, then produced the finish that settled the game. That is the mark of a top winger.
  • Declan Rice (7.5/10): Covered ground relentlessly and screened the back four as Sunderland chased the game.
  • William Saliba (7.5/10): Dominant in the air and calm in possession during the tense opening half-hour.
  • Martin Ødegaard (7/10): Kept Arsenal ticking in midfield but was crowded out in the final third for long periods.

Sunderland

  • Anthony Patterson (7/10): Made several sharp saves and could do little about either goal.
  • Dan Ballard (7/10): Won his duels and defended the box with authority before tiring late.
  • Jobe Bellingham (6.5/10): Energetic and progressive in midfield, but his penalty miss proved costly.
  • Wilson Isidor (6/10): Worked the channels diligently without testing Raya enough from open play.

What This Result Means for the Title Race

Arsenal's three-point cushion at the top is significant because Liverpool and Chelsea both stumbled on the same weekend. Winning away at a newly promoted side with a hostile crowd is the kind of fixture that has cost Arsenal points in past campaigns. Doing it without a dominant performance suggests this squad has learned to grind out results when fluency is missing.

For Sunderland, the display offered encouragement. They created enough to take something from the game, and the penalty miss will sting, but the structure and intensity under their manager suggest they can compete at this level. The margins, as this match showed, remain brutal.

Buyer's Guide: What Fans Should Watch Next

Arsenal's next fixture will test whether this gritty win becomes a pattern or a one-off. Supporters tracking the title race should watch two things: whether Raya's penalty-saving form continues, and whether Martinelli and Saka can carry the goalscoring load if the central strikers endure a dry spell. Sunderland's home form against top-six sides will define their season.
