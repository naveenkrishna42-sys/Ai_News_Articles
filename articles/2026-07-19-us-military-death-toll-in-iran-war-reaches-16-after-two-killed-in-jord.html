US military death toll in Iran war reaches 16 after two killed in Jordan attack

📅 2026-07-19 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two US soldiers killed in attack on base in Jordan, one missing
  • Total US military deaths in Iran conflict rises to 16
  • US launches retaliatory airstrikes on Iran for eighth night
  • President Trump says soldiers killed is 'a sad thing'

The United States military confirmed on Saturday that two American service members were killed and one remains missing after an Iran-linked attack on a base in Jordan. The deaths bring the total US military death toll in the ongoing war with Iran to 16.

President Donald Trump described the loss of soldiers killed in action as 'a sad thing' in a brief statement from the White House. The administration has not provided further details on the identity of the troops or the circumstances surrounding the missing soldier.

Attack on Jordan base

The assault on the US base in Jordan marks a significant escalation in the conflict that has now stretched into its second week. According to initial reports, the base was struck by a barrage of missiles and drones launched from Iranian-backed positions across the border.

US Central Command confirmed the attack, stating that the two troops were killed while defending the installation. Search operations for the missing soldier are ongoing. Officials have not confirmed whether he is believed to be dead or captured.

The attack in Jordan is part of a broader pattern of Iranian retaliation following weeks of escalating tensions. Iran has repeatedly targeted US assets across the region since the US began its bombing campaign eight days ago.

US retaliates with eighth night of airstrikes

In response to the attack, the United States launched a fresh wave of airstrikes against Iranian military targets late Saturday. Explosions were reported in multiple cities across Iran as US warplanes and missiles struck for the eighth consecutive night.

The strikes targeted what the Pentagon described as 'command and control centers, weapons storage facilities, and launch sites' used by the Iranian military and its proxy forces. Tehran has not released an official toll from the latest bombings.

The back-and-forth strikes have now become a nightly routine, with no signs of de-escalation from either side. The US has vowed to continue its campaign until Iran ceases all attacks on American forces and allies in the region.

Escalating regional tensions

Jordan's role as a staging ground for US operations has drawn the kingdom deeper into the conflict. Amman has officially condemned the attack on its soil and called for restraint, but has not publicly taken sides.

Neighbouring countries, including Saudi Arabia and Iraq, are watching the developments with growing alarm. The conflict risks drawing in a wider circle of nations as Iranian proxies in Yemen, Syria and Lebanon have also stepped up attacks.

The United Nations has called for an immediate ceasefire, but both Washington and Tehran have rejected the appeal. Diplomatic channels remain largely frozen as the two sides trade fire.

What comes next

With the death toll rising and no end in sight to the nightly bombings, both militaries are preparing for a prolonged confrontation. The US is expected to reinforce its regional presence, while Iran may seek to strike at softer targets in the Gulf and beyond. The coming days will test whether either side blinks first or the conflict expands further.
