Supreme Court Orders SIT Probe Into Student Protest Violence, Directs Release of Detainees Without Criminal Re

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court orders a Special Investigation Team (SIT) probe into the violence during student protests.
  • States are allowed to investigate FIRs related to the protests.
  • Court directs release of detained students who have no criminal record and bars coercive action against them.

Supreme Court Orders SIT Probe into Student Protest Violence

The Supreme Court on Tuesday ordered a Special Investigation Team (SIT) to probe the violence that erupted during student protests across the country. The court's directive came amid multiple petitions seeking a fair investigation into the incidents, which saw clashes between protesters and authorities.

The bench, while hearing the matter, made it clear that any excesses committed during the protests must be dealt with strictly. "Whosoever committed excesses must be taken to task," the court observed, signaling a zero-tolerance approach to the violence.

States Allowed to Investigate FIRs, But With Conditions

The Supreme Court permitted state governments to investigate the First Information Reports (FIRs) filed in connection with the protests. However, the court imposed conditions to ensure transparency and fairness in the process.

The court barred any coercive action against the detained students, especially those with no criminal record. It directed the immediate release of such detainees, emphasizing that peaceful protesters should not be penalized for the actions of a few.

Release of Detained Students Without Criminal Past

In a significant relief for student activists, the top court ordered states to release all detained protesters who do not have a criminal background. The directive applies to those held during the recent wave of demonstrations.

The court's order is expected to de-escalate tensions on campuses, where many students were arrested amid allegations of heavy-handed policing. Legal experts say the ruling could set a precedent for how future student protests are handled.

Court Bars Coercive Action, SIT to Investigate Excesses

The Supreme Court also restrained authorities from taking any coercive steps against the released students. This means no fresh arrests or legal harassment can be initiated against them solely for their participation in the protests.

The newly formed SIT will now examine the role of both protesters and law enforcement agencies. The court has asked the SIT to submit a preliminary report within four weeks, detailing the findings on the violence and any misconduct by officials.

What Happens Next?

The SIT is expected to begin its investigation immediately, with a focus on identifying those responsible for the violence. The court will review the progress in the next hearing, scheduled for late August. For now, students and activists await the implementation of the release orders, while authorities prepare to comply with the court's directives.
