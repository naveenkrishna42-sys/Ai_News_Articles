Bengaluru’s Infrastructure Crisis: A Crisis of Accountability

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-08-26 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Unscientific road engineering and poor maintenance are primary contributors to fatalities.
  • Lack of accountability among civic agencies like BBMP and BMRCL remains a core issue.
  • Rapid, unplanned urban expansion is outpacing infrastructure durability.

The Anatomy of Neglect

Bengaluru’s roads have transitioned from mere thoroughfares to hazardous terrain. The frequent reports of fatal accidents are not merely statistical anomalies; they are the logical conclusion of years of deferred maintenance and poor urban planning.

When a city grows at the breakneck speed seen in the IT capital, infrastructure must be anticipatory rather than reactive. Instead, we see a cycle of 'patchwork' repairs that fail to withstand the city’s monsoon cycles, creating a persistent cycle of potholes and uneven surfaces.

Systemic Failures in Road Design

The core issue lies in the lack of an integrated urban transport policy. Multiple agencies—the BBMP, BMRCL, and state utility departments—often work in silos. This leads to the 'dig-and-forget' culture where roads are excavated for utility lines and left in a state of disrepair for months.

Scientific road engineering requires proper drainage, load-bearing surfacing, and clear lane markings. Currently, these are treated as optional luxuries rather than safety requirements. The result is a chaotic environment where heavy machinery and two-wheelers compete for space on crumbling asphalt.

The Cost of Inaction

Financial costs aside, the human toll is mounting. Families are often left to navigate a labyrinth of bureaucratic apathy when seeking compensation or accountability for accidents caused by road defects. The legal framework exists, but enforcement is lethargic.

Accountability must shift from the contractor level to the executive level. Until civic officials face professional consequences for negligence in road maintenance, the status quo will remain unchanged.

  • Proactive Maintenance: Routine, scheduled resurfacing instead of emergency patchwork.
  • Inter-agency Coordination: Unified digital mapping to prevent repetitive road digging.
  • Public Accountability: A transparent, time-bound grievance portal for reporting road hazards.
  • Engineering Audits: Mandatory third-party safety audits for all arterial roads.

Looking Ahead

Technology offers a path forward through automated road monitoring and AI-driven traffic management. However, technology is only a tool; it cannot replace the political will required to prioritize citizen safety over short-term fiscal savings. Bengaluru requires a fundamental overhaul of how it views road safety—not as a maintenance chore, but as a critical component of its economic viability.
