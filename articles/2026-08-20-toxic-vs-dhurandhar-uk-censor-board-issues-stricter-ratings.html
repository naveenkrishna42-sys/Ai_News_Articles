Certification Disparities in International Markets

The British Board of Film Classification (BBFC) has released its latest ratings for two major upcoming Indian film releases. Yash’s highly anticipated project Toxic has been assigned a harsher age rating compared to Ranveer Singh’s Dhurandhar. This classification decision has drawn attention to how international boards interpret the content of Indian commercial cinema.

While both films represent high-budget productions targeting global audiences, the disparity in their UK ratings is notable. Industry observers suggest that specific scenes involving intense action or thematic elements in Toxic likely triggered the more restrictive classification. The BBFC maintains rigorous standards regarding the impact of violence and tone on younger viewers.

Understanding the BBFC Process

The UK rating system operates independently of the Central Board of Film Certification (CBFC) in India. Films are evaluated based on factors such as strong language, sexual references, and depictions of physical harm. A stricter rating often limits the potential demographic reach of a film in international markets.

Dhurandhar, featuring Ranveer Singh, appears to have navigated these criteria with fewer regulatory hurdles. By securing a more accessible rating, the film may find a broader base of theatre-goers in the United Kingdom. Producers typically adjust international cuts to avoid such ratings, though it remains unclear if any modifications were attempted for these specific titles.
