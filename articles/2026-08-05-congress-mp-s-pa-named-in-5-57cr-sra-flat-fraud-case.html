Congress MP's PA Named in ₹5.57cr SRA Flat Fraud Case

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A personal assistant to a Congress MP has been named in a ₹5.57 crore SRA flat fraud case.
  • The fraud allegedly involves irregularities in allotment of redevelopment flats under the Slum Rehabilitation Authority scheme.
  • Mumbai police have registered a case and are investigating the matter; no arrests have been reported yet.

The personal assistant of a Congress Member of Parliament has been named in a ₹5.57 crore fraud linked to Slum Rehabilitation Authority (SRA) flats in Mumbai. The development has put the spotlight back on alleged irregularities in the city's redevelopment projects, which have long been a contentious issue.

According to the complaint, the fraud involves manipulation in the allotment of SRA flats, a scheme meant to provide housing to slum dwellers. The PA, whose identity has not been officially disclosed, is accused of being part of a racket that siphoned off funds meant for rehabilitation.

How the fraud came to light

The case was registered after a formal complaint was filed with the Mumbai police. The complainant alleged that the accused, including the PA, forged documents and misrepresented facts to secure flats under the SRA scheme. These flats, meant for eligible slum residents, were allegedly diverted to ineligible beneficiaries, causing a loss of ₹5.57 crore to the state exchequer.

Police sources said the investigation is in its early stages. They are examining bank records, property documents, and communication trails to establish the exact role of each accused. The PA's alleged involvement is said to have been uncovered during a preliminary inquiry into the matter.

Political connections under scanner

The case has taken a political turn, given the PA's association with a sitting Congress MP. While the MP has not been named in the complaint, opposition parties are likely to seize on the issue to target the Congress ahead of upcoming civic elections in Mumbai. The party has so far remained silent on the matter.

This is not the first time SRA projects have faced allegations of corruption. Over the years, several cases of fraudulent allotments and builder-politician nexus have surfaced, but convictions have been rare. The latest case adds to the growing scrutiny of the redevelopment sector in the city.

What investigators are looking into

The police are probing whether the PA acted alone or in collusion with builders and SRA officials. They are also checking if similar frauds have occurred in other SRA projects in the city. A forensic audit of the project's records has been recommended to trace the flow of funds.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether any arrests will be made. The accused have been served notices to appear for questioning, but their responses are still awaited. The complainant has demanded a thorough investigation and swift action against all those involved.

Reactions and next steps

Local residents and housing rights activists have expressed outrage, calling for transparency in SRA allotments. Some have demanded that the state government conduct a comprehensive audit of all SRA projects to restore public trust.

The case is expected to be heard in court soon, where the police will likely seek custody of the accused for further interrogation. Meanwhile, the Congress MP has not issued a public statement, leaving room for speculation about the political fallout.

As the investigation progresses, all eyes will be on how the Mumbai police handle this high-profile case and whether it leads to broader reforms in the SRA framework. For now, the focus remains on unravelling the full extent of the fraud and ensuring accountability.
