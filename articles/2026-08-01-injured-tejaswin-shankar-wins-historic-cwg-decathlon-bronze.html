Injured Tejaswin Shankar wins historic CWG decathlon bronze

📅 2026-08-01 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tejaswin Shankar wins bronze in decathlon at CWG 2026, a first for India.
  • He overcame a knee injury sustained earlier in the competition.
  • His high jump performance pushed him into medal contention.
  • Neeraj Chopra took silver in javelin on the same day.
  • Indian boxers also dominated the medal tally on Day 9.

A historic leap despite the pain

Tejaswin Shankar did not just win a medal. He rewrote the record books. On Day 9 of the Commonwealth Games, the 27-year-old from Haryana became the first Indian to secure a decathlon medal, clinching bronze despite nursing a knee injury that could have ended his campaign.

The decathlon is athletics' most punishing event—ten disciplines spread over two days, testing speed, strength, and endurance. For Shankar, the challenge was compounded by the injury, which flared up during the competition. Yet, he pushed through, delivering a performance that will be remembered for years.

The turning point: high jump heroics

Shankar's march to the podium was anchored in his signature event, the high jump. A leap that placed him second in the standings mid-competition proved decisive. It was a moment that shifted momentum, lifting him from the chasing pack into genuine medal contention.

His scores across the remaining events held up, and as the final standings were tallied, the bronze was confirmed. The achievement is all the more remarkable given the physical toll the decathlon exacts. Officials have not yet detailed the extent of the knee injury, but Shankar's grit was evident to all who watched.

A day of mixed fortunes for India

Shankar's bronze was one of several headline results on a busy day for Indian athletics. Neeraj Chopra, the Olympic champion, added a silver in the javelin throw—a result that, while below his soaring standards, still underlined his consistency at the highest level.

The boxing ring brought further cheer, with Indian pugilists dominating their bouts and adding multiple medals to the tally. The combined haul from track, field, and boxing gave India its most productive day of the Games so far.

What this means for Indian athletics

The decathlon has long been a domain where Indian athletes struggled to make a mark. The event demands versatility—sprints, jumps, throws, and distance races—and Shankar's bronze signals a shift in the country's athletic depth. His journey, from a high jump specialist to a decathlon medalist, is a testament to his versatility and hard work.

For Shankar, the focus now shifts to recovery and the road ahead. The injury will need careful management, but the confidence from this performance could spur him toward the Asian Games and the next World Championships.

As the Commonwealth Games enter their final days, India's athletics contingent has already exceeded expectations. Shankar's historic medal is a benchmark—one that future decathletes from the country will now aim to surpass. The immediate question is how he recovers, and whether this bronze becomes a stepping stone to greater glory.
