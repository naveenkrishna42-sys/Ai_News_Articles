Deepika Padukone And Ranveer Singh Seek Blessings At Siddhivinayak

Deepika Padukone and Ranveer Singh visited Mumbai's Siddhivinayak Temple on Friday, days ahead of the arrival of their second child. The couple, already parents to a daughter born in 2024, were photographed seeking blessings at the Prabhadevi shrine. Padukone's maternity wardrobe has been closely tracked through this pregnancy, and this temple visit delivered her most traditionally rooted look yet.

The ensemble came from Delhi-based couturier Mayyur Girotra, a designer she has worn repeatedly at weddings and festive occasions. The choice signals a deliberate styling strategy: devotional occasions call for Indian craftsmanship, not international labels.

What Deepika Wore: The Mayyur Girotra Couture Breakdown

The custom outfit paired a richly embroidered kurta-style top with a flowing, relaxed lower garment — a silhouette engineered for late-stage pregnancy comfort without sacrificing occasion-appropriate formality. Girotra's signature includes gota patti detailing, mirror work, and hand-embroidered thread work rooted in Punjabi and Rajasthani craft traditions.

Colour selection matters here. Deepika has favoured warm, auspicious tones — think sindoori reds, marigold yellows, and deep greens — for temple visits across both pregnancies. The palette photographs well under Siddhivinayak's artificial lighting and reads as respectful rather than performative.

Why This Look Matters For Maternity Fashion

Indian maternity fashion remains underserved at the premium end. Most labels scale up standard sizes rather than design for changing bodies. Deepika's pregnancy wardrobe — mixing Girotra couture with relaxed daywear — demonstrates that maternity styling can hold couture-level craft. For buyers, this is the practical takeaway: demand bespoke tailoring with adjustable panels, empire waistlines, and lightweight linings. These details decide whether an outfit survives a two-hour temple queue in Mumbai humidity.

Her repeat patronage of Girotra also carries commercial weight. A single high-visibility appearance by Padukone historically drives measurable search interest in the designer's label. For a couturier operating in the Rs 1.5-5 lakh bridal and festive segment, this is equivalent to a prime-time campaign.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Comfort-first couture: Relaxed silhouettes and breathable fabrics suit late-stage pregnancy in Mumbai's September heat.
  • Cultural alignment: Traditional Indian craft at a religious site reads as authentic, not trend-driven.
  • Designer equity: Repeat wear of Mayyur Girotra builds long-term brand association beyond one-off red-carpet placements.
  • Buyer signal: Premium ethnic wear labels should note rising demand for made-to-measure maternity festive wear.
  • Privacy boundary: The couple has not disclosed the baby's arrival date; reports remain speculative.

Pricing And Value Context

Custom festive couture from Girotra's tier typically starts around Rs 1.5 lakh and climbs past Rs 5 lakh for heavy hand embroidery. Off-the-rack festive wear with similar visual impact retails between Rs 15,000 and Rs 60,000. The gap buys fit precision, fabric quality, and artisan hours — not just a label. For expectant mothers unwilling to compromise on occasion dressing, made-to-measure remains the only reliable route.

Deepika and Ranveer have kept details of the pregnancy private, consistent with their approach during their first child's birth. What the public sees is a carefully calibrated wardrobe that treats maternity as a design problem worth solving properly.
