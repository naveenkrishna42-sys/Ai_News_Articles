Bombay High Court denies interim relief to journalist expelled by Mumbai Press Club

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bombay High Court denied interim relief to a journalist expelled from the Mumbai Press Club.
  • The expulsion stemmed from organising a meeting related to the Bhima-Koregaon anniversary.
  • The court's decision leaves the journalist's membership status unchanged for now.

The Bombay High Court on Wednesday refused to grant interim relief to a journalist who was expelled from the Mumbai Press Club over the organisation of a meeting on the Bhima-Koregaon anniversary. The court's order means the journalist will not be reinstated into the club's membership for now.

Case Background

The journalist was expelled by the Mumbai Press Club's managing committee earlier this year. The club's decision followed a meeting the journalist had helped organise to mark the anniversary of the Battle of Bhima-Koregaon.

The meeting had become a flashpoint. The club's leadership argued that the event violated its code of conduct. The journalist challenged the expulsion in court, arguing that the action was arbitrary and violated natural justice.

Court's Reasoning

Justice G.S. Patel, hearing the matter, observed that the journalist had not made out a case for interim relief. The court noted that the club's internal processes had been followed.

The judge pointed out that the journalist's removal was not a simple matter of membership rights. The club, as a private body, had the authority to regulate its own affairs. The court did not find any prima facie illegality in the expulsion at this stage.

The bench also considered the journalist's argument that the club's action had damaged his professional reputation. However, the court said that reputational harm alone was not enough to grant interim relief without a stronger legal basis.

What the Journalist Argued

Counsel for the journalist submitted that the expulsion was a direct attack on press freedom. They argued that the meeting was a legitimate journalistic exercise and that the club's committee had acted with a predetermined bias.

The lawyer also contended that the club's code of conduct was vague. The journalist had not been given a proper hearing before the decision was made, they said. The court, however, was not convinced that these points warranted immediate intervention.

Press Club's Stance

The Mumbai Press Club defended its decision. Its lawyers argued that the meeting in question had created a disturbance among members. The club maintained that it had acted within its rights to maintain discipline.

The club's committee stated that the event had violated its guidelines. It added that the decision to expel was taken after due deliberation. The court's interim order effectively upholds the club's position for the time being.

Next Steps

The court has not disposed of the main petition. The journalist's legal challenge against the expulsion will still be heard on its merits. The next hearing is expected in the coming weeks.

The case raises questions about the limits of internal disciplinary actions by professional bodies. The final outcome will likely hinge on whether the court finds the club's procedure and reasoning legally sound.
