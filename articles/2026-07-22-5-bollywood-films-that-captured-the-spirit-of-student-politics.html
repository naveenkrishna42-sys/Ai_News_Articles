5 Bollywood films that captured the spirit of student politics

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bollywood has occasionally depicted student politics with realism and impact.
  • Five films stand out for capturing the energy and conflict of campus life.
  • These movies range from iconic dramas to recent hits.

Student politics in India has long been a crucible for future leaders and a mirror of societal tensions. Bollywood, despite its penchant for escapism, has occasionally turned its lens on this charged world. A recent report by Marksmen Daily highlights five films that managed to capture the spirit of student politics with authenticity and impact.

Rang De Basanti (2006)

Rakeysh Omprakash Mehra's film remains the gold standard. It follows a group of Delhi University students whose apathy transforms into radical activism after they participate in a documentary about freedom fighters. The film blurs the line between past and present, showing how the same fire that drove Bhagat Singh can ignite modern youth.

The movie resonated because it didn't preach. It showed students grappling with corruption, friendship, and the cost of conviction. Its climax, where the characters take justice into their own hands, sparked debates that are still alive.

Hazaaron Khwaishein Aisi (2003)

Set during the Emergency of the 1970s, Sudhir Mishra's film is a quiet masterpiece. It follows three characters—a firebrand student leader, a disillusioned activist, and a woman caught between them—against the backdrop of India's most turbulent political period.

The film captures the idealism and the compromises of student politics. It doesn't romanticise the movement; it shows the personal costs, the betrayals, and the slow erosion of hope. The performances by Kay Kay Menon, Shiney Ahuja, and Chitrangada Singh are haunting.

Dil Chahta Hai (2001)

While not a political film in the traditional sense, Farhan Akhtar's debut features a subplot that defines student politics for a generation. Aamir Khan's character, Akash, is a wealthy, carefree student who clashes with a college radical, played by Akshay Kapoor.

The confrontation on the college steps, with Akash mocking the activist's slogans, captures the apathy of privilege versus the passion of ideology. It's a brief moment, but it perfectly encapsulates the divide that exists on every Indian campus.

Chak De! India (2007)

Again, not a campus film, but the opening sequence is essential. Shah Rukh Khan's Kabir Khan, a former hockey player, is accused of betraying the nation. The film uses the setting of a university to show how student politics can turn toxic, with mob mentality overriding reason.

The scene where Kabir is humiliated by student leaders is a stark reminder of how quickly political fervour can become persecution. It's a powerful statement about the responsibility that comes with influence.

Article 15 (2019)

Anubhav Sinha's film is a thriller about caste violence, but it opens with a crucial sequence on a university campus. Ayushmann Khurrana's character, a police officer, investigates the murder of two Dalit girls. The film shows how student politics is often a front for entrenched caste hierarchies.

The campus scenes are chilling in their realism. They depict how student unions can perpetuate discrimination under the guise of ideology. The film forces viewers to confront uncomfortable truths about the spaces we consider progressive.

These five films, each in its own way, remind us that student politics is not just about elections and slogans. It is about power, identity, and the choices that define a generation. As campus activism continues to evolve, Bollywood's gaze on it remains sporadic but potent.

What remains to be seen is whether future filmmakers will continue to explore this terrain with the same honesty, or retreat into safer, more commercial narratives.
