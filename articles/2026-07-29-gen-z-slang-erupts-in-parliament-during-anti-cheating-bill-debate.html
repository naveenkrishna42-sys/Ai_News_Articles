Gen Z Slang Erupts in Parliament During Anti-Cheating Bill Debate

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Opposition MPs used Gen Z slang like 'delulu' and 'clock it' in parliament.
  • The 'cockroach' protest and new vocabulary marked a generational shift in parliamentary debate.
  • The Public Examinations Amendment Bill 2026 was passed by Lok Sabha.
  • The bill proposes a minimum 5-year jail term and 10-year maximum for exam malpractices.

Slang Meets Statute in Lok Sabha

India's parliament saw a rare eruption of Gen Z vocabulary this week as opposition MPs debated the Public Examinations Amendment Bill 2026. Terms like 'delulu', 'clock it', and even a shouted reference to a 'cockroach' protest echoed through the Lok Sabha chamber.

The language marked a sharp departure from the usual parliamentary lexicon. Young MPs and their aides brought street slang into the House to criticise the government's anti-paper leak bill. The bill, which cleared the Lok Sabha, proposes a minimum five-year jail term and steeper fines for exam malpractices.

Decoding the Debate

'Delulu' — short for delusional — was used by opposition members to describe the government's claim that the bill alone would end exam fraud. 'Clock it', a phrase meaning to notice or call out something, was hurled across the aisle when a ruling party MP tried to interrupt a speech.

The 'cockroach' protest drew the most attention. An MP likened the persistence of paper leaks to cockroaches that survive all attempts at extermination. The remark drew both laughter and sharp retorts from treasury benches.

Veteran MPs appeared baffled. Several asked the Speaker to explain terms like 'kuchu-puchu' and 'delulu' before ruling them unparliamentary.

The Bill's Tough Provisions

The Public Examinations Amendment Bill 2026 aims to curb rampant paper leaks that have disrupted exams for lakhs of students. The bill mandates a minimum five-year jail term, extendable to ten years, for those found guilty of leaking question papers or operating exam fraud syndicates.

Fines have been raised substantially. Offenders could be penalised up to Rs 1 crore. The bill also introduces provisions for attaching property of accused individuals.

However, implementation challenges remain. The backlog of cases in trial courts means many accused could remain out on bail for years. Officials have not yet confirmed how the new law will fast-track these cases.

A Generational Shift in Politics

The use of Gen Z slang signals a changing of the guard in Indian politics. Younger MPs, many elected in the 2024 general elections, are bringing their lived language into parliament. Political analysts note that this shift makes legislative debates more relatable to young voters.

But the trend has also drawn criticism. Some argue that casual slang dilutes the seriousness of parliamentary proceedings. The Speaker has not yet issued a formal advisory on the use of such terms.

The bill will now move to the Rajya Sabha. The opposition has indicated it will demand stricter oversight mechanisms and faster trial courts before giving its final nod.
