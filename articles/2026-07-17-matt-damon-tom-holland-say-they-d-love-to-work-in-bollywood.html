Matt Damon, Tom Holland say they'd love to work in Bollywood

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Matt Damon and Tom Holland have expressed interest in working in Bollywood.
  • The actors praised Indian cinema's energy and storytelling.
  • No specific projects have been announced yet.

Hollywood heavyweights Matt Damon and Tom Holland have said they are ready for a Bollywood break. In a recent interaction, both actors spoke warmly about Indian cinema and did not rule out starring in a Hindi film.

Damon, known for the Bourne series and The Martian, said he would "jump at the opportunity" to work in Bollywood. Holland, who plays Spider-Man, echoed the sentiment, calling Indian films "incredibly vibrant."

What the stars said

Damon described Bollywood as a space with immense energy. He said he has watched several Indian films and admires the scale and emotion they bring to storytelling.

Holland added that he finds Indian cinema unique in its ability to blend drama, music and spectacle. He said he would love to be part of a project that captures that spirit.

No concrete plans yet

While both actors have expressed enthusiasm, no official offers or projects have been announced. Industry insiders say such statements often generate buzz but rarely lead to immediate collaborations.

Indian producers have in the past approached Hollywood stars for cameos or lead roles. However, logistical challenges and cultural differences have limited such crossovers.

Growing Hollywood interest in India

This is not the first time international stars have shown interest in Bollywood. Actors like Priyanka Chopra and Deepika Padukone have successfully bridged the two industries, and filmmakers are increasingly looking at global talent.

Damon and Holland's comments come at a time when Indian cinema is expanding its global footprint. Films like RRR and Pathaan have drawn international audiences, making Bollywood a more attractive destination for foreign actors.

Whether the two stars actually make their Bollywood debut remains to be seen. But for now, their words have added to the buzz around cross-cultural filmmaking. Industry watchers will be keen to see if any producer picks up the thread.
