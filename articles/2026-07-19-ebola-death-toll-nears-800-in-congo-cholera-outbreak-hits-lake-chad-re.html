Ebola Death Toll Nears 800 in Congo; Cholera Outbreak Hits Lake Chad Region

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ebola death toll in DR Congo approaches 800
  • WHO warns transport of dead bodies risks further spread of the virus
  • Cholera outbreak reported in the Lake Chad region
  • Ebola response in Congo hit by attacks, strikes, and staff exodus
  • Bundibugyo virus strain identified in DR Congo and Uganda

The death toll from the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has climbed to nearly 800, even as health workers grapple with a separate cholera crisis unfolding in the Lake Chad basin. The dual health emergencies are stretching resources across central and west Africa.

Ebola toll climbs amid response setbacks

The World Health Organization has warned that the transport of dead bodies within Congo is creating new risks for further Ebola spread. Traditional burial practices, which involve close contact with the deceased, have been a known driver of transmission in past outbreaks.

The current outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, a strain of Ebola that has been identified in both the Democratic Republic of Congo and neighbouring Uganda. Health authorities are racing to contain the virus as the case count continues to rise.

Attacks, strikes hamper containment efforts

The Ebola response in Congo has been severely disrupted by a series of attacks on health facilities, strikes by health workers, and a significant exodus of staff from affected areas. These disruptions have slowed contact tracing, vaccination campaigns, and safe burial efforts.

According to the WHO, the insecurity has made it difficult to reach affected communities. Some health workers have fled their posts, leaving gaps in the response infrastructure. The situation has drawn comparisons to earlier outbreaks where community mistrust and violence hindered containment.

‘Walking Ebola’ phenomenon complicates containment

Health experts have pointed to the phenomenon of “walking Ebola” as a key reason the outbreak has been so difficult to stop. The term refers to infected individuals who are still mobile and can travel long distances before showing severe symptoms, silently spreading the virus.

Understanding this pattern has helped shape treatment strategies. Health workers are now focusing on early detection and isolation, even among those who do not yet appear critically ill. The approach aims to break the chain of transmission before it widens.

Cholera outbreak hits Lake Chad region

Separately, a cholera outbreak has been reported in the Lake Chad region, an area already grappling with conflict, displacement, and food insecurity. The waterborne disease spreads rapidly in areas with poor sanitation and limited access to clean drinking water.

Health agencies are mobilising resources to set up treatment centres and distribute oral rehydration salts. The outbreak adds another layer of strain to a region where health systems are already fragile.

The WHO has not yet confirmed the exact number of cholera cases or deaths in the Lake Chad outbreak. Officials are working with local health ministries to assess the scale of the emergency and coordinate a response.

The coming weeks will be critical for both outbreaks. In Congo, restoring security and trust among health workers and communities will be essential to slowing Ebola’s spread. In the Lake Chad region, rapid access to clean water and medical care will determine how far the cholera outbreak reaches.
