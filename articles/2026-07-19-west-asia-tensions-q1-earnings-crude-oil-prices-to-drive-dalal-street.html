West Asia tensions, Q1 earnings, crude oil prices to drive Dalal Street this week

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • West Asia tensions, particularly the US-Iran conflict, are pushing crude oil prices higher, impacting market sentiment.
  • First-quarter earnings season continues with major results from Infosys and other index heavyweights.
  • Rupee volatility against the dollar and flash PMI data releases will be closely watched by traders.

Dalal Street is bracing for a volatile week as a cocktail of global and domestic factors takes centre stage. Investors will juggle escalating West Asia tensions, a sharp rise in crude oil prices, the ongoing first-quarter earnings season, and key macroeconomic data releases.

The benchmark indices ended the previous week on a mixed note. While the Nifty managed to hold above the 24,500 mark, concerns over geopolitical instability kept gains in check. The coming sessions are expected to test market resilience.

West Asia Conflict and Crude Oil Jitters

The primary overhang on the market remains the conflict in West Asia. The escalating US-Iran confrontation has pushed global crude oil prices higher, with Brent crude trading near multi-month highs. A sustained spike in oil prices could worsen India's trade deficit and stoke inflationary pressures.

Higher crude prices also hurt corporate margins, particularly for oil marketing companies and sectors like aviation. Analysts warn that any further escalation could trigger foreign portfolio investor (FPI) outflows as risk appetite shrinks globally.

Q1 Earnings: Infosys and More

The earnings calendar is packed this week. Infosys is set to report its April-June quarter numbers, and its performance will set the tone for the IT sector. Other major companies scheduled to declare results include HDFC Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, and Reliance Industries.

Market participants will scrutinise management commentary on demand environment, deal pipelines, and margin guidance. The financial sector's earnings, especially from private banks, will be crucial for index direction.

Analysts expect tepid earnings growth in Q1 due to weak rural demand and election-related disruptions. However, any positive surprise could lift sentiment.

Rupee, PMI Data, and FII Activity

The rupee has been under pressure against the US dollar, breaching the 83.50 mark last week. A weaker rupee adds to import costs, especially for crude oil. The Reserve Bank of India's intervention will be watched closely.

Flash Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) data for July is due later this week. Services and manufacturing PMI readings are expected to remain in expansion territory, but any sharp deviation could influence market moves.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have been net sellers in recent sessions, and their continued activity will be a key sentiment driver. Domestic institutional buying has partially offset outflows, but global cues remain dominant.

Initial public offerings (IPOs) and listings will also keep the primary market buzzing. Several small and mid-sized companies are expected to debut this week, providing cues for investor appetite.

What to Watch

Markets will closely track any diplomatic developments in West Asia and the trajectory of crude oil prices. The RBI's stance on the rupee and any fresh FII flow data will determine near-term direction. With earnings season in full swing, stock-specific action is likely to dominate.
