Guwahati Carnage: West Indies Breach the 400 Mark

The Barsapara Cricket Stadium in Guwahati bore witness to an unrelenting display of power hitting as West Indies posted a commanding 405 against India in the second One-Day International. Capitalising on true bounce and a short boundary layout, the Caribbean batting lineup mounted their assault from the opening powerplay and maintained high scoring velocity throughout the 50 overs.

India's bowling attack struggled for containment, failing to exploit early morning moisture or generate reverse swing in the latter stages. West Indies made their tactical intentions evident early, leveraging calculated aerial aggression to push India's fielders onto the ropes and prevent bowlers from settling into tight lines.

Campbell's Record-Breaking Blitzkrieg

Opener John Campbell set the tone with a devastating display of clean hitting, reaching his century in just 65 deliveries. His whirlwind knock eclipsed historical records for the fastest ODI century by a West Indian against India, outstripping milestones set by Chris Gayle and Rovman Powell. Campbell targeted the straight boundary, dismantling both front-line seam and spin with ruthless efficiency.

His aggressive approach disrupted India's bowling rotations inside the first ten overs. Rather than allowing defensive fielding sets to stem the flow of runs, Campbell consistently cleared mid-on and mid-off, creating an insurmountable scoring rate that left the Indian captain scrambling for answers.

Hope and Jangoo Complete the Demolition

While Campbell provided the initial explosion, captain Shai Hope operated as the tactical anchor before accelerating late in his innings. Hope controlled the middle overs with pristine strike rotation and targeted placement, compiling a measured 108 that blunted India's middle-overs squeeze. His partnership with Amir Jangoo eliminated any chance of an Indian fightback.

Jangoo, stepping up in the late overs, produced an authoritative maiden ODI century. His calculated assault against death-bowling yorkers and slower bouncers converted a strong 320-run base into a match-defining total above 400. The trio of centuries marked a historic collective milestone, highlighting West Indies' strategic resurgence in modern 50-over cricket.

Key Match Realities and Tactical Takeaways

  • Bowling Execution Failures: India's pacers conceded excess boundary balls during the powerplay, failing to execute yorkers during the death overs.
  • Guwahati Pitch Dynamics: The true surface offered zero lateral movement off the seam, penalizing standard hit-the-deck lengths.
  • Mid-Innings Consolidation: Hope and Jangoo denied Indian spinners breakthroughs through active sweep shots and calculated strike rotation.
  • Monumental Chase: Requiring over eight runs per over from ball one, India's top order faces immense pressure to produce a historically flawless chase.
