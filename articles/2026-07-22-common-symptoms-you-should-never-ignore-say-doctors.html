Common Symptoms You Should Never Ignore, Say Doctors

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Persistent chest pain, shortness of breath, or unexplained weight loss should never be ignored.
  • Doctors urge people to pay attention to changes in bowel habits, persistent cough, or unusual bleeding.
  • Early detection of conditions like heart disease or cancer greatly improves treatment outcomes.

When a Symptom Becomes a Warning

It starts with something small. A persistent cough that refuses to leave. An ache that lingers for weeks. Many Indians brush off these signs as part of daily stress or ageing. Doctors, however, say such symptoms are often the body's way of signalling deeper trouble.

According to a recent report in The Hindu, healthcare professionals across the country are urging people to stop 'learning to live with' symptoms that could indicate serious illness. The message is simple: if a symptom persists beyond two weeks, it is time to see a doctor.

Symptoms That Demand Immediate Attention

Some signs should never be ignored. Chest pain or discomfort, especially if accompanied by breathlessness or sweating, could point to a heart attack. Sudden weakness on one side of the body, difficulty speaking, or a severe headache might signal a stroke.

Unexplained weight loss — losing more than 5% of your body weight in six months without trying — is another red flag. So is persistent fever, night sweats, or a cough that brings up blood. These can indicate infections like tuberculosis or even cancer.

Changes in Bowel and Bladder Habits

A change in bowel habits lasting more than a few weeks — such as constipation, diarrhoea, or blood in the stool — warrants a check-up. Similarly, blood in the urine or pain while passing urine should not be dismissed as a simple infection. These symptoms can indicate colorectal or bladder cancers, which are more treatable when caught early.

Unusual Bleeding or Lumps

Any unexplained bleeding — from the nose, gums, or in vomit — should be investigated. Women should also watch for abnormal vaginal bleeding between periods or after menopause. Lumps or swellings in the breast, testicle, or anywhere on the body that do not go away need medical evaluation.

Why Early Detection Matters

Doctors stress that ignoring symptoms does not make them go away. In many cases, it just delays diagnosis. Conditions like heart disease, diabetes, and cancer progress silently. By the time symptoms become severe, treatment options may be limited.

The Indian healthcare system already faces a heavy burden of late-stage diagnoses. Simple screenings — blood tests, imaging, or endoscopies — can catch problems early. But they require patients to come forward with their complaints first.

Doctors also advise paying attention to changes in skin moles, persistent indigestion, or difficulty swallowing. These are often dismissed as minor issues but can be early signs of serious illnesses.

What to Do Next

If you have any symptom that has lasted for more than two weeks, do not wait for it to get worse. Make an appointment with a general practitioner. Keep a record of when the symptom started, how often it occurs, and what makes it better or worse.

Doctors also recommend regular health check-ups, even if you feel fine. Blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol levels can be managed before they cause damage. The goal is not to live in fear, but to be aware.

In the coming months, public health campaigns are expected to focus on symptom awareness. The message is clear: your body is not supposed to hurt all the time. Listen to it.
