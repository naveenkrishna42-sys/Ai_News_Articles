Amex Expands Centurion Lounge Network to Continental Europe

American Express has confirmed it will open its first Centurion Lounge in Continental Europe, marking a significant expansion of its proprietary airport lounge network beyond the United States, Hong Kong, and Australia. The move, reported by Forbes, signals Amex's intent to capture high-spending travellers who transit through Europe's busiest hubs. For Indian cardholders—who hold Platinum, Platinum Travel, and Centurion cards in growing numbers—this development adds a new premium stopover option on routes to the US and UK.

What the New Lounge Offers: Specifications and Access

While Amex has not disclosed the exact airport or opening date, industry patterns suggest a major hub such as Frankfurt, Paris Charles de Gaulle, or Amsterdam Schiphol. Centurion Lounges are known for locally inspired cuisine, premium bar service, shower suites, and dedicated workspaces. The table below summarises typical Centurion Lounge specifications, based on existing locations, to help Indian travellers benchmark what to expect.

Why This Matters for Indian Cardholders

Indian travellers on Amex Platinum or Centurion cards currently rely on Priority Pass or third-party lounges for European layovers. A Centurion Lounge in Continental Europe offers a consistent, high-quality experience that Amex controls directly. For frequent flyers on routes like Mumbai–Frankfurt–New York or Delhi–Paris–London, this means a reliable place to refresh, dine, and work between connections. It also strengthens the value proposition of Amex's premium cards, which carry annual fees of ₹60,000 (Platinum) to ₹5 lakh (Centurion) in India.

Practical Implications for Points and Cashback Optimisation

Amex cardholders should note that Centurion Lounge access does not consume reward points or cashback. However, the lounge benefit is tied to the card's travel category, not to spending thresholds. Indian cardholders planning Europe trips in late 2026 or 2027 should verify access rules closer to the opening date, as Amex may adjust guest policies or introduce spending requirements to manage crowding—a common challenge at popular Centurion Lounges.

Pros and Cons: Real-World Highlights

  • Pro: Complimentary access for eligible Indian Amex Platinum and Centurion cardholders adds tangible value to high annual fees.
  • Pro: Consistent food, beverage, and shower quality—often superior to third-party lounges at European hubs.
  • Pro: Strategic location likely to serve major transit routes between India, Europe, and the US.
  • Con: No confirmed opening date or airport, creating uncertainty for near-term travel planning.
  • Con: Potential crowding and capacity limits, which have led to entry restrictions at some US Centurion Lounges.
  • Con: Guest fees for Platinum cardholders may apply, reducing the benefit for family travellers.

Competitive Context and Buyer Guide

Amex faces growing competition from Visa and Mastercard, which offer lounge access through programs like LoungeKey and Priority Pass. However, Centurion Lounges are proprietary, meaning Amex controls the experience and can tailor it to its premium base. For Indian consumers evaluating premium credit cards, this expansion is a differentiator. If you frequently transit Europe, the Amex Platinum's lounge network—now extending into Continental Europe—justifies a larger share of its fee. If your travel is mostly domestic or Asia-Pacific, the incremental value is lower.

Before applying or upgrading, compare Amex's lounge access against competitors like HDFC Infinia or Axis Magnus, which offer Priority Pass memberships. Amex's Centurion Lounge access remains exclusive to its own cards, making it a unique perk for cardholders who value airport luxury. As Amex finalises the location, Indian cardholders should watch for official announcements to plan their itineraries accordingly.
