India Weighs Tax Cuts to Lure Foreign Investors: A Strategic Pivot?

As India gears up for its Union Budget 2027, significant discussions are underway regarding potential tax reforms aimed at enhancing the nation's appeal to foreign investors. Central to these deliberations is the prospect of reconsidering the Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) tax, particularly for Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs). This move, advocated by key financial bodies like the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) and the Federation of Indian Chambers of Commerce & Industry (FICCI), signals a strategic intent to inject greater liquidity and confidence into Indian markets.

India's ambitious economic growth targets necessitate robust capital inflows. The government's deliberations reflect a pragmatic approach to ensure India remains a top-tier destination for global capital, navigating a competitive international investment landscape. The stakes are high, balancing the imperative to attract foreign funds with prudent fiscal management.

The Proposed Shift: Reforming LTCG Tax for FPIs

Understanding Long Term Capital Gains (LTCG) in India

Long Term Capital Gains tax applies to profits generated from the sale of assets held for over a specified period. For listed equities in India, gains exceeding ₹1 lakh from investments held for more than 12 months are currently taxed at 10%, without the benefit of indexation. This tax was reintroduced in the 2018 Budget, ending a long period of exemption, primarily to align the tax treatment of equity investments with other asset classes and broaden the tax base.

While designed to ensure equitable taxation, its reintroduction raised concerns among foreign investors regarding India's tax predictability and competitiveness. The current discussions aim to address these apprehensions, specifically for FPIs, acknowledging their crucial role in market dynamics and capital formation.

SEBI and FICCI's Advocacy for Change

SEBI, as the market regulator, has reportedly proposed alleviating the LTCG tax burden on FPIs. Their rationale centers on making Indian markets more attractive to global capital, thereby enhancing liquidity and market depth. A more favorable tax regime could encourage greater FPI participation, which often acts as a significant catalyst for market growth and stability.

Concurrently, FICCI has urged the government to consider a broader removal of LTCG tax on listed securities, coupled with a potential increase in the Securities Transaction Tax (STT). FICCI's argument underscores that a simpler, more predictable tax structure could significantly boost investor confidence, both domestic and foreign, and streamline investment processes, ultimately contributing to higher market valuations and economic expansion.

Potential Impact on India's Investment Landscape

Boosting Foreign Capital Inflows and Market Liquidity

A reduction or removal of LTCG tax for FPIs could immediately enhance the net returns for foreign investors, making Indian equities more appealing compared to other emerging markets. This direct financial incentive is expected to attract fresh capital, leading to increased demand for Indian stocks and potentially driving up market valuations. Higher FPI participation typically translates to greater market liquidity, making it easier for investors to buy and sell securities without significantly impacting prices.

Enhanced liquidity and depth are critical for a mature market, attracting larger institutional investors who prioritize ease of entry and exit. This influx of foreign capital not only supports listed companies but also indirectly signals global confidence in India's economic trajectory, potentially spurring further domestic and international investment across sectors.

Balancing Fiscal Prudence with Investor Appeal

The government's challenge lies in balancing the desire to attract foreign capital with the need for fiscal stability. While a tax cut promises increased investment, it also implies a potential reduction in tax revenue. This is where proposals like increasing the Securities Transaction Tax (STT) come into play. STT is levied on every transaction, offering a more stable and broad-based revenue stream compared to a capital gains tax, which is only realized upon profit-making sales.

The shift from LTCG to a higher STT, as suggested by FICCI, represents a strategic trade-off. It aims to reduce the burden on long-term investors (especially FPIs) while ensuring consistent revenue from high-frequency trading and overall market activity. This rebalancing could provide a robust framework that is both investor-friendly and fiscally responsible.
