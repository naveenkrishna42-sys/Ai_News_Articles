RBI proposes stricter data governance norms for banks, NBFCs

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI proposes new norms for data governance in banks and NBFCs.
  • Focus on strengthening risk management frameworks through better data handling.
  • Regulations aim to ensure data accuracy, security, and accountability.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has proposed a stricter set of data governance norms for commercial banks and Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs). The move is aimed at strengthening the overall risk management framework within the financial system.

The central bank released a draft circular on Wednesday, outlining fresh guidelines for data management, quality, and security. The new rules intend to bring more discipline and accountability in how financial institutions handle data.

Key provisions of the draft norms

The RBI has proposed that banks and NBFCs create a dedicated data management function. This unit will be responsible for ensuring data accuracy, completeness, and timeliness.

Institutions will also need to appoint a Chief Data Officer (CDO) or an equivalent senior official. The CDO will oversee data governance and report directly to the board or a board-level committee.

The draft circular also mandates a comprehensive data governance policy. This policy must cover data classification, data quality standards, data retention, and archival practices.

Focus on data quality and risk management

The RBI has emphasised that poor data quality can lead to flawed risk assessments and weak decision-making. Inaccurate data often results in incorrect reporting, which can mask the true health of an institution.

The new norms require financial entities to put in place automated data quality checks. Frequent validation and reconciliation of data across different systems will be mandatory.

Lenders will also have to maintain a data dictionary for all critical data elements. This will ensure a common understanding of data definitions across the organisation.

Security and third-party risks

The draft norms tighten data security requirements. Institutions must implement strong access controls and encryption protocols for sensitive financial data.

Banks and NBFCs that rely on third-party vendors for data processing will face additional scrutiny. The RBI wants these entities to conduct thorough due diligence on vendors and ensure data remains within India.

The circular also mandates periodic audits of data governance frameworks. Any gaps found during these audits must be addressed promptly and reported to the RBI.

Implementation timeline and feedback

The RBI has invited feedback on the draft circular from stakeholders. Comments are due within 30 days from the date of the circular.

Once finalised, the norms will be implemented in a phased manner. Larger banks and NBFCs may be given a shorter timeline to comply compared to smaller entities.

Financial institutions have been urged to start preparing their systems and processes for the new regime. Non-compliance could invite penal action from the regulator.

The next steps will involve the RBI reviewing stakeholder responses. The final circular, with any modifications, is expected to be issued later this year.
