Sunny Deol faces backlash over Dharmendra's 'Mausi' remark on Pakistan

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sunny Deol recalled Dharmendra's old remark referring to Pakistan as 'mausi' during a promotional event in Patna.
  • The comment has triggered a sharp backlash on social media, with critics questioning the timing ahead of 'Batwara 1947' release.
  • The film, based on the 1947 Partition, is already facing scrutiny over its historical and political narrative.
  • Neither Sunny Deol nor Dharmendra has officially responded to the controversy so far.

Controversial remark resurfaces during promotion

Actor Sunny Deol has found himself at the centre of a social media storm after recalling an old remark by his father and veteran actor Dharmendra, who had once referred to Pakistan as 'mausi' (aunt). The comment was made during a promotional event for Deol's upcoming film Batwara 1947, held in Patna.

In a video that has since gone viral, Sunny Deol is seen narrating the anecdote, seemingly in a light-hearted manner. However, the timing of the recollection — just weeks before the release of a film centred on the Partition — has drawn sharp criticism from several quarters.

Social media split over 'mausi' comment

The backlash has been swift and polarised. Critics have accused the actor of being insensitive, arguing that calling Pakistan a 'mausi' trivialises the trauma of Partition and the ongoing cross-border tensions. Many have pointed out that such remarks, even if made in jest, can be misconstrued at a time when bilateral relations remain fragile.

Supporters, on the other hand, have defended Sunny Deol, saying the comment was made in a familial context and should not be blown out of proportion. They argue that Dharmendra's original remark was made years ago and was never intended as a political statement.

Prominent political figures have also weighed in, with some opposition leaders questioning whether the film is being used to soften the ruling establishment's stance on Pakistan. The ruling party's supporters, however, have dismissed the controversy as a non-issue manufactured by detractors.

A film already under scrutiny

Batwara 1947, which deals with the Partition of India, has been in the news even before this controversy. The film, based on the writings of Asghar Wajahat, has been praised by some for its nuanced portrayal of quiet resistance during a turbulent period. However, it has also faced questions about historical accuracy and whether it adequately represents the complexities of the era.

Sunny Deol, known for his action-hero roles, has been promoting the film as a deeply personal project. During the Patna event, he also spoke about visiting Takht Sri Patna Sahib with his son Karan Deol, a gesture that was seen as an attempt to underline the film's cultural and spiritual roots.

No official response yet

As of now, neither Sunny Deol nor Dharmendra has issued an official statement addressing the backlash. The actor's team has not responded to requests for comment, and it remains unclear whether the controversy will affect the film's box-office prospects.

Industry insiders suggest that such controversies often fade quickly, especially if the film performs well commercially. However, the political overtones of this particular remark could keep the debate alive in the coming days.

What remains to be seen is whether the makers of Batwara 1947 will attempt to recalibrate their promotional strategy or address the issue head-on. With the release date approaching, the spotlight is now firmly on how Sunny Deol navigates this unexpected storm.
