Madhya Pradesh Cabinet Approves UCC Bill, Proposes Jail for Married Live-In Partners

📅 2026-07-20 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Madhya Pradesh cabinet approves the draft Uniform Civil Code (UCC) Bill.
  • Bill proposes up to 5-year jail for married individuals in live-in relationships.
  • Polygamy and nikah halala prohibited; Scheduled Tribes exempted.
  • Bill to be introduced in the assembly's monsoon session.

Cabinet Clears Draft UCC Bill

The Madhya Pradesh cabinet on Monday approved the draft Uniform Civil Code (UCC) Bill. Chief Minister Mohan Yadav chaired the meeting that gave the green signal to the legislation. The Bill is now set to be introduced in the state assembly's upcoming monsoon session.

The draft law seeks to bring uniformity in personal laws across communities. It covers marriage, divorce, inheritance, and adoption.

Jail Term for Married Persons in Live-In Relationships

A key provision in the Bill imposes a jail term of up to five years for married individuals who enter live-in relationships. The clause is aimed at enforcing monogamy. The Bill states that any married person cohabiting with someone other than their spouse will face criminal liability.

Live-in relationships between unmarried adults, however, are not criminalised under the draft. The provision specifically targets those who are already married.

Polygamy, Nikah Halala Banned

The Bill prohibits polygamy and nikah halala across all communities. Polygamy, the practice of having more than one spouse, is currently permitted under Muslim personal law. The UCC Bill removes that exemption.

Nikah halala, a practice where a divorced woman must marry and divorce another man before remarrying her former husband, is also banned.

Equal Inheritance Rights, ST Exemption

The draft grants equal inheritance rights to sons and daughters. This applies to all communities covered under the Bill. The government has stated this is a move toward gender equality.

Scheduled Tribes (STs) in the state have been exempted from the UCC provisions. The exemption is in line with the government's stance that tribal customs and traditions must be preserved. The exact scope of the exemption will be clarified when the Bill is tabled.

CM's Remarks on 'Ram, Rahim, Ravinder, Robin'

Chief Minister Mohan Yadav described the Bill as historic. He said, "Equal rights for Ram, Rahim, Ravinder and Robin." His statement underscored the government's intention to apply uniform civil laws irrespective of religion or caste.

Yadav added that the Bill balances personal liberty with social responsibility. He said the provisions on live-in relationships are meant to protect the institution of marriage.

The Bill is expected to face debate in the assembly. Opposition parties have indicated they will scrutinise the provisions, especially those affecting personal laws and live-in relationships.

What Happens Next

The Madhya Pradesh assembly will take up the Bill during its monsoon session. Once passed, it will be sent for the governor's assent. The implementation timeline will depend on how soon the law is notified after clearance.
