The vivo X500 has surfaced in a leaked specifications page, and the headline number is hard to ignore: a 7500mAh battery. If accurate, that is a substantial jump from the 5800mAh cell in the vivo X200, and it places the X500 in rare territory among mainstream flagship Android phones. The leak, first reported by Notebookcheck, also points to a chipset described as new but familiar, a phrase that deserves scrutiny before buyers get excited.

What the Leaked Spec Sheet Actually Says

The leak confirms the 7500mAh battery and a chipset that appears to be a refreshed or rebranded flagship platform rather than a ground-up design. Separately, model numbers for the X500, X500 Pro, and X500 Pro Max have appeared in Malaysia's SIRIM database, signalling an imminent global launch. GSMArena has also published hands-on colour images of the X500 Pro Max, while My Mobile India reports the Watch 6 and Buds Clip will launch alongside the phones on September 21.

Why a 7500mAh Battery Matters for Real Buyers

Battery capacity is the single most tangible upgrade for Indian users juggling long commutes, hotspot tethering, and camera-heavy days. A 7500mAh cell could deliver close to two full days of moderate use, or roughly 8 to 10 hours of screen-on time under heavy load. That directly reduces dependence on fast chargers and power banks, which matters in cities where charging access is inconsistent.

The trade-off is physical. Larger cells add thickness and weight, and vivo will need to manage thermals carefully. Buyers should expect a phone that is slightly heavier than the X200, likely crossing the 210g mark. The value justification depends on whether the extra endurance outweighs the added heft.

The Chipset Question: New but Familiar

The phrase new but familiar usually means a known silicon platform with a new marketing name, higher clock speeds, or a revised modem. For buyers, this is not automatically bad. A tuned, proven chipset often delivers more stable thermals and better battery efficiency than an untested design. The risk is that vivo may position it as a flagship while it trails the latest Snapdragon or Dimensity flagship in raw benchmarks.

Practical benchmark expectations: expect strong sustained performance in gaming and multitasking, but not necessarily chart-topping peak scores. The real-world difference will show in heat management and battery drain during extended camera or gaming sessions.

Pros and Cons at a Glance

  • Pros: 7500mAh battery offers class-leading endurance; global certification suggests wider availability; companion Watch 6 and Buds Clip add ecosystem value; proven chipset may mean better thermal stability.
  • Cons: Likely heavier and thicker than the X200; familiar chipset may lag newer flagship silicon in peak performance; pricing could rise given the larger battery and Pro Max variant.

Pricing Context and Buying Advice

vivo's X-series has historically launched in India between Rs 60,000 and Rs 90,000 depending on variant. A 7500mAh battery and a Pro Max model could push the top variant past the Rs 1 lakh mark. Buyers should wait for official Indian pricing, as leaks rarely capture taxes or launch offers.

If battery life is your priority, the X500 is worth tracking. If raw performance and camera hardware matter more, compare it against the latest iPhone and Samsung flagship before committing. The September 21 launch will clarify how much of this leak holds up.
