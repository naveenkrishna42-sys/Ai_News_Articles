Keelkattalai lake restored, eco-park work completed

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Restoration of Keelkattalai lake and eco-park completed in Chennai.
  • Project cost around ₹20 crore, funded by Chennai Corporation and agencies.
  • Eco-park features walking tracks, native plants, and bird-watching areas.

Revival of a water body

After months of work, the Keelkattalai lake in Chennai has been fully restored, and the adjoining eco-park is ready for public use. The project, which began in late 2025, aimed to rejuvenate the water body and turn the surrounding area into a green haven for residents. Officials from the Greater Chennai Corporation confirmed that the work was completed on schedule, with the lake now holding water through the dry months.

The restoration involved desilting, strengthening the bunds, and creating a natural filtration system using wetlands. Native plant species have been introduced along the edges to prevent erosion and attract local birds. The lake, which had been neglected for years, now shows signs of improved water quality, according to preliminary tests.

Eco-park: a new community asset

The eco-park, spread over several acres, includes walking and cycling tracks, shaded seating areas, and a dedicated bird-watching zone. Families have already begun using the space in the evenings, with children enjoying the open lawns and elders using the exercise stations. The park has been designed with minimal concrete to preserve the natural character of the lake ecosystem.

Local residents, who had long demanded action to clean the lake, have welcomed the completion. "We used to avoid this area due to the smell and garbage. Now it's a place we can be proud of," said a resident who has lived near the lake for over a decade. The corporation has also installed lighting and security cameras to ensure the park remains safe after dark.

Cost and funding

The total expenditure for the restoration and eco-park development is estimated at around ₹20 crore. Funding came from the Chennai Corporation's budget and state government grants under the urban development scheme. The project was executed by the corporation's engineering department with technical support from environmental consultants.

Officials noted that the work included the construction of a sewage diversion system to prevent untreated waste from entering the lake. This was a critical step, as previous pollution had made the water unfit for any use. The system now channels household waste to treatment plants, keeping the lake clean.

Beyond aesthetics: ecological impact

The restored lake is expected to recharge groundwater in the surrounding neighbourhoods, which have faced water scarcity in recent summers. The wetland areas act as natural sponges, holding rainwater and releasing it slowly into the soil. This is a small but significant step toward Chennai's larger goal of improving water security.

Birdwatchers have already spotted several species, including herons and kingfishers, returning to the lake. The corporation plans to conduct regular water quality monitoring and maintain the park's greenery. Volunteers from local colleges have offered to help with cleanliness drives.

What's next

The corporation is now focusing on similar restoration projects in other parts of the city, such as the Ambattur and Korattur lakes. Officials say lessons learned from Keelkattalai will guide future efforts, especially in community engagement and maintenance. For now, residents of Keelkattalai can enjoy their new green space, a quiet victory for urban ecology.
