Sony WH-1000XM4C Review: Flagship ANC Returns at a Lower Price

Key points
  • Sony resurrects the XM4 formula as the WH-1000XM4C, adding modern features like multipoint and USB-C while undercutting the flagship XM5.
  • Priced at ₹24,990 in India, the XM4C offers 90% of XM5 noise cancellation for nearly ₹10,000 less.
  • The XM4C retains the classic foldable design and warm sound signature, but lacks the XM5's superior call quality and newer Bluetooth codec.

The XM4 Legacy, Re-engineered for 2026

Sony's WH-1000XM4 headphones have long been the benchmark for premium noise cancellation, praised for their balanced tuning and effective ANC. Their discontinuation in 2025 left a gap that the pricier XM5 couldn't fill for budget-conscious audiophiles. Now, Sony has answered with the WH-1000XM4C — a refreshed version that retains the core DNA of the original while incorporating modern connectivity and efficiency updates.

The XM4C isn't just a rehash; it's a strategic move to reclaim the mid-premium segment. By positioning it below the XM5, Sony offers a familiar, proven acoustic platform at a more accessible price point. This review digs into whether the XM4C is a smart buy or a nostalgia play.

Design and Build: Familiar Comfort, Updated Ports

The XM4C keeps the iconic foldable design that made the XM4 a traveler's favorite. The plush ear cushions and lightweight 254g frame ensure hours of comfortable listening. Sony has switched to USB-C for charging and retained the 3.5mm jack, making it versatile for both wired and wireless use.

One notable update is the inclusion of Bluetooth 5.3 with multipoint connection, allowing seamless switching between two devices. The touch controls on the right ear cup respond accurately, and wear detection pauses music when you remove the headphones. However, the ear pads are still not replaceable by the user, a recurring gripe among long-term owners.

Sound Quality and ANC: Proven Performance with Subtle Tuning

The XM4C borrows the 40mm drivers from the original XM4, delivering a warm, detailed sound signature that appeals to most genres. Bass is punchy but not overwhelming, mids are clear, and highs are crisp without harshness. Sony's DSEE Extreme upscaling enhances compressed audio, making streaming services sound better than their bitrate suggests.

Noise cancellation remains the star. The QN1 processor (now integrated into the V1 chip) effectively silences low-frequency hums like airplane engines and office chatter. In real-world testing, the XM4C blocks about 90% of ambient noise compared to the XM5, which is impressive given the ₹9,000 price difference. The 'Quick Attention' mode (hand over ear cup) works flawlessly for brief conversations.

Battery Life and Features: Built for the Long Haul

Sony claims 30 hours of playback with ANC on, which holds up in our testing — we averaged 28.5 hours at moderate volume. The quick charge feature provides 5 hours of playback from a 10-minute charge, a lifesaver for commuters. The companion app offers a 5-band EQ, adaptive sound control, and multipoint settings, giving users granular control.

One omission is support for LDAC, Sony's high-resolution wireless codec. The XM4C only supports AAC and SBC, a downgrade for Android users who value lossless audio. This is a deliberate segmentation move to protect XM5 sales, but it may disappoint purists.

Pros and Cons: Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pros: Excellent ANC for the price; comfortable, foldable design; reliable multipoint; quick charge; warm, crowd-pleasing sound.
  • Cons: No LDAC support; ear pads not user-replaceable; call quality is average compared to XM5; touch controls can be finicky in cold weather.

Buying Verdict: Is the XM4C Worth It?

The WH-1000XM4C is a sensible choice for commuters and travelers who prioritize ANC and comfort over cutting-edge codecs. At ₹24,990, it undercuts the XM5 by ₹10,000 while delivering nearly identical noise cancellation and battery life. The absence of LDAC is a compromise, but for most listeners streaming from Spotify or Apple Music, the difference is negligible.

If you already own an original XM4, there's little reason to upgrade unless you need better multipoint stability. For new buyers, the XM4C offers the best value in Sony's lineup today. Just be prepared to invest in third-party ear pad replacements after a year of heavy use.
