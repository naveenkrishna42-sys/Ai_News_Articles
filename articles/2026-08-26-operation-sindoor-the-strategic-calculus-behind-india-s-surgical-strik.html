Operation Sindoor: The Strategic Calculus Behind India’s Surgical Strike

Key points
  • Operation Sindoor was launched as a direct retaliation for the Pahalgam terror attack.
  • The tactical shift from Skardu to Kirana Hills highlights India's evolving 'soch ke bahar' military doctrine.
  • Anil Chauhan suggests external pressure, possibly from the US, led to the premature disclosure of ceasefire terms.

The Strategic Pivot: Beyond Skardu

The recent revelations by former Chief of Defence Staff (CDS) Anil Chauhan regarding Operation Sindoor offer a rare look into the decision-making process within India’s top security brass. For weeks, analysts speculated that the Skardu airbase in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK) was the primary target. However, Chauhan’s admission that the military shifted its focus to the Kirana Hills suggests a more nuanced tactical objective.

By selecting Kirana Hills, India demonstrated an ability to strike deep into the Pakistani heartland, bypassing the more heavily fortified and internationally monitored Skardu base. This was not a failure of intelligence but a calculated move to maximize psychological impact while managing the risk of escalation.

The 'Soch ke Bahar' Doctrine

Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s directive for a response that was 'soch ke bahar'—or beyond conventional thinking—forced military planners to abandon textbook retaliatory strikes. The use of loitering munitions against the Kirana Hills facility marks a departure from traditional airstrikes, signaling that India is increasingly comfortable with asymmetric warfare capabilities.

Intelligence Leaks and Diplomatic Pressure

Chauhan’s comments on the premature disclosure of ceasefire details add a layer of complexity to the narrative. The suggestion that Pakistan may have leaked operational data to the US to force a de-escalation reflects the precarious diplomatic environment in which Indian security forces operate.

This 'leaked ceasefire' strategy is a known tactic by Islamabad to prevent further degradation of its military infrastructure. By bringing the US into the loop, Pakistan essentially outsourced its security to global powers, effectively creating a temporary shield for its remaining assets.

  • Pro: Successfully demonstrated India's deep-strike precision.
  • Pro: Effectively adhered to the 'soch ke bahar' political mandate.
  • Con: Diplomatic backchannels may have limited the full potential of the strike.
  • Con: Vulnerability to external intelligence leaks remains a critical concern.

Assessing the Military Impact

Critics might argue that bypassing Skardu allowed Pakistan to preserve a key strategic asset. However, the military objective was never solely about the destruction of a specific runway. It was about testing the operational readiness of the Indian armed forces to execute a complex, non-linear strike package under intense international scrutiny.

If Operation Sindoor was meant to be a warning, it succeeded. The focus on Kirana Hills proved that the Indian military can reach deep into the enemy’s strategic interior without relying on traditional air superiority over the border. The real success of this operation lies in the shift from reactive defense to proactive, unpredictable force projection.
