India Probes University Entrance Exam Irregularities Amid Student Outcry

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Allegations of question paper leaks and grace marks awarded for lost time have sparked protests.
  • The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) is probing the irregularities.
  • Over 1,500 students received grace marks, leading to a surge in high scores and admission disputes.

The Indian government has launched a formal investigation into widespread irregularities surrounding the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test for undergraduate medical courses (NEET-UG). The exam, a gateway for admissions to medical colleges across the country, has been engulfed in controversy over allegations of question paper leaks and the awarding of grace marks for lost time.

Grace Marks Controversy

The controversy centers on a decision by the National Testing Agency (NTA), the exam-conducting body, to award grace marks to over 1,500 candidates. These students were reportedly given extra marks to compensate for time lost during the exam due to administrative delays.

This move resulted in an unusually high number of top scores, with 67 candidates securing a perfect score of 720 out of 720. Officials have acknowledged that the grace marks disrupted the merit list, leading to confusion and anger among students who did not receive the benefit.

Leak Allegations and Legal Action

Beyond the grace marks, more serious allegations have surfaced. Reports of question paper leaks in several states have prompted the Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) to step in. The CBI has registered cases and is examining the chain of custody of the exam materials.

The Supreme Court of India has also taken note of the matter. It has issued notices to the NTA and the central government, seeking responses to petitions challenging the exam's integrity. The court has warned against any immediate declaration of results until the investigation progresses.

Student Protests and Broader Impact

The unfolding crisis has triggered protests from medical aspirants across cities like Delhi, Patna, and Jaipur. Students are demanding a re-examination or a transparent redressal mechanism. Many argue that the current system has unfairly benefited a few while compromising the future of lakhs of honest candidates.

The government, for its part, has defended the NTA's initial move to award compensatory marks. However, it has now conceded the need for a full probe. The investigation will examine not just the leak allegations but also the decision-making process behind the grace marks.

The NEET-UG exam is a high-stakes test, with over 24 lakh candidates competing for roughly 1.1 lakh medical seats this year. Any perception of unfairness in such a competitive process can have serious consequences, including legal battles and delayed academic years.

What Happens Next

The CBI's findings will be crucial. The Supreme Court is expected to hear the matter again soon, and its directives will determine whether a fresh exam is ordered. For now, lakhs of students remain in limbo, waiting for clarity on their academic futures.
