Overview of Balaji’s Antiques and Collectibles

Located on the bustling Avenue Road in Bengaluru, Balaji’s Antiques and Collectibles stands as one of the city’s oldest surviving antique establishments. Founded in the early 1960s by the late Shri Balaji Rao, the shop has evolved from a modest stall dealing in brass utensils to a curated space housing artefacts from across India and beyond. Its longevity reflects both the enduring demand for heritage objects and the trust built through consistent quality and transparent dealings.

Historical Significance

Avenue Road has long been a commercial spine of Bengaluru, linking the old city with modern business districts. Balaji’s Antiques opened during a period when the city’s industrial growth spurred interest in colonial-era collectibles, temple art and traditional handicrafts. Over the decades, the store has supplied items to museums, interior designers and private collectors, contributing to the preservation of Karnataka’s cultural material heritage.

What You Can Find

Metalware and Brass Artefacts

The shop’s brass collection includes lamps, ritual vessels (pancharatna), colonial-era inkstands and reproductions of Vijayanagara-style icons. Many pieces bear clear maker’s marks or patina that helps experts date them to the late 19th or early 20th century. Prices typically start at INR 1,200 for small utilitarian items and rise to INR 45,000 for large, intricately chased showpieces.

Wooden Furniture and Carvings

Balaji’s carries a selection of teak and rosewood furniture ranging from colonial chairs to Chettinad-style swings. Carved panels depicting mythological scenes and traditional jaali work are also available. Condition varies; most items have undergone minimal restoration to preserve original joinery. Price brackets are INR 8,000–2,50,000 depending on size, age and intricacy.

Porcelain, Glass and Jewellery

Vintage porcelain tea sets, Belgian glassware and antique jewellery (including silver filigree and semi-precious stone sets) form the smaller but high‑value segment. Provenance documentation is provided where possible, and prices reflect market comparables: porcelain sets from INR 3,500, glass decanters from INR 2,200 and jewellery pieces from INR 5,000 upward.

Buying Guide for Collectors

When purchasing from Balaji’s, request a written description that includes material, approximate era and any known provenance. Examine items for cracks, repairs or excessive polishing that may affect value. For high‑value pieces, consider obtaining an independent appraisal or consulting a specialist in the relevant category (e.g., brassware expert for ritual objects). Factor in preservation costs such as climate‑controlled display or periodic cleaning when budgeting.

Pros of Buying at Balaji’s Antiques

  • Established reputation with over 60 years of continuous operation.
  • Transparent pricing and willingness to provide provenance details.
  • Diverse inventory covering multiple eras and material types.

Cons / Considerations

  • Limited online catalogue; physical visit often required for assessment.
  • Some items may show wear that needs professional restoration.
  • High‑value pieces demand independent verification to avoid overpaying.
