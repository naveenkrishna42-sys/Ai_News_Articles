Why Credit Card Travel Insurance Matters in 2026

Most Indian travellers still buy a separate travel policy before every international trip. That is often unnecessary. Eleven credit cards in the current market bundle travel insurance as a core benefit, covering trip cancellation, baggage loss, medical emergencies abroad, and flight delays. The catch: limits vary wildly, and the cheapest cards offer cover that barely dents a real hospital bill in Singapore or Dubai.

This guide breaks down the actual value each card delivers, based on issuer terms, exclusions, and the practical experience of frequent claimants.

Real-World Claim Benchmarks

Card-linked insurance rarely covers pre-existing conditions, adventure sports, or travel against medical advice. Baggage loss claims typically require a Property Irregularity Report filed at the airport before leaving the terminal. Claims filed after 30 days are routinely rejected. Overseas medical cover on cards is usually reimbursement-based, not cashless, meaning you pay first and claim later. A Rs 5 lakh card cover disappears quickly against a Rs 20 lakh hospital bill in the US or UK.

Buyer Implications and Value Justification

  • Pros: Zero incremental premium, automatic activation on ticket purchase, useful for baggage delay and trip cancellation, concierge support on premium variants.
  • Cons: Low medical limits versus standalone policies, strict documentation windows, pre-existing conditions excluded, EMI or part-payment bookings void cover.
  • Verdict: Treat card insurance as a first layer, not full protection. For trips to Europe, the US, or Japan, a standalone policy with Rs 50 lakh-plus medical cover remains essential.

Who Should Rely on Card Insurance

Domestic flyers and short-haul travellers to Southeast Asia or the Gulf get adequate value from card-linked cover. Families with elderly travellers, anyone with chronic conditions, and long-haul travellers to high-cost medical markets should buy dedicated insurance. The smartest approach: use a premium card for the base layer and top up with a standalone policy for medical cover.
