Meghwal's high jump silver, Shahnavaz's bronze mark U20 Worlds history

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Basant Meghwal becomes first Indian male high jumper to win a medal at World Athletics U20 Championships, securing silver.
  • Shahnavaz Khan clinches bronze in long jump, adding to India's medal count.
  • India's overall tally at the championships now stands at three medals, with Meghwal's feat being a historic first for the country.
  • Meghwal hails from Sriganganagar, Rajasthan, and his performance has been widely celebrated as a breakthrough for Indian athletics.
  • The achievements have sparked optimism for India's future in field events on the global stage.

Indian athletics witnessed a landmark moment on Sunday as Basant Meghwal soared to a silver medal in the high jump at the World Athletics Under-20 Championships. The 19-year-old from Sriganganagar, Rajasthan, cleared a personal best to become the first Indian male high jumper to stand on the podium at this prestigious event.

Hours later, Shahnavaz Khan added to the celebrations with a bronze in the long jump, taking India's medal tally at the championships to three. The double podium finish marks a significant step for the country's young athletes, who have often struggled to make an impact in field events on the global stage.

A historic leap for Indian high jump

Meghwal's silver is not just a personal milestone but a breakthrough for Indian high jumping. Competing against the world's best juniors, he delivered a series of consistent jumps, eventually clearing a height that forced him into contention for the top spot. Though gold eluded him, his performance rewrote the record books.

The athlete, who trains at the Sports Authority of India's centre in Sriganganagar, has been steadily rising through the ranks. His achievement has drawn praise from coaches and former athletes, who see it as proof that Indian field event athletes can compete with the best. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact height cleared, but the silver is being celebrated as a historic first.

Shahnavaz's bronze adds to the shine

Shahnavaz Khan, competing in the long jump, produced a leap that secured him the bronze medal. His performance was marked by composure under pressure, as he delivered his best effort when it mattered most. The medal adds depth to India's showing at the championships, where the team has now claimed three podium finishes.

Khan's success follows a pattern of improvement among Indian jumpers, who have benefited from better training facilities and exposure in recent years. His bronze, alongside Meghwal's silver, signals a promising future for the country's athletics programme, particularly in events that have traditionally been dominated by athletes from the United States, Europe, and the Caribbean.

India's rising tally at the U20 Worlds

With these two medals, India's tally at the World Athletics Under-20 Championships stands at three, a commendable achievement for a young squad. The third medal came earlier in the competition, and the team's overall performance reflects the growing depth of Indian athletics. Coaches accompanying the squad have attributed the success to rigorous preparation and a focus on technical refinement.

The championships, held this year, have seen strong competition from emerging talents worldwide. India's showing has been particularly notable given the challenges of travel and acclimatisation that young athletes often face. The results are expected to boost funding and support for junior athletics programmes back home.

Looking ahead

Meghwal and Khan will return to India as trailblazers, with their performances likely to inspire a new generation of jumpers. The Athletics Federation of India is expected to review their progress and plan targeted training camps ahead of future international events, including the next Asian and World Championships.

For now, the focus shifts to the remaining events at the U20 Worlds, where Indian athletes are still in contention in other disciplines. The team's management remains hopeful of adding to the medal count before the championships conclude. Whatever the final tally, this edition will be remembered as the one where Indian high jump and long jump announced themselves on the world stage.
