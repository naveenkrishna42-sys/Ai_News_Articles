Mother Thanks PM Modi for 'Jeevandaan' After Daughter's Abuse Case Dropped

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi Police sources confirm no case against minor accused of abusing PM Modi.
  • Mother of the Noida teen thanks PM Modi, calls his forgiveness 'jeevandaan' (a gift of life).
  • Opposition parties accuse BJP of hypocrisy over the PM's youth message.
  • The mother expresses wish that PM Modi 'adopts' her daughter.

NEW DELHI: The mother of a teenage girl from Noida, who was accused of abusing Prime Minister Narendra Modi in a social media post, has publicly thanked the PM for forgiving her daughter, calling his decision a 'jeevandaan' (a gift of life). The gratitude comes as Delhi Police sources confirmed that no case has been registered against the minor, who was earlier questioned in connection with the offensive remarks.

The incident, which unfolded over the past week, saw the girl summoned for questioning after a complaint about her posts targeting the Prime Minister. Sources said the police concluded that given her age and the nature of the remarks, no formal charges would be pressed, and the matter has been resolved with a warning to the family.

Mother's Emotional Appeal

Speaking to reporters outside her residence in Noida, the girl's mother broke down while expressing her relief and gratitude. She said that the Prime Minister's decision to forgive her daughter has given her a new lease on life, and that the family was overwhelmed by his magnanimity.

"He gave her jeevandaan by forgiving her," the mother said, her voice choked with emotion. "We never expected this. I want to tell the PM that he has not just forgiven my daughter, but he has saved our entire family. I want him to adopt her as his own daughter, so she can learn from his values and serve the nation."

The mother's appeal has resonated across social media, with many hailing the PM's decision as a compassionate gesture. However, others have questioned the selective application of forgiveness, pointing to past instances where similar remarks against opposition leaders were treated with far less leniency.

Opposition Cry of Hypocrisy

The Prime Minister's youth outreach message, delivered earlier this week, urged young Indians to focus on nation-building and avoid spreading hatred online. It was this message that reportedly influenced the decision to drop the case against the minor.

Opposition parties were quick to pounce on the development. Leaders from the Congress and other parties reminded the BJP of several instances where individuals had to publicly apologise for allegedly abusive remarks against the PM or his government. "The PM talks about forgiveness for a minor, but where was this compassion when a young student was jailed for a tweet during the protests?" asked a senior opposition leader, speaking on condition of anonymity.

BJP spokespersons defended the move, arguing that the PM's message was clear: constructive criticism is welcome, but abuse and personal attacks have no place in public discourse. They also noted that the girl is a minor and that the law requires a rehabilitative approach rather than punitive action in such cases.

Legal and Social Dimensions

Legal experts point out that under the Juvenile Justice Act, minors are entitled to special protection, and criminal proceedings are typically avoided for first-time, non-violent offences. The decision to drop the case aligns with this principle, they say.

Social commentators, however, see a deeper political undertone. The girl's mother has now become an unlikely symbol in a narrative that the PM's leadership is rooted in forgiveness. The family has been flooded with media requests, and neighbours have gathered outside their home, offering support.

The girl, whose identity is protected under juvenile law, has reportedly been counselled by police and is said to be 'deeply regretful' of her actions. Her mother insisted that the family had always taught her children to respect all leaders, and that the incident was a momentary lapse of judgement.

What Happens Next

As the dust settles, the focus shifts to the PM's upcoming interactions with youth groups and whether this incident will set a precedent for handling similar cases involving minors. The mother's request for the PM to 'adopt' her daughter, while symbolic, has opened a new conversation about reconciliation in Indian politics.

For now, the family is hoping to return to normal life, with the mother saying she plans to take her daughter to a temple to offer thanks. "We have been given a second chance," she said. "We will not waste it."
