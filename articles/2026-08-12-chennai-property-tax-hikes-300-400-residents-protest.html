Chennai property tax hikes 300-400%, residents protest

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Property tax in Chennai has increased by 300-400% for many residents
  • Residents across zones are protesting, demanding a rollback
  • Civic officials attribute the hike to revised assessment guidelines

Steep hikes stun homeowners

Chennai's property tax bills have shot up by 300–400% for a large number of residents, triggering widespread anger and protests across the city. The steep increase, reflected in the latest demand notices, has caught many homeowners off guard, with some seeing their annual tax outgo jump from a few thousand rupees to tens of thousands.

Residents in several zones, including T. Nagar, Adyar, and Anna Nagar, have reported receiving revised tax assessments that are three to four times their previous amounts. The civic body, Greater Chennai Corporation, has attributed the spike to a revision in the base value of properties and a reassessment of built-up areas, following guidelines issued by the state government.

Why the sudden jump?

Officials say the hike is a result of the periodic revision of property tax, which was last updated several years ago. The new calculations factor in current market value, age of the building, and the nature of usage. However, residents argue that the increase is disproportionate and lacks transparency.

"We were not given any prior notice or an opportunity to object," said a resident of Velachery, who received a notice showing a 350% increase. "The calculation seems arbitrary, and there is no clear explanation for such a massive jump in one go."

Protests and petitions

Local resident welfare associations have begun collecting signatures and planning protests outside zonal offices. Many have also filed formal objections with the corporation, demanding a rollback and a more gradual revision mechanism.

The issue has also reached social media, with hashtags like #ChennaiPropertyTax trending as citizens share their bill amounts and express frustration. Some have even threatened to approach the Madras High Court if the civic body does not address their grievances.

Civic body defends move

Corporation officials, however, maintain that the revision is in line with the Tamil Nadu Town and Country Planning Act and is necessary to augment revenue for infrastructure projects. They point out that many properties were undervalued for years, and the current revision corrects that anomaly.

A senior official said the corporation has set up a grievance redressal mechanism where residents can challenge their assessments. "We are open to reviewing individual cases," the official said, "but the overall revision was long overdue and follows the state government's directive."

Financial strain on middle class

For middle-class families, the hike comes at a time of rising costs of living, adding to their burden. Several pensioners and fixed-income households have expressed concern about their ability to pay the new amounts in one go. Some have requested the option to pay in instalments, but the corporation has not yet confirmed any such facility.

Local traders and small businesses, who pay tax on commercial properties, are also affected. A shopkeeper in Purasawalkam said his tax has gone up by nearly four times, which he fears will force him to increase prices.

What happens next

With protests gaining momentum, the corporation is likely to hold a meeting with resident welfare associations next week to discuss the issue. The outcome of these talks will determine whether the tax revision is softened or implemented as is. Residents, meanwhile, are preparing to escalate their demands if no relief is offered.
