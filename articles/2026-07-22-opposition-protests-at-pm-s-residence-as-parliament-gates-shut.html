Opposition protests at PM's residence as Parliament gates shut

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Opposition leaders were locked out of Parliament and protested at PM Modi's residence.
  • Tear gas and lathi charges were used to disperse youth protesters.
  • This marks the first broad-based protest against PM Modi, not limited to specific interest groups.

Parliament doors shut, protests shift to PM's doorstep

Opposition leaders on Tuesday found themselves locked out of Parliament, prompting them to take their protest directly to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's residence. The move came after a series of disruptions in the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha over the recent paper leaks and student unrest.

As the doors of Parliament remained shut, the opposition marched towards the PM's residence in what they called a 'sabka vishwas' check. The phrase, a twist on the government's 'sabka saath, sabka vikas' slogan, underlines the growing distrust between the ruling party and the opposition.

Tear gas and lathi charge youth protesters

However, the protest was not without incident. As hundreds of students and youth activists gathered near the PM's residence, police resorted to tear gas and lathi charges to disperse the crowd. Visuals from the scene showed young protesters running for cover as smoke filled the air.

The heavy-handed response has drawn criticism from opposition leaders, who argue that it reflects the government's intolerance of dissent. 'You cannot have sabka vishwas when you greet your own people with tear gas and lathis,' said a senior opposition leader, speaking on condition of anonymity.

First broad-based protest against Modi

Political analysts point out that this is perhaps the first time Prime Minister Modi is facing a broad-based protest that is not limited to specific interest groups. Previous protests, such as those by farmers or caste groups, were seen as sectoral. This time, the anger is more diffuse.

The triggers include the recent paper leaks that have affected thousands of students, along with a perception that the government is not listening to the youth. 'This is not just about one issue. It's about a feeling that the system is rigged against the common person,' said a political commentator.

Paper leaks and student plight lost in noise?

Amidst the political drama, there are concerns that the core issues—the paper leaks and the plight of affected students—are being lost. Several student groups have accused both the government and the opposition of using their plight for political mileage.

Videos circulating on social media show students holding placards that read 'Student Plight Or Political Fight?'—a question that underscores the confusion. While the opposition claims to champion the students' cause, the government insists it is taking strict action against those involved in the leaks.

With Parliament in turmoil and protests on the streets, the coming days will determine whether this anger translates into sustained political pressure on the Modi government. The opposition is expected to continue its protests, while the government will likely try to regain the narrative by focusing on action against the paper leak culprits.
