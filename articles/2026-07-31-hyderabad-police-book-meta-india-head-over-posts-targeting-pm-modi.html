Hyderabad Police Book Meta India Head Over Posts Targeting PM Modi

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • FIRs name Meta India head and multiple social media users over posts targeting PM Modi.
  • The case stems from objectionable content, including AI-generated videos, shared during youth protests in Telangana.
  • Police action follows complaints about posts violating legal norms during the protests.

FIRs Filed in Hyderabad

The Hyderabad cybercrime police have registered First Information Reports (FIRs) against the head of Meta India and several social media users in connection with posts targeting Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The action comes amid youth protests in Telangana, during which objectionable content, including AI-generated videos, was shared online.

Officials confirmed that the FIRs were filed after complaints highlighted the offensive nature of the posts. The case names the Meta India head in addition to individuals who allegedly circulated the content on social media platforms.

Context of the Protests

The protests, which have drawn significant attention in the state, reportedly saw a surge in inflammatory posts. Some of the content involved manipulated videos of the Prime Minister, raising concerns about the spread of misinformation.

Police have not yet disclosed the specific provisions under which the FIRs were registered. However, sources indicate that the investigation will focus on the role of platforms in permitting such content to remain accessible.

Legal Implications for Meta

This is not the first time that social media companies have faced legal scrutiny in India over user-generated content. The involvement of a senior Meta executive suggests that authorities are increasingly holding platform heads accountable for content moderation failures.

Legal experts note that the case could set a precedent for how intermediaries are treated under Indian law, particularly when content targets public figures. The police are expected to examine whether Meta complied with existing IT rules regarding timely removal of flagged content.

Next Steps in Investigation

Cybercrime officials are likely to issue notices to the accused, including the Meta India head, seeking their responses. The investigation may also involve technical analysis of the flagged posts and videos to trace their origin.

Meta has not yet issued a public statement on the FIRs. The company, which operates Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp, has previously maintained that it follows Indian laws and acts on valid legal requests.

As the case unfolds, all eyes will be on how the platform responds to the allegations and whether the police broaden the probe to include other executives or entities.
