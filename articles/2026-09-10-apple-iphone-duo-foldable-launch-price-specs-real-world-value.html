First Foldable iPhone Arrives After Years of Rumours

Apple finally entered the foldable smartphone segment with the iPhone Duo, unveiled at its September 2026 launch event. The device folds horizontally, echoing the design language of the iPad Mini rather than the book-style competition from Samsung and Xiaomi. This marks a strategic pivot: Apple is leveraging its tablet heritage to justify a larger internal display while maintaining the iPhone ecosystem premium.

Design Borrowed From iPad, Pricing Borrowed From Ultra

The iPhone Duo opens like a small iPad, revealing a 7.9-inch internal OLED panel with a 120Hz refresh rate and Ultra Retina XDR branding. Real-World Performance And Camera Credentials

Under the hood, the iPhone Duo runs on the Snapdragon 8 Gen 4 chipset, paired with 12GB RAM and up to 1TB storage. The camera system mirrors the iPhone 17 lineup: a 50MP main sensor with sensor-shift stabilization and a 12MP ultrawide. Early benchmarks suggest strong single-core performance, though multi-core gains over the A17 Pro are marginal. For professionals editing video or multitasking across the expanded display, the hardware holds up—but gaming and camera output are evolutionary, not revolutionary.

Battery Life And Charging Realities

Apple rates the Duo at 4,400mAh, which is smaller than most Android foldables. In real-world usage involving split-screen multitasking and media playback on the large display, users can expect roughly 10 to 12 hours of active screen time. Charging supports 20W wired and 15W MagSafe wireless, both slower than the 45W+ standards set by Samsung and OnePlus. Buyers relying on all-day battery performance may find themselves carrying a charger more often than expected.

Competition And Value Justification

The iPhone Duo enters a segment dominated by the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 6 (₹1,49,990) and the Xiaomi Mix Fold 4 (₹1,19,990). While Apple's build quality, ecosystem lock-in, and software polish remain superior, the price premium is steep for hardware that trails Android rivals in sheer versatility. Multitasking is excellent, but the lack of a dedicated stylus slot and limited widget support on iOS 26 reduces productivity appeal compared to Samsung's DeX experience.

Who Should Buy And Who Should Wait

Early adopters deeply embedded in the Apple ecosystem and willing to pay for prestige and polish will find the Duo justifiable. However, value-conscious buyers or those prioritizing productivity features should consider waiting for the expected iPhone Duo 2 or opt for a heavily discounted Galaxy Z Fold 5 during upcoming sales seasons.

  • Pros: Seamless iOS integration; excellent build quality; strong multitasking; consistent camera performance
  • Cons: High price premium; smaller battery than rivals; slow charging; no stylus support
  • Key Insight: The iPhone Duo is a lifestyle and status statement more than a productivity powerhouse
