Control Resonant PC Port: The Verdict at a Glance

Remedy's Control Resonant has arrived on PC, and the port quality is being described by reviewers as almost picture perfect. Digital Foundry and TechPowerUp have collectively tested the game across 35 GPUs, and the results show a technically mature release that avoids the stutter problems that plagued several 2025 AAA launches. For Indian buyers weighing a Rs 3,499 purchase, the port's stability matters as much as its visuals.

The game expands Remedy's connected universe into a wider urban sandbox while retaining the destructible environments and ray-traced reflections that defined the original. More importantly, it ships without Denuvo, includes full ultrawide and DLSS 3.5 support, and offers a refund-friendly launch window on Steam.

Verified Performance Benchmarks Across 35 GPUs

TechPowerUp's 35-GPU benchmark suite provides the clearest picture of what hardware you actually need. At 1080p High, the RTX 4060 averages 68fps, while the RX 7600 manages 61fps. At 1440p High, the RTX 4070 Super hits 72fps and the RX 7800 XT reaches 69fps. Native 4K High remains demanding: the RTX 4080 Super delivers 58fps, and the RTX 4090 pushes 89fps.

Ray tracing changes the equation. RT High at 1440p drops the RTX 4070 Super to 41fps, making DLSS Quality or FSR 3.1 frame generation effectively mandatory. Older RTX 20-series cards struggle with RT enabled, so owners should either disable ray tracing or upgrade.

Story, Gameplay and the David Lynch Tribute

Control Resonant continues the Federal Bureau of Control's investigation into altered world events, but shifts the action into a denser urban sandbox. Gameplay retains the telekinetic combat and environmental destruction of the original while adding new traversal routes and side investigations. Remedy has also honored the late David Lynch with a Twin Peaks quote embedded in the game, a tribute that IGN India highlighted as powerful and fitting for the studio's surreal storytelling lineage.

The wider sandbox introduces performance variability that a linear corridor shooter would not. Digital Foundry noted that frame pacing remains stable even during large-scale destruction sequences, which is a meaningful achievement for an open-ended urban environment.

Real-World Buyer Implications for Indian Gamers

At Rs 3,499, Control Resonant sits in the premium AAA bracket, roughly the cost of two months of a mid-tier game subscription. The Deluxe Edition at Rs 4,999 adds cosmetic items and expansion access, but the standard edition already includes the full campaign. Buyers with an RTX 3060 Ti or RX 6700 XT can expect 1080p High at 60fps, which matches the recommended spec sheet.

Laptop buyers should note that the game scales well with DLSS but remains CPU-sensitive in urban hubs. A 6-core processor and 16GB RAM are the practical floor for smooth play. Pre-launch discounts are unlikely given Remedy's pricing history, so waiting for a sale may mean a three-to-six-month delay.

  • Pros: No shader compilation stutter, no Denuvo, stable frame pacing, full DLSS 3.5 and FSR 3.1 support, excellent ultrawide implementation.
  • Pros: Mid-range GPUs deliver 60fps at 1080p High, and the Deluxe Edition offers reasonable expansion value at Rs 4,999.
  • Cons: Ray tracing is prohibitively heavy on RTX 20-series cards, and native 4K High requires an RTX 4080 Super or better.
  • Cons: Rs 3,499 standard pricing is steep for Indian buyers, and no launch discount has been announced.

Final Assessment: Should You Buy at Launch?

Control Resonant is the rare PC port that respects the platform. The absence of Denuvo, the inclusion of all major upscalers, and verified 35-GPU benchmark stability make it a safe day-one purchase for anyone with an RTX 3060 Ti or better. Indian buyers on older GTX 1060-class hardware should verify performance at 1080p Low before committing Rs 3,499.

Remedy has delivered a technically polished expansion of its universe that justifies the premium price for fans of the series. For everyone else, the benchmark table above is the clearest guide to whether your rig can handle the urban sandbox at the frame rates you expect.
