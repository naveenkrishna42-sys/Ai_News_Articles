KBR Park Petitioners Deny Filing Complaint Against CEC

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Petitioners in KBR National Park case deny filing any complaint with the CEC.
  • The alleged complaint had raised concerns about election code violations related to the park.
  • The denial has cast doubt on the authenticity of the document submitted to the Election Commission.

Petitioners in the ongoing KBR National Park case have categorically denied filing any complaint with the Chief Election Commissioner (CEC). The denial comes after a document purportedly submitted by them surfaced, alleging violations of the model code of conduct.

Denial of Complaint Submission

The individuals who have been actively pursuing the case concerning the KBR National Park in Hyderabad stated that they never approached the Election Commission with any such complaint. They have expressed surprise over the emergence of the document.

Sources close to the petitioners said the document in question appears to be fabricated. They have demanded a thorough investigation into who might have submitted it and for what purpose.

Document Raises Questions

The alleged complaint had raised specific concerns about activities within the KBR National Park, claiming they violated the model code of conduct in place during the elections. The code is enforced to ensure free and fair polls.

With the petitioners now disowning the complaint, questions are being raised about the authenticity of the document. Election officials have not yet commented on the development or whether they acted on the complaint.

Background of the KBR Case

The KBR National Park case has been a contentious issue in Hyderabad. The park, a sprawling green lung in the city, has been at the centre of legal battles over its preservation and alleged encroachments.

The petitioners have been fighting to protect the park from what they claim are illegal constructions and commercial activities. The case has seen multiple hearings in the High Court and has drawn attention from environmental groups.

What Happens Next

The denial is likely to complicate matters for those who may have relied on the complaint. The Election Commission may now have to verify the document's origin and decide if any further action is warranted.

Legal experts say the incident could also lead to a separate inquiry into the fabrication of documents. The petitioners have indicated they may file a formal complaint with the police regarding the matter.

Watch for the Election Commission's response in the coming days, as well as any legal steps the petitioners take to clear their name.
