LED streetlights can cut power use by 61%, says Minister; flags rollout delays

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Minister highlights 61% potential power savings from LED streetlights
  • Rollout delays attributed to procurement and civic coordination issues
  • Officials yet to confirm revised timelines for full installation

Bengaluru's ambitious plan to switch all streetlights to LED could slash electricity consumption by up to 61%, according to a state minister who spoke on Tuesday. However, the same official also flagged significant delays in the rollout, casting doubt on whether the city will meet its energy-efficiency targets on schedule.

The minister, speaking at a review meeting, said the technology's potential is clear: replacing conventional lamps with LEDs across the city's roughly 4.5 lakh streetlights would not only cut power use sharply but also reduce maintenance costs and carbon emissions. But implementation has stumbled, with several wards still awaiting installation despite earlier deadlines.

What's holding up the switch

Officials familiar with the project point to a mix of procurement bottlenecks and coordination gaps between the civic body and utility companies. Tenders for some zones were delayed, and in other areas, faulty wiring or missing poles have slowed the fitting of new fixtures.

The minister did not specify a fresh completion date, saying only that "work is underway" and that agencies have been told to speed up. Local residents in some neighbourhoods have complained about inconsistent lighting, with roads remaining dark in patches even as neighbouring streets shine bright.

Savings that matter for the city

The 61% figure is not trivial. For a city that spends crores annually on streetlight power bills, such a reduction could free up funds for other civic projects. Energy experts have long argued that LED retrofits are among the cheapest and fastest ways to cut urban electricity demand.

Yet the delay means Bengaluru continues to burn more power than necessary, even as summer demand peaks. The minister acknowledged the frustration, noting that "every month of delay is a month of lost savings."

What the numbers mean

  • 61% potential reduction in streetlight power consumption
  • 4.5 lakh streetlights targeted for replacement
  • Multiple wards still pending installation

No official timeline yet

Neither the civic body nor the power utility has issued a revised schedule for completing the project. Officials said a status report is being prepared, but they stopped short of promising a new deadline.

Residents' groups have urged the authorities to prioritise high-traffic and accident-prone corridors. Some have also suggested publicising ward-wise progress to build accountability.

What to watch

The next few weeks will show whether the minister's push translates into faster execution on the ground. If procurement issues are resolved quickly, the city could still achieve most of the projected savings within the financial year. Otherwise, the 61% promise may remain a target, not a reality.
