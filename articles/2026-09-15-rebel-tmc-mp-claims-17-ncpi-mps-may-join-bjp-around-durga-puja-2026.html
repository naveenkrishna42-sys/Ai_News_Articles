Introduction

A recent statement by rebel Trinamool Congress (TMC) MP Jagadish Chandra Barma Basunia has reignited speculation about political realignments in West Bengal. Basunia asserted that 17 out of 20 MPs belonging to the Nationalist Congress Party of India (NCPI) are poised to join the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) either before or after the Durga Puja festivities in October 2026. The claim, reported by multiple outlets, has triggered denials from both the BJP and the TMC, while highlighting underlying fissures within regional alliances.

Details of the Claim and Source Reactions

Basunia, speaking to a local news channel, said the alleged defectors are dissatisfied with NCPI’s current leadership and see better prospects in the BJP’s national agenda. He added that the timing coincides with the Durga Puja period, a traditionally sensitive window for political maneuvering in Bengal. The Hindu, The Indian Express, and The New Indian Express all carried the story, each noting that BJP state officials dismissed the figure as baseless, while TMC spokespersons labelled it an attempt to destabilise the opposition.

Political Implications for BJP and TMC

For the BJP, absorbing a sizable bloc of NCPI MPs would expand its footprint in West Bengal, a state where it has historically struggled to break the TMC’s dominance. An increase to roughly 35 Lok Sabha seats would give the party a stronger negotiating position in national coalition talks and bolster its narrative of expanding influence beyond the Hindi heartland.

Conversely, the TMC stands to lose not only legislative strength but also a symbolic signal of unity among regional parties opposed to the BJP. The alleged defections could embolden other smaller parties to reconsider their alliances, potentially fragmenting the opposition further ahead of the 2026 state assembly and Lok Sabha elections.

Historical Context of Defections in Bengal

West Bengal has witnessed several waves of cross‑party migrations, often timed around major festivals or election cycles. In 2019, a number of Congress and Left Front legislators joined the TMC ahead of the Lok Sabha polls, while 2021 saw a reverse flow of TMC leaders to the BJP following the state assembly results. Analysts note that such movements are frequently driven by perceptions of patronage, access to central schemes, and the prospect of ministerial berths.

The Durga Puja period, marked by heightened public gatherings and media attention, has traditionally served as a backdrop for political announcements. Leaders often use the festive window to gauge public sentiment before making formal moves, which may explain Basunia’s reference to the timing.

What This Means for the 2026 Electoral Landscape

If the claimed defections are substantiated, the BJP’s strategic calculus in West Bengal would shift from incremental gains to a more aggressive push for dominance. Campaign resources could be reallocated to consolidate the new MPs’ constituencies, while the TMC would need to address internal grievances to prevent further erosion.

\

At present, the lack of corroborative evidence from official party registers or the Election Commission leaves the claim in the realm of political speculation. Nonetheless, the episode underscores the fluidity of alliances in Bengal politics and the enduring influence of regional events like Durga Puja on national-level calculations.
