The Stalled BRICS Bid: What We Know

Background of the Application

Pakistan formally applied for membership in the BRICS bloc — comprising Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa — several years before the group's landmark 2024 expansion. Despite submitting its bid, Pakistan remains outside the partnership. The delay has sparked intense debate among foreign policy analysts about the geopolitical, economic, and strategic barriers preventing Islamabad's entry.

The 2024 BRICS expansion, which welcomed new members including the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Ethiopia, and Egypt, did not include Pakistan or Bangladesh. Both countries had expressed interest, making their exclusion a notable outcome of the summit deliberations held in Rio de Janeiro.

The India Factor: A Strategic Veto

The central question dominating discussions is whether India holds the practical power to block Pakistan's membership. While BRICS operates on a consensus-based model rather than a formal veto system, India's position as a founding member gives it enormous influence over internal decisions. New Delhi has historically viewed Pakistan's growing ties with China and Russia as a strategic concern in the context of long-standing bilateral tensions.

Analysts note that even if BRICS does not formally recognise a veto mechanism, China's own position on India-Pakistan relations adds another layer of complexity. Beijing's close ties with Islamabad are balanced against its need to maintain working relations with New Delhi, particularly on trade and border matters. This delicate equilibrium often results in Pakistan's bid being quietly sidelined rather than explicitly rejected.

Pros and Cons of BRICS Membership for Pakistan

Potential Benefits

Joining BRICS would offer Pakistan access to a collective market of over three billion people and alternative financial architectures that reduce dependence on Western-led institutions like the IMF and World Bank. For a country grappling with balance-of-payments crises and volatile foreign exchange reserves, diversified economic partnerships carry significant appeal. Membership could also facilitate technology transfer and infrastructure investment through initiatives linked to China's Belt and Road framework.

Risks and Drawbacks

On the other hand, BRICS membership could deepen Pakistan's strategic dependence on China, a relationship already criticised for opaque debt terms and asymmetric trade imbalances. Additionally, alignment with Russia and Iran on geopolitical positions may further isolate Pakistan from Western markets and military cooperation channels. The economic benefits of BRICS remain largely theoretical, with no binding trade agreements or guaranteed investment flows attached to membership.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pros: Access to alternative financial systems reducing reliance on Western institutions; potential for increased trade with emerging economies; strategic diversification of foreign partnerships.
  • Pros: Collective bargaining power on global commodity prices and climate financing that benefits developing economies.
  • Cons: Risk of deepening debt dependency on China without transparent renegotiation mechanisms; potential diplomatic friction with Western allies.
  • Cons: No guaranteed trade concessions or investment commitments come with membership; geopolitical alignments within BRICS remain internally contradictory.
  • Geopolitical Reality: India's influence within BRICS, combined with its strained relationship with Pakistan, makes admission a near-term impossibility barring a fundamental shift in bilateral dynamics.

What Comes Next

Pakistan's BRICS aspirations are unlikely to disappear from the diplomatic agenda. Islamabad has signalled continued interest in engaging with the bloc through observer status or alternative partnership frameworks. However, until the India-Pakistan relationship undergoes meaningful thawing and BRICS clarifies its enlargement criteria, Pakistan's membership will remain a aspirational goal rather than an achievable milestone. Bangladesh faces a similar trajectory, though its ties with India are comparatively less adversarial, potentially giving it a marginally stronger case in future deliberations.

Pakistan's BRICS bid exposes a fundamental truth about multilateral geopolitics: formal applications mean little without the political goodwill of influential member states. The bloc's expansion choices in 2024 reflected strategic calculations that excluded South Asian rivals, and that calculus shows little sign of shifting in the immediate future.
