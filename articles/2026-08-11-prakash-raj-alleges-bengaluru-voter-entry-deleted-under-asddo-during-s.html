Prakash Raj alleges Bengaluru voter entry deleted under ASDDO during SIR

  • The deletion was reportedly done under the ASDDO category during a Special Intensive Revision (SIR).

Allegation of voter roll deletion

Bengaluru, Aug 11: Actor Prakash Raj has alleged that his voter registration in Bengaluru was deleted under the ASDDO category during a Special Intensive Revision (SIR) drive. The claim, made public on Tuesday, has raised questions about the accuracy and transparency of the electoral roll update process.

Prakash Raj, known for his roles in Tamil, Telugu, and Kannada cinema, said the deletion was unexpected and has left him concerned about the integrity of the voter list. He did not specify the exact date or location of the incident but urged authorities to look into the matter.

What is ASDDO and SIR?

ASDDO stands for Assistant State Deputy District Officer, a designation often involved in electoral roll revisions. The SIR, or Special Intensive Revision, is a periodic exercise conducted by election authorities to update voter lists, correct errors, and remove duplicate or ineligible entries.

Deletions under such drives are meant to clean up the rolls, but errors can occur, leading to eligible voters being dropped. Prakash Raj's case has now become a talking point among citizens and political circles, with many demanding a thorough audit of the process.

Reactions and demands for clarity

The actor's statement has triggered calls for immediate verification by the Election Commission. Several social media users and local activists have echoed his concern, asking for a transparent mechanism to address grievances related to voter deletions.

Officials in Bengaluru have not yet issued a formal response to the specific claim. Sources suggest that the matter may be taken up with the district election office, which handles such revision-related issues.

Legal and procedural remedies

Under existing rules, any voter whose name is deleted from the electoral roll can file a claim for inclusion using Form 6, along with necessary documents. The process is designed to be simple, but delays and procedural hurdles often frustrate applicants.

Prakash Raj has not indicated whether he plans to file a formal complaint or approach the Election Commission directly. His public disclosure, however, has already put the spotlight on how such deletions are carried out during SIR drives.

Context and wider implications

This incident comes amid ongoing debates about the reliability of electoral rolls in the country. While the Election Commission maintains that revisions are conducted with due diligence, isolated cases of wrongful deletion can undermine public trust.

As a prominent public figure, Prakash Raj's experience could prompt a broader review of the deletion process, especially in urban constituencies where voter mobility is high. The coming days may see the election authorities clarify their position and possibly re-verify the actor's entry.

For now, the onus is on the Bengaluru district administration to respond swiftly, ensuring that no eligible voter is disenfranchised due to administrative errors.
