Houthi attacks kill seven in Yemen, Saudi refinery hit

📅 2026-08-09 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Houthi attacks kill seven in Yemen and target Saudi refinery
  • Iran denies direct talks with US on Strait of Hormuz
  • Attack follows Saudi defence pact; regional tensions rise

Deadly strikes in Yemen

At least seven people were killed in Yemen on Sunday in a series of Houthi attacks, according to reports. The strikes come as the Iran-aligned group claimed responsibility for an assault on an oil refinery in Saudi Arabia. The refinery attack marks a significant escalation in cross-border hostilities.

The exact location of the fatalities in Yemen has not been independently confirmed. Local sources suggest the deaths occurred in areas under Houthi control, but details remain murky. Officials have not yet released a full casualty breakdown.

Saudi refinery targeted

The Houthis said they launched the refinery attack in retaliation for recent actions by the Saudi-led coalition. This is not the first time the group has struck Saudi infrastructure, but the timing is notable. It comes just days after Riyadh signed a defence pact with a key regional ally, a move that has irked the Houthis and their backers in Tehran.

Saudi authorities have not yet confirmed the extent of damage at the refinery. Early reports indicate the attack may have caused a fire, but there are no verified reports of casualties on Saudi soil. Energy markets are likely to watch for any disruption in supply, though no immediate impact on output has been announced.

Iran denies Strait of Hormuz talks

Amid the escalating violence, Iran has denied any direct negotiations with the United States over the Strait of Hormuz. The denial comes after speculation that Washington and Tehran might be exploring a deal to keep the strategic waterway open. A senior Iranian official dismissed the reports as 'baseless rumours', but stopped short of ruling out future talks.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. Any threat to its navigation could send shockwaves through energy markets. The Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia add to the region's volatility, raising the stakes for all parties involved.

Regional fallout and diplomatic moves

The refinery attack has drawn international condemnation, though major powers have so far stopped short of direct intervention. The Saudi defence pact, signed earlier this week, was widely seen as a deterrent against precisely such strikes. Yet the Houthis appear undeterred, signalling that they can strike at will.

Meanwhile, diplomatic efforts to end the Yemen war remain stalled. The UN has called for restraint, but no concrete peace initiative has gained traction. The Houthis continue to demand the lifting of a blockade and a full withdrawal of coalition forces, conditions that Riyadh is unlikely to accept.

What to watch

In the coming days, all eyes will be on Saudi Arabia's response. A major military retaliation could widen the conflict, while a more measured approach might open the door for renewed diplomacy. Also watch for any movement on the Strait of Hormuz talks, as even indirect signals could shift market sentiment.
