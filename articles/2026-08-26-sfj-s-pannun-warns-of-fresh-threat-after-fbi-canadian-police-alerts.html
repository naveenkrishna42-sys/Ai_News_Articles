SFJ's Pannun warns of fresh threat after FBI, Canadian police alerts

  • Pannun claims fresh threat to his life, citing agency alerts

SFJ leader claims new threat after US, Canadian agencies issue warnings

Khalistan separatist leader Hardeep Singh Pannun has claimed that he has received fresh threats to his life after the FBI and Canadian police warned him about a credible assassination plot. Pannun, who was the leader of the Sikh For Justice (SFJ) movement, has been a vocal critic of the Indian government and has been involved in several high-profile protests. The SFJ has been a key player in the Khalistan movement, which seeks to create an independent Sikh state in Punjab.

Background of previous threats and attacks

Pannun's claims come amid a backdrop of increasing violence and threats within the Sikh separatist movement. In 2023, Pannun was the target of an assassination attempt, in which a bomb was detonated near his residence in Punjab. The attack resulted in the death of one person and injuries to several others. The SFJ has blamed the attack on the Indian government and has accused it of being involved in a campaign of intimidation against the separatist movement.

SFJ accuses rival Sikh group of orchestrating threats

The SFJ has accused the Goldy Brar group, another Sikh separatist organization, of orchestrating the threats against Pannun. The Goldy Brar group has been a rival to the SFJ and has been involved in several violent clashes with the Indian security forces. The SFJ has claimed that the Goldy Brar group is being backed by the Indian government and is involved in a campaign of intimidation against the separatist movement.

Expert analysis: Growing violence in Sikh separatist movement

Experts on Sikh separatism have warned that the growing violence and threats within the movement pose a serious challenge to India's national security. The separatist movement has been a key issue of contention between India and Pakistan, with both countries supporting different factions within the movement. The growing violence and threats within the movement have raised concerns about the potential for a wider conflict between India and Pakistan.
