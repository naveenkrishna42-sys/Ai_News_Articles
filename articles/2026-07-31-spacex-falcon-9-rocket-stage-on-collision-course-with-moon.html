SpaceX Falcon 9 Rocket Stage on Collision Course With Moon

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • A SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket stage is on a collision course with the Moon.
  • The impact is expected to occur on August 5.
  • Scientists are uncertain what the crash will reveal about the Moon's interior.
  • The rocket stage has been drifting in space since its launch years ago.

A discarded SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket stage, drifting through space for years, is now on a high-speed collision course with the Moon. The impact is expected to occur on August 5, and scientists are bracing for an event that could offer a rare glimpse into the lunar surface.

An Accidental Trajectory

The rocket stage was originally launched as part of a SpaceX mission, but after completing its task, it was left in a chaotic orbit. Over time, gravitational forces pulled it into a path that intersects with the Moon's trajectory. Astronomers tracking the object confirmed that the collision is inevitable.

This is not a planned impact. Unlike deliberate lunar missions that target specific regions for scientific study, this is an accidental crash. The rocket stage, weighing several tonnes, is travelling at high speed, and when it hits the Moon, it will create a new crater.

What Scientists Expect

Experts are uncertain about the exact outcome. The Moon has no atmosphere, so the rocket stage will strike the surface directly, with no braking effect. The impact could eject debris and expose material from beneath the lunar crust, offering clues about the Moon's composition.

Some scientists hope the collision will provide data on the Moon's interior, which remains largely unexplored. Others are concerned about the potential for contamination, as the rocket stage carries residual fuel and other materials that could mix with lunar soil.

A Rare Opportunity

While the crash is accidental, it presents a unique chance to study the Moon's response to a high-velocity impact. The resulting crater will be observed by orbiting spacecraft and Earth-based telescopes, providing a natural experiment that would be difficult to replicate deliberately.

Similar events have occurred before, but this is the first time a SpaceX rocket stage will hit the Moon. The lack of prior data means scientists are unsure what to expect, but the event will be closely monitored.

Looking Ahead

The collision is set to take place on August 5, and astronomers are working to pinpoint the exact location. Once the impact occurs, the focus will shift to analysing the debris and crater formation, which could take weeks or months.

This incident also raises questions about the management of space debris. As more rockets are launched, the risk of accidental collisions with celestial bodies increases. The event will likely prompt discussions about better tracking and disposal of spent rocket stages.
