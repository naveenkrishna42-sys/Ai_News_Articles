Tingling or Burning Feet? 7 Causes Apollo Doctors Warn You Never to Ignore

✍️ By TIVRA News Science & Health Desk 📅 2026-09-14 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tingling or burning feet (paraesthesia) is a symptom, not a diagnosis — the underlying cause determines whether it is a minor deficiency or a progressive nerve disorder.
  • Vitamin B12 and vitamin D deficiency are among India's most common and most reversible causes, yet they are routinely missed without targeted blood tests.
  • Diabetic peripheral neuropathy affects a large share of India's diabetic population and is the single most important cause to rule out early, as nerve damage becomes irreversible over time.

Persistent tingling, numbness, or a burning sensation in the feet is one of the most commonly dismissed symptoms in Indian outpatient clinics. Patients attribute it to sitting cross-legged for too long, tight footwear, or simply "age." An Apollo Hospitals specialist recently flagged seven causes that deserve a proper clinical workup rather than home remedies — and the list spans nutritional, metabolic, neurological, and vascular origins.

Burning feet at night, a pins-and-needles sensation while walking, or numbness that makes you miss a step are all forms of paraesthesia. The symptom itself is not a disease. It is a signal, and the cause decides whether treatment is a three-month course of supplements or long-term nerve management.

Why Tingling Feet Demand a Real Diagnosis

Peripheral nerves are the longest in the body, running from the lower spine to the toes. That length makes them vulnerable: any systemic problem — poor blood sugar control, a vitamin shortfall, thyroid imbalance, or alcohol toxicity — shows up in the feet first. This is why neurologists treat foot symptoms as an early warning system rather than a local foot problem.

Delay carries real cost. Nerve damage that is caught in the deficiency stage is often fully reversible. The same damage caught after two years of uncontrolled diabetes may only be manageable, not curable.

The 7 Causes Apollo Doctors Flag

1. Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy

High blood sugar damages the small blood vessels that feed nerves. Symptoms usually begin symmetrically in both feet and worsen at night. Anyone with a family history of diabetes should get an HbA1c test at the first sign of tingling.

2. Vitamin B12 Deficiency

Widespread among vegetarians, people on long-term metformin, and those with acid-reflux medication use. It is cheap to test and cheap to correct, which makes missing it particularly unfortunate.

3. Vitamin D Deficiency

Linked to nerve irritation and muscle cramps. India's indoor, sunscreen-conscious urban population has high deficiency rates despite abundant sunlight.

4. Hypothyroidism

An underactive thyroid can cause nerve compression and fluid retention that presses on peripheral nerves. A simple TSH test rules it in or out.

5. Alcohol-Related Neuropathy

Chronic alcohol use is directly toxic to nerves and also blocks B-vitamin absorption, producing a double hit. Symptoms often improve substantially with abstinence and supplementation.

6. Lumbar Spine Problems

A slipped disc or spinal stenosis can pinch the nerve root supplying the foot. Here the tingling often follows a specific pattern along one leg rather than affecting both feet evenly.

7. Peripheral Arterial Disease

Narrowed leg arteries reduce blood flow, causing burning and cramping pain while walking that eases with rest. Smokers and people over 50 face the highest risk, and this condition carries cardiovascular implications beyond the feet.

Real-World Buyer and Patient Implications

  • Do not self-medicate with random B-complex tablets. Untargeted supplementation can mask a deficiency while the real cause — diabetes or a spinal issue — progresses.
  • Insist on a defined test panel. HbA1c, serum B12, vitamin D, and TSH cover the majority of reversible causes for under ₹3,500 at most private labs.
  • Foot symptoms are a cardiovascular signal. Burning pain while walking can indicate arterial disease, which raises heart-attack and stroke risk.
  • Night-time worsening is a red flag. Symptoms that disturb sleep rarely resolve on their own and warrant a neurologist referral.
  • One-sided symptoms point to the spine. Even, both-feet symptoms usually point to a systemic cause.

When to See a Doctor Immediately

Book an appointment without waiting if you notice foot wounds that heal slowly, sudden weakness, loss of balance, or numbness that spreads upward. These suggest advanced nerve or vascular compromise. For everyone else, a single consultation with basic blood work resolves most cases quickly and cheaply.

The Apollo guidance is straightforward: tingling and burning feet are common, but they are not normal. Treat them as your body's earliest available warning — and act while the cause is still fixable.
