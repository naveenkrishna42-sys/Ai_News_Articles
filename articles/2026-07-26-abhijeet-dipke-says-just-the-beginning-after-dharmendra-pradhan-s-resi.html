Abhijeet Dipke Says 'Just The Beginning' After Dharmendra Pradhan's Resignation

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Abhijeet Dipke released a morning video post calling the protest 'just the beginning'.
  • The protest at Jantar Mantar followed Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan's resignation.
  • Dipke referenced CJI Surya Kant's 'cockroaches' remark as a turning point for the Gen-Z movement.
  • The resignation is seen as a victory for India's first major Gen-Z led protest movement.

Morning Message from Jantar Mantar

Student leader Abhijeet Dipke released a video post on Sunday morning from Jantar Mantar, where the youth protest has been ongoing for weeks. In the clip, he declared the movement a success but warned it was far from over.

“Just the beginning, long way to go,” Dipke said, standing against the backdrop of the protest site. The video comes hours after Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan submitted his resignation.

The Remark That Sparked a Movement

The protest traces its origins to a remark made by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant 71 days ago. In a courtroom exchange, the CJI reportedly referred to the protesting students as “cockroaches.” The comment went viral and galvanised young Indians across the country.

“If you had not called us 'cockroaches'… none of this would have happened,” Dipke said in his message to the CJI. The movement, now being called India’s first Gen-Z led protest, quickly escalated into demands for Pradhan’s resignation over policy failures and alleged mismanagement of the education sector.

A Victory for 'Cockroach Power'

What began as a small student demonstration at Jantar Mantar swelled into a national movement. The Telegraph India called it “cockroach power,” describing how Dipke became the face of a generation that refused to be dismissed.

Pradhan’s resignation is being seen as a direct outcome of this pressure. Frontline Magazine described it as a case where “a youth protest forced the education minister out.” The resignation marks a rare instance of a union minister stepping down following sustained student agitation.

What Dipke Said Next

In his video, Dipke did not name specific next steps but hinted at broader demands. He said the movement would now focus on systemic changes in education policy and accountability mechanisms.

He also thanked the thousands of students who camped at Jantar Mantar for weeks. “This is your victory,” he said. “But we cannot stop here.”

The protest site remains active, with students vowing to stay until their remaining demands are addressed. Officials have not yet confirmed the timeline for accepting Pradhan's resignation or naming a successor.

What to Watch

The government is expected to announce a new education minister in the coming days. Observers will be watching whether Dipke and the student coalition pivot to policy advocacy or maintain street-level pressure. The movement's longevity will test whether Gen-Z's political awakening translates into sustained change.
