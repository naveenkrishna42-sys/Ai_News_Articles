Bangladesh PM seeks 'conducive environment' to reset ties with India

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bangladesh PM urges India to expedite Sheikh Hasina's extradition
  • Both nations signal willingness for back-channel talks and thaw in relations
  • High-level Indian team expected in Dhaka amid diplomatic reset efforts

Bangladesh's Prime Minister has called for a 'conducive environment' to reset strained ties with India, signalling a potential thaw in bilateral relations that have been marked by tension since the political upheaval in Dhaka. The remarks come amid growing pressure from Bangladesh's interim government for India to expedite the extradition of former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, who remains in exile.

Diplomatic overtures and extradition demand

In his latest public statement, the Bangladesh PM emphasised the need for mutual trust and a stable atmosphere to move forward. "We want a conducive environment to reset our relations with India," he said, without elaborating on specific timelines or conditions. The call follows repeated requests from Dhaka for New Delhi to hand over Hasina, who fled Bangladesh last year amid widespread protests.

India has not publicly responded to the extradition demand, but diplomatic sources indicate that New Delhi is treading cautiously, balancing its historical ties with Hasina's Awami League and the reality of the new dispensation in Dhaka. The issue has become a sticking point in bilateral talks, with Bangladesh's leadership pressing for legal action against the former PM on charges of corruption and alleged human rights abuses.

Signals of a thaw

Despite the friction, recent developments suggest both sides are exploring avenues for de-escalation. Reports indicate that back-channel communications have intensified, with a high-level Indian team expected to visit Dhaka in the coming weeks. The visit, though unconfirmed officially, is seen as a positive step towards restoring normalcy in ties that have been strained over border security, trade, and the treatment of minorities in Bangladesh.

Analysts point to several factors driving the cautious rapprochement. For India, a stable Bangladesh is crucial for its 'Neighbourhood First' policy, especially in the context of China's growing influence in the region. For Bangladesh, India remains a vital partner for trade, energy, and connectivity, despite domestic political pressures to adopt a more assertive stance.

Domestic pressures and political dynamics

Inside Bangladesh, the interim government faces significant political challenges, including managing public sentiment that is largely hostile to Hasina and her close ties with New Delhi. The PM's recent comments appear aimed at balancing these domestic expectations with the pragmatic need to maintain functional relations with India. Opposition groups, including the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP), have also weighed in, with some leaders suggesting that Tarique Rahman, the BNP's acting chairman, could play a role in future diplomatic engagements with India.

Observers note that the extradition demand is likely to remain a contentious issue, as India's legal framework and historical precedents make such transfers difficult, especially for a former head of government. However, the willingness of both parties to engage in dialogue signals a pragmatic shift away from the rhetoric of confrontation.

What to watch next

All eyes are now on the proposed Indian delegation's visit to Dhaka and whether it will lead to concrete agreements on extradition, trade, or security cooperation. The coming weeks will be crucial in determining whether this 'conducive environment' translates into tangible outcomes or remains an aspirational goal.
