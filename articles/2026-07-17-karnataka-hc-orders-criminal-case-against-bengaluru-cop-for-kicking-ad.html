Karnataka HC orders criminal case against Bengaluru cop for kicking advocate

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Karnataka High Court ordered a criminal case against a Bengaluru police officer for kicking an advocate.
  • The incident occurred during a road confrontation, with the advocate alleging assault by the officer.
  • The court found prima facie evidence of misconduct, rejecting the police version of events.

The Karnataka High Court has ordered the registration of a criminal case against a Bengaluru police officer for allegedly kicking an advocate during a road incident. The directive came from a single-judge bench, which observed that the officer's actions appeared to be a clear case of misconduct.

Incident sparks legal action

The case stems from an altercation on a Bengaluru road. The advocate claimed that the police officer, identified as a sub-inspector, kicked him during a confrontation. The advocate filed a complaint, but the police initially failed to register an FIR.

The High Court stepped in after the advocate approached it seeking justice. The court examined the available evidence, including video footage of the incident. It noted that the officer's behaviour seemed disproportionate and unprofessional.

Court rejects police version

The police had argued that the incident was a minor scuffle and that the advocate had been aggressive. However, the High Court dismissed this explanation. It stated that the footage clearly showed the officer kicking the advocate, which constituted assault.

The bench remarked that such actions by a law enforcement officer undermine public trust. It directed the Bengaluru police commissioner to ensure a thorough investigation. The court also warned against any attempts to shield the officer.

Legal precedent and implications

This order reinforces the principle that no one, including police personnel, is above the law. Legal experts have noted that the High Court's intervention sends a strong message about accountability. The case is expected to proceed under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code.

The advocate's lawyer argued that the incident highlighted a pattern of police brutality. The court agreed that prima facie evidence warranted a criminal case. It instructed the investigating officer to submit a status report within four weeks.

Authorities have not yet confirmed the officer's current posting or any disciplinary action taken. The Bengaluru police department has declined to comment on the ongoing case.

What happens next

The criminal case will now proceed through the lower courts. The investigation will determine whether the officer faces charges of assault or other offences. The High Court has directed the police to file a charge sheet within 90 days. Observers will watch how the Bengaluru police handle this case, given its potential to set a precedent for police accountability in Karnataka.
