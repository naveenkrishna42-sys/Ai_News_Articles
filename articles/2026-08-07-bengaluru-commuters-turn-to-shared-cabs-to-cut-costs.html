Bengaluru commuters turn to shared cabs to cut costs

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shared cabs and carpooling gain popularity in Bengaluru amid rising fuel costs.
  • Commuters report savings of up to 40% on daily travel expenses.
  • Experts suggest policy support could further boost shared mobility adoption.

Bengaluru's notorious traffic and rising fuel prices are pushing commuters to rethink how they travel. A growing number of residents are now turning to shared cabs and carpooling as a practical way to cut daily expenses. What began as an occasional option has become a regular habit for many.

Cost savings drive the shift

For daily commuters, the math is simple. Splitting a cab fare between two or three passengers can reduce travel costs by up to 40% compared to a solo ride. With petrol prices hovering near the ₹100 mark in the city, that saving adds up quickly over a month.

Take the example of a typical office-goer travelling from Whitefield to Electronic City. A solo cab ride can cost anywhere between ₹500 and ₹700 one way. Sharing that ride with two others brings the cost down to roughly ₹200 per person. Over 20 working days, the annual saving crosses the ₹1 lakh mark.

Apps make sharing easier

Ride-hailing platforms have simplified the process. Features that match passengers headed in the same direction are now built into popular apps. Users can select a shared ride option at booking, and the fare is calculated accordingly. This convenience has removed much of the friction that earlier made carpooling a logistical challenge.

Corporate campuses in Bengaluru have also stepped in. Several companies run internal carpool groups or tie up with mobility startups to offer shared transport for employees. These initiatives not only reduce costs for staff but also ease parking pressure at office complexes.

Traffic and environment benefits

The benefits extend beyond individual savings. Fewer cars on the road mean less congestion and lower emissions. Bengaluru, which consistently ranks among the world's most traffic-clogged cities, could see measurable relief if shared mobility gains wider acceptance. City planners have long pushed for higher vehicle occupancy as a key strategy to tackle gridlock.

However, challenges remain. Safety concerns, especially for women commuters, and the lack of dedicated lanes for high-occupancy vehicles are hurdles. Some users also complain about longer travel times due to multiple pickups and drop-offs. These issues need addressing for shared mobility to scale up.

What lies ahead

Experts believe that policy support could accelerate the trend. Incentives such as reduced tolls for shared vehicles or preferential parking spots could nudge more people to opt for shared rides. Some cities abroad have implemented such measures with success, and Bengaluru could follow suit.

For now, commuters are voting with their wallets. As fuel prices show no sign of easing and traffic worsens, the shared seat is fast becoming the preferred choice for many in the city. The road ahead may still be congested, but it is clear that the journey is cheaper when taken together.
