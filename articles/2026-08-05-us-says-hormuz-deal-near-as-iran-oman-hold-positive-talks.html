US says Hormuz deal near as Iran, Oman hold 'positive' talks

📅 2026-08-05 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US officials say a deal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz could be reached 'today or tomorrow'.
  • Iran and Oman held 'positive' talks, with reports of a temporary single shipping corridor being discussed.
  • A ship was hit in the Red Sea, though details remain unconfirmed.

The United States says an agreement to reopen the strategic Strait of Hormuz is close, following what Tehran and Muscat described as 'positive' talks. A senior aide to President Donald Trump indicated a deal could be signed as early as 'today or tomorrow', raising hopes of easing a standoff that has disrupted global oil shipments.

The comments came as Iranian and Omani officials met for discussions focused on creating a temporary shipping corridor through the strait, according to reports. The proposed arrangement, described as a 'single route', would allow safe passage for tankers under specific terms, though details remain under wraps.

Talks gain momentum

Trump himself acknowledged the progress, saying the US and Iran are having 'very good discussions'. His remarks mark a notable shift in tone from months of heightened tension, during which the strait — a conduit for about a fifth of the world's oil — saw several seizures and attacks.

Oman, which has long served as a diplomatic bridge between Washington and Tehran, is hosting the negotiations. The sultanate's role appears central to bridging differences, with both sides reportedly open to a limited, time-bound arrangement rather than a comprehensive settlement.

What the corridor could look like

The Times of India reported that the talks centre on establishing a 'single route' for shipping, potentially monitored by regional and international authorities. This would be a temporary measure, likely aimed at de-escalating while broader nuclear and sanctions disputes remain unresolved.

Iranian officials have not publicly detailed their bottom line, but analysts suggest Tehran wants guarantees against cargo interference or future asset freezes. Oman's mediators are said to be testing formulas that could satisfy both sides without requiring a full lifting of US sanctions.

Red Sea incident adds urgency

Meanwhile, a ship was hit in the Red Sea, though the extent of damage and the vessel's identity have not been confirmed. The attack, reported on the same day as the Hormuz talks, underscores the fragility of regional shipping lanes and raises the stakes for a swift resolution.

It is unclear whether the Red Sea incident is linked to the Hormuz negotiations or the wider Iran-Israel conflict that has simmered in recent months. Naval forces in the region are on heightened alert, but no group has yet claimed responsibility.

Oil markets watch closely

Oil prices have fluctuated sharply on every twist in the Hormuz story, and traders are now pricing in a possible reopening within days. A confirmed deal would likely ease supply fears, though analysts caution that any agreement could be fragile and open to collapse if ground conditions change.

Global shipping firms, which have rerouted vessels around the Arabian Peninsula at significant cost, are awaiting official confirmation of a safe corridor. Insurance premiums for tankers transiting the region remain elevated, reflecting lingering risk.

The coming hours are critical. If the US and Iran finalise the arrangement as suggested, it would mark a rare diplomatic breakthrough in a year dominated by confrontation. But with details still scarce and the Red Sea incident unresolved, the window for a durable deal remains narrow.
