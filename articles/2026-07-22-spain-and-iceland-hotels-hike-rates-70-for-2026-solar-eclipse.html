Spain and Iceland hotels hike rates 70% for 2026 solar eclipse

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hotel prices along the 2026 solar eclipse path in Spain and Iceland have surged up to 70% compared to normal rates.
  • Some properties in prime viewing locations are charging over €500 per night.
  • Travel experts advise booking early and checking cancellation policies due to high demand.

Eclipse-driven price surge hits Spanish and Icelandic hotels

Hotels along the path of the 2026 solar eclipse in Spain and Iceland are charging rates up to 70% higher than normal, according to a report by Hospitality Net. The price hike reflects the massive demand from eclipse chasers and tourists who want to witness the celestial event on 12 August.

In Spain, coastal towns like those in Galicia and the Balearic Islands are seeing the steepest increases. A standard double room that usually costs €120 a night is now listed at over €200. Some luxury properties in prime viewing spots have crossed the €500 mark.

Iceland's remote viewing spots command premium

Iceland, which will experience a total solar eclipse in 2026, is seeing even sharper price jumps. Hotels near the capital Reykjavik and in the northern fjords are reporting near-full occupancy. A budget guesthouse that typically charges €80 is now quoting €150 or more for the eclipse week.

Travel industry data from PR Newswire shows that search volumes for flights and accommodation in both countries have spiked dramatically since the start of 2026. Tour operators have also launched special eclipse packages, bundling guided viewing with accommodation and transport.

What travellers should check before booking

Experts warn that not all high-priced rooms offer a guaranteed view. Weather conditions in Iceland can be unpredictable, with cloud cover a real risk. Travellers are advised to book refundable or flexible rates where possible.

Some hotels in Spain are offering eclipse-viewing packages that include telescopes, solar glasses, and a rooftop dinner. These packages come at a premium, often exceeding €600 per person for a single night.

Festivals and special events add to the rush

A festival in Iceland, guided by the wonder of the eclipse, is drawing a younger crowd. Organisers have set up campsites and pop-up accommodation near the path of totality. But even these budget options are filling up fast.

In Cornwall, UK, which will see a partial eclipse, local businesses are also cashing in. Bed and breakfasts that normally charge £90 are now asking £150. Some pubs have announced eclipse-viewing brunches at £45 per head.

The price surge is not limited to hotels. Car rentals in Iceland are up 40%, and domestic flights within Spain have seen a 25% increase in fares for the eclipse week.

Watch for last-minute cancellations and alternatives

Analysts say some travellers may cancel closer to the date if weather forecasts look poor. This could free up rooms, but at still-elevated prices. Others are looking at alternative locations along the eclipse path, including parts of Portugal and northern Africa.

For those still hoping to book, the advice is simple: act fast, read the fine print, and be prepared to pay a premium. The 2026 solar eclipse is expected to be one of the most-viewed celestial events in years, and the hospitality industry is responding accordingly.

As the eclipse day approaches, more inventory may open up, but prices are unlikely to drop significantly. The best bet remains securing a room now with a flexible cancellation policy.
