Rahul Gandhi vs Amit Shah: Inside Parliament Showdown Over Student Protests

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rahul Gandhi accused Amit Shah of ordering police brutality against student protesters, demanding his removal.
  • BJP hit back, alleging Gandhi spread misinformation and demanding an apology for his remarks.
  • The Lok Sabha witnessed a heated debate but later passed the anti-paper leak bill amid the uproar.

New Delhi: A routine parliamentary session turned into a battlefield on Wednesday after Congress leader Rahul Gandhi directly accused Home Minister Amit Shah of orchestrating police action against student protesters. The remarks, made on the floor of the Lok Sabha, triggered a fierce showdown between the ruling BJP and the Opposition, forcing the House into chaos.

Gandhi's direct attack on Shah

Rahul Gandhi did not mince words. He alleged that the Home Minister had personally ordered police to use force against young demonstrators who were demanding action on exam paper leaks. “The Prime Minister must sack Amit Shah for this brutality,” Gandhi said, his voice cutting through the din of the House.

The Congress leader’s statement was a direct escalation. For weeks, student groups in several states have been protesting against repeated leaks of competitive examination papers. The issue has become a political flashpoint, with the Opposition accusing the government of failing to protect the youth.

BJP's counterattack

The ruling party was quick to respond. Senior BJP leaders accused Rahul Gandhi of spreading “blatant misinformation” about the police action. “He is trying to defame the Home Minister and the government with baseless allegations,” said a party spokesperson. The BJP demanded an unconditional apology from Gandhi, both inside and outside Parliament.

The exchange turned the Lok Sabha into a verbal slugfest. Ruling party MPs shouted down Opposition members, while Congress and its allies accused the Speaker of not allowing them to speak. At one point, proceedings had to be adjourned briefly as tempers flared.

What sparked the protest

The student protests that triggered this political storm began after reports emerged of large-scale paper leaks in state and central recruitment exams. Thousands of students took to the streets in cities like Patna, Lucknow, and Delhi, demanding a crackdown on the leak mafia and compensation for affected candidates.

Police in some states resorted to lathi charges and water cannons to disperse crowds. Videos of the action went viral, prompting outrage from Opposition parties. Rahul Gandhi’s remarks in Parliament were aimed squarely at holding the Home Minister accountable for what he termed “state-sponsored violence against the youth.”

Anti-paper leak bill passed

Despite the heated debate, the Lok Sabha managed to pass the much-awaited anti-paper leak bill. The legislation proposes stringent punishment for those involved in leaking examination papers, including up to ten years in prison and heavy fines.

The government defended the bill as a necessary step to protect the integrity of the country’s examination system. “This law will act as a strong deterrent,” said Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan during the debate. The Opposition, while supporting the bill in principle, argued that the government was using it as a shield to divert attention from the police action on protesters.

The bill now moves to the Rajya Sabha, where it is expected to face further scrutiny. Political observers say the real contest will be over who gets credit for the legislation — and who takes the blame for the crackdown.

With Parliament still in session, all eyes are now on whether the government will respond formally to Rahul Gandhi’s demand for Amit Shah’s removal. The BJP has shown no signs of backing down, and the Opposition is gearing up for more confrontations. The student protests, meanwhile, continue in several states, adding pressure on the government to deliver on its promises of exam reform.
