Telangana-Zepto MoU: Gig Worker Resting Spaces in Hyderabad

Key points

  • Initiative aims to improve working conditions and provide relief to delivery and gig economy workers
  • MoU signed as part of broader efforts to regulate and support the growing gig workforce in the region

State-Gig Partnership for Worker Welfare

In a move aimed at improving working conditions for gig workers, the Telangana government has signed a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) with quick-commerce platform Zepto to establish resting spaces across Hyderabad. The agreement marks one of the first formal collaborations between a southern state government and a private gig platform focused on worker infrastructure.

Background and Context

Hyderabad, home to over 1.2 million gig workers according to recent industry estimates, has seen rising concerns over long working hours, lack of break areas, and inadequate access to basic amenities for delivery and service-based gig workers. The Telangana government’s initiative aligns with national discussions on gig worker rights and social security, including the four Labour Codes implemented across India.

Details of the MoU

The MoU outlines plans to set up branded resting pods and micro-break zones at strategic locations frequented by gig workers, including near commercial hubs, metro stations, and residential layouts. These spaces are expected to offer seating, charging points, hydration stations, and digital lockers for secure storage. Zepto will reportedly fund the initial setup, while the Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) will facilitate site allocation and maintenance.

Benchmarking Worker-Focused Infrastructure

Similar initiatives in cities like Bengaluru and Gurugram have shown mixed results. While some corporate-run break lounges have improved short-term comfort, long-term sustainability often depends on public-private coordination. Hyderabad’s model introduces a hybrid approach with government-backed zoning and private operational support, potentially offering better scalability.

Real-World Implications

For gig workers, these spaces could reduce daily fatigue and improve productivity, especially during peak delivery hours. From a buyer’s perspective—whether investors in gig platforms or policymakers—investing in worker infrastructure may lower attrition and increase service reliability. Early adopters like Zepto may also gain a competitive edge in talent acquisition and brand perception among India’s $15 billion gig economy.

Key Insights and Outlook

  • Worker-Centric Design: Spaces include ergonomic seating and charging facilities tailored for extended shifts.
  • Scalable Framework: Model can be replicated in other metro cities with similar gig density.
  • Government Backing: GHMC involvement ensures civic integration and long-term upkeep.
  • Limited Coverage: Initial rollout limited to select zones; citywide expansion timeline unclear.
  • Sustainability Concerns: Ongoing maintenance and usage monitoring will determine real impact.

With implementation expected to begin in Q4 2026, this MoU sets a precedent for how state governments can partner with gig platforms to formalize informal work ecosystems in urban India.
