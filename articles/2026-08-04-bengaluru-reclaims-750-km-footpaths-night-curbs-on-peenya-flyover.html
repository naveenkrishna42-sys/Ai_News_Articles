Bengaluru reclaims 750 km footpaths, night curbs on Peenya flyover

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BBMP reclaims 750 km of footpaths from encroachments across Bengaluru.
  • Night traffic curbs on Peenya flyover from Aug 5 for repair work, 11 pm to 5 am.
  • Drive part of wider pedestrian safety push; commuters advised to plan alternate routes.

Bengaluru's footpaths are getting a new lease of life. The city civic body has reclaimed 750 km of pavements that had been swallowed by encroachments, illegal parking and makeshift shops. The drive, which has been underway for months, is part of a broader push to make the city safer for pedestrians.

Officials said the reclaimed stretches span all zones, with major corridors in central and south Bengaluru seeing the most action. BBMP teams have removed debris, hawker structures and vehicles that had turned walking spaces into obstacle courses.

Peenya flyover repairs at night

In a separate move, traffic on the Peenya flyover will face night-time restrictions starting August 5. The flyover will be partially closed from 11 pm to 5 am for repair work. Commuters using the busy stretch during those hours should expect delays.

The repair is part of routine maintenance, but the timing is meant to minimise disruption. Daytime traffic will remain unaffected. Authorities have not yet confirmed how long the night curbs will last.

Pedestrian safety in focus

The footpath reclamation is a direct response to long-standing complaints. Walkers in areas like Shivajinagar, Malleshwaram and Koramangala often had to step onto the road to get past parked two-wheelers or extended shopfronts. The civic body has now marked clear boundaries and is urging residents to report fresh encroachments.

Some stretches have also been re-laid with tactile paving for the visually impaired. But activists say the real test is enforcement. Past attempts to clear footpaths have faltered when encroachers returned within weeks.

What commuters should know

For those using the Peenya flyover at night, alternate routes via the service roads are available. BBMP has said it will put up signboards and deploy traffic marshals to guide vehicles. The flyover connects several industrial areas, so night-shift workers should factor in extra travel time.

On the footpath front, the civic body plans to geotag all reclaimed stretches and conduct periodic audits. A senior official said the focus is now on preventing re-encroachment through regular patrolling and fines.

Looking ahead

Both initiatives reflect a city trying to balance infrastructure repair with pedestrian rights. The night repair on Peenya flyover is expected to wrap up within weeks, but the footpath drive is a long-term project. Bengalureans will be watching whether the reclaimed pavements stay clear once the spotlight fades.
