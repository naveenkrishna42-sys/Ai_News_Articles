Delhi Rain Chaos: 81 Waterlogging Calls, 32 Trees Fall as City Chokes

📅 2026-08-08 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi received 81 waterlogging calls and 32 fallen tree reports amid heavy rain.
  • The India Meteorological Department (IMD) issued a red alert for the city.
  • Roads across Delhi and NCR remain waterlogged, with more rain forecast until next Friday.

Rain Batters Delhi, Emergency Lines Flooded

New Delhi: Heavy rain lashed Delhi on Saturday, throwing the national capital into chaos as civic agencies scrambled to respond to dozens of waterlogging complaints. The city's emergency helplines received at least 81 calls related to waterlogging, while 32 reports of fallen trees were logged, officials said.

The downpour, which began in the early hours, quickly overwhelmed drainage systems in several low-lying areas, turning major roads into shallow pools. Commuters faced hours of delays as traffic crawled through waterlogged stretches, with many vehicles breaking down mid-road.

Red Alert Issued, More Rain Predicted

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued a red alert for Delhi, signalling the possibility of very heavy rain over the next 24 hours. The weather office has forecast light to moderate rain continuing till next Friday, with the National Capital Region (NCR) also expected to remain waterlogged.

A slight dip in the minimum temperature was recorded on Saturday morning, offering little respite from the humidity that followed the rain. The IMD has advised residents to avoid unnecessary travel and to stay away from vulnerable structures.

Roads Under Water, Videos Capture Chaos

Videos shared on social media showed submerged underpasses, water entering ground-floor shops, and commuters wading through knee-deep water at several intersections. Areas such as Gurgaon were also severely affected, with multiple videos capturing the deluge on camera.

The city's traffic police reported long jams on key arterial roads, including stretches near ITO, Minto Bridge, and parts of Outer Ring Road. Several buses were diverted, and the Delhi Metro reported minor delays on select corridors due to water seepage at a few stations.

Civic Bodies on Alert, But Gaps Remain

Municipal corporations and the Public Works Department (PWD) said they had deployed additional pumps and teams to clear water from affected stretches. However, the sheer volume of rain exposed persistent gaps in the city's drainage infrastructure, which has historically struggled to cope with intense downpours.

Residents in several colonies reported that water had entered their homes, with some calling for compensation from authorities. Officials have not yet confirmed the total number of people affected or the extent of property damage.

What Next

With more rain predicted over the coming week, the IMD has urged civic agencies to remain on high alert. Authorities are expected to review drainage preparedness and issue fresh advisories as the monsoon system continues to hover over the region.
