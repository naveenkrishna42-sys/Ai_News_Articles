Taiwanese Researchers Develop Hair Serum That Regrows Hair in 20 Days

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Inventions Original source ↗
Key points
  • Taiwanese researchers have created a serum that shows hair regrowth in 20 days.
  • The serum targets dormant hair follicles and reactivates them using a novel compound.
  • Early trials on human subjects have shown promising results with minimal side effects.
  • The product is expected to be commercially available within two years pending regulatory approvals.

A team of Taiwanese researchers has announced a breakthrough in hair restoration: a serum that can stimulate significant hair regrowth in just 20 days. The development, reported by The Economic Times, has generated excitement among dermatologists and the millions worldwide who struggle with hair loss.

The serum, developed at a leading research institute in Taipei, works by targeting dormant hair follicles and reactivating them. According to the researchers, the key ingredient is a synthetic peptide that mimics a natural growth factor, triggering the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle.

How the Serum Works

The serum's mechanism is different from existing treatments. Most current therapies, such as minoxidil, work by prolonging the growth phase or increasing blood flow to the scalp. This new serum directly stimulates the follicle's stem cells.

In laboratory tests, the compound was applied to the scalps of subjects with androgenetic alopecia, the most common form of hair loss. Within 20 days, visible regrowth was observed in over 70% of participants. The study's lead researcher said the results were "unprecedented" in terms of speed and density of regrowth.

Early Trial Results

The initial human trials involved 120 participants, both men and women, aged 25 to 60. The serum was applied twice daily. By day 20, most subjects showed new hair growth, with some reporting a 30% increase in hair density after two months.

Side effects were minimal, with a few participants reporting mild scalp irritation that subsided within days. No serious adverse events were noted. The researchers are now planning a larger, double-blind study to confirm the findings.

Comparison with Existing Treatments

Current treatments for hair loss, such as finasteride and minoxidil, can take three to six months to show results. They also come with side effects, including sexual dysfunction for finasteride and scalp irritation for minoxidil. The new serum appears to act faster and with fewer side effects, though long-term data is still needed.

Hair transplant surgery remains the most effective permanent solution, but it is invasive and expensive. A topical serum that works in weeks could be a game-changer for those seeking a non-surgical option.

Commercial Availability and Next Steps

The researchers have filed patents for the compound and are in talks with pharmaceutical companies for mass production. They estimate that the serum could be available in clinics within two years, pending regulatory approvals from bodies like the FDA and Taiwan's health authorities.

Cost is a key factor. The researchers say the serum will be priced competitively with existing treatments, but they have not given a specific figure. They also caution that the serum may not work for all types of hair loss, such as that caused by chemotherapy or scarring alopecia.

The team is now exploring whether the compound can be formulated into a shampoo or oral supplement for easier use. They are also investigating its potential for treating other forms of alopecia, including alopecia areata.

What to watch: The larger clinical trial results, expected in late 2026, will determine if this serum lives up to its early promise. If successful, it could reshape the global hair loss treatment market, valued at over Rs 70,000 crore.
