Supreme Court quashes FIRs, CJP cancels protest

Key points

  • CJP leader Saurav Das withdraws September 5 protest

Supreme Court Cancels FIRs Against CJP Protesters

The Supreme Court of India has taken a significant step in the ongoing political unrest by canceling all First Information Reports (FIRs) against the Communist Party of India (Marxist) (CPI-M) and Communist Party of India (CPI) protesters. This decision, however, has an exception for those with criminal antecedents, who will continue to face trial.

This move comes as a response to the Centre's efforts to prevent further unrest, which has been escalating in recent weeks. The decision is seen as a compromise between the government and the protesting groups, aiming to restore peace and order.

CJP Leader Withdraws September 5 Protest

In a significant development, the leader of the Communist Party of India (Marxist) (CPI-M), Saurav Das, has announced the withdrawal of the September 5 protest. This decision was made after a meeting with the Supreme Court, where he presented his party's demands to the Centre.

Das's decision to withdraw the protest is a major shift in the ongoing political unrest. It signals a potential end to the protests, which have been ongoing for several weeks. However, the broader impact of this decision remains to be seen.

Centre Takes Action To Prevent Unrest

The Centre has been taking several measures to prevent further unrest. These include the cancellation of FIRs against the protesting groups, as well as the deployment of additional security forces in the affected areas.

However, the Centre's actions have been met with mixed reactions. While some see them as a necessary step to restore peace, others argue that they are a sign of the government's weakness and inability to handle the situation.

Broader Impact Of The Decision

The decision by the Supreme Court and the CJP leader to withdraw the protest has significant implications. It could potentially lead to a return to normalcy in the affected areas, but it also raises questions about the future of political protests in India.

Moreover, the decision to exempt hardened criminals from the cancellation of FIRs is a contentious one. It could potentially lead to a situation where only the most serious offenders are punished, while others are left to continue their activities.
