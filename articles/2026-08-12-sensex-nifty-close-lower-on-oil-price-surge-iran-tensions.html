Sensex, Nifty close lower on oil price surge, Iran tensions

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty ended in the red on August 12, 2026.
  • Rising oil prices and Iran-related geopolitical concerns dampened market mood.
  • Broader indices also declined, with most sectoral indices closing lower.

Indian stock markets closed in the red on Wednesday, August 12, 2026, as investors weighed the impact of surging crude oil prices and escalating geopolitical tensions involving Iran. The benchmark Sensex and Nifty both ended lower, extending the cautious tone that has prevailed in recent sessions.

The sell-off was broad-based, with most sectoral indices closing in negative territory. Banking, auto, and energy stocks were among the worst hit, while defensive sectors like IT and pharma offered some resilience. Market participants said the mood was cautious ahead of any further escalation in the Middle East.

Oil prices and Iran in focus

Brent crude oil prices climbed sharply during the day, crossing a key psychological level, as supply concerns resurfaced following fresh developments in Iran. Traders feared that any disruption to oil supplies from the region could stoke inflation and hurt economic growth, particularly for oil-importing nations like India.

Rising crude prices typically pressure India's fiscal metrics and current account deficit, and also push up domestic fuel costs. This weighed on investor sentiment, prompting profit-booking in energy and manufacturing stocks.

Sensex and Nifty performance

The 30-share Sensex closed down by over 300 points, while the broader Nifty 50 settled below the 24,500 mark. Intraday, both indices swung between gains and losses before settling firmly in the red, indicating sustained selling pressure at higher levels.

Among the top losers were heavyweight stocks like Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, and ICICI Bank. On the other hand, select IT stocks such as Infosys and TCS provided minor support, though the gains were not enough to lift the indices.

Broader market and sectoral trends

The broader market also felt the heat, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices slipping by nearly a percent each. Analysts said retail investors remained cautious, and volatility is likely to stay elevated in the near term.

Sectorally, the Nifty Oil & Gas index fell the most, followed by PSU Bank and Auto indices. The Nifty IT index was the only major gainer, rising marginally as investors sought safety in export-oriented stocks amid a weak rupee.

What to watch next

Market participants will now track crude oil prices and any diplomatic moves regarding Iran over the next few sessions. A further spike in oil could trigger another round of selling, while any de-escalation might bring some relief to the markets.

Investors are also eyeing domestic macroeconomic data and global cues, including US Federal Reserve commentary, for directional cues. Analysts advise staying selective and focusing on quality stocks with strong fundamentals in this uncertain environment.
