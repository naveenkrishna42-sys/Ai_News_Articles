NHAI Kickstarts Cooum River Revamp

The National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) has finally embarked on its promise to remove temporary bunds along the Cooum River in Chennai. This project, long-awaited by city residents, aims to mitigate the recurrent flooding issues that have plagued the city during monsoons.

Bunds: A Brief History

Temporary bunds, or embankments, were constructed along the CooumRiver in the 1970s to prevent the river from meandering and encroaching upon nearby roads. However, these structures have over time contributed to increased flooding, trapping debris and detritus and restricting the river's natural flow.

Project Timeline and Scope

The project, estimated to cost ₹50 crore, is set to span over 18 months. It will involve the demolition of these bunds and the restoration of the river's natural course. The project also includes plans for improved drainage systems and the construction of retention walls to protect nearby roads.

Expert Opinions: Cautious Optimism

While many welcome the move, experts caution against unrealistic expectations. Dr. A. Ravi, hydraulic engineering expert at IIT Madras, notes, "The bunds are indeed a problem, but removing them alone won't solve the flooding issue. We need a comprehensive plan that addresses encroachment, waste disposal, and urban planning."

  • Pros: Addresses a long-standing issue, improves river flow, and eases flooding.
  • Cons: Timing of the project raises concerns about the impending monsoon season.
  • Key Facts: The Cooum River is one of the longest and most polluted rivers in South India, with a length of around 77 km.

While the NHAI's project marks a significant step towards improving Chennai's flood resilience, it remains to be seen whether it will provide a long-term solution. The success of this project hinges not just on the removal of bunds, but on a holistic approach to urban river management.
