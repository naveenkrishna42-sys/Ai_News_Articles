Foreign banks lead rate transmission in RBI easing cycle: bulletin

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Foreign banks have shown the strongest pass-through of RBI's repo rate cuts to their lending rates.
  • Private sector banks and public sector banks trail in rate transmission during the current easing cycle.
  • The RBI bulletin highlights varying degrees of monetary policy transmission across bank groups.

Foreign banks operating in India have demonstrated the highest degree of monetary policy transmission in the current rate easing cycle, according to the Reserve Bank of India's latest bulletin. The data suggests that foreign lenders are passing on the central bank's repo rate cuts to borrowers more aggressively than their domestic peers.

The bulletin, released on Wednesday, examined how effectively different categories of banks have transmitted the RBI's policy rate reductions to their lending rates since the easing cycle began. Foreign banks topped the list, followed by private sector banks and then public sector banks.

What the data shows

The RBI bulletin's analysis covers the period from the start of the current easing cycle, which began when the central bank first cut the repo rate in early 2024. Foreign banks have reduced their weighted average lending rates by a larger margin compared to other bank groups.

Private sector banks have also transmitted rate cuts, but at a slower pace. Public sector banks lag behind both foreign and private lenders in passing on the benefits to customers.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact percentage points of transmission for each bank group. The bulletin did not name specific foreign banks that performed best.

Why transmission varies

Differences in transmission are tied to each bank group's funding mix and lending portfolio. Foreign banks typically rely more on wholesale funding and have a higher share of loans linked to external benchmarks like the repo rate.

Public sector banks, by contrast, have a larger share of deposits linked to older, stickier rates and a higher proportion of loans tied to the marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR), which adjusts more slowly. This structural gap explains the slower pass-through.

The RBI has repeatedly urged banks to improve transmission so that policy rate cuts reach households and businesses faster. Better transmission supports economic growth by lowering borrowing costs for home loans, auto loans, and working capital.

Broader implications

The findings come at a time when the RBI is closely monitoring how its monetary policy actions affect the real economy. The central bank has cut the repo rate by a cumulative 100 basis points over the past year to spur growth.

If transmission remains uneven, the effectiveness of further rate cuts could be blunted. Borrowers with loans from public sector banks may not feel the full benefit of RBI's easing, while those with foreign banks enjoy lower EMIs sooner.

Industry watchers say the gap in transmission could influence where customers choose to borrow. It may also push the RBI to consider more structural measures to align lending rates across the banking system.

The RBI bulletin did not indicate any immediate policy action on this front. The central bank is expected to release its next monetary policy review in August, where the issue of transmission is likely to be discussed.

For now, foreign banks appear to be the most responsive to RBI's signals, while public sector banks have work to do in bridging the transmission gap.
