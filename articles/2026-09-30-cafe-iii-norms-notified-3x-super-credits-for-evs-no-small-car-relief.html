CAFE III Norms Notified: 3x Super-Credits for EVs, No Small Car Relief

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-30 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ministry notifies stringent Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) III norms for passenger vehicles.
  • Automakers gain 3x regulatory credit for pure battery electric vehicles to offset fleet emissions.
  • Government retains the unadjusted weight-based formula, offering no special relief or lower targets for entry-level small cars.

Stricter Fleet Targets Shake Up Automaker Compliance

The Ministry of Power, along with the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), has officially notified the Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) Phase III standards for passenger vehicles. The revised regulatory regime enforces aggressive fleet-wide carbon emission cuts, pushing original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) to overhaul their powertrain strategies. Under CAFE III, automakers must lower their overall corporate fleet average CO2 emissions significantly compared to CAFE II thresholds.

To accelerate zero-emission adoption, the government introduced a decisive multiplier: battery electric vehicles (BEVs) will earn carmakers 3x super-credits toward fleet compliance. This framework allows manufacturers with robust EV portfolios to offset higher-polluting internal combustion engine (ICE) models, creating a strong commercial incentive to expand electric product lineups across price segments.

Weight-Based Formula Retained Without Small-Car Concessions

Automakers heavily reliant on entry-level, lightweight petrol hatchbacks lobbied extensively for dedicated exemptions or relaxed slopes in the compliance curve. The notified norms firmly reject these concessions. The BEE preserved the standard weight-based target formula, meaning lighter vehicles do not receive special mathematical adjustments.

Because the baseline formula links allowable fuel consumption directly to vehicle kerb weight, small cars must achieve exceptionally low absolute fuel consumption figures. Automakers producing entry-level vehicles face immediate engineering pressure to adopt idle start-stop systems, mild-hybrid setups, or alternate fuels like CNG to meet regulatory ceilings without pushing retail price tags out of reach for budget-conscious Indian car buyers.

Market Impact and Practical Buyer Implications

The structural tilt toward high-credit green powertrains will reshape showroom floors over the next several years. Car buyers can anticipate noticeable changes in vehicle portfolios, pricing tiers, and powertrain availability.

  • Aggressive EV Model Rollouts: Automakers will accelerate affordable EV launches priced between ₹10 lakh and ₹18 lakh to generate the 3x credits needed to shield their high-margin, ICE-powered SUVs from punitive compliance penalties.
  • Small Car Price Pressures: Without structural regulatory relief, sub-₹6 lakh petrol hatchbacks require costlier powertrain upgrades—such as integrated starter generators (ISG) or dual-fuel CNG kits—inevitably increasing entry-level on-road pricing.
  • Push for Strong Hybrids: Fleet managers will lean on strong hybrid electric vehicles (SHEVs) to cut average CO2 emissions immediately, balancing customer range concerns while meeting compliance goals.
  • Phasing Out Inefficient Naturally Aspirated Engines: Older multi-point fuel injection (MPFI) engines face elimination, replaced by direct-injection turbocharged petrol and micro-hybrid options.

The Regulatory Roadmap Ahead

Automakers failing to meet notified fleet limits face severe financial penalties under the Energy Conservation Act, which levies steep per-car fines on non-compliant volume. With CAFE III establishing clear rules, carmakers operating in India must choose between fast-tracking EV investments or absorbing hefty fiscal penalties on fossil-fuel vehicle sales.
