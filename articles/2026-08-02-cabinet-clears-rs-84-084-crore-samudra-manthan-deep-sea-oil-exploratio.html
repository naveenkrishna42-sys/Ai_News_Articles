Cabinet clears Rs 84,084 crore 'Samudra Manthan' deep-sea oil exploration plan

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cabinet approves National Offshore Exploration Scheme with ₹84,084 crore outlay
  • Scheme funds 60 deep-sea wells at ₹650 crore per well
  • Aims to reduce India's oil import dependence through domestic production

Cabinet nod for deep-sea push

The Union Cabinet has approved 'Samudra Manthan', a national offshore exploration scheme with a total outlay of ₹84,084 crore. The ambitious programme is designed to ramp up domestic crude production from India's deep-sea basins, a move that could reshape the country's energy import calculus.

The scheme, cleared this week, will fund drilling of 60 deep-sea wells at an estimated cost of ₹650 crore per well. Officials familiar with the matter said the investment is aimed at unlocking hydrocarbon reserves in India's exclusive economic zone, which has so far remained largely unexplored.

Why the ocean floor matters

India currently imports over 85 per cent of its crude oil requirements, leaving the economy vulnerable to global price shocks and supply disruptions. The government's push to drill deeper into the seabed is part of a broader strategy to raise domestic output and reduce this dependence.

Deep-sea exploration is technically complex and capital-intensive, which has historically deterred private investment. The Samudra Manthan scheme addresses this by providing government-funded risk capital, allowing state-owned and private firms to take on high-cost, high-reward projects.

How the scheme works

Under the programme, the government will bear the cost of drilling exploratory wells in ultra-deep water, typically at depths exceeding 200 metres. The data gathered from these wells will help map India's offshore hydrocarbon potential and attract future investment.

Each well is expected to cost around ₹650 crore, reflecting the advanced technology and specialised vessels required for deep-sea operations. The scheme will be implemented in phases over the next several years, with drilling planned across multiple sedimentary basins.

Potential impact on energy security

If successful, the scheme could significantly boost India's domestic crude production, which currently stands at about 2.5 million barrels per day against a demand of over 5 million barrels. Even a modest increase in output would translate into substantial savings on import bills.

Energy analysts note that deep-sea discoveries typically take 5-7 years from exploration to first production. The government's upfront investment is therefore a long-term bet, with returns expected only in the next decade.

Challenges and risks

Deep-sea drilling carries inherent risks, including technical failures, harsh weather conditions, and cost overruns. The government has not yet disclosed contingency plans for wells that do not yield commercially viable reserves.

Environmental concerns also remain, as offshore drilling can impact marine ecosystems. Officials have said that exploration will follow all regulatory norms, though specific environmental safeguards under the scheme have not been detailed.

The move has drawn comparisons to 'Drill, Baby, Drill' campaigns in other oil-producing nations, though the Indian approach is more measured, focusing on state-funded exploration rather than immediate production targets.

What to watch next

The first tranche of drilling is expected to begin in the next fiscal year, with tenders for specialised rigs and vessels likely to be floated soon. Industry watchers will be keen to see how quickly the government can execute the scheme and whether initial results justify the massive outlay.

For now, Samudra Manthan remains a bold bet on India's undersea potential — one that could either ease the country's import burden or become a costly lesson in the limits of deep-sea exploration.
