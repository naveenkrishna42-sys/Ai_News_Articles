Sensex falls 388 pts, Nifty at 24,471 as crude oil prices surge

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex closed 388 points lower, Nifty settled at 24,471
  • Rising crude oil prices triggered selling across sectors
  • Investors await US inflation data and oil price trajectory

Indian equity benchmarks ended sharply lower on Tuesday, August 11, as surging crude oil prices rattled investor sentiment. The BSE Sensex dropped 388 points, while the NSE Nifty slipped to 24,471, erasing early gains and marking a broad-based sell-off.

The decline came amid fears that higher oil prices could stoke inflation, widen the current account deficit, and put pressure on the rupee. Brent crude hovered near elevated levels, raising concerns about input costs for Indian companies and their profit margins.

Crude oil worries weigh on Dalal Street

Oil prices have been climbing steadily over the past few sessions, driven by supply concerns and geopolitical tensions. For India, which imports over 80 per cent of its crude requirement, every dollar increase in oil prices has a direct impact on the economy.

Rising crude typically translates into higher fuel costs, which can feed into broader inflation. This, in turn, could prompt the Reserve Bank of India to keep interest rates elevated for longer, a scenario that markets are not comfortable with. The fear of sustained high rates weighed on rate-sensitive sectors such as banking, realty, and auto.

Sectoral indices bleed; IT and banks lead losses

Most sectoral indices on the National Stock Exchange ended in the red. Financials, information technology, and energy stocks were the top drags. Heavyweights like Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, and Infosys contributed significantly to the index decline.

Midcap and smallcap stocks also came under pressure, with the broader market mirroring the weakness in large caps. Analysts said the mood was cautious ahead of key US inflation data, which could influence global central bank policy and capital flows into emerging markets like India.

What is driving the sell-off?

Apart from crude oil, concerns over global economic growth and tight monetary conditions have kept investors on edge. The US Federal Reserve's next move remains a key watch, and any surprise in inflation numbers could trigger further volatility.

Domestic institutional investors were net sellers, while foreign investors showed mixed activity. Market participants said the absence of strong positive triggers, coupled with expensive valuations, made the market vulnerable to profit-booking at higher levels.

Outlook: Volatility likely to persist

With crude oil prices remaining a key overhang, analysts expect the market to stay volatile in the near term. The upcoming US inflation print and domestic macroeconomic data are likely to guide direction. A sustained rise in oil prices could keep the upside capped for the indices.

Investors are advised to keep a close watch on global cues, the rupee's movement, and oil price action. Any easing in crude prices or a cooler inflation reading could provide some respite to the market in the coming sessions.
