Michael Burry Exits Alibaba, Rotates to JD.com: What the Smart Money Is Doing

📅 2026-08-25 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Michael Burry has reportedly exited his entire Alibaba position
  • Alibaba raised $10.2 billion via a landmark Hong Kong share placement
  • The capital raise will fund Alibaba's aggressive AI expansion push
  • Burry appears to be rotating into JD.com as a potentially cleaner China e-commerce play
  • The moves reflect growing investor skepticism about Alibaba's valuation dilution

Burry's Calculated Exit from Alibaba

Michael Burry, the activist investor made famous for his prescient bet against subprime mortgages in 'The Big Short', has liquidated his entire stake in Alibaba Group. The move marks a decisive break from one of China's most prominent technology conglomerates at a time when the company is undergoing significant structural changes.

The exit comes just weeks after Alibaba completed a $10.2 billion share placement—the largest such offering in Hong Kong's recent history. The capital raise was explicitly earmarked to accelerate the company's artificial intelligence capabilities, signaling an aggressive pivot toward next-generation technology infrastructure.

Why Alibaba Needed to Dilute

Alibaba's decision to tap equity markets for such a massive sum wasn't arbitrary. The company faces intensifying competition in core e-commerce while simultaneously trying to establish itself as a serious contender in the AI race against domestic rivals like Baidu and ByteDance. Running both plays simultaneously requires enormous capital reserves.

Critics argue the share placement represents a significant dilution of existing shareholder value. When a company issues new shares at scale, it reduces the ownership percentage—and often the voting power—of current investors. For long-term holders like Burry, this structural shift can fundamentally alter the investment thesis.

Alibaba's launch of the Wan3.0 AI video model, announced alongside the share sale, illustrates the company's ambition. But ambition alone doesn't guarantee returns, and investors are increasingly questioning whether Alibaba can execute on its AI promises while defending its e-commerce market share.

The Rotation to JD.com: Substance or Speculation?

If Burry is indeed rotating into JD.com, the logic isn't immediately obvious to casual observers. JD.com, Alibaba's primary e-commerce competitor, faces many of the same structural challenges: regulatory scrutiny, consumer spending pressures, and the need to invest in AI-driven logistics and personalization.

However, some analysts suggest JD.com may represent a cleaner play on Chinese consumer recovery. Unlike Alibaba's sprawling ecosystem—which includes cloud computing, entertainment, and logistics—JD.com's business model remains more concentrated on direct retail and fulfillment. That focus could translate to clearer earnings visibility.

JD.com has also maintained a more conservative capital allocation strategy, avoiding the aggressive diversification that sometimes complicates Alibaba's narrative. For investors who view simplicity as a virtue, JD.com's story may be easier to model and value.

What This Signals for China Tech Sentiment

Burry's move shouldn't be viewed in isolation. It reflects a broader recalibration among institutional investors regarding their China tech allocations. After years of regulatory uncertainty, slowing growth, and geopolitical tensions, many funds have reduced their exposure significantly.

When a high-profile investor like Burry makes a decisive rotation, it sends a signal to the market: the risk-reward calculus for Alibaba has shifted. Whether that assessment is correct remains to be seen, but the move underscores that even Alibaba's scale and dominance no longer guarantee institutional loyalty.

The Bottom Line for Investors

Burry's exit from Alibaba and apparent move toward JD.com represents a bet on focus over scale. It's a reminder that in China's tech sector, bigger isn't always better—and that capital allocation decisions can reshape investment theses overnight.

  • Alibaba's Dilution Problem: The $10.2 billion share placement significantly impacts existing shareholders' stakes and signals aggressive capital needs.
  • AI Pivot Uncertainty: Alibaba's push into artificial intelligence is ambitious but faces stiff competition from better-positioned rivals.
  • JD.com's Cleaner Narrative: Concentrated business model may appeal to investors seeking simpler, more predictable China exposure.
  • Sentiment Shift: Burry's move reflects growing institutional skepticism toward sprawling Chinese tech conglomerates.
  • Execution Risk: Both companies face meaningful challenges, but JD.com's reduced capital requirements could prove advantageous in uncertain markets.
