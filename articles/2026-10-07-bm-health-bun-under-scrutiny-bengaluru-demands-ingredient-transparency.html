BM Health Bun Under Scrutiny: Bengaluru Demands Ingredient Transparency

  • Preservatives such as calcium propionate are present at levels that exceed typical daily intake recommendations for sensitive individuals.
  • Consumer advocacy groups call for stricter labeling norms and quicker response from food safety authorities in Bengaluru.

Overview of the BM Health Bun Controversy

In early October 2026, Bengaluru residents raised concerns after social media posts highlighted possible health risks associated with a widely sold bun from the BM Health brand. The posts questioned the ingredient list, prompting local consumer forums to request an independent analysis. The Bangalore Mirror subsequently launched an investigation to verify claims and assess public health implications.

Laboratory Findings: What the Tests Revealed

Samples were sent to a FSSAI‑accredited lab in Bengaluru for proximate analysis and additive screening. Results indicated that each 100 g serving of the bun contained approximately 68 g of carbohydrates, of which 42 g were sugars, and only 6 g of dietary fiber. The fat content stood at 9 g, with saturated fats contributing 2.5 g.

Nutritional Breakdown

The bun’s refined wheat flour made up roughly 78 % of the total weight, far exceeding the 55 % threshold recommended for whole‑grain alternatives. Added sugars, listed as sucrose and glucose syrup, accounted for nearly 62 % of the total carbohydrate load. Preservative testing detected calcium propionate at 0.25 % w/w, a level that approaches the acceptable daily intake limit for children when consumed in multiple servings.

Health Implications for Consumers

High refined flour and sugar intake are linked to rapid glucose spikes, which may exacerbate insulin resistance in vulnerable populations. Regular consumption of such bakery items can contribute to weight gain and elevated triglyceride levels, especially when paired with a sedentary lifestyle. The presence of calcium propionate, while generally recognized as safe, may trigger mild gastrointestinal discomfort in individuals with sensitivities to propionates.

Regulatory Response and Industry Practices

Following the BBB’s inquiry, the Karnataka Food Safety Commissioner issued a notice to BM Health requesting clarification on its labeling practices and formulation details. The authority emphasized that any product claiming health benefits must substantiate those claims with verifiable nutritional data. BM Health responded by stating it would review its recipe and consider offering a whole‑grain variant.

What Bengaluru Residents Can Do

Consumers are advised to scrutinize ingredient lists, prioritize products with higher fiber and lower added sugars, and report any adverse reactions to the local food safety office. Community groups have also started a petition urging mandatory front‑of‑pack labeling for sugar and refined flour content in baked goods sold across the city.

Key Takeaways for Buyers

  • The BM Health bun delivers a high sugar and refined flour load, offering limited nutritional benefit.
  • Preservative levels, while within legal limits, may affect sensitive individuals with frequent consumption.
  • Increased transparency and reformulation efforts are necessary to align the product with public health guidelines.
