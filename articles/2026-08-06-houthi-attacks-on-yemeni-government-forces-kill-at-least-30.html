Houthi attacks on Yemeni government forces kill at least 30

📅 2026-08-06 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Houthi attacks on Yemeni government forces have killed at least 30 people.
  • Houthis claim the strikes were a response to a Saudi military buildup.
  • Reports suggest an Iran-Oman deal on the Strait of Hormuz is close.
  • The attacks come amid heightened regional tensions and ongoing conflict.

Deadly Strikes Hit Government Positions

At least 30 Yemeni government forces have been killed in a series of Houthi attacks, according to reports. The strikes, which occurred on Thursday, targeted multiple military positions across the country. Casualty figures remain unconfirmed by independent sources, and officials have not yet released a detailed breakdown.

The Houthi movement, which controls much of northern Yemen, claimed responsibility for the assaults. In a statement, the group said the attacks were a direct response to what it described as a 'Saudi military buildup' near its territory. Saudi Arabia has led a coalition backing the internationally recognised Yemeni government since 2015.

Escalation Amid Diplomatic Moves

The latest violence comes as reports emerge that a deal between Iran and Oman over the Strait of Hormuz is close to being finalised. The strait, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments, has been a flashpoint in regional tensions. Diplomatic sources suggest that Omani mediators have been shuttling between Tehran and other capitals to secure an agreement.

Iran has long been accused of supplying weapons and logistical support to the Houthis, a claim Tehran denies. The proximity of the reported Hormuz deal to the Houthi escalation raises questions about the broader regional calculus. Analysts note that the Houthis have often acted in tandem with Iranian interests, though the group insists its operations are independent.

Regional Reactions and Fallout

The attacks have drawn sharp condemnation from Yemeni government officials, who have called for international intervention. Saudi Arabia has not yet issued an official statement on the reported military buildup, but state media have been closely following the developments. The United Nations has urged restraint, warning that further escalation could derail fragile peace efforts.

On the ground, medics and local residents describe chaotic scenes at the attack sites. Hospitals in affected areas are struggling to cope with the influx of casualties, and emergency supplies are reportedly running low. The death toll could rise, as rescue teams continue to search through rubble.

Context of a Long-Running War

Yemen's civil war has been raging for over a decade, leaving hundreds of thousands dead and millions displaced. The Houthis have frequently targeted government and coalition positions, while Saudi-led airstrikes have hammered Houthi-held areas. Despite multiple ceasefire attempts, a lasting political settlement remains elusive.

Thursday's attacks mark one of the deadliest single incidents in recent months. Observers point to a pattern of escalation whenever diplomatic initiatives appear to gain traction. The timing, coinciding with the Hormuz deal reports, suggests that military and diplomatic tracks are increasingly intertwined.

What to Watch Next

All eyes will now be on whether the reported Iran-Oman deal materialises and whether it influences Houthi behaviour. The Yemeni government has called for an emergency Security Council session, though no date has been announced. For now, the risk of further retaliatory strikes remains high, and civilians continue to bear the heaviest cost.
