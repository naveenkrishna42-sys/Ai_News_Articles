Handling Lionel Messi will be 'huge challenge' for Spain, admits Mikel Merino

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mikel Merino calls stopping Lionel Messi a 'huge challenge' for Spain in World Cup final
  • Andres Iniesta says 'shutting down Messi is impossible', advises Spain on approach
  • Merino emphasises focus on being a good human first, then a good footballer

Spain midfielder Mikel Merino has admitted that handling Lionel Messi will be a 'huge challenge' when his side faces Argentina in the FIFA World Cup final. The 2026 showpiece, set to be played in the United States, pits La Roja against the reigning champions in what promises to be a clash of styles.

Merino, speaking ahead of the final, did not mince words. 'Messi is the greatest to have ever played the game. You don't stop him. You just try to make his life difficult,' he said.

Iniesta: 'Shutting down Messi is impossible'

Spain legend Andres Iniesta, who played alongside Messi at Barcelona for years, echoed Merino's sentiment. 'Shutting down Messi is impossible. You cannot focus on one player. Spain must control the game as a team,' Iniesta told The Times of India.

Iniesta added that Spain's best bet is to deny Argentina possession and cut off supply lines to Messi. 'If you give him time on the ball, he will punish you. That is what every team has learned the hard way.'

Merino: 'Focus on being a good human first'

In a separate interview with The Guardian, Merino revealed his personal philosophy ahead of the biggest match of his career. 'The focus is on being a good human first, then a good footballer,' he said. 'This is just a game. Yes, it is the World Cup final. But who you are as a person matters more.'

The 30-year-old midfielder, who has been a key figure for Spain throughout the tournament, said the team is calm. 'We have a plan. We know what we want to do. But at the end of the day, football is played by humans, not robots.'

Every plan to stop Messi has failed

History is not on Spain's side. As The Indian Express noted, 'Every plan to stop Messi has failed. Spain has one more shot.' The Argentine captain has been in sublime form, single-handedly dragging his team past several opponents.

Mexico, Poland, and England all tried different strategies. Man-marking, zonal defence, and even tactical fouling. None worked. Messi has scored in every knockout game so far, including a hat-trick in the semi-final against Brazil.

Spain's defence, led by Aymeric Laporte and Pau Torres, will have to be at its absolute best. But as Al Jazeera pointed out, 'Tackling Messi is a huge challenge for Spain in the World Cup final.'

The final is expected to be a tactical battle. Spain will likely dominate possession, while Argentina will look to hit on the counter. The key question: can Spain's midfield, featuring Merino and Rodri, control the tempo and keep Messi quiet?

All eyes will be on the Estadio Metropolitano in New Jersey. The world waits to see if Spain can finally solve the Messi puzzle, or if the legend adds another chapter to his story.
