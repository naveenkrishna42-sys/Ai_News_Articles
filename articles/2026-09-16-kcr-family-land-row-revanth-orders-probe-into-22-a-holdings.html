The Assembly Flashpoint: What Revanth Reddy Actually Announced

On 16 September 2026, Telangana Chief Minister Revanth Reddy used the Assembly floor to order a probe into alleged land irregularities involving KCR's farmhouse and properties linked to the BRS first family. The trigger was a pointed question: how much land does the KCR family actually hold? The government's answer, delivered alongside the debate, referenced a prohibited properties figure of 98.31 lakh acres across the state under the contentious Section 22-A regime.

For property buyers, this is not merely political theatre. Section 22-A of the Registration Act allows the state to bar registration of specified lands, and the size of that prohibited list determines how easily—or how painfully—title transfers move in Telangana.

Understanding Section 22-A and the 98.31 Lakh Acre Number

Section 22-A empowers the government to prohibit registration of documents on lands that are government-assigned, endowment, or otherwise disputed. The 98.31 lakh acre figure cited in the House represents the cumulative prohibited list, a number that has ballooned over successive governments. When a parcel sits on this list, sub-registrars simply refuse registration—no caveat, no conditional acceptance.

BRS working president KTR has demanded a judicial probe into the 22-A row, arguing the list is being weaponised. The government counters that the list protects public land from encroachment. Both claims carry consequences for anyone holding a sale agreement on the city's fringe.

Why Buyers in Hyderabad's Growth Corridors Should Care

The most acute impact lands on the peri-urban belt—Shamirpet, Medchal, Sangareddy, Yadadri, and the corridor toward the proposed future city. These are exactly the areas where farmhouse conversions, layout approvals, and agricultural-to-residential mutations occur, and where 22-A entries are most common.

A buyer who pays 30-40% of consideration as advance on a plot that later surfaces on the prohibited list faces a limbo that can run into years. Registration is blocked, litigation is expensive, and resale becomes near-impossible. The practical benchmark: always obtain a 22-A status certificate and an encumbrance certificate covering 30 years before releasing any token amount.

Valuation and Pricing Context

Land within 40-50 km of Hyderabad's Outer Ring Road currently trades between ₹15 lakh and ₹60 lakh per acre depending on access and approval status. A clouded 22-A title can knock 40-60% off realisable value and freeze transactions entirely. That discount is the market's way of pricing legal risk.

Pros, Cons and Real-World Takeaways

  • Pro: A formal probe creates a paper trail, which ultimately helps buyers distinguish clean titles from disputed ones.
  • Pro: Renewed scrutiny of the 22-A list may force periodic review and de-notification of wrongly included parcels.
  • Con: Political uncertainty slows registrations in affected mandals, delaying genuine transactions.
  • Con: Buyers with pending agreements in the Shamirpet-Medchal belt face extended timelines and possible price renegotiation.
  • Action point: Insist on a Dharani portal verification, a 22-A certificate, and a title search before payment.

What Happens Next

The government has ordered a probe, not filed a chargesheet—no findings exist yet, and no court has ruled on the family's holdings. KTR's demand for a judicial probe signals the fight will move from the Assembly to either a commission or the High Court. Until then, treat every headline number as an allegation under investigation, not a verdict.

For buyers, the signal is clear: in Telangana's fringe markets, verification is not paperwork—it is the asset. Budget for a lawyer's title opinion, verify 22-A status independently, and never rely on a broker's assurance about government land. The political row will resolve in time; a bad title will not.
