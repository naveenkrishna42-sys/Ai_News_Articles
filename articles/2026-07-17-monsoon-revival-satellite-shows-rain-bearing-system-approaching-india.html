Monsoon Revival: Satellite Shows Rain-Bearing System Approaching India

📅 2026-07-17 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Satellite imagery captures a low-pressure system over the Bay of Bengal, likely to intensify.
  • IMD issues heavy rain alerts for Odisha and over 15 states; heatwave continues in southern India.
  • North India faces hot and humid conditions as the monsoon revival brings widespread rainfall.

The monsoon, which had shown signs of a lull, is now gearing up for a strong revival. Satellite images have captured a well-defined low-pressure area brewing over the Bay of Bengal. This system, packed with moisture, is expected to intensify and bring heavy rainfall to several Indian states in the coming days.

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has already sounded an alert. Odisha is likely to be the first to feel the impact, with the system expected to drench the state. But the rain will not stop there. Forecasts indicate that over 15 states across central, eastern, and northern India will receive a significant soaking.

Heavy Rain Alert for Multiple States

The IMD has warned of heavy to very heavy rainfall in Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, and parts of Maharashtra. The system, currently a low-pressure area, is predicted to intensify into a depression over the next 24 to 48 hours. This will further enhance the rainfall activity.

States like West Bengal, Jharkhand, and Bihar are also on alert. The weather office has advised fishermen along the Odisha and West Bengal coasts not to venture into the sea. The rough conditions are likely to persist until the system moves inland.

Heatwave and Humidity: A Tale of Two Indias

While the monsoon gears up for a comeback, southern India continues to bake. A heatwave is gripping parts of Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu. Temperatures in these regions remain several notches above normal, offering little respite from the scorching sun.

Meanwhile, North India is experiencing a different kind of discomfort. Hot and humid conditions persist across the plains. The moisture-laden air, a precursor to the rains, is making the heat feel more oppressive. Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, and Punjab are all sweltering under this sticky blanket.

What Travellers Need to Know

For those planning travel, the next few days could be challenging. The IMD has issued a red alert for parts of Odisha and Chhattisgarh. Heavy downpours could lead to waterlogging and disruption of road and rail traffic. Travellers are advised to check forecasts before heading out.

The system is also expected to bring relief to regions that have been rain-deficient so far. The monsoon trough, which had shifted northwards, is now likely to move south again. This will ensure a more widespread distribution of rainfall across the country.

The Lopar Effect in Odisha

Interestingly, Odisha has already received a preview of what is to come. The state was lashed by what locals call the 'Lopar' rains — intense, short-duration showers that have already saturated the soil. With the new system approaching, the risk of flooding in low-lying areas has increased.

District administrations in Odisha have been put on standby. The state government has directed officials to remain in the field and ensure that drainage systems are clear. Emergency response teams have been mobilised in vulnerable districts like Balasore, Bhadrak, and Jagatsinghpur.

The satellite imagery showing this system is a clear sign that the monsoon is far from over. It is reloading for a fresh spell of intense activity. The next 72 hours will be crucial as the system moves closer to the coast and begins its journey inland. Authorities are watching closely, and residents in the alert zones should stay prepared for rapidly changing weather conditions.
