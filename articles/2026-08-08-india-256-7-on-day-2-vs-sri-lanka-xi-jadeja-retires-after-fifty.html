India 256/7 on Day 2 vs Sri Lanka XI; Jadeja retires after fifty

  • India ended Day 2 at 256/7 in the warm-up match against Sri Lanka XI.
  • Ravindra Jadeja retired after scoring a half-century.
  • Manav Suthar is nearing a fifty, steadying the innings after early wickets.
  • Sri Lanka XI bowlers had frustrated India's seamers on Day 1.

Jadeja's steady hand

Ravindra Jadeja walked out with the kind of calm that has defined his career, and by the time he walked back, India had a foothold in this warm-up match. The all-rounder retired after reaching his fifty, a measured knock that lifted India to 256/7 on Day 2 against Sri Lanka XI in the three-day tour game.

His departure, though voluntary, left the lower middle order with work to do. The scoreboard, at that stage, suggested a contest that was far from one-sided.

Early blows, then a recovery

The day had started with India in a spot of bother. Dhruv Jurel and Rishabh Pant fell cheaply, both unable to convert their starts. The Sri Lanka XI bowlers, who had toiled gamely on the opening day, found early reward and pressed home the advantage.

But Jadeja, along with young left-arm spinner Manav Suthar, stemmed the flow. Suthar, closing in on his own fifty, has looked composed, rotating the strike and punishing the loose ball. The pair's partnership has given India a semblance of control after the top-order wobble.

Seamers made to work

Day 1 had set the tone. Sri Lanka XI's batters made India's seamers toil, frustrating them with patient defence and timely aggression. The pitch, by all accounts, has offered little for the quicker bowlers, and the Indian attack had to fall back on discipline rather than venom.

That patience from the hosts carried into the second morning. Their bowlers kept a tight line, and the early wickets were earned rather than gifted. India's response, through Jadeja and Suthar, has been equally measured.

What this means for the Test build-up

This is, after all, a warm-up. But for a side fine-tuning combinations ahead of the Test series, these sessions carry weight. Jadeja's form with the bat will reassure the think-tank, and Suthar's application with the bat only adds to his credentials as a bowling all-rounder.

The lower order, though, remains a concern. The fall of Jurel and Pant in quick succession was a reminder that the middle order cannot afford lapses, even in a practice game. India will want to push past 300 and give their bowlers a decent spell before declaring, should the opportunity arise.

The road ahead

Day 3 will decide how much mileage India can extract from this innings. The pitch is expected to wear, and spin could come into play earlier than either side anticipates. For now, Suthar's unbeaten vigil and the tail's resistance will determine the final total.

All eyes will be on how quickly India choose to wrap up their innings and how the Sri Lanka XI respond in their second outing. The match may be a warm-up, but the lessons from it could shape selections for the Test opener.
