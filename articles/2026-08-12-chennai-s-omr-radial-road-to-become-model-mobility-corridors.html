Chennai's OMR, Radial Road to become model mobility corridors

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • OMR and Radial Road to be developed as model urban mobility corridors
  • Focus on pedestrian safety, cycling infrastructure and public transport integration
  • Project aims to reduce congestion and promote sustainable commuting

Chennai's Key Arteries Get a Mobility Makeover

The Old Mahabalipuram Road (OMR) and the Radial Road, two of Chennai's busiest stretches, are set to be transformed into model urban mobility corridors. The initiative, announced by state authorities, aims to overhaul the way thousands of daily commuters travel along these congested routes.

The project is part of a broader push to make Chennai's transport network safer, smarter, and more sustainable. Officials said the corridors will prioritise pedestrians, cyclists, and public transport users over private vehicles, a significant shift from the current car-centric design.

What the Upgrade Includes

The planned upgrades include wider footpaths, dedicated cycling tracks, and improved street lighting. Bus stops and metro stations along the corridors will be better integrated, with last-mile connectivity options such as shared autos and e-rickshaws.

Traffic signals will be synchronised to reduce waiting times, and smart sensors will monitor congestion in real time. The authorities have also proposed green spaces and tree cover to make the stretches more walkable and pleasant.

Why These Corridors Matter

OMR is the city's IT corridor, hosting hundreds of tech parks and employing lakhs of professionals. Radial Road, which connects the city to the southern suburbs, is equally vital for daily commuters. Together, they handle a significant share of Chennai's vehicular traffic.

For years, these roads have been plagued by bottlenecks, especially during peak hours. The new model corridor plan is expected to ease congestion and cut travel time, though officials have not yet shared specific timelines or cost estimates.

Public Transport at the Core

A key aspect of the plan is to make public transport the backbone of these corridors. Dedicated bus lanes are being considered, along with better signage and real-time arrival displays at stops. The idea is to encourage more people to leave their cars at home.

Chennai's Metropolitan Transport Corporation (MTC) and Chennai Metro Rail are likely to play central roles in the execution. However, detailed designs and feasibility studies are still underway, and the authorities have invited public feedback before finalising the blueprint.

Challenges Ahead

Executing such a large-scale transformation on active roads will not be easy. Utility shifting, land acquisition, and minimising disruption to daily traffic are major hurdles. Past infrastructure projects in Chennai have faced delays due to these very issues.

Environmental clearances and coordination between multiple agencies will also be critical. The authorities say they are committed to completing the project with minimal inconvenience, but they have not announced a firm deadline.

As Chennai grows, the success of these model corridors could set a template for other Indian cities grappling with urban mobility. Citizens and commuters alike will be watching closely to see if the vision translates into reality on the ground.
