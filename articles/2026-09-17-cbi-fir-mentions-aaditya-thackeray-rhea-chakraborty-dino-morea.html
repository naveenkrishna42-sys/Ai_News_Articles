CBI FIR Mentions Aaditya Thackeray, Rhea Chakraborty, Dino Morea

Background of the Disha Salian Case

Disha Salian, a talent manager in the Mumbai film industry, died on June 8, 2020 after falling from a building in Malad. The Mumbai Police initially registered the death as an accidental fall and later concluded it was a suicide after forensic and statements review. The case attracted public attention due to its temporal proximity to the death of actor Sushant Singh Rajput in June 2020, leading to widespread speculation and multiple petitions for a fresh probe.

Over the ensuing years, several petitions were filed in courts demanding a CBI investigation, citing alleged links to the Sushant Singh Rajput case and claims of foul play. The Bombay Court and later the Supreme Court examined these petitions, eventually directing the CBI to register a preliminary inquiry in 2023. The inquiry was later converted into a formal FIR after the agency deemed there was sufficient material to warrant a criminal investigation.

Details of the CBI FIR and Named Individuals

The FIR, filed under sections of the Indian Penal Code related to abetment of suicide and criminal conspiracy, explicitly mentions Aaditya Thackeray, Rhea Chakraborty and Dino Morea. The agency states that these names appear based on complaints received from private individuals and NGOs alleging that the three persons had interactions with Disha Salian prior to her death and may have influenced her mental state.

It is important to clarify that an FIR mentioning a name does not constitute an accusation or a charge. Under criminal procedure, the FIR serves as the first information report that triggers investigation; the named persons are treated as persons of interest until evidence either substantiates or refutes the allegations. All three have publicly denied any involvement, with Aaditya Thackeray calling the mention a "character assassination" and Rhea Chakraborty stating she never met Disha Salian.

Legal and Political Implications

Legal analysts point out that the inclusion of high‑profile names in an FIR raises the standard of evidence required for the CBI to proceed. The agency must now demonstrate a credible link between the alleged actions of the named individuals and the death, satisfying both the investigative and judicial thresholds. Any misstep could lead to accusations of misuse of prosecutorial power, prompting judicial oversight.

Politically, the mention has intensified the existing rivalry between the Shiv Sena‑led Maharashtra government and the opposition BJP. Aaditya Thackeray, a senior Shiv Sena leader and Maharashtra cabinet minister, has framed the FIR as a politically motivated move. Conversely, BJP leaders have argued that the CBI is merely following due process on complaints submitted by citizens. The exchange has played out in press statements, social media and legislative debates, underscoring how criminal investigations can become intertwined with electoral narratives.

Pros and Cons of the CBI‑Led Investigation

  • Pros: Central agency brings resources, forensic expertise and perceived impartiality to a case that has attracted intense public scrutiny.
  • Cons: Prolonged investigation may exacerbate speculation; naming individuals without charges can affect reputations and raise concerns about trial by media.
  • Key Insight: The outcome will likely hinge on forensic evidence and witness testimonies rather than the prominence of the names mentioned.
