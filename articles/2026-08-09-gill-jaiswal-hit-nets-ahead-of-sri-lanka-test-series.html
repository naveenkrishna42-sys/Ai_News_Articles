Gill, Jaiswal hit nets ahead of Sri Lanka Test series

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • India beat Sri Lanka XI by six wickets in a warm-up match in Colombo.
  • Gill and Jaiswal got batting practice ahead of the Test series.
  • Mohammed Siraj smashed three sixes in the final over to seal the win.

India's Test specialists got down to business in Colombo on Sunday, with Shubman Gill and Yashasvi Jaiswal spending lengthy sessions in the nets a day after the team's six-wicket warm-up victory over a Sri Lanka XI. The pair are expected to open the batting when the two-Test series begins later this week.

The warm-up fixture, played at the P Sara Oval, served its purpose as India fine-tuned their preparations on spin-friendly tracks. Both Gill and Jaiswal spent time against throwdowns as well as facing the spinners in the middle, working on footwork and sweep shots, according to team sources.

Siraj's late fireworks seal the win

The match itself was a low-key affair, but Mohammed Siraj provided the entertainment with a blazing final over. Chasing a modest target, India needed a quick finish, and Siraj obliged by smashing three consecutive sixes to complete the chase with six wickets in hand.

The fast bowler's knock, brief as it was, drew smiles in the dressing room. It also served as a reminder of the lower-order hitting that India have often relied on in overseas conditions.

Batting practice proves useful

Jaiswal, who has been in prolific form in recent months, looked solid in the middle, while Gill, returning to the side after a break, spent considerable time at the crease. Both batsmen faced a mix of pace and spin, with the Sri Lanka XI fielding a side packed with fringe Test players.

The management's decision to give the top order a proper workout rather than treat the game as a mere formality was evident. Gill and Jaiswal's partnership in the middle will be key to India's fortunes on pitches that are likely to take turn from day one.

Spin threat looms large

Sri Lanka's spinners, led by left-arm orthodox bowler Praveen Jayawickrama, troubled the Indian batsmen in patches during the warm-up. The hosts are expected to field two frontline spinners in the Tests, and India's batsmen will need to counter the turning ball effectively.

The Indian think tank, led by head coach Gautam Gambhir, is expected to finalise the playing XI for the first Test in the coming days. The team management has a few selection headaches, particularly in the spin department, with Ravichandran Ashwin, Ravindra Jadeja, and Kuldeep Yadav all in contention.

What to watch

The first Test begins on Wednesday at the Galle International Stadium, a venue known for assisting spinners. India will look to start the series on a positive note, with Gill and Jaiswal expected to set the tone at the top of the order. The team's preparation has been steady, but the real test begins once the series gets underway.
