World Book Fair 2026 Opens in Delhi, Showcasing Global Literature

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • The fair features hundreds of publishers from India and abroad.
  • Cultural performances and author interactions are part of the lineup.
  • The event aims to promote reading and literary exchange.

A Grand Opening for Book Lovers

The World Book Fair 2026 kicked off in New Delhi on Saturday, drawing crowds of literature enthusiasts to the sprawling venue at Pragati Maidan. Organised by the National Book Trust, the fair promises a vibrant mix of books, cultural events, and discussions.

Hundreds of publishers from across India and more than 30 countries have set up stalls. From fiction to academic texts, the range of titles on display is vast. The fair, which runs for over a week, is expected to attract lakhs of visitors.

Spotlight on Indian Languages

A special focus this year is on regional Indian literature. Stalls dedicated to Hindi, Bengali, Tamil, and other languages have drawn significant attention. Publishers hope this will help bridge the gap between vernacular works and a wider readership.

Several leading authors are scheduled for panel discussions and book signings. The organisers have also arranged storytelling sessions for children, ensuring the event appeals to all age groups.

Cultural Performances Add Flavour

Beyond books, the fair features cultural performances, including folk dances and musical acts. These events, held at designated stages, aim to create a festive atmosphere. Visitors can also explore food stalls offering cuisines from different states.

The organisers have stressed the importance of such events in promoting a reading habit. With digital distractions on the rise, the fair serves as a reminder of the joy of physical books.

International Participation Grows

This year's edition has seen a notable increase in international participants. Countries like France, Japan, and Brazil have pavilions showcasing translated works. Literary agents and translators are using the platform to network and strike deals.

For many smaller publishers, the fair is a crucial opportunity to connect with distributors and readers. The buzz around the event suggests strong sales ahead.

The fair will remain open until January 18, with special discounts on bulk purchases. Organisers have made arrangements for online ticket bookings to manage long queues.

As the event unfolds, the focus will be on how it fosters a love for reading in an increasingly digital age. The coming days will reveal whether this year's fair sets new records for attendance and sales.
