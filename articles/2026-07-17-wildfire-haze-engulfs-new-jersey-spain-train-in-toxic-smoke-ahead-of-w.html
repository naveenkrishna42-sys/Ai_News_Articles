Wildfire haze engulfs New Jersey, Spain train in toxic smoke ahead of World Cup final

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Wildfire smoke from Canada has caused hazardous air quality in New York and New Jersey.
  • Spain's national team was forced to train indoors due to toxic smoke.
  • FIFA and local officials are monitoring conditions ahead of the World Cup final.
  • Weekend rain is forecast to clear the haze, reducing the threat to the match.

Haze descends on training grounds

Thick, acrid smoke from wildfires burning in Canada has engulfed large parts of New Jersey, pushing air quality into the hazardous zone and forcing the Spanish national football team to abandon outdoor practice sessions. The team, in the final stretch of preparations for the FIFA World Cup 2026 final, trained indoors on Friday as a grey-orange haze blanketed the region.

Players were seen wearing masks during transit, and coaching staff expressed concern over the visibility and respiratory conditions. The Spanish federation has not officially commented on whether the smoke has affected player health, but sources indicate the squad is following medical advice to limit exposure.

Final in question

The World Cup final is scheduled to be held at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, less than 20 kilometres from where the Spanish team is based. With air quality index readings crossing 250 in parts of the tri-state area, organisers face a difficult call. FIFA has not yet issued a formal statement on contingency plans, but officials are understood to be in daily consultation with weather and environmental agencies.

This is not the first time a major sporting event has been disrupted by wildfire smoke. In recent years, matches in the United States and Canada have been postponed or moved indoors due to similar conditions. The difference this time is the scale — the World Cup final is one of the most-watched events on the planet.

Rain offers a sliver of hope

Meteorologists are predicting that a weather system moving in over the weekend could bring much-needed rain, which would help clear the haze. If the forecast holds, air quality could improve significantly by Sunday, the day of the final. However, the situation remains fluid, and any change in wind direction could bring more smoke into the region.

Local authorities have already issued health advisories, urging residents to stay indoors and limit physical activity. The New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection has warned that the smoke contains fine particulate matter that can cause serious respiratory issues.

Fan and player safety first

The immediate concern for FIFA and local organisers is the health of players, officials, and the hundreds of thousands of fans expected to travel to the stadium. Talks are ongoing about the possibility of delaying the match or moving it to a backup venue, though no official decision has been announced.

Spain is not the only team affected. The opposing finalists, yet to be determined, have also been forced to adjust their training schedules. Both sides are expected to make a final decision on match-day protocols in the next 24 hours, depending on the air quality readings.

What to watch: The next 48 hours will be critical. If rain does not arrive as forecast, FIFA may have to make an unprecedented call on the world's biggest football match.
