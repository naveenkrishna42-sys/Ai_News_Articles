Chennai police launch specialised anti-corruption unit

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • New special unit set up to investigate corruption cases involving police personnel
  • Unit will handle complaints of bribery, extortion and misconduct
  • Move follows growing public concerns over police integrity

The Chennai police have established a dedicated anti-corruption special unit to investigate allegations of bribery, extortion and misconduct involving its own personnel, officials said on Tuesday.

The new unit, which began operations this week, will function under the direct supervision of the city police commissioner. Its mandate includes probing complaints against police officers and staff at all ranks, from constables to inspectors.

Why the unit was formed

The decision comes amid increasing public complaints about corrupt practices in the force, particularly in traffic enforcement, licensing and investigation wings. Complainants often struggle to register cases against police personnel because the accused are themselves officers.

A senior police officer familiar with the development said the unit will act as a transparent channel for citizens to report misconduct. The officer, who did not want to be named, said the unit will also conduct suo motu inquiries into suspicious assets or unexplained wealth of personnel.

How it will function

The unit will be headed by an officer of the rank of assistant commissioner of police. It will have dedicated investigating teams and a separate complaint cell, which will be accessible through phone, email and in-person visits.

Complaints will be acknowledged within 48 hours, and preliminary inquiries will be completed within two weeks. If prima facie evidence is found, a departmental inquiry or criminal case will be initiated as per law.

Officials said the unit will coordinate with the Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption for cases involving senior officers. It will also maintain a confidential database of repeat offenders within the force.

Reaction from the public

Citizens' groups and legal aid activists have welcomed the move, though some expressed scepticism about its effectiveness. “The real test will be whether the unit acts independently and punishes errant officers,” said a resident of T. Nagar who asked not to be named.

The police have urged the public to come forward with concrete evidence, such as audio or video recordings, to support their complaints. Anonymous complaints will also be accepted but will be verified before action.

What happens next

The unit will publish a quarterly report on the number of complaints received and actions taken, to ensure accountability. The first such report is expected by the end of this year.

Observers will be watching whether this initiative translates into visible disciplinary actions, or remains another bureaucratic layer. The police leadership has said it is committed to cleaning the force from within.
