Two dead, over 150 injured in crowd surge during Puri Rath Yatra

📅 2026-07-17 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least two devotees died and over 150 were injured in a crowd surge at Puri's Jagannath Yatra.
  • The incident occurred during the 'Pahandi' procession on Grand Road, which was choked with pilgrims.
  • Five others fell ill amid the chaos and rain, officials said.

Chaos on Grand Road

At least two devotees died and more than 150 others were injured in a crowd surge during the annual Jagannath Rath Yatra in Puri on Thursday. The incident unfolded on Grand Road, where lakhs of pilgrims had gathered for the 'Pahandi' procession — the ritual carrying of the deities from the temple to the chariots.

Witnesses described a sudden swell in the crowd that left little room to move. The narrow stretch of Grand Road, already choked with devotees, became the epicentre of the stampede-like situation.

Five other devotees fell ill amid the chaos and the intermittent rain that lashed the town. Officials confirmed that the injured were rushed to nearby hospitals. The exact sequence of events that triggered the surge is still being pieced together.

What triggered the surge

Preliminary reports suggest the crowd scare began when a section of the procession slowed down unexpectedly. This created a bottleneck, with thousands pressing forward from behind. The 'Pahandi' ritual, which involves carrying the idols on a set route, is one of the most anticipated moments of the yatra.

Devotees had been waiting for hours in the humid weather. The sudden rain may have added to the panic, as people scrambled for cover under makeshift awnings and shop fronts. Authorities have not yet confirmed whether a specific trigger — such as a rumour or a slip — caused the surge.

Odisha's Special Relief Commissioner has ordered a detailed inquiry into the incident. The district administration has been asked to submit a report within 48 hours.

Hospital response and casualties

At least 150 injured devotees were treated at the district headquarters hospital and several private clinics in Puri. Officials said most suffered from bruises, fractures and breathing difficulty. Two persons were declared dead on arrival.

Five others who fell ill were treated for dehydration and exhaustion. Medical teams have been deployed at key points along the yatra route to handle any further emergencies. The state government has announced an ex-gratia of Rs 4 lakh each for the families of the deceased.

Crowd management under scrutiny

The Rath Yatra draws lakhs of devotees every year, and crowd management has often been a challenge. This year, despite additional barricades and watchtowers, the sheer number of pilgrims overwhelmed the arrangements.

Local police had deployed over 5,000 personnel along the 3-km route. But the narrow lanes and the emotional fervour of the procession made it difficult to control the flow. Civic volunteers and disaster response teams were also present at the site.

Former administrators have pointed out that the 'Pahandi' procession, by its very nature, creates unpredictable crowd dynamics. The ritual requires the deities to be carried slowly, which can cause sudden halts and pile-ups.

What happens next

The yatra continued after a brief pause, with the chariots being pulled to the Gundicha Temple later in the evening. The state government has ordered a review of crowd control protocols for the remaining days of the festival. A detailed report is expected within a week.
