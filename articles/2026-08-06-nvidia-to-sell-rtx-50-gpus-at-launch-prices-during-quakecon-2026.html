Nvidia to Sell RTX 50 GPUs at Launch Prices During QuakeCon 2026

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nvidia will sell RTX 50-series GPUs at MSRP during QuakeCon 2026.
  • The event takes place in Grapevine, Texas, and has become a major attraction.
  • The move highlights ongoing market challenges in finding GPUs at retail prices.

A Rare Deal in Grapevine

At this year's QuakeCon, Nvidia is trying something unusual: selling its latest graphics cards at their original launch prices. The company has confirmed that the GeForce RTX 5090, RTX 5080, and RTX 5070 will be available at MSRP during the event, which runs in Grapevine, Texas.

For gamers who have grown used to seeing inflated street prices, this is a notable shift. The announcement has quickly become one of the show's biggest talking points, according to multiple tech outlets covering the convention.

Why MSRP Matters

Graphics card pricing has been a sore subject for PC enthusiasts for years. Since the RTX 30-series era, retail prices have often drifted far above the suggested launch price, thanks to supply constraints and reseller activity. The RTX 50-series launched earlier this year to strong demand, but finding one at the official price has been a challenge.

By offering these cards at MSRP, Nvidia is giving attendees a rare chance to buy without the usual markup. The company has not said how many units will be available, nor whether there will be limits per person. Officials have not yet confirmed if the stock will be sufficient to meet the expected demand.

QuakeCon as a Retail Experiment

QuakeCon has traditionally been a gathering for fans of id Software and the broader PC gaming community. This year, it doubles as a retail experiment. The event, held in the Dallas suburb of Grapevine, is known for its bring-your-own-computer LAN parties and community atmosphere.

Nvidia's decision to use the venue for a direct sales push is a departure from its usual approach. Typically, the company relies on board partners and online retailers to move product. Selling directly at a convention is a more hands-on strategy, and it appears to be drawing attention from attendees who might otherwise skip the show.

What This Signals for the Market

The move could be a response to persistent complaints about GPU availability. While the RTX 50-series has been on shelves for months, many shoppers still report difficulty in finding cards at the suggested retail price. By staging a public sale at MSRP, Nvidia is making a visible statement that it is aware of the issue.

It also creates a sense of urgency. Attendees who want a card at launch price will need to be at the convention, which is happening now. The limited-time nature of the offer adds a layer of exclusivity.

Some observers speculate that this could be a test for future events. If the sale is a success, Nvidia might consider similar pop-up sales at other gaming conventions later this year. The company has not commented on those possibilities.

What to Watch Next

As QuakeCon continues, the focus will be on whether the MSRP stock lasts. Reports from the venue suggest that the GPU sale has become a major draw, but the exact number of cards on hand remains unclear.

For gamers who missed out, the question is whether Nvidia will extend the offer online or at other events. The company has not announced any follow-up plans, but the buzz from Grapevine could push it to reconsider. For now, the message is simple: if you want a new RTX card at launch price, QuakeCon is the place to be.
