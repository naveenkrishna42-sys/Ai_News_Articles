The Crumbling Reality on Pottery Road

Bengaluru, a city often lauded for its technological prowess, frequently grapples with foundational urban infrastructure challenges. A stark example is the deteriorating condition of footpaths along Pottery Road, where extensive cracking and uneven surfaces have become a daily hazard for pedestrians. What appears as a localized issue is, in fact, symptomatic of broader systemic failures in urban planning, material selection, and civic oversight across the city.

The visible cracks are more than mere aesthetic blemishes; they represent immediate safety risks. Walkers, particularly the elderly, children, and those with mobility impairments, face increased risks of trips and falls. This degradation diminishes the basic utility of pedestrian infrastructure, forcing citizens onto busy roads and exacerbating traffic congestion and accident risks.

Beyond Surface Cracks: Unpacking the Root Causes

Substandard Materials and Construction Practices

The rapid deterioration of footpaths like those on Pottery Road often points to compromises in material quality and construction methodology. A robust footpath requires a proper sub-base, adequate compaction, and durable surfacing materials like concrete or interlocking pavers designed for specific load-bearing capacities and weather conditions. Using inferior aggregates, insufficient cement content, or skipping crucial preparation steps inevitably leads to premature failure, especially under the relentless urban pressures of Bengaluru.

The Utility Trenching Cycle

A primary culprit behind footpath damage is the incessant digging for utility lines. Be it optical fibre cables, water pipelines, or drainage systems, civic agencies and private providers frequently excavate footpaths. The problem arises when these trenches are not properly backfilled and reinstated. Often, excavated soil is loosely refilled, and the top layer is patched with inadequate materials, leading to subsidence, uneven surfaces, and subsequent cracking under pedestrian load and rainfall.

Lack of Integrated Urban Planning

The fragmented approach to urban development further compounds the issue. Different civic bodies operate independently, often without coordinated planning. This results in newly laid footpaths being dug up weeks later for another utility project, creating a perpetual cycle of damage and temporary repairs. A holistic urban planning strategy, incorporating a utility duct network and strict reinstatement protocols, is essential to break this pattern.

The Human Cost: Safety, Accessibility, and Urban Livability

Beyond the structural integrity, the state of footpaths directly impacts the quality of life for Bengaluru's residents. For individuals using wheelchairs, crutches, or prams, a cracked and uneven footpath transforms a simple stroll into an insurmountable obstacle course. This lack of accessibility is a direct violation of urban inclusion principles, marginalizing vulnerable sections of the population.

Furthermore, poorly maintained pedestrian infrastructure discourages walking, a sustainable mode of transport. This pushes more commuters towards private vehicles, contributing to Bengaluru's notorious traffic woes and increased air pollution. Investing in durable, accessible footpaths is not merely a civic duty but a strategic investment in public health, environmental sustainability, and overall urban livability.

A Path Forward: Prioritising Durable Urban Infrastructure

Addressing the challenges exemplified by Pottery Road requires a multi-pronged approach. Strict adherence to quality standards for materials and construction, coupled with rigorous oversight by civic authorities, is non-negotiable. Implementing utility corridors or ducts during initial infrastructure development can significantly reduce the need for repeated digging.

Accountability must be established for contractors and utility providers responsible for reinstatement work. Penalties for shoddy repairs and incentives for durable solutions could drive better outcomes. Ultimately, a shift from reactive, temporary fixes to proactive, long-term infrastructure planning is paramount for Bengaluru to truly live up to its aspirations as a global city.
