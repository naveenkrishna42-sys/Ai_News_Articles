EXIM Bank Plans $300 Million Bond Sale Under RBI Hedging Facility

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • EXIM Bank plans a $300 million dollar-denominated bond sale
  • The issuance will use the RBI's hedging facility to manage currency risk
  • Move aims to lower borrowing costs and support export financing

EXIM Bank to Tap International Market

India's Export-Import Bank (EXIM Bank) is preparing to raise $300 million through a dollar-denominated bond sale. The issuance will be structured under the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) newly introduced hedging facility, a move meant to make offshore borrowing cheaper and more predictable.

Banking sources familiar with the plan said the bonds will be marketed to global investors in the coming weeks. The EXIM Bank, which finances Indian exports and trade projects, typically borrows in foreign currencies to on-lend to domestic firms. The RBI facility allows it to hedge the currency risk more efficiently.

How the RBI Hedging Facility Works

The RBI's hedging window lets state-owned entities like EXIM Bank lock in a fixed rupee-dollar exchange rate for the bond's tenure. This eliminates the volatility that usually inflates the cost of foreign debt for Indian borrowers.

Under the facility, the central bank offers a forward cover at a pre-determined premium. For EXIM Bank, this means the effective cost of the $300 million loan will be known upfront, making it easier to pass on competitive rates to exporters.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact coupon rate or maturity date of the bond. Market watchers expect the tenor to be around five years, given current demand for emerging market debt.

Impact on Export Financing

The bond sale comes at a time when Indian exporters face tight margins due to global demand slowdown and currency fluctuations. Cheaper dollar funding from EXIM Bank could help them offer better credit terms to overseas buyers.

EXIM Bank's borrowing costs directly affect the interest rates it charges on export credit. A successful $300 million issuance under the hedging facility could set a precedent for other state-owned banks to follow suit, potentially unlocking cheaper capital for the trade sector.

The bank's move also signals confidence in the RBI's hedging mechanism, which was introduced last year to address a persistent problem: the high cost of hedging for public sector borrowers. Until now, many entities had to rely on costly derivatives or private swaps.

Market Conditions and Investor Appetite

Global bond markets have been choppy, but dollar-denominated Indian sovereign and quasi-sovereign paper has seen steady demand. The EXIM Bank bond is expected to appeal to pension funds and insurance firms looking for relatively safe yields in a low-rate environment.

Analysts point out that the hedging facility makes the paper more attractive because it removes exchange rate risk. For investors, the risk shifts to the RBI's creditworthiness rather than the rupee's performance.

The bond sale is likely to be completed within the current quarter. EXIM Bank's treasury team is currently in talks with lead arrangers, though the names of the banks have not been disclosed.

What to watch: The exact pricing of the bond will signal how much premium the market demands for Indian quasi-sovereign credit under the new hedging framework. A tight spread could encourage other state-owned entities to line up similar issuances.
