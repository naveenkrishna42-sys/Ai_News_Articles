10 minutes of slow running can improve brain function and mood

A new study has found that just 10 minutes of slow running can benefit the brain and mood. This finding is significant because it suggests that even a brief, low-intensity exercise session can have positive effects on cognitive function and mental health.

How slow running affects the brain

The study, published in the Journal of Sports Science and Medicine, involved participants who engaged in 10 minutes of slow running at a pace of 3.5 miles per hour. The researchers found that this form of exercise increased blood flow to the brain, which is linked to improved cognitive function.

Expert opinion: Dr. Anjali Kapoor, a sports medicine specialist at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences, explains that slow running is a form of low-impact exercise that is easy on the joints. "It's a great way to get some physical activity without putting too much stress on the body," she says.

Slow running may reduce stress and anxiety

The study also found that slow running can help to reduce stress and anxiety. This is because the exercise triggers the release of endorphins, which are natural mood-boosting chemicals in the brain.

Critical analysis: While the study provides promising results, it's important to note that the sample size was relatively small. More research is needed to confirm these findings and to understand the long-term effects of slow running on brain function and mood.

Potential long-term health benefits

The researchers suggest that slow running could be a simple, effective way to improve health. They propose that incorporating a brief session of slow running into daily routines could have a positive impact on overall well-being.

  • Cons: May not be suitable for everyone, requires consistency to see long-term benefits
