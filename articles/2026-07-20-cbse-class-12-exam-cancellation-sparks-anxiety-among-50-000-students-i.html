CBSE Class 12 Exam Cancellation Sparks Anxiety Among 50,000 Students in West Asia

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • CBSE has cancelled Class 12 board exams for students in West Asia due to regional instability.
  • Over 50,000 students are affected, with many worried about university admissions and future plans.
  • The board is yet to announce alternative assessment methods or a revised schedule for these students.

The Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) has cancelled the Class 12 board examinations for students in West Asia, leaving over 50,000 students in a state of deep uncertainty. The decision, announced late Tuesday, comes amid escalating regional tensions that have disrupted normal life across several countries in the region.

For students, the timing could not be worse. The Class 12 board results are a critical gateway to university admissions, both in India and abroad. Many had been preparing for months, banking on the exams to secure their academic futures.

Uncertainty Over University Admissions

“It’s a crucial year, we need to apply to universities,” said one student from Dubai, echoing the sentiment of thousands. The cancellation has thrown admission timelines into disarray. Students now face the prospect of missing application deadlines or relying on alternative assessment criteria that have not yet been finalised.

Parents and school administrators have expressed concern that the lack of a clear plan from CBSE could disadvantage students from West Asia compared to their peers in India. Unlike domestic students who will still appear for exams, those abroad are left in limbo.

CBSE Yet to Announce Alternative Plan

CBSE officials have stated that they are working on an alternative assessment framework. However, no concrete details have been provided so far. The board has not confirmed whether students will be evaluated based on internal assessments, pre-board marks, or a special online examination.

Schools across the Gulf region are scrambling to advise students. Many are urging them to keep in touch with their counsellors and to explore provisional admission options wherever possible. The uncertainty has also prompted some families to consider shifting their children back to India for the remainder of the academic year.

Broader Regional Context

The exam cancellations are part of a wider disruption caused by political and security challenges in parts of West Asia. Several Indian schools in the region have already shifted to online classes or suspended operations temporarily. The CBSE decision mirrors similar steps taken by other educational boards in the region.

For now, students are left counting the days. The board has promised to release a detailed notice within a week, but for many, that week feels like an eternity. With university deadlines looming, every day without clarity adds to the pressure.

All eyes are now on CBSE’s next move. Students, parents, and schools are waiting for a transparent and time-bound road map that can restore some semblance of normalcy to their disrupted academic year.
