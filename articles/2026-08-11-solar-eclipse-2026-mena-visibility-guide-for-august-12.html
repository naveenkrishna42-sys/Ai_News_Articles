Solar Eclipse 2026: MENA Visibility Guide for August 12

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Total solar eclipse on August 12, 2026, will be visible in parts of the MENA region.
  • The path of totality passes through select areas; other regions will see a partial eclipse.
  • Viewers are advised to use proper eye protection and check local timings.

MENA in the Shadow Path

The total solar eclipse on August 12, 2026, will carve a narrow path across the Earth, and parts of the Middle East and North Africa are set to witness the full spectacle. Unlike the partial phases visible across a wider swath, totality — where the moon completely blocks the sun — will be limited to a specific corridor.

According to projections, the path of totality enters the MENA region from the west, crossing through northern Egypt and potentially grazing parts of Libya. Major cities like Cairo will experience a deep partial eclipse, with the sun nearly fully obscured, but not the complete darkness of totality.

Key Viewing Locations and Timings

For those in the region, the exact timing of the eclipse will vary by location. In Egypt's northern coastal areas, totality is expected to last for several minutes, while inland cities will see a significant partial eclipse. The Gulf states, including Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar, will witness a pronounced partial eclipse, with the sun appearing as a crescent.

Observers in Lebanon, Jordan, and Syria will also see a substantial partial phase, though the degree of coverage will be less than in the path of totality. The eclipse will begin in the late afternoon, with the maximum point occurring close to sunset in some eastern locations, adding a dramatic reddish hue to the sky.

Safety First: Eye Protection Essential

Authorities and astronomy groups across the region are emphasising the need for certified eclipse glasses. Looking directly at the sun, even during a partial eclipse, can cause permanent eye damage. The rush for glasses has been reported globally, including in the UK, and MENA viewers are advised to purchase them from reputable sellers in advance.

For those unable to obtain glasses, indirect viewing methods — such as pinhole projectors or watching live streams from space agencies — are safe alternatives. The next total solar eclipse visible from a similar region is not expected for decades, making this a rare opportunity for many.

Live Coverage and Tracking Tools

Several space agencies and news outlets, including Al Jazeera and Space.com, are providing live updates and eclipse trackers to help viewers pinpoint the exact time of the eclipse in their location. These tools are particularly useful for those outside the path of totality who want to catch the maximum obscuration.

In the MENA region, local astronomical societies are organising public viewing events, especially in Egypt, where the phenomenon will be most prominent. These events will feature telescopes with solar filters and expert guidance.

Weather and Visibility Concerns

Clear skies will be crucial for viewing. The Mediterranean coast of Egypt typically enjoys sunny August weather, but afternoon cloud cover can occasionally develop. Forecasters will issue updates closer to the event, and viewers are advised to check local conditions beforehand.

In desert areas of Libya and Egypt, the dry climate generally favours clear skies, but dust haze could reduce visibility. The eclipse's timing near sunset in some eastern regions may also affect viewing conditions.

For the uninitiated, the experience of totality is brief — a sudden drop in temperature, the appearance of the sun's corona, and stars emerging in the daytime sky. It is a phenomenon that many travel thousands of kilometres to witness, and for MENA residents, it is happening on their doorstep.

What to Watch For

As the eclipse approaches, last-minute weather forecasts will be critical. Those in the path of totality should be ready to travel a short distance if needed to escape cloud cover. For everyone else in the region, a partial eclipse is still a spectacular sight — with proper eye protection.

Following the event, attention will turn to the next major eclipse in the region, expected in 2027, which will cross parts of North Africa. For now, all eyes are on the sky this August 12.
