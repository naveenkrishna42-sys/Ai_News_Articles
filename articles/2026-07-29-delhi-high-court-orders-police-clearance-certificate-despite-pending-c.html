Delhi High Court orders police clearance certificate despite pending case

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi High Court ordered issuance of Police Clearance Certificate to an Indian national with a pending criminal case.
  • The court ruled that a pending case alone cannot be a ground to deny the certificate.
  • The directive was issued to the Union government through the Ministry of External Affairs.

Court overrules denial of certificate

The Delhi High Court has directed the Union government to issue a Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) to an Indian national despite a criminal case pending against him. The order was passed by a single bench while hearing a petition challenging the refusal to grant the certificate.

The petitioner had applied for a PCC, a document often required for employment or immigration abroad. The authorities had turned down the request citing the pending criminal proceedings.

Pending case not an automatic bar

Justice Prathiba M. Singh observed that merely having a pending criminal case does not disqualify a person from obtaining a PCC. The court noted that the certificate is a record of the applicant's criminal history and not a declaration of guilt.

The ruling clarified that the certificate should reflect the pendency of the case rather than outright denial. The bench said the authorities must issue the document with an appropriate remark about the ongoing matter.

What the court directed

The High Court instructed the Ministry of External Affairs to issue the PCC within a stipulated timeframe. The order applies specifically to the petitioner but sets a precedent for similar cases.

The court also directed that the certificate must mention the pending case details so that the receiving authority abroad is fully informed. This balances the individual's right to travel or work overseas with the need for transparency.

Background of the case

The petitioner, an Indian citizen, had applied for the PCC through the Passport Seva Kendra. The application was rejected on the ground of pending criminal litigation. The petitioner then moved the High Court challenging the decision.

The Union government argued that the refusal was in line with existing guidelines that treat pending cases as a valid ground for denial. The court disagreed, holding that such an interpretation was too broad.

Implications for applicants

This judgment could benefit many Indian nationals who face similar hurdles while applying for PCCs. The ruling makes it clear that authorities cannot use pending cases as a blanket reason to deny the certificate.

Legal experts say the decision reinforces the principle of presumption of innocence until proven guilty. The court has effectively separated the concept of a pending investigation from a conviction.

What happens next

The Union government will now have to comply with the court's order and issue the certificate. The Ministry of External Affairs is expected to update its internal guidelines based on this ruling. Similar petitions pending in other courts may also see swift disposal following this precedent.
