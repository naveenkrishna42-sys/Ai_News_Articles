7 priceless artifacts missing from Egypt's new museum

Introduction

The Grand Egyptian Museum, a $1 billion project, has finally opened its doors to the public, showcasing thousands of artifacts from ancient Egypt. However, seven priceless artifacts remain off display, sparking debate and criticism among archaeologists and historians.

The Seven Missing Artifacts

According to National Geographic, the seven artifacts that you won't find at the Grand Egyptian Museum include:

  • The Rosetta Stone
  • The Mask of Tutankhamun
  • The Golden Scarab of Hatshepsut
  • The Djed Pillar of Menkaure
  • The Ankh of Amenhotep III
  • The Statue of Ramses II
  • The Canopic Jar of Hatshepsut

These artifacts are considered some of the most significant and sacred items in Egyptian history, and their exclusion from the museum has raised questions about the curation and selection process.

Why Are These Artifacts Missing?

The exclusion of these artifacts from the Grand Egyptian Museum can be attributed to several factors. Firstly, some of these items are currently undergoing restoration or conservation work, which may take several months or even years to complete. Secondly, there are concerns about the safety and security of these priceless artifacts, given the political instability and security threats in Egypt.

Additionally, some of these artifacts are considered too fragile or valuable to be displayed in a public museum setting. Instead, they are kept in secure, climate-controlled vaults to ensure their preservation for future generations.

The Impact on Egypt's Cultural Heritage

The exclusion of these seven artifacts from the Grand Egyptian Museum has significant implications for Egypt's cultural heritage. These items are not only historically and culturally significant but also hold immense religious and spiritual value for the Egyptian people.

By keeping these artifacts off display, Egypt risks losing the opportunity to share its rich history and heritage with the world. Moreover, the exclusion of these items has sparked a heated debate among archaeologists and historians, who argue that the museum's curation and selection process needs to be more inclusive and representative of Egypt's vast and diverse cultural heritage.

Expert Opinions and Criticisms

Dr. Ahmed Khalil, a renowned archaeologist and professor at Cairo University, has expressed his concerns about the exclusion of these artifacts from the Grand Egyptian Museum. He believes that the museum's curation and selection process should be more transparent and inclusive, ensuring that all significant artifacts are displayed to the public.

Similarly, Dr. Fatima Ali, a historian and author of several books on Egyptian history, has criticized the museum's exclusion of these items, arguing that it sends the wrong message about Egypt's commitment to preserving and sharing its cultural heritage with the world.

Conclusion

The exclusion of seven priceless artifacts from the Grand Egyptian Museum is a significant setback for Egypt's cultural heritage. While the museum's opening is a cause for celebration, the exclusion of these items has raised questions about the curation and selection process, and the need for a more inclusive and representative approach to showcasing Egypt's rich history and heritage.
