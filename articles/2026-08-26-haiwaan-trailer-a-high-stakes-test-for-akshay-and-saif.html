Haiwaan Trailer: A High-Stakes Test for Akshay and Saif

📅 2026-08-26 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Akshay Kumar shifts to a villainous persona to break his recent box office slump.
  • Saif Ali Khan undertakes a high-difficulty role as a visually impaired protagonist.
  • Director Priyadarshan returns to dark psychological thriller territory after years.

The Star Power Gamble

The digital release of the Haiwaan trailer on August 25 marks a pivotal moment for two of Bollywood’s most enduring stars. With Akshay Kumar stepping into a cold-blooded antagonist role and Saif Ali Khan portraying a visually impaired lead, the film attempts a sharp pivot from their recent commercial choices. The industry is watching closely; both actors have faced scrutiny regarding their recent box office performances, making this collaboration a potential course correction.

Priyadarshan’s Directorial Precision

Priyadarshan, a filmmaker often associated with mass-market comedies, is returning to the psychological thriller genre. His reported advice to Saif—to avoid the trope of 'acting blind' by keeping eyes open and unblinking—suggests a move toward understated, grit-heavy realism. This stylistic shift is crucial. If the execution avoids melodrama, it could provide the narrative weight the script requires to distinguish itself in a crowded streaming and theatrical market.

Critical Analysis: Can It Deliver?

The success of Haiwaan hinges on the chemistry between the antagonist and the protagonist. Akshay Kumar has historically struggled when playing characters that lack a clear moral compass, often reverting to heroic mannerisms. To succeed here, he must shed his 'Khiladi' image entirely. Saif Ali Khan, meanwhile, has found success in nuanced, character-driven roles, but playing a visually impaired character is a technical tightrope walk that risks falling into caricature if not managed with absolute restraint.

  • Pro: The reunion of two major stars who share a long-standing professional rapport.
  • Pro: Priyadarshan’s history with high-tension thrillers provides a solid creative foundation.
  • Con: The risk of the film feeling dated if the screenplay relies on exhausted thriller tropes.
  • Con: High audience expectations may lead to disappointment if the performances appear rehearsed rather than organic.

The Broader Impact

This film is not just another project; it is a barometer for how legacy stars are adapting to the post-pandemic cinema landscape. Audiences are increasingly rejecting 'safe' star vehicles in favor of high-concept storytelling. If Haiwaan leans into its dark, experimental premise, it could signal a shift in how A-list actors approach mid-career risks. If it fails, it serves as a stark reminder that star power alone is no longer enough to carry a mediocre script in an era where global content is only a click away.
