Monsoon Session: Rijiju Challenges Rahul Gandhi to Face Amit Shah in Parliament

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kiren Rijiju challenged Rahul Gandhi to face Amit Shah in Parliament, saying 'Don't Run Away'
  • Kharge said opposition's demand has been consistent from the beginning, not changing goalposts
  • Govt sets terms for Amit Shah's reply on Gen Z stir; FCRA bill splits opposition

The Monsoon Session of Parliament took a combative turn on Tuesday with Union Minister Kiren Rijiju throwing down the gauntlet at Congress leader Rahul Gandhi. Rijiju's challenge was blunt: face Union Home Minister Amit Shah in a direct debate on the floor of the House.

"Don't run away," Rijiju said, addressing Gandhi directly. The minister's remarks came amid a continued logjam that has paralysed proceedings in both Houses, with the opposition pressing for a statement from Shah on the handling of the recent Gen Z protests.

Kharge's Counter to Government's Claims

Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge, however, dismissed suggestions that the opposition was shifting its stance. "We are not changing goalposts. Our demand has been consistent from the beginning," Kharge asserted, pushing back against the government's narrative that the opposition was being unreasonable.

The core demand remains unchanged: a detailed statement from Amit Shah in Parliament regarding the police action during the Gen Z stir, an issue that has become the flashpoint of the current session.

Government Sets Terms for Shah's Reply

Sources indicate the government has now set specific terms under which Amit Shah could respond to the opposition's queries. The conditions, reportedly aimed at limiting the scope of discussion, have not been formally disclosed but are believed to have complicated negotiations between the treasury and opposition benches.

The terms have also laid bare a fresh rift within the opposition ranks. The contentious FCRA (Foreign Contribution Regulation Act) amendment bill has split opposition parties, with some expressing willingness to discuss it while others remain firmly against its provisions.

The Question of Force and Silence

Outside the formal parliamentary exchanges, the political battlefield has expanded. Rahul Gandhi has publicly stated that "the country wants to know if Shah ordered the shooting" during the Gen Z protests. This comes in direct contrast to BJP president J.P. Nadda's categorical claim that "no shots were fired" during the police operation.

The discrepancy between these two positions has become the central point of contention. Civil liberties groups and commentators have weighed in, with one prominent editorial describing the Home Minister's silence on alleged police brutality as "a betrayal of constitutional morality."

Government benches have dismissed such criticism as politically motivated, insisting that the security forces acted within the law and that the opposition is manufacturing a crisis to derail legislative business.

Legislative Business in Limbo

The impasse has effectively stalled crucial legislative work. With the opposition demanding a structured debate on the Gen Z stir before any other business, several bills, including the FCRA amendment, remain pending. The government, for its part, accuses the opposition of using procedural tactics to avoid discussing bills it finds politically inconvenient.

The parliamentary affairs ministry has called for an all-party meeting to break the deadlock, but no breakthrough has been announced so far. Both sides seem dug in, with each accusing the other of intransigence.

As the session progresses, the next few sitting days will be crucial. Whether Rijiju's challenge leads to a face-off between Gandhi and Shah or whether the logjam persists remains to be seen. What is clear is that the parliamentary arithmetic has taken a backseat to a high-stakes political confrontation that shows no signs of cooling.

All eyes are now on the Business Advisory Committee's next meeting, where the schedule for the remainder of the session will be charted. The opposition has indicated it will not relent until its demand is met, setting the stage for another round of adjournments and heated exchanges.
