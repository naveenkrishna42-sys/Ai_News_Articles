Chennai: Over one lakh people dine at Amma Unavagams daily

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Daily diners at Amma Unavagams in Chennai have crossed one lakh
  • The canteens offer subsidised meals including idli, sambar rice, and curd rice
  • Officials say the increase reflects growing trust in the quality and hygiene of the food served

Record footfall at city-run canteens

Chennai's Amma Unavagams have hit a new milestone. Daily diners at these subsidised canteens have crossed the one-lakh mark for the first time. Officials from the Greater Chennai Corporation confirmed the figure on Friday.

The canteens, operated by the civic body, serve affordable meals to the city's residents. A typical meal costs just a few rupees. The most popular items include idli, sambar rice, and curd rice.

Why the numbers are rising

Corporation officials said the increase reflects growing public trust. Many residents now prefer these canteens over private eateries due to the consistent quality and hygiene standards maintained at the outlets.

“We have ensured that the food is not only affordable but also prepared under strict supervision. This has encouraged more people, including office workers and daily wage earners, to opt for Amma Unavagams,” a senior official said.

Expansion plans on the cards

The corporation currently operates over 250 Amma Unavagams across Chennai. Each outlet serves between 400 and 500 meals daily during peak hours. The footfall has been steadily rising over the past year.

Officials are now considering opening more outlets in areas with high demand. They are also exploring the introduction of new menu items to cater to changing tastes.

Impact on the public

For many, these canteens are a lifeline. Auto-rickshaw driver R. Murugan, who eats at an Amma Unavagam in T. Nagar, said, “I can get a filling meal for Rs 10. That is less than what I would spend on tea and snacks elsewhere.”

Students and senior citizens also form a large part of the daily clientele. The low prices help them stretch their budgets further in a city where living costs are rising.

The one-lakh mark is not just a number. It indicates the scale of the programme's success in providing affordable nutrition to a large urban population.

Challenges ahead

Sustaining such a large operation comes with its own challenges. The corporation must manage procurement, cooking, and distribution efficiently to avoid wastage and maintain quality. Officials say they are monitoring the system closely.

Any disruption in the supply chain could affect the canteens' ability to serve this many people daily. The civic body is working to ensure that the kitchen operations remain robust even as demand grows.

What happens next: The corporation plans to collect feedback from diners to fine-tune the menu and service. If the current trend holds, daily footfall could soon touch 1.2 lakh, pushing the need for more outlets and better logistics.
