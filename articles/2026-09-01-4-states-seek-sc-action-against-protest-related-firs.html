4 States Seek SC Action Against Protest-Related FIRs

States Seek SC Intervention Over Protest-Related FIRs

Four states have approached the Supreme Court, urging it to quash First Information Reports (FIRs) filed against student protesters. The protests, which took place across multiple states, were primarily over grievances related to the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET) exam. The Centre has cited its commitment to the Chief Justice of India (CJI) in its plea to the apex court.

Centre's Commitment to CJI in Plea

The Centre's plea to the Supreme Court highlights its commitment to the CJI, emphasizing the need for a fair and just resolution to the matter. The government has argued that the FIRs filed against the protesters are not in line with the principles of justice and fairness. The Centre has sought the court's intervention to ensure that the rights of the protesters are upheld.

Protests Over NEET Exam Grievances

The protests were a response to various grievances related to the NEET exam, which is conducted for admission to medical and dental colleges across India. The students, who were primarily from economically weaker sections, had expressed their concerns over the exam's pattern, marking scheme, and the lack of transparency in the admission process. The protests turned violent in some instances, leading to the filing of FIRs against the protesters.

Key SC Hearing Expected Today

A key hearing on the matter is expected to take place today in the Supreme Court. The CJI has been quoted as saying, 'no defects please,' indicating the seriousness of the matter. The court is likely to consider the Centre's plea and the arguments put forth by the states in its decision to quash the FIRs.

Broader Implications of the Matter

The matter has broader implications for the relationship between the Centre and the states, as well as for the administration of justice in the country. The protests and the subsequent FIRs have raised questions about the transparency and fairness of the NEET exam and the admission process. The Supreme Court's decision on the matter will set a precedent for similar cases in the future.
