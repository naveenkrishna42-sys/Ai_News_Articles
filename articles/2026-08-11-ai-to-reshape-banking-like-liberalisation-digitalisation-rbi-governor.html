AI to reshape banking like liberalisation, digitalisation: RBI Governor

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra likens AI's impact on banking to past reforms.
  • He stresses the need for innovation alongside regulatory safeguards.
  • The remarks come amid growing global focus on AI in financial services.

Mumbai: Artificial intelligence is set to transform the banking sector as fundamentally as liberalisation and digitalisation did in earlier decades, Reserve Bank of India Governor Sanjay Malhotra said on Tuesday. Speaking at an industry event, Malhotra argued that AI is not merely an incremental tool but a structural force that will redefine how banks operate, serve customers, and manage risk.

The Governor's remarks come at a time when Indian lenders are increasingly deploying AI for fraud detection, credit scoring, and customer service chatbots. However, he cautioned that the technology's benefits must be weighed against new vulnerabilities, including data privacy concerns and algorithmic bias.

A Historic Parallel

Malhotra drew a direct line from the 1991 economic liberalisation and the later wave of digital banking to the current AI moment. "Just as those shifts rewrote the rules of the game, AI will do the same," he said, according to a transcript of his speech. He noted that each prior transformation brought both disruption and opportunity, and AI would follow a similar trajectory.

The Governor emphasised that banks that fail to adapt risk obsolescence, while those that embrace AI responsibly could gain a competitive edge. He pointed to the rapid adoption of AI in areas like personalised financial advice and real-time transaction monitoring as early signs of this shift.

Balancing Innovation and Regulation

Malhotra stressed that innovation must not outpace oversight. "We need to ensure that the adoption of AI is safe, secure, and aligned with the broader public interest," he said. The RBI has been studying AI's implications for financial stability, and Malhotra hinted at upcoming guidelines to address ethical and operational risks.

The central bank's approach, he suggested, would be consultative rather than prescriptive, allowing the industry to experiment while maintaining guardrails. He cited the success of India's Unified Payments Interface (UPI) as an example of how regulation and innovation can coexist to produce world-class outcomes.

Industry Response and Challenges

Bankers present at the event welcomed the Governor's forward-looking stance but also flagged practical hurdles. These include the high cost of AI infrastructure, the need for skilled talent, and the difficulty of integrating AI with legacy systems. Some executives privately expressed concern about over-regulation stifling experimentation.

Malhotra acknowledged these challenges but insisted that the long-term benefits outweigh short-term frictions. "The banking sector has always been resilient," he said. "AI is another test of that resilience."

What to Watch

The RBI is expected to release a detailed discussion paper on AI in banking within the next few months. Stakeholders will be watching closely for how the central bank balances its dual mandate of fostering innovation and ensuring financial stability. For now, Malhotra's message is clear: the AI era in Indian banking has begun, and it will be as transformative as anything that came before.
