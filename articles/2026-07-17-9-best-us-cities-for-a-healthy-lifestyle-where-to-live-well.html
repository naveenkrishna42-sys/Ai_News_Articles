9 Best US Cities for a Healthy Lifestyle: Where to Live Well

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • The list focuses on access to outdoor activities, healthy food options, and wellness culture.
  • Cities like Boulder, Honolulu, and Portland are ranked high for active lifestyles.
  • Factors include farmer's markets, gyms, parks, and air quality.
  • The ranking is based on data from sources like the American Fitness Index and WalletHub.
  • Travel + Leisure emphasizes walkability and bikeability as key metrics.

Where you live shapes how you live. For Americans chasing a healthier lifestyle, zip code matters as much as diet and exercise. Travel + Leisure has released its latest ranking of the best U.S. cities for healthy living, and the list leans heavily on access to nature, fresh food, and active commuting.

What Makes a City Healthy?

The ranking draws on data from the American Fitness Index, WalletHub, and local health departments. Key metrics include the number of farmer's markets per capita, gym density, park acreage, and air quality scores. Walkability and bikeability are weighted heavily – cities where you can ditch the car score higher.

Mental health resources also figured into the final cut. The report notes that stress levels and access to green spaces directly affect chronic disease rates. The result is a mix of coastal hubs, mountain towns, and Midwest surprises.

The Top Spots

Boulder, Colorado, takes the top spot. The city boasts over 300 days of sunshine annually, a network of hiking trails, and a strong farm-to-table dining scene. Residents here walk or bike to work at rates five times the national average.

Honolulu, Hawaii, ranks second. The tropical climate allows year-round outdoor activity – surfing, hiking, and swimming are woven into daily life. The city also leads in access to fresh seafood and local produce.

Portland, Oregon, comes in third. Known for its bike lanes and vegan-friendly eateries, Portland has more farmer's markets per person than any other major U.S. city. Its urban growth boundary keeps green spaces close.

Other Notable Entries

Minneapolis, Minnesota, cracks the top five. Despite harsh winters, the city invests heavily in indoor recreation centers and heated bike paths. Its network of lakes and parks makes summer active living a draw.

San Francisco, California, places sixth. High cost of living is offset by excellent walkability and a strong culture of wellness. The city has one of the highest yoga studio densities in the country.

Denver, Colorado, and Seattle, Washington, also feature. Both offer robust outdoor recreation and clean air, though traffic and housing costs remain challenges. Denver's altitude even adds a cardio benefit, experts note.

The Role of Community Design

The report underscores that healthy living isn't just personal willpower – it's infrastructure. Cities that prioritize bike lanes, public transit, and mixed-use zoning see lower obesity rates. Walkable neighborhoods encourage incidental exercise, like walking to the store or a café.

Air quality emerged as a major differentiator. Cities with strict emissions standards and ample tree canopy performed better. Honolulu and Boulder both score high on air quality indexes, while some industrial Midwest cities lag behind.

What This Means for You

If you're considering a move for health reasons, the report suggests focusing on cities that align with your lifestyle. Outdoor enthusiasts might prefer Boulder or Portland, while those seeking year-round sun might lean toward Honolulu. Budget-conscious buyers should note that Minneapolis and Denver offer lower costs than coastal peers.

Travel + Leisure's list is a starting point. Local factors like neighborhood safety and commute times also matter. The healthiest city on paper may not fit everyone's reality.

The ranking will likely shift as cities invest in wellness infrastructure. Watch for updates from the American Fitness Index later this year, as more metros compete for the title of America's healthiest zip code.
