US loses 23,000 jobs in July as labour force participation drops

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • US economy shed 23,000 jobs in July, a steeper loss than expected.
  • Unemployment rate ticked lower, but labour force participation slumped.
  • Rate futures now price lower odds of a September Fed rate cut.
  • The weak report lands as a political setback for the Trump administration.

The US labour market lost steam in July, with employers cutting 23,000 jobs, according to fresh data. The decline marks a sharp reversal from recent months and has injected fresh uncertainty into the economic outlook.

Unemployment ticked lower, but for reasons that are less comforting than they appear. A slump in labour force participation — the share of working-age Americans either employed or actively job-hunting — drove the drop, masking underlying weakness in hiring.

Job losses across sectors

The 23,000 net decline in nonfarm payrolls came as a surprise. Economists had pencilled in modest gains, and the miss has reignited concerns about the durability of the expansion.

Details on which industries shed the most workers were not immediately available, but the broad-based softness suggests weakening demand across the economy.

Unemployment lower, but for the wrong reason

The unemployment rate moved lower in July, yet the improvement was not born of robust job creation. Instead, fewer people are actively seeking work, pulling down the participation rate.

That dynamic complicates the narrative of a resilient labour market. A shrinking workforce can ease unemployment statistics even as overall employment stalls.

Rate-cut bets retreat

US rate futures reacted swiftly. Traders trimmed the probability of a September rate hike by the Federal Reserve, according to Reuters, after the jobs data landed.

The Fed has been walking a tightrope between containing inflation and supporting growth. A softer labour market weakens the case for tighter policy, but the participation slump muddies the signal.

Political fallout

The report delivers a political setback to the Trump administration, which has touted job creation as a key achievement. The Associated Press framed the stall as a blow to the White House's economic narrative.

With the 2026 midterm cycle heating up, the labour market's trajectory is set to become an even sharper political battleground.

What the numbers can't explain

As CNN noted, the jobs report cannot fully capture this confounding economic moment. Employers are navigating high interest rates, shifting consumer demand, and lingering uncertainty about the path ahead.

Participation rates, in particular, remain a puzzle — demographic shifts, health concerns, and early retirements are all in play, but the data alone cannot disentangle them.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether July's dip is a one-off or the start of a sustained cooling. Economists will be watching August figures closely for clues.

The next jobs report is due in early September, and it will be scrutinised for any sign of stabilisation — or further deterioration. For now, the labour market is sending mixed signals, and the Fed's next move remains contingent on how those numbers evolve.
