Epic Games and Google Withdraw Settlement as Third-Party App Stores Arrive on Android

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google and Epic Games have withdrawn their proposed settlement after a US judge mandated third-party app stores on Android.
  • Starting July 22, Android users will be able to install rival app stores directly, bypassing Google Play Store.
  • The order stems from Epic's 2020 antitrust lawsuit over Google's app store monopoly and high commission fees.

In a major shake-up for the Android ecosystem, Google and Epic Games have formally withdrawn their previously proposed settlement, clearing the way for third-party app stores to launch on the platform starting July 22. The decision follows a US federal judge's order requiring Google to open its mobile operating system to rival marketplaces — a direct result of the high-profile antitrust battle between the two companies.

What the Court Order Means

The judge's ruling, handed down earlier this year, found that Google had illegally maintained a monopoly over Android app distribution and in-app payment processing. As a remedy, the court mandated that Google must allow third-party app stores to be installed on Android devices without interference.

From next week, Android users will be able to download and install alternative app stores — such as the Epic Games Store — directly from their browsers or through other distribution methods. Google can no longer block these stores or impose warning screens that discourage installation.

The order also prohibits Google from requiring developers to use its own billing system. Developers can now direct users to their own payment portals, potentially avoiding the 15% to 30% commission fee that Google charges on Play Store transactions.

Epic Games and Google Drop Their Settlement

Both companies had been negotiating a settlement to resolve the antitrust dispute out of court. However, with the judge's order now in effect, the settlement has become moot. The two sides jointly moved to withdraw their proposed agreement, which had included terms for a temporary truce and a framework for future negotiations.

Legal experts say the withdrawal indicates that both parties accept the court's mandate as the new baseline for competition on Android. Google has not publicly commented on whether it will appeal the order, but the company has previously argued that opening the platform to third-party stores could compromise security and user experience.

Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney has long criticised Google's Play Store policies as anticompetitive. The company launched its own antitrust lawsuit in 2020, accusing Google of using its market power to stifle competition.

What Changes for Android Users and Developers

For the average Android user, the change means more choice. Instead of being limited to the Google Play Store, users can install the Epic Games Store, the Amazon Appstore, or other rival marketplaces. These stores will offer their own curated selections of apps, games, and in some cases, exclusive titles.

For developers, the shift is significant. They can now bypass Google's commission entirely by distributing their apps through third-party stores or directly from their own websites. Smaller developers, in particular, may benefit from lower fees and greater control over their revenue.

However, Google has warned that sideloading apps — installing them from outside the Play Store — could expose users to malware or untrustworthy software. The company says it will continue to invest in security features like Google Play Protect to scan sideloaded apps for threats.

The Road Ahead

The July 22 deadline marks the beginning of a new era for Android. Third-party app stores are expected to roll out gradually, with the Epic Games Store likely to be among the first available. Other players, including Microsoft and Amazon, may follow suit.

Industry observers will watch closely to see how Google responds — whether it appeals the order, adjusts its policies, or introduces new security measures that could limit the impact of rival stores. For now, the battle over app store monopolies has taken a decisive turn, and the consequences will ripple across the global mobile ecosystem.
