Rajasthan police's public shaming violates law, human rights

📅 2026-08-31 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Police in Rajasthan publicly humiliate accused, violating legal norms
  • NGO documents 12 cases of humiliation, with 8 resulting in deaths
  • High Court orders probe, but no arrests or action against officers
  • Experts warn this undermines justice, fuels public outrage
  • Rajasthan government denies allegations, blames 'misinterpretation'

Brutal Humiliation Tactics

Police in Rajasthan are publicly humiliating accused individuals, a practice that violates both legal norms and human dignity. An NGO has documented 12 cases where police have used humiliation tactics, with 8 of these cases resulting in deaths. The tactics include forced nudity, public shaming, and even being made to eat excrement.

Legal and Ethical Violations

These practices are in direct violation of the Indian Penal Code and the Code of Criminal Procedure. The Supreme Court has repeatedly emphasized the importance of maintaining human dignity during police interrogations. Yet, despite these clear legal violations, the police continue with these practices.

Government Response and Criticism

The Rajasthan government has denied the allegations, stating that the NGO's report is based on 'misinterpretation' of events. However, experts argue that these practices are not only illegal but also serve to undermine the very system of justice that the police are supposed to uphold.

  • Pros: Can serve as a deterrent to crime, can bring about a sense of justice.

Conclusion

These practices are not only illegal but also serve to undermine the very system of justice that the police are supposed to uphold. It is high time that the government takes stringent action against the erring officers and ensures that such practices are stopped immediately.
