Sensex, Nifty Extend Losses as Financials, Private Banks Drag

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty extended losses on August 7, 2026.
  • Financial and private bank stocks were the main drags.
  • Market breadth was negative, with most sectors trading lower.

Markets Open Lower, Extending Previous Session's Decline

Indian equity benchmarks opened lower on Friday, August 7, 2026, extending losses from the previous session. The BSE Sensex and the NSE Nifty both slipped in early trade, dragged by selling pressure in financial and private banking stocks.

The weakness in the market comes amid a lack of fresh positive triggers. Investors appeared cautious, with global cues offering little support.

Financials and Private Banks Lead the Slide

Shares of private sector banks and financial institutions were the biggest contributors to the indices' decline. Heavyweights in the banking space traded in the red, pulling down the overall market sentiment.

Analysts said the selling was broad-based within the financial segment, with both large and mid-sized lenders facing pressure. The Nifty Bank index underperformed the benchmark indices.

Broader Market Also Under Pressure

The broader market also mirrored the weak trend, with midcap and smallcap stocks trading lower. Sectorally, most indices on the National Stock Exchange were in the red, except for a few defensive pockets.

Market breadth was negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. This suggested that the selling pressure was not limited to specific sectors.

Investors Await Global Cues and Earnings

Traders are now looking ahead to global cues, including the US Federal Reserve's policy stance and any fresh data points. Domestic earnings season is also being watched for further direction.

Any unexpected geopolitical development or a sharp move in crude oil prices could influence the market's trajectory in the coming sessions.

Market participants are advised to keep a close watch on the movement of the rupee and foreign institutional investor flows.

The next major trigger for the market will be the release of key economic data and any commentary from the Reserve Bank of India. Until then, volatility is likely to remain elevated.
