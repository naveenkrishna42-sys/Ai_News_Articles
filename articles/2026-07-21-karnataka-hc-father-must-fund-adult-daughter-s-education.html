Karnataka HC: Father must fund adult daughter's education

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Karnataka High Court holds father liable for adult daughter's education expenses
  • Ruling emphasizes parental duty under Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act
  • Judgment sets precedent for financial support of adult children in education

Court affirms father's financial duty

The Karnataka High Court has ruled that a father is legally obligated to bear the educational expenses of his adult daughter. The decision came in a case where a young woman sought maintenance from her father to pursue higher studies.

Justice M. Nagaprasanna, who presided over the matter, made it clear that a father's obligation does not end when a daughter turns 18. The court observed that education is a fundamental right and cannot be denied due to financial disputes between parents.

Case details and legal basis

The petitioner, a 21-year-old woman, had approached the court after her father refused to pay for her college fees. She argued that she was financially dependent on him and that he had the means to support her education.

The father contended that he had no legal duty to maintain an adult child. However, the court rejected this argument, citing Section 20 of the Hindu Adoption and Maintenance Act, 1956. This provision mandates that a parent must maintain a child, whether minor or adult, if the child is unable to maintain themselves.

Education seen as essential need

The bench noted that education in modern times is not a luxury but a necessity. “An educated daughter is an asset to society and the nation,” the court remarked, while dismissing the father's objections.

The ruling aligns with earlier Supreme Court judgments that expanded the scope of maintenance. The court directed the father to pay a specific sum towards the daughter's annual tuition and hostel fees until she completes her degree.

Broader implications

Legal experts say this judgment reinforces a parent's duty beyond mere subsistence. It sends a clear signal that courts will intervene when a child's educational prospects are jeopardised by parental neglect or refusal to pay.

The ruling applies primarily under Hindu personal law, but similar principles exist under other personal laws and the general law of maintenance. The court also noted that the father's financial capacity was not in dispute, making the order enforceable.

The judgment has been welcomed by women's rights groups. They argue that financial dependence should not become a barrier to a girl's career and independence.

What happens next: The father has been given time to comply with the order. If he fails, the daughter can seek enforcement through the family court. The ruling may also influence similar cases pending in other courts across the state.
