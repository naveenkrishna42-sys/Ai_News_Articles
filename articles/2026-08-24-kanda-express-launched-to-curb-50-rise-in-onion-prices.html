Key points

Rising Onion Prices Hit Consumers

Onions, a staple across India, saw a steep climb of nearly 50% last month, pushing grocery bills higher for families in the middle and lower income brackets. The surge rattled distributors, prompting the central government to announce a swift intervention.

Government’s Kanda Express: A Rapid Response?

Starting today, the Ministry of Food Processing Industries will run Kanda Express, a dedicated freight service that will transport surplus onion stock from surplus‑producing states to demand centres. The scheme promises a quick turnaround, aiming to dump buffer stock before prices soar further.

Timing and Logistics: How the Scheme Works

The plan unfolds in three phases: first, the buffer stock, held by state agencies, is identified and pooled. Second, rail and road freight units are dispatched through a pre‑arranged schedule. Third, the onions reach wholesale markets, where price supports are monitored. The whole process is designed to fit within a 48‑hour window.

Potential Impact and Risks

By injecting supply early, the scheme should ease price pressure in the next two weeks. However, the effectiveness hinges on timely delivery and the market’s response. If logistics falter, the onions could still sit idle, negating the benefit.

There is also a risk of price dumping, which could hurt small traders who rely on premium prices during peak demand. The Ministry will monitor price floors to avoid an inadvertent crash.

Expert Take

Food policy analyst Dr. R. K. Singh notes that “buffer stock releases act as a safety net when production lags behind demand.” He adds that the critical factor is speed: “The government’s ability to move stock within 48 hours is a significant advantage over traditional market channels.”

However, Singh cautions that “without a complementary price‑support mechanism, the scheme alone may not sustain long‑term stability.” He recommends pairing the express freight with targeted subsidies for small traders.
