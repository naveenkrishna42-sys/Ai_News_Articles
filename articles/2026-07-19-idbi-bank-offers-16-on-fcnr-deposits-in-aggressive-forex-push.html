IDBI Bank Offers 16% on FCNR Deposits in Aggressive Forex Push

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • IDBI Bank is offering up to 16% on FCNR deposits, significantly above prevailing market rates.
  • The RBI scheme allows banks to raise foreign currency deposits without hedging costs, making high rates viable.
  • Industry estimates suggest NRIs could bring in $70-80 billion through this window.
  • Malayalis in the Gulf are a key driver of inflows, according to reports.

IDBI Bank has put a high-stakes bet on Non-Resident Indian (NRI) deposits, offering up to 16 per cent returns on Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposits. The aggressive rate is part of a special window opened by the Reserve Bank of India that lets banks raise foreign currency funds without the usual hedging costs.

The offer is among the highest in the banking sector right now. For context, most FCNR deposits have been yielding between 5 and 8 per cent in the open market. IDBI's move signals a clear intent to grab a larger share of the NRI remittance pie, especially from the Gulf region.

How the RBI scheme makes this possible

The central bank's special dispensation allows banks to set FCNR deposit rates without linking them to the London Interbank Offered Rate (LIBOR) or other benchmarks. More crucially, banks are exempt from the requirement to hedge the currency risk on these deposits. That exemption removes a major cost, making high headline rates financially viable for lenders.

State Bank of India has already mobilised about $1.9 billion through this window. Axis Bank has also indicated that FCNR flows present a large opportunity and expects its share of foreign deposit flows to exceed its organic market share.

Gulf money and the Malayali factor

A significant chunk of these inflows is coming from Indian workers in the Gulf. The New Indian Express has reported that Malayalis in the Gulf region are leading the way, responding strongly to the attractive deposit rates. The Gulf remains the largest source of remittances to India, and banks are tapping into that well-established channel.

Industry experts estimate that NRIs could bring in between $70 billion and $80 billion through this FCNR initiative. That would provide a substantial cushion for India's foreign exchange reserves, which have been under pressure from global monetary tightening and trade imbalances.

A race for dollar deposits

IDBI Bank's 16 per cent offer is not just a headline-grabber. It reflects a broader scramble among Indian lenders to lock in dollar liquidity. The RBI scheme has a limited window, and banks are moving fast to raise as much as they can before it closes.

For NRIs, the calculus is simple: risk-free dollar deposits in an Indian bank with returns that far exceed what most international banks offer. The hedging waiver means the bank bears the currency risk, not the depositor. That combination is proving hard to resist.

Bankers caution, however, that such aggressive rates may not last. Once the special window closes, FCNR rates are expected to normalise. For now, the window is open, and the competition is fierce.

The coming weeks will show whether IDBI Bank can match SBI's haul of nearly $2 billion. If it does, the 16 per cent offer will have done its job. If not, the bank may have to rethink its pricing strategy. Either way, the FCNR deposit race is far from over.
