Samsung cuts hundreds of US jobs, offers relocations ahead of HQ shift

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Samsung Electronics America is cutting 739 positions in New Jersey.
  • The company is relocating its consumer electronics headquarters from New Jersey to another US location.
  • Employees affected by the cuts have been offered relocation options as part of the restructuring.

Job cuts and HQ relocation

Samsung Electronics America is cutting hundreds of jobs in the United States as it shifts its consumer electronics headquarters out of New Jersey. The South Korean tech giant has confirmed it will eliminate 739 positions in the state, according to a regulatory filing.

The company has offered affected employees the option to relocate to other offices as part of the restructuring. The move comes as Samsung looks to consolidate its US operations and respond to changing market conditions.

Details of the layoffs

The cuts are concentrated in New Jersey, where Samsung's consumer electronics division has been based for years. The 739 job losses represent a significant portion of the company's workforce in the state.

Samsung has not disclosed the exact number of employees who have accepted relocation offers. The company is expected to finalise the headquarters move in the coming months.

Broader business challenges

The restructuring comes amid a challenging period for Samsung's mobile business globally. The company has faced stiff competition from Chinese rivals like Xiaomi and Oppo, as well as Apple in the premium segment.

In the US market, Samsung's smartphone market share has been under pressure. The company has also been grappling with a slowdown in consumer electronics demand, which has affected its television and home appliance sales.

What this means for employees

Workers who are not willing to relocate face layoffs with severance packages. Samsung has not publicly detailed the terms of the severance or the timeline for the job cuts.

Local officials in New Jersey have expressed concern over the loss of jobs. The state's economy has been gradually recovering, and the Samsung cuts add to a string of corporate downsizing announcements in the region.

Industry analysts note that the restructuring is part of a larger trend of tech companies reassessing their real estate needs. Remote work and hybrid models have led many firms to reduce office space and centralise operations.

Watch for further announcements from Samsung regarding the new headquarters location and any additional restructuring in other US offices. The company's next quarterly earnings report in October will likely provide more clarity on the financial impact of these moves.
