Bengaluru's first flyover gets Rs 9.6cr renovation

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • The 51-year-old flyover will undergo a major renovation costing Rs 9.6 crore.
  • The project aims to strengthen the structure and enhance safety for commuters.
  • Work is expected to begin soon, with minimal disruption planned for traffic.

Bengaluru's first flyover, a relic of the city's rapid urbanisation from the 1970s, is finally getting a much-needed facelift. The civic body has sanctioned Rs 9.6 crore for a comprehensive makeover of the structure, which has served millions of commuters for over five decades.

The flyover, which opened to traffic in 1975, was a pioneering infrastructure project in the city. Over the years, it has weathered heavy monsoon rains, dense traffic, and the wear and tear of daily use. Now, engineers say the structure needs urgent attention to ensure its longevity.

A Landmark Nears Its 51st Year

This flyover holds a special place in Bengaluru's history. It was the first of its kind in the city, built to ease congestion at one of the busiest junctions. At the time, it was a symbol of progress and modernity, drawing curious onlookers who had never seen a road elevated above the ground.

Today, it stands as a testament to an era when the city's population was a fraction of what it is now. The structure has silently witnessed Bengaluru's transformation from a pensioner's paradise to India's tech capital.

What the Makeover Entails

The Rs 9.6 crore project will focus on strengthening the flyover's foundations, deck, and bearings. Officials have also flagged the need to repair expansion joints, which have suffered due to constant thermal expansion and heavy vehicle movement.

Workers will also resurface the road and improve drainage systems to prevent waterlogging during the monsoon. Safety features, including crash barriers and lighting, will be upgraded to meet current standards.

Minimising Disruption

Civic authorities are aware that any prolonged closure could worsen traffic chaos in the area. As a result, they plan to carry out the renovation in phases, with partial lane closures during off-peak hours. Commuters can expect some inconvenience, but officials have assured that the project will be executed with minimal disruption.

The exact timeline for the work has not been disclosed yet. However, similar projects in other parts of the city have taken anywhere from six months to a year to complete.

Residents and daily commuters have welcomed the move, though many have raised concerns about the contractor's track record. The civic body has yet to name the agency that will execute the project.

Ageing Infrastructure Across the City

This flyover is not alone in showing signs of age. Many of Bengaluru's older bridges and underpasses are in need of similar repairs. Experts argue that a proactive maintenance schedule is essential to prevent sudden failures, which can have catastrophic consequences.

In recent years, the city has seen several incidents of bridge girders cracking and road surfaces caving in. The renovation of the first flyover could set a precedent for how the city handles its ageing infrastructure.

For now, the focus is on getting the work started. Once completed, the flyover is expected to serve the city for another few decades, though officials have not specified its intended lifespan after the repair.

As Bengaluru continues to grow, maintaining its old structures will be as important as building new ones. The coming months will show how well the city balances heritage with modernity.
