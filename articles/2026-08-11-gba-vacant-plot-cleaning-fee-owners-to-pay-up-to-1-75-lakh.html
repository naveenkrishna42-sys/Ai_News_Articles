GBA vacant plot cleaning fee: owners to pay up to ₹1.75 lakh

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • GBA to recover cleaning costs from owners of vacant plots
  • Fees range from ₹25,900 to ₹1.75 lakh per plot
  • Move aimed at tackling garbage and encroachment on idle land

Proposed fee structure for neglected plots

The Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) has floated a proposal that could make owners of vacant plots pay for cleanliness. If the plan goes through, those who fail to maintain their land could be billed between ₹25,900 and ₹1.75 lakh for cleaning operations carried out by the civic body.

The exact amount would depend on the size and location of the plot, officials said. The move is designed to pressure owners into keeping their land free of debris, weeds, and illegal dumping, which have become a common eyesore across the city.

Why the GBA is stepping in

Vacant plots in Bengaluru have long been a magnet for garbage dumping, stray vehicle parking, and encroachments. Residents’ groups have repeatedly complained that such sites turn into breeding grounds for mosquitoes and rodents, posing health risks to neighbourhoods.

The GBA’s proposal aims to shift the burden of maintenance squarely onto owners. Instead of the civic body spending public money to clean private land, it would recover the costs directly from those who own the plots.

Officials have not yet confirmed how many plots could be affected or when the fee structure might be finalised. A public consultation process is expected before any notification is issued.

How the fee will be calculated

The proposed charges are not uniform. Sources indicate that smaller plots would attract the lower end of the scale, while larger or commercially located plots would face the maximum levy of ₹1.75 lakh.

The GBA is also considering a penalty clause for repeat offenders. Owners who ignore the cleaning bill could face additional fines or legal action, though details of such provisions have not been spelled out.

Mixed reactions from owners and residents

Some property owners have welcomed the proposal, arguing that it would deter unscrupulous elements from using their land as dumping grounds. Others, however, say the fee is too steep, especially for those who are already struggling with property taxes and maintenance costs.

Residents’ welfare associations, on the other hand, have largely backed the move. They believe it will force absentee owners to either fence their plots or engage private cleaners, reducing the civic burden.

The GBA has not yet released an official cost breakdown for its cleaning operations. It says the proposed charges are based on current market rates for debris removal, labour, and transportation of waste.

What happens next

The proposal will now be tabled before the GBA board for discussion. If approved, it will be sent to the state government for final clearance, following which a public notice will be issued.

Plot owners who wish to avoid the fee can do so by keeping their land clean and complying with existing maintenance rules. The GBA has indicated that it will conduct inspections to identify defaulters before initiating any recovery process.

Watch for the board’s decision in the coming weeks, as it could set a precedent for other cities grappling with similar urban cleanliness challenges.
