Milky Way was flipped upside down in ancient galactic crash, astronomers say

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Milky Way's disk may have been flipped by more than 90 degrees due to a collision with the Gaia Sausage dwarf galaxy around 10 billion years ago.
  • The event, known as the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus merger, is considered a major chapter in our galaxy's early history.
  • Astronomers used data from the Gaia space telescope to track the motion of stars and reconstruct the ancient tilt.

Ancient crash tilted our galaxy

Our Milky Way galaxy was not always the stable, spiraling disk we see today. Around 10 billion years ago, it collided with a smaller dwarf galaxy known as the Gaia Sausage. The impact was so violent that it flipped the Milky Way's disk by more than 90 degrees, according to new research.

Astronomers have long known that our galaxy has a violent past. Mergers with smaller galaxies were common in the early universe. But the scale of this particular event has surprised many. The findings were published recently and have been covered by multiple outlets, including The Times of India and The Guardian.

What the Gaia data revealed

The key to this discovery lies in data from the European Space Agency's Gaia space telescope. Gaia has been mapping the positions and motions of nearly two billion stars in the Milky Way. By analyzing these precise movements, scientists were able to reconstruct the galaxy's ancient history.

Researchers found that stars in the galactic halo — the outer region of the Milky Way — show a distinct pattern. This pattern matches what would happen if the entire disk of the galaxy had been tilted by a massive collision. The tilt was not gradual. It was a sudden, dramatic flip.

The culprit: Gaia Sausage dwarf galaxy

The dwarf galaxy responsible has been named the Gaia Sausage, or more formally, the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus. It was a small galaxy that merged with the Milky Way about 8 to 11 billion years ago. The name comes from the sausage-like shape of the orbits of stars it left behind.

This merger is already known to have contributed many stars to the Milky Way's halo. But the new research suggests its impact was even more profound. It appears to have physically reoriented the entire galactic disk.

Even the Sun's path changed

The flip was not just a cosmic curiosity. It had real consequences for everything in the galaxy. The tilt changed the orbits of stars, including the Sun. Our solar system's path through the galaxy was altered by this ancient crash.

Astronomers are still working out the exact details. But the evidence points to a galaxy that was once pointing in a very different direction. Over time, the disk slowly settled back into its current orientation. But the scars of that ancient collision remain imprinted in the motions of stars today.

Officials have not confirmed whether any other galaxies in the local group experienced similar flips. The research is ongoing, and more data from Gaia is expected in the coming years.

What happens next will depend on further analysis of Gaia's data and computer simulations. Scientists hope to pinpoint the exact timing and mechanics of the flip. For now, the picture is clear: our galaxy was once turned upside down by a smaller neighbor, and it has never been quite the same since.
