Sensex, Nifty Fall on July 20: Key Reasons Behind the Market Decline

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 624 points to 79,895; Nifty dropped 188 points to 24,337.
  • IT stocks were the biggest laggards, with TCS, Infosys, and HCL Tech down 2-3%.
  • Weak global cues from US and European markets added selling pressure.
  • Rising crude oil prices and FII outflows contributed to the bearish sentiment.

Indian stock markets witnessed a sharp sell-off on Monday, July 20, with benchmark indices closing deep in the red. The BSE Sensex tumbled 624 points to end at 79,895, while the NSE Nifty slipped 188 points to settle at 24,337. The fall wiped off nearly Rs 3 lakh crore from investor wealth in a single session.

IT stocks lead the decline

Information technology stocks were the biggest drag on the indices. Heavyweights like Tata Consultancy Services (TCS), Infosys, and HCL Technologies fell between 2% and 3% during the day. Analysts attributed the sell-off to renewed concerns over demand in the US and European markets—key revenue regions for Indian IT firms.

The IT sector index on the NSE dropped over 2%, making it the worst-performing sector of the day. Broader indices also ended lower, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices falling around 1% each.

Global cues turn weak

Weakness in global markets added to the selling pressure at home. US stock futures pointed to a lower open on Monday, while European indices traded in the red. Investor sentiment took a hit after disappointing corporate earnings from some major US tech companies, which raised fears of a slowdown in the global economy.

Asian markets also ended mixed, with Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng closing lower. The cautious mood overseas prompted foreign institutional investors (FIIs) to trim their positions in Indian equities.

Crude oil and FII outflows

Rising crude oil prices further dampened sentiment. Brent crude hovered around $82 a barrel, stoking concerns about higher import costs for India, a net importer of oil. Higher fuel prices could stoke inflation and put pressure on the Reserve Bank of India to keep interest rates elevated.

Foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) were net sellers of Indian equities on Monday, offloading shares worth Rs 1,200 crore in the cash segment, according to provisional exchange data. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) bought shares worth Rs 950 crore, but that was not enough to stem the decline.

What triggered the sell-off in IT stocks?

Market participants pointed to a mix of factors. The US Federal Reserve's hawkish stance on interest rates has made investors cautious about high-growth tech stocks globally. Additionally, some brokerages downgraded Indian IT stocks on valuation concerns, citing slower-than-expected deal wins in the June quarter.

TCS, Infosys, and HCL Tech together account for a significant weight in the Nifty 50, and their fall dragged the index lower. The banking index also ended in the red, with HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank losing around 0.5% each.

Broader market impact

The sell-off was not limited to large-caps. Midcap and smallcap stocks also saw profit-booking after a strong run in recent weeks. The Nifty Midcap 100 index fell 0.8%, and the Nifty Smallcap 100 index dropped 1.1%. Sectoral indices like auto, pharma, and FMCG also ended lower, reflecting broad-based weakness.

Market breadth was negative, with more than two stocks declining for every one advancing on the BSE. The volatility index, India VIX, rose 5% to 14.5, indicating increased nervousness among traders.

Investors will watch for cues from US markets overnight and corporate earnings from Indian companies due later this week for direction. Any escalation in global trade tensions or a further spike in oil prices could keep the market under pressure in the coming sessions.
