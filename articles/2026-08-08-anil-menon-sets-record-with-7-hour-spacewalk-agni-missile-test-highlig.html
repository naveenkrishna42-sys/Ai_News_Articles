Anil Menon sets record with 7-hour spacewalk; Agni missile test highlights

Historic Spacewalk for Solar Upgrades

NASA astronauts Anil Menon and Jessica Meir stepped outside the International Space Station (ISS) on Friday for a marathon spacewalk lasting seven hours. The primary objective was to modify the station's power systems, paving the way for new solar array installations scheduled in the coming months.

The duo worked meticulously on the station's truss structure, loosening bolts and repositioning equipment to accommodate the upgraded arrays. NASA officials confirmed the spacewalk was completed successfully, with all key tasks accomplished ahead of schedule.

Menon, who is of Indian origin, has now etched his name in the record books — this being the longest spacewalk by an astronaut of Indian descent. The feat has sparked celebrations both in the United States and in India, where space enthusiasts have hailed it as a milestone for the country's growing presence in human spaceflight.

Preparing for the Future of ISS Power

The spacewalk was part of a series of excursions aimed at boosting the ISS's electrical capacity. The new solar arrays, once installed, will provide a significant power increase, supporting expanded scientific experiments and future commercial activities on the orbital laboratory.

NASA had earlier outlined a multi-phase plan to upgrade the station's aging solar wings. Friday's work involved preparing the mounting brackets and securing the pathways for the roll-out arrays, which are lighter and more efficient than their predecessors.

Jessica Meir, a veteran of several spacewalks, worked alongside Menon with precision. The pair coordinated every move with ground controllers in Houston, ensuring safety protocols were followed throughout the seven-hour excursion.

India's Agni Missile Test

In a separate development, India's strategic forces conducted a successful test of the Agni ballistic missile, a key component of the country's nuclear deterrence posture. The test, which took place earlier this week, was part of routine user trials and validated the missile's reliability and accuracy.

Defence sources said the missile followed its expected trajectory and met all mission parameters. The Agni series, developed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), forms the backbone of India's land-based nuclear delivery systems, with ranges varying from short to intercontinental.

The timing of the test has drawn attention, coming amid regional security discussions. However, officials have stressed that such trials are a normal part of military readiness and not aimed at any specific nation.

Significance for India's Strategic Capabilities

The Agni test underscores India's commitment to a credible minimum deterrence, a policy that has remained consistent across successive governments. Experts note that the missile's performance is critical for maintaining strategic stability in the region.

With both the spacewalk and the missile test grabbing headlines, August 8 has become a day of notable achievements for India in science and defence. The spacewalk, in particular, has inspired a new generation of Indians to look toward the stars, while the missile test reaffirms the country's technological self-reliance.

As NASA prepares for the next phase of solar array installations, Menon's record will likely stand for some time. Meanwhile, India's defence establishment is expected to continue its scheduled test programme, with more launches planned in the coming months.

All eyes will now be on the next spacewalk, tentatively scheduled for later this month, and on any further announcements from the DRDO regarding upcoming Agni variants. For now, the achievements of August 8 serve as a reminder of human ingenuity and strategic foresight.
