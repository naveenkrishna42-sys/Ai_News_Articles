Why the Choice Matters

Investors with ₹30 lakh often look for safe, interest‑bearing instruments that generate a reliable monthly cash flow. Fixed Deposits (FDs), the Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS) and the Post Office Monthly Income Scheme (MIS) dominate the low‑risk segment. Each product differs in tenure, interest rate, tax treatment and liquidity, which directly affect the net monthly payout.

Real‑World Buyer Implications

1. Fixed Deposits – Flexibility at a Cost

💳 Apply for Lifetime Free Card (Instant Approval)

FDs are the most accessible product; any bank account holder can open one. The 6.75% rate translates to a modest ₹1.68 lakh monthly payout before tax. Early withdrawal penalties (usually 0.5%–1% of principal) erode returns, making FDs less attractive for investors whose primary goal is steady cash flow.

2. SCSS – Premium Yield for Seniors

💳 Apply for Lifetime Free Card (Instant Approval)

SCSS offers the highest nominal return. Quarterly compounding boosts the effective annual yield to roughly 8.2%, delivering around ₹2 lakh per month on ₹30 lakh. The scheme’s partial‑withdrawal clause after one year provides limited liquidity, but senior investors typically value the higher income over absolute flexibility.

3. Post Office MIS – Balanced Payout with Lower Tax Drag

💳 Apply for Lifetime Free Card (Instant Approval)

The MIS pays interest monthly, simplifying cash‑flow planning. At 7.40% p.a., the net monthly income sits between FD and SCSS levels. Because the Post Office does not deduct TDS for modest interest, senior citizens with lower taxable income may retain a larger share of the ₹1.85 lakh payout.

Practical Benchmarks and Pricing Context

All three instruments are government‑backed or bank‑insured, so credit risk is negligible. However, the effective yield diverges once tax is accounted for. Assuming a 20% marginal tax rate, the post‑tax monthly incomes become:

  • FD: ₹1,35,000
  • SCSS: ₹1,60,000
  • Post Office MIS: ₹1,48,000

For investors in the 30% tax bracket, SCSS still leads, but the gap narrows, making MIS a compelling alternative if liquidity constraints are acceptable.

Pros, Cons and Key Takeaways

  • SCSS – Pros: Highest interest, decent partial‑withdrawal option; Cons: Age restriction, limited early‑exit flexibility.
  • Post Office MIS – Pros: Monthly payouts, lower tax drag for small investors; Cons: No premature withdrawal, limited to ₹4.5 lakh per holder.
  • Fixed Deposit – Pros: Unlimited principal, easy early exit; Cons: Lowest yield, TDS applies early.

Final Recommendation for a ₹30 Lakh Stake

If you are a senior citizen (≥ 60 years) seeking the maximum monthly cash flow, SCSS remains the clear winner despite its five‑year lock‑in. Non‑senior investors should weigh the Post Office MIS against a high‑interest FD; MIS offers a smoother monthly payout with marginally higher net income after tax. For those who value liquidity above all, a laddered FD strategy—splitting the ₹30 lakh across multiple tenures—can mitigate early‑withdrawal penalties while preserving a predictable cash stream.
