"I Had To Be My Own Father": Rahul Bhatt on independence and money

Rahul Bhatt has spoken publicly about a relationship with his father, filmmaker Mahesh Bhatt, that he describes as fractured. In an interview reported by NDTV, the actor said he grew up effectively parenting himself: "I had to be my own father." The remark reframes a conversation that usually stops at blame, pointing instead at a young man who built his own routines, ambitions and financial expectations without leaning on the family name.

That candour matters because the Bhatt family sits at a specific intersection of Bollywood privilege and public scrutiny. Mahesh Bhatt is one of Hindi cinema's most recognised directors and producers; his daughters Pooja Bhatt and Alia Bhatt are established names in their own right. Rahul, the eldest, has carved a narrower path through acting and fitness, and his comments suggest the distance between siblings is not only emotional but structural.

Who actually pays the bills

The most concrete claim in Rahul's remarks concerns money. Citing the interview, Hindustan Times reported Rahul saying Mahesh Bhatt bears the household expenses and cares for his mother, Kiran Bhatt, adding plainly: "I'm not the breadwinner." For buyers of celebrity interviews and for readers parsing family dynamics, this is the load-bearing detail. It separates Rahul's stated independence from financial self-sufficiency — he describes autonomy of identity, not autonomy of income.

Why the distinction changes the story

Read literally, the statement undercuts the harder-edged framing of a son abandoned by his father. It paints a household where the father still carries domestic responsibility while the son carries a psychological burden. Readers weighing these accounts should treat the two as compatible rather than contradictory: provision and emotional availability are different ledgers, and Bollywood families rarely reconcile them in public.

The Parveen Babi question and the family's public file

Rahul also addressed one of his father's most reported personal chapters — Mahesh Bhatt's widely documented relationship with actor Parveen Babi in the 1970s. India TV reports Rahul saying he was lucky not to hear about it. The answer is notable for what it avoids: he neither confirms a household rupture nor dismisses the reporting, keeping the family's most sensitive history at arm's length.

He has been more forthcoming about his siblings. The Indian Express quotes him calling Pooja Bhatt "more talented, intelligent than Alia Bhatt," while noting he has met Ranbir Kapoor five to seven times — a detail that situates him within, rather than outside, the film industry's social circuit.

Key real-world highlights for readers

  • Independence ≠ income: Rahul frames self-parenting as emotional, while acknowledging his father funds the household.
  • The word is "fractured": he does not claim total estrangement, which leaves room for reconciliation reporting.
  • Old history stays sealed: he declines to engage with the Parveen Babi narrative beyond a deflective remark.
  • Sibling comparisons invite pushback: ranking Pooja above Alia is an opinion that will be clipped and contested.
  • Verify before amplifying: multiple outlets carry fragments of the same interview; NDTV holds the primary quote.

Rahul's account is best read as one side of a two-sided family record. He claims emotional self-reliance while crediting his father with the practical load — a tension that explains the "negativity" he says has surrounded the bond. Any follow-up reporting should ask the same question of Mahesh Bhatt before the fracture hardens into a verdict.
