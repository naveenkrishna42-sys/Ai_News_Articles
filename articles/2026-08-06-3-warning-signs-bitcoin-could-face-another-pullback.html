3 Warning Signs Bitcoin Could Face Another Pullback

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin's price has remained resilient near recent highs, but underlying metrics suggest vulnerability.
  • On-chain data reveals a spike in profit-taking by long-term holders, often a precursor to corrections.
  • Derivatives markets show excessive leverage, with funding rates at elevated levels.
  • Technical indicators hint at fading momentum, with the RSI approaching overbought territory.

Momentum Stalls as Euphoria Builds

Bitcoin has spent the past few weeks hovering near its all-time high, and the mood among retail investors is unmistakably buoyant. Social media chatter is loud, trading volumes are solid, and every small dip gets bought up within hours. That kind of enthusiasm, however, has historically been a double-edged sword.

When the crowd gets this confident, the market often finds a way to humiliate it. Several metrics that track investor behaviour and derivative positioning are now flashing caution, suggesting the cryptocurrency could be setting up for another sharp pullback.

Long-Term Holders Start Cashing Out

One of the more telling signals comes from on-chain data that tracks the activity of long-term holders. These are wallets that have held bitcoin for at least six months and are generally considered the "smart money" of the ecosystem. After months of accumulation, this cohort has begun moving coins to exchanges in noticeable quantities.

Historically, such moves have preceded local tops. When long-term holders start realising profits after a sustained rally, it often marks the beginning of a distribution phase. The current trend suggests that some of the most patient investors are trimming their positions, which could add selling pressure in the coming weeks.

Profit-Taking at Cycle Peaks

This behaviour is not unusual at cycle peaks, but the speed of the recent increase is what catches the eye. The spike in spent output profit ratio, or SOPR, indicates that more coins are being sold at a profit than at any point in the past several months. While profit-taking is natural, a sustained surge can overwhelm demand and flip the price trajectory.

If the pattern follows previous cycles, the market may need a cooling-off period to absorb this supply. That could mean a retracement of 10-20 percent before the next leg up.

Leverage Is Piling Up in Derivatives

The derivatives market tells a similar story. Funding rates on major exchanges have climbed to levels that were last seen just before the previous correction. These rates represent the cost of holding long positions in perpetual futures contracts, and when they get too high, it signals that leveraged longs are paying a premium to stay in the game.

Such conditions are fragile. Any sudden drop in price can trigger a cascade of liquidations, forcing automated selling that amplifies the decline. The open interest in bitcoin futures has also surged, indicating that a large number of traders are betting on further upside. That crowded trade could unwind violently if the market turns.

Liquidation Risk Builds

A modest dip of even 5 percent could be enough to wipe out the most aggressive leveraged positions. In the past, such liquidation events have acted as catalysts for deeper corrections. The market's current positioning leaves little room for error.

Momentum Indicators Losing Steam

Technical charts are also sending mixed signals. The Relative Strength Index, or RSI, on the daily timeframe has been hovering near the overbought threshold for several days. While an overbought reading alone is not a sell signal, it does suggest that the buying momentum is slowing.

Trading volumes have started to taper off even as prices remain elevated, a divergence that often precedes a reversal. The market is essentially running on fumes, with fewer new buyers stepping in at these levels. If volume continues to dry up, the price may struggle to hold its ground.

Weak Hands May Be Shaken Out

Analysts point out that a pullback, if it comes, may not be catastrophic. Corrections are a healthy part of any bull market, shaking out weak hands and resetting leverage. The question is how deep and how fast the drop will be.

For now, the signs are accumulating. Between profit-taking by long-term holders, excessive leverage in derivatives, and fading technical momentum, there are enough red flags to warrant caution. But in crypto, anything can change in a day.

The coming week will be crucial. If bitcoin fails to break decisively above its recent high, the probability of a pullback increases significantly. Traders would be wise to watch funding rates and exchange inflows closely. A sudden spike in either could be the first domino to fall.
