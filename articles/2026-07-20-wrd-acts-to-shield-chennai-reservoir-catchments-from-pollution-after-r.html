WRD acts to shield Chennai reservoir catchments from pollution after resident complaints

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Residents reported encroachment, waste dumping, and sewage inflow into catchment areas.
  • WRD will construct boundary walls and deploy patrols to secure key reservoirs.
  • Measures target Poondi, Red Hills, Chembarambakkam, and Cholavaram lakes.

The Water Resources Department (WRD) has initiated steps to protect the catchment areas of Chennai's major reservoirs from pollution and encroachment. The move follows complaints from residents and local bodies about deteriorating water quality in these vital sources.

Residents flag encroachment and waste dumping

For months, residents living near the Poondi, Red Hills, Chembarambakkam, and Cholavaram reservoirs had raised alarms. They reported that parts of the catchment zones were being used for illegal dumping of construction debris and household waste.

Several areas also saw sewage flowing into the water bodies during rains. Encroachments by unauthorised structures further reduced the effective storage capacity of these lakes. Complaints were filed with the WRD and the Chennai Metropolitan Water Supply and Sewerage Board (CMWSSB).

WRD's response: walls, patrols, and signage

The WRD has now drawn up a multi-pronged action plan. The first step involves constructing permanent boundary walls around the most vulnerable stretches of the catchment areas. These walls will physically block vehicles from entering the zones to dump waste.

Regular patrols by WRD staff and local police have also been arranged. Watchtowers will be erected at key points to monitor illegal activity. Warning boards in Tamil and English are being put up to deter trespassers.

Officials said the department will coordinate with the Chennai Corporation and the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board (TNPCB) to identify and penalise repeat offenders. The TNPCB will test water samples more frequently to track contamination levels.

Focus on Poondi and Red Hills first

The initial phase of the project will concentrate on the Poondi and Red Hills reservoirs. These two supply a large portion of Chennai's drinking water. Work on the boundary walls has already begun in a few identified hotspots.

Chembarambakkam and Cholavaram will be taken up in the second phase. The WRD estimates that the entire fencing and patrolling system will be operational within six months, subject to fund availability.

Residents cautiously optimistic

Local resident welfare associations have welcomed the WRD's plan. They said constant vigilance is needed to prevent the catchments from being encroached upon again. Some activists pointed out that similar clean-up drives in the past lost momentum after a few months.

The WRD has assured that this time the measures will be sustained. A dedicated helpline will be set up for residents to report violations directly to the department.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total budget allocated for these works. They said a detailed estimate is being prepared and will be submitted to the state government for approval.

The next few months will test whether the WRD can translate its plan into lasting action. With Chennai's water supply increasingly dependent on these reservoirs, keeping their catchments clean is no longer optional.
