Serum Institute, Gates MRI Sign Pact for TB Vaccine Manufacture

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Serum Institute of India has signed an agreement with Gates MRI to manufacture an experimental tuberculosis vaccine.
  • The company has committed over USD 100 million for production and supply of the vaccine candidate.
  • The partnership aims to increase global access to TB prevention, especially in high-burden countries.

Pact Aims to Scale Up Experimental TB Vaccine

The Serum Institute of India (SII) has signed a manufacturing agreement with Gates MRI, a non-profit research organisation backed by the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, to produce an experimental tuberculosis vaccine. The deal involves a commitment of over USD 100 million from SII for production and supply of the vaccine candidate.

The vaccine, still in clinical testing, is being developed as a potential new tool against tuberculosis, which remains one of the deadliest infectious diseases worldwide. India accounts for a significant share of the global TB burden, and a new vaccine could reshape prevention strategies.

Why This Vaccine Matters

The only currently available TB vaccine is the Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine, developed a century ago. While it offers partial protection in children, its efficacy against pulmonary TB in adults is limited. The experimental vaccine being manufactured by SII aims to address this gap.

Gates MRI has been leading research into next-generation TB vaccines. The partnership with SII is expected to enable large-scale manufacturing at a lower cost, making the vaccine accessible to low- and middle-income countries where TB is most prevalent.

Financial and Production Commitments

SII has pledged over USD 100 crore (approximately USD 100 million) towards the production and supply of the vaccine. The company, already the world's largest vaccine manufacturer by volume, will use its existing infrastructure in Pune to produce the candidate.

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for commercial launch, as the vaccine must still clear late-stage clinical trials and receive regulatory approval. SII has stated that it is preparing for rapid scale-up once the candidate is approved.

Global Health Implications

Tuberculosis kills roughly 1.5 million people each year, according to the World Health Organization. Drug-resistant strains are a growing concern, and existing treatments require months of therapy. A highly effective vaccine could dramatically reduce both incidence and mortality.

The Gates MRI-SII collaboration is part of a broader push by global health organisations to accelerate TB vaccine development. Several candidates are in various stages of clinical trials, but this pact represents one of the largest manufacturing commitments for a single experimental vaccine.

Industry analysts note that SII's track record in producing low-cost vaccines, including those for COVID-19 and pneumococcal disease, makes it a crucial partner for any global immunisation effort.

What Comes Next

The vaccine candidate still needs to demonstrate safety and efficacy in ongoing Phase III trials. Results are expected within the next two years. If successful, SII's manufacturing capacity could allow for hundreds of millions of doses to be produced annually, potentially transforming TB prevention worldwide.
