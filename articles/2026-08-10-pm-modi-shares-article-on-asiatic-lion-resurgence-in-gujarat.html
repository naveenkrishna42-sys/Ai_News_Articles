PM Modi shares article on Asiatic lion resurgence in Gujarat

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi shared an article on the remarkable comeback of India's Asiatic lion.
  • Asiatic lion population rose from 523 in 2015 to 891 in 2025.
  • Lions may roam 50,000 sq km by 2047, expanding beyond Gujarat.
  • World Lion Day highlights challenges for African big cats.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday shared an article highlighting the remarkable comeback of India's Asiatic lion, a conservation success story that has captured global attention. The article, originally published by The Times of India, notes that the roar of Gujarat's lions is set to grow even wider, with projections suggesting they could roam across 50,000 square kilometres by 2047.

The Prime Minister's endorsement comes on the heels of World Lion Day, a moment when wildlife enthusiasts and conservationists reflect on the status of big cats worldwide. While Africa's lions face an uphill battle for survival, India's Asiatic lion population tells a different tale—one of recovery and cautious optimism.

A population on the rise

Union Minister for Environment, Forest and Climate Change Bhupender Yadav had earlier confirmed the encouraging numbers: the Asiatic lion population has grown from 523 in 2015 to 891 in 2025. This represents a steady increase of over 70 per cent in a decade, a testament to focused conservation efforts in the Gir forest region of Gujarat.

The growth is not just a statistic; it is a reflection of sustained habitat management, anti-poaching measures, and community involvement. Local communities in and around Gir have played a crucial role in reducing human-wildlife conflict, allowing the lions to thrive in a landscape shared with people.

Expanding territory

The article shared by the Prime Minister points to an ambitious future: lions expanding beyond their traditional stronghold in Gir. Currently, the big cats are largely confined to Gujarat, but the projection of 50,000 square kilometres by 2047 suggests a deliberate strategy to establish new populations in other states, potentially including Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan.

Such expansion, however, is not without challenges. It requires careful planning to ensure corridors are safe, prey bases are adequate, and local communities are prepared for coexistence. Conservationists argue that the key lies in proactive management rather than reactive measures. If handled well, the lions' range could become a model for large carnivore conservation in densely populated landscapes.

Global context

The timing of the Prime Minister's share is significant, coming as it does around World Lion Day. While India celebrates its Asiatic lion success, the global picture for lions remains grim. In Africa, lion populations have plummeted due to habitat loss, poaching, and human-wildlife conflict. The contrast between the two continents is stark, and it underscores the importance of tailored conservation strategies.

India's approach—combining legal protection, community engagement, and scientific monitoring—offers lessons that could be adapted elsewhere. The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) lists the Asiatic lion as endangered, but the trend in India provides hope that recovery is possible with sustained political will and public support.

What lies ahead

The government's plans for lion conservation are likely to be a key topic in the coming months, especially as the 2047 target for a much larger roaming range approaches. Officials have not yet confirmed specific timelines for translocation projects, but discussions are underway.

For now, the Prime Minister's share serves as a reminder that conservation success stories are possible, even in a fast-developing nation. The next few years will be critical in determining whether the Asiatic lion's comeback can be sustained and expanded, or whether the challenges of a growing economy and human population will test the limits of coexistence. All eyes will be on Gujarat and the corridors beyond.
