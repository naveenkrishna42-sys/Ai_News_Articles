Bandra’s new restaurant reimagines Southeast Asian street food

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • New Bandra restaurant focuses on Southeast Asian street food with a contemporary approach.
  • The menu features dishes from Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, and Indonesia.
  • Founders aim to recreate authentic flavours while using local, fresh ingredients.

Bandra’s buzzing food scene has a new player. A restaurant that opened its doors this week is betting on Southeast Asian street food — but with a twist. The menu draws from the hawker stalls of Ho Chi Minh City, the night markets of Bangkok and the warungs of Bali, all filtered through a contemporary Mumbai lens.

A menu that travels

The kitchen plates out familiar favourites: pho, pad thai, laksa and nasi goreng. But the execution leans towards lighter, fresher versions. A bowl of Vietnamese pho arrives with a clear, aromatic broth that simmers for hours, layered with star anise and ginger. The pad thai avoids the cloying sweetness that often weighs down the dish in Indian restaurants.

Smaller plates include crispy spring rolls, grilled pork skewers with a tangy tamarind dip, and banana blossom salad. The menu also features a section of rice bowls — think fragrant jasmine rice topped with lemongrass chicken or slow-cooked beef rendang.

Local ingredients, global techniques

The founders, who have spent years travelling across Southeast Asia, say the goal was never to copy a specific stall or city. “We wanted to bring the energy of street food — the bold flavours, the quick eats — but make it work for Mumbai’s palate and ingredients,” one of them explained. So you will find kokum used in place of tamarind in some dishes, and locally sourced coconut milk in the curries.

The dessert list is short but deliberate: coconut panna cotta with passion fruit, and a pandan-flavoured crème brûlée. The drinks menu includes iced Thai tea, fresh sugarcane juice and a selection of craft sodas.

The space

The restaurant occupies a compact ground-floor space on a busy Bandra lane. The interiors lean industrial — exposed brick, concrete floors, pendant lights — but are warmed by wooden furniture and green plants. There is no reservation system for now; the idea is to replicate the walk-in, no-fuss vibe of a street food market.

Seating is limited, with a mix of high stools and communal tables. The open kitchen lets diners watch the chefs work, adding to the casual, energetic feel. The founders say they plan to introduce a weekend brunch menu in the coming weeks.

Prices are mid-range, with most dishes falling between ₹250 and ₹600. The restaurant is open for lunch and dinner, and will start delivery services next month.

What to watch

The restaurant is still in its soft-launch phase. The team is tweaking recipes based on early feedback and plans to introduce a rotating specials menu drawn from different Southeast Asian regions. Whether Bandra’s discerning diners will embrace this version of street food remains to be seen — but for now, the queues are forming.
