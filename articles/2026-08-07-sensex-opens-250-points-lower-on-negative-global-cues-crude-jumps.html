Sensex Opens 250 Points Lower on Negative Global Cues, Crude Jumps

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex shed about 250 points in early trade on Friday.
  • Negative global cues and surging crude oil prices weighed on sentiment.
  • All eyes on GIFT Nifty trends and oil prices for further direction.

Indian equity benchmarks opened in the red on Friday, with the BSE Sensex losing about 250 points in early trade. The sell-off came amid negative signals from global markets, where concerns over economic growth and rising crude oil prices kept investors on edge.

The NSE Nifty50 also traded lower, mirroring the weakness in the broader market. The decline was broad-based, with banking, auto, and energy stocks among the top drags. Market participants were watching the GIFT Nifty trends, which pointed to a weak start for domestic indices.

Crude Oil Prices Add to Pressure

Surging crude oil prices emerged as a key worry for investors. Higher oil prices can widen India's trade deficit and stoke inflation, which in turn could influence the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy stance. With global benchmarks like Brent trading firm, the impact was felt across Asian markets, including India.

Oil marketing companies and aviation stocks were particularly under pressure, as input costs rise. Analysts noted that sustained high crude prices could also hurt corporate margins in sectors dependent on raw materials.

Global Cues Weigh on Sentiment

Overseas markets offered little comfort. Weak manufacturing data from major economies and uncertainty over interest rate trajectories kept risk appetite low. The overnight performance of US indices and early trends in Asian peers set a cautious tone for Dalal Street.

Foreign institutional investors have been a key factor to watch. Any sustained selling by FIIs could add to the downward pressure, though domestic institutional buying has often provided a cushion in recent sessions.

What to Watch Today

Traders will closely track the movement of crude oil prices and any fresh cues from global markets through the day. The rupee's movement against the dollar will also be on the radar, as a weaker currency can exacerbate inflationary concerns.

On the corporate front, individual stock reactions to any news or announcements could drive selective buying. However, the overall mood remains cautious, with the market likely to stay volatile until clearer direction emerges from global data points.

Technical Levels and Support

Market technicians are eyeing key support levels for the Nifty, with a breach potentially triggering further downside. On the upside, the index needs to reclaim recent highs to regain investor confidence. The options market suggests traders are bracing for choppy sessions.

While the opening has been weak, a recovery cannot be ruled out if global cues improve during the day. But with crude and global sentiment in focus, the bias remains tilted toward caution.

Going ahead, the market's trajectory will largely depend on how oil prices and overseas indices behave in the coming sessions. Any escalation in geopolitical tensions or surprise data prints could add to volatility. Investors would do well to keep a close watch on these factors before making fresh bets.
