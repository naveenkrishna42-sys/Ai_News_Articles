Why “10-year SIP returns” matter for large-cap fund selection

SIP performance over a decade is usually a better signal than short-term rallies. It blends multiple market cycles—bull, consolidation, and drawdowns—so it tells you how the fund has behaved when equity risk was not “easy.” In October 2026, Upstox’s focus on the top large-cap funds by 10-year SIP returns is a practical lens for investors who want compounding with discipline.

That said, returns alone can be misleading. Some funds reach higher SIP outcomes with greater volatility or deeper drawdowns. For SIP investors, the “ability to stay invested” during market stress is part of the outcome, because cashflow discipline matters when returns temporarily fall.

Large-cap funds also differ in portfolio approach—market-cap distribution, sector tilt, and how concentrated they are. Those choices show up as volatility, tracking behaviour versus benchmarks, and the magnitude of drawdowns from peak to trough.

How to compare funds beyond returns: size, volatility, and investor fit

When you compare large-cap funds, treat fund size as a proxy for scalability and process maturity—but not a guarantee of superior results. Larger AUM can help with research depth and trading liquidity, yet can also reduce flexibility if inflows force higher benchmark-like positioning.

Volatility is your best “real-world” proxy for how emotionally and financially survivable the fund is during market corrections. A high-return fund with high volatility might still suit long-term investors, but only if their SIP commitment is resilient and their horizon is truly long.

In practical SIP planning, also look at consistency: does the fund show steady compounding with fewer extreme drawdowns, or does it swing more sharply? This is where risk-adjusted thinking—without getting lost in complex ratios—helps most retail buyers.

Real-world buyer check: benchmarks, AUM, and volatility sanity tests

Before committing SIP money, compare each fund’s behaviour during at least one major correction window within the 10-year period. If you’re seeing volatility-based rankings, also sanity-check whether the fund’s drawdowns were unusually deep compared to peers in the same category.

Next, examine fund size relative to how concentrated its top holdings are. Very large AUM with highly concentrated bets can behave more like a risk-managed thematic fund than a typical diversified large-cap portfolio. That mismatch can surprise investors who expected steadier performance.

Finally, match the fund to your “do not quit” rule. If you would stop SIP during sharp drawdowns, a fund with higher volatility may not be appropriate—even if its 10-year SIP return is top tier. For SIP success, your behaviour needs to align with the risk you’re accepting.
