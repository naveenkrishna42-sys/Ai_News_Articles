Kohima District Health Review Highlights Immunization Gains and Ongoing HIV Challenges

Overview of the Kohima District Health Meeting

The district health officials convened on 9 September 2026 to assess three core programmes: routine immunization, child health services, and HIV/AIDS control. Representatives from the Nagaland State Health Department, local NGOs, and community health workers presented data collected over the past fiscal year.

Agenda items focused on performance metrics, resource allocation, and corrective actions for under‑performing blocks. The meeting concluded with a set of actionable recommendations aimed at sustaining gains while addressing persistent gaps.

Immunization Progress and Gaps

State‑wide campaigns, bolstered by the Mission Indradhanush II rollout, lifted full‑dose coverage for DPT‑HepB‑Hib to 92% in Kohima district. Urban wards reported near‑universal uptake, whereas hill‑top villages lagged at 78% due to cold‑chain constraints.

Health workers highlighted the introduction of solar‑powered refrigerators in three remote PHCs, a move that cut vaccine spoilage by 30% in the first quarter. Nonetheless, logistical bottlenecks during monsoon months still impede timely outreach.

Child Health Outcomes

Under‑five mortality declined from 48 per 1,000 live births in 2024 to 42 per 1,000 in 2025, reflecting improved nutrition counseling and early‑infant screening. However, the district’s mortality rate remains 15% higher than the national average.

Stunting prevalence dropped to 22% from 27% two years earlier, driven by the Integrated Child Development Services (ICDS) supplementary feeding program. Acute malnutrition (wasting) held steady at 9%, indicating the need for targeted therapeutic feeding centers.

HIV/AIDS Response and Future Priorities

The district’s HIV testing sites recorded 4,200 screenings in the last twelve months, a 12% rise from the previous year. Positivity rates stabilized at 1.4%, aligning with national trends.

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) enrollment reached 1,150 patients, yet adherence monitoring shows only 68% of patients maintain >95% dose compliance. Community‑led support groups are being scaled to address stigma and improve follow‑up.

  • Child stunting improves, yet under‑five mortality stays above national average, signalling service quality gaps.
  • HIV testing expands, but treatment adherence lags, requiring stronger community engagement.
