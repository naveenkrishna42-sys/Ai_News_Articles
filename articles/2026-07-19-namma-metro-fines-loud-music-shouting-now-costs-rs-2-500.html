Namma Metro fines: Loud music, shouting now costs Rs 2,500

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Namma Metro imposes Rs 2,500 fine for loud music and shouting.
  • The rule aims to curb noise and disturbances in trains and stations.
  • Penalty applies to commuters violating decorum from July 2026.

New fine for noise violations on Namma Metro

Bengaluru's Namma Metro has introduced a Rs 2,500 fine for commuters playing loud music or shouting inside trains and station premises. The rule took effect from July 2026, targeting disturbances that have long irked passengers.

Officials said the penalty applies to anyone using speakers, mobile phones, or other devices at high volume. Shouting or creating a public nuisance is also covered under the new directive.

What the rule covers

The fine is part of a broader push to maintain decorum in the metro network. Metro staff and security personnel have been authorised to issue spot fines to violators.

Commuters who refuse to pay or argue may face further action, including being asked to leave the premises. Repeat offenders could attract higher penalties, though authorities have not detailed specific escalation procedures.

Why this move now

Namma Metro sees over 5 lakh passengers daily. Complaints about blaring music, phone conversations on speaker, and loud arguments have been rising. Many passengers raised concerns about the lack of a quiet environment, especially during peak hours.

In a statement, a Bangalore Metro Rail Corporation Limited (BMRCL) official said, “We want to ensure a comfortable journey for all. This fine is a deterrent against behaviour that disturbs others.”

Other metro rules in place

Namma Metro already prohibits eating, drinking, and spitting inside trains. Violators face fines of up to Rs 500. The new noise penalty is one of the steepest for behavioural violations in the network.

Security checks and CCTV monitoring will help enforce the rule. Officials have asked commuters to cooperate and report violations to station staff.

The fine will be collected on the spot. Those unable to pay may be detained until they comply or produce identification for follow-up action.

Reaction from commuters

Many passengers welcomed the move. “Loud music on earphones is fine, but some people play it so loud everyone hears. This is a good step,” said Priya R., a regular commuter.

Others expressed concern about harassment. “What if someone just talks loudly? We need clear guidelines on what counts as shouting,” said Amit K., a software engineer. BMRCL has not yet issued detailed clarifications on decibel levels or specific scenarios.

The fine does not apply to emergency announcements or authorised personnel using public address systems.

Namma Metro plans to review the rule’s impact in three months. If violations drop, the fine may stay; if not, stricter measures could follow.
