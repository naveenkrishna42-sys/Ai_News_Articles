PoK Protests: 'Teens Killed, We Are Not Terrorists' Chants Echo as Unrest Grows

📅 2026-08-04 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Protests erupt in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir over the killing of teenagers, with locals rejecting the 'terrorist' label.
  • Security forces have opened fire on protesters refusing to disperse, worsening the situation.
  • India's Centre calls the PoK elections a 'complete farce', while the JAAC urges a boycott, terming polls 'fraud at gunpoint'.

Streets in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK) are boiling over. What began as localized anger has spiraled into region-wide protests, with demonstrators chanting slogans that cut to the heart of the matter: 'Teens killed, we are not terrorists.' The unrest, which has been simmering for weeks, has now drawn a sharp response from security forces, who have opened fire on crowds refusing to disperse.

What triggered the protests?

The immediate trigger is the reported killing of several teenagers in recent clashes. While official details remain murky, local accounts and social media posts have amplified the outrage. Families of the deceased have come forward, demanding accountability and an end to what they describe as a brutal crackdown.

The chants of 'we are not terrorists' are not new, but they have gained a sharper edge in this round of protests. For many in PoK, the label 'terrorist' has been a persistent and painful part of state discourse, used to delegitimize dissent. Now, that word is being thrown back with defiance.

Security forces open fire, situation worsens

According to reports, security personnel have been deployed in large numbers across several towns. On multiple occasions, they have fired at protesters who refused to disperse, leading to further casualties and injuries. The exact number of those affected has not been officially confirmed, but eyewitness accounts describe a tense and volatile atmosphere.

Internet and mobile services have reportedly been disrupted in several areas, a common tactic during unrest. This has made independent verification of events difficult, and has also hampered the ability of residents to coordinate or seek help. The situation, as per ground reports, shows no signs of immediate de-escalation.

India's response: 'Complete farce'

Across the border, the Indian government has weighed in. The Centre has dismissed the ongoing electoral process in PoK as a 'complete farce', stating that hollow exercises cannot hide the ground reality. This is a familiar refrain from New Delhi, which has long maintained that PoK is an integral part of India and that Pakistan's administration there is illegitimate.

The statement came amid the backdrop of elections in the region, which India has consistently criticized. The government's position is that any political activity in PoK is a cover for Pakistan's occupation and does not reflect the will of the people.

JAAC calls for boycott, labels polls 'fraud at gunpoint'

Adding to the political turmoil, the Joint Awami Action Committee (JAAC), a prominent political alliance in PoK, has urged the public to boycott the elections. The JAAC has termed the polls 'fraud at gunpoint', arguing that they are being held under duress and do not represent genuine democratic choice.

The committee's call has found resonance among protesters, many of whom see the elections as an attempt to distract from the core issues of rights and representation. The boycott movement, if it gains traction, could further delegitimize the electoral exercise and deepen the crisis for the local administration.

The road ahead

The coming days will be critical. The combination of teenage casualties, aggressive crowd control, and a contested election is a combustible mix. All eyes are now on whether the local administration will attempt dialogue or continue with the hardline approach. The chants from the streets are clear, but whether they are heard is another matter.
