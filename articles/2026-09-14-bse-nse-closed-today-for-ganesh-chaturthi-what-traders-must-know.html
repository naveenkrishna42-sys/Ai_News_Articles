BSE, NSE Closed Today for Ganesh Chaturthi: What Traders Must Know

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-14 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • BSE and NSE equity, F&O and currency segments are shut on September 14, 2026 for Ganesh Chaturthi, with trading resuming on September 15.
  • Commodity derivatives on MCX open in the evening session, so bullion and energy traders retain partial market access.
  • Settlement obligations shift by a day, and overnight risk cannot be hedged until the next session, making position sizing critical.

Indian equity markets are shut on Monday, September 14, 2026, with both the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) and the National Stock Exchange (NSE) observing a full holiday for Ganesh Chaturthi. The exchange holiday covers the equity cash segment, equity derivatives, and the currency derivatives segment, as per the standard BSE and NSE trading holiday calendar published at the start of the year. Normal trading resumes on Tuesday, September 15, 2026.

What Exactly Is Closed Today

The shutdown is comprehensive across the equity ecosystem. No pre-open session, no continuous trading, no closing auction, and no post-close derivatives window will operate. Broking back-office and depository functions also run on a reduced schedule, though some intermediaries process limited customer requests on a best-effort basis.

Mutual fund NAV computation, however, continues. Schemes take the previous business day's closing prices to compute NAV on a market holiday, so investors redeeming or switching units today are priced on the last available market close, not on any live traded price.

Commodity Traders Retain Partial Access

The Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX) operates a truncated schedule on most equity holidays. The morning session for agricultural and base metal contracts remains shut, but the evening session covering bullion, crude oil, and natural gas runs as usual, typically from 5:00 pm to 11:30 pm or 11:55 pm depending on US daylight saving adjustments.

This matters for anyone holding gold or silver positions into the festival week. With global Comex and London Bullion Market Association pricing active through the Indian holiday, an unhedged overnight position carries real gap risk. Traders who want continuous exposure to metals should route orders through MCX's evening window rather than waiting for the equity market to reopen.

Settlement and Obligation Shifts

Under the T+1 settlement cycle that India adopted fully in January 2023, a trade executed on the last working day before a holiday settles on the next working day. Trades from Friday, September 11, therefore settle on Tuesday, September 15, not on the holiday itself. Clearing corporations and depositories do not process pay-in and pay-out on the holiday.

For delivery-based buyers, this means shares purchased on Friday will reflect in the demat account only after the settlement cycle completes. Sellers should not expect funds before the same window. Intraday traders who squared off positions on Friday face no additional obligation, since their trades were already closed.

Key Real-World Implications

  • Global cues still move during the holiday: US, European, and Asian markets trade normally, so accumulated sentiment will hit Indian indices in Tuesday's opening gap.
  • F&O expiry positions cannot be adjusted today, which amplifies gap risk for weekly and monthly contracts.
  • Currency derivatives being shut means importers and exporters cannot hedge dollar exposure until Tuesday, leaving them exposed to weekend and holiday currency swings.
  • Systematic investment plan (SIP) debits scheduled for September 14 typically get processed on the next business day, with units allotted at that day's NAV.
  • Broking helplines and grievance redressal run at reduced strength, so unresolved trade disputes will see delayed responses.

How Many Market Holidays Remain This Year

Indian exchanges typically observe around 14 to 16 trading holidays in a calendar year, and Ganesh Chaturthi is one of the fixed ones tied to the Hindu lunisolar calendar, which is why the date shifts annually. Buyers and traders planning around the festive season should treat the published BSE and NSE holiday lists as the only authoritative reference, since broker apps and third-party calendars occasionally carry outdated dates.

The practical takeaway is straightforward. Equity and currency traders have a forced pause today. Commodity participants retain partial evening access. Everyone carrying overnight exposure should size positions assuming a one-day information gap, because Tuesday's opening price will absorb two days of global news in a single tick.
