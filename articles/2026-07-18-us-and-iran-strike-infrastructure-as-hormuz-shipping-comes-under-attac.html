US and Iran Strike Infrastructure as Hormuz Shipping Comes Under Attack

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • US and Iran target infrastructure in tit-for-tat strikes.
  • Two oil tankers explode after hitting mines in Hormuz, Iran's Guards say.
  • IRGC claims it stopped four ships in combined missile and drone attack.
  • Iran targets Thai-flagged vessel over unauthorized transit, report says.

The United States and Iran have escalated their conflict by targeting each other's infrastructure, as shipping through the strategic Strait of Hormuz comes under renewed attack. The developments mark a sharp deterioration in what has already been a tense standoff in the region.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on Friday that two oil tankers exploded after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz. The Guards did not name the vessels or provide details on casualties. The incident follows a series of attacks that have raised fears of a broader confrontation.

IRGC Claims It Stopped Four Ships

Separately, the IRGC said it had 'stopped' four ships attempting to traverse the Hormuz waterway in a combined missile and drone attack. The claim, reported by The Times of Israel, suggests Iran is now actively interdicting maritime traffic through one of the world's most vital oil transit chokepoints.

Reports from Anadolu Ajansı indicated that Iran targeted a Thai-flagged vessel in the strait over what it called 'unauthorized transit'. The exact identity of the ship and its crew were not immediately known. Officials in Bangkok have not yet commented on the incident.

US and Iran Exchange Strikes

In response to the attacks, the US military carried out strikes on Iranian-linked infrastructure, according to officials. The targets were not immediately disclosed, but the strikes represent a direct military action against assets associated with Tehran. Iran, in turn, launched its own strikes against what it described as US-linked infrastructure.

The tit-for-tat strikes signal a shift from proxy engagements to direct confrontation. Both sides have warned of severe consequences if the other does not de-escalate. The Pentagon has not yet released a full assessment of the damage inflicted.

Hormuz Flashpoint Deepens

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway through which about one-fifth of the world's oil passes. Any disruption to shipping there can send global energy prices soaring. The latest attacks have already caused a spike in crude oil futures, with traders bracing for a prolonged disruption.

Firstpost reported that the Hormuz flashpoint is deepening as tanker explosions, fresh strikes and military threats fuel the Iran conflict. The situation has drawn international concern, with the United Nations calling for restraint from both sides. The US Navy's Fifth Fleet, based in Bahrain, has heightened its alert level in the region.

What to Watch

The immediate focus is on whether the attacks on shipping and infrastructure will continue or if a diplomatic off-ramp can be found. Both Washington and Tehran have shown little appetite for talks in recent weeks. The next few hours may determine whether the conflict escalates into a full-blown naval engagement or remains a series of targeted strikes.
