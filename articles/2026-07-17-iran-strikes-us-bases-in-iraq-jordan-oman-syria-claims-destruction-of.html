Iran Strikes US Bases in Iraq, Jordan, Oman, Syria, Claims Destruction of Jets

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran targeted US military facilities in Iraq, Jordan, Oman, and Syria
  • IRGC claims it destroyed many US jets and caused 'serious damage' to more
  • Attacks come after sixth consecutive night of US strikes on Iranian positions

Tehran launched a coordinated wave of missile and drone strikes against US military bases across four countries on Friday, escalating a conflict that has entered its seventh night of continuous exchanges.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said it struck American fighter jets in Jordan, claiming to have destroyed multiple aircraft and caused what it described as 'serious damage' to many more. The operation also targeted US facilities in Iraq, Oman, and Syria.

Claims of Damage

The IRGC statement, carried by state media, asserted that its precision-guided missiles and drones had successfully hit US fighter jets parked at a base in Jordan. The claim could not be independently verified, and US Central Command has not yet released a damage assessment.

Several Indian news outlets, including NDTV and India Today, reported the Iranian claims of destroying many US jets. The scale of the alleged damage remains unclear, with no visual evidence or official US confirmation provided so far.

Renewed Offensive After US Campaign

The fresh attacks came after the sixth consecutive night of US airstrikes on Iranian positions across the region. The US campaign, which began last week, has targeted IRGC facilities, missile sites, and drone launch pads in what Washington calls a defensive response to earlier Iranian provocations.

ThePrint reported that Iran launched its latest salvo following the sustained American bombardment. The pattern of tit-for-tat strikes has raised fears of a broader regional war drawing in multiple nations and proxy forces.

Geographic Spread

Friday's Iranian operation hit targets in four countries simultaneously, marking a significant expansion of the battlefield. In addition to Jordan, where the IRGC claimed it struck fighter jets, sites in Iraq, Oman, and Syria were also targeted.

Iraqi officials have not yet commented on the extent of damage or casualties at bases hosting US personnel. Omani authorities have remained silent on any strikes within their territory. Syria, where Iranian forces have long maintained a presence, saw continued exchanges near positions held by both sides.

The multi-front nature of the attack suggests Iran is attempting to stretch US air defence capabilities and force a dispersal of American assets across the region.

Regional and Global Reaction

Arab News reported the developments as part of a fast-unfolding crisis that has drawn in multiple capitals. Deccan Herald confirmed the Iranian claims of hitting US jets in Jordan, while India Today carried similar reports from the region.

No major power has called for de-escalation so far. The United Nations has not issued a statement on the latest round of attacks. Gulf states, which host US bases, are watching nervously as the conflict edges closer to their borders.

Diplomatic channels appear dormant as both sides double down on military action. The US has not signalled any intention to halt its bombing campaign, and Iran has vowed to continue until all American forces leave the region.

What to Watch

The next hours will likely bring independent assessments of damage at the targeted bases. If the US confirms significant aircraft losses, it could trigger a major shift in strategy — including possible strikes on Iranian mainland targets. If Iran's claims prove exaggerated, the momentum may shift back to Washington's advantage.
