Superstitious Milei skips World Cup final over lucky jacket

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • President Milei cites superstition for skipping the final.
  • He believes his 'lucky jacket' tradition will break if he attends.
  • The decision has sparked controversy and debate in Argentina.

Argentine President Javier Milei will not attend the 2026 FIFA World Cup final. The reason? Superstition.

Milei, known for his eccentric style, believes showing up would break a good luck streak. A lucky jacket is central to this belief.

The lucky jacket and an old curse

Sources close to the president say Milei has worn a specific jacket during Argentina's past World Cup wins. He thinks his presence at the stadium would jinx the team.

Reports from Asianet Newsable mention an "old curse" tied to the garment. Milei has not confirmed the details himself.

The president's office has not issued an official statement. But aides have confirmed he will watch the match from home.

Controversy and criticism

The decision has not gone down well. Critics call it irresponsible for a head of state during a major sporting event.

“This is not about personal quirks. It is about national representation,” one political analyst said. Others have mocked the move on social media.

Argentina faces a tough opponent in the final. The team, led by Lionel Messi, is aiming for a second consecutive title. Milei's absence has become a distraction.

Supporters defend the move

Not everyone is angry. Milei's supporters argue he is being honest about his beliefs.

“He is not pretending. He is superstitious like many Argentines,” one supporter posted online. The president's approval ratings remain steady despite the controversy.

Milei has a history of unusual behaviour. He has previously spoken about communicating with his dead dog and criticised mainstream politicians. This latest episode fits his outsider persona.

What happens next

The World Cup final will be played as scheduled. Milei will not be in the stands.

Argentina's players have not commented on the president's decision. They are focused on the game.

Whether the lucky jacket works remains to be seen. If Argentina wins, Milei may claim credit. If they lose, the criticism will only grow.
