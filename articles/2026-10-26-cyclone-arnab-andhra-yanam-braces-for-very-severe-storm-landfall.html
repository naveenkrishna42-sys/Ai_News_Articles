Cyclone Arnab: An In-Depth Analysis of the Bay of Bengal's Latest Threat

As the Bay of Bengal continues its cyclonic season, a new and formidable threat, Cyclonic Storm 'Arnab', is rapidly intensifying. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued a critical alert, projecting 'Arnab' to cross the Andhra Pradesh-Yanam coast as a 'Very Severe Cyclonic Storm' by the evening of October 28, 2026. This development necessitates a comprehensive understanding of its trajectory, potential impact, and the robust preparedness measures being implemented across multiple Indian states.

The system, which formed over the central Bay of Bengal, has steadily gained strength, drawing warm ocean waters and favorable atmospheric conditions. Its current classification as a 'Very Severe Cyclonic Storm' signifies sustained wind speeds between 120-165 kmph, capable of causing extensive damage to infrastructure, agriculture, and coastal ecosystems. TIVRA News provides an authoritative overview of this unfolding meteorological event and its crucial implications.

Tracking 'Arnab': Meteorological Trajectory and Impact Zone

Formation and Intensification Dynamics

Cyclone Arnab originated as a low-pressure area, gradually consolidating into a depression before rapidly intensifying into a cyclonic storm and subsequently a 'Very Severe Cyclonic Storm'. This intensification is primarily driven by high sea surface temperatures and low vertical wind shear in the Bay of Bengal, providing optimal conditions for its growth. The IMD's sophisticated numerical models have consistently indicated a west-northwestward track towards the central-eastern Indian coast.

Expected Landfall and Key Parameters

The most critical phase of Cyclone Arnab is its anticipated landfall. Current projections indicate it will cross between Kakinada (Andhra Pradesh) and Yanam (Puducherry) by the late evening of October 28. This region is highly vulnerable to storm surges and wind damage due to its low-lying coastal areas and dense population. The IMD's precise forecasts are guiding immediate response strategies.

Preparedness and Evacuation Efforts

Multi-State Mobilization

In anticipation of Arnab's landfall, the state governments of Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and neighbouring regions have activated their disaster management protocols. Andhra Pradesh, as the primary impact zone, has initiated extensive measures including the deployment of National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and State Disaster Response Force (SDRF) teams. These specialized units are equipped for rescue operations, tree cutting, and immediate relief.

Mass Evacuation and Shelter Management

Evacuation is a cornerstone of cyclone preparedness. Over 89,000 people from low-lying coastal villages in Andhra Pradesh are being moved to designated cyclone shelters. Odisha has also taken proactive steps, including a ban on fishing activities in the sea and restricting tourist entry in six coastal districts. Ensuring the safety, sanitation, and provision of essential supplies at these shelters represents a significant logistical challenge for local administrations.

Public Advisory and Emergency Services

Continuous public advisories are being broadcast through various channels, urging residents in vulnerable areas to follow evacuation orders and take necessary precautions. Emergency control rooms are operational 24/7, coordinating response efforts and providing real-time updates. Power distribution companies are pre-positioning crews and equipment to address potential outages swiftly.

Real-World Implications for Coastal Communities

Economic and Livelihood Disruption

The impending cyclone poses a severe threat to the livelihoods of coastal communities. The fishing ban, while crucial for safety, impacts daily earnings for thousands of families. Agricultural sectors, particularly paddy and horticulture, face significant losses from heavy rainfall and strong winds. Infrastructure, including roads, power lines, and telecommunication networks, is highly susceptible to damage, leading to prolonged disruption and substantial recovery costs.

Public Safety and Environmental Concerns

Beyond immediate structural damage, 'Arnab' presents risks of flooding, particularly in urban areas with inadequate drainage. The storm surge can inundate coastal villages, contaminating freshwater sources and displacing communities. Environmental impacts include beach erosion and potential damage to fragile coastal ecosystems like mangroves, which act as natural barriers against such storms. The long-term recovery will require sustained effort and resources.

  • Landfall is expected on the Andhra Pradesh-Yanam coast by October 28 evening, with sustained winds up to 130 kmph.
  • Over 89,000 residents are undergoing evacuation from vulnerable coastal areas in Andhra Pradesh.
