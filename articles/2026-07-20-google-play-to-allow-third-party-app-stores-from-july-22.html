Google Play to Allow Third-Party App Stores From July 22

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Third-party app stores can be listed on Google Play starting July 22.
  • The move follows a court order from the Epic Games antitrust case.
  • Google will allow rival storefronts within its own app marketplace for the first time.

Google Play Store Opens Doors to Rival App Marketplaces

Google will begin allowing third-party app stores to be listed on the Google Play Store from July 22, marking a major shift in how Android users discover and download applications. The change comes after a court order in the ongoing Epic Games antitrust case.

For the first time, rival storefronts will appear within Google's own marketplace. The company confirmed the timeline after withdrawing a separate settlement with Epic Games.

Court Order Triggers Policy Change

The decision to open up the Play Store stems from a legal challenge by Epic Games, which argued that Google's control over app distribution on Android was anticompetitive. A court ruled that Google must allow alternative app stores access to its platform.

Google had initially reached a settlement with Epic, but later withdrew from that agreement. The company now says it is complying with the court's directive, which takes effect next week.

How It Works

Third-party app stores will be available as listings inside the Google Play Store. Users can browse and install these storefronts just like any other app. Once installed, users can access apps from those alternative stores directly.

Google has not detailed all the technical requirements for third-party stores. But the company has indicated that they must meet certain security and content standards to be listed. The move is expected to increase competition among app marketplaces on Android.

Industry Reactions

The change has been closely watched by developers and regulators. App store operators like Samsung, Amazon, and independent stores such as Aptoide have expressed interest in appearing on Google Play. Epic Games, which runs its own store for Fortnite, is likely to be among the early participants.

Consumer advocates have welcomed the move, saying it gives users more choice. However, some security experts have raised concerns about potential risks if users install apps from unverified stores.

What This Means for Users

From next week, Android users in India and globally will see new app store options when they open Google Play. The change does not affect the existing sideloading options on Android, where users can already install apps from outside the Play Store.

Google has said it will continue to enforce its safety policies for all apps, including those from third-party stores. The company has not yet announced whether it will charge fees to third-party store operators for being listed.

The rollout begins July 22. How quickly rival stores appear and how many users switch will determine whether this shake-up reshapes the Android app economy or remains a niche option.
