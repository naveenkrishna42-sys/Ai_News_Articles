Google Assistant to exit Android Auto in September, Gemini not ready

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google Assistant will be discontinued on Android Auto and Wear OS starting September 4.
  • Gemini is set to replace Assistant but is not yet fully prepared for all functions.
  • Users on older devices may face disruptions as the transition unfolds.

End of an era for Google Assistant

Google has confirmed that Google Assistant will be officially shut down on Android Auto and Wear OS starting September 4. The move marks the end of a decade-long run for the voice assistant that became a staple in millions of cars and on countless wrists.

The company has been gradually steering users toward Gemini, its newer AI-powered assistant. However, the transition has raised concerns among users and tech analysts alike, as Gemini is not yet equipped to handle every task Assistant currently performs.

What changes on September 4

From the first week of September, Android Auto will no longer launch Google Assistant when you press the voice command button or say “Hey Google.” Instead, Gemini will take over, provided it is installed and set as the default assistant on the connected phone.

Similarly, on Wear OS devices, the Assistant will cease to function, leaving Gemini as the sole voice assistant option. For users who haven’t updated their devices or installed Gemini, the voice assistant feature may simply stop working altogether.

Google has not yet clarified whether there will be a fallback option for those who prefer the older assistant or whose devices are incompatible with Gemini.

Gemini’s readiness under scrutiny

Despite Google’s push, Gemini is still missing several core features that Assistant users rely on daily. For instance, certain smart home commands, media playback controls, and some third-party app integrations are not yet fully supported by Gemini.

Early adopters have reported inconsistent performance, especially in noisy environments like a moving car, where voice recognition accuracy drops. These gaps have led to criticism that Google is phasing out a stable product before its replacement is truly ready for prime time.

Tech publications, including XDA and Ars Technica, have highlighted these issues, noting that the September deadline may leave many users stranded without a fully functional voice assistant.

Impact on users and devices

The shutdown will affect a wide range of devices, from older Android Auto head units to the latest Wear OS smartwatches. Users with older phones that do not support Gemini may find themselves without any voice assistant in their car or on their watch.

Google has advised users to ensure Gemini is installed and updated before September 4. However, the company has not detailed how it plans to handle devices where Gemini cannot run, leaving a significant portion of its user base in limbo.

For those who rely on Assistant for hands-free navigation, messaging, or calls while driving, the transition could prove disruptive. The lack of a clear migration path has added to the confusion.

What to watch next

In the coming weeks, Google is expected to release a timeline for Gemini’s feature parity with Assistant. The company may also roll out updates to bridge the most critical gaps before the September cutoff.

Users should monitor official announcements from Google regarding compatibility and any last-minute changes. Whether Gemini can step up in time remains an open question, but the countdown has already begun.
