Iran mocks US 'sanctions addiction' as Trump seeks compensation

📅 2026-08-11 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iranian officials ridiculed the US threat of 'suffocating' sanctions.
  • Trump said Iran should 'pay for damages' over the last 50 years.
  • Iran's central bank chief rejected US claims linking Tehran to cryptocurrency.
  • Tensions remain high amid ongoing US-Iran hostilities.

Tehran hits back at Washington's latest pressure tactics

Iran has sharply ridiculed the Trump administration's latest threat to impose 'suffocating' sanctions, accusing Washington of having a 'compulsive addiction' to punitive measures. The sharp rebuke came hours after US President Donald Trump suggested that Tehran should compensate America for damages incurred over the past five decades.

The exchange marks another escalation in the long-running standoff between the two nations, with both sides trading barbs even as the threat of military confrontation looms.

'Addicted' to sanctions, says Iran

In an official response, Iranian authorities dismissed the US threat as hollow, stating that Washington's reflex to resort to sanctions reflects a deeper dysfunction in its foreign policy. The phrase 'America's compulsive addiction to sanctions' was used in state media, framing the US approach as both predictable and ineffective.

Iranian officials argue that decades of sanctions have failed to force a change in Tehran's behaviour, and that the latest threat is merely another symptom of the same failed strategy.

Trump demands compensation for 50 years of 'damages'

President Trump, meanwhile, has raised the stakes by asserting that Iran should pay for the harm it has caused over the last half-century. His remarks, which were reported by multiple outlets including ETV Bharat, have been interpreted in Tehran as a provocation designed to justify further economic pressure.

The demand for reparations is unusual in diplomatic practice and has drawn criticism from analysts who see it as a move that could further inflame tensions rather than open a path to negotiation.

Central bank rejects cryptocurrency claims

In a related development, Iran's central bank chief has firmly rejected US allegations that Tehran is using cryptocurrencies to bypass sanctions. The denial, reported by Crypto Briefing, comes amid growing scrutiny of digital assets as a potential loophole in the sanctions regime.

The central bank's statement sought to distance Iran from any covert financial operations, insisting that its dealings remain transparent and within legal frameworks.

A familiar cycle of escalation

The current war of words is part of a broader pattern of escalation that has defined US-Iran relations in recent years. Washington has repeatedly tightened sanctions, while Tehran has responded with defiance and threats of its own.

Diplomatic efforts to de-escalate have so far yielded little, and both sides appear entrenched in their positions.

As the rhetoric intensifies, the international community watches closely for any sign that the standoff could spiral into a broader conflict. So far, neither side has shown willingness to back down.

What to watch next

The coming weeks will reveal whether Trump's compensation demand is a rhetorical flourish or a precursor to concrete policy action. Iran's response to any new sanctions will also be closely monitored, as will the role of cryptocurrencies in the ongoing financial war.

For now, the impasse remains, with both capitals seemingly locked in a cycle of provocation and retaliation.
