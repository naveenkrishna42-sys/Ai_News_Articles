Hackers Poison Adform Script to Swap Crypto Wallet Addresses

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Attackers compromised Adform's ad-serving script to alter crypto wallet addresses.
  • The malicious code affected multiple customer websites using Adform's platform.
  • Users were redirected to send cryptocurrency to attacker-controlled wallets.
  • Adform is investigating the breach and has not yet disclosed full scope.

Ad Platform Compromised in Sophisticated Supply-Chain Attack

Cybercriminals have breached Adform, a prominent digital advertising platform, and poisoned its script to swap cryptocurrency wallet addresses on customer websites. The attack, which came to light over the weekend, has raised fresh concerns about the security of third-party code in the digital advertising supply chain.

The tampered script was served to multiple Adform customers, redirecting crypto payments meant for legitimate recipients to attacker-controlled wallets. Researchers who flagged the incident say the modification was subtle, making it difficult for users and site owners to detect.

Adform, which powers ad serving and programmatic advertising for thousands of publishers and brands, has not yet confirmed how many sites were affected or the total financial impact. The company said it is conducting a forensic review and has taken affected scripts offline.

How the Attack Worked

Attackers gained access to Adform's script distribution mechanism, likely through compromised credentials or an internal breach, and altered the JavaScript code that customers embed on their websites. The poisoned script was designed to detect cryptocurrency wallet addresses on payment pages and replace them with the attackers' addresses.

This type of attack, known as a clipboard or address-swapping exploit, has been seen in browser extensions and fake apps, but compromising a major ad platform marks a significant escalation. The malicious code was delivered alongside legitimate ad content, making it harder for security tools to flag.

Security researchers note that the attack targeted only crypto-related transactions, leaving other site functions untouched. This selective behavior suggests the attackers had specific financial motives and may have monitored the infected sites for high-value payments.

Implications for Digital Advertising and Crypto Users

The incident highlights a growing risk in the ad-tech ecosystem, where third-party scripts are trusted by default. Websites relying on Adform's tag may have unknowingly exposed their visitors to wallet-swapping fraud, even if the sites themselves were not directly compromised.

For crypto users, the attack underscores the importance of verifying wallet addresses before confirming transactions, especially on sites that use third-party services. Security experts recommend double-checking addresses manually and using hardware wallets with transaction previews.

Adform has advised customers to review their integrations and monitor for any unauthorised changes. The company has also engaged external cybersecurity firms to assist with the investigation and to prevent future incidents.

Response and Broader Concerns

This is not the first time a supply-chain attack has targeted the ad industry, but the crypto-specific focus adds a new layer of concern. Previous incidents, such as the Magecart attacks on e-commerce checkout pages, have shown how compromised scripts can siphon sensitive data at scale.

Law enforcement agencies have been alerted, and regulators may scrutinise Adform's security practices. The breach could prompt calls for stricter vetting of third-party code and more transparent reporting of such incidents.

For now, affected customers are being urged to purge cached scripts, rotate API keys, and audit recent crypto transactions. Adform has promised to release a detailed post-mortem once its investigation concludes.

As the probe continues, the wider ad-tech industry will be watching closely. The attack serves as a stark reminder that even trusted platforms can become vectors for cybercrime, and that vigilance is critical when handling digital assets.
