Suvendu Adhikari offers house to Taslima Nasrin; Mamata, Buddhadeb didn't

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Suvendu Adhikari promises a house to Taslima Nasrin in West Bengal
  • Neither Buddhadeb Bhattacharjee nor Mamata Banerjee made such an offer earlier
  • Nasrin has been in exile since 1994 after a fatwa was issued against her

Adhikari's surprise pledge

Leader of Opposition in West Bengal, Suvendu Adhikari, has promised to provide a house to exiled author Taslima Nasrin. The announcement came on Sunday, adding a fresh twist to the state's political discourse. Adhikari's offer is notable because neither former chief minister Buddhadeb Bhattacharjee nor current chief minister Mamata Banerjee ever made such a commitment.

The BJP leader's statement signals an attempt to position his party as a champion of free speech, a charge his rivals have often levelled against the Trinamool Congress government. However, the promise is yet to be formalised, and officials have not confirmed any concrete plan.

Nasrin's long exile

Taslima Nasrin, a Bangladeshi-born author and physician, has lived in exile since 1994. A fatwa was issued against her following the publication of her controversial novel Lajja, which dealt with communal riots in Bangladesh. She has since resided in various countries, including the United States and India, but has often faced resistance in settling permanently.

Her relationship with West Bengal's political establishment has been fraught. During the Left Front regime, Buddhadeb Bhattacharjee's government kept her at arm's length, wary of diplomatic repercussions with Bangladesh. Mamata Banerjee's Trinamool Congress, too, has not extended a formal invitation, despite her occasional visits to Kolkata.

Political context and reactions

Adhikari's announcement comes amid a charged atmosphere. Nasrin has recently made headlines for her outspoken views, including a call for a Uniform Civil Code across the subcontinent and a demand to ban madrasas. She has also drawn parallels between the protests at Jadavpur University and the 2024 unrest in Bangladesh, a comparison that has sparked sharp debate.

The BJP has welcomed her comments, with some leaders calling her a 'voice of reason'. In contrast, the Trinamool Congress has dismissed the promise as a publicity stunt. A senior TMC leader, speaking on condition of anonymity, said, "This is yet another empty gesture from a man who cannot even manage his own constituency properly."

The CPI(M), which once ruled the state, has remained silent on the matter. Meanwhile, the Bangladesh government has not issued any official reaction to Adhikari's offer, though relations between Dhaka and New Delhi have been delicate since the political changes in Bangladesh.

What Nasrin has said recently

Nasrin has been vocal in her support for Sheikh Hasina, the ousted Bangladeshi prime minister, saying she wants Hasina to return and the ban on her party to be lifted. She has also expressed solidarity with the students protesting at Jadavpur University, calling the incident involving a student 'unfortunate'. Her statements have made her a polarising figure once again, with supporters hailing her courage and critics accusing her of stoking divisions.

Her demand for a ban on madrasas has particularly angered Muslim organisations in India. Several groups have held protests in Kolkata, but Nasrin has remained defiant, insisting that her views are based on reason and secularism.

The road ahead

Whether Adhikari will follow through on his promise remains to be seen. The offer, if implemented, would require state government cooperation, which seems unlikely given the adversarial relationship between the BJP and the TMC. Political analysts suggest that the move is more about electoral messaging than practical governance.

For now, Taslima Nasrin has not publicly responded to Adhikari's offer. Her next move—whether she chooses to accept or ignore it—will be closely watched in both India and Bangladesh. The coming weeks may reveal if this is a serious commitment or just another chapter in West Bengal's theatrical politics.
