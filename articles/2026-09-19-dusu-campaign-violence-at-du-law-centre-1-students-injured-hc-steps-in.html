Violence Erupts at Law Centre-1 as DUSU Campaigning Turns Ugly

Campaigning for the Delhi University Students' Union (DUSU) elections 2026 turned violent at Law Centre-1, with clashes between rival student groups leaving several students injured. The incident, first reported by legal news outlet Bar and Bench, marks yet another flashpoint in a poll season already defined by allegations of intimidation and muscle-flexing.

The confrontation reportedly broke out between supporters of competing panels during active campaigning on campus. Injured students required medical attention, though no independent casualty figures have been officially confirmed. DU authorities have not issued a detailed statement on the number of those hurt.

Delhi High Court Bans Victory Processions, Warns of Action

The Delhi High Court responded swiftly, banning victory processions after the DUSU election results and warning of strict action against violators. The court's intervention follows a pattern of poll-related disruptions that have repeatedly pulled campus politics into litigation.

For students and observers, the HC order is a direct signal that celebratory rallies, often the flashpoint for fresh violence, will not be tolerated this cycle. Enforcement, however, remains the open question — campus security and Delhi Police coordination will determine whether the ban holds once results are declared.

Money and Muscle: The Persistent Underbelly of DU Politics

Reporting by The Hindu noted that while the Jantar Mantar protest fuelled public interest in student issues, money and muscle power persist in DU politics. Candidates and panels continue to face accusations of funded campaigning, outsider involvement, and coercive mobilisation.

This matters beyond campus. DUSU office-bearers often use the platform as a launchpad into larger political careers, making the union a proxy battleground for national parties and their student wings. The cost of that proxy fight is borne by ordinary students navigating a tense campus.

The ABVP vs NSUI vs AISA-SFI Contest: What's at Stake

Live coverage from The Economic Times framed the 2026 race as a three-way contest: ABVP, NSUI, and the AISA-SFI combine. Each brings a distinct organisational base and ideological pitch, and each has a history of confrontational campaigning at DU.

Real-World Implications for Students and Voters

For the student body, the violence at Law Centre-1 carries practical consequences: disrupted academic schedules, safety concerns during polling, and a chilling effect on first-time voters who may avoid the process altogether.

For DU administration, the immediate test is logistical — securing booths, screening campaign activity, and coordinating with police. For political parties, the optics of violence in a law faculty are particularly damaging, inviting judicial scrutiny and public backlash.
