Market Overview: Losses Extend Amid Global Pressures

Indian equity benchmarks continued their downward trajectory on 01 Oct 2026, with both the Nifty 50 and the Sensex posting noticeable declines. The sell‑off was triggered by a combination of weak global cues, rising US Treasury yields, and escalating geopolitical tensions that prompted risk‑averse behavior among market participants. Domestic investors observed heightened volatility as intraday swings widened, reflecting uncertainty about near‑term earnings growth and monetary policy direction.

Foreign Institutional Outflow: ₹19,000 Crore Sell‑off

Data released by exchanges showed foreign institutional investors (FIIs) net selling shares valued at approximately ₹19,000 crore during the trading session. This outflow ranked among the largest single‑day FII exits in recent months, underscoring a swift reversal of earlier inflows that had supported the market rally. Analysts attribute the move to profit‑taking after a prolonged upswing, coupled with concerns over tightening financial conditions in major economies.

Oil Price Drop and Its Ripple Effect

Simultaneously, benchmark crude oil prices experienced a steep decline, slipping more than three percent to trade near $78 per barrel. The drop was driven by expectations of softer demand from China and a stronger US dollar, which together reduced the commodity’s appeal. Energy‑sector stocks, which hold a significant weight in the Nifty, felt immediate pressure, with several oil‑and‑gas companies recording declines of over two percent. The lower oil outlook also eased near‑term inflation concerns, providing a modest relief to bond markets.

Sector‑wise Impact and Investor Takeaways

Energy

Exploration and production firms faced the brunt of the oil price slide, while refining margins showed mixed reactions depending on feedstock costs. Investors with heavy exposure to upstream assets may need to reassess position sizes and consider hedging strategies.

Banking

Bank stocks reacted negatively to the FII outflow, as foreign ownership in large private sector lenders remains substantial. Rising yields and potential credit‑quality pressures in a slowing global environment added to the sector’s woes.

Information Technology

IT services displayed relative resilience, buoyed by steady dollar‑denominated revenue streams and limited direct commodity exposure. However, the sector remained sensitive to broader risk‑off sentiment, leading to modest intraday corrections.
