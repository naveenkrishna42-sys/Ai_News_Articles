Delhi High Court Orders Winding Up of Paytm Payments Bank

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi High Court ordered winding up of Paytm Payments Bank on RBI's petition.
  • The bank must stop all operations and hand over records to a liquidator.
  • RBI cited persistent non-compliance with banking regulations and supervisory concerns.
  • Customer deposits up to Rs 5 lakh are insured under the DICGC scheme.

Court Directs Immediate Closure

The Delhi High Court on Tuesday ordered the winding up of Paytm Payments Bank Ltd (PPBL), acting on a petition filed by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The court directed the bank to cease all operations and appointed a liquidator to oversee the process.

The order comes after months of regulatory friction between the RBI and the payments bank, which is promoted by One97 Communications. The central bank had earlier restricted PPBL from accepting new deposits and conducting credit transactions.

RBI's Case Against the Bank

In its petition, the RBI argued that PPBL had repeatedly violated banking norms, including know-your-customer (KYC) requirements and outsourcing guidelines. The central bank said the bank's functioning was "detrimental to the interests of depositors and the financial system."

The court accepted the RBI's submission that the bank's continued operations posed a systemic risk. The bench noted that the bank had failed to address supervisory concerns despite multiple warnings and show-cause notices.

Impact on Customers and Deposits

The winding-up order affects millions of account holders who used PPBL for transactions, including Unified Payments Interface (UPI) payments, savings accounts, and FASTag services. The bank had a significant customer base among small merchants and gig economy workers.

Depositors' money is protected under the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) scheme, which covers up to Rs 5 lakh per depositor per bank. The RBI has assured that claims will be processed expeditiously. Users have been advised to link their UPI handles and other services to alternative bank accounts.

What Happens Next

The court has appointed a provisional liquidator to take control of the bank's assets and records. The liquidator will invite claims from depositors and creditors within 30 days. The bank's license, issued under the Banking Regulation Act, stands cancelled with immediate effect.

Paytm's parent company, One97 Communications, had earlier moved out of the payments bank business by selling its stake in PPBL. The company has said it will cooperate with the liquidation process. The court has directed the liquidator to submit a status report within three months.

  • All PPBL accounts are frozen except for withdrawal of insured deposits.
  • UPI handles linked to PPBL will need to be migrated to other banks.
  • FASTag users should switch to alternative providers to avoid toll plaza disruptions.

The RBI has stated that the winding-up order is a necessary step to maintain discipline in the banking sector. It remains to be seen how this development affects the broader fintech ecosystem and investor confidence in digital payment firms.
