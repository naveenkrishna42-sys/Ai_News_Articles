Global Warming to Exceed 1.5°C Limit: UN Report Reveals Overshoot Risk

The United Nations has released a landmark report indicating that global warming is on track to exceed the critical 1.5°C limit, with projections suggesting a rise to at least 1.8°C. This report, titled 'Limiting Overshoot - Navigating exceedance of 1.5°C and pathways towards return,' outlines the potential consequences and the necessary actions to mitigate further warming.

Projected Warming and Immediate Risks

According to the UN report, global temperatures are expected to rise to at least 1.8°C above pre-industrial levels by the end of the century. This overshoot of the 1.5°C limit is projected to occur by 2040, with the risk of exceeding 2°C by 2060. The report emphasizes that even a temporary exceedance of the 1.5°C limit could lead to severe and irreversible impacts on the planet.

Pathways to Return Below the Danger Zone

The UN report outlines several pathways that could potentially bring global temperatures back below the 1.5°C limit within 20 years. These pathways involve significant reductions in greenhouse gas emissions, increased use of renewable energy, and enhanced carbon capture and storage technologies.

Key Pathways

  • Rapid Emissions Reduction: Immediate and substantial cuts in greenhouse gas emissions to slow down the rate of warming.
  • Renewable Energy Transition: Accelerated adoption of renewable energy sources to replace fossil fuels.
  • Carbon Capture and Storage: Deployment of advanced carbon capture and storage technologies to remove CO2 from the atmosphere.

Urgent Action Required

The UN report underscores the urgency of taking immediate action to avoid the severe impacts of climate change. The report highlights the need for global cooperation and significant investment in sustainable technologies to mitigate the risks of overshooting the 1.5°C limit.

Key Recommendations

  • Global Cooperation: Enhanced international collaboration to share best practices and technologies.
  • Investment in Sustainability: Increased funding for research and development of renewable energy and carbon capture technologies.
  • Policy Implementation: Strong and effective policies at the national and international levels to drive emissions reductions.
