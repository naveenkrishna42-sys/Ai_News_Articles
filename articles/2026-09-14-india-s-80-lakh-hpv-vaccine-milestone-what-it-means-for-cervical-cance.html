India's HPV Vaccination Drive Crosses 80 Lakh Doses

India has administered more than 80 lakh (8 million) HPV vaccine doses under its nationwide cervical cancer prevention campaign, according to reporting by BW Healthcare World and corroborated by The Telegraph and Medical Dialogues. The milestone was reached in roughly six months, with free vaccination now covering close to two-thirds of the country's 14-year-old girls.

The programme targets cervical cancer, which remains the second-most common cancer among Indian women. India records an estimated 1.2 lakh new cervical cancer cases and over 75,000 deaths annually — a burden that a well-executed HPV immunisation drive can sharply reduce over the next two decades.

Why The 80 Lakh Figure Matters

Reaching 80 lakh doses in one campaign cycle is a supply-chain and cold-chain achievement, not just a headline number. HPV vaccines require strict temperature control from warehouse to sub-centre, and the campaign has leaned on existing immunisation infrastructure built during the universal immunisation and COVID-19 drives.

Two-thirds coverage among 14-year-olds is a strong early signal. At this pace, India can plausibly approach the World Health Organization's 90% HPV vaccination target for adolescent girls, provided second-dose completion and school-based delivery hold up in the coming quarters.

States Leading The Coverage Race

Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Mizoram have reported 100% coverage, per ThePrint. The presence of high-population, high-burden states like Uttar Pradesh and Madhya Pradesh in this list is significant — it shows the campaign is not confined to better-resourced southern or western states.

Mizoram's full coverage also demonstrates that smaller northeastern states can serve as delivery models for hard-to-reach geographies. District-level microplanning, school tie-ups and ASHA worker mobilisation appear to be the common thread across these performers.

Real-World Implications And Gaps

  • Strong first-dose momentum: Two-thirds coverage in six months signals that parents and schools are accepting the vaccine at scale.
  • Second-dose risk: HPV regimens require completion; dropout between doses could dilute real-world protection if follow-up systems are weak.
  • High-burden state participation: UP and MP hitting 100% is a meaningful equity gain, since these states carry a large share of cervical cancer mortality.
  • Data transparency gap: State-wise dashboards and age-disaggregated data remain limited, making independent verification of coverage quality harder.
  • Boys excluded: The current campaign focuses on girls, leaving male HPV transmission and related cancers largely unaddressed.

What Comes Next

The immediate priority is converting first-dose coverage into full regimen completion, particularly in states still below the two-thirds mark. Sustained domestic vaccine manufacturing — including India's indigenous HPV vaccine — will determine whether the programme can scale without supply shocks.

For public health observers, the 80 lakh milestone is best read as a proof of concept. If India holds coverage above 80% through adolescence, cervical cancer incidence could fall substantially by the 2040s, making this one of the highest-return preventive health investments the country has made.
