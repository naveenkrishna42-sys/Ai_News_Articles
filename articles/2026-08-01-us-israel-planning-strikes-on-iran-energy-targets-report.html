US, Israel planning strikes on Iran energy targets: report

📅 2026-08-01 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • CBS News reports US and Israel are planning strikes on energy-related targets in Iran
  • Trump says 'We've done an amazing job' as new attacks are considered within days
  • Iran warns of strikes on Israeli and US energy infrastructure in retaliation

Washington/Tel Aviv: The United States and Israel are reportedly preparing to bombard energy-related targets in Iran, according to a CBS News report. The development comes as former President Donald Trump indicated that fresh attacks on Iranian soil could be launched within days.

Strikes on energy infrastructure

Citing unnamed officials, CBS said the two allies are coordinating on military action aimed at crippling Iran's energy sector. The report did not specify which facilities—oil refineries, gas terminals, or power plants—would be in the crosshairs, but the strategic objective appears to be cutting off revenue streams that fund Tehran's regional activities.

The news has already sent tremors through global oil markets, with traders bracing for potential supply disruptions. Neither the Pentagon nor Israel's military has officially commented on the report, but the silence from both sides is telling.

'We've done an amazing job'

Trump, in a brief interaction with reporters, said, "We've done an amazing job," referring to the pressure campaign against Iran. Axios, citing sources, reported that Trump is actively considering strikes on Iranian energy targets within days, a move that would mark a significant escalation in the ongoing confrontation.

The White House has not issued a formal statement, but officials familiar with the deliberations say the option of hitting energy infrastructure is "very much on the table." The exact timing and scope of any operation remain unclear, with military planners said to be weighing the risks of Iranian retaliation.

Iran's warning

Tehran has responded with a stark warning of its own. Iranian officials have threatened strikes on Israeli and US energy infrastructure if the reported plans go ahead. This reciprocal threat raises the spectre of a wider regional conflict, one that could draw in Gulf states and disrupt shipping lanes in the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran's energy sector is the backbone of its economy, and any sustained bombing campaign would likely cause severe damage. In recent years, the country has faced sabotage and cyberattacks on its nuclear and oil facilities, but a coordinated US-Israel operation would be of a different magnitude.

Diplomatic fallout

The reported plans have already drawn sharp criticism from several quarters. Russia and China have called for restraint, while European allies have urged Washington to return to diplomatic channels. The UN Security Council is expected to hold an emergency session if strikes are confirmed.

For now, the world watches as two adversaries edge closer to a direct clash. The coming days will test whether this is a rhetorical bluff or a prelude to a full-scale assault on Iran's energy lifeline.

As the situation develops, the focus will be on the White House's next move and whether Tehran makes good on its threat to hit back at US and Israeli assets. The window for de-escalation is narrowing, but it is not yet closed.
