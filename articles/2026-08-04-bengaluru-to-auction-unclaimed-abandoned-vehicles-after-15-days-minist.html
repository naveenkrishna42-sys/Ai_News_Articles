Bengaluru to auction unclaimed abandoned vehicles after 15 days: Minister

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru will auction unclaimed abandoned vehicles after 15 days under the GBA drive.
  • Minister Krishna Byre Gowda made the announcement, aiming to clear city roads.
  • Vehicle owners have a 15-day window to claim their vehicles before auction.
  • The move is part of a broader effort to ease traffic congestion in Bengaluru.

Bengaluru Development Minister Krishna Byre Gowda announced on Tuesday that unclaimed abandoned vehicles will be auctioned after 15 days under the GBA (Greater Bengaluru Authority) drive. The decision aims to clear city roads of derelict vehicles that have been occupying precious space and adding to traffic woes.

The minister said the move is part of a larger push to streamline urban mobility and reclaim public spaces. Vehicles left unattended for an extended period, often for months, have become a common sight across Bengaluru, particularly in commercial hubs and residential areas.

What is the GBA drive?

The GBA drive is an ongoing initiative by the Bengaluru Development Authority to address infrastructure and civic issues in the city. Under this umbrella, the auction of abandoned vehicles is one of several measures to improve road usability and reduce clutter.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total number of vehicles that could be affected, but the drive is expected to target vehicles parked illegally or left unused for long stretches. The 15-day notice period gives owners a final chance to claim their property before it goes under the hammer.

How will the auction process work?

Once the 15-day window closes, unclaimed vehicles will be towed to designated lots and auctioned through a transparent process. The minister did not specify whether the auction would be online or offline, but sources suggest a digital platform could be used to ensure wider participation.

Proceeds from the auctions are likely to be directed to the state exchequer or used for civic improvements, though details are yet to be finalised. Vehicle owners who miss the deadline will lose their right to the vehicle, with no further recourse.

Impact on Bengaluru's traffic

Abandoned vehicles have long been a headache for Bengaluru's traffic police and municipal authorities. They block parking spaces, narrow roads, and sometimes become shelters for anti-social elements. The auction drive is expected to free up significant road space, especially in areas like MG Road, Koramangala, and Whitefield.

Residents have welcomed the decision, though some have raised concerns about the short notice period. "Fifteen days is enough if you're in town, but what about people who are away for work?" asked a local shopkeeper, who did not wish to be named. The minister has assured that adequate public notices will be issued, including through local media and civic body websites.

What happens next?

The Bengaluru Development Authority is expected to release a detailed schedule of the drive in the coming days, including zones and timelines. Owners of abandoned vehicles should act quickly to claim their property or risk losing it.

As the GBA drive gathers pace, the focus will be on ensuring a smooth, dispute-free auction process. The city will be watching to see if this initiative brings lasting relief to its congested streets.
