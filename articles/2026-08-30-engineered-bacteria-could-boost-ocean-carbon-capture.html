Engineered Bacteria Could Revolutionize Ocean Carbon Capture

Researchers at the Indian Institute of Science have discovered that engineered bacteria could capture 2.6 times more carbon than natural processes. The study, published in the journal Nature Sustainability, suggests that these bacteria could be used to create artificial reefs or seaweed farms, which would help to remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.

How the Engineered Bacteria Work

The engineered bacteria are able to capture carbon dioxide from the ocean and convert it into organic matter. This process is known as carbon fixation, and it is a natural process that occurs in the ocean. However, the engineered bacteria are able to fix carbon dioxide more efficiently than natural processes.

The bacteria are engineered to express a gene from a marine bacterium called Prochlorococcus, which is responsible for carbon fixation in the ocean. The gene is then inserted into a strain of Escherichia coli, a common laboratory bacterium.

This could have a significant impact on the fight against climate change. Carbon capture and storage (CCS) technologies are seen as a key part of the solution to climate change. However, CCS technologies are expensive and energy-intensive. The use of engineered bacteria could make CCS technologies more affordable and efficient.
