Sensex Today: 600-Point Fall Puts Tech Under the Scanner

The Sensex dropped 600 points in Tuesday's session on September 15, 2026, with technology stocks absorbing the bulk of the selling pressure. The fall is not a broad-based panic; it is concentrated in IT and new-age tech counters that have run ahead of near-term earnings visibility. For retail investors, the key question is whether this is a routine pullback or the start of a deeper correction.

Market breadth was weak but not disorderly. Banking and FMCG names held relatively firm, which suggests the decline is sector-specific rather than a macro risk-off event. That distinction matters when deciding whether to average down or wait for confirmation.

Why Tech Stocks Are Leading the Decline

Indian IT majors derive a large share of revenue from US and European clients. When global tech spending commentary turns cautious, the sector re-rates faster than the broader market. Currency movement adds another layer: a weaker rupee helps margins, but sharp swings create hedging uncertainty for mid-tier firms.

New-age tech listings, including food delivery and fintech platforms, remain sensitive to rate expectations. Any delay in US rate cuts compresses the present value of future earnings, which hits high-multiple stocks first. This explains why a 600-point Sensex fall can translate into a 1.5-2.5% drawdown in individual tech counters.

Real-World Implications for Buyers and Investors

A 600-point Sensex move sounds dramatic, but in percentage terms it depends on the index level. If the Sensex is trading near 80,000, a 600-point fall is under 1%. If it is closer to 60,000, the same move is closer to 1%. Investors should always convert point drops into percentages before reacting.

For SIP investors, days like this change little. For lump-sum buyers, staggered entry across tech and non-tech sectors reduces timing risk. Traders holding leveraged positions in IT futures or options face margin pressure and should reassess stop-loss levels.

Pros and Cons of Buying the Tech Dip Today

  • Pro: Large-cap IT firms offer dividend yield and strong balance sheets, cushioning further downside.
  • Pro: Corrections often create entry points for long-term investors with a 3-5 year horizon.
  • Con: Earnings visibility for FY27 remains unclear if US clients delay discretionary spending.
  • Con: Mid-cap and small-cap tech stocks can fall 2-3x more than large caps in a sustained selloff.
  • Con: Rupee volatility makes near-term margin forecasts unreliable for exporters.

What to Track Before the Next Session

Watch foreign institutional investor (FII) flows, the rupee-dollar rate, and overnight US tech earnings. A single strong US tech result can reverse sentiment within a session. Conversely, weak guidance from a major global client can extend the fall in Indian IT counters.

Domestic cues matter too. Any commentary from the RBI on rates or liquidity will influence banking and rate-sensitive sectors, which currently act as the market's shock absorber. Keep an eye on advance-decline ratios rather than headline index points.

Bottom Line for Investors

The 600-point Sensex fall is a tech-led correction, not a broad market breakdown. Large-cap IT stocks remain fundamentally sound but face near-term earnings uncertainty. Investors with a long horizon can use staggered buying; short-term traders should respect stop-losses and avoid averaging into leveraged positions.
