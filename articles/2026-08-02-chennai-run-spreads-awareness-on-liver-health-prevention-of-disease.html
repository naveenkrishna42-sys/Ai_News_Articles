Chennai run spreads awareness on liver health, prevention of disease

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The run aimed to educate the public on liver health and disease prevention.
  • Participants highlighted the importance of early detection and healthy lifestyles.
  • The event was held in Chennai, with organisers stressing regular check-ups.

Chennai residents laced up their running shoes on Sunday morning for a unique cause — liver health. The event, a run organised to spread awareness about liver diseases and their prevention, drew participants of all ages to the city's streets, combining physical activity with a serious public health message.

A morning for a vital organ

The run was conceived as a simple but effective way to engage the public. Organisers said the idea was to use the energy of a mass-participation event to get people talking about an organ that often gets ignored until something goes wrong. The liver, they pointed out, is central to digestion, metabolism, and detoxification, yet many are unaware of the everyday habits that can damage it.

Participants gathered early, with many wearing themed t-shirts and carrying placards with messages about liver care. The atmosphere was upbeat, but the underlying message was serious: liver diseases are on the rise, and much of it is preventable.

Prevention over cure

Medical professionals present at the event stressed that lifestyle is a major factor in liver health. They spoke about the dangers of excessive alcohol consumption, fatty diets, and sedentary routines, all of which contribute to conditions like fatty liver disease. The emphasis was on moderation and regular screening.

“The liver is remarkably resilient, but it has its limits,” one doctor at the venue said, explaining that early detection can make a significant difference in treatment outcomes. The run served as a reminder that a healthy lifestyle is the first line of defence against chronic liver conditions.

The silent threat of fatty liver

One of the key messages of the morning was about non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), a condition that is increasingly common in urban India. Often symptomless in its early stages, it can progress to more severe forms of liver damage if left unchecked. The run organisers highlighted that routine health checks, including simple blood tests and ultrasounds, are essential for catching such issues early.

Diet, too, came under focus. Nutritionists at the event advised cutting down on processed foods and refined sugars, which are known contributors to fat accumulation in the liver. They recommended a diet rich in vegetables, fruits, and whole grains, along with regular exercise.

Community participation

The run saw participation from school children, college students, and senior citizens alike. For many, it was a personal cause. Some participants shared that they had family members affected by liver disease, which motivated them to take part in the awareness drive.

Local organisations and hospitals also set up stalls at the venue, offering free health check-ups and distributing informational pamphlets. The initiative appeared to strike a chord with the crowd, as several runners were seen stopping by the stalls after finishing the course.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of participants, but the turnout reflected a growing public interest in preventive healthcare. The event concluded with a pledge ceremony, where attendees vowed to adopt healthier habits and encourage their families to do the same.

As the morning sun rose higher, the message of the day was clear: a healthy liver is a cornerstone of overall well-being. Organisers said they plan to make this an annual affair, hoping to reach an even larger audience next year and turn awareness into sustained action.
