GBA calls for large turnout at Bengaluru Hasiruru green drive

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • GBA appeals for widespread public participation in Bengaluru Hasiruru
  • Campaign focuses on increasing green cover in Bengaluru
  • Residents and organizations encouraged to join the initiative

The Garden City's green future hinges on collective action, and the Gandhibazar Association (GBA) is making a fervent appeal to Bengalureans to turn out in large numbers for the 'Bengaluru Hasiruru' campaign. The initiative, aimed at significantly boosting the city's tree cover, needs more than just official push—it needs people power.

In a statement released on Saturday, GBA officials underscored that the success of the drive depends on active participation from residents, local businesses, and civic groups. The association has been a longstanding advocate for urban greening and sees this campaign as a pivotal moment for the city's environmental health.

A Call to Action for Every Resident

The GBA is not just asking for passive support. It wants residents to roll up their sleeves, pick up saplings, and get their hands dirty. The association has urged apartment complexes, residential welfare associations, and even individual households to dedicate time and resources to planting and nurturing trees in their neighbourhoods.

Officials pointed out that while the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) and other agencies have their roles, the ground-level impact will come from citizens taking ownership of their local environment. A tree planted today, they stressed, is a legacy for the next generation.

Why the Push for Participation Matters

Bengaluru has seen its green cover dwindle over the years, succumbing to rapid urbanisation and infrastructure projects. The 'Bengaluru Hasiruru' campaign is an attempt to reverse that trend, but it cannot succeed on sapling distribution alone. Survival rates of young trees depend heavily on aftercare—watering, guarding against pests, and ensuring they aren't damaged by construction or vehicular movement.

The GBA's appeal highlights a simple truth: a government-run drive without community involvement often fizzles out. By contrast, when residents treat trees as their own, the chances of them thriving multiply manifold. The association has promised to provide guidance and saplings to interested groups, making participation easier for those willing to contribute.

Community and Organisations on Board

Several resident welfare associations and local NGOs have already signalled their support, but the GBA wants more. The campaign is expected to unfold across multiple phases, with different wards and zones being targeted over the coming months. Schools and colleges have also been approached to involve students, turning the drive into an educational experience as much as an environmental one.

Local businesses, too, are being roped in to sponsor saplings and maintenance efforts. The GBA believes that a collaborative approach will not only increase the number of trees planted but also foster a sense of community pride and responsibility.

Challenges Ahead

Despite the enthusiasm, challenges remain. Water scarcity during summer months, encroachment on tree pits, and lack of awareness in certain pockets of the city are hurdles that the campaign will need to overcome. The GBA has acknowledged these issues but remains optimistic, noting that sustained public pressure can lead to better civic support and infrastructure for green initiatives.

Officials have not yet confirmed specific targets for the number of trees to be planted, but they have indicated that the scale will be substantial. The association is also exploring the use of technology to map planting sites and track growth, ensuring transparency and accountability.

As the campaign gears up, the GBA is leaving no stone unturned to get the word out. Social media campaigns, door-to-door appeals, and partnerships with local influencers are all part of the outreach strategy. The message is clear: Bengaluru's green future is in the hands of its citizens.

The coming weeks will reveal how many heeded the call. For now, the GBA is counting on the city's famed civic spirit to turn 'Bengaluru Hasiruru' from a slogan into a movement. The first planting events are expected to be announced shortly, and all eyes will be on the turnout.
