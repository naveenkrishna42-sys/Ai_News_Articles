Bengaluru RTO seizes 263 bike taxis in fresh crackdown

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • RTO seized 263 bike taxis in Bengaluru in a fresh crackdown
  • Drive targets two-wheeler taxis operating without valid permits
  • Authorities warn of continued enforcement against violations

Bengaluru's transport department has intensified its action against bike taxi aggregators, seizing 263 two-wheelers in a fresh enforcement drive. The crackdown, carried out across multiple zones in the city, targets vehicles operating without valid permits, officials said on Wednesday.

The drive comes months after the state government's earlier attempts to regulate bike taxi services, which have sparked legal tussles and pushback from aggregator platforms. Regional Transport Office (RTO) teams conducted inspections at major junctions, tech parks, and metro stations, impounding vehicles found in violation of the Karnataka Motor Vehicles Rules.

Renewed enforcement after lull

The latest seizures mark a significant escalation, following a period of relative quiet on the enforcement front. Officials said the operation was triggered by complaints from conventional auto and cab drivers, who allege unfair competition from bike taxis operating legally in a grey area.

"We have been receiving multiple grievances, and our field units have been instructed to act against any two-wheeler taxi plying without proper authorisation," a senior transport official said. The 263 vehicles seized over the past few days have been moved to various RTO yards across the city.

What the rules say

Under existing Karnataka regulations, bike taxis require a transport licence and must comply with specific safety and insurance norms. Aggregators like Rapido, Uber, and Ola have been operating in the city for years, but their legal status remains contested in court.

The state government had earlier proposed a dedicated policy for bike taxis, but it is yet to be notified. Until then, RTO authorities maintain that any bike taxi service without a permit is illegal and subject to seizure. Each impounded vehicle attracts a penalty, which owners must pay to secure its release.

Aggregators cry foul

Bike taxi platforms have criticised the move, arguing that it disrupts livelihoods of thousands of riders and reduces affordable mobility options for commuters. Industry representatives claim they have sought clarity from the government but have received no clear directive.

"We are operating in full compliance with the law as it stands, and we have approached the court for a resolution," a spokesperson for a leading aggregator said. The companies have previously obtained interim relief from the Karnataka High Court, which had restrained authorities from seizing vehicles until a final decision.

Commuters face uncertainty

For daily users, the crackdown adds to commuting uncertainty. Many rely on bike taxis for quick and inexpensive trips through congested city roads, especially during peak hours. "I use bike taxis almost every day to reach the metro station. If they are removed, my commute becomes longer and costlier," said Priya N., a software professional in Whitefield.

Transport officials, however, remain firm. "The law is clear. We will continue our drives until compliance is ensured," the official added, hinting that more checks are planned in the coming weeks.

The legal battle over bike taxis in Bengaluru is set to continue, with the High Court yet to deliver a final verdict on the validity of the state's rules. Until then, riders and aggregators face an uncertain road ahead, with enforcement likely to remain sporadic but sustained.
