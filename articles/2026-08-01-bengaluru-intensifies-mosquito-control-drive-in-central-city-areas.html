Bengaluru intensifies mosquito-control drive in central city areas

📅 2026-08-01 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • BBMP has intensified mosquito-control measures in Central City Corporation areas
  • Drive includes fogging, anti-larval spraying, and removal of breeding sites
  • Residents urged to cooperate in keeping surroundings clean

Drive intensified amid monsoon concerns

The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has stepped up its mosquito-control operations across the Central City Corporation areas, officials said on Friday. The move comes as intermittent rains in the city have created favourable conditions for mosquito breeding, raising concerns over vector-borne diseases.

Field teams have been deployed in multiple wards, focusing on areas with high mosquito density and reported cases of dengue and malaria. The drive is part of a broader seasonal strategy to curb the spread of disease before the peak transmission period.

Fogging and source reduction in focus

Anti-larval teams are conducting fogging operations in residential neighbourhoods, parks, and storm-water drains. Officials said special attention is being given to construction sites, vacant plots, and discarded containers where stagnant water tends to accumulate.

“We are not just fogging; we are also identifying and eliminating breeding sources,” a BBMP health officer said. The civic body has instructed its staff to clear blocked drains and remove solid waste that can hold water, particularly after rainfall.

Residents asked to pitch in

The BBMP has appealed to residents to check their premises for stagnant water in coolers, flower pots, and other household items. Door-to-door awareness campaigns are being carried out in affected pockets, with pamphlets distributed in Kannada and English.

Citizens are also being urged to report mosquito hotspots through the civic body’s helpline and mobile app. Officials said prompt reporting helps teams respond faster and prevents the situation from worsening.

Health surveillance remains key

While the primary focus is on larval control, health authorities are also tracking fever cases in the central zones. Surveillance teams have been asked to share data on suspected dengue and chikungunya cases with the health department for early intervention.

Hospitals in the area have been alerted to keep diagnostic facilities ready. However, officials have not yet confirmed any spike in cases, saying the drive is a precautionary measure.

The intensified drive is expected to continue over the next few weeks, with weekly reviews by senior BBMP officials. The civic body has said it will adjust its strategy based on rainfall patterns and ground feedback, ensuring that mosquito control remains proactive rather than reactive.
