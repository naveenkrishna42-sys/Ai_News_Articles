DMK, BJP and AIADMK join hands over Chennai civic issues

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • DMK, BJP, and AIADMK leaders held a rare joint meeting on Chennai's civic issues.
  • The meeting focused on flooding, road conditions, and garbage disposal in the city.
  • All three parties agreed to form a common delegation to approach the state government.

CHENNAI: Political rivals in Tamil Nadu set aside their fierce rivalry on Wednesday to speak in one voice about the crumbling civic infrastructure in Chennai. Leaders from the ruling DMK, the main opposition AIADMK, and the BJP shared a platform to flag the city's worsening problems.

Uncommon unity on common problems

The meeting, held at a private hall in T. Nagar, saw party functionaries from all three camps seated together. This is a rare sight in a state where political discourse is often marked by sharp exchanges and street protests.

Sources present at the gathering said the agenda was strictly limited to civic issues. Discussions revolved around waterlogging during the monsoon, pothole-riddled roads, overflowing garbage bins, and the slow pace of metro rail work in residential areas.

One DMK councillor, who did not wish to be named, told TIVRA News, "We have been raising these issues in the council for months. But when all parties speak together, the government has to listen."

Joint delegation planned

After nearly two hours of deliberation, the three parties agreed to form a joint delegation. This delegation will meet the Chennai Corporation commissioner and the state municipal administration minister next week.

BJP state secretary K. R. R. said, "This is not about politics. This is about the people of Chennai who suffer every day because of bad roads and flooding. We are ready to work with anyone to solve this."

AIADMK's deputy floor leader in the assembly echoed the sentiment. "We will not let political differences come in the way of the city's welfare. The DMK government must act now," he said.

Waterlogging tops the list

Among the issues discussed, waterlogging received the most attention. Leaders pointed out that many low-lying neighbourhoods still get submerged after just a few hours of rain. They demanded immediate desilting of major stormwater drains before the northeast monsoon arrives.

Road safety was another major concern. Leaders cited recent accidents on the Chennai-Bengaluru highway stretch within the city limits, where deep potholes have caused injuries to motorcyclists.

Garbage collection was flagged as an "everyday nightmare" by one participant. The leaders noted that several wards in North Chennai have not seen regular waste collection for weeks.

What this means for politics

Political analysts say this rare camaraderie is a sign of growing public pressure on elected representatives. With local body elections due next year, all parties are keen to show they are working for the people, not just fighting each other.

However, whether this unity will last beyond the issue of civic infrastructure remains to be seen. The three parties are expected to be back at each other's throats on national and state-level policy matters soon.

For now, Chennai's residents can hope that a united political voice might finally force the authorities to act. The joint delegation is expected to submit its memorandum within the next ten days.
