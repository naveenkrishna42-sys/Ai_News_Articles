RBI set to resume urban co-operative bank licences

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI to restart urban co-operative bank licensing after a pause
  • Move signals regulatory confidence in the sector's stability
  • New licences could boost financial inclusion in urban areas

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is preparing to resume issuing licences for urban co-operative banks, a move that marks a notable shift in its regulatory approach after a prolonged halt. The central bank's decision comes after a period of heightened scrutiny and consolidation within the sector, which had seen several weak lenders either merged or shut down.

Sources familiar with the matter indicate that the RBI is now comfortable with the improved health of many urban co-operative banks, following years of cleanup and stricter oversight. The resumption of licensing is expected to open the doors for new entrants, particularly in under-served urban and semi-urban areas where these banks play a vital role in local lending.

Why the pause mattered

The licensing freeze was part of a broader RBI strategy to stabilise the co-operative banking sector, which faced a series of crises, most notably the collapse of Punjab and Maharashtra Co-operative (PMC) Bank in 2019. That incident exposed deep governance lapses and prompted the central bank to impose stringent regulatory measures, including tighter capital norms and frequent supervisory audits.

During the freeze, the RBI also facilitated mergers of smaller, weaker banks with stronger counterparts, a process that has reportedly improved the overall resilience of the system. The central bank's comfort now likely stems from these structural improvements, which have reduced the risk of systemic failures.

What this means for the sector

For the co-operative banking ecosystem, the resumption of licensing is a shot in the arm. New licences would allow promoters to set up banks in localities where traditional commercial banks have limited presence, thereby deepening credit penetration. It also signals that the RBI views the sector as a credible partner in its financial inclusion agenda, rather than a perpetual liability.

However, the central bank is expected to enforce strict entry criteria. Applicants will likely need to demonstrate robust governance frameworks, adequate capital, and a clear business plan. The RBI has not yet specified a timeline for accepting applications, but industry watchers expect guidelines to be issued in the coming months.

Challenges remain

Despite the optimism, the sector continues to face hurdles. Many existing urban co-operative banks struggle with technology adoption and professional management, issues that could resurface as new players enter the fray. The RBI's supervisory capacity is also a concern, given the sheer number of such banks across the country.

Analysts say the key test will be whether the RBI can balance growth with vigilance. While the resumption of licensing is a positive signal, it must be accompanied by robust monitoring to prevent a repeat of past lapses.

The central bank's decision is part of a wider effort to strengthen the co-operative banking framework, including the recent enactment of the Banking Regulation (Amendment) Act, which brought these lenders under stricter RBI oversight. The move is also in line with the government's push to expand banking access to every corner of the country.

Looking ahead

Observers will closely watch the RBI's formal notification, which is expected to outline the application process, eligibility norms, and capital requirements. The central bank's willingness to resume licensing after years of caution suggests it believes the sector is now on firmer footing, but only time will tell if new entrants can sustain the discipline required.

The next few months will be crucial as the RBI finalises its framework and potential applicants start preparing their pitches. For now, the announcement offers a fresh start for urban co-operative banking in India, with the promise of greater financial access for millions.
