Sensex drops 300 points, Nifty ends below 23,800: key reasons

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex dropped 300 points, Nifty 50 ended below 23,800.
  • Weak global cues and sustained FII outflows weighed on sentiment.
  • IT, banking and auto stocks led the decline.
  • Investors await next week's macroeconomic data for direction.

Markets take a hit in Friday's session

Indian benchmark indices ended in the red on Friday, with the Sensex falling 300 points and the Nifty 50 closing below the 23,800 mark. The sell-off erased gains from the previous session, as concerns over global economic growth and persistent foreign capital outflows dampened investor sentiment.

The BSE Sensex opened lower and stayed in negative territory throughout the day, closing near the day's low. The broader Nifty 50 also struggled, slipping below the psychologically important 23,800 level. Volatility was elevated, with the India VIX rising over 4% during the session.

FII selling and weak global cues weigh

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) continued their selling spree, adding pressure on domestic equities. According to provisional data, FIIs were net sellers in the cash market, extending a trend seen over the past few sessions. This selling was triggered by a cautious stance ahead of key US economic data and rising bond yields in developed markets.

Asian markets also traded mixed to lower on Friday, tracking overnight losses on Wall Street. The US dollar strengthened, which typically leads to capital outflows from emerging markets like India. The rupee weakened slightly against the dollar, adding to the nervousness among traders.

Sectoral drag: IT, banking and auto lead losses

Most sectoral indices ended in the red, with IT, banking and auto stocks taking the biggest hit. The Nifty IT index fell over 1%, dragged by heavyweights like Infosys and TCS, amid concerns over a slowdown in IT spending in key markets like the US and Europe.

Banking stocks also faced selling pressure. The Nifty Bank index slipped nearly 0.8%, with HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and SBI contributing to the decline. Auto stocks, including Maruti Suzuki and Tata Motors, saw profit booking after recent gains. Only select pharma and FMCG stocks managed to hold ground, providing some support to the broader market.

Midcap and smallcap indices underperform

The broader market witnessed sharper cuts than the benchmarks. The BSE Midcap index fell over 1%, while the Smallcap index declined 1.2%. This indicates that risk appetite was low, and traders were reducing positions across the board. Analysts pointed out that valuations in the mid and smallcap segments remain elevated, making them vulnerable to corrections.

What to watch next week

Market participants will closely monitor key macroeconomic data due next week, including domestic inflation numbers and industrial production data. Globally, the US Federal Reserve's stance on interest rates and monthly jobs data will be in focus. Any surprise in these numbers could trigger further volatility.

For now, analysts suggest that the market may consolidate in a range, with the 23,500-23,800 band being crucial for the Nifty. Sustained FII inflows or a positive trigger from global markets could help the indices recover. Until then, caution is likely to prevail.
