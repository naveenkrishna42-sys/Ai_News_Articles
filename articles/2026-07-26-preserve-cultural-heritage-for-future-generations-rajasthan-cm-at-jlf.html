Preserve Cultural Heritage for Future Generations: Rajasthan CM at JLF

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rajasthan Chief Minister addressed the Jaipur Literature Festival.
  • He emphasised the need to preserve cultural heritage for future generations.
  • The CM urged the youth to connect with their roots and traditions.
  • JLF continues as a major platform for discussions on culture and literature.

Jaipur Literature Festival Draws CM's Call for Heritage

The Jaipur Literature Festival (JLF) saw a strong message from Rajasthan's Chief Minister on Thursday. Speaking at the event, he urged the audience to preserve cultural heritage for future generations. The CM stressed that traditions and history must not be lost in the rush of modernity.

His comments came during a session focused on the role of cultural institutions. The festival, now in its 19th edition, has become a global stage for such discussions. This year's lineup features writers, thinkers, and policymakers from across the world.

Why Heritage Matters Now

The CM warned that rapid urbanisation and digital distractions are eroding connections to the past. He called on young Indians to actively engage with their cultural roots. "Our heritage is not just in museums; it lives in our languages, crafts, and festivals," he said.

He pointed to Rajasthan's own rich history as an example. The state is known for its forts, folk music, and textiles. However, many traditional art forms face declining patronage and support. The CM suggested that literature festivals like JLF can help bridge this gap.

Youth as Custodians of Culture

A key part of his address focused on the younger generation. The CM argued that children should learn about local history and art in schools. He also encouraged families to pass down stories and skills at home.

"Technology can be a tool, not a replacement," he noted. He cited examples of digital archives and online platforms that document Rajasthani folklore. These efforts, he said, can make heritage accessible to urban youth.

JLF's Role in Cultural Discourse

The Jaipur Literature Festival has long hosted debates on identity and tradition. This year, sessions cover everything from ancient poetry to contemporary fiction. Organisers say the CM's speech aligns with the festival's core mission.

Many attendees welcomed the call for preservation. A local artist told TIVRA News, "We need more such reminders. Our crafts are dying because people don't value them." Others noted that government support remains uneven.

The CM did not announce any new policies during his speech. However, cultural activists expect follow-up action. The state has recently launched a scheme to support traditional artisans.

What Comes Next

The festival continues over the weekend with more panels on culture and literature. Organisers hope the CM's words will spark concrete steps. For now, the focus remains on dialogue and awareness.
