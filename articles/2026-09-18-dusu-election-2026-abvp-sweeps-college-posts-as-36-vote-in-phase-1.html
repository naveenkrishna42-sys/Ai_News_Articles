DUSU Election 2026: Phase One Closes at 36% Turnout

Polling for the Delhi University Students' Union (DUSU) Election 2026 concluded its first phase on Friday with an overall turnout of roughly 36%, according to preliminary figures reported by Hindustan Times. The figure sits within the historical band for DUSU elections, which typically swing between 30% and 45% depending on college, course, and the intensity of campus campaigning.

Phase one covered a majority of DU's constituent colleges, with voting conducted through ballot boxes under the supervision of the Chief Election Officer. The second phase, covering remaining colleges and departments, is expected to follow the same schedule notified by the university.

ABVP Claims Wins at Multiple Colleges

The Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP) has claimed victories at several colleges in the first phase, according to NDTV and Hindustan Times reports. The organisation's college-level wins matter because DUSU's central panel — President, Vice President, Secretary, and Joint Secretary — is decided by a university-wide vote, while college union posts are fought on local issues such as hostel facilities, canteen pricing, and internal assessment delays.

For students, college-level results often have more direct day-to-day impact than the central panel. A college president who controls the students' union budget can influence fee-waiver recommendations, event funding, and grievance escalation to the principal's office.

Delhi High Court Cracks Down on Violations

The Delhi High Court banned victory processions across DU campuses and warned of contempt action if the order was breached. The court also pulled up both ABVP and NSUI over poll code violations, asking pointedly, "Are you people students?" — a rebuke reported by The Hindu.

The HC further threatened to stay the counting process over violence during polling. Congress leader Kanhaiya Kumar alleged harassment of NSUI workers, a charge that adds political weight to an election already being watched closely in Delhi's wider political circles.

Why This Election Matters Beyond Campus

DUSU is among the most visible student bodies in India, and its results are read as a barometer of youth political sentiment in the national capital. A strong ABVP showing at college level strengthens its organisational claim ahead of the central panel count, while NSUI's performance will shape how the Congress positions itself with first-time voters.

The HC's intervention also sets a precedent: campus elections with documented violence can attract judicial stays, which raises the compliance bar for every future DUSU cycle.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Phase one turnout of 36% is healthy for DUSU, indicating strong campus engagement despite security concerns.
  • ABVP's college-level wins give it momentum, but central panel results remain the decisive metric.
  • The HC's ban on victory processions directly curbs the traditional post-result show of strength.
  • Violence allegations and harassment claims could delay or disrupt counting if the court acts on its stay warning.
  • Students should track college-level results for practical outcomes on fees, hostels, and academic grievances.

What to Watch Next

Counting schedules, the HC's next hearing, and the second phase of polling will determine the final shape of DUSU 2026. Students and observers should rely on official Delhi University notifications rather than social media claims, particularly where victory processions and result announcements are concerned.
