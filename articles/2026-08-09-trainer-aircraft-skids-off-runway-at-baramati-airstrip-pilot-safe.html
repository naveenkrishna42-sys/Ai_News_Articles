Trainer Aircraft Skids Off Runway at Baramati Airstrip, Pilot Safe

📅 2026-08-09 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • A trainer aircraft of a private company skidded off the runway at Baramati airstrip in Maharashtra on Sunday.
  • The pilot is reported safe; no casualties have been reported in the incident.
  • This is the second aircraft incident at the airstrip since the death of Maharashtra Deputy Chief Minister Ajit Pawar in a crash months ago.
  • Authorities have not yet confirmed the cause of the skid-off, and an investigation is expected.
  • The airstrip, used for training flights, has come under renewed scrutiny following the latest mishap.

A trainer aircraft operated by a private company skidded off the runway at the Baramati airstrip in Maharashtra on Sunday, officials said. The pilot escaped unhurt, and there were no reports of any casualties. The incident marks the second such mishap at the airstrip since the fatal crash that killed Maharashtra Deputy Chief Minister Ajit Pawar earlier this year.

What Happened at Baramati Airstrip

The aircraft, a trainer used for flying lessons, veered off the tarmac during what officials described as a routine operation. Visuals from the spot showed the aircraft resting on the ground adjacent to the runway, with emergency teams rushing to the site. The pilot was safely evacuated, and the airstrip was temporarily closed for operations.

Local authorities have not yet confirmed the sequence of events leading to the skid-off. Weather conditions at the time of the incident were not immediately known. Police and aviation officials have reached the spot and are conducting preliminary checks.

Second Incident Since Ajit Pawar's Accident

The crash site holds particular significance. In April this year, Ajit Pawar, then Deputy Chief Minister of Maharashtra, died when his helicopter crashed near the same airstrip. That accident had triggered a high-level inquiry and raised questions about the safety protocols at smaller airstrips used by political leaders and training schools.

Sunday's incident is the second involving an aircraft at Baramati since that tragedy. While the earlier crash involved a helicopter, the latest one involved a fixed-wing trainer. The repeated mishaps have put the spotlight back on the airstrip's infrastructure and the adequacy of safety measures for training flights.

No Casualties, Pilot Safe

Officials confirmed that the pilot, whose name has not been released, was safe and did not require hospitalisation. The aircraft, belonging to a private flying school, sustained visible damage. A senior police officer said an investigation has been initiated to determine whether the skid-off was due to a technical snag, pilot error, or runway conditions.

The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has been informed, though its officials have not yet issued a formal statement. The airstrip, which handles both civilian and training traffic, is expected to remain shut until the wreckage is cleared and the runway is inspected.

What This Means for Baramati's Air Operations

Baramati, a political stronghold of the Pawar family, has seen increased air traffic over the years. The airstrip is used by private charter companies, training institutes, and occasionally by VVIPs. Following Ajit Pawar's death, there were demands for stricter oversight of non-scheduled flight operations in the region.

Sunday's incident is likely to intensify those demands. Aviation experts point out that trainer aircraft, often used by student pilots, require well-maintained runways and clear communication with ground control. Any lapse can turn a routine landing into a serious accident.

What Happens Next

The immediate focus is on completing the on-ground investigation and restoring the airstrip to operational status. The DGCA is expected to submit a preliminary report in the coming days, which will clarify whether the incident was an isolated technical failure or a sign of systemic issues. Meanwhile, local flying schools have been advised to exercise caution during take-off and landing procedures. The coming weeks will show whether Baramati's airstrip gets upgraded safety measures or continues to operate under the shadow of repeated mishaps.
