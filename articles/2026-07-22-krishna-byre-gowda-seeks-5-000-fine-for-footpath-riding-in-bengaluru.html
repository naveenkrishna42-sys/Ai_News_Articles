Krishna Byre Gowda seeks ₹5,000 fine for footpath riding in Bengaluru

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Krishna Byre Gowda wrote to the Transport Minister proposing a ₹5,000 penalty for footpath riding.
  • Current fine of ₹500 is seen as too low to deter two-wheeler riders from using footpaths.
  • The proposal aims to improve pedestrian safety in Bengaluru's congested areas.

Minister pushes for stricter penalty on footpath riders

Bengaluru Revenue Minister Krishna Byre Gowda has written to the state Transport Minister, urging an immediate hike in the fine for riding two-wheelers on footpaths. He has proposed a penalty of ₹5,000, a significant jump from the current ₹500.

The move comes amid growing concerns over pedestrian safety in the city. Footpath riding has become a common menace, forcing people to walk on roads and risking accidents.

Why the current fine is not working

Gowda argued that the existing fine of ₹500 does little to deter violators. Many riders consider it a minor cost for the convenience of bypassing traffic.

He pointed out that footpaths are meant exclusively for pedestrians. Allowing motorised vehicles to use them erodes public trust in traffic enforcement.

Focus on pedestrian safety

The minister highlighted that Bengaluru's footpaths are already narrow and poorly maintained. Riders on footpaths make them unusable for the elderly, children, and persons with disabilities.

He noted that several pedestrian fatalities have occurred due to vehicles mounting footpaths. A higher fine would act as a strong deterrent.

What the government is considering

The Transport Department is expected to review the proposal in the coming weeks. Officials have not yet confirmed if the fine will be raised across the state or only in Bengaluru.

Traffic police have also been asked to intensify enforcement against footpath riding. The government is exploring the use of CCTV cameras to catch violators automatically.

Gowda's letter is part of a broader push to make Bengaluru's streets safer for non-motorised users. Similar proposals have been made for other traffic violations, including wrong-side driving.

The transport minister is likely to respond formally after consultations with traffic experts and the police department. A decision is expected before the next meeting of the state road safety council.
