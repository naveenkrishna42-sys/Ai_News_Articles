Noida Police files hate crime charges after Supreme Court rap in Muslim assault case

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court had earlier pulled up Noida Police for not filing hate crime charges in the assault case.
  • Noida Police has now invoked relevant hate crime sections against the accused.
  • The case involves an attack on a Muslim man in Noida, with communal undertones highlighted by the court.

Court's rap leads to police action

The Noida Police has added hate crime charges in a case involving the assault of a Muslim man, acting days after the Supreme Court pulled up the force for its handling of the investigation. The top court had questioned why the police had not invoked provisions related to hate crimes despite the communal angle in the attack.

Sources confirmed that the police have now slapped charges under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code that deal with acts committed on the basis of religion or community. The move comes after a stern observation by the Supreme Court bench, which noted that the initial investigation appeared to avoid the hate crime aspect of the case.

What the case is about

The incident involves a Muslim man who was allegedly assaulted in a Noida locality earlier this year. The victim had claimed that the attackers targeted him over his religious identity. The case had sparked concerns about communal violence in the region.

Following the assault, the police initially registered a case under non-hate crime sections. The victim's family and community leaders had protested, demanding that the police treat the attack as a hate crime. The matter reached the Supreme Court after a petition was filed seeking a fair investigation.

Supreme Court's intervention

During a hearing earlier this week, the Supreme Court expressed dissatisfaction with the Noida Police's approach. The bench remarked that the police seemed reluctant to apply hate crime laws despite clear evidence of a communal motive. The court gave the police a deadline to rectify the charges.

The court's rap prompted the police to review the case file and consult legal experts. Officials said the decision to invoke hate crime sections was taken after a thorough assessment of the evidence provided by the victim and witnesses.

Hate crime laws in focus

Hate crime provisions in India allow for enhanced punishment for crimes motivated by prejudice against a person's religion, caste, or ethnicity. Legal experts say the application of such laws can help deter communal violence and ensure justice for targeted victims.

In this case, the accused now faces charges that carry stricter penalties. The police have also been directed to record statements from key witnesses again and ensure no gaps remain in the investigation.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact sections invoked, but sources said the charges fall under the broader category of hate crimes. The accused is currently in judicial custody.

The victim's lawyer welcomed the development, stating that the Supreme Court's intervention was crucial in pushing the police to act. The case is now scheduled for further hearing in the top court next month.

Watch for the Supreme Court's next hearing, where it will review whether the police have complied with its orders and whether the investigation is being conducted without bias.
