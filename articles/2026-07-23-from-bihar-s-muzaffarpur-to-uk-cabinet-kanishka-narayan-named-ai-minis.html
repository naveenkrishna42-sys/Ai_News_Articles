From Bihar’s Muzaffarpur to UK cabinet: Kanishka Narayan named AI minister

📅 2026-07-23 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kanishka Narayan, a British-Indian Labour MP, has been promoted to Cabinet as UK's first AI minister.
  • Born in Muzaffarpur, Bihar, Narayan's family moved to the UK when he was a child.
  • PM Andy Burnham's reshuffle has swept away several Starmer supporters, elevating Narayan and Lisa Nandy.
  • The appointment comes amid the sudden dissolution of the UK's department of science, raising questions about research funding.

In a move that underscores the shifting priorities of the UK government, Prime Minister Andy Burnham has elevated British-Indian Labour MP Kanishka Narayan to the cabinet as the country's first dedicated minister for artificial intelligence. Narayan, born in Muzaffarpur, Bihar, now holds a brief that will shape Britain's response to one of the most transformative technologies of the century.

His promotion is part of a broader cabinet reshuffle that has seen several allies of former leader Keir Starmer swept aside.

From Muzaffarpur to Whitehall

Kanishka Narayan's journey is a classic immigrant success story. He was born in Muzaffarpur, a city in Bihar known for its lychee orchards, before his family moved to the United Kingdom when he was a child.

He studied at Oxford University and later worked as a management consultant before entering politics. Elected as a Labour MP in the last general election, Narayan quickly made a name for himself as a tech-savvy legislator with a focus on digital policy.

His rise to the cabinet is being watched closely by the Indian diaspora, which now has two ministers in Burnham's team — the other being Lisa Nandy, who is of Indian origin on her mother's side.

What the new AI ministry means

Narayan's appointment comes at a critical juncture. The UK government has suddenly dissolved its department of science, innovation and technology, merging its functions into other ministries.

The move has left researchers and industry bodies scrambling for clarity. The new AI minister will now be responsible for coordinating the government's strategy on artificial intelligence, including regulation, investment, and skills development.

Britain has been vying to position itself as a global hub for AI safety and innovation. Whether Narayan can steer that ambition through a reorganised bureaucracy remains to be seen.

A reshuffle that resets Labour's direction

PM Burnham's cabinet changes are being interpreted as a decisive break from the Starmer era. Several MPs who were closely associated with the former leader have been moved to backbench roles.

By promoting relative newcomers like Narayan, Burnham is signalling that his government will focus on future-facing industries rather than internal party battles. The inclusion of two Indian-origin ministers also reinforces the Labour government's commitment to diversity at the highest levels.

Narayan's portfolio, however, will be judged on delivery. AI policy involves thorny questions about job displacement, data privacy, and national security. As the first person to hold this specific cabinet post, he has no blueprint to follow.

The coming months will show whether the son of Muzaffarpur can help shape the future of British technology policy.
