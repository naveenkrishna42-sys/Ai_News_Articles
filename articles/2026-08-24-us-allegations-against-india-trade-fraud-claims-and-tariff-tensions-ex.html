US allegations against India: Trade fraud claims and tariff tensions explained

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-08-24 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US alleges India is part of a transhipment scam routing Chinese goods to evade tariffs
  • Washington also claims Indian tariff structures discriminate against American exports
  • Experts say allegations lack concrete proof and could worsen trade friction
  • India has denied the charges, calling for evidence-based dialogue
  • Broader US-China trade war is driving these accusations

What exactly is the US alleging?

The United States has levelled two broad sets of allegations against India. First, that Indian firms—along with entities in Azerbaijan, Georgia, Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan—are involved in a transhipment scam. The claim is that Chinese goods are being rerouted through these countries, including India, to dodge US import duties and anti-dumping measures.

Second, Washington has long complained that India's tariff structure is discriminatory. The US Trade Representative (USTR) has repeatedly flagged high duties on American products like motorcycles, whiskey and medical devices. These are not new grievances, but they are being revived with unusual intensity now.

The timing matters. These allegations come as the US-China trade war intensifies, and Washington is cracking down on customs fraud globally. The Financial Times recently noted that Donald Trump's war on customs fraud will be costly and difficult—a hint that the US is serious, but also that it may be overreaching.

Do the transhipment charges hold up?

So far, the evidence is thin. A report by the Washington-based Vajiram & Ravi group accuses these countries of facilitating Chinese exports, but independent analysts say the proof is circumstantial. The Hans India has reported that the US accusations lack concrete documentation.

Customs data shows some increase in Indian imports from China and re-exports to the US, but that is not proof of fraud. Many Indian firms legitimately process Chinese components and add value before exporting. The US would need to show specific instances of mislabelling or false country-of-origin declarations—something it has not done publicly.

There is also a geopolitical angle. The US is pressuring India to reduce its dependence on Chinese goods, especially in electronics and pharmaceuticals. Accusing India of transhipment could be a lever to force faster supply chain diversification—whether or not the fraud claims are fully proven.

Why the tariff allegations are more concrete

The tariff complaints are on firmer ground. India's average applied tariff on US goods is significantly higher than US tariffs on Indian goods. This asymmetry has been a persistent irritant in trade talks, and the US has every right to raise it under WTO rules.

However, India is not alone in this. Many developing countries maintain higher tariffs to protect domestic industry. India's recent tariff reductions on items like bourbon and motorcycles show it is willing to negotiate. But Washington wants faster, more sweeping changes—and may be using the fraud allegations as bargaining chips.

The bigger issue is that the US is shifting from multilateral dispute resolution to unilateral actions. This is a dangerous trend for global trade, as it undermines the WTO and creates uncertainty for businesses in both countries.

What does this mean for India-US ties?

These allegations could derail the momentum in bilateral relations. India and the US have deepened defence and technology ties, but trade remains a sore point. If Washington imposes punitive tariffs or sanctions on Indian firms, New Delhi will likely retaliate.

The broader impact is on global supply chains. India is positioning itself as an alternative manufacturing hub to China. If the US treats India as a suspect in customs fraud, that ambition takes a hit. Investors will worry about regulatory risks.

But there is room for pragmatism. Both sides need each other—the US wants India as a counterweight to China, and India needs US capital and technology. The smart move is to use these allegations as a catalyst for a comprehensive trade deal, rather than letting them spiral into a full-blown dispute.

  • Pros: Could push India to reform tariffs; may lead to stronger customs enforcement.
  • Cons: Risk of trade war; undermines trust; may hurt Indian exporters in the short term.

For now, the ball is in Washington's court. It must provide hard evidence or drop the transhipment claims. India, meanwhile, should accelerate its tariff rationalisation to remove legitimate grievances. If both sides act rationally, this can be managed. If not, it will be another scar on a relationship that has too much potential to waste on unproven allegations.
