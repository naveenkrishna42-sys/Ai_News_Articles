US war with Iran could weaken Asian defences against China, allies fear

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Wars & Conflicts Original source ↗
Key points
  • US allies in Asia fear a prolonged Iran conflict will drain American military resources.
  • Concerns centre on reduced naval presence and weakening of deterrence against China.
  • Officials in Tokyo, Seoul and other capitals are watching the situation closely.

America's treaty allies in Asia are growing uneasy. They fear that a widening war with Iran could drain the US military's attention and resources, leaving them exposed to an increasingly assertive China.

The anxiety, reported by Reuters, is not new. But it has sharpened as US forces redeploy carrier strike groups and air assets to the Middle East, raising questions about Washington's ability to maintain its commitments in the Indo-Pacific.

Worries over a thinning fleet

For decades, the cornerstone of Asian security has been the US Navy's forward presence. Carriers, destroyers and submarines based in Japan and Guam serve as a visible deterrent to Beijing's territorial ambitions in the South China Sea and beyond.

Yet every warship sent to the Gulf is one fewer hull in the Pacific. Allies in Tokyo, Seoul and Canberra are quietly calculating how many ships the Navy can spare — and the numbers are not reassuring.

"The US has to be everywhere at once, and it doesn't have enough ships to do that," one regional diplomat told Reuters. "An Iran conflict makes that math much worse."

Deterrence against China

The core worry is that a prolonged conflict would erode the credibility of US extended deterrence. If Beijing sees a distracted and overstretched America, it might be tempted to test its neighbours — in the East China Sea, the Taiwan Strait or along India's northern borders.

Japan and South Korea, both hosting tens of thousands of US troops, are particularly sensitive. Their defence planners are already drafting contingency scenarios that assume a reduced American combat presence for months or even years.

Australia, too, is recalibrating. Its 2024 defence review stressed the need to operate independently, but officials privately concede that no amount of AUKUS submarines can fully replace American carrier power in a crisis.

India watches from the wings

New Delhi has not publicly echoed these fears, but its strategic calculus is shifting. India's own military modernisation is aimed at countering China, and it relies on US intelligence-sharing and logistics support to do so.

A US-Iran war could also spike global oil prices, hitting India's economy hard. And a distracted America might be less willing to push back against Chinese incursions along the Line of Actual Control.

Indian officials have not commented on the record, but defence analysts note that New Delhi is quietly expanding its exercises with Quad partners and deepening ties with France and Japan as a hedge.

What Washington says

US officials have publicly insisted that the Indo-Pacific remains a priority. The Pentagon's latest National Defense Strategy, released last year, designates China as the "pacing threat" and Iran as a regional challenge.

But allies have heard such assurances before. The memory of the Trump administration's transactional approach to alliances, combined with the sheer scale of a Middle Eastern war, has left many unconvinced.

"There is a gap between what Washington says and what it can do," one Asian diplomat told Reuters. "We are planning for that gap."

What comes next

The coming weeks will be telling. If the US begins to pull additional combat units from Japan or Guam, the fears will become concrete. For now, allies are watching the White House's next moves — and quietly accelerating their own military spending.
