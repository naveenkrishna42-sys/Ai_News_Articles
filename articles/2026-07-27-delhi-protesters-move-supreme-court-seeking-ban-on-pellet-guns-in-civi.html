Delhi Protesters Move Supreme Court Seeking Ban on Pellet Guns in Civilian Protests

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two victims of alleged pellet gun firing during a July 20 protest in Delhi have filed a petition in the Supreme Court.
  • The petition seeks a complete ban on the use of metallic pellet guns against civilians in protest situations.
  • A separate probe found a RAF personnel fired seven rounds, five of which hit protesters during a related march.
  • The CRPF has announced a 'professional post-event assessment' of the RAF's actions at the student protests.

Two individuals who were allegedly struck by pellets during a protest in Delhi on July 20 have moved the Supreme Court. They are seeking a complete ban on the use of metallic pellet guns against civilians in crowd control situations.

The petition, filed through lawyers, argues that such weapons cause grievous and often irreversible injuries. It contends that their use in civilian protests violates fundamental rights, including the right to life under Article 21 of the Constitution.

Alleged Incident During July 20 Protest

The petitioners claim they were shot at by personnel from the Rapid Action Force (RAF) during a demonstration near the Parliament area. One of them, a woman from Gurugram, stated she was hit by a pellet while standing in a crowd. The incident has reignited a public debate on the use of non-lethal but potentially maiming weapons by security forces.

An investigation by The Indian Express has revealed that a single RAF personnel fired seven rounds from his pellet gun during the march that accompanied the Chief Justice of India's visit to Parliament. The probe found that five of those rounds struck protesters.

Demand for a Ban

The Supreme Court petition directly challenges the continued deployment of these weapons. The petitioners argue that pellet guns, which fire metal or plastic projectiles, can cause blindness, fractures, and deep tissue damage. They have urged the court to declare their use against unarmed civilians as unconstitutional.

This is not the first time pellet guns have faced legal scrutiny. Previous petitions after the 2016 agitation in Kashmir had raised similar concerns, though the weapons remain in the arsenal of paramilitary forces for specific crowd control scenarios.

Official Response and Internal Assessments

The Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF), which oversees the RAF, has stated it will conduct a 'professional post-event assessment' of the actions taken during the July 20 protests. The internal review is expected to examine whether standard operating procedures were followed.

Separately, reports from The Times of India and The Indian Express have detailed the sequence of firing during the Parliament march. These reports have put pressure on the authorities to clarify the rules of engagement for such units in urban protest zones.

The Ministry of Home Affairs has not yet issued a public statement on the Supreme Court petition or the probe findings. Officials have not confirmed whether any disciplinary action has been initiated against the RAF personnel involved.

The Supreme Court is expected to hear the matter in the coming weeks. The outcome could set a significant precedent for how Indian law enforcement is permitted to manage large civilian gatherings.
