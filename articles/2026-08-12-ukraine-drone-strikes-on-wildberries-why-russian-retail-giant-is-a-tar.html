Ukraine drone strikes on Wildberries: Why Russian retail giant is a target

📅 2026-08-12 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ukrainian drones struck 20 Wildberries warehouses across Russia, killing one person in Voronezh.
  • The attacks target Russia's largest online retailer, co-founded by Russia's richest woman, Tatyana Bakalchuk.
  • Analysts say the strikes aim to disrupt Russia's economy and logistics, not just military supply chains.

Drone attacks on Wildberries

Ukrainian drones have struck at least 20 warehouses belonging to Wildberries, Russia's largest online retailer, in a coordinated campaign that has disrupted one of the country's most visible commercial operations. The attacks, which spanned multiple regions, culminated in a deadly strike on a logistics centre in Voronezh, where at least one person was killed.

The sheer scale of the strikes — hitting two dozen facilities in a single wave — marks an escalation in Ukraine's strategy of targeting Russian economic infrastructure far from the front lines.

Who owns Wildberries?

Wildberries is co-founded by Tatyana Bakalchuk, widely recognised as Russia's richest woman. Her rags-to-riches story — from a language teacher to a retail mogul worth billions — made her a symbol of Russia's post-Soviet entrepreneurial class. The company, which began as an online clothes seller in 2004, now dominates Russian e-commerce, shipping everything from groceries to electronics to millions of households.

Bakalchuk's prominence has turned Wildberries into a high-profile target. By hitting her empire, Ukraine's military is sending a message that no corner of the Russian economy is off-limits.

Why target a retailer?

At first glance, an e-commerce warehouse seems an unlikely military objective. But analysts point out that Wildberries is not just a shop — it is a critical node in Russia's supply chain. The company's logistics network spans the country, and its warehouses often store goods that sustain both civilian life and, indirectly, the war effort.

Ukrainian officials have framed the strikes as part of a broader campaign to "crash Russian banks" and destabilise the economy. Drones hitting warehouses disrupt deliveries, force companies to reroute goods, and sow uncertainty among consumers and investors alike.

Economic warfare in the sky

The attacks also carry a psychological weight. "People are losing everything," one observer noted, capturing the sense of vulnerability among ordinary Russians who rely on Wildberries for daily necessities. When a warehouse burns, it is not just stock that goes up in flames — it is the trust that the war will not touch civilian life.

For Ukraine, the message is clear: the cost of the invasion will be felt far beyond the battlefield.

Impact on Russia's digital economy

Wildberries is a linchpin of Russia's digital economy, processing millions of orders daily. A strike on its infrastructure ripples through suppliers, delivery drivers, and small businesses that depend on the platform to reach customers.

The Voronezh attack, which killed one person, underscores the human toll of these strikes. Local authorities have not yet confirmed the full extent of the damage, but images from the scene showed smoke rising over the sprawling logistics complex.

What happens next?

Moscow has condemned the strikes as terrorism and vowed to retaliate. But for Wildberries, the immediate challenge is rebuilding and reassuring customers that their orders will arrive. The company has not announced when operations at the affected warehouses will resume.

As Ukraine continues to develop longer-range drones, the question is no longer whether Russian businesses will be hit, but which one will be next. For Bakalchuk and other Russian tycoons, the war has come home — literally to their loading docks.
