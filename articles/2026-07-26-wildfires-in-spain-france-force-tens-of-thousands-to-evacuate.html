Wildfires in Spain, France force tens of thousands to evacuate

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Environment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tens of thousands evacuated across Spain and southern France due to raging wildfires
  • Flames have destroyed homes and scorched thousands of hectares of forest
  • Authorities deploy water bombers and ground crews to contain the blazes

Firefighters struggle as flames spread rapidly

Wildfires have erupted across swathes of Spain and southern France, forcing tens of thousands of residents and tourists to flee their homes and holiday spots. Strong winds and dry conditions have fuelled the flames, making containment difficult for emergency crews.

In Spain, the worst-hit regions include Catalonia and the Valencian Community. Local authorities have reported multiple active fire fronts, with flames advancing toward populated areas. Firefighters are using water-dropping aircraft and ground teams to slow the spread.

In southern France, the Bouches-du-Rhône and Var departments have seen large-scale evacuations. Officials have set up temporary shelters in schools and sports halls to accommodate displaced families.

Evacuations disrupt lives and travel

Roads have been closed, and train services suspended in several areas as a precaution. Many evacuees were given only minutes to grab essentials before leaving their homes. Some have described watching their neighbourhoods go up in smoke from a distance.

Tourists have also been caught in the chaos. Campsites along the Mediterranean coast have been emptied, and holidaymakers redirected to safer locations. Airlines and ferry operators have adjusted schedules to help people leave affected zones.

Officials have not confirmed the total number of evacuees, but estimates run into tens of thousands. No casualties have been reported so far, but several firefighters have sustained minor injuries.

Climate conditions worsen fire risk

The fires come amid an intense heatwave gripping southern Europe. Temperatures have topped 40 degrees Celsius in parts of Spain and France, creating perfect conditions for wildfires. Meteorologists warn that the hot, dry weather is likely to persist for several more days.

Emergency services are bracing for further flare-ups. Authorities in both countries have appealed to the public to avoid non-essential travel to affected areas and to follow evacuation orders without delay.

The European Union has offered support, with member states sending firefighting aircraft and personnel through the EU's civil protection mechanism.

What to watch

Firefighting efforts will continue through the weekend. Officials warn that the situation remains volatile and that more evacuations could be ordered if the wind shifts. The focus now is on containing the largest fire fronts before they reach major towns.
