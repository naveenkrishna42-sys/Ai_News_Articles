Hyderabad woman alleges forced labour, torture in Oman, seeks rescue

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Woman alleges 15-hour workdays for four months without salary.
  • She claims she was tortured and not allowed to leave the premises.
  • She has appealed to Indian authorities for rescue and repatriation.

A woman from Hyderabad has alleged that she was forced to work 15 hours a day without pay for four months in Oman, and subjected to torture. She is now appealing to the Indian government for rescue and repatriation.

The woman, whose identity has not been officially disclosed by authorities, said she travelled to Oman on a work visa but was made to work under brutal conditions. She claims she was not paid any salary during her time there and was physically abused.

Allegations of forced labour and abuse

According to reports, the woman was made to work continuously for long hours without breaks. She alleged that when she protested or requested her dues, she was tortured. The exact nature of the torture has not been detailed in official statements.

She also claimed she was not allowed to leave the premises or contact her family freely. For four months, she said, she received no wages despite working daily from early morning until late at night.

Appeal to Indian authorities

The woman has reached out to Indian diplomatic channels in Oman and family members back in Hyderabad have also contacted local leaders. They have sought immediate intervention to secure her release and safe return to India.

Officials at the Indian embassy in Muscat have not yet issued a formal statement on the case. The Ministry of External Affairs in New Delhi is likely to take up the matter with Omani authorities.

Previous cases of Indian workers stranded abroad

This is not an isolated incident. Several Indian workers, particularly from Telangana, have reported similar exploitation in Gulf countries in recent years. Many have been stranded without pay or proper documentation after falling prey to recruitment agents.

The Hyderabad woman's case has once again highlighted the vulnerabilities of unskilled and semi-skilled workers hired through informal channels. Labour rights groups have repeatedly called for stricter oversight of recruitment agencies.

What happens next

The Indian embassy is expected to verify the woman's claims and coordinate with Omani authorities to facilitate her return. Officials have not confirmed a timeline for the rescue operation.
