Bengaluru joins Karnataka's drought-prone list

Bengaluru, the tech capital of India, has joined Karnataka's list of drought-prone districts as the city faces a severe rainfall deficit. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has reported that the city has received only 40% of its usual monsoon rainfall, a stark contrast to the 80% received last year. This development has raised concerns about water security and agricultural productivity in the region.

Rainfall deficit exceeds 50%

The rainfall deficit in Bengaluru is not isolated. Across Karnataka, the rainfall deficit has exceeded 50% in several districts, including Mysuru and Tumakuru. The IMD has attributed this anomaly to changing monsoon patterns, which are increasingly influenced by climate change. The situation is particularly dire in rural areas, where agriculture is heavily dependent on monsoon rains.

Water security risks mount

The severe rainfall deficit has put immense pressure on Bengaluru's water resources. The city's water supply is already stretched, and the drought conditions could lead to water rationing. The Karnataka government has urged residents to conserve water and use it judiciously. The situation is a stark reminder of the vulnerability of water resources in the face of climate change.

Changing monsoon patterns linked to climate change

Climate change is increasingly being linked to the changing monsoon patterns. The IMD has reported that the monsoon has been erratic in recent years, with prolonged dry spells interspersed with heavy rainfall. This unpredictability has made it difficult for farmers and water managers to plan and prepare for the monsoon. The situation in Bengaluru is a case in point, where the city is grappling with a severe rainfall deficit.
