Iran Attack on US Base in Jordan Kills 3, Oil Prices Surge

📅 2026-07-29 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • A drone attack killed three US service members at a base in Jordan, with Washington blaming Iran.
  • The US and Saudi Arabia launched retaliatory strikes inside Iraq.
  • Oil prices jumped over 6% following President Trump's promise to hit Iran 'hard'.

Deadly Drone Strike on US Base

A drone attack on a US military base in Jordan near the Syrian border has killed three American service members and wounded at least 34 others. US officials have attributed the attack to Iran-backed militia groups operating in the region. The White House confirmed the casualties on Wednesday, marking the first US military deaths in the region since the onset of the Israel-Hamas war.

The attack targeted a small logistics and support base known as Tower 22. The base has been used to support US forces in Syria. No immediate claim of responsibility was made by any group, but US intelligence assessments point squarely at Tehran's proxies.

US and Saudi Arabia Strike Back in Iraq

In a swift response, the United States and Saudi Arabia launched coordinated airstrikes inside Iraq. The strikes targeted what Pentagon officials described as 'Iranian-linked weapons storage facilities and command nodes.' The Saudi role in the operation signals a significant escalation in the kingdom's involvement in the conflict. Riyadh has previously sought to de-escalate tensions but appears to have shifted its posture.

Iraqi officials condemned the strikes, saying they violated Iraqi sovereignty. The Iraqi government has called for an emergency session of the United Nations Security Council. The strikes threaten to further destabilize Iraq, which has been caught between its alliance with the United States and its proximity to Iran.

Oil Markets in Turmoil

The escalating conflict sent shockwaves through global energy markets. Brent crude futures jumped more than 6% on Wednesday, crossing the $85 per barrel mark. Analysts said the market is pricing in the risk of a direct confrontation between the US and Iran, which could disrupt the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments.

President Donald Trump, speaking to Fox News, said the US would be 'hitting Iran hard.' He did not specify the targets. 'They hit us, and they will pay a price like they have never paid before,' Trump said. The comments triggered a fresh wave of buying in oil futures.

Financial Markets Wobble

Beyond oil, the broader financial markets are feeling the heat. The tech-heavy Nasdaq index fell again as the AI-driven rally continued to unwind. Chip stocks, which had soared earlier in the year, were hit particularly hard. Investors are now weighing the impact of higher energy costs on inflation and corporate profits. The yield on the 10-year US Treasury note edged higher as traders adjusted their expectations for interest rates.

What Comes Next

The White House is now weighing a range of options against Iran, from direct strikes on Iranian military assets to cyber attacks. The key question is whether Tehran will retaliate further or seek to de-escalate. Diplomatic channels remain open, but the rhetoric from both sides is hardening. The United Nations has called for restraint, but with both Washington and Tehran in a combative mood, the risk of a wider war is higher than it has been in years.
