NASA Astronaut Anil Menon's First Spacewalk Prepares ISS for Solar Array Upgrade

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Anil Menon's maiden spacewalk lasted 6.5 hours.
  • The spacewalk was to prep the ISS for a solar array upgrade.
  • This is part of ongoing efforts to boost station power.
  • Menon is the first Indian-origin astronaut to do a spacewalk.

NASA astronaut Anil Menon stepped outside the International Space Station on Thursday for the first time in his career, spending six and a half hours in the vacuum of space. The mission: prepare the orbital laboratory for a new set of solar arrays that will boost its power supply.

The spacewalk began at 8:05 AM GMT and concluded without incident, according to NASA's live coverage. Menon, who is of Indian origin, was joined by a fellow crew member as they worked on the station's truss structure, laying the groundwork for the upcoming solar array installation.

A Historic First for Menon

Menon's debut spacewalk marks a significant milestone. He is the first astronaut of Indian origin to perform this kind of extravehicular activity, a fact that has drawn attention from space enthusiasts in India and around the world. The Deccan Chronicle and The Times of India both highlighted the achievement in their coverage on Thursday.

During the spacewalk, Menon and his colleague focused on tasks that will streamline the future installation of the new solar arrays. NASA described the work as a preparatory step, calling it "one more step" in the station's ongoing power upgrade.

Why the ISS Needs New Solar Arrays

The ISS has been in orbit for over two decades, and its original solar arrays are showing signs of wear. Over time, exposure to micrometeoroids and the harsh space environment has degraded their efficiency. The new arrays, which are more compact and efficient, will ensure the station has enough power to support scientific research and future commercial activities.

Thursday's spacewalk was not about installing the arrays themselves, but rather about modifying the station's structure to accommodate them. This includes adjusting mounting brackets and securing connections, tasks that require careful maneuvering in the bulky spacesuit.

What the Spacewalk Involved

The crew spent the first part of the spacewalk preparing the workspace, then moved on to the more complex modifications. Each movement was choreographed to the minute, with mission control in Houston monitoring every step. The astronauts used specialized tools tethered to their suits to avoid losing anything in the void.

By the end of the 6.5-hour excursion, the station was one step closer to its upgraded power system. NASA officials confirmed that all primary objectives were met, though they did not release specific details about the condition of the hardware.

Reactions and Significance

For India, Menon's achievement is a source of pride. The country's space agency, ISRO, has been making strides with its own missions, and Menon's spacewalk symbolizes a broader collaboration in human spaceflight. Social media in India buzzed with congratulations, though NASA has not yet commented on any formal recognition from the Indian government.

Menon's journey to this moment was long. He was selected as part of NASA's astronaut corps after a career in medicine and aviation, bringing a unique skill set to the crew. His ability to perform under pressure in a spacewalk is a testament to the rigorous training astronauts undergo.

The success of this spacewalk also sets the stage for the actual solar array installation, which is expected to happen over the coming months. Each new array will unfurl like a giant accordion, increasing the station's power generation capacity by up to 30%.

As the ISS continues to age, upgrades like these are crucial. NASA has committed to keeping the station operational through at least 2030, and the new arrays are a key part of that plan.

Looking ahead, the crew will now review data from the spacewalk and prepare for the next one, likely scheduled within a few weeks. For Menon, this first spacewalk is just the beginning of what promises to be a distinguished orbital career.
