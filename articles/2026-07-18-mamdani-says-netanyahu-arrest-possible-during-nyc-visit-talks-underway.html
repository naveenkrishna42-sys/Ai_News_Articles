Mamdani says Netanyahu arrest possible during NYC visit, talks underway

📅 2026-07-18 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Zohran Mamdani confirmed discussions about arresting Benjamin Netanyahu during a possible September visit to New York City.
  • Mamdani cited the International Criminal Court warrant against Netanyahu for alleged war crimes in Gaza.
  • The NYC council member said he cannot rewrite laws but is exploring legal avenues for an arrest.

New York lawmaker reignites debate over Netanyahu arrest

New York City Council member Zohran Mamdani has publicly stated he is holding talks to arrest Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu if the leader visits New York City in September. The statement, made in a series of media interviews on Saturday, has reignited a global debate over the reach of international criminal warrants.

Mamdani, a progressive Democrat representing parts of Queens, told reporters that Netanyahu "belongs in the Hague" when asked about his plans. He acknowledged the city is reviewing its authority to detain the Israeli premier.

ICC warrant and legal hurdles

Mamdani's remarks come in the context of an arrest warrant issued by the International Criminal Court (ICC) against Netanyahu for alleged war crimes in Gaza. The US does not recognize the ICC's jurisdiction, creating a complex legal landscape for any local arrest attempt.

"I am holding talks to bring about Netanyahu's arrest in September," Mamdani said. He was careful to note that he would not "rewrite laws" but insisted the city has a role to play in enforcing international justice.

Legal experts have pointed out that New York City law enforcement does not typically execute ICC warrants. The US has consistently opposed the court's authority over American allies.

Political firestorm expected

The Israeli Prime Minister's office has not commented on the reports. Netanyahu has previously described the ICC warrant as politically motivated and antisemitic.

Mamdani's position has drawn sharp reactions. Supporters of the Palestinian cause have praised his stance. Critics accuse him of grandstanding and risking a diplomatic incident.

New York City hosts the United Nations General Assembly each September. World leaders, including Netanyahu, often attend the gathering. The timing of Mamdani's announcement appears calibrated to the diplomatic calendar.

What happens next

Mamdani said he will continue discussions with legal advisors and city officials. No formal motion or legislation has been introduced. The New York City Council has not taken a position on the matter.

Whether the city can or will act remains uncertain. The administration of Mayor Eric Adams has not responded to requests for comment. For now, the threat of arrest hangs over Netanyahu's potential visit, but the legal path forward remains unclear.

Observers will watch for any official statement from the Israeli government or the US State Department in the coming days.
