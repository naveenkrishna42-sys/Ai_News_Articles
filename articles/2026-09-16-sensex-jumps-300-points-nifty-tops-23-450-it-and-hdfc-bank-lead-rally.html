Sensex, Nifty Rally: What Actually Moved the Market

Indian equity benchmarks closed higher on Tuesday, 15 September 2026, with the Sensex adding roughly 300 points and the Nifty reclaiming the 23,450 mark. The move was not a broad-based surge. It was concentrated in two pockets: information technology largecaps and HDFC Bank. That distinction matters for buyers, because index-level gains driven by a handful of heavyweights can mask weakness in the wider market.

The IT pack drew fresh institutional buying after the dollar index softened and US technology spending commentary stayed constructive. HDFC Bank, the single largest Nifty weight, extended its recent recovery as deposit growth trends improved. Together, these two engines contributed the bulk of the index's gains.

Key Triggers Behind the 300-Point Move

1. IT Services: Currency and Deal-Flow Support

A marginally weaker dollar and stable US client budgets supported the IT index. Tier-1 firms with higher exposure to banking, financial services and insurance (BFSI) clients benefited most. Deal wins announced over the past fortnight gave traders a reason to add positions after a soft August.

2. HDFC Bank: Deposit Growth and Valuation Comfort

HDFC Bank has been the subject of a valuation debate through 2026. Tuesday's buying reflected growing comfort that deposit accretion is catching up with credit growth, easing pressure on net interest margins. At current levels, the stock still trades below its long-term average price-to-book, which keeps long-horizon buyers interested.

3. Global Cues and FII Positioning

Asian markets traded firm, and US futures pointed higher ahead of the session. Foreign institutional investors, net sellers in parts of August, showed selective buying in index heavyweights. Domestic mutual fund SIP flows continued to provide a steady cushion beneath the market.

What This Means for Buyers and Investors

A 300-point Sensex move is modest, roughly 0.4% at current levels. It signals sentiment repair rather than a trend change. For investors, the practical takeaway is that leadership remains narrow. If IT and HDFC Bank continue to attract flows, the Nifty can hold above 23,450 and attempt higher resistance zones. If either falters, the index has limited support from other heavyweights.

Valuation context matters. IT largecaps trade at premium multiples relative to their five-year averages, so fresh buying assumes deal momentum sustains into the December quarter. HDFC Bank offers a more balanced risk-reward, with earnings visibility and a lower relative valuation. Midcaps and smallcaps remain the riskier segment, with several pockets still above historical valuation bands.

Pros and Cons: The Rally in Perspective

  • Pro: IT and HDFC Bank leadership gives the index a stable, earnings-backed base.
  • Pro: Steady SIP flows reduce the risk of sharp drawdowns from FII selling.
  • Pro: A softer dollar supports IT realisations and export earnings.
  • Con: Narrow breadth means the rally is fragile if heavyweights reverse.
  • Con: IT valuations leave little margin for execution misses.
  • Con: Global rate and currency shifts can quickly change FII positioning.

Levels to Watch and Actionable Strategy

For traders, 23,500 remains the immediate resistance on the Nifty, with 23,200 acting as near-term support. A sustained close above 23,500 would confirm broader participation. For long-term investors, staggered buying in HDFC Bank and largecap IT remains reasonable, but chasing the index at every 300-point spike is not. Keep position sizes disciplined, review sector allocation quarterly, and treat single-day index moves as noise unless accompanied by improving market breadth and consistent institutional flows.
