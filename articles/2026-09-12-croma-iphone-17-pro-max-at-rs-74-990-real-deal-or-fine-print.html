Croma's iPhone 17 Pro Max Offer: What's Actually on the Table

Croma has listed the iPhone 17 Pro Max at Rs 74,990, a figure that undercuts Apple's official India pricing by a wide margin. The catch: this is not a flat discount. The price assumes a maximum exchange value for your old smartphone, plus a bank cashback that varies by card issuer. Without a trade-in, the effective price climbs significantly. Buyers must read the fine print before queuing up.

Breaking Down the Rs 74,990 Figure

The Rs 74,990 sticker is a post-exchange, post-cashback number. Croma's exchange program typically values older iPhones and select Android flagships at Rs 30,000–45,000, depending on condition. Bank offers—usually 10% instant discount capped at Rs 5,000–7,500—apply only to specific credit cards. Stack them correctly, and the net cost can fall near Rs 75,000. Miss one, and you pay closer to Rs 1.1–1.2 lakh.

Why India Pays Nearly Rs 1 Lakh More Than the US

India's iPhone pricing includes basic customs duty, GST, and local levies that push the landed cost far above US levels. The Telegraph India notes the iPhone duo costs nearly Rs 1 lakh more in India. That gap explains why Croma's offer—however conditional—attracts attention. It also explains why Apple's price hikes on older models are nudging buyers toward refurbished units, as Indian Television Dot Com reports.

Exchange Bonus: The Real Lever

The exchange bonus is the single biggest variable. Croma partners with third-party refurbishers who assess your device on the spot. A two-year-old iPhone 14 Pro might fetch Rs 35,000; a cracked older Android could fetch Rs 8,000. The Rs 74,990 headline assumes a near-flagship trade-in. If your old phone is mid-range or damaged, your effective price rises sharply.

  • Pro: Genuine Croma warranty and GST invoice, unlike grey-market imports.
  • Pro: Stacking exchange and bank offers can save Rs 40,000–50,000.
  • Con: The lowest price requires a high-value trade-in and a specific bank card.
  • Con: Exchange values are opaque and vary by store and device condition.
  • Con: Without exchange, the deal is no better than Apple's own festive offers.

Apple's Foldable and Refurbished Push: What It Means for Buyers

The Times of India reports Apple's foldable debut will lift the market and test Samsung. For buyers, that means more competition and potentially better trade-in values on older iPhones as Apple clears inventory. Simultaneously, The Economic Times highlights Apple's surprise price twist for old iPhone models, exposing smartphone price pain. Together, these forces make Croma's offer a tactical move to capture upgraders before the foldable launch.

Should You Buy at Rs 74,990?

Run the math. If you hold a recent iPhone or flagship Android in good condition and have a participating credit card, the net cost can fall below Rs 80,000—a genuine saving. If you lack either, the deal collapses. Compare Croma's final quote against Apple's official exchange and Amazon/Flipkart festive offers. Never accept the first exchange valuation; get a second quote.

Verdict: Conditional Value, Not a Blanket Discount

Croma's Rs 74,990 iPhone 17 Pro Max is a legitimate but conditional offer. It rewards buyers who plan their trade-in and payment method. For everyone else, it is a reminder that India's iPhone pricing remains among the world's highest, and the best deals require legwork. Verify the final invoice total before committing.
