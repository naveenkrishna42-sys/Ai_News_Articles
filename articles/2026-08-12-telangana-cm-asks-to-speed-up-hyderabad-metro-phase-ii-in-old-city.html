Telangana CM asks to speed up Hyderabad Metro Phase II in old city

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • CM Revanth Reddy orders expediting Hyderabad Metro Phase II works in old city.
  • Directs officials to work out modalities for adding more coaches to existing lines.
  • Move aims to improve connectivity and decongest the old city area.

Telangana Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy has directed officials to fast-track the Hyderabad Metro Rail phase II works in the old city and simultaneously work out modalities for deploying additional coaches on the existing corridors. The instructions came during a review meeting on Tuesday, where the CM stressed the need to improve connectivity in the densely populated areas of the city.

The old city, which includes areas like Charminar, Falaknuma, and surrounding localities, has long been underserved by the Metro network. With phase I already operational on major corridors like Miyapur-LBG Nagar and Ameerpet-HITEC City, the extension into the old city is seen as a critical step to ease congestion and provide faster transit options for residents.

Focus on old city connectivity

During the review, the Chief Minister was briefed about the current status of phase II, which proposes to link the old city with the rest of Hyderabad through new corridors. Officials from the Hyderabad Metro Rail Limited (HMRL) and the state municipal administration department presented the detailed project report and the land acquisition challenges that have slowed progress so far.

Revanth Reddy asked the officials to resolve land-related hurdles on a priority basis and ensure that the civil works commence without further delay. He also underscored the importance of coordinating with the central government for necessary clearances, given that the project involves multiple approvals.

Additional coaches to meet rising demand

The CM also turned his attention to the existing Metro corridors, which have seen a steady increase in ridership since the full launch of phase I. Commuters on busy routes, especially during peak hours, have often complained of overcrowding, prompting the demand for more coaches.

Revanth Reddy directed the HMRL and the state transport department to work out the modalities for adding coaches to the current fleet. This includes assessing the technical feasibility, cost implications, and the timeline for procurement. The officials were told to prepare a detailed proposal and submit it within a month.

The move is expected to benefit lakhs of daily commuters who rely on the Metro for their daily travel. Currently, the Hyderabad Metro operates with three-coach and six-coach trains on different corridors, and adding more coaches could significantly enhance passenger capacity.

Broader urban mobility plan

The CM's directives come amid a broader push by the state government to strengthen public transport infrastructure in Hyderabad. The city has been grappling with increasing vehicular traffic and pollution, and the expansion of the Metro network is seen as a key solution.

Officials have also been asked to explore integration with other modes of transport, such as buses and suburban rail, to create a seamless travel experience. The phase II project, once completed, is expected to connect the old city with key business districts, educational institutions, and healthcare facilities.

While no specific timeline has been announced, the CM's emphasis on expediting the works suggests that the government is keen to see visible progress in the coming months. The additional coaches, if approved, would be a relatively quicker intervention compared to the full phase II construction.

Officials have not yet confirmed the estimated cost or the exact number of coaches that might be added, but the proposal is expected to be finalised in the next few weeks. The state government is likely to fund the expansion through a mix of internal resources and external borrowing.

What to watch

Over the next few months, the focus will be on how quickly the HMRL can clear the land acquisition hurdles for phase II and whether the central government grants the necessary approvals. Commuters will also be watching for a concrete timeline on the additional coaches, which could be announced in the upcoming state budget session.
