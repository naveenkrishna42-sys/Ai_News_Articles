Amit Shah to table Kerala renaming bill, NCDC financing expansion in Lok Sabha

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bill to change Kerala's name to 'Keralam' likely to be tabled
  • Another bill seeks to expand NCDC's financing powers
  • Oversight body for 16 tribunals also on today's agenda

Union Home Minister Amit Shah is expected to introduce a batch of bills in the Lok Sabha today, including a proposal to rename Kerala as 'Keralam' and another to broaden the financing capabilities of the National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC). The move comes during the ongoing Monsoon Session of Parliament, with four bills listed for introduction.

Kerala Renaming Bill

The bill to change Kerala's name has been a long-pending demand from certain quarters in the state, who argue that 'Keralam' is the correct Malayalam pronunciation. If passed, the state would be officially recognised as 'Keralam' in all central government records and communications.

The proposal has sparked political debate, with some parties supporting the change while others question the timing and necessity. The state government had earlier passed a resolution in this regard, which was forwarded to the Centre for consideration.

NCDC Financing Powers

The second bill aims to amend the NCDC Act to expand the corporation's financing powers. This is seen as a move to strengthen cooperative institutions across the country, aligning with the government's focus on the cooperative sector.

The proposed amendments are expected to allow NCDC to finance a wider range of activities, potentially boosting rural credit and cooperative farming initiatives. Details of the exact scope of the expanded powers are yet to be made public.

Other Bills on Agenda

In addition to these two, the government is also set to introduce a bill to create an oversight body for 16 tribunals. This is part of an ongoing effort to streamline the functioning of tribunals and ensure greater accountability.

The fourth bill's specifics have not been officially disclosed, but parliamentary sources suggest it could be related to administrative or governance reforms. The opposition is likely to scrutinise all proposals, particularly the Kerala renaming bill, which has historical and linguistic implications.

Political Reactions

Congress leader Jairam Ramesh has separately demanded that the National Green Tribunal make public the report of a panel that reviewed approvals for the Great Nicobar project. While not directly linked to today's bills, this reflects the charged atmosphere in Parliament during the session.

The Monsoon Session has seen several heated exchanges between the treasury and opposition benches, with the government pushing its legislative agenda while the opposition raises issues of transparency and regional concerns.

Observers note that the Kerala renaming bill could face a tough passage in the Rajya Sabha, where the government does not enjoy a majority. The NCDC bill is expected to receive broader support given the cooperative sector's importance.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact time of introduction, but the bills are listed for the day's business. The Lok Sabha will take them up after question hour, subject to the Chair's approval.

As the session progresses, all eyes will be on how these bills are received and whether the government manages to build consensus. The outcome will likely set the tone for the remainder of the Monsoon Session, with several other key legislations awaiting consideration.
