Skyroot Vikram-1 Lifts Off: India’s First Private Orbital Rocket Makes History

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vikram-1 is India's first privately built orbital-class rocket.
  • Launched from Sriharikota, it carried six payloads into low-Earth orbit.
  • The mission marks a milestone for India's private space sector.
  • Former ISRO scientists lead Skyroot Aerospace.

Historic Liftoff from Sriharikota

India's space sector crossed a new frontier on Saturday. Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1, the country's first privately developed orbital-class rocket, lifted off from Sriharikota. The launch was textbook — no delays, no anomalies.

The rocket rose from the same Satish Dhawan Space Centre that has hosted ISRO's missions for decades. But this time, the control room belonged to a private company. Engineers at Skyroot's mission control burst into applause as Vikram-1 climbed through the morning sky.

Six Payloads in Orbit

Vikram-1 successfully placed six payloads into low-Earth orbit. The exact nature of each payload has not been fully disclosed, but sources indicate a mix of communication and earth-observation cubesats. Officials confirmed all satellites are communicating with ground stations.

The mission was the rocket's first orbital attempt. Skyroot had previously conducted suborbital tests, but this flight demonstrated full orbital capability. Getting it right on the first try is rare in rocketry. Indian media described it as a 'textbook launch'.

The Team Behind the Rocket

Skyroot Aerospace was founded by former ISRO scientists. The team includes veterans who worked on India's Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle and other key programmes. Their experience was evident in the mission's flawless execution.

Speaking to reporters after the launch, Skyroot's CEO said the company's focus is now on scaling production. Vikram-1 is designed to be a small satellite launcher, targeting the growing global demand for dedicated rideshare missions.

What This Means for India's Space Industry

The success places India alongside the United States and a handful of other nations that have produced private orbital rockets. It also validates the government's 2020 decision to open the space sector to private players. The Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Centre (IN-SPACe) facilitated the launch permissions.

Industry observers say this could unlock a wave of investment. Skyroot is one of several Indian space startups, but it is the first to achieve orbital flight. Competitors are likely to accelerate their own development timelines.

The Road Ahead

Skyroot has already announced plans for Vikram-2, a larger rocket capable of lifting heavier payloads. The company is also exploring reusability. For now, the team is basking in the success of a mission that rewrote the rules of Indian spaceflight.

What to watch: Skyroot's next launch window and whether commercial customers — both Indian and foreign — will line up for Vikram-1's services. The rocket's price point and reliability will determine how fast this new chapter unfolds.
