CAFE 3 Norms Notified: Automakers Face Stricter Targets and 3x EV Credits

  • Regulatory leniency for internal combustion small cars ends, forcing entry-level segments toward hybridization.

India Formalizes CAFE 3 Emission Framework

The Ministry of Power, in coordination with the Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE), has officially notified the Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) Phase 3 standards. Scheduled to take effect from April 2027, the updated regulatory framework substantially lowers allowable fleet-wide carbon dioxide emissions across all passenger vehicle manufacturers operating in India.

Under CAFE regulations, an automaker's regulatory compliance is evaluated not on individual models, but across its total sales volume weighted by vehicle curb weight. The impending phase aims to push fleet-average CO2 emissions below the current CAFE 2 target of roughly 113 g/km toward an aggressive threshold expected near 91 to 95 g/km.

The 3x Super-Credit Engine for Pure EVs

The most consequential structural mechanism inside CAFE 3 is the triple credit multiplier awarded to Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs). For every zero-emission electric car registered, automakers can account for it as three units in their corporate fuel-efficiency balance sheet. This incentivizes legacy carmakers to aggressively launch BEVs to offset the higher carbon footprints of their internal combustion SUVs.

Plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs) and strong hybrids will also receive differentiated credit multipliers, though pure BEVs capture the highest compliance leverage. Automakers with zero electric presence will face severe monetary penalties under the Energy Conservation Act if their fleet emissions breach defined tolerances.

End of Small Car Concessions

Historically, high-volume manufacturers balanced their fleet portfolios by selling hundreds of thousands of compact petrol hatchbacks. Small curb weights and naturally aspirated engines offered natural carbon offsets against heavier utility vehicles without requiring expensive electrification components.

CAFE 3 closes this window by flattening the compliance curve and eliminating implicit concessions for entry-level internal combustion vehicles. Standard 1.0-liter and 1.2-liter petrol hatchbacks alone will no longer generate sufficient margins to insulate carmakers. To meet the revised thresholds, entry segments will need mild-hybrid, strong-hybrid, or compressed biogas (CBG) systems, inevitably lifting factory floor costs on budget models.

Strategic Shifts for Automakers and Buyers

The new standards will accelerate portfolio transformations across every showroom in the country. Car buyers can expect several market adjustments over the 2026 to 2028 product rollout cycles.

  • Escalating EV Model Launches: Major domestic and foreign OEMs will rush mass-market electric models to harvest the 3x credits needed to keep their popular diesel and turbo-petrol SUVs on sale.
  • Sub-₹10 Lakh Price Pressures: Entry-level petrol hatchbacks and compact sedans will see price inflation as manufacturers integrate 12V or 48V micro-hybrid hardware to trim nominal gram-per-kilometer numbers.
  • Consolidation of Pure ICE Portfolios: Large-displacement naturally aspirated and non-hybrid turbo engines face accelerated obsolescence in favor of localized strong-hybrid systems.

By coupling steep fleet penalties with massive EV multipliers, CAFE 3 eliminates regulatory loopholes. Manufacturers must commit to tangible zero-emission volumes rather than relying purely on downsized petrol platforms.
