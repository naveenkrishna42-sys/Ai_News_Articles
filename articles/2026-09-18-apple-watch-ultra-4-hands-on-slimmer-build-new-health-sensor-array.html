Apple Watch Ultra 4 Hands-On: What Actually Changed

Apple's flagship rugged wearable has shed bulk without losing its titanium toughness. In a brief hands-on session, the Ultra 4 felt noticeably lighter on the wrist than the Ultra 2, with a slimmer case profile that sits closer to the skin. The redesigned health sensor array on the back is the centrepiece of this refresh, and Apple is making bold claims about heart sensing accuracy that buyers should scrutinise carefully.

The launch lineup includes the Apple Watch Series 12 alongside the Ultra 4, and early reviews of the Series 12 suggest Apple is catching up to rivals in some metrics while still leading in others. For Indian buyers considering a Rs 89,900-plus purchase, the question is simple: does the Ultra 4 justify the premium over the standard Series 12?

Health Sensing: Science or Marketing?

Apple has publicly explained the science behind its "most accurate heart sensing in a wearable" claim, citing improved optical channels and better skin contact from the slimmer chassis. That contact point matters more than raw sensor count. A watch that sits flush against the wrist reduces motion artefacts during running and swimming, which is where most wearables fail.

Wareable's early analysis supports the improved accuracy narrative, though independent lab validation is still pending. Buyers should treat launch-day claims as directional until third-party testing emerges over the next few weeks.

Real-World Performance and Battery Reality

The Ultra line has always traded battery life for capability. The Ultra 4's rated 36 hours in normal use and 72 hours in low power mode remain unchanged on paper, but faster charging softens the blow. A 30-minute top-up should cover most multi-day trekking scenarios.

Engadget's Series 12 review noted that model "catching heat" for thermal behaviour under load, and Ultra 4 buyers should watch for similar feedback once units reach athletes in volume.

Pros and Cons for Indian Buyers

  • Pros: Slimmer, lighter titanium build improves all-day comfort; redesigned sensor array improves heart tracking during high-motion activity; faster charging reduces downtime; strongest dive and rugged certifications in the category.
  • Cons: Battery life still trails dedicated Garmin and Coros multisport watches; premium pricing starts near Rs 89,900; accuracy claims await independent verification; incremental upgrade for existing Ultra 2 owners.

Should You Buy the Ultra 4?

First-time Ultra buyers get the most compelling package yet: a wearable that finally feels proportionate on smaller wrists while retaining expedition-grade durability. Existing Ultra 2 owners can comfortably skip this generation unless heart-sensing accuracy is mission-critical for training.

For everyone else, the Ultra 4 is a statement purchase that delivers on hardware, with software and battery life remaining the honest trade-offs.
