IOCL posts Rs 1,141 crore net loss in Q1FY27 on lower marketing margins

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • IOCL reported a consolidated net loss of Rs 1,141 crore for Q1FY27
  • Loss attributed to lower marketing margins amid high crude prices and stable fuel prices
  • Revenue rose 19% quarter-on-quarter, but margins remained under pressure

Indian Oil Corporation Ltd (IOCL) on Friday reported a consolidated net loss of Rs 1,141 crore for the first quarter of fiscal 2026-27 (Q1FY27), as lower marketing margins outweighed a sharp rise in revenue. The state-run refiner and fuel retailer said the quarter was hit by a combination of firm global crude prices and the government's decision to keep retail petrol and diesel prices unchanged.

The loss marks a sharp reversal from the year-ago period, when the company had posted a profit. According to preliminary figures, revenue for the quarter rose about 19 per cent quarter-on-quarter, but the increase in input costs far outpaced the growth in realisations, squeezing margins across the company's core marketing business.

Crude spike and price freeze pressure margins

Industry analysts tracking the company said the Q1 numbers were broadly in line with expectations, though some had forecast an even wider loss. The loss was attributed primarily to the marketing segment, where IOCL buys crude and sells refined products at regulated or administratively controlled prices.

International crude oil prices surged during the April-June 2026 quarter, driven by supply concerns and geopolitical tensions. However, retail fuel prices in India remained unchanged, with the government keen to shield consumers from inflationary pressures. This left oil marketing companies like IOCL to absorb the difference, leading to negative marketing margins on petrol and diesel.

Revenue up, but costs climb faster

IOCL's top line grew on the back of higher product prices and improved refining throughput, but the cost of raw materials—crude oil—rose even more steeply. The company's gross refining margin (GRM) also came under pressure during the quarter, though it remained positive.

Other income, including interest and dividend receipts, provided some cushion, but was insufficient to offset the operating losses in the marketing business. The company's board is expected to review the results in detail at its next meeting, with a focus on working capital and inventory losses.

First quarterly loss in 15 years

This is the first time in 15 years that IOCL has reported a quarterly net loss, underscoring the severity of the margin squeeze. The last such instance was during the global financial crisis in 2008-09, when crude prices crashed and the company had to write down inventory.

The loss has also reignited concerns about the financial health of state-run oil marketing companies, which together control over 90 per cent of India's retail fuel market. While the government has previously compensated these firms for under-recoveries, no such mechanism is currently in place for the latest quarter.

What analysts are watching

Market participants are now closely monitoring any policy signals from the government on fuel pricing. A hike in retail prices, or a subsidy package, could help restore margins in the coming quarters. IOCL's management has not yet commented on the results, but a detailed earnings call is expected next week.

The company's shares closed lower on Friday following the announcement, though the decline was capped by expectations of a possible government intervention. Investors are also factoring in the possibility of a modest recovery in GRMs as global refining capacity remains tight.

Going ahead, the trajectory of crude prices and any decision on retail fuel prices will be crucial for IOCL's performance. For now, the company is banking on a pick-up in refining margins and a stabilisation of the rupee to limit further damage.
