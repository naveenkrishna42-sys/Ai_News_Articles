Rupee's Resilience: HDFC Bank Predicts 91.50/$ Target Amidst Equity Surge

✍️ By TIVRA News Business Desk 📅 2026-11-24 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • HDFC Bank's treasury head projects the Indian Rupee strengthening to 91.50 per US dollar.
  • Robust foreign equity inflows are identified as the primary catalyst for this anticipated appreciation.
  • The forecast offers a positive outlook despite recent rupee volatility, with significant implications for trade and investment.

The Rupee's Shifting Tides: A Forecast for Appreciation

As of November 23, 2026, the Indian Rupee (INR) has been a subject of intense market scrutiny, oscillating amidst global economic crosscurrents. While recent trends saw the rupee depreciate, settling around 92.85-92.92 against the US dollar, a significant bullish forecast has emerged from HDFC Bank. The institution's treasury head has publicly stated that the rupee could strengthen considerably, potentially reaching 91.50 per US dollar, primarily on the back of sustained equity inflows.

This projection offers a compelling counter-narrative to the immediate depreciation observed, suggesting underlying strengths are poised to reassert themselves. For businesses engaged in international trade and investors eyeing Indian markets, such a shift in currency valuation carries substantial implications. TIVRA News delves into the factors driving this optimism and its real-world impact.

Equity Inflows: The Primary Catalyst

The core of HDFC Bank's optimistic outlook hinges on the expectation of robust foreign equity inflows into India. Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) and Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) have historically played a crucial role in shoring up the rupee's value. When foreign capital flows into the Indian stock markets or directly into businesses, it increases the demand for rupees, thereby strengthening the currency.

India's sustained economic growth trajectory, coupled with policy stability and attractive market valuations, continues to position it as a preferred destination for global capital. This influx of foreign currency to purchase Indian assets creates a fundamental demand-side pressure that can push the rupee higher, counteracting other depreciatory forces.

Understanding the Rupee's Volatility and Carry Trade Appeal

The rupee's journey has been marked by periods of volatility, influenced by a confluence of global and domestic factors. Recent dips, such as the 30-paise tumble to 92.92 or the 23-paise decline to 92.85, often reflect broader dollar strength driven by global economic uncertainties, rising crude oil prices impacting India's import bill, or shifts in central bank policies worldwide.

However, the rupee has also emerged as an 'accidental carry star' for many investors. India's relatively higher interest rates compared to developed economies make it attractive for the carry trade, where investors borrow in low-interest currencies and invest in high-interest ones. This yield differential continuously draws foreign capital, providing a structural support base for the rupee, even during periods of global risk aversion.

Implications for Businesses and Individuals

A stronger rupee at 91.50/$ would have multifaceted impacts across the Indian economy. For **importers**, a lower exchange rate means they pay less in rupees for dollar-denominated goods, potentially reducing input costs and making imported products cheaper for consumers. Conversely, **exporters** would face headwinds, as their dollar earnings would convert to fewer rupees, potentially impacting profit margins and competitiveness.

For **investors**, FPIs holding Indian assets stand to benefit from currency appreciation in addition to market gains. Indian individuals planning **overseas travel, education, or investments** would find their foreign currency purchases more affordable. This shift could stimulate outbound tourism and facilitate foreign education for many Indian families.

Outlook and Key Considerations

While the HDFC Bank forecast presents a bullish scenario, currency markets remain inherently dynamic and susceptible to various influences. Key factors to monitor include global crude oil prices, which significantly impact India's trade deficit, and the trajectory of the US Dollar Index (DXY). Sustained global dollar strength could temper the rupee's appreciation despite robust inflows.

Furthermore, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) plays a critical role in managing rupee volatility through interventions, aiming for stability rather than any specific level. Geopolitical developments and shifts in global investor sentiment will also continue to shape the rupee's near-term trajectory. Investors and businesses must consider these broader economic indicators alongside expert predictions to navigate the currency landscape effectively.
