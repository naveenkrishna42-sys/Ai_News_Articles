Introduction to Puri Jaganath Temple

The Puri Jaganath Temple, located in the coastal town of Puri in the state of Odisha, India, is one of the most revered and sacred Hindu temples in the country. The temple is dedicated to Lord Jaganath, a form of Lord Krishna, and is considered one of the four sacred Char Dham pilgrimage sites in India.

History of Puri Jaganath Temple

The history of the Puri Jaganath Temple dates back to the 12th century when it was built by King Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva of the Eastern Ganga dynasty. The temple has undergone several renovations and expansions over the centuries, with the current structure being built in the 16th century. The temple's architecture is a mix of Kalinga and Dravidian styles, with a towering spire that rises to a height of over 214 feet.

Significance of Puri Jaganath Temple

The Puri Jaganath Temple is considered one of the most sacred temples in Hinduism, and its significance extends beyond its stunning architecture. The temple's main festival, the Rath Yatra, is a grand procession where the deities are taken out of the temple and pulled through the streets of Puri on massive chariots.
