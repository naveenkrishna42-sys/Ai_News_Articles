Disha Salian CBI FIR: Aaditya Thackeray Denies Ever Meeting Ex-Manager

The FIR That Reopened a Six-Year-Old Case

A fresh Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) First Information Report has named Shiv Sena (UBT) leader Aaditya Thackeray in connection with the death of Disha Salian, the former manager of late actor Sushant Singh Rajput. Salian died on 8 June 2020, days before Rajput's own death on 14 June 2020. The new FIR treats her death as a murder investigation rather than a closed accidental-fall case.

Thackeray's response was blunt: "Never met." He described the FIR as character assassination and questioned why he was named without any documented interaction with Salian. His statement marks his first direct public comment since the case was formally reopened.

What the CBI FIR Actually Names

Multiple outlets, including NDTV, The Times of India, Telegraph India, and India Today, report that the FIR lists several public figures alongside Thackeray. These include actor Sooraj Pancholi, Rhea Chakraborty, and model-actor Dino Morea. The central question the probe appears to be pursuing is who was present at the gathering Salian attended before her death, and whether the original Mumbai Police investigation missed material evidence.

Political Fallout and Legal Standing

The timing has become the story. Nitesh Raut of the ruling alliance linked the fresh CBI probe directly to Thackeray's ongoing campaign against the Fadnavis government, suggesting the FIR is retaliatory rather than evidentiary. Thackeray's camp frames it the same way from the opposite direction, calling the move a pressure tactic.

Legally, being named in an FIR is not equivalent to being charged. An FIR records allegations and sets the scope of investigation. No charge sheet naming Thackeray has been filed or verified at the time of writing. Readers should treat the FIR as the start of a process, not a finding of guilt.

Why This Case Keeps Returning

The Salian and Rajput deaths have been politically volatile since 2020. Every procedural development, from the transfer of the case to the CBI to the emergence of new witnesses, has been contested. A "mystery woman" angle reported by NDTV adds another unverified thread to a case already crowded with competing narratives.

For the public, the practical takeaway is procedural: watch for a charge sheet, not a headline. FIRs can name dozens of people who are later cleared. Until the CBI files its findings, the only confirmed facts are the dates, the agencies involved, and the named individuals' public denials.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Denial is on record: Thackeray has publicly stated he never met Salian, giving his legal team a clear factual position to defend.
  • FIR is not a charge sheet: Naming in an FIR does not establish criminal liability under Indian procedure.
  • Political overlap is unavoidable: The probe's timing coincides with an active political confrontation in Maharashtra.
  • Multiple names, one investigation: Pancholi, Chakraborty, and Morea face the same procedural position as Thackeray until evidence is filed.
  • Verification gap: The specific evidentiary basis for naming Thackeray has not been publicly disclosed.

What Buyers of News Should Watch Next

The next meaningful checkpoint is whether the CBI converts the FIR into a charge sheet and whether it produces documented evidence of any meeting between Thackeray and Salian. Until then, coverage should be read as allegation plus denial, not established fact. TIVRA News will track the CBI's filings and any court proceedings that follow.
