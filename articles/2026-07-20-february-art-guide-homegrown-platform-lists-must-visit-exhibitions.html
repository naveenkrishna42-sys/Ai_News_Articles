February Art Guide: Homegrown Platform Lists Must-Visit Exhibitions

Key points
  • The guide covers art events in major Indian cities including Mumbai, Delhi, and Bengaluru.
  • It includes a mix of contemporary art exhibitions, traditional craft fairs, and cultural workshops.
  • The platform focuses on promoting local artists and homegrown cultural initiatives.

February is shaping up to be a packed month for art and culture enthusiasts across India. A homegrown digital platform has released a curated guide listing exhibitions, workshops, and performances worth visiting this month.

Curated Picks for City Dwellers

The guide features events in major metropolitan cities, including Mumbai, Delhi, and Bengaluru. Each listing highlights a mix of contemporary art shows, traditional craft fairs, and interactive sessions.

In Mumbai, a solo exhibition by a young painter explores urban landscapes through mixed media. Delhi's calendar includes a photography showcase documenting the city's evolving neighbourhoods. Bengaluru hosts a weekend workshop on indigenous textile dyeing techniques.

Spotlight on Local Talent

The platform has made a deliberate effort to feature lesser-known artists alongside established names. Several galleries are using the February window to launch debut shows for emerging painters and sculptors.

One notable listing is a group exhibition in Pune that brings together ten artists from small towns in Maharashtra. Their works use recycled materials to comment on environmental degradation.

Beyond Galleries: Festivals and Markets

The guide also includes outdoor cultural festivals. A two-day literature and music festival in Jaipur will host discussions on regional folklore. In Chennai, a weekend market will sell handmade pottery and handloom fabrics directly from artisans.

Several venues are offering free entry on specific days to attract first-time visitors. Organisers hope this will broaden the audience for traditional art forms.

Practical Information for Visitors

Most events require prior registration due to limited capacity. The guide provides direct links to booking portals and contact details for each venue. Timings vary, with some exhibitions open late on weekends.

For those unable to travel, a few galleries are offering virtual walkthroughs. The platform plans to update the list weekly as new events are confirmed.

February's art calendar reflects a growing appetite for cultural experiences outside mainstream entertainment. With this guide, organisers aim to make art more accessible to casual visitors and serious collectors alike.
