The Corn Moon: A Celestial Event to Mark Your Calendar

The September full moon, known as the Corn Moon, will peak on September 19, 2026. This celestial event is the last full moon of the Northern Hemisphere's summer season and is expected to be visible worldwide.

What is the Corn Moon?

The Corn Moon is the name given to the September full moon by Native American tribes. It is so named because it marks the time of year when corn, or maize, is harvested. This full moon is also known as the Barley Moon, as it coincides with the time when barley is harvested in Europe.

When and How to See the Corn Moon

The Corn Moon will be visible in the night sky on September 19, 2026. The best time to view the full moon is when it is at its highest point in the sky, which is around midnight. To get the best view, find a location with minimal light pollution and look towards the east.

The Corn Moon will be visible for several days before and after its peak, so even if you miss it on September 19, you can still catch a glimpse of it on September 18 or 20.

Why Does the Corn Moon Matter?

The Corn Moon is significant for several reasons. Firstly, it marks the end of the summer season in the Northern Hemisphere, which is a time of year when many people look forward to the cooler weather and the changing of the leaves.

Secondly, the Corn Moon is an important event for astronomers and skywatchers. It is a great opportunity to observe the moon's phases and learn more about its movements in the night sky.

Finally, the Corn Moon is a reminder of the importance of agriculture and the role it plays in our daily lives. As the name suggests, the Corn Moon coincides with the time of year when corn is harvested, which is a crucial crop for many parts of the world.

Expert Opinions and Critical Analysis

Dr. Sarah Johnson, an astronomer at the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), says that the Corn Moon is a great opportunity for people to get out and enjoy the night sky. She advises that people should find a location with minimal light pollution and use a pair of binoculars or a telescope to get a better view of the moon.

However, Dr. Johnson also warns that the Corn Moon may not be visible in some parts of the world due to weather conditions or other factors. She advises that people should check the weather forecast before heading out to view the full moon.
