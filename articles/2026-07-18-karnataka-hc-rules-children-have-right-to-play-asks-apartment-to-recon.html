Karnataka HC rules children have right to play, asks apartment to reconsider football ban

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Karnataka High Court says children have a fundamental right to play and recreation.
  • Court asks apartment association to reconsider its ban on children playing football in common areas.
  • Judgment underscores balance between right to play and residents' need for safety and peace.

Bengaluru: The Karnataka High Court has ruled that children have a fundamental right to play and recreation, directing an apartment association in the city to rethink its ban on football in common areas. The order, delivered on Wednesday, underscores that recreational activities are essential for a child's development and cannot be arbitrarily curtailed.

The court was hearing a petition filed by a group of parents who challenged the apartment's blanket prohibition on children playing football in the complex's designated play areas. The association had argued that the game posed a safety risk to other residents and damaged property.

Right to play is integral to development

Justice M. Nagaprasanna, who presided over the case, observed that the right to play is not a mere luxury but a crucial component of a child's physical and mental growth. He cited previous Supreme Court rulings that recognise recreation as part of the right to life under Article 21 of the Constitution.

The judge noted that children spend significant time in school and studies, leaving limited opportunities for unstructured play. Banning an active sport like football entirely, he said, would be disproportionate and could deprive children of essential exercise and social interaction.

Balance between safety and freedom

The court did not outright strike down the ban but asked the apartment association to revisit its decision within four weeks. It suggested that the body could impose reasonable restrictions, such as designated playing hours or supervised play, rather than a complete prohibition.

The bench emphasised that residents' concerns about safety and property damage were not without merit. However, it said these issues could be addressed through dialogue and practical solutions, not by denying children their right to play.

The judgment comes amid growing tensions in many Bengaluru apartments over children's recreational activities. Several resident welfare associations have imposed restrictions on sports like cricket and football, citing noise and risk of injury.

Legal precedent in earlier cases

Lawyers familiar with the matter pointed out that the Karnataka High Court had previously ruled on similar issues, including a 2022 order that allowed children to play cricket in apartment complexes. The present judgment builds on that line of reasoning.

The court also noted that apartment associations cannot act arbitrarily and must respect the rights of all residents, including children. It urged both sides to arrive at a consensus through mutual consultation.

Parents who filed the petition welcomed the order, saying it vindicated their stance that play is not an optional activity but a necessity for healthy childhood. The apartment association has not yet indicated whether it will appeal the decision.

The case is likely to set a precedent for similar disputes across the city, where urban living spaces often leave little room for children to play freely. Legal experts say the ruling reinforces the principle that developmental needs must be weighed against community rules.

The court has posted the matter for further hearing after four weeks, by which time the association is expected to present a revised policy. Until then, the interim arrangement allowing limited football play continues.
