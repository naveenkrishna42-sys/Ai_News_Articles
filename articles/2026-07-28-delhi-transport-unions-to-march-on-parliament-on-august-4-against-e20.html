Delhi Transport Unions to March on Parliament on August 4 Against E20 Fuel

📅 2026-07-28 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi transport unions plan a protest march to Parliament on August 4 against the mandatory E20 fuel blend.
  • Union leaders accuse MPs of being 'asleep' and ignoring the impact on vehicles and commuters.
  • The protest follows a social media campaign and previous demonstrations against the ethanol policy.
  • The government and BJP have launched counter-campaigns to promote E20 as an environmentally friendly measure.

Transport Unions Call for Action

Delhi transport bodies have announced a protest march to Parliament on August 4, 2026, against the government's E20 fuel policy. The unions represent thousands of auto-rickshaw, taxi, and bus drivers in the national capital. They argue that the mandatory 20% ethanol blend in petrol is damaging engines and increasing costs for operators.

Leaders of the joint front stated that MPs are 'sleeping' and need a 'wake-up call' on the issue. The march is expected to draw significant participation from drivers and small fleet owners who rely on petrol vehicles for their livelihoods.

Growing Discontent Over E20 Policy

The E20 mandate, introduced to reduce emissions and cut oil import bills, has faced resistance from transport unions across the country. Critics say the blend reduces fuel efficiency and can void warranties on older vehicles not designed for higher ethanol content. The Delhi unions have been vocal in their opposition, with some forming the 'E20 Janta Party' on social media after a previous protest involving cockroaches at a government event.

Union leaders claim the policy was rolled out without adequate consultation or compensation for drivers. They demand a phased implementation and subsidies for vehicle retrofitting.

Government's Counter-Offensive

The government and the BJP have launched a coordinated social media campaign to counter the protests. Officials argue that E20 will reduce pollution and dependence on fossil fuels. Union Minister Nitin Gadkari has been a key proponent of the policy, defending it as a step toward energy independence.

However, transport unions remain unconvinced, accusing the government of pushing the policy without addressing ground-level challenges. The August 4 march is intended to pressure lawmakers ahead of the winter session of Parliament.

What Comes Next

The protest marks an escalation in the months-long standoff between transport bodies and the government. With the march set for August 4, authorities are bracing for traffic disruptions in central Delhi. The unions have vowed to continue their agitation until their demands are met, while the government shows no signs of rolling back the E20 mandate. All eyes will be on Parliament's response to the growing chorus of dissent.
