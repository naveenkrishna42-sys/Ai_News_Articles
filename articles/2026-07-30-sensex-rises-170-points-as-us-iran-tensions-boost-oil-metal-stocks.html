Sensex Rises 170 Points as US-Iran Tensions Boost Oil, Metal Stocks

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex closed 170 points higher amid renewed US-Iran geopolitical tensions.
  • Rising crude oil prices boosted energy and metal stocks on the bourse.
  • Broader markets also gained, with the Nifty ending above key levels.

Indian stock markets ended in positive territory on Thursday, with the Sensex climbing 170 points, as renewed tensions between the United States and Iran triggered a rally in oil and metal stocks.

The 30-share BSE benchmark settled at 57,846, while the broader Nifty closed at 17,225, up 0.4 percent. Gains were led by energy, oil & gas and metal counters, which jumped on the back of rising crude prices.

What Drove the Rally

Fresh US-Iran tensions pushed global crude oil prices higher, with Brent crude hovering near $94 a barrel. This directly benefited Indian oil marketing companies and exploration firms, including ONGC, Reliance Industries and Oil India.

Metal stocks also saw strong buying interest. Hindalco, Tata Steel and JSW Steel were among the top gainers on the Nifty. Analysts said the geopolitical uncertainty prompted investors to shift into commodities and defensive sectors.

Sectoral Performance

BSE Oil & Gas index surged over 2 percent, while the BSE Metal index added 1.8 percent. Banking stocks were mixed, with HDFC Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank trading flat, while SBI edged higher.

IT stocks lagged, with Infosys and TCS closing marginally lower. Auto stocks also had a subdued session, with Maruti Suzuki and M&M ending in the red.

Broader Market and Volatility

The broader market mirrored the benchmark. The BSE Midcap index gained 0.5 percent, while the Smallcap index rose 0.3 percent. Market breadth was positive, with about 1,700 stocks advancing against 1,400 declining on the BSE.

Volatility remained elevated. The India VIX, which measures market fear, rose 3 percent to 18.5, reflecting investor caution over the geopolitical flashpoint.

What to Watch

Investors are now watching for any further escalation in US-Iran tensions and its impact on oil prices. The trajectory of foreign portfolio investor flows and the Reserve Bank of India's policy stance will also shape market direction in the coming sessions.
