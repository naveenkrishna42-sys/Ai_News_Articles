Oil prices set for weekly gain as Middle East tensions push crude above $100

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil prices are on pace for a weekly gain as Middle East tensions escalate.
  • Brent crude has crossed the $100 a barrel mark amid the Iran war.
  • Overseas buyers are aggressively pursuing US crude as wars escalate.
  • Oil companies fear a hit to their finances from the ongoing conflict.
  • Crude oil futures eased 2% on profit-booking after recent highs.

Brent Crude Crosses $100 a Barrel

Oil prices are set for a weekly gain as the escalating conflict in the Middle East, particularly the Iran war, pushes crude higher. Brent crude has crossed the $100 a barrel mark for the first time in months, a direct result of fresh tensions in the region.

The surge reflects growing supply fears. Markets are pricing in the risk of a wider conflict that could disrupt major oil shipping routes. This has sent shockwaves through global energy markets.

Global Buyers Chase US Crude

Overseas buyers are now in hot pursuit of American crude. As Middle Eastern supply becomes riskier, the US has emerged as a key alternative. Reports from Bloomberg and other outlets confirm a sharp uptick in demand for US barrels.

This shift is remaking global trade flows. Tankers are rerouting, and US export terminals are running near capacity. The scramble for supply is a direct consequence of the geopolitical turmoil.

Oil Companies Warn of Financial Hit

Major oil companies are bracing for a financial hit. The Iran war and the resulting price spike are creating new uncertainties. Firms face higher operational costs and potential disruptions to their investments in the region.

The Times of India reports that companies fear the prolonged instability will strain their balance sheets. Profitability is under pressure even as revenues rise, due to the volatile operating environment.

Profit-Booking Tempers Rally

On Friday, crude oil futures eased 2% to Rs 8,876 per barrel on the domestic exchange. Traders attributed this to profit-booking after the sharp run-up earlier in the week. The dip is seen as a temporary correction.

Analysts remain cautious. The underlying trend is still bullish, driven by supply-side risks. Any de-escalation in the conflict could trigger a sharper sell-off, but no such signs have emerged yet.

What to Watch Next

All eyes are now on diplomatic efforts to de-escalate the Iran situation. Any progress could cool oil prices quickly. Conversely, a further escalation could push Brent well above $100.
