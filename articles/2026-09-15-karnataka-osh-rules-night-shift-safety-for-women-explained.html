Karnataka Clarifies Legal Cover for Women on Night Shifts

The Karnataka Labour Department has confirmed that safeguards for women employees working night shifts are governed under the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions (OSH) Rules. The clarification, reported by The Hindu, settles a long-running ambiguity for Bengaluru's 24x7 IT services, BPO, healthcare and manufacturing employers who have operated under a patchwork of state notifications and factory-specific exemptions.

For HR heads and compliance officers, the message is direct: night-shift protection is not a discretionary perk. It is a codified condition of employment, enforceable through routine labour inspections.

What the OSH Rules Actually Require

The OSH framework, operationalised through state rules, sets out specific obligations for establishments employing women between 7 PM and 6 AM. These include written consent from the employee, safe and verified transport to and from the workplace, rest and washroom facilities, adequate lighting, and in many cases, a minimum number of women on a shift or a designated supervisor.

Employers must also maintain registers documenting shift rosters, consent forms and transport logs. These records are the first documents an inspector will request during a compliance audit.

Why This Matters for Bengaluru Employers

Bengaluru's tech and healthcare corridors run some of India's largest night-shift operations. A typical IT services firm with 2,000 employees may deploy 600 to 800 staff on night rotations, each requiring documented consent and transport arrangements. The cost of compliant cab services, security escorts and rest facilities can run into several lakh per month for mid-sized units.

Treating these costs as optional has proven expensive. Penalties under the OSH framework, combined with inspection-driven shutdowns and reputational fallout, dwarf the recurring expense of doing it right.

Practical Buyer and Employer Implications

  • Transport contracts matter: Verify vendor background checks, GPS tracking and driver verification before signing annual cab agreements.
  • Consent is not permanent: Women can withdraw consent; rosters must be updated immediately.
  • Audit readiness: Maintain digital registers for consent, transport and shift allocation.
  • Insurance linkage: Several group health and accident policies now ask for night-shift compliance proof before underwriting.
  • Vendor selection: Facility management and security vendors with OSH audit experience command a 10-20% premium but reduce inspection risk.

Pros and Cons of the Current Framework

  • Pro: Clear legal anchor removes ambiguity across sectors.
  • Pro: Standardised safeguards improve women's workforce participation in night operations.
  • Con: Compliance cost is significant for small and mid-sized units.
  • Con: Enforcement quality varies across districts, creating uneven playing fields.
  • Con: Employers still cite ambiguity on shift-timing exemptions in specific industries.

What Employers Should Do Now

Conduct an internal audit against OSH Rule requirements within the next quarter. Map every woman employee on night shifts, verify consent documentation, and reconcile transport vendor contracts with actual shift timings. Assign a named compliance owner, ideally within HR or EHS, and schedule quarterly reviews.

For employees, the clarification strengthens your position. If transport, rest facilities or consent records are missing, you can escalate to the establishment's grievance mechanism or the jurisdictional labour officer.

The Karnataka Labour Department's position aligns with a broader national push to formalise night-shift safety. Employers who treat this as an operational checkbox will face rising inspection risk. Those who build it into workforce planning gain a measurable edge in retaining women talent in 24x7 roles.
