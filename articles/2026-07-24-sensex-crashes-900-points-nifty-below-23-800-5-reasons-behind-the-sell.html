Sensex crashes 900 points, Nifty below 23,800: 5 reasons behind the sell-off

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex plunged 900 points, Nifty50 closed below 23,800
  • Brent crude crossed $100 per barrel amid US-Iran tensions
  • US trade tariffs and West Asia war escalation triggered sell-off
  • Markets extended losses for fifth consecutive day
  • Investors worried about inflation, corporate earnings, and global slowdown

Indian equity markets faced a brutal session on Friday, with the Sensex crashing over 900 points and the Nifty50 closing below the crucial 23,800 mark. The sell-off marked the fifth straight day of losses as a combination of global and domestic factors spooked investors.

The benchmark indices opened weak and never recovered. The BSE Sensex settled 900 points lower, while the Nifty50 ended deep in the red. Broader market indices also suffered heavy losses, with midcap and smallcap stocks falling sharply.

Oil above $100 fuels panic

The biggest trigger for Friday's crash was the surge in global crude prices. Brent crude topped $100 per barrel for the first time in months as the conflict between the US and Iran escalated in West Asia. Market participants feared that a prolonged military confrontation would disrupt oil supplies from the region, pushing inflation higher and hurting corporate margins.

Rising oil prices directly impact India's import bill and widen the current account deficit. Fuel and raw material costs rise, squeezing profits for companies in sectors like aviation, paints, tyres, and FMCG. The rupee also came under pressure against the dollar, adding to the nervousness.

US trade tariffs add to uncertainty

Another major reason for the downturn was the fresh round of US trade tariffs announced earlier this week. The new levies target several countries, including India, and are expected to hit export-oriented sectors such as IT, pharma, and textiles. Investors are worried that a full-blown trade war will slow global growth and hurt Indian companies' earnings.

Foreign portfolio investors have been pulling money out of emerging markets, including India, amid the uncertainty. In the past five sessions, FIIs have sold heavily in the cash market, adding to the selling pressure from domestic institutions.

West Asia war fears rattle sentiment

The escalating military conflict in West Asia has become a major overhang for markets. The US-Iran war has raised concerns about a broader regional conflict that could disrupt trade routes and energy supplies. Defence stocks saw some buying, but most other sectors were in the red. Banking, auto, and metal stocks were among the worst hit.

Airline stocks tumbled as jet fuel costs are expected to rise sharply. Hotel and tourism stocks also fell on fears of reduced travel to the region. Analysts said the market is pricing in a prolonged period of geopolitical instability.

Inflation fears and rate outlook

Rising crude prices and supply chain disruptions have reignited inflation worries. The Reserve Bank of India had earlier paused its rate-cutting cycle, but the recent developments may force it to keep interest rates higher for longer. Higher borrowing costs could slow down economic growth and dampen consumer demand.

Bond yields rose on Friday, reflecting the market's expectation of tighter monetary policy. Real estate and auto stocks, which are sensitive to interest rates, saw sharp declines.

Weak global cues and technical selling

Global markets were also under pressure. US and European indices fell overnight, and Asian markets followed suit on Friday. The sell-off in India was amplified by technical factors — the Nifty broke key support levels, triggering stop-losses and automated selling.

Options data showed heavy put writing at lower levels, indicating that traders expect further downside in the near term. Volatility index VIX spiked sharply, reflecting high anxiety among market participants.

Market breadth was extremely negative. On the BSE, more than 2,500 stocks declined, while only about 600 advanced. All sectoral indices ended in the red, with realty, auto, and bank indices falling the most.

Investors are now waiting for clarity on the geopolitical front and any intervention by the government or RBI to calm the markets. A sharp correction in global crude prices or a de-escalation in West Asia could provide some relief. Until then, volatility is likely to remain high.
