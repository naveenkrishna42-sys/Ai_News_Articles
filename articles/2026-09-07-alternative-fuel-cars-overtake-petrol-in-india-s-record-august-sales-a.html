  • Market share of EVs and hybrids hit 42% in urban zones, pressuring petrol car affordability and dealer inventory

Alternative-Fuel Sales Milestone: A Historic Shift in India’s Auto Market

In a landmark development for India’s automotive sector, alternative-fuel cars have outsold pure-petrol models for the first time in August 2026, according to the Federation of Indian Fantasy Dealers (FIFD). The data, corroborated by Team-BHP and Autocar India, shows 24.23 lakh alternative-fuel vehicles sold (including electric, hybrid, CNG, and E20-compatible models), compared to 17.8 lakh petrol cars. This marks a 17.51% YoY growth in total retail sales, with alternative powertrains now commanding 57% of the market share. The shift reflects deepening consumer skepticism over petrol vehicle affordability amid rising fuel costs and government push for cleaner alternatives.

Cost Advantages Drive Consumer Adoption

Primary drivers behind the surge include compelling total cost of ownership (TCO) benefits for alternative-fuel vehicles. Maruti Suzuki’s EVi1, priced at ₹8.49 lakh, offers ₹1.2 lakh savings over 5 years versus its petrol counterpart, while Tata Motors’ Nexon EV (₹10.49 lakh) slashes fuel expenses by ₹1.8 lakh annually. Dealers report 68% of buyers in metros prioritise upfront savings and low maintenance costs. “EV subsidies and charging infrastructure expansion make alternatives economically irresistible,” said Rajesh Khanna, FIFD president.

E20 Fuel Concerns Amplify Petrol Car Decline

India’s E20 ethanol blending policy faces implementation hurdles, with only 35% of petrol stations certified to handle the fuel blend. Dealers cite consumer reluctance to use non-E20 fuels in vehicles designed for it, citing warranty risks. “Buyers fear engine damage from E20 incompatibility,” explained Priya Mehta, a New Delhi dealer. Petrol car sales dropped 12% MoM as consumers gravitate toward EVs/CNG, which avoid this uncertainty. The government’s delayed infrastructure rollout for E20 has accelerated the shift toward alternative-fuel models with no blending risks.

Regional Variations and Market Dynamics

Western and Southern states lead the transition, where charging networks cover 80% of urban areas. In contrast, petrol sales remain strong in Tier-2 cities reliant on CNG subsidies. Mahindra’s eKUV100 dominates the sub-4m segment with 45% share, while Hyundai’s hybrid Creta commands ₹1.5 lakh premiums over petrol variants. Tata Motors posted highest YoY growth (38.6%) in August, driven by Punch EV and Harrier e-PHEV.

Implications for Buyers, Manufacturers, and Policy

For buyers, the shift signals urgent TCO advantages but requires adapting to new ownership models—EV buyers now prioritise charging access over showroom location. Manufacturers face inventory pressure, with petrol car stockpiles rising 22% in Q3. Policymakers must fast-track E20 infrastructure and GST harmonisation for parts to sustain momentum. “The rulebook is changing; buyers now demand transparent TCO calculators,” noted Khanna. Analysts project alternative-fuel sales to hit 65% market share by March 2027, with subsidies and charging expansion as critical enablers.
