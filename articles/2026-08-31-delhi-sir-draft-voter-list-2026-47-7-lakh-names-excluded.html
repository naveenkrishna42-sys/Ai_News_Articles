Delhi's Draft Electoral Roll for 2026 Released

Delhi's draft electoral roll for the 2026 elections has been released, showing a significant number of names excluded after the State Intelligence Report (SIR). The draft roll, which is a preview of the final voter list, includes 47.7 lakh names that have been struck off. This means nearly one in three voters in Delhi have been removed from the list.

Why the Exclusions?

The exclusions are based on the SIR, which is a comprehensive report prepared by the Election Commission of India (ECI) to identify potential electoral malpractices. The report includes details of voters who have died, migrated, or are otherwise ineligible to vote. The ECI uses this report to clean up the electoral rolls and ensure that only eligible voters are included in the final list.

How to Check Your Name on Delhi's Draft Voter Roll

If you're a resident of Delhi, you can check whether your name is included in the draft electoral roll for 2026. Here's a step-by-step guide to help you do that:

  • Visit the official website of the Election Commission of India (eci.gov.in).
  • Click on the 'Voter Services' tab and select 'Download Draft Electoral Rolls'.
  • Select the state of Delhi and the relevant district.
  • Download the draft electoral roll for your area.
  • Search for your name in the draft roll.

Broader Implications

The release of Delhi's draft electoral roll is a significant development in the country's electoral process. It provides a preview of the final voter list and allows voters to check whether their names are included. The exclusions based on the SIR are a necessary step to ensure the integrity of the electoral process. However, the high number of exclusions raises questions about the accuracy of the SIR and the potential impact on the upcoming elections.

Expert Opinion

Electoral expert, Dr. Rajiv Kumar, says that the exclusions based on the SIR are a routine process. He explains that the ECI uses the SIR to identify and remove ineligible voters. However, he also warns that the high number of exclusions in Delhi could have an impact on the upcoming elections. 'The draft roll is a preview, but the final roll will be released soon. The exclusions could affect the voter turnout and the overall results,' he says.
