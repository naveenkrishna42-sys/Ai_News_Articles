Hyderabad: Madhu Park Ridge residents plan NGT move over Musi Riverfront project

📅 2026-07-16 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Madhu Park Ridge residents to move NGT against Musi Riverfront project
  • Concerns include environmental impact and lack of proper consultation
  • Project aims to rejuvenate the Musi river but faces local opposition

Residents Gear Up for Legal Battle

Residents of Madhu Park Ridge in Hyderabad are preparing to approach the National Green Tribunal (NGT) against the Musi Riverfront Development Project. The decision comes amid growing concerns over the project's environmental impact and the manner in which it is being executed.

The residents' welfare association has been meeting regularly to discuss the legal strategy. They argue that the project violates several environmental norms and that the authorities have not conducted adequate public consultations.

Environmental Concerns Take Centre Stage

At the heart of the dispute is the fear that the riverfront development could harm the local ecosystem. Residents point to potential loss of green cover, disruption of natural water flow, and increased pollution during construction.

They also claim that the project's environmental impact assessment (EIA) is flawed and does not account for the cumulative effect on the area's biodiversity. The association has documented several instances of tree felling and soil erosion near the construction site.

Lack of Transparency Fuels Anger

Many residents feel left in the dark about the project's details. They say the Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority (HMDA) has not shared the full project plan or responded to their queries adequately.

“We are not against development, but it must be sustainable and transparent,” a senior resident told Telangana Today. The association has also written to local elected representatives but received no satisfactory response.

Project Aims vs Local Reality

The Musi Riverfront Development Project is a flagship initiative of the Telangana government. It seeks to rejuvenate the Musi river, which has long suffered from pollution and encroachment, and create a recreational space for citizens.

Officials have said the project will include parks, walkways, and sewage treatment plants. However, residents of Madhu Park Ridge, a locality that abuts the river, say they were never consulted about the design or its potential impact on their homes.

Legal Route and Next Steps

The residents' association has engaged a lawyer to file a petition before the NGT. They are expected to seek a stay on construction until a fresh environmental assessment is done.

The petition will also highlight alleged violations of the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act and the Environment Protection Act. The NGT has often taken a strong stance on such issues in the past.

As the legal process unfolds, both sides are bracing for a protracted battle. The HMDA has not yet commented on the planned NGT move.

What to Watch

The NGT will likely hear the matter in the coming weeks. The outcome could set a precedent for how urban riverfront projects are planned and executed in Hyderabad. For now, Madhu Park Ridge residents are digging in for a fight they believe is about more than just their backyard.
