India names 27 Arunachal locations to counter Chinese claims

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • India has formally named 27 locations in Arunachal Pradesh, including 21 land areas, four passes and a lake.
  • The move counters China's use of Mandarin names for these border locations.
  • The names have been standardised on official Indian maps and records.

New names on the map

The Indian government has formally assigned names to 27 geographical locations in Arunachal Pradesh, a strategic border state. This includes 21 land areas, four mountain passes and one lake. The move is aimed at countering China's use of Mandarin names for these sites, which New Delhi sees as an attempt to assert territorial claims.

Officials familiar with the development said the names have been standardised and incorporated into official records and maps. The locations are spread across the upper reaches of the state, near the Line of Actual Control (LAC) with China.

Countering Mandarin labels

China has been naming places on its own maps, often using Mandarin transliterations that differ from Indian usage. This has been a persistent irritant in bilateral relations. By officially naming these 27 sites, India is signalling that it will not accept unilateral nomenclature from Beijing.

The list includes prominent passes that have strategic importance for military movement and logistics. The newly named lake is believed to be a high-altitude water body, though its exact location has not been disclosed in public records yet.

What the names signify

The names chosen are drawn from local tribal languages and historical usage. This is a deliberate attempt to reflect the cultural and geographical reality of the region. Many of these places were previously referred to by descriptive terms or unofficial labels, making coordination between agencies difficult.

Standardising names also helps in disaster response, infrastructure planning and military operations. It ensures that all official communication refers to the same location without ambiguity.

A history of such exercises

This is not the first time India has undertaken such naming exercises. In recent years, the government has renamed or officially designated several border features, including peaks and valleys, to assert administrative control. These actions are part of a broader strategy to strengthen India's presence in border areas.

China has often protested such moves, calling them attempts to legitimise what it terms disputed territory. India, however, maintains that Arunachal Pradesh is an integral part of the country and that naming its own geographical features is a sovereign right.

Reactions and implications

No official statement from the Chinese government has been issued yet in response to the latest naming. Analysts say the move is likely to be met with a formal protest from Beijing, as has happened in previous cases.

The naming exercise also has domestic significance. It comes at a time when India is ramping up infrastructure along the LAC, including roads and bridges. Having standardised names for locations helps in coordinating these projects and in maintaining a clear record of territorial boundaries.

The government has not released the full list of names publicly, but officials say they are based on local usage and have been verified by multiple agencies. This, they argue, ensures that the names are not arbitrary but rooted in the region's heritage.

What to watch next

In the coming weeks, India is expected to publish detailed maps with the new names for official use. It remains to be seen how Beijing reacts and whether this leads to a fresh round of diplomatic exchanges. For now, the naming is a quiet but firm assertion of India's position on its northern borders.
