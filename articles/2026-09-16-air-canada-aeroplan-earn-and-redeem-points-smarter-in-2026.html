What Aeroplan Actually Is in 2026

Air Canada's loyalty programme is no longer the distance-based scheme it was before its 2020 rebuild. Aeroplan now runs on a revenue-linked earning model, which means the points you collect scale with the cash price of your ticket and the cabin you book, not the kilometres you fly. For a frequent flyer in India routing to North America via Toronto, Montreal or Vancouver, that distinction changes the maths entirely.

Air Canada is a Star Alliance founding member, and Aeroplan points can be burned across roughly 35 partner airlines including Air India, Singapore Airlines, Lufthansa, United and ANA. That partner breadth, not the Air Canada product itself, is where most of the outsized value sits.

How You Earn Aeroplan Points

Flying on Air Canada and Star Alliance Partners

On Air Canada-operated flights, earning is tiered by fare class and cabin. A discounted economy fare on a Delhi–Toronto routing typically credits far fewer points than a flexible economy or business fare on the same aircraft. Elite status tiers (25K through Super Elite 100K) add bonus percentages on top of the base accrual.

Credit Cards, Hotels and Shopping

Co-branded Aeroplan cards issued in Canada and the United States remain the fastest accelerant, with category bonuses on Air Canada purchases, groceries and dining. Hotel transfers from Marriott Bonvoy and card points transfers from Amex Membership Rewards and Chase Ultimate Rewards are the other practical tap. Indian cardholders should note that most Aeroplan co-brand cards are issued outside India, so transferable-currency cards are the realistic entry point.

Redeeming Points: Where the Value Lives

Fixed Partner Awards and Stopovers

Aeroplan publishes fixed award pricing for partner-operated flights, which means a business class seat on a partner carrier costs a predictable number of points regardless of the cash fare that day. The one free stopover on a one-way award is genuinely rare in the industry and lets a Delhi–Singapore–Sydney routing include a multi-day break in Singapore at no extra points cost.

Dynamic Air Canada Pricing

On Air Canada's own metal, pricing is dynamic and tracks cash fares. During peak travel, a business class award can spike well above the published partner rates. Buyers should compare the Aeroplan redemption against a partner-operated alternative on the same route before committing points.

Real-World Buyer Implications

  • Pros: Fixed partner award chart, one free stopover on one-ways, strong Star Alliance coverage including Air India, family point pooling for up to eight members, and no last-minute booking fee on Air Canada awards.
  • Cons: Revenue-linked earning penalises cheap fares, carrier surcharges apply on some partners, dynamic pricing on Air Canada metal erodes predictability, and 18-month inactivity expiry is unforgiving for occasional flyers.
  • Best use case: Transferring points from a flexible currency card to book long-haul business class on a partner airline, ideally with a stopover built in.
  • Worst use case: Burning points on short-haul Air Canada economy during peak season, where cents-per-point value often falls below one rupee equivalent.

Verdict: Is Aeroplan Worth Your Spend?

Aeroplan rewards travellers who plan around partner awards rather than Air Canada's own dynamic pricing. For Indian flyers with access to transferable credit card currencies, the programme offers a credible route to Star Alliance business class at fixed rates, plus a stopover benefit that few competitors match. The catch is discipline: earn through cards and transfers rather than cheap fares, book partner metal early, and keep the account active every 18 months. Treat Aeroplan as a redemption engine, not an accrual engine, and the value justifies the effort.
