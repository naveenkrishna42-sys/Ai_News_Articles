Dr Devi Prasad Shetty’s Call for Universal Cholesterol Screening

Renowned cardiac surgeon Dr Devi Prasad Shetty has issued a strong public health advisory urging every Indian above the age of 18 to undergo regular cholesterol level checks. Speaking at a recent health summit in Bengaluru, Shetty emphasized that cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are no longer confined to older demographics, with younger Indians increasingly presenting with critical heart conditions. His recommendation stems from alarming trends in lipid profiles among adults, where high LDL cholesterol and low HDL cholesterol are linked to early-onset heart attacks and strokes.

Underlying Health Risks in the Indian Population

Shetty’s stance aligns with growing epidemiological data showing a 30% rise in CVD cases among adults under 40 over the past decade. Contributing factors include diet-related metabolic syndrome, rampant alcohol and tobacco use, and sedentary urban lifestyles. Genetic predisposition further exacerbates risks, as studies reveal that South Asians develop atherosclerosis at lower BMI levels compared to global counterparts. The Deccan Herald reported that unchecked hypercholesterolemia in young adults often leads to silent plaque buildup, culminating in life-threatening events like myocardial infarctions.

Why Young Indians Are Facing Elevated Heart Risks

Recent reports from the Hindustan Times highlight a surge in heart palpitations and metabolic disturbances among college students and young professionals. Cardiologist Dr Arneja notes that stress-induced tachycardia and anxiety-related lipid spikes are now common presentation symptoms. A study cited by Republic World found that 40% of Indians aged 25–35 have abnormal cholesterol ratios, with triglyceride-to-HDL ratios exceeding safe thresholds. This shift underscores the urgency of early intervention, as conventional wisdom linking heart disease to age is rapidly becoming obsolete.

Dietary and Lifestyle Drivers of Hypercholesterolemia

Processed foods, trans fats, and sugary beverages dominate the modern Indian diet, displacing traditional grains and legumes. Urban populations consume thrice the saturated fats compared to rural areas, according to the National Health Portal. Additionally, 70% of adults report skipping physical activity due to work-related stress. These factors collectively disrupt lipid metabolism, accelerating arterial stiffness and endothelial dysfunction. The hitavada.com analysis stressed that dietary interventions, such as replacing ghee with mustard oil and increasing fiber intake, yield measurable improvements in LDL reduction within six months.

Reimagining Heart Health Protocols for Indians

Shetty advocated for a reformed approach to cardiac care, emphasizing preventive screenings over reactive treatments. The Indian Medical Association now recommends annual lipid profiles starting at age 20, a significant shift from the previous 30-year benchmark. Genetic screening for familial hypercholesterolemia—a condition affecting 1 in 250 Indians—is also gaining traction, enabling early statin therapy. However, experts caution that accessibility remains a barrier, with rural diagnostic centers lacking advanced lipid testing equipment.
