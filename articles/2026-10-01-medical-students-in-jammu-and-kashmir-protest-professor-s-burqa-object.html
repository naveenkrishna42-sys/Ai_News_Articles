Medical Students in Jammu and Kashmir Protest Professor’s Burqa Objection in Labour Room

Background of the Incident

On 30 September 2026, a group of GNM nursing students at Government Medical College Doda reported that a senior professor objected to their wearing of burqas while attending to patients in the labour room. The students said the faculty member asked one of them to remove her abaya, citing concerns over visibility and infection control. The incident quickly spread through peer networks, prompting a formal complaint to the college administration.

Student Reaction and Protest Details

Organising the Demonstration

Within hours of the allegation, over a hundred nursing students gathered outside the college’s main auditorium, holding placards that read "Respect Our Attire" and "Let Us Learn in Peace". The demonstration remained peaceful, with participants chanting slogans that emphasized their right to observe religious dress while performing clinical duties. Student representatives submitted a written memorandum to the dean, demanding an apology and a clear policy on permissible attire in clinical zones.

Voices from the Protest

Several protesters spoke to local media, stating that the burqa is an integral part of their identity and that asking them to remove it undermines their dignity. They argued that standard infection‑control protocols, such as wearing disposable gowns and gloves, already address hygiene concerns without requiring alteration of personal clothing. The students also noted that similar objections have not been raised against other forms of head covering worn by peers of different faiths.

Institutional Response and Inquiry

Committee Constitution

The college administration responded by constituting a three‑member inquiry committee comprising the dean, a senior obstetrician, and a representative from the student welfare office. The committee’s mandate includes interviewing the concerned professor, the students involved, and any witnesses present during the alleged incident. Preliminary findings are expected within ten days, after which recommendations will be forwarded to the governing body for appropriate action.

Administrative Statements

In an official press release, GMC Doda affirmed its commitment to providing a discrimination‑free environment for all learners and patients. The statement clarified that while infection‑control guidelines are strictly enforced in the labour room, they do not prescribe specific religious attire. The administration urged all parties to await the committee’s outcome before drawing conclusions.

Broader Implications for Medical Education

The episode has sparked debate across other medical institutions in Jammu and Kashmir regarding the balance between cultural accommodation and clinical safety standards. Experts suggest that clear, universally applicable dress‑code policies—grounded in evidence‑based infection control—could prevent similar misunderstandings. Meanwhile, student unions have called for workshops on sensitivity and inclusivity to be incorporated into the curriculum, aiming to foster mutual respect among diverse cohorts.
