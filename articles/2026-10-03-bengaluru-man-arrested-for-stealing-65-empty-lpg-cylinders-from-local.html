Bengaluru Man Arrested for Stealing 65 Empty LPG Cylinders from Local Agency

Overview of the Incident

On 30 September 2026, Bengaluru police arrested a man for stealing 65 empty LPG cylinders from a licensed gas agency located in the southern part of the city. The theft was discovered during a routine inventory check when the agency reported a shortfall of the cylinders, which are typically exchanged for refilled units.

Details of the Theft

The stolen cylinders, though empty, retain resale value in the informal market where they are often refilled illegally or sold as scrap. Agency officials estimated the combined market value of the 65 units at roughly ₹1.3 lakh. CCTV footage from the agency’s premises showed a lone individual loading the cylinders into a small van during the early morning hours.

Police Investigation and Arrest

Based on the video evidence, investigators traced the vehicle’s registration number to a resident of the Kengeri area. A tip‑off from a local informant confirmed the suspect’s involvement. Police detained a 34‑year‑old man at his residence, recovered 58 of the cylinders, and seized the van used in the theft. The remaining seven cylinders were later found abandoned near a vacant lot.

Legal Implications and Preventive Measures

The accused has been charged under Section 379 of the Indian Penal Code for theft and Section 411 for dishonestly receiving stolen property. If convicted, he could face imprisonment up to three years, a fine, or both. The case highlights a growing trend of LPG cylinder pilferage driven by demand for illicit refilling operations.

To curb such incidents, gas agencies are encouraged to implement RFID‑based cylinder tracking, conduct surprise audits, and install tamper‑evident seals on valve outlets. Collaboration with local police for regular patrols around storage yards can also deter opportunistic thefts.
