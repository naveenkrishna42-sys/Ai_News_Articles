WHO chief warns Ebola outpacing response as Congo aid workers strike

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • WHO chief warns Ebola outbreak is outpacing response in eastern Congo
  • Aid workers strike over pay, hampering containment efforts
  • Outbreak kills 1,700, becomes world's second-largest Ebola epidemic

Outbreak spirals as response falters

The head of the World Health Organization has issued a stark warning: the Ebola outbreak in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo is now spreading faster than the efforts to contain it. The admission comes as aid workers on the ground downed tools over unpaid wages, crippling an already fragile response.

The epidemic, now the world's second largest on record, has claimed at least 1,700 lives. It is driven by the Bundibugyo virus, a strain distinct from the one that ravaged West Africa between 2014 and 2016.

Strike paralyses containment work

The strike by local health workers has shut down key parts of the response. Vaccination teams, contact tracers, and burial units have all been affected. Workers say they have not been paid for months, despite risking their lives in a region wracked by militia violence.

WHO officials have not confirmed the exact number of workers on strike or how long the disruption may last. But the timing could not be worse, with new cases emerging in remote, hard-to-reach communities.

WHO chief visits Congo amid crisis

During a visit to the affected region, the WHO director-general acknowledged the gravity of the situation. He met with Congolese health authorities and international partners, urging a renewed push to fund and staff the response.

The visit underscores the international community's growing alarm. Aid agencies warn that without immediate action, the outbreak could spiral further, spilling into neighbouring countries and overwhelming fragile health systems.

A perfect storm of violence and neglect

Eastern Congo has been a hotspot of armed conflict for decades, and the Ebola response has repeatedly been hampered by attacks on health facilities. In this context, the strike over pay adds a new layer of crisis.

Health workers on the frontline are demanding not just their dues, but also better protection and support. Their grievances highlight a deeper problem: the international funding that pours in during emergencies often fails to reach local staff who do the heaviest lifting.

What happens next

Talks between health workers and authorities are reportedly underway, but no breakthrough has been announced. The WHO chief has called for a global surge in support, both financial and logistical.

All eyes will be on whether the strike is resolved quickly and whether the international community can match its rhetoric with resources. The coming weeks will determine whether this outbreak becomes a contained chapter or a full-blown continental catastrophe.
