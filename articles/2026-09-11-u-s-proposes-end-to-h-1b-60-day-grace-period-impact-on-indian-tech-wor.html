U.S. Proposes End to H-1B 60-Day Grace Period: Impact on Indian Tech Workforce

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security has proposed eliminating the 60-day grace period for H-1B visa holders who lose their jobs, potentially affecting thousands of Indian technology professionals working in the United States. This policy change, if implemented, would require foreign workers to leave the country immediately upon job termination rather than having a temporary period to find new employment.

Policy Background and Implementation Details

The proposal, announced on September 11, 2026, represents a significant shift in U.S. immigration policy regarding temporary work visas. Currently, H-1B holders who lose their employment are granted a 60-day period to either find a new job in a similar capacity or leave the country. The new rule would remove this safety net, creating immediate pressure on visa holders to secure alternative employment or depart within days of termination.

According to the Department of Homeland Security, the change aims to protect American workers by ensuring that foreign professionals are not remaining in the country without valid employment. The policy would apply to all H-1B visa holders, including those working in technology, healthcare, and other professional sectors.

The proposed rule has significant implications for both employers and employees. The table below summarizes the key technical specifications and market impact metrics:

The proposal comes amid growing concerns about workforce mobility and the role of foreign professionals in the U.S. economy. The technology sector, which heavily relies on H-1B workers, could face significant talent shortages if the policy is implemented without adjustments.

Real-World Implications for Indian Professionals

For Indian tech workers, the potential consequences are severe. Many professionals rely on the 60-day grace period to transition between jobs or to find new employment opportunities. The elimination of this period could lead to immediate deportation for those unable to secure alternative employment quickly.

Employers may also face challenges in maintaining their workforce, potentially leading to increased costs and operational disruptions. The policy change could also affect the U.S.-India technology partnership, which has been a cornerstone of the global tech ecosystem.

  • Pros/Key Benefits: Potential protection for American workers from competition with foreign professionals
  • Cons/Key Risks: Increased risk of unemployment and deportation for H-1B holders
  • Economic Impact: Possible talent shortages in technology and other professional sectors
  • Employer Burden: Increased costs and operational disruptions for companies relying on H-1B workforce
  • Policy Implementation: Complex transition period for affected workers and employers

1. Current H-1B Visa Holders

Approximately 1.5 lakh Indian professionals currently hold H-1B visas, working primarily in technology, engineering, and other professional services. These workers contribute significantly to the U.S. economy and represent a substantial portion of the tech workforce.

2. Technology Sector Impact

The technology sector, which heavily relies on skilled foreign professionals, would face the most significant impact. Companies like Infosys, TCS, and Wipro have thousands of employees working in the U.S. under H-1B visas. The immediate deportation requirement could disrupt operations and lead to talent flight.

3. Economic Consequences

The policy change could have broader economic implications, including potential increases in labor costs as companies may need to hire more American workers or increase wages to attract domestic talent. The impact on the U.S. tech industry's productivity and innovation could also be significant.

4. Legal and Administrative Challenges

The implementation of such a policy would require significant administrative changes and legal adjustments. The Department of Homeland Security would need to develop new procedures for handling job loss cases and ensuring compliance with the new regulations.
