Supreme Court Questions UP Police Over Noida Cleric Assault Probe

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • SC questioned UP Police over lack of arrest in Noida Muslim cleric assault case.
  • Court used phrase 'hide and seek with court' while seeking probe details.
  • Case involves a hate crime; next hearing scheduled in two weeks.

SC Bench Takes Strong Exception to Delay

The Supreme Court of India on Tuesday pulled up the Uttar Pradesh Police over the investigation into the assault of a Muslim cleric in Noida, a case widely seen as a hate crime. A bench of Justices remarked that the police appeared to be playing 'hide and seek with the court'.

The observation came during a hearing on a petition seeking a fair and impartial probe into the attack, which took place earlier this month. The court noted that despite the incident being recorded and widely reported, no arrests had been made so far.

Court Seeks Status Report on Probe

Senior advocates appearing for the petitioner argued that the police had not acted on available evidence and were deliberately dragging their feet. The bench asked the Uttar Pradesh government's counsel to explain the delay in filing a charge sheet or making arrests.

“Why is there no progress? This is a case of a hate crime against a religious figure. The court expects the state to act swiftly and without fear or favour,” the bench observed. The court directed the state to file a detailed status report within two weeks.

Background of the Incident

The assault took place in Noida's Sector 39 area, where the cleric was attacked by a group of men while he was returning home from prayers. The victim sustained injuries and was admitted to a local hospital. The attackers allegedly made communal remarks during the assault.

Local police initially registered an FIR under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code, but critics accused them of not invoking hate speech or organised crime provisions. The case was later transferred to the Uttar Pradesh Special Task Force (STF) following a directive from the Allahabad High Court.

Petitioner Alleges Police Inaction

The petitioner, a civil rights activist, told the Supreme Court that the police had not even recorded the statements of key witnesses. The court expressed displeasure and warned that it would not tolerate any 'delay tactics' by the investigating agency.

Additional Solicitor General representing the UP government assured the bench that the probe was on track and that the STF was working on leads. He requested two weeks to submit a comprehensive report.

Broader Concerns Over Hate Crimes

The case has reignited debate over the rising number of hate crimes in Uttar Pradesh and the state's handling of such incidents. Rights groups have repeatedly flagged the need for swift justice to deter communal violence.

The Supreme Court's intervention is seen as a signal that it will monitor the case closely. The bench has scheduled the next hearing for early May.

What to watch: The UP STF's status report will determine whether the court takes a stricter view, including possible contempt proceedings against police officers if the probe is found to be shoddy.
