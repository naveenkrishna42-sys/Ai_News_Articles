Overview of the 2026 Ganesh Chaturthi Photo Coverage

The Hindu published a dedicated image set for Ganesh Chaturthi 2026, focusing on Mumbai’s public processions and household celebrations. The collection spans three days, from the arrival of the idol to the visarjan on Anant Chaturdashi. Photographers used full‑frame DSLRs and mirrorless bodies equipped with 24‑70 mm f/2.8 lenses, capturing scenes in varying light conditions.

Key Locations Highlighted in the Gallery

The coverage concentrates on three major public sites: Lalbaugcha Raja’s pandal in central Mumbai, the Girgaon Chowpatty beachfront where dozens of idols immerse, and the lanes around Siddhivinayak Temple in Prabhadevi. Smaller household celebrations in Dahisar, Borivali, and Navi Mumbai also appear, providing a geographic spread.

What the Images Reveal About Festival Trends

The 2026 set shows a noticeable increase in eco‑friendly idols made from natural clay and soluble dyes, reflecting heightened environmental awareness among organizers. Crowd density at Chowpatty peaked at roughly 150 000 devotees on the final immersion day, according to police estimates cited in the accompanying captions. Light‑trail shots of fireworks reveal a shift toward low‑noise, LED‑based displays.

Pros and Cons of Using This Image Set

  • High resolution enables large‑format prints and detailed zooming for research.
  • Consistent colour grading across the set simplifies bulk editing for publishers.
  • Broad geographic coverage offers a comprehensive view of Mumbai’s festivities.
  • Editorial‑only licence limits commercial reuse, such as in advertising or merchandise.
  • File sizes, while moderate, require sufficient bandwidth for bulk downloads.
  • No RAW files are provided, restricting post‑processing flexibility for advanced users.
