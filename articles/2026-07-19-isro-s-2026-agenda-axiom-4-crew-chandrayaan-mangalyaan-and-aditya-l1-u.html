ISRO's 2026 Mission Portfolio Takes Shape

India's space agency has laid out an ambitious plan for 2026. The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) is set to execute a series of high-profile missions, from crewed flights to deep space exploration. The list includes the Axiom-4 commercial astronaut mission, the Chandrayaan-4 lunar sample-return effort, the Mangalyaan-2 Mars orbiter, and extended operations for the Aditya L1 solar observatory.

These missions represent a significant step up in complexity and international collaboration for ISRO. Each project targets distinct scientific or technological goals, reflecting the agency's growing capabilities.

Axiom-4: Indian Astronauts Head to the ISS

The Axiom-4 mission, a joint venture with the US-based Axiom Space, will send Indian astronauts to the International Space Station (ISS). This marks a major milestone for India's human spaceflight programme, following the Gaganyaan project's preparatory flights.

ISRO has been training a pool of astronauts for the mission. The crew will conduct microgravity experiments and technology demonstrations over a planned stay at the ISS. The exact launch date is yet to be confirmed.

Chandrayaan-4: Bringing Lunar Soil Back to Earth

Chandrayaan-4 is ISRO's most ambitious lunar mission yet. Unlike its predecessor, which landed a rover near the south pole, this mission aims to collect and return samples from the Moon's surface.

The mission architecture involves multiple modules – a lander, an ascender, and a return vehicle – to drill, collect, and transfer lunar material to Earth. This will be India's first sample-return effort, positioning the country alongside the US, Russia, and China in that elite capability.

Mangalyaan-2: A New Mars Orbiter

Mangalyaan-2, the successor to India's first Mars orbiter, is designed to study the Red Planet's surface and atmosphere in greater detail. The original Mangalyaan (MOM-1) operated for over eight years, far exceeding its planned six-month life.

The new orbiter will carry an improved suite of scientific instruments, including a high-resolution camera, a thermal infrared spectrometer, and a methane sensor. These tools will help scientists map Martian geology and search for signs of past or present life.

Aditya L1: Extended Solar Watch

Aditya L1, India's first solar mission, continues to operate from the Lagrange point L1, about 1.5 million km from Earth. Launched in 2023, it has been observing the Sun's corona, solar flares, and space weather.

ISRO plans to extend the mission's operations through 2026. The data gathered will aid in predicting solar storms that can disrupt satellites and power grids on Earth. No new hardware is planned for this platform, but the science output will remain high.

With these missions, ISRO is demonstrating a broad and sustained push into space. The agency is also working on the Gaganyaan crewed orbital flight, which could launch as soon as 2027.

What to watch: The exact timelines for Axiom-4 and Chandrayaan-4 will depend on technical readiness and launch window alignments. ISRO is expected to announce firm dates later this year.
