VIBE Review: A Spy Comedy That Swings Big and Occasionally Misses

Kunal Kemmu steps behind the camera for VIBE, a spy comedy that trades the polished glamour of Bollywood espionage for chaotic, street-level humour. The film's central conceit, a crew of 'Bahumuliya Gadhas' (high-value donkeys) doing the dirty work of intelligence agencies, is genuinely fresh for Hindi cinema. Early audience reactions on X and Reddit call it the year's funniest Hindi film, while mainstream critics flag a runtime that overstays its welcome by a good 25 minutes. Both things are true.

What VIBE Gets Right: Casting and Comic Timing

Kunal Kemmu's Dual Role

Kemmu directs himself with surprising restraint, letting co-stars land the bigger punches rather than hogging the frame. His comic timing, honed across films like Lootcase and Malang, carries several scenes that would collapse in lesser hands. The script's best gags come from bureaucratic absurdity, not slapstick.

Preity Zinta's Return to Form

Zinta's presence gives the film a nostalgic anchor. She plays the straight woman to Kemmu's chaos, and the pairing works better than the marketing suggests. For audiences who grew up on Kal Ho Naa Ho and Veer-Zaara, this is a welcome, if undemanding, comeback vehicle.

Where VIBE Struggles: Runtime and Tonal Drift

The Hindu's review called it 'rudderless and overlong,' and that criticism holds. The first half crackles with energy, but the second act introduces subplots that dilute the central spy mission. A tighter edit could have trimmed 20-25 minutes without losing a single laugh. The tonal shifts between farce and genuine thriller beats also feel jarring in places.

Pros and Cons: The Real-World Verdict

  • Pros: Genuinely funny first half, strong ensemble chemistry, fresh satirical premise targeting India's intelligence bureaucracy.
  • Pros: Preity Zinta's comeback adds star value; Kemmu proves a capable director of actors.
  • Cons: Overlong runtime tests patience; second half loses narrative focus.
  • Cons: Tonal inconsistency between comedy and thriller elements.
  • Cons: Limited theatrical window likely given mid-budget positioning.

Box Office and Buyer Implications

For a film estimated under Rs 40 crore, VIBE does not need a Rs 100 crore opening to succeed. Its fate rests on weekday footfalls driven by word-of-mouth, particularly in metro multiplexes where adult comedies perform steadily. Streaming rights, likely to a major OTT platform within 8-10 weeks, will determine final profitability. Shilpa Shetty and Raj Kundra's endorsement on social media signals the film's appeal to the 30-plus urban demographic.

Final Verdict: Worth Your Ticket?

VIBE is not the saviour Bollywood needs, but it is a step in the right direction: original, mid-budget, and unafraid to be silly. If you enjoy Lootcase-style humour and can tolerate a saggy middle, this is a solid weekend watch. Catch it in theatres for the audience energy, or wait for streaming if runtime is a dealbreaker. Either way, Kemmu's directing career is worth tracking.
