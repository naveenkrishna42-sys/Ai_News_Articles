Udhayanidhi Stalin released after questioning at Sengipatti police station

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Udhayanidhi Stalin was released after questioning at Sengipatti police station.
  • He said he had no intention of disparaging women.
  • The questioning follows a controversy over his 'Trisha' remark, drawing BJP criticism.

Udhayanidhi Stalin, Tamil Nadu's Minister for Youth Welfare and Sports Development, was released on Tuesday afternoon after being questioned at the Sengipatti police station in Thanjavur district. The questioning, which lasted several hours, was in connection with a complaint filed over his recent remarks. The minister's release was confirmed by police officials, who did not disclose details of the questioning.

Context of the questioning

The police action stems from a controversy ignited by Udhayanidhi's comments during a public meeting, where he referred to actor Trisha in a manner that drew sharp criticism. The remarks were perceived by some as disparaging towards women, leading to a formal complaint and subsequent police summons.

In a statement following his release, Udhayanidhi maintained that he had no intention of disparaging women. He stressed that his words were taken out of context and expressed regret over the misunderstanding. The minister's clarification, however, has not quelled the political storm.

Political reactions

The BJP's Khushbu Sundar was among the first to attack Udhayanidhi, questioning his remarks with a pointed challenge: "If You're Man Enough..." The former actress-turned-politician's sharp retort amplified the debate, with her post going viral on social media. Sundar's criticism reflects a broader political tussle, as opposition parties seek to corner the ruling DMK over the incident.

Meanwhile, the newly formed TVK (Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam) has also weighed in. The party's decision to support the police action, as discussed in The News Minute's podcast 'Powertrip', indicates a strategic move by the Vijay-led party to position itself as a champion of women's rights. This has added a new layer to Tamil Nadu's complex political dynamics, with multiple parties jostling for relevance ahead of upcoming elections.

Legal and procedural aspects

The questioning at Sengipatti police station, located in the minister's home district, was conducted under tight security. Sources indicate that Udhayanidhi was cooperative and answered all queries put to him. Legal experts note that the case could take various turns, depending on the evidence and the complaint's validity.

Police have not yet confirmed whether a case has been formally registered or if further questioning is anticipated. The minister's legal team has expressed confidence that the matter will be resolved in his favour, citing his clean record and the absence of malicious intent.

Broader implications

The incident has sparked a wider conversation about political discourse and the treatment of women in public life. Women's rights groups have called for greater accountability, while political analysts see this as a test for the DMK's handling of sensitive issues. The party, led by Chief Minister M.K. Stalin, has so far backed Udhayanidhi, but the pressure is mounting.

As the story unfolds, all eyes are on the legal proceedings and the political fallout. The coming days will reveal whether this controversy fades or escalates, potentially impacting the state's political landscape in the run-up to the 2026 assembly elections.

For now, Udhayanidhi Stalin is free, but the questions linger, and the political machinery in Tamil Nadu is already in motion.
