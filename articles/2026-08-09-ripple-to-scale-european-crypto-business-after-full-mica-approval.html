Ripple to Scale European Crypto Business After Full MiCA Approval

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ripple has obtained full approval under the EU's MiCA framework.
  • The license allows Ripple to offer crypto services across all EU member states.
  • Ripple plans to scale its European business operations following the approval.

Ripple is preparing to significantly expand its cryptocurrency operations across Europe after securing full approval under the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA). The company announced the regulatory milestone, which grants it a passport to offer services across all 27 EU member states.

A Single License for the Entire EU

Under MiCA, crypto firms that receive approval in one EU country can operate across the entire bloc without needing separate licenses in each member state. This so-called passporting right is central to the regulation's design.

Ripple's approval means it can now offer its payment and settlement solutions to institutional clients throughout Europe, marking a strategic shift from its earlier country-by-country approach. The company has not disclosed which national regulator granted the license or the exact date of approval.

This development comes as the EU's comprehensive crypto rulebook, which came into full effect in stages through 2025 and 2026, continues to reshape the industry. MiCA imposes strict requirements on issuers of stablecoins and crypto service providers, covering areas such as capital reserves, governance, and consumer protection.

What MiCA Approval Means for Ripple

The license allows Ripple to scale its ODL (On-Demand Liquidity) and cross-border payment solutions, which use its XRP token. Europe has long been a key market for the company, and the approval removes a major legal hurdle.

Ripple's leadership has previously emphasized that regulatory clarity is essential for institutional adoption. With MiCA in place, the firm can now approach European banks and financial institutions with a compliant, fully licensed offering.

Industry observers note that Ripple's move could pressure competitors to accelerate their own MiCA applications. The approval also signals that the EU's regulatory framework is attractive to major crypto players, despite concerns about its strictness.

European Market Potential

Europe represents one of the largest crypto markets in the world, with significant demand for cross-border payment efficiency. Ripple's technology is designed to reduce settlement times from days to seconds, a value proposition that resonates with European businesses.

The company has not yet specified which countries it will target first, though it already has a presence in key financial hubs like London and Zurich. With the MiCA passport, it can now expand more quickly across the continent.

Ripple's announcement follows a period of intense regulatory activity in the EU, as national authorities work to implement MiCA's provisions. The regulation is widely seen as a global benchmark, and its full application marks a turning point for crypto firms seeking to operate in Europe.

What Comes Next

Ripple is expected to announce specific product launches and partnerships in the coming months. The company's focus will likely be on deepening relationships with European banks and payment providers.

Observers will be watching how Ripple leverages its MiCA license to gain a competitive edge over rivals that are still navigating the approval process. The firm's success in Europe could also inform its strategy in other jurisdictions, including the United States, where crypto regulation remains fragmented.

For now, the approval provides Ripple with a solid foundation to build its European business, and the market will be watching for the first tangible signs of expansion.
