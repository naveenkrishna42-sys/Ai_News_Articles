Trump Claims US Has 'Total Control' of Hormuz, Calls Iran 'Bully of Middle East'

📅 2026-08-12 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says US has 'total control' of Hormuz, will keep it
  • Calls Iran 'bully of Middle East' amid rising tensions
  • Demands Tehran pay compensation for '50 years of damage'

In a sharply worded statement that is likely to escalate tensions in the Gulf, former US President Donald Trump has declared that the United States has 'total control' over the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. Speaking to reporters, Trump said, 'Praise be to Allah, I think we'll keep it,' a remark that has already drawn a mix of surprise and criticism.

The comments come at a time when diplomatic efforts to revive any understanding with Tehran appear to be faltering. Trump's language was blunt, describing Iran as the 'bully of the Middle East' and accusing the Islamic Republic of decades of disruptive behaviour in the region.

Compensation Demand Raises Eyebrows

Trump went a step further, suggesting that Iran should pay compensation for what he called '50 years of damage' caused by its policies. He did not specify the amount or the mechanism for such a claim, but the demand signals a hardening stance that could complicate any future negotiations.

This is not the first time Trump has floated the idea of seeking reparations from Iran. However, his latest remarks come amid reports that deal hopes are dimming, with both sides seemingly entrenched in their positions.

Strategic Implications for Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between Oman and Iran through which about a fifth of the world's oil passes. Any assertion of control by the US military is a serious matter, as it directly affects global energy markets and the security calculus of multiple nations, including India, which relies heavily on Gulf crude.

US officials have not yet clarified the operational meaning of 'total control', leaving room for ambiguity. Some analysts suggest the statement is more rhetorical than strategic, aimed at projecting strength ahead of domestic political timelines.

Regional Reactions and Concerns

Iran has yet to formally respond to Trump's latest comments, but its officials have previously warned that any disruption in the Strait would have severe consequences. The US has maintained a naval presence in the region for decades, but a unilateral claim of control could provoke a strong reaction from Tehran.

Meanwhile, Gulf nations are watching closely. Saudi Arabia and the UAE, both major oil exporters, rely on the strait for their shipments. Any instability there would hit their economies hard, and they have often urged Washington to avoid provocative rhetoric.

India, too, has a significant stake in the matter. New Delhi imports a large share of its oil from the Gulf, and any blockade or conflict in Hormuz would have immediate repercussions for its energy security and inflation levels.

Diplomatic channels remain open, but the gap between the US and Iran appears wide. Trump's demand for compensation, coupled with his dismissive characterisation of Tehran, suggests that a negotiated settlement is not imminent.

Observers note that the use of the phrase 'Praise be to Allah' by a former US president is unusual, and it may have been intended to signal a pragmatic, even conciliatory, approach to the region's Muslim-majority states. But the overall tone of the statement is unambiguously confrontational.

What happens next is unclear. The US administration has not issued an official policy document outlining its Hormuz strategy, and Iran's response could range from diplomatic protest to military posturing.

For now, the world waits to see if this is a negotiating tactic or a precursor to a more aggressive US posture in the Gulf.
