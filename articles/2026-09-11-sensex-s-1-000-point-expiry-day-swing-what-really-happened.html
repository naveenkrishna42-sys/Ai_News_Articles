Expiry Day Drama: Sensex's 1,000-Point Rollercoaster

On Thursday, 10 September 2026, the BSE Sensex staged a dramatic intraday recovery, surging over 1,000 points from its day's low before settling with a gain of just 138 points. The wild swing, typical of futures and options (F&O) expiry sessions, caught many traders off guard. The Nifty 50 mirrored the volatility, oscillating in a wide band before ending marginally higher. This session underscored the fragile sentiment prevailing on Dalal Street, where every rally is being sold into amid global uncertainties.

What Drove the Volatility?

Short Covering and Bargain Hunting

The initial surge was triggered by short covering in banking stocks, which had been beaten down over the previous three sessions. HDFC Bank and Axis Bank witnessed strong buying interest, contributing significantly to the index's recovery. Market participants also cited value buying at lower levels, as valuations in select large-caps became attractive after the recent correction.

Global Cues and Crude Oil

However, the rally fizzled out as elevated crude oil prices and lingering geopolitical tensions capped upside. Asian markets were mixed, and GIFT Nifty signaled a cautious start earlier in the day. The expiry-day squaring of positions added to the chaos, with traders scrambling to adjust their derivatives exposure.

What This Means for Retail Investors

Expiry-day volatility is a double-edged sword. While it offers profit opportunities for savvy traders, it can also lead to significant losses for the unprepared. Retail investors should avoid chasing intraday spikes and instead focus on positional trades with strict stop-losses. The narrow closing gain despite a massive swing indicates that the market lacks conviction at higher levels. Until global headwinds subside, expect continued choppiness.

Pros and Cons of Expiry-Day Trading

  • Pros: High liquidity, quick profit potential, and opportunities to hedge existing positions.
  • Cons: Extreme volatility, risk of slippage, and emotional decision-making that can erode capital.
  • Key Insight: Expiry-day moves often reverse in the following session; avoid overnight leveraged positions.

Outlook: Caution Prevails

With crude oil prices showing no signs of cooling off and geopolitical tensions simmering, the near-term outlook remains cautious. The market's inability to hold onto a 1,000-point rally suggests that bears are still in control. Investors should monitor global cues, FII flows, and currency movements before taking fresh positions. For now, sticking to quality large-caps and maintaining adequate cash levels appears prudent.
