West Bengal SIR: 37 Lakh Appeals Pending as Municipal Polls Near

The Election Commission of India (ECI) has told the Supreme Court that more than 37 lakh appeals arising from the West Bengal Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls remain pending before appellate tribunals. Only around 1.02 lakh appeals have been heard, and fewer than 3% have been disposed of. With municipal elections approaching, the backlog raises direct questions about who can vote and on what basis.

The data comes from an ECI affidavit filed in ongoing proceedings, as reported by The Hindu, LiveLaw, Telegraph India and Scroll.in. The scale of pendency is unusual for any state election cycle, and it is already shaping political and legal narratives in West Bengal.

What the Numbers Reveal

The SIR process required voters to re-establish eligibility through documentary proof. Those excluded or flagged could appeal before appellate tribunals. The ECI's own submission shows that the appellate machinery has processed only a fraction of the caseload.

According to the affidavit, less than 3% of appeals have been decided. A separate report by Scroll.in places the figure at 2.6%. The Indian Express has noted that the ECI affidavit does not provide a break-up of appeals seeking inclusion versus those challenging exclusion, which makes it harder to assess the exact impact on the final voter list.

Why This Matters for Municipal Polls

Municipal elections are fought on local wards, where margins can be narrow. A pending appeal means a voter's name may not appear on the final roll, or may appear with unresolved objections. If tribunals do not clear the backlog before notification, thousands of eligible voters could be left out of the electoral process.

The ECI has not publicly announced a revised timeline for disposing of the remaining appeals. Political parties across the spectrum have used the pendency to question the roll revision's fairness. The Supreme Court is monitoring the matter, which adds a layer of judicial oversight to the election preparation.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • 37 lakh+ appeals is a massive administrative backlog for any state election authority.
  • Less than 3% disposal rate indicates tribunals are severely under-resourced or procedurally stalled.
  • No inclusion/exclusion break-up in the ECI affidavit limits independent verification of voter impact.
  • Municipal polls could proceed with a voter list that is still legally contested.
  • Supreme Court scrutiny means the ECI may face pressure to file updated status reports.

What Buyers and Citizens Should Watch

For ordinary voters, the practical takeaway is to verify their name on the electoral roll well before municipal poll notification. Those with pending appeals should track their tribunal case number and keep documentary proof ready. For political parties and candidates, the pending list is a live database of potentially disenfranchised voters, and ward-level outreach may need to account for it.

The ECI has not indicated whether it will seek additional tribunal benches or extend deadlines. Until a clear disposal schedule is public, the 37 lakh figure remains the single biggest administrative risk hanging over the West Bengal municipal elections.
