Thomas Müller disgusted by England's surrender to Argentina in World Cup semi-final

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Thomas Müller expressed disgust at England's performance in the World Cup semi-final loss to Argentina.
  • Gary Neville questioned Thomas Tuchel's 'English DNA' claims after the 2-1 defeat.
  • England had Messi under control until a tactical shift turned the game.
  • Müller's comments add to growing criticism of England's approach.

Müller's Blunt Verdict

Thomas Müller did not hold back. The German veteran, watching from afar, said he was left disgusted by how England surrendered to Argentina in the World Cup semi-final. His words, reported by Bavarian Football Works, cut deep.

England lost 2-1. But for Müller, the scoreline was not the issue. It was the manner of the defeat. He saw a team that had control, then let it slip without a fight.

Neville Takes Aim at Tuchel

Gary Neville joined the chorus of criticism. The former England defender took issue with Thomas Tuchel's pre-match claims about 'English DNA'. Speaking on Sky Sports, Neville questioned what that DNA meant when the team crumbled under pressure.

Tuchel had built a system around discipline and structure. But against Argentina, that structure collapsed. Neville argued that talking about identity is pointless if it vanishes when it matters most.

The Messi Factor

England had Lionel Messi under control for most of the game. That is what makes the loss harder to digest. According to an analysis by The Guardian, a single subtle shift from Argentina turned the match.

Messi drifted into spaces he had not occupied earlier. England's marking system, rigid until then, could not adjust. The decisive goal came from that moment of tactical flexibility.

Messi's numbers from the tournament are staggering. Stats from ESPN show he outperformed Mbappé 33-25, Pelé 10-4, and Maradona 99-71 in various metrics. But on the night, it was not just about stats. It was about a team that bent, then broke.

Tuchel's Horror Show

The Times of India described Tuchel's performance as 'the greatest English horror'. The German coach, hired to bring a ruthless edge, instead oversaw a collapse. His substitutions were too late. His tactics were too passive.

England had chances. But they lacked the killer instinct that Argentina showed. Müller's disgust reflects a wider sentiment: England did not lose to a better team. They lost because they stopped playing.

What Comes Next

Tuchel's position is under scrutiny. The English FA has not made any statements about his future. But the noise is growing. Müller's comments, Neville's critique, and the media's verdict all point to a team that needs a reset.

For now, Argentina march on. England are left to wonder what might have been. The next World Cup cycle begins soon. Whether Tuchel will lead it remains unclear.
