Delhi HC Refuses to Transfer Sonam Wangchuk to Medanta, Activist Sets Conditions to End Fast

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi High Court refuses to direct transfer of Sonam Wangchuk to Medanta hospital
  • Court says hospitalisation was to save his life, not confine him
  • Sonam Wangchuk sets three conditions to end his hunger strike
  • CPI(M) stages protest against activist's forceful removal during hunger strike
  • Wangchuk says he will continue fast until government fixes accountability

Court Refuses Transfer to Medanta

The Delhi High Court on Monday declined to order the transfer of climate activist Sonam Wangchuk to Medanta hospital. The court observed that his hospitalisation was meant to save his life, not confine him.

The appeal was filed against the refusal of authorities to shift Wangchuk from the government hospital where he is currently admitted. The activist has been on a hunger strike, demanding accountability from the government on climate action.

Activist Sets Three Conditions

Sonam Wangchuk has announced three conditions to break his fast. Speaking to reporters, he said he will continue his hunger strike until the government fixes accountability on the issue.

The conditions reportedly include a clear government commitment on climate policy and action. Wangchuk has been adamant that his protest will not end without concrete steps from the authorities.

Political Protests Erupt

The CPI(M) staged a protest against what it called the forceful removal of Sonam Wangchuk during his hunger strike. Party workers gathered outside the hospital, demanding his immediate transfer to a private facility of his choice.

The protestors accused the state of trying to break the activist's resolve through administrative measures. The situation remains tense as supporters continue to gather in solidarity.

Legal Background

Legal experts point out that hunger strikes occupy a grey area in Indian law. While the right to protest is constitutionally protected, the state's duty to preserve life often leads to interventions like forced hospitalisation.

The Delhi High Court's observation that hospitalisation was lifesaving, not punitive, reflects this delicate balance. The court has not yet issued a final order on the broader petition.

What Happens Next

The case is likely to be heard again as Wangchuk's legal team prepares to challenge the refusal order. With the activist refusing to eat until his demands are met, the medical team continues to monitor his vitals closely.

All eyes are now on the government's response to the three conditions. A breakthrough in the standoff could come if the authorities signal willingness to engage on climate accountability.
