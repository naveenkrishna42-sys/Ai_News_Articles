Rs 15.56 crore: The money behind the medal at 2026 Commonwealth Games

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rs 15.56 crore per athlete allocated for 2026 Commonwealth Games preparation
  • Funds cover training, foreign exposure, equipment, and support staff
  • Sports ministry aims to boost medal tally with targeted investment

Big spend for big dreams

The Sports Ministry has earmarked Rs 15.56 crore for each Indian athlete preparing for the 2026 Commonwealth Games. The figure, revealed in a recent parliamentary response, underscores the government's push to improve the country's medal haul at the quadrennial event.

The allocation covers a wide range of expenses. Training camps, foreign exposure trips, high-performance equipment, and specialized coaching all fall under this umbrella. The ministry believes this level of per-athlete investment is necessary to compete with traditional powerhouses like Australia, England, and Canada.

Where the money goes

A significant chunk of the funds is directed towards foreign training stints. Athletes in disciplines like athletics, boxing, and shooting often need exposure to international competition to sharpen their skills. The ministry has also increased spending on sports science, including physiotherapy, nutrition, and mental conditioning.

Support staff, including personal coaches and analysts, account for a large portion of the budget. The idea is to create a professional ecosystem around each athlete, similar to what top global sports nations offer. Officials argue that such investments have already shown results in recent international events.

Targeting a record haul

India's performance at the 2022 Birmingham Games—where the country finished seventh with 61 medals—has set a benchmark. The new funding model aims to surpass that tally in 2026, with a particular focus on gold medals. Sports administrators have identified athletics and wrestling as high-priority disciplines.

The preparation cycle began shortly after the last Games concluded. Athletes are being tracked through a centralized monitoring system, with periodic assessments to ensure that the money is translating into performance. The ministry has also tied funding to results, with incentives for those who meet specific milestones.

Concerns and criticism

Not everyone is convinced that high spending guarantees medals. Critics point to past instances where large budgets failed to yield expected outcomes. They argue that grassroots development and infrastructure at the district level need equal attention.

There are also questions about transparency. While the per-athlete figure is clear, how the funds are disbursed and monitored remains opaque. The ministry says it has a robust audit mechanism in place, but independent observers have called for more public scrutiny.

Despite these concerns, the government remains bullish. The 2026 Games, scheduled to be held in Glasgow, will feature a streamlined programme with fewer sports. That could work in India's favour, as resources will be concentrated on a smaller set of disciplines.

What happens next

With two years to go, the focus is on selection trials and early training camps. The ministry is expected to release a detailed list of funded athletes and their specific plans in the coming months. The effectiveness of this Rs 15.56 crore investment will ultimately be measured on the podium in Glasgow.
