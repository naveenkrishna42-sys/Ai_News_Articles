Sensex, Nifty Trade Flat as Oil Drops Below $90 on US-Iran Tensions

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty remained range-bound during early trade on Tuesday
  • Brent crude slipped below the $90 mark for the first time in weeks
  • Markets weighed falling oil prices against geopolitical uncertainty from US-Iran tensions

Markers Open Flat Amid Global Crosscurrents

Indian equity benchmarks opened on a muted note Tuesday, with the Sensex and Nifty trading in a narrow range as investors juggled falling crude oil prices against heightened geopolitical risks. The BSE Sensex hovered near its previous close, while the NSE Nifty 50 stayed flat in early deals, reflecting cautious sentiment across sectors.

Traders pointed to a lack of clear directional triggers, with gains from lower input costs offset by worries over the widening US-Iran conflict. Volatility remained subdued compared to recent sessions, suggesting participants were waiting for more clarity.

Oil Prices Slide Below $90 a Barrel

Brent crude futures slipped below the psychological $90 mark for the first time in several weeks, dropping sharply amid reports of de-escalation efforts in West Asia. The decline in oil prices, typically a positive for net-importing nations like India, failed to ignite a broad rally as investors questioned how long the dip would last.

Analysts noted that while lower crude reduces India's import bill and inflation pressures, it also signals weakening global demand. "The oil drop is a double-edged sword," one market observer said. "It helps our fiscal math but also mirrors the uncertainty around the conflict."

Sectoral Moves and Broader Market

Most sectoral indices struggled for direction. Banking and auto stocks saw mild buying interest on hopes of lower fuel costs, while oil marketing companies such as HPCL, BPCL, and IOC gained as their margins got a breather. On the flip side, metal and IT shares faced selling pressure.

The broader market mirrored the benchmarks, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices trading flat. Advancing and declining stocks were nearly evenly matched on the BSE, indicating a tug-of-war between bulls and bears.

Geopolitical Shadow Lingers

The US-Iran standoff remained a key overhang, keeping risk appetite in check. While no fresh escalation was reported overnight, diplomatic channels remained active. Markets are now pricing in the possibility of a prolonged period of tension, which could keep oil prices volatile.

Foreign portfolio investors have been cautious in recent sessions, though domestic institutional buying has provided some support. The rupee too held steady against the dollar, reflecting the absence of panic.

What to watch: All eyes will be on any fresh diplomatic breakthrough or military development in West Asia. Crude price trajectory and FII flows will dictate near-term direction for Dalal Street.
