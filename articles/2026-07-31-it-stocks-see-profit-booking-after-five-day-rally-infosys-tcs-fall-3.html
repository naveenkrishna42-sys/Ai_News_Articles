IT stocks see profit booking after five-day rally; Infosys, TCS fall 3%

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Infosys and TCS fell up to 3% on Friday amid profit booking after a five-day rally.
  • The decline comes as AI optimism returns to global markets, though Indian IT stocks had surged earlier.
  • Nifty IT gained 21% in July, but analysts warn of volatility and sector rotation.

Profit booking hits IT sector after strong run

Indian IT stocks witnessed a sharp pullback on Friday as investors chose to lock in gains following a five-day rally. Infosys and Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) fell up to 3% in early trade, dragging the sector lower after a period of sustained buying.

The profit booking comes at a time when global sentiment towards artificial intelligence has turned more optimistic, a factor that had earlier propelled the sector to record highs.

AI optimism vs. market reality

The recent rally in Indian IT stocks was largely driven by renewed interest in AI-related opportunities, even as global AI stocks have seen volatility. The Nifty IT index surged 21% in July, outperforming the broader market, with the Sensex and Nifty gaining around 2% during the same period.

However, Friday's decline suggests that the market is reassessing valuations after such a sharp run-up. Analysts point out that while the long-term story for IT services remains intact, short-term corrections are inevitable after steep gains.

What's behind the pullback?

Several factors are at play. First, the rapid rise in stock prices has made valuations less attractive, prompting institutional investors to trim positions. Second, the divergence between Indian IT stocks and their global counterparts — where AI stocks have plunged — is creating uncertainty about the sustainability of the rally.

Third, broader market data shows that despite the headline indices gaining, a significant portion of BSE 500 stocks have underperformed. This suggests that the rally has been narrow, concentrated in a few large-cap names, making the sector more susceptible to sharp reversals.

Fund performance and investor sentiment

The surge in IT stocks has also benefited technology-focused mutual funds. Seven technology funds with over 50% allocation to the sector have delivered strong returns in July, mirroring the Nifty IT's gains. However, Friday's dip serves as a reminder that such concentrated bets carry higher risk.

Investors are now watching whether the profit booking will extend into the next week or if the dip will attract fresh buying at lower levels. The coming days will likely see increased volatility as traders adjust positions ahead of monthly derivatives expiry.

What to watch next

Market participants will closely monitor global cues, especially moves in AI-linked technology stocks in the US, for direction. Any sustained weakness in global tech could put further pressure on Indian IT names, while a stabilisation could trigger a rebound.

For now, the consensus is that the fundamental demand for digital transformation and AI services remains strong, but the path forward could be bumpier than the smooth rally seen in July.
