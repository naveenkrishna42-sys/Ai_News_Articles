India's Semiconductor Aspirations: A Progress Assessment

Ashwini Vaishnaw's progress report marks a pivotal moment in India's strategic effort to position itself as a global semiconductor manufacturing hub. The initiative is anchored by substantial policy frameworks and increasing international attention, reflecting a deliberate national commitment to technological self-reliance.



Policy Architecture and Infrastructure Build-Outs

The government's Semiconductors Mission combines massive capital outlays with integrated skill development centers across multiple regions. These combined efforts support both fab construction and the cultivation of a specialized workforce capable of managing advanced process technologies.



Supply Chain Security Amid Geopolitical Pressures

Global debates concerning supply chain weaponization have amplified India's case for becoming a trusted destination. The Prime Minister's recent framing underscores reliability and ethical sourcing as central pillars rather than simply emphasizing production volumes.



Market Dynamics and Buyer Implications

For procurement professionals, several interlocking factors determine the commercial viability of Indian semiconductor solutions. Timing aligns closely with planned infrastructure completions, cost competitiveness stems from reduced logistical burdens, and partnership access is facilitated through state-funded collaborative schemes.



Key Strategic Initiatives

1. National Integrated Multifunctional Monetisation Development Platform (NIMMDP)

This pillar focuses on building indigenous design capability through targeted investment incentives that lower risks for small-to-medium enterprises entering the space.


2. Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme

The scheme directly targets wafer fabrication capacity

Production Linked Incentive (PLI) Scheme – Deep Dive

The scheme directly targets wafer fabrication capacity by offering financial incentives linked to incremental sales of domestically produced semiconductors. Under the PLI framework, eligible firms receive a cash grant of up to 50 % of their eligible capital expenditure for setting up new fabs or expanding existing lines, subject to achieving predefined production milestones.

  • Eligibility: Companies with a minimum investment of ₹ 5,000 crore in semiconductor manufacturing.
  • Incentive structure: 4‑5 % of net sales over five years, capped at ₹ 12,000 crore per applicant.
  • Technology focus: Nodes ranging from 28 nm to 5 nm, with special encouragement for advanced packaging and SiP (System‑in‑Package) solutions.
  • Monitoring: Quarterly reporting to the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) with third‑party audits.

Other Strategic Initiatives

3. Design Linked Incentive (DLI) Scheme

Complementing the PLI, the DLI scheme supports fabless design houses by providing up to 50 % reimbursement of qualifying design‑related expenses, including EDA tool licences, IP procurement, and tape‑out costs. The goal is to nurture a pipeline of indigenous IP that can be fed into domestic fabs.

4. Semiconductor Skill Development Mission (SSDM)

SSDM establishes regional centres of excellence in partnership with premier engineering institutes (IITs, NITs) and industry consortia. Curricula cover process integration, lithography, metrology, and reliability testing, aiming to certify over 1  lakh skilled technicians by 2027.

5. International Collaboration Framework

India has signed memoranda of understanding with leading semiconductor hubs in Taiwan, South Korea, the United States, and the European Union. These agreements facilitate technology transfer, joint R&D projects, and mutual recognition of standards, enhancing India’s credibility as a trusted supplier.

Takeaways

  • India’s semiconductor strategy combines fiscal incentives (PLI, DLI) with human‑capital development (SSDM) to create a self‑sustaining ecosystem.
  • Early‑node fabs (28 nm–14 nm) are projected to achieve cost parity or modest advantage versus established players, making them attractive for cost‑sensitive applications such as automotive, IoT, and power management.
  • Advanced nodes (7 nm–5 nm) will require continued reliance on foreign equipment and expertise, but the PLI scheme’s milestone‑linked payouts reduce financial risk for investors.
  • Supply‑chain positioning—emphasizing reliability, ethical sourcing, and regional diversification—adds a non‑price value proposition that can command premium contracts with multinational OEMs.
  • Successful execution hinges on timely infrastructure completion, stable policy environment, and sustained skill‑pipeline output; delays in any of these areas could erode the projected cost benefits.
