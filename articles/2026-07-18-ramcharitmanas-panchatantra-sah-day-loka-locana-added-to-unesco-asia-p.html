Ramcharitmanas, Panchatantra, Sahṛdayāloka-Locana added to UNESCO Asia-Pacific register

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Three Indian literary works—Ramcharitmanas, Panchatantra, and Sahṛdayāloka-Locana—added to UNESCO's Memory of the World Asia-Pacific Regional Register.
  • The register recognizes documentary heritage of regional significance across Asia-Pacific.
  • Inclusion highlights India's rich literary and philosophical traditions for global archival preservation.

The Ramcharitmanas, the Panchatantra, and the Sahṛdayāloka-Locana have been officially inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Asia-Pacific Regional Register. The decision was announced by the Press Information Bureau on 13 May 2024.

The register recognises documentary heritage of outstanding significance to the Asia-Pacific region. This inclusion places three pillars of Indian literature alongside other culturally vital works from across the continent.

What the works represent

The Ramcharitmanas, composed by Goswami Tulsidas in the 16th century, is a retelling of the Ramayana in Awadhi. It remains a cornerstone of devotional literature in northern India, widely recited and performed.

The Panchatantra is an ancient collection of animal fables, believed to have been compiled around 200 BCE by Vishnu Sharma. It has influenced storytelling traditions from Persia to Europe.

The Sahṛdayāloka-Locana, written by the 10th-century Kashmiri scholar Anandavardhana, is a seminal work on Sanskrit poetics. It explores the theory of dhvani, or suggestion, in literature.

Why this matters

UNESCO's Memory of the World programme aims to preserve and promote documentary heritage. The Asia-Pacific Regional Register is one of several regional lists under this programme.

Inclusion on the register does not come with funding but provides international recognition. It also encourages preservation efforts by national archives and libraries.

India now has several entries on the register, including the Rigveda manuscripts and the collected works of Rabindranath Tagore. The latest additions reinforce the depth of India's textual traditions.

What happens next

UNESCO will review the nominations for the global Memory of the World Register at a later stage. For now, the three works gain visibility among scholars and archivists worldwide.

The recognition also puts pressure on Indian institutions to digitise and protect original manuscripts. Many copies of the Ramcharitmanas and Panchatantra exist in fragile condition across libraries in Varanasi, Jaipur, and other cities.

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for digitisation or public access. But cultural experts say the UNESCO listing will help prioritise such efforts.

The next cycle of nominations for the Asia-Pacific register will open in 2025. India is expected to submit more works, including possibly the Vedas and early Buddhist texts.
