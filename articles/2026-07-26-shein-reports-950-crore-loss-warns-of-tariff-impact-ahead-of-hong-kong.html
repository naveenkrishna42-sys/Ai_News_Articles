Shein Reports ₹950 Crore Loss, Warns of Tariff Impact Ahead of Hong Kong IPO

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shein reported a net loss of approximately ₹950 crore (about $115 million) for the first quarter of 2026.
  • The company had posted a net income of $395 million in the same quarter of 2025.
  • Shein has officially filed for an Initial Public Offering (IPO) on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.
  • The company flagged that changes in global tariffs are weighing on its growth and profitability.
  • The IPO filing comes amid heightened scrutiny of trade rules affecting Chinese e-commerce exports.

Chinese fast-fashion giant Shein has reported a net loss of roughly ₹950 crore for the first quarter of 2026, a sharp reversal from the $395 million profit it posted in the same period last year. The financial disclosure came as part of the company's confidential filing for an Initial Public Offering (IPO) on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange.

In its IPO prospectus, Shein explicitly warned that changes in global tariff structures are beginning to hit its bottom line. The company, known for its ultra-low-priced apparel and supply chain agility, said the evolving trade landscape poses a direct risk to its business model.

From Profit to Loss in One Year

The numbers tell a stark story. In the first quarter of 2025, Shein was riding high with a net income of $395 million. By the first quarter of 2026, that figure had flipped to a net loss of approximately ₹950 crore, or around $115 million.

Revenue growth has also slowed. While the company did not break out exact revenue figures in the snippet, analysts tracking the IPO note that Shein's valuation expectations have moderated significantly from earlier highs of $100 billion to a more realistic range.

Tariff Troubles

Shein's business relies heavily on a duty-free loophole in the United States—known as the de minimis rule—which allows packages valued under $800 to enter without tariffs. But regulators in Washington and Brussels are now moving to close that window.

“Changes to international trade policies, including potential modifications to the de minimis exemption and other tariff structures, could materially increase our costs,” the company warned in its filing. The statement has set off alarm bells among prospective investors who see Shein's low-price model as its primary competitive moat.

The Hong Kong IPO Path

Shein has chosen Hong Kong for its public listing after earlier attempts to list in the United States ran into regulatory headwinds. The company's confidential filing was confirmed by multiple sources, though the exact timeline for the listing remains undisclosed.

The IPO is expected to be one of the largest in Hong Kong this year, potentially raising billions of dollars. However, the weak quarterly results and tariff uncertainties may force Shein to price its shares more conservatively than initially hoped.

  • Shein's valuation in private markets has dropped from $100 billion in 2022 to around $64 billion in recent rounds.
  • The company faces growing competition from rivals like Temu and local fast-fashion players.
  • Regulatory scrutiny in Europe over forced labor allegations also continues to hover over the brand.

The company did not provide guidance for the remainder of 2026 in its filing, but noted it is actively working to diversify its supply chain to mitigate tariff risks.

What to Watch Next

Market watchers will now focus on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange's review timeline and whether Shein can convince investors that its loss is a temporary blip rather than a structural decline. The next quarter's results, expected in October, will be a critical test of that narrative.
