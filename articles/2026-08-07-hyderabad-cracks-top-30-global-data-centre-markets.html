Hyderabad cracks top 30 global data centre markets

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Hyderabad now ranks among the top 30 data centre markets globally
  • Growth attributed to digital demand, connectivity and state incentives
  • City competes with established hubs like Mumbai and Chennai

A New Global Milestone

Hyderabad has broken into the top 30 global data centre markets, a significant leap for the city that has rapidly transformed into a digital infrastructure hub. The ranking, based on industry assessments of capacity, connectivity and investment momentum, places the Telangana capital alongside established global players.

The development reflects the city's growing appeal to cloud service providers, enterprises and hyperscalers. Industry experts point to a combination of factors — from reliable power supply and robust fibre networks to a business-friendly policy environment — that have made Hyderabad a preferred destination for large-scale data centre projects.

What Drove the Surge

The city's data centre capacity has grown steadily over the past few years, with several global and domestic players setting up facilities in and around the metropolitan region. Availability of large land parcels at competitive prices, compared to Mumbai or Chennai, has been a key advantage.

State government initiatives, including dedicated IT and investment policies, have streamlined clearances and offered incentives for infrastructure projects. The presence of a skilled workforce, drawn from the city's strong engineering and tech talent pool, has further strengthened its case among global investors.

Connectivity has also played a pivotal role. Hyderabad's position as a major internet gateway, with multiple submarine cable landing stations nearby and extensive terrestrial fibre links, ensures low-latency access to both domestic and international markets.

Competing With the Big Guns

While Mumbai remains India's largest data centre market, Hyderabad's rise signals a broader shift in the country's digital geography. Chennai and Delhi-NCR continue to attract significant investment, but secondary hubs are now emerging as viable alternatives for operators looking to diversify risk and optimise costs.

Industry analysts note that the COVID-19 pandemic accelerated cloud adoption across sectors, from banking to healthcare, which in turn fuelled demand for data storage and processing. Hyderabad, with its strong IT services ecosystem, has been a direct beneficiary of this surge.

Power availability and sustainability have become critical factors in site selection, and Hyderabad has scored well on both fronts. The city's utilities have managed to keep outages minimal, while several new facilities are being designed with renewable energy integration and advanced cooling technologies.

What This Means for the City

For Hyderabad, the top-30 ranking is more than a status symbol. It is likely to attract further investment in ancillary sectors such as cooling equipment manufacturing, security services and network infrastructure. Real estate developers are also eyeing the corridor around the city for specialised industrial parks.

The economic impact is expected to be substantial, with data centres generating employment both during construction and in ongoing operations. Local authorities have indicated that more projects are in the pipeline, though specific timelines have not been disclosed.

There are challenges too. Data centres are power-hungry, and the city will need to expand its renewable energy capacity to meet sustainability targets. Water usage for cooling remains a concern, and operators are exploring recycled water and air-based cooling solutions to address it.

Looking Ahead

The global data centre market is becoming increasingly competitive, with cities across Asia, the Middle East and Europe vying for the same pool of investment. Hyderabad will need to keep upgrading its infrastructure and policy framework to hold onto its position.

Industry observers expect the city to announce several new projects over the next year, potentially pushing it higher up the global rankings. The coming months will show whether Hyderabad can convert this milestone into sustained long-term growth.
