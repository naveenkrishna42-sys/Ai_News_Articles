LIC OFS sees 3.32x bids on Day 1; retail window opens Wednesday

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • LIC OFS subscribed 3.32 times on first day of bidding
  • Government to exercise greenshoe option to sell additional stake
  • Retail investors can bid from Wednesday
  • Offer price fixed at ₹382 per share, a 10% discount to market price

Strong start for LIC offer for sale

The government's offer for sale (OFS) in Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC) got off to a robust start, with the issue subscribed 3.32 times on the first day of bidding. The strong demand comes even as the government priced the shares at a 10% discount to the prevailing market price.

The OFS, which opened on Tuesday for institutional investors, will see the government sell up to 6.5% of its stake in the country's largest insurer. At the offer price of ₹382 per share, the sale is expected to fetch around ₹31,000 crore, adding a significant sum to the government's disinvestment kitty.

Greenshoe option to be exercised

With demand exceeding the base issue size, the government has decided to exercise the greenshoe option, which allows it to sell additional shares from its holding. This means the final quantum of shares sold could be higher than the initial 6.5% stake offered.

The greenshoe mechanism is typically used in OFS to stabilise the share price and meet excess demand. The decision to exercise it reflects the strong appetite among institutional buyers for LIC shares, despite the discount pricing.

Retail investors get their chance

Retail investors will be able to bid for LIC shares in the OFS starting Wednesday. The retail portion is usually kept separate in such offerings, and the government has reserved a portion of the issue for individual investors.

Analysts say the 10% discount could make the offer attractive for retail buyers, especially those who missed out on the LIC initial public offering (IPO) in 2022. The stock has been volatile since listing, and the government's decision to offload a larger stake at a discount has put some pressure on the share price.

Market reaction and portfolio shift

LIC shares fell around 8% on Tuesday as the OFS opened, reflecting the discount pricing and the supply overhang from the government's sale. Despite the dip, the insurer's long-term investment strategy has been evolving, with its portfolio shifting decisively away from traditional loans towards market-linked instruments.

This shift is part of a broader strategy to improve returns and align with changing market dynamics. The government's stake sale, however, remains the immediate focus for investors tracking the stock.

What to watch

The final subscription numbers for the retail portion will be closely watched, as will the overall demand for the OFS. If the retail segment is fully subscribed, the government may complete the sale by the end of the week.

The success of this OFS could also set the tone for future divestment plans, as the government looks to meet its disinvestment targets for the fiscal year. Investors will be keeping an eye on how LIC's stock performs once the OFS concludes.
