Second H5 bird flu case confirmed in New Zealand as testing widens

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Second H5 bird flu case detected in New Zealand, this time in a native bird.
  • Surveillance efforts have been expanded following the initial case.
  • Australia's H5N1 detections have increased to 17.
  • Tourism businesses are preparing for potential disruptions from the virus spread.

New Zealand confirms second H5 bird flu case

New Zealand has reported its second case of H5 bird flu, this time detected in a native bird. The confirmation comes as the country expands its surveillance efforts following the initial detection earlier this month.

The infected bird was found as part of increased testing protocols now in place. Officials have not yet disclosed the specific species or exact location of the latest case. The development signals that the virus may be more widespread than initially thought.

Surveillance ramped up nationwide

Biosecurity authorities have widened testing across multiple regions. The goal is to track any further spread among wild bird populations. Domestic poultry farms are also being closely monitored for signs of infection.

New Zealand had previously been free of H5 bird flu, making these back-to-back detections significant. The country's isolation and strict biosecurity measures had kept it safe from the global outbreak that has affected other nations. This new case challenges that status.

Australia's situation worsens

Across the Tasman Sea, Australia has reported a sharp rise in H5N1 detections, now standing at 17 confirmed cases. The increase has put Australian authorities on high alert. Several states have implemented movement restrictions on poultry and wild bird handling.

The Australian outbreak has been linked to migrating birds. This pattern raises concerns for New Zealand, which shares a similar flyway for migratory species. Experts warn that more cases are likely as the southern hemisphere winter sets in.

Tourism sector on edge

New Zealand's tourism industry, a major economic driver, is now bracing for potential fallout. Tour operators, especially those offering wildlife and bird-watching experiences, are preparing for cancellations. The sector is still recovering from the pandemic-era disruptions.

Some operators have already reported a dip in forward bookings. Industry bodies are in talks with the government about contingency plans. A full-blown outbreak could lead to park closures and travel advisories, hitting regional economies hard.

Health authorities say the risk to humans remains low. No human cases have been reported in either country. Still, officials are not taking chances. Protective gear for culling teams and antiviral stockpiles are being reviewed.

What to watch next

All eyes are on the next round of test results from both New Zealand and Australia. The coming weeks will show whether the virus is contained or continues to spread. The tourism sector's contingency plans may soon become operational if more cases emerge.
