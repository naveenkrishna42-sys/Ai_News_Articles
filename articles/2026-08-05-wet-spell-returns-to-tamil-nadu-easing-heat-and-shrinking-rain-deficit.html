Wet spell returns to Tamil Nadu, easing heat and shrinking rain deficit

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Widespread rains in Tamil Nadu bring down temperatures and ease humid conditions.
  • The wet spell has helped shrink the state's seasonal rain deficit.
  • Farmers and water managers see the rainfall as a boost for crops and reservoirs.

After weeks of sweltering heat and parched fields, Tamil Nadu is witnessing a welcome return of the monsoon. A fresh wet spell has swept across many parts of the state, bringing down temperatures and offering relief to residents and farmers alike. The rains have also helped narrow the seasonal rain deficit, which had been a growing concern for water managers and agricultural planners.

Rainfall spreads across districts

Over the past few days, several districts in Tamil Nadu have recorded moderate to heavy rainfall. The downpour has been particularly notable in the delta and coastal regions, where the skies have remained overcast for extended periods. The India Meteorological Department has attributed the wet spell to a combination of atmospheric conditions, including a low-pressure area that has formed over the Bay of Bengal.

Residents in Chennai and other urban centres have welcomed the break from the oppressive heat, with temperatures dropping by several notches. The city's roads, however, have seen waterlogging in low-lying areas, a familiar sight during such spells. Civic authorities have deployed pumps and teams to clear blocked drains, though some commuters faced delays during peak hours.

Rain deficit narrows

The seasonal rain deficit, which had widened earlier in the monsoon season, has now shrunk considerably. According to data from the regional meteorological office, the cumulative rainfall for the state has moved closer to the long-period average. This is a significant turnaround, as the deficit had touched worrying levels just a fortnight ago.

Water managers in the state are keeping a close watch on reservoir levels. Several major dams, including those in the Cauvery basin, have seen an uptick in inflows. While the storage is still below full capacity, the recent rains have provided a much-needed cushion for drinking water and irrigation needs in the coming months.

Farmers welcome the showers

For the farming community, the wet spell has come as a timely boost. Samba paddy nurseries, which were struggling due to dry conditions, are now expected to recover. Groundnut and sugarcane crops, too, stand to benefit from the increased soil moisture. Agriculture officials say that the rains could help reduce the dependence on groundwater pumping, which had been depleting aquifers in several districts.

However, the rains have also brought some challenges. In a few districts, standing crops have been damaged due to strong winds and waterlogging. Extension officers have advised farmers to drain excess water from fields and delay fertiliser application until the soil dries slightly. The state agriculture department is assessing the extent of any crop loss, though officials have not yet confirmed specific figures.

What lies ahead

Forecasters suggest that the wet spell could continue for a few more days, with scattered showers expected across the state. The regional meteorological centre has issued a yellow alert for a few districts, cautioning about possible heavy rainfall in isolated pockets. Fishermen have been advised to avoid venturing into the sea until conditions improve.

As the monsoon season progresses, the focus will now shift to how the remaining rains pan out. A healthy finish to the season could not only erase the deficit but also set the stage for a robust rabi campaign. For now, the state can breathe a little easier, with the skies having opened up just when they were needed most.
