Overview

Meghna Gulzar’s Daayra enters the crowded legal‑thriller space with a premise that fuses courtroom procedure and vigilante justice. Kareena Kapoor plays a tenacious lawyer driven by personal loss, while Prithviraj Sukumaran portrays a conflicted enforcer whose actions blur the line between law and retribution. Set against a contemporary urban backdrop, the film interrogates how systemic failures can push individuals toward extrajudicial measures.

Performances

Kareena Kapoor’s Portrayal

Kapoor steps away from her usual glamorous image to deliver a restrained, internally layered performance. Her courtroom scenes are marked by precise diction, subtle facial shifts, and a palpable sense of urgency that conveys both vulnerability and steely resolve. Critics have noted that this role allows her to showcase a depth rarely seen in her recent work.

Prithviraj Sukumaran’s Role

Sukumaran matches Kapoor’s intensity with a physically grounded portrayal of a man torn between duty and personal vendetta. His body language—tight jaw, measured steps—communicates an internal struggle that fuels the film’s tension. The actor’s ability to shift from calm deliberation to explosive action adds credibility to the vigilante angle.

Conclusion

The film demonstrates that cinema can serve as both a mirror to societal failings and a catalyst for reflection. While Meghna Gulzar's direction elevates Daayra beyond conventional thriller tropes, the narrative risks over-intellectualizing its central question: when does justice become vengeance? Despite these concerns, the performances remain compelling and the cinematography noteworthy, making Daayra a significant entry in the Indian legal-thriller genre.

Direction and Narrative

The climax benefits from meticulous shot composition, with Gulzar employing long takes that gradually build tension until the final confrontation. This approach mirrors the slow-cooked nature of institutional corruption, allowing viewers to sit with uncomfortable questions rather than rushing to resolution. The screenplay balances dialogue-driven character moments with scenes set against India's bustling metropolitan landscape, grounding the abstract discussion of law and fairness in tangible urban reality.

Soundtrack and Music

Composed by Anirudh Revji, the score weaves traditional Indian instruments with modern electronic textures, creating an atmosphere of uneasy anticipation. The leitmotif associated with Meghna Gulzar’s characters evolves throughout the film, signaling their psychological transformation from legal professionals to something more morally ambiguous.

Critical Reception

  • Lauded for its bold departure from typical police procedural narratives.
  • Praised for Kareena Kapoor's nuanced performance, which avoids melodramatic excess.
  • Noted for Prithviraj Sukumaran's embodiment of the tragic enabler archetype.
  • Critics debated whether the film's themes resonate culturally or feel overly preachy.
  • Visuals and cinematography received consistent acclaim across review sites.
