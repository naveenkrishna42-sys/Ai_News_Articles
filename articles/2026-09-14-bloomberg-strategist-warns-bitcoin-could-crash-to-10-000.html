Bloomberg Strategist Flags $10,000 Bitcoin Scenario

A Bloomberg Intelligence strategist has warned that Bitcoin could fall to $10,000 if a stock market crash triggers a broad risk-off event across global markets. The call, reported by finance.biggo.com, arrives as Bitcoin trades well above its historical bear-market lows, and it revives a debate that has followed crypto for years: is Bitcoin a hedge against equity weakness, or a high-beta risk asset that amplifies it?

The strategist's framework is straightforward. Bitcoin, like other speculative assets, relies on liquidity and risk appetite. If equity markets enter a sustained crash, margin calls and forced deleveraging would likely hit crypto hardest, pushing Bitcoin toward levels that seem implausible in a bull market. A move to $10,000 would represent a drawdown of more than 80% from recent price levels, depending on the starting point.

What a $10,000 Bitcoin Would Mean

A $10,000 Bitcoin would wipe out a large portion of the market capitalisation built up during the most recent cycle. For retail investors who entered near the top, the losses would be severe. For long-term holders with lower cost bases, it would be a painful but survivable drawdown, similar in scale to previous bear markets.

It is worth noting that Bitcoin has already experienced drawdowns of this magnitude. After the 2017 peak near $20,000, Bitcoin fell roughly 84% to around $3,200 by late 2018. After the 2021 peak near $69,000, it fell about 77% to roughly $15,500 by late 2022. A fall to $10,000 from a hypothetical cycle high of $100,000 would be a 90% drawdown, which is at the extreme end but not outside historical precedent.

Bitcoin's Correlation With Equities

The central issue is Bitcoin's relationship with stock markets. Since 2020, Bitcoin has traded with a rising correlation to the Nasdaq 100, particularly during periods of macroeconomic stress. When the Federal Reserve tightened policy aggressively in 2022, both tech stocks and crypto fell together. Bitcoin did not act as a safe haven.

That correlation matters for the $10,000 scenario. If a stock market crash is driven by recession fears, earnings downgrades, or a liquidity crunch, Bitcoin would likely fall alongside equities, not decouple from them. The strategist's warning is essentially a bet that Bitcoin's risk-asset characteristics dominate its store-of-value narrative in a crisis.

Pros and Cons of the Bear Case

  • Pro: Bitcoin has a documented history of 70-90% drawdowns, so a $10,000 target is not mathematically impossible.
  • Pro: Rising correlation with equities means a stock crash would likely drag Bitcoin lower.
  • Pro: Leverage in crypto markets can accelerate sell-offs during liquidity crunches.
  • Con: Bitcoin's market structure has matured, with spot ETFs and institutional custody reducing some extreme tail risks.
  • Con: Predicting a specific low is notoriously difficult; strategists have made similar calls that did not materialise.
  • Con: A $10,000 price would require a macroeconomic shock far worse than a typical correction.

What This Means for Investors

For Indian investors, the warning is a reminder that crypto allocations should be sized for extreme volatility. A 10% portfolio allocation to Bitcoin that falls 80% would still cost 8% of total portfolio value. Position sizing and stop-loss discipline matter more than directional bets.

The $10,000 scenario is a tail risk, not a base case. But tail risks are precisely what investors should stress-test for. If Bitcoin can fall 80% in a crash, portfolios should be built to survive that outcome, not just the upside case.

Long-term believers may view such a drawdown as an accumulation opportunity. Short-term traders should respect the possibility. Either way, the Bloomberg strategist's warning is a useful counterweight to the euphoria that often dominates crypto headlines.
