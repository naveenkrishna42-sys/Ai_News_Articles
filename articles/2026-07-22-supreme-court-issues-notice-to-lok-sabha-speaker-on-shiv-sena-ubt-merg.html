Supreme Court Issues Notice to Lok Sabha Speaker on Shiv Sena UBT Merger Challenge

📅 2026-07-22 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court issued notice to Lok Sabha Speaker on Shiv Sena (UBT) challenge
  • Court declined interim stay on Speaker's approval of merger of six MPs with Shinde faction
  • Plea argues the merger violates constitutional provisions and party dissent rules

SC Seeks Speaker's Response on Merger Approval

The Supreme Court on Wednesday issued a formal notice to Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla, responding to a petition filed by the Shiv Sena (Uddhav Balasaheb Thackeray) faction. The party has challenged the Speaker's decision to approve the merger of six of its MPs with the rival Eknath Shinde-led faction.

A bench headed by the Chief Justice heard the matter. The court declined to grant an interim stay on the Speaker's order, meaning the merger remains in effect for now.

What the Petition Alleges

The Shiv Sena (UBT) has termed the Speaker's approval 'constitutional hara-kiri'. The petition argues that the merger of the six MPs violates the Tenth Schedule of the Constitution, commonly known as the anti-defection law.

According to the petitioner, the merger was not based on a legitimate split or merger of the original political party. Instead, they claim it was an individual decision by the MPs to join the Shinde camp, which does not meet the legal threshold for a valid merger.

The party has submitted that the Speaker's nod effectively legitimises defection. They have urged the court to set aside the decision and restore the original party affiliation of the six MPs.

Background of the Political Dispute

The case is the latest twist in the protracted legal battle between the Uddhav Thackeray and Eknath Shinde factions. The dispute began in 2022 when Shinde led a rebellion, splitting the Shiv Sena and eventually forming a government with the BJP.

In 2023, the Speaker approved the merger of the six MPs, citing a split within the party. The Uddhav faction has consistently contested this, arguing that the majority of the party's legislators and organisational structure remain with them.

The Supreme Court had earlier delivered a verdict on the larger political dispute. However, the question of the Speaker's powers under the anti-defection law for this specific merger remained pending.

What Happens Next

The court has given the Lok Sabha Speaker and the Shinde faction four weeks to file their responses. The matter is likely to be heard after the replies are submitted.

Legal experts say the case could set a significant precedent on the interpretation of merger and defection under the Tenth Schedule. For now, the six MPs continue to sit in the Lok Sabha as part of the Shinde faction.

The next hearing will determine whether the Speaker's decision withstands judicial scrutiny. Both sides are expected to present detailed arguments on the constitutional validity of the merger approval.
