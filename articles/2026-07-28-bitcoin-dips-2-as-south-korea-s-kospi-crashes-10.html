Bitcoin dips 2% as South Korea's Kospi crashes 10%

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin fell 2% following US market close on Monday
  • South Korea's Kospi index suffered a 10% crash, its steepest drop in years
  • The moves signal growing risk aversion among global investors

Bitcoin slid 2% in early Asian trading after US markets closed on Monday, while South Korea's benchmark Kospi index suffered a brutal 10% plunge. The dual sell-off underscores a broad shift in investor sentiment away from risk assets.

The world's largest cryptocurrency was trading near $65,000 at the time of the dip, paring gains from a brief rally earlier in the session. The move came as traders assessed mounting headwinds for speculative assets.

Kospi crash stokes contagion fears

South Korea's Kospi index recorded its worst single-day drop in years, tumbling 10% amid a wave of panic selling. The sell-off was driven by a combination of global trade tensions and domestic economic uncertainty.

Investors fled Korean equities as export data disappointed and geopolitical risks escalated. The crash wiped out billions of dollars in market value within hours, triggering circuit breakers on the Korea Exchange.

Bitcoin follows equities lower

Bitcoin's 2% decline after the US close mirrored the risk-off mood sweeping through traditional markets. The cryptocurrency had been hovering in a tight range for days before the sudden move lower.

Analysts pointed to a breakdown in the correlation between Bitcoin and US tech stocks, with the digital asset now tracking Asian equity weakness more closely. The Kospi's collapse appeared to act as a catalyst for the pullback.

“Crypto markets are increasingly sensitive to shocks in regional equity indices,” one trader noted. “When a major index like the Kospi drops 10%, it forces margin calls and liquidity crunches that ripple into crypto.”

Global risk appetite shrinks

The simultaneous slide in Bitcoin and Korean stocks reflects a broader retreat from risk assets worldwide. US futures also pointed lower ahead of the Asian session, signalling a cautious open for Wall Street.

Safe-haven assets such as gold and the Japanese yen saw modest gains, as investors sought shelter from the volatility. The US dollar held steady against a basket of major currencies.

Market participants are now watching for further signs of stress. The Bank of Korea has not yet commented on the crash, but traders expect emergency measures if the sell-off deepens.

What comes next

Traders are eyeing Bitcoin's key support level near $63,000. A break below that could trigger another wave of selling, especially if the Kospi fails to recover in Tuesday's session.

All eyes are on the Bank of Korea and the Federal Reserve for any signals on policy intervention. For now, the crypto market remains on edge, waiting for the next catalyst.
