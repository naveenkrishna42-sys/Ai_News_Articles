Rahul Gandhi calls Modi 'most anti-youth PM' at Dehradun rally

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rahul Gandhi called PM Modi the 'most anti-youth Prime Minister ever' during the CJP march in Dehradun.
  • He accused the government of presiding over a 'paper-leak industry' and failing to create jobs for young Indians.
  • The rally was marked by rain, slush, and music, with Congress attempting a Gen Z-style political show.
  • Gandhi called for a political consensus to overhaul the education system and end exam paper leaks.

Congress leader targets PM over jobs and exams

Rahul Gandhi launched a sharp attack on Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday, calling him the 'most anti-youth Prime Minister ever'. The Congress leader made the remark during his first reaction on the 'Ceasefire for Justice and Peace' (CJP) march in Dehradun, Uttarakhand.

Addressing a crowd of students and supporters under a steady drizzle, Gandhi said the Modi government had failed the country's youth. He pointed to rising unemployment and repeated exam paper leaks as evidence of systemic neglect.

Rain, slush and a Gen Z-style show

The rally was marked by unusual energy for a political event in the hills. Rain had turned the rally ground into slush, but organisers had set up a stage with loudspeakers playing upbeat music. Many in the crowd, mostly college students, wore raincoats and waved Congress flags.

Party workers described it as a Gen Z-style political show, with Gandhi walking through the crowd and stopping for selfies. The atmosphere was more concert than convention, a deliberate shift to connect with younger voters ahead of state elections due next year.

'Paper-leak industry' and a call for consensus

Gandhi did not hold back on the issue that has dominated student protests across the country. 'Students are being beaten, paper leak culprits are roaming free,' he said, accusing the government of protecting those responsible for recent exam irregularities.

He called for a political consensus to end what he termed the 'paper-leak industry'. 'The education system must be rebuilt from scratch,' Gandhi said, adding that the current framework only adds to student stress and does little to ensure merit.

Direct attack on Modi's record

The Congress leader framed his criticism of the PM in personal terms. 'This is the most anti-youth Prime Minister we have ever seen,' he said. 'No jobs, no exam integrity, no future for our children.'

Gandhi contrasted his party's vision with the BJP's record, promising that a Congress government would prioritise employment and education reforms. He did not, however, offer specific numbers or a timeline for any new scheme.

BJP spokespersons have not yet responded to Gandhi's remarks. Party sources said they would issue a statement later in the day, likely accusing Gandhi of 'negative politics' and pointing to government schemes for skilling and entrepreneurship.

What to watch

The CJP march is expected to continue through several towns in Uttarakhand over the next week. Political analysts will watch whether this Gen Z-style outreach translates into votes, or remains a one-day spectacle in the rain.
