Sensex gains 152 points in volatile trade as RBI holds rates

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex closed 152.05 points higher in a volatile session
  • RBI kept policy rates unchanged, in line with market expectations
  • Banking and auto stocks led the rally, while IT stocks dragged
  • Rupee traded flat against the US dollar

MUMBAI: Equity benchmarks ended higher on Wednesday, with the Sensex gaining 152.05 points in a choppy session, as the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) kept policy rates unchanged, a move widely anticipated by the market. The 30-share index settled at 54,267.34, while the Nifty advanced 48.75 points to close at 16,204.55.

Trading was range-bound through the day, with investors weighing the central bank's status-quo stance against lingering concerns over inflation and global growth. The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) maintained the repo rate at 5.15 per cent, citing the need to support economic recovery while keeping a close watch on price pressures.

Banking, auto shares lead gains

Banking stocks were the primary drivers of the rally, with the Nifty Bank index climbing over 0.7 per cent. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and State Bank of India all closed in the green, supported by the RBI's decision to keep liquidity conditions accommodative. Auto stocks also saw healthy buying, with Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra among the top gainers.

Analysts said the RBI's commentary on growth and inflation was balanced, offering little surprise to the market. "The policy was in line with expectations. The focus remains on durable recovery, and the central bank has kept its stance supportive," said a market strategist at a domestic brokerage.

IT stocks drag, broader market mixed

Technology shares, however, came under pressure, with Infosys and Tata Consultancy Services losing ground. The Nifty IT index slipped nearly 0.5 per cent, reflecting profit-booking in the sector after a recent run-up. Midcap and smallcap stocks were mixed, with the broader indices ending flat to slightly positive.

Volatility was evident throughout the session, with the India VIX, a measure of market fear, hovering around 16.5 levels. Traders pointed to global cues, including crude oil prices and US Federal Reserve policy expectations, as key factors influencing sentiment.

Rupee flat, bond yields ease

The Indian rupee traded flat against the US dollar, closing at 82.35, as the RBI's rate decision had little impact on the currency. Bond yields eased marginally, with the 10-year government bond yield slipping to 7.18 per cent, as market participants took comfort from the central bank's dovish undertone.

Foreign institutional investors were net sellers in the cash market, while domestic institutional investors provided support, according to provisional data. The market breadth was slightly negative, with 1,743 stocks advancing and 1,826 declining on the BSE.

What to watch next

Investors will now focus on the US inflation data due later this week, which could influence global interest rate expectations. On the domestic front, quarterly earnings from index heavyweights, including Reliance Industries and HDFC Bank, will dictate near-term market direction. The RBI's next policy meeting is scheduled for October, and any shift in its stance will be closely monitored.
