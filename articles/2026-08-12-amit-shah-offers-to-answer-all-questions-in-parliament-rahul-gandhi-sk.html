Amit Shah offers to answer all questions in Parliament; Rahul Gandhi skips debate

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Amit Shah writes to Lok Sabha Speaker seeking time for a debate on NEET student protests.
  • Shah says he is ready to answer any question in Parliament; accuses Rahul Gandhi of avoiding discussion.
  • Kiren Rijiju expresses frustration, says Opposition is 'running away' from a debate in the Lok Sabha.

NEW DELHI — Union Home Minister Amit Shah has said he is ready to answer any question in Parliament, but has accused Congress leader Rahul Gandhi of showing no interest in a debate. The statement comes amid a prolonged standoff over student protests against the NEET examination.

In a letter to the Lok Sabha Speaker, Shah has requested that time be allocated starting immediately for a discussion on the issue. The Home Minister's move is seen as an attempt to break the parliamentary logjam that has stalled proceedings for days.

Shah's challenge to the Opposition

Addressing reporters in the Parliament complex, Shah said he was open to answering all questions that members may have. He maintained that the government had nothing to hide and was willing to engage in a full-fledged debate on the NEET controversy.

However, he noted that Rahul Gandhi, the Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, appeared unwilling to participate. "I am ready to answer any question in Parliament, but Rahul Gandhi is not interested," Shah said, according to officials present.

'Totally frustrated': Rijiju's remarks

Parallel to Shah's letter, Union Minister Kiren Rijiju expressed his exasperation with the Opposition's stance. "I am totally frustrated," Rijiju said, accusing Opposition parties of evading a structured discussion on the NEET issue in the Lok Sabha.

Rijiju's comments reflect the government's growing impatience with what it describes as a refusal by the Opposition to engage in parliamentary debate. He suggested that the Opposition was deliberately avoiding the floor of the House to escape scrutiny on the matter.

A 'unique' situation

Shah described the current parliamentary situation as "unique," pointing out that while the government was seeking a debate, the Opposition seemed to be avoiding it. This is a reversal of the usual dynamic, where the Opposition typically demands discussions and the government resists them.

Political analysts note that the NEET issue has become a rallying point for the Opposition, which has been pressing for a joint parliamentary committee probe. The government, meanwhile, has offered a discussion in both Houses, but the stalemate persists.

What the debate would cover

The proposed debate is expected to address the concerns of students who have been protesting against alleged irregularities in the NEET examination. The agitation has drawn significant public attention, with students demanding transparency in the examination process.

Sources indicate that the government is keen to present its case in Parliament, arguing that it has already taken corrective steps. The Home Minister's letter is intended to pressure the Opposition into accepting a floor debate rather than continuing protests outside Parliament.

The standoff has led to repeated adjournments of both Houses, with little legislative work being transacted. The ruling party has blamed the Opposition for the disruption, while the Opposition maintains that it is pressing for accountability.

The road ahead

As the winter session continues, the Speaker's response to Shah's letter will be crucial. If time is allocated, the onus will be on the Opposition to either participate or face criticism for avoiding a discussion.

Observers will be watching whether Rahul Gandhi and other Opposition leaders agree to the debate, or if the stalemate continues. The government appears determined to force a parliamentary showdown on the issue, and the coming days are likely to determine whether the logjam can be broken.
