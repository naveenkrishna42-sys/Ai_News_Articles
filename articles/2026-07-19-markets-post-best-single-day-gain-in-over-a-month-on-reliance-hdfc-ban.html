Markets post best single-day gain in over a month on Reliance, HDFC Bank rally

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty posted their biggest single-day gains in over a month
  • Reliance Industries and HDFC Bank were the top contributors to the rally
  • Broad-based buying was seen across most sectoral indices

Indian equity benchmarks surged on Friday, logging their best single-day gain in more than a month. The rally was led by heavyweights Reliance Industries and HDFC Bank, which together added significant heft to the indices.

Sensex, Nifty jump over 1%

The BSE Sensex climbed over 700 points, while the NSE Nifty 50 rose past the 24,800 mark. Both indices closed with gains of more than 1 per cent, their strongest performance since mid-June.

Market breadth was positive, with advances outpacing declines on both exchanges. Most sectoral indices ended in the green, led by oil and gas, banking, and IT stocks.

Reliance and HDFC Bank lead the charge

Reliance Industries was the top contributor to the Sensex gain, rising nearly 3 per cent. The stock saw heavy buying after reports of a potential acquisition in the retail space, though the company has not confirmed any such deal.

HDFC Bank added over 2 per cent, recovering from recent weakness. Analysts attributed the move to value buying after the stock had underperformed peers in recent weeks. The bank's strong fundamentals and expectations of stable earnings also supported sentiment.

Broader markets join the rally

The midcap and smallcap indices also gained, rising over 1 per cent each. This broad-based participation indicated that the rally was not limited to just a few large-cap stocks.

IT stocks, including Infosys and TCS, also contributed to the uptick. The Nifty IT index rose nearly 1.5 per cent, tracking positive cues from global technology peers.

What drove the sentiment?

Market participants said the rally was partly driven by positive global cues. Asian and European markets were mostly higher after US inflation data came in softer than expected, raising hopes that the Federal Reserve may ease its monetary tightening cycle.

Domestically, falling crude oil prices also provided a tailwind. A sustained drop in oil prices benefits India, a major importer, by reducing the import bill and easing inflationary pressures.

Foreign portfolio investors were net buyers in the cash market, adding to the positive momentum. Domestic institutional investors also supported the market with steady buying.

Volatility eased, with the India VIX, the fear gauge, dropping over 5 per cent. This decline in volatility suggested that traders were turning more confident about near-term market direction.

The rupee also strengthened against the US dollar, closing at 83.45, up from the previous day's close of 83.60. A stronger rupee typically supports market sentiment by reducing import costs and attracting foreign inflows.

Looking ahead, traders will watch for quarterly earnings from Reliance Industries and HDFC Bank next week. Their results could determine whether the rally has further legs or if profit-booking sets in.
