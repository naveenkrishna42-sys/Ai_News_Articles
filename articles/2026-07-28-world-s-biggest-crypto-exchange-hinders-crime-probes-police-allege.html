World's Biggest Crypto Exchange Hinders Crime Probes, Police Allege

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Law enforcement agencies report increased difficulty in tracing illicit funds on the platform.
  • The exchange's recent policy changes have allegedly reduced cooperation with authorities.
  • Experts warn of a growing gap between crypto industry practices and regulatory needs.

Police Allege Obstruction in Crypto Crime Probes

Law enforcement agencies across multiple jurisdictions have voiced strong concerns that the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange is making it significantly harder to investigate financial crimes. A report from The New York Times cites police officials who say recent policy changes and operational decisions by the exchange have created new obstacles for tracking illicit funds.

The exchange, which handles billions of dollars in daily trading volume, has long been a focal point for regulators. But police now claim that its current practices actively hinder criminal investigations rather than support them. Officials from agencies in the United States, Europe, and Asia have described a pattern of delayed responses, incomplete data sharing, and reduced cooperation with legal requests.

Key Policy Changes Under Scrutiny

According to the report, one major sticking point is the exchange's decision to relax its know-your-customer (KYC) requirements for certain account tiers. This has allowed users to move substantial amounts of money with minimal identity verification. Police say this directly contradicts earlier promises to prioritize compliance and transparency.

Another issue is the platform's handling of subpoenas and court orders. Investigators claim the exchange often takes weeks to respond to urgent requests, by which time funds have been moved or laundered. A senior police official quoted in the report said, 'By the time we get the data, the trail is cold. They are effectively helping criminals cover their tracks.'

Growing Global Frustration

This is not the first time the exchange has faced criticism. It has been fined by regulators in several countries for anti-money laundering lapses. However, the latest police complaints suggest the situation is deteriorating. In one case, authorities say a ransomware group moved over Rs 50 crore through the platform in a single month without triggering any internal alerts.

The exchange has defended its practices, stating that it operates within the legal frameworks of the countries where it is licensed. It has also pointed to its cooperation in some high-profile cases. But police argue that such cooperation is selective and often comes too late to be effective.

A Widening Trust Gap

Crypto crime experts warn that the exchange's stance could have broader implications for the industry. If law enforcement loses trust in the largest market player, they may push for stricter regulations that affect all exchanges. 'This is a classic case of the tail wagging the dog,' said a cybersecurity analyst familiar with the matter. 'If the biggest exchange won't play ball, the entire system looks suspect.'

Several governments are now considering new laws that would mandate real-time transaction monitoring for all crypto platforms. The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime has also flagged the issue in its latest report on cyber-enabled financial crimes.

What happens next will depend on whether the exchange responds to the mounting pressure. Some insiders suggest it may announce a compliance overhaul in the coming weeks, but no official confirmation has been made. For now, police remain frustrated, and the clock is ticking on the crypto industry's ability to self-regulate.
