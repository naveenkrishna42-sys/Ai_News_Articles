JD Vance calls PM Modi, discusses India-US ties amid Russia sanctions

📅 2026-08-09 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • US Vice President JD Vance called PM Modi to discuss enhancing India-US relations.
  • PM Modi congratulated Vance on the birth of his son during the call.
  • The conversation comes amid US discussions on Russia sanctions.
  • Both leaders reaffirmed commitment to deepening the bilateral partnership.

US Vice President JD Vance telephoned Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Saturday to discuss ways to strengthen India-US ties, in a call that comes at a moment of heightened diplomatic activity between the two countries.

The Prime Minister's Office confirmed the conversation, stating that the two leaders reviewed ongoing bilateral cooperation and exchanged views on regional and global issues of mutual interest. The call, placed from Washington, lasted around 30 minutes, though officials did not provide a detailed readout.

Congratulatory exchange

During the conversation, PM Modi congratulated Vance on the recent birth of his son, a personal touch that underscored the friendly tenor of the dialogue. Vance, who took office in January 2025, has made India a priority in his diplomatic outreach.

The Vice President's office in a brief statement said the two leaders discussed "the importance of the US-India comprehensive global strategic partnership" and agreed to remain in touch. No specific agreements or announcements were disclosed.

Russia sanctions backdrop

The call happens against the backdrop of a pending US Senate bill on Russia sanctions, which has drawn attention in New Delhi. India has maintained a delicate balancing act between its longstanding ties with Moscow and its growing partnership with Washington.

While the readout did not mention the sanctions bill directly, diplomatic observers note that Vance's outreach signals Washington's intent to keep India engaged on the matter. India has not joined Western sanctions on Russia, instead ramping up purchases of discounted Russian crude.

Growing defence and tech ties

The two leaders also touched upon ongoing defence cooperation, including co-production of military hardware, and collaboration in critical technologies such as semiconductors and artificial intelligence. These areas have emerged as pillars of the bilateral agenda in recent years.

Trade and investment were also part of the discussion, with both sides expressing a desire to expand economic engagement. The US remains one of India's largest trading partners, and bilateral trade crossed the $200 billion mark last year.

What to watch

Diplomatic sources suggest this call could pave the way for a more formal engagement in the coming months, possibly a summit-level meeting. With the US presidential election cycle heating up, the Biden administration is keen to lock in progress on key bilateral fronts.

Observers will watch for any movement on the Russia sanctions bill in the US Senate and how India responds. The two governments have so far not announced any date for the next high-level visit.
