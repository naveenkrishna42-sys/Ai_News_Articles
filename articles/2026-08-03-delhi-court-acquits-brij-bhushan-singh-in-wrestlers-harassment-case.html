Delhi Court Acquits Brij Bhushan Singh in Wrestlers' Harassment Case

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi court acquits Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh and Vinod Tomar in wrestlers' sexual harassment case.
  • Case stemmed from allegations by women wrestlers that sparked prolonged protests in 2023.
  • Singh reacted, saying no allegation was proven against him.
  • The verdict closes a high-profile legal battle that drew national attention.
  • Observers await detailed judgment to understand the court's reasoning.

New Delhi: In a significant turn of events, a Delhi court on Monday acquitted former Wrestling Federation of India (WFI) chief Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh and co-accused Vinod Tomar in the sexual harassment case filed by women wrestlers. The verdict brings to a close a legal saga that had not only shaken the sporting establishment but also triggered nationwide protests and debates about the safety of women athletes.

The court's decision was pronounced amid tight security at the Rouse Avenue Court complex. Both Singh and Tomar were present in the courtroom when the judgment was read out. The acquittal comes after a trial that saw emotional testimonies from prominent wrestlers, including Olympic medallists, who had accused Singh of inappropriate behaviour during his tenure as the head of the national federation.

The Allegations and the Protests

The case dates back to 2023 when several top women wrestlers, including Vinesh Phogat, Bajrang Punia, and Sakshi Malik, staged a prolonged protest at Jantar Mantar in New Delhi. They alleged that Singh had sexually harassed them over a period of years, and they demanded his resignation and arrest. The protests gained widespread public sympathy and political traction, forcing the government to take the matter seriously.

Following the allegations, the Delhi Police registered a case against Singh under various sections of the Indian Penal Code, including those related to sexual harassment and criminal intimidation. Vinod Tomar, then an assistant secretary of the WFI, was also named as an accused. The trial saw multiple hearings, with the prosecution presenting witnesses and the defence arguing that the allegations were a conspiracy to tarnish Singh's image.

Defence and Reaction

Throughout the trial, Singh maintained his innocence, calling the charges baseless. His legal team argued that the complaints were motivated and lacked corroborative evidence. After the verdict, Singh addressed the media outside the courthouse, saying, "If even one allegation was proved, I would have accepted it, but not a single charge was substantiated. This is a victory for truth."

The reaction among the wrestlers was one of disappointment. They had hoped for a conviction, and many have expressed their intention to appeal the verdict in a higher court. Legal experts say that the prosecution is likely to challenge the acquittal before the Delhi High Court, though no official announcement has been made yet.

What the Verdict Means

The acquittal is a major relief for Singh, a six-term Member of Parliament from the Bharatiya Janata Party. He had stepped down as WFI chief shortly after the protests began, but he remained a polarising figure in Indian sports administration. For the wrestlers, the verdict is a setback in their long fight for justice, though they have vowed to continue their struggle.

The detailed judgment is yet to be made public, and it will be scrutinised by legal observers to understand the court's reasoning. The case has also raised broader questions about the handling of sexual harassment complaints in sports bodies and the accountability of powerful officials. The government, which had constituted a committee to investigate the allegations, has not yet commented on the verdict.

The Road Ahead

As the dust settles on this verdict, all eyes will be on the next legal steps taken by the wrestlers. An appeal to the Delhi High Court remains a distinct possibility. This case has already left a lasting impact on Indian sports, prompting reforms and greater awareness about the rights of athletes. For now, the acquittal stands, but the debate over justice in this case is far from over.
