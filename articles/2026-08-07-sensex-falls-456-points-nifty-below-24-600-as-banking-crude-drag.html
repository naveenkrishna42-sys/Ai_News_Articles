Sensex Falls 456 Points, Nifty Below 24,600 as Banking, Crude Drag

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 456 points to close lower, Nifty settled below 24,600.
  • Banking stocks led the decline, with Trent dropping 4% and Jio Financial 2%.
  • Rising crude oil prices and weak global cues pressured market sentiment.
  • Analysts say high valuations and global uncertainty could keep markets volatile.
  • Investors now watch for next week's inflation data and global central bank moves.

Indian equities ended the trading session on a sombre note on Friday, August 7, with the BSE Sensex shedding over 450 points and the Nifty 50 closing below the 24,600 mark. The sell-off was broad-based, led by banking and financial stocks, while a continued climb in crude oil prices added to investor unease.

The Sensex closed 456 points lower, while the Nifty slipped to settle under 24,600. Market participants pointed to weak global cues, with concerns over rising energy costs and their impact on inflation and corporate margins.

Banking stocks drag, Trent and Jio Fin lead losses

Banking stocks were the biggest drag on the indices. The Nifty Bank index fell sharply as investors booked profits after a recent run-up. Among individual stocks, Trent dropped 4 per cent, while Jio Financial Services slipped 2 per cent, contributing significantly to the benchmark's decline.

Other financial heavyweights also traded weak, though specific figures for the broader sector were not immediately available. Analysts said the profit-taking in banking names was expected after the sector's outperformance in recent weeks.

Crude oil prices weigh on sentiment

Rising crude oil prices were a key factor behind the market's slide. As a net importer of oil, India's trade balance and inflation outlook are sensitive to global energy prices. A sustained increase in crude could pressure the rupee and widen the current account deficit, prompting foreign investors to pull back.

The rise in oil prices comes amid tightening global supply concerns and renewed demand optimism. This has also weighed on other Asian markets, which traded lower through the day.

Global cues remain negative

Negative sentiment from global markets added to the domestic pressure. US and European futures pointed to a weak start, while Asian peers ended mixed. Investors remained cautious ahead of key economic data releases and central bank commentary due next week.

Market watchers noted that valuations in the Indian market remain elevated relative to historical averages, leaving little room for error. Any further deterioration in global risk appetite could trigger more selling.

What lies ahead for investors

With the earnings season largely behind, traders will now turn their attention to macroeconomic data, including inflation numbers and industrial production figures due in the coming days. Global oil price movements and the US Federal Reserve's stance on interest rates will also be closely tracked.

Analysts advise caution in the near term, suggesting that volatility is likely to persist until there is clarity on these fronts. For now, the market remains in a corrective phase, with support for the Nifty seen near the 24,300-24,400 zone.
