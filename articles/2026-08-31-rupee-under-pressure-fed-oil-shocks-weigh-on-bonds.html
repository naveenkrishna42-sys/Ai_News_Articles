Rupee under pressure: Fed, oil shocks weigh on bonds

📅 2026-08-31 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rupee falls 13 paise to 95.56 against dollar
  • Government bonds at risk of further selloff
  • Fed rate hikes and rising oil prices key drivers

Rupee weakens as dollar strengthens on Fed hikes

The Indian rupee has weakened significantly against the U.S. dollar, falling 13 paise to 95.56 in early trade. This decline is partly due to the strengthening of the dollar, which is driven by expectations of more Federal Reserve rate hikes. The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) is expected to keep interest rates unchanged, but the dollar's strength is a concern for the rupee.

Oil prices rise, adding to rupee's woes

Rising oil prices are also putting pressure on the rupee. Crude oil prices have been on the rise, which is expected to increase the import bill for India. This is a significant concern for the economy, as it could lead to higher inflation and a weaker rupee.

Government bonds at risk of further selloff

The weakening rupee and rising oil prices are also putting government bonds at risk of a further selloff. This is because the rupee's weakness could lead to higher borrowing costs for the government, and rising oil prices could increase the cost of imports. This could lead to a selloff in government bonds, which could further weaken the rupee.

What does this mean for the economy?

The weakening rupee and rising oil prices are significant concerns for the Indian economy. A weaker rupee could lead to higher inflation, as it makes imports more expensive. Rising oil prices could also increase the cost of living, as they are a significant component of the import bill. Additionally, the selloff in government bonds could lead to higher borrowing costs for the government, which could further strain the economy.
