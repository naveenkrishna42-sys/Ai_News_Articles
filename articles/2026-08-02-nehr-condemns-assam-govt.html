NEHR Condemns Assam Government

The North East Human Rights (NEHR) organisation has strongly condemned the Assam government for booking land rights activist Pranab Doley under the National Security Act (NSA). The move has been criticised by the Congress as an attempt to silence dissent and weaponise extraordinary laws.

Background of the Case

Pranab Doley was detained under the NSA a day after he was granted bail in a case related to his protest against a luxury hotel in Kaziranga. The activist had been opposing the construction of the hotel, citing concerns over the potential impact on the environment and the local community.

The Assam government's decision to invoke the NSA against Doley has been widely criticised by human rights organisations and opposition parties. The Congress has accused the government of using extraordinary laws to silence dissent and crush opposition.

Reaction from Opposition Parties

The Congress has termed the move as an attempt to stifle democracy and suppress the voices of those who are fighting for their rights. The party has demanded that the Assam government immediately revoke the NSA charges against Doley and release him from detention.

Legal Implications

The invocation of the NSA against Doley has raised concerns over the misuse of extraordinary laws in the country. The NSA allows for detention without trial for up to 12 months, and its provisions have been criticised for being draconian and open to abuse.

Legal experts have pointed out that the NSA can only be invoked in cases where there is a threat to national security, and that the Act cannot be used to silence dissent or crush opposition. The Assam government's decision to book Doley under the NSA is likely to be challenged in court, and the case is expected to have significant implications for human rights and civil liberties in the country.

The case is also likely to have political implications, with opposition parties expected to raise the issue in parliament and demand action against the Assam government. The NEHR and other human rights organisations are also likely to continue their campaign against the NSA and other extraordinary laws, demanding that they be repealed or amended to prevent their misuse.

What happens next will depend on the outcome of the legal challenges to the NSA charges against Doley, as well as the response of the Assam government and the central government to the criticism and opposition from human rights organisations and opposition parties.
