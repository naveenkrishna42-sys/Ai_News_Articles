IDP Education to Shift IELTS to Computer-Based Format From September

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • IDP Education will discontinue the paper-based IELTS exam from September.
  • All IELTS tests will be conducted in a computer-based format only.
  • The exam pattern, content, and scoring remain unchanged.
  • The move is aimed at faster result delivery and enhanced test security.

IDP Education Confirms End of Paper-Based IELTS

IDP Education, one of the co-owners of the International English Language Testing System (IELTS), has announced that from September 2026, the exam will be offered exclusively in a computer-based format. The paper-based version will be discontinued globally.

The decision marks a significant shift for millions of test-takers in India and abroad. IDP Education officials stated the move is intended to streamline the testing process and improve efficiency.

Exam Pattern and Content Remain Unchanged

Despite the change in delivery mode, IDP Education has confirmed that the exam pattern, question types, and scoring system will remain identical to the current format. Test-takers will not face any changes in syllabus or difficulty level.

This means the Listening, Reading, Writing, and Speaking sections will follow the same structure. The Speaking test will continue to be conducted face-to-face with an examiner, as before.

Faster Results and Enhanced Security

One of the key advantages of the computer-based format is faster result processing. IDP Education expects results to be available within 3 to 5 days, compared to the current 13-day wait for paper-based tests.

Computer-based testing also reduces the risk of answer sheet mishandling and provides a more secure testing environment. The shift aligns with global trends in standardised testing, where many major exams have already transitioned to digital platforms.

What Test-Takers Should Do Now

Candidates who have registered for paper-based IELTS exams before September 2026 will be able to take them as scheduled. IDP Education has assured that there will be no disruption for existing registrations.

For those planning to take the test after September, familiarising themselves with the computer-based interface is advisable. IDP Education offers free online practice tests and tutorials to help candidates adapt.

The move has been broadly welcomed by educators and students, though some have expressed concerns about digital access in rural areas. IDP Education says it will continue to provide support and test centres across India, including in smaller cities.

With this change, the IELTS exam joins the growing list of high-stakes tests going fully digital. The next few months will be crucial for candidates to prepare for the new format.
