Indian-Origin Woman Falsely Accused of Visa Fraud in Viral Post; US Official Responds

📅 2026-08-08 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sadhana Gollapudi, an Indian-origin woman, was falsely accused of visa fraud in a viral social media post.
  • Her attorney confirmed she is a US citizen, and the accusations are baseless.
  • A US official responded to the misinformation, highlighting the dangers of online vigilantism.

Viral Accusation Sparks Online Firestorm

A seemingly innocuous video of an Indian-origin woman dancing has triggered a whirlwind of misinformation, with anti-immigration vigilantes accusing her of visa fraud. The woman, identified as Sadhana Gollapudi, became the target of a viral post that alleged she was in the United States illegally. The post, which circulated widely across social media platforms, claimed she was misusing a visa, prompting a wave of online abuse and scrutiny.

However, the allegations quickly unraveled. Gollapudi's attorney stepped forward to clarify that she is, in fact, a US citizen. The lawyer dismissed the accusations as baseless, stating that the viral post was built on false assumptions and a complete misreading of her status. The damage, though, had already been done, with the video being shared thousands of times before the truth emerged.

US Official Weighs In on Misinformation

The situation escalated to the point where a US official felt compelled to respond. The official, whose name has not been disclosed, addressed the incident, underscoring the dangers of social media users acting as self-appointed immigration enforcers. The response aimed to correct the record and caution against the spread of unverified claims that can ruin reputations and incite harassment.

This is not an isolated incident. In recent years, several Indian-origin individuals have been targeted by similar online campaigns, often based on flimsy or fabricated evidence. The trend has raised concerns among diaspora communities, who fear that a single viral post could lead to real-world consequences, including job loss or legal trouble, even when the accusations are false.

The Role of Social Media in Visa Policing

Experts point to a growing phenomenon where ordinary users take it upon themselves to police immigration status online. These digital vigilantes often rely on stereotypes, racial profiling, and incomplete information, leading to wrongful accusations. In Gollapudi's case, the video of her dancing was twisted to fit a narrative of visa misuse, with no evidence to back the claim.

Her attorney's statement highlighted the emotional toll such false accusations can take. "She is a law-abiding citizen who has done nothing wrong," the lawyer said, adding that the family has been subjected to threats and harassment. The legal team is now considering options to address the defamation, though no formal action has been announced yet.

Community Response and Broader Implications

The Indian-American community has rallied around Gollapudi, with many taking to social media to condemn the misinformation. Several community leaders have called for greater awareness about the dangers of online vigilantism, urging platforms to act faster in removing false content. The incident has also sparked debates about the need for stricter verification processes before such posts go viral.

Meanwhile, the US official's response has been seen as a step in the right direction, though some argue it is not enough. The official's remarks serve as a reminder that misinformation can have serious consequences, not just for individuals but for public trust in immigration systems. As the story continues to unfold, it highlights the fragile line between free speech and defamation in the digital age.

What Happens Next

Gollapudi's legal team is reportedly weighing its options, including potential defamation suits against those who propagated the false claims. For now, the focus remains on clearing her name and deterring future incidents. The episode serves as a cautionary tale for social media users to verify facts before sharing, and for platforms to prioritize accuracy over virality. Watch for any legal filings in the coming weeks, as this case could set a precedent for how such misinformation is handled.
