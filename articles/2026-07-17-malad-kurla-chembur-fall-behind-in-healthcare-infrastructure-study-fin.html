Malad, Kurla, Chembur fall behind in healthcare infrastructure, study finds

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Malad, Kurla, and Chembur have fewer hospital beds per capita than other Mumbai suburbs.
  • The shortage of primary health centres forces residents to travel long distances for basic care.
  • Civic officials acknowledge the gap but have not yet announced specific timelines for new facilities.

Suburban healthcare gaps exposed

Mumbai's western and central suburbs are facing a stark healthcare deficit, according to a recent assessment by the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC). Malad, Kurla and Chembur have emerged as the worst-performing wards in terms of hospital beds and primary health centres (PHCs).

The report, reviewed by TIVRA News, shows these three areas have less than one bed per 1,000 residents. The World Health Organisation recommends a minimum of five beds per 1,000 people. Residents in these pockets often travel to Bandra or South Mumbai for even routine treatments.

Why Malad, Kurla and Chembur are struggling

Malad, a densely packed suburb with a population exceeding 8 lakh, has only two major public hospitals. Kurla, a transport hub, relies heavily on a single civic dispensary. Chembur, though relatively older, has seen its health infrastructure stagnate despite a growing population.

Local activists point to unplanned real estate growth as a key reason. Builders have focused on housing projects without contributing to civic amenities. The BMC has not added a new PHC in these areas in over a decade.

Residents bear the brunt

For a fever or minor injury, people in these areas often queue for hours at overcrowded clinics. Emergency cases face delays. Ambulance response times are longer because facilities are concentrated in other parts of the city.

In Kurla, a recent spike in monsoon-related illnesses overwhelmed the lone public health centre. Patients were referred to Sion Hospital, nearly 10 kilometres away. Similar stories emerge from Malad, where residents have been demanding a trauma centre for years.

Civic response and the road ahead

BMC officials confirmed the findings but said land constraints make it difficult to build new hospitals in these crowded wards. They are exploring public-private partnerships to set up smaller clinics.

No firm deadlines have been announced. The corporation is currently conducting a feasibility study for mobile health units in Malad and Kurla. Chembur may get a new dispensary by next year, but plans remain tentative.

What happens next depends on how quickly the BMC can acquire land and allocate funds. Until then, residents of these three suburbs will continue to rely on overburdened facilities, hoping for a break in the city's uneven healthcare map.
