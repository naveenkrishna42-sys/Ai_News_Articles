Sensex Crashes 800 Points, Nifty Below 24,000 in Broad Sell-off

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 800 points in morning trade
  • Nifty dropped below the 24,000 level
  • Selling pressure was broad-based across sectors

Markets Open Deep in Red

Indian equity benchmarks tumbled sharply on Wednesday morning, with the BSE Sensex crashing over 800 points and the NSE Nifty slipping below the psychological 24,000 mark. The sell-off was broad-based, dragging down almost all sectoral indices.

The Sensex was trading at 78,450 levels around 10:45 am, down 1.1% from the previous close. The Nifty hovered near 23,950, losing nearly 1.2% in early deals.

What Triggered the Panic?

Traders pointed to a combination of global and domestic factors. Weak cues from Asian markets, a strengthening dollar, and rising crude oil prices weighed heavily on sentiment.

On the domestic front, sustained foreign portfolio investor (FPI) outflows and concerns over stretched valuations added to the pressure. Market participants said the lack of any major positive triggers led to profit-booking at higher levels.

Broad-Based Selling Across Sectors

All sectoral indices on the NSE were in the red. Banking, financial services, metals, and IT stocks led the decline. The Nifty Bank index fell over 800 points, dragged down by heavyweights like HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India.

Midcap and smallcap stocks also took a hit, with the Nifty Midcap 100 index sliding 1.3%. Analysts said the broader market was due for a correction after recent gains.

Key Stocks Under Pressure

Index heavyweights Reliance Industries, HDFC Bank, and Infosys contributed the most to the Sensex fall. Reliance shares dropped 1.5%, while HDFC Bank lost nearly 1.8%. Infosys was down 1.2%.

Auto stocks also faced selling pressure, with Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra declining over 1% each. The only silver lining was a mild uptick in select pharma counters, but the gains were too small to offset the broader weakness.

Market breadth was firmly negative. On the BSE, about 2,000 stocks declined against 800 advances, signalling a clear bearish mood.

What to Watch Now

Investors will closely track global cues, especially the US Federal Reserve's policy stance and movements in the dollar index. Any further FPI selling or a spike in crude prices could deepen the decline.

Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) have been absorbing some of the selling pressure, but their buying has not been enough to arrest the fall. Traders advise caution and suggest avoiding fresh positions until the market finds a stable base.
