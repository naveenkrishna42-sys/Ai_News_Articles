UGC allows state colleges to become deemed universities, off-campus centres

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • State colleges can now apply for deemed-to-be-university status under UGC's new regulations.
  • Colleges can also become off-campus centres of existing deemed universities or central universities.
  • The move aims to provide more autonomy and flexibility to state higher education institutions.

The University Grants Commission (UGC) has announced a significant policy change, allowing state-run colleges to apply for deemed-to-be-university status. Under new regulations, these institutions can also operate as off-campus centres of other universities, including deemed and central universities.

What the new UGC regulations allow

The decision, announced on Friday, offers state colleges two distinct pathways to expand their academic footprint. First, eligible colleges can seek recognition as deemed-to-be-universities, a status that grants them greater autonomy in designing courses and setting fees.

Second, colleges that do not meet the criteria for deemed status can become off-campus centres of existing deemed universities or central universities. This allows them to offer programmes under the parent institution's name and curriculum.

UGC officials clarified that the move is designed to improve the quality of higher education in states by giving colleges more room to innovate. The commission has invited applications from interested institutions.

Eligibility and process

Colleges seeking deemed-to-be-university status must meet specific infrastructure, faculty, and academic performance benchmarks set by the UGC. Those applying to become off-campus centres need to have the consent of the host university and demonstrate adequate facilities.

The UGC has not specified a timeline for processing applications, but officials indicated that the review would be expedited. The commission will assess each proposal on a case-by-case basis.

This policy shift comes amid ongoing efforts to increase the gross enrolment ratio in higher education. By allowing state colleges to affiliate with well-established universities, the UGC hopes to reduce regional disparities in access to quality education.

Impact on students and institutions

For students, this change could mean greater access to recognised degrees from reputed universities without having to relocate. It may also lead to more diverse course offerings at the college level.

For state governments, the policy provides a tool to upgrade their institutions without heavy capital investment. Many state colleges have struggled with outdated curricula and limited resources.

However, experts caution that the success of the policy will depend on effective implementation. Ensuring that off-campus centres maintain the same academic standards as the parent institution will be a challenge.

The UGC has said it will monitor compliance through periodic inspections and audits. Institutions found violating norms could face penalties, including revocation of the new status.

What to watch: The first batch of applications and the UGC's response will set the tone. State governments are expected to push their top colleges to apply, but the final list may take months to emerge.
