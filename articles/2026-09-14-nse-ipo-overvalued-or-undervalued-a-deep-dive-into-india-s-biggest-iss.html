NSE IPO: The Wait Ends

After a decade-long wait, the National Stock Exchange (NSE) is finally hitting Dalal Street with its much-anticipated initial public offering (IPO). The issue, reduced to ₹22,600 crore, opens on September 17 and is set to be India's largest IPO in recent years. But with a hefty price band and a complex business model, the big question remains: Is the NSE IPO overvalued or undervalued? We dissect the numbers, business fundamentals, and market dynamics to help you decide.

Understanding NSE's Business Model

NSE is not your typical company. As India's largest stock exchange, it earns revenue primarily from transaction fees, listing fees, and data services. Its dominance in the derivatives segment, where it commands over 90% market share, is a key strength. However, this also exposes it to regulatory risks, as SEBI has been tightening norms for derivatives trading to curb speculation. In FY24, NSE reported a revenue of ₹12,000 crore and a net profit of ₹6,500 crore, showcasing robust profitability. But the sustainability of these earnings hinges on market volumes and regulatory environment.

Valuation: Rich or Reasonable?

At the upper end of the price band, NSE is valued at a price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of 40x, which is at a premium to global peers like Hong Kong Exchanges (30x) and London Stock Exchange (25x). However, NSE's growth trajectory and monopoly-like position in Indian derivatives justify a premium. The exchange's revenue has grown at a CAGR of 18% over the last five years, and with India's retail participation rising, future growth seems assured. Yet, the valuation leaves little room for error, and any regulatory crackdown could dent investor sentiment.

Pros and Cons of Investing

  • Pros:
    • Market leader with a near-monopoly in derivatives trading.
    • Consistent revenue growth and high profitability.
    • Strong brand and network effects.
    • Exposure to India's growing equity culture.
  • Cons:
    • Rich valuation leaves little margin of safety.
    • Regulatory risks from SEBI's tightening of derivative norms.
    • Competition from BSE, which is gaining market share.
    • Revenue concentration in transaction fees.

Peer Comparison

Compared to BSE, which trades at a P/E of 45x, NSE's valuation seems reasonable. However, BSE has a more diversified revenue stream, including listing and data services. Globally, NSE's P/E is higher than HKEX and LSE, but its growth rate outpaces them. Investors must consider whether the premium is justified by future growth.

Should You Subscribe?

For long-term investors, NSE offers a unique opportunity to own a piece of India's financial infrastructure. The exchange's dominant position and growth prospects make it a compelling bet, despite the high valuation. However, short-term traders might find the listing gains limited given the hefty premium. We recommend subscribing for the long term, but with a clear understanding of the regulatory and competitive risks. As always, consult your financial advisor before investing.
