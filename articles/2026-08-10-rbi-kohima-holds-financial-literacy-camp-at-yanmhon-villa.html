RBI Kohima holds financial literacy camp at Yanmhon villa

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI Kohima conducted a financial literacy camp at Yanmhon villa
  • Camp aimed to educate residents on banking and digital payment safety
  • Part of RBI's ongoing outreach to boost financial inclusion in Nagaland

The Reserve Bank of India's Kohima office took its financial literacy drive to Yanmhon villa on Monday, organising a camp aimed at helping residents navigate the everyday world of banking and digital payments. The initiative, part of the central bank's broader outreach efforts, sought to bridge the gap between formal financial systems and communities that often remain on the margins of mainstream banking.

Focus on practical knowledge

At the camp, officials from RBI Kohima walked participants through the basics of savings accounts, recurring deposits, and the importance of maintaining a good credit history. They also addressed common grievances related to banking services and explained how customers can approach the RBI's ombudsman scheme when disputes with banks remain unresolved.

Digital payment safety was another key theme. With the rising use of UPI and mobile banking in the region, the RBI team demonstrated how to spot phishing messages, avoid sharing OTPs, and report fraudulent transactions promptly. The sessions were conducted in a mix of English and local languages to ensure clarity.

Community engagement and feedback

The camp was not a one-way lecture. Residents were encouraged to share their experiences with banks, including issues such as delayed loan processing, ATM malfunctions, and difficulties in accessing government subsidy transfers. RBI officials noted down these concerns and promised to take them up with the concerned institutions.

According to a brief statement from the RBI Kohima office, the camp was well attended, with participation from women's groups, self-help groups, and local youth. The central bank has been organising similar camps across Nagaland over the past year, focusing on villages and semi-urban areas where financial awareness remains limited.

Financial inclusion in the Northeast

The initiative is part of a larger push by the RBI to strengthen financial inclusion in the northeastern states. While bank penetration has improved in recent years, officials acknowledge that awareness lags behind. Many residents still rely on informal lending channels, often at high interest rates, due to a lack of trust or understanding of formal banking products.

By holding camps in places like Yanmhon villa, the RBI hopes to demystify banking and encourage more people to open accounts, use digital payment platforms, and plan their finances better. The camp also included a session on government insurance and pension schemes that are linked to bank accounts.

Officials at the camp stressed that financial literacy is not just about knowing how to operate an account, but about making informed decisions that protect families from debt traps and fraud. They cited examples of common scams, including fake loan offers and lottery messages, to illustrate the risks of financial illiteracy.

The RBI has not yet released detailed figures on the number of participants or the specific outcomes of the camp. However, the response from the community has been encouraging, with several residents reportedly opening new bank accounts on the spot with the help of officials from a nearby public sector bank.

Over the coming months, the RBI Kohima office is expected to continue its outreach in other parts of the district, targeting areas with low banking density. The central bank has also indicated plans to collaborate with local schools and colleges to introduce financial concepts early in the academic curriculum.

For Yanmhon villa, Monday's camp was a small but significant step toward financial empowerment. Residents left with not just pamphlets and contact numbers, but a clearer sense of how to use the banking system to their advantage.

Next on the RBI's calendar is a series of similar camps in nearby villages, with a focus on digital literacy for older residents and small business owners. The central bank is also expected to release a district-wise report on financial inclusion metrics later this year.
