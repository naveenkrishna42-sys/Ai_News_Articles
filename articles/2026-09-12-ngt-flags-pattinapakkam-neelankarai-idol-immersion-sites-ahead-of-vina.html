NGT Flags Pattinapakkam, Neelankarai Idol Immersion Sites Ahead of Vinayaka Chathurthi

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-12 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The NGT has asked the Tamil Nadu government to re-examine Pattinapakkam and Neelankarai as designated Vinayaka Chathurthi idol immersion points.
  • Both sites sit on ecologically sensitive Chennai coastline, raising concerns over plaster of Paris residue, paint chemicals and debris choking the Adyar and Kovalam creek mouths.
  • Devotees face possible relocation to alternate ghats and beaches, which could increase travel distances and crowd pressure at remaining sites during the festival.

Why the NGT Stepped In

The National Green Tribunal has directed the Tamil Nadu government to reconsider Pattinapakkam and Neelankarai as authorised idol immersion sites for Vinayaka Chathurthi. The order follows concerns that both locations sit on ecologically fragile stretches of the Chennai coast, where thousands of idols are immersed within a 24 to 48 hour window each year.

Pattinapakkam lies near the Adyar river mouth, a zone already stressed by sewage inflow and sediment load. Neelankarai fronts the Kovalam creek ecosystem, where tidal flushing is limited. Environmental petitioners argued that non-biodegradable remnants from plaster of Paris idols, synthetic paint and plastic garlands settle in these pockets rather than dispersing.

What the Two Sites Actually Face

Immersion debris is not uniform. Clay idols break down within days, but plaster of Paris idols can persist for weeks, releasing gypsum and heavy-metal pigments into the water column. At Pattinapakkam, the shallow near-shore shelf traps this material close to the beach used by fishermen. At Neelankarai, the creek mouth acts as a settling basin, concentrating residue in a nursery habitat for fish and crustaceans.

Practical Implications for Devotees and Organisers

If either site is dropped or restricted, immersion traffic shifts to remaining beaches and temple tanks. That raises two costs: travel time for families carrying idols, and crowd density at the surviving ghats. A family in Neelankarai rerouted to a northern Chennai beach could add 45 to 90 minutes each way on festival day.

Pandal organisers also face logistics. Large idols need crane access, deep water and a ramp. Many temporary immersion points do not have this infrastructure, which is why authorities historically leaned on Pattinapakkam and Neelankarai. Any relocation must come with cranes, barricades, volunteers and waste collection bins, or the debris problem simply moves elsewhere.

  • Pros of re-examining the sites: protects two stressed coastal ecosystems and reduces long-term cleanup costs.
  • Pros for residents: cleaner beaches and creek fronts within weeks, not months, of the festival.
  • Cons for devotees: longer travel to alternate immersion points and possible crowding.
  • Cons for organisers: higher transport, crane and security expenditure.
  • Key gap: Tamil Nadu must publish the alternate site list well before the festival to avoid last-minute confusion.

What a Workable Fix Looks Like

Experts favour a graded approach. Household idols go into dedicated mobile tanks or temple ponds, which capture residue at source. Medium idols use designated beach points with containment booms. Only large pandal idols reach open sea immersion, handled by licensed contractors who retrieve structural frames.

Enforcement matters more than designation. A 2023–2025 pattern in Chennai showed that compliance rises sharply when civic bodies supply separate bins for flowers, plastic and thermocol at each immersion point. Without that, even a well-chosen site degrades within a single season.

The Buyer and Taxpayer Angle

Beach cleanup after Vinayaka Chathurthi is publicly funded. Greater Chennai Corporation spends on debris removal, sand sifting and water testing each year, and recurring expenditure at problem sites runs into several lakh rupees per season. Choosing immersion points that disperse waste naturally lowers that bill.

For devotees buying idols, the NGT push is a nudge toward clay and eco-friendly options. Clay idols cost roughly 20 to 40 percent more than plaster of Paris versions of the same size, but they dissolve fully and avoid the chemical load. Buyers should check for natural dyes and avoid thermocol crowns, which never break down.

The tribunal has not banned immersion at either location. It has asked the state to justify or replace them. The state's response, expected before the festival, will decide where Chennai's idols actually enter the water this year.
