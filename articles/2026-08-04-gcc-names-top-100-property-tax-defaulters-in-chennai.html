GCC names top 100 property tax defaulters in Chennai

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • GCC releases names of top 100 property tax defaulters
  • Move part of intensified recovery drive by civic body
  • Defaulters urged to clear dues to avoid further action

The Greater Chennai Corporation (GCC) has made public the names of the top 100 property tax defaulters in the city, escalating its push to recover pending dues. The list, released on Tuesday, includes residential and commercial property owners who owe substantial amounts to the civic body.

Officials said the move is part of a broader crackdown on chronic defaulters who have ignored multiple notices. By naming them, the GCC aims to increase pressure on those who have repeatedly failed to pay their property tax, a key revenue source for the corporation.

Recovery drive intensifies

The publication of the list follows a series of measures by the GCC to improve tax collection. The corporation has been tightening enforcement, and this latest step is seen as a warning to others who may be tempted to default.

According to sources, the cumulative arrears from these top 100 defaulters run into crores of rupees. While the exact figures were not disclosed in the initial release, officials indicated that the amounts vary significantly, with some owing several lakhs.

Names in public domain

The list has been uploaded on the GCC's official website and is also being displayed at zonal offices. Property owners can check if their names appear on the list, which includes details of the amount due and the respective zone.

A senior corporation official said that this transparency measure is intended to encourage voluntary compliance. “We have given enough time and sent reminders. Now, we are taking stricter steps,” the official told The New Indian Express.

Next steps for defaulters

The GCC has warned that if the defaulters do not clear their dues within a specified period, further action will be taken. This could include attachment of property or other legal proceedings under the City Municipal Corporation Act.

Residents and business owners who have outstanding tax payments have been advised to settle their dues immediately to avoid being named in future lists. The corporation has also reminded citizens that property tax can be paid online through its portal.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether more names will be added to the list in the coming weeks, but indicated that the exercise may be repeated if necessary.

The move has drawn mixed reactions, with some residents welcoming the transparency and others expressing concern over the public naming of defaulters. However, the GCC maintains that the measure is lawful and necessary to ensure fair collection.

Going forward, the corporation is expected to continue its drive and may extend the list to include other categories of defaulters, such as those owing professional tax or water charges. The focus remains on recovering the outstanding amounts and improving the city's financial health.
