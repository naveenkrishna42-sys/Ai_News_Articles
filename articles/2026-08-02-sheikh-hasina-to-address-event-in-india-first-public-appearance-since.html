Sheikh Hasina to address event in India, first public appearance since ouster

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sheikh Hasina will make her first public appearance in India on August 5, exactly two years after leaving Dhaka.
  • Bangladesh's interim government has demanded her extradition; a minister warned she would be arrested upon return.
  • India's foreign ministry told a parliamentary panel it is examining the extradition request as ties with Dhaka face strain.

New Delhi: Two years after she fled Dhaka in the face of a mass uprising, former Bangladesh prime minister Sheikh Hasina is set to make her first public appearance in India on Wednesday, August 5 — the same date she left office in 2024. The event, confirmed by multiple sources, marks a carefully chosen moment in a political drama that continues to shadow relations between New Delhi and Dhaka.

A symbolic date

The choice of August 5 is anything but accidental. It was on this day in 2024 that Hasina, facing weeks of student-led protests, resigned and flew to India in a military aircraft. Her arrival here was sudden, and she has remained in the country since, mostly out of public view.

Now, exactly two years on, she is expected to address a gathering in the national capital. Details of the venue and the audience have not been officially disclosed, but organisers say the event is intended to mark her political legacy and her continued engagement with supporters and allies in India.

Hasina's public silence over the past 24 months has been notable. She has issued occasional statements through aides but has not appeared before cameras or crowds. That changes this week.

Extradition push from Dhaka

The appearance comes at a fraught moment. Bangladesh's interim government, led by Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus, has formally requested Hasina's extradition. A Bangladeshi minister has been blunt about what awaits her: "Sheikh Hasina will be arrested the moment she returns."

Dhaka's legal team has argued that she faces charges related to deaths during the 2024 protests, in which hundreds were killed. The interim government has also accused her administration of widespread human rights abuses. Hasina's supporters dismiss the cases as politically motivated.

India's response has been cautious. The Ministry of External Affairs recently told a parliamentary panel that it is "examining" the extradition request, a formulation that suggests no early decision. The panel's discussion, which included MPs from several parties, also touched on a broader push to reset ties with Bangladesh.

Strained ties, quiet diplomacy

Relations between the two neighbours have cooled considerably since Hasina's ouster. Dhaka has criticised New Delhi's perceived reluctance to hand her over, while Indian officials have stressed the need for due process. The parliamentary panel's engagement on the issue signals that domestic political pressure is building in India too.

Some MPs have argued for a more conciliatory approach towards Bangladesh's current leadership, viewing it as essential for regional stability. Others caution against abandoning a longstanding ally. The government's line so far has been to keep channels open while not committing to a timeline.

For Hasina, Wednesday's appearance is a chance to reassert her narrative. For Dhaka, it is a reminder that she remains a presence in Indian politics and media. And for New Delhi, it is a delicate balancing act between legal obligations, strategic interests, and the messy reality of hosting a deposed leader.

What to watch

All eyes will be on Hasina's speech — whether she addresses the charges against her, signals any move towards Bangladesh, or simply seeks to keep her political relevance alive. The extradition request, meanwhile, continues to sit with Indian authorities, with no public deadline for a decision.

The coming weeks may also see fresh diplomatic exchanges between Delhi and Dhaka, as both sides weigh how to manage a relationship that neither can afford to abandon. For now, August 5 will be a date with renewed meaning.
