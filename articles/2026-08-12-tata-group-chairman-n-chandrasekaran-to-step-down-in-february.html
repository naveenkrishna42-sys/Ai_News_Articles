Tata Group Chairman N Chandrasekaran to Step Down in February

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • N Chandrasekaran to step down as Tata Sons chairman in February.
  • Move follows reported tensions with Tata Trusts, the group's controlling shareholder.
  • Tata Group market valuation dropped by Rs 26,800 crore following the announcement.

N Chandrasekaran, chairman of Tata Sons, the holding company of the $165-billion Tata Group, will step down from his position in February, the company announced on Wednesday. The decision brings an end to a tenure that began in 2017, when he was handpicked by the late Ratan Tata to lead the salt-to-software conglomerate.

The announcement, made after a board meeting, did not specify a reason for the departure. However, multiple reports point to growing friction between Chandrasekaran and the Tata Trusts, the charitable arm that controls 66 per cent of Tata Sons' equity. The trusts, chaired by Ratan Tata until his death in 2024, have reportedly been at odds with Chandrasekaran over strategic decisions and governance matters.

The development sent ripples through the markets. The Tata Group's combined market capitalisation fell by Rs 26,800 crore in a single day, reflecting investor unease over leadership uncertainty at India's largest conglomerate.

A Rise From Humble Beginnings

Chandrasekaran's journey to the top of the Tata empire is a classic Indian corporate story. Born in a small town in Tamil Nadu, he joined Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) as a trainee in 1987, at the age of 24. Over three decades, he climbed the ranks to become the CEO of TCS in 2009, a position he held until his elevation to the group chairmanship in 2017.

His appointment was seen as a break from tradition. Chandrasekaran was the first non-Parsi chairman of the Tata Group, and his selection by Ratan Tata was widely interpreted as a vote of confidence in his operational acumen, honed during his years at TCS, where he oversaw the company's rise to become India's most valuable IT services firm.

What Went Wrong

The exact causes of the reported rift remain a matter of speculation. Rediff, in its analysis, has questioned the strategic missteps during Chandrasekaran's tenure, including the group's foray into consumer technology businesses and the handling of certain legacy assets. The Tata Trusts, which have traditionally played a hands-on role in group affairs, are said to have been unhappy with the pace of decision-making and the chairman's approach to board appointments.

Industry watchers note that the relationship between the chairman and the trusts had been under strain for months. The trusts, which are the ultimate arbiters of the group's direction, have the power to appoint and remove the chairman, and their influence has been a defining feature of Tata Sons' governance structure.

Market Reaction and Succession

The immediate market reaction was sharp but not panicked. The Rs 26,800-crore loss in market value, while significant, is a fraction of the group's overall worth, which stood at over Rs 30 lakh crore as of early 2026. Analysts say the long-term impact will depend on who succeeds Chandrasekaran and how smoothly the transition is managed.

The Tata Sons board is expected to begin the search for a new chairman in the coming weeks. Names being discussed in corporate circles include senior executives from within the group, though the trusts may also look outside for a candidate with fresh perspective. The group has not yet confirmed a timeline for the appointment, and officials have not commented on the succession process.

Chandrasekaran, who is 63, will leave behind a mixed legacy. Under his leadership, the group consolidated its position in core sectors like steel, autos, and IT, but also faced setbacks in aviation and retail. His tenure was marked by a push into new-age businesses, including superapps and semiconductors, but some of these bets have yet to yield returns.

As the Tata Group prepares for a leadership change, the focus will now shift to the board's ability to find a successor who can balance the demands of the trusts with the interests of minority shareholders. The coming months will reveal whether the group can navigate this transition without further erosion of investor confidence.
