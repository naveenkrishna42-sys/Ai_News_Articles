Bangladesh PM Tarique Rahman raises Hasina extradition with Indian envoy

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tarique Rahman raised Hasina's extradition with Indian envoy
  • India's Ministry of External Affairs confirms request under examination
  • Move signals hardening of Dhaka's stance and tests bilateral ties

Dhaka's Push for Extradition

Bangladesh's Prime Minister Tarique Rahman has formally raised the extradition of former premier Sheikh Hasina with India's high commissioner in Dhaka, escalating a diplomatic standoff that has simmered for months. The meeting, confirmed by both sides on Tuesday, marks the first time the issue has been raised at the highest level of government.

According to officials familiar with the discussion, Rahman pressed for a speedy resolution, citing an existing bilateral extradition treaty between the two neighbours. The Indian envoy, who was summoned to the Prime Minister's Office, listened without committing to a timeline, sources said.

India's Measured Response

New Delhi has so far responded with caution. The Ministry of External Affairs, in a brief statement, said the request is "being examined" under the provisions of the treaty. This formulation, diplomats note, allows India to buy time while assessing legal and political ramifications.

Sheikh Hasina fled to India in August 2024 after a student-led uprising toppled her government, ending her 15-year rule. Since then, she has remained in India, though her exact whereabouts have not been officially confirmed. Dhaka has repeatedly sought her return to face charges including corruption and alleged human rights abuses during her tenure.

Legal Hurdles and Political Calculus

The extradition treaty, signed in 2013, requires both countries to honour requests for return of fugitives. However, Indian legal experts point out that the process is rarely straightforward. "It involves multiple layers of judicial scrutiny," said a former diplomat who declined to be named. "The political signal from Dhaka is clear, but the legal machinery will take its own course."

Observers believe India is weighing its options carefully. A swift extradition could alienate Hasina's political allies and risk instability in Bangladesh, while a refusal could strain ties with the current administration in Dhaka, which has grown increasingly assertive under Rahman's leadership.

Regional Fallout and Memory Politics

The issue has also sparked a war of words in the media. Bangladeshi commentators have framed the extradition demand as a matter of national sovereignty, while Indian analysts warn of the "cost of memory politics" — a reference to how historical grievances are being weaponised on both sides. Some outlets have gone further, suggesting Hasina has "pulled a political masterstroke" by seeking refuge in India, turning herself into a bargaining chip in regional diplomacy.

For now, the Indian government has made no public move to fast-track or reject the request. The silence is telling, say experts, as it reflects a desire to avoid a rupture in relations that are already fragile over border disputes and trade imbalances.

What Happens Next

The ball is now in India's court. Legal proceedings, if initiated, could take months or even years, especially if Hasina's lawyers challenge the extradition on humanitarian grounds. Meanwhile, Rahman's government is expected to keep the pressure on through diplomatic channels, possibly linking the issue to broader bilateral talks.

All eyes will be on New Delhi's next formal response, which may come during the upcoming foreign secretary-level talks between the two countries. Until then, the extradition request remains a test of whether India can balance legal obligations with strategic interests in a rapidly shifting neighbourhood.
