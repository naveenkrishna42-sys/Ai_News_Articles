Karnataka cabinet portfolios allocated; Shivakumar keeps finance, Muniyappa gets social welfare

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Portfolios allocated ahead of Monsoon session; CM keeps finance and cabinet affairs.
  • DK Shivakumar continues as finance minister; KH Muniyappa assigned social welfare.
  • Zameer Ahmed Khan gets housing; cabinet expansion with women inductees is planned.

Bengaluru: The Karnataka cabinet portfolios were finally allocated on Wednesday evening, just a day before the Monsoon session of the legislature is set to begin. Chief Minister Siddaramaiah has retained the finance and cabinet affairs departments, while Deputy Chief Minister DK Shivakumar will continue to hold the finance portfolio, a key responsibility he has managed since the government was formed.

The allocation ends days of speculation and internal jostling among ministers, with several lobbies pushing for plum departments. The Times of India reported that the portfolio list was finalised after multiple rounds of consultations, and the same was shared with the Raj Bhavan late in the day.

Key portfolios and ministers

KH Muniyappa, a veteran Congress leader and former Union minister, has been given the social welfare department. This is being seen as a move to strengthen the party's outreach to backward classes ahead of upcoming elections. Zameer Ahmed Khan, who represents the minority community, has been allotted the housing department.

While the CM has kept the crucial finance and cabinet affairs portfolios, other senior ministers have been assigned key departments. The exact list of all portfolios has been circulated among party leaders, but the official gazette notification is expected shortly.

Timing and political context

The portfolio allocation comes on the eve of the Monsoon session, which is expected to be a stormy affair with the opposition BJP and JD(S) planning to raise several issues, including alleged irregularities in the state administration. The delay in allocating portfolios had drawn criticism from opposition parties, who accused the government of indecisiveness.

Interestingly, The New Indian Express reported that the allocation was initially pushed to Thursday, with some sources citing the observance of amavasye (new moon day) as a factor. However, the government went ahead with the announcement on Wednesday evening, indicating a sense of urgency to have ministers in place before the session begins.

Women's induction in cabinet

In a related development, the state government is planning to induct two to three women ministers into the cabinet. This follows criticism that the current ministry lacks adequate female representation. Sources said the inductions could happen in the coming weeks, though no formal announcement has been made yet.

The move is being viewed as a damage-control exercise after the initial cabinet formation drew flak for the absence of women. The Congress leadership is keen to project a more inclusive image, especially with the 2028 assembly elections on the horizon.

Portfolio allocation has been a delicate balancing act for the CM, who had to accommodate various caste and regional equations. The final list is said to reflect an attempt to satisfy most factions within the ruling party, though some ministers are believed to be unhappy with their departments.

As the Monsoon session gets underway, all eyes will be on how the newly assigned ministers perform, particularly in departments that are likely to face tough questioning from the opposition. The government will also need to ensure that the allocation does not become a new flashpoint within the Congress.

The cabinet's first test will come during the session, where several bills and administrative matters are slated for discussion. With the portfolio logjam cleared, the focus now shifts to governance and delivery.
