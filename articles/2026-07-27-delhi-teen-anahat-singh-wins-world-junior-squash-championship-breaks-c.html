Delhi teen Anahat Singh wins World Junior Squash Championship, breaks 'curse'

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Anahat Singh wins the World Junior Squash Championship, a first for India.
  • She overcame a personal 'curse' of losing in previous finals.
  • PM Modi congratulated her on the historic achievement.
  • Anahat now aims for success at the Asian Games and Olympics.

History in Birmingham

Anahat Singh did not just win a squash match on Monday. The 17-year-old from Delhi demolished a mental block that had haunted her for years. She became the first Indian ever to win the World Junior Squash Championship, defeating top seed Aira Azman of Malaysia in the final in Birmingham.

The victory is a landmark for Indian squash. No Indian, male or female, had ever touched this title. Anahat, the second seed, played with a composure that belied her age, closing out the match in straight games.

The 'Curse' That Almost Broke Her

For Anahat, the path to the crown was littered with painful memories. She had reached the finals of major junior tournaments four times before. She lost all four. She called it her 'curse'.

“I was always the runner-up. It was becoming a mental block,” she told reporters after the win. The pressure of being the favourite had weighed her down. In Birmingham, she decided to treat it as just another match.

A Taunt That Fueled the Fire

The week before the championship, her mentor made a pointed remark. “Are you carrying your braincells to Birmingham?” he asked her. The question was snide, a challenge to her mental toughness. Anahat did not get angry. She got focused.

“He knows me well. He knew I needed a jolt,” she said. The taunt became her motivation. She used it to sharpen her game and silence the doubts that had crept in during those previous final losses.

PM Modi Leads the Praise

Back home, the victory did not go unnoticed. Prime Minister Narendra Modi congratulated Anahat for her “historic feat”. In a post on social media, he called her a “shining star” and praised her dedication and spirit.

The squash community in India erupted. For a sport that often struggles for mainstream attention, this was a moment of breakthrough. Anahat has been a prodigy since she picked up a racket at age five, but this win cements her as a serious contender on the world stage.

Eyes on Asian Games, Olympic Bonus

Anahat is not resting. She has already set her sights on the Asian Games. “That is the immediate target,” she said. She also acknowledged that squash’s inclusion in the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics is an “added bonus”.

She knows the gap between junior success and senior glory is wide. But after breaking the 'curse', she believes she can bridge it. The next stop is the senior circuit, where the competition is older, stronger, and more experienced.

What to watch: Anahat’s transition to senior-level competition. Her next major test will likely be at the Asian Games, where she will face seasoned professionals from Malaysia and Egypt.
