Chennai Teen Dies by Suicide Days After Father's Death

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A 17-year-old boy died by suicide in Chennai, days after his father's death.
  • Police suspect the teen was depressed over the loss of his father.
  • The incident has been reported and an investigation is underway.

Chennai: A 17-year-old boy died by suicide at his home in Chennai, days after his father passed away. Police said the teenager was reportedly depressed following the loss.

The incident came to light on Tuesday morning. The boy's family members found him hanging in his room and rushed him to a nearby hospital, where doctors declared him brought dead.

Police probe into the incident

Police have registered a case and are investigating the circumstances leading to the death. Initial inquiries suggest the teenager was struggling to cope with his father's recent demise.

"The boy was very close to his father and was deeply affected by his death," a police officer said, on condition of anonymity. "We are recording statements from family members and friends to understand what exactly happened."

No suicide note found

According to officials, no suicide note was recovered from the spot. The body has been sent for post-mortem at a government hospital.

The family is yet to give a formal statement. Neighbours described the teenager as a quiet and studious boy who rarely stepped out of his house after his father's death.

Mental health concerns

This tragedy highlights the often-overlooked mental health impact of losing a parent on adolescents. Counsellors say grieving teenagers may exhibit signs of withdrawal, changes in sleep or appetite, and a loss of interest in activities they once enjoyed.

Experts urge families to watch for such behavioural shifts and seek professional help if needed. "The loss of a parent is a profound trauma," a city-based psychologist noted. "It is crucial to create a supportive environment where the child feels safe to express grief."

Help available

Mental health professionals in Chennai have repeatedly stressed the importance of early intervention. Helplines such as Sneha and the state's suicide prevention cell operate round-the-clock for those in distress.

Police have urged people to report any signs of suicidal behaviour to family members or helplines immediately.

The investigation is ongoing, and authorities are expected to file a report with the local magistrate. Further details, including the exact date of the father's death, have not been officially confirmed.

As the family mourns this double tragedy, the focus now shifts to how such incidents can be prevented. Mental health awareness drives in schools and neighbourhoods may offer a way forward.
