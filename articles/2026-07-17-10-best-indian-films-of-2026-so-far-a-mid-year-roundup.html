10 Best Indian Films of 2026 So Far: A Mid-Year Roundup

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • The list includes films from multiple Indian languages, highlighting regional cinema's growing impact.
  • Genre diversity ranges from hard-hitting dramas to lighthearted comedies and experimental narratives.
  • Several debut directors and breakthrough performances feature alongside established filmmakers.

The first half of 2026 has already delivered a remarkable crop of Indian films. From intimate character studies to sweeping spectacles, the lineup proves that Indian cinema continues to push boundaries across languages and genres. Here is a look at ten films that have defined the year so far, as compiled by The Hollywood Reporter India.

A Year of Regional Powerhouses

This year's standout list leans heavily on regional cinema. Malayalam and Tamil films dominate, offering stories that feel both rooted and universal. Chithram 2, a long-awaited sequel, manages to honour its predecessor while carving its own identity. The film blends family drama with sharp social commentary, earning praise for its layered script and natural performances.

Tamil cinema contributed Neelakuyil, a noir thriller set in the backwaters of Kerala. Director Anjali Menon, known for her nuanced storytelling, delivers a taut narrative about guilt and redemption. Critics have called it her most mature work to date.

Bollywood Finds Its Groove

Hindi cinema, after a sluggish 2025, bounced back with two major hits. Gulmohar, a family saga starring a veteran ensemble cast, explores generational conflict in a changing India. The film's quiet power lies in its refusal to take sides, letting the audience sit with uncomfortable truths.

Bandish, a musical drama about a struggling folk singer, became a sleeper hit. Its soundtrack, rooted in Rajasthani folk traditions, has already become a streaming favourite. The lead actor's raw performance earned comparisons to early Irrfan Khan.

Debut Directors and Breakthroughs

Two debut directors made an indelible mark. Kai Po Che! (no relation to the 2013 film) is a Gujarati-language film about three friends navigating life in a small-town textile mill. First-time director Rohan Desai uses handheld cameras and natural light to create an almost documentary-like intimacy.

In Kannada, Mookajjiya Kanasugalu (A Mute Grandmother's Dreams) tells the story of an elderly woman who begins seeing visions after a stroke. Debutant director Shweta Acharya blends magical realism with stark rural reality. The film won the Best Feature Film award at the Bengaluru International Film Festival.

Other Notable Mentions

  • Vettaiyan (Tamil): A police procedural that doubles as a critique of custodial violence.
  • Mithunam (Telugu): A quiet, black-and-white love story set against the backdrop of a drought.
  • Shakuntalam (Hindi-Sanskrit bilingual): An experimental retelling of the ancient play, using VR technology for select sequences.
  • Pulijanmam (Malayalam): A surrealist drama about a man who believes he is a tiger reincarnated.

The diversity of storytelling is striking. Not a single film on this list fits neatly into the commercial masala mould. Even the most accessible titles—like Chithram 2—demand patience and emotional investment from the viewer.

Audiences have responded positively. Box office numbers for regional films in the first half of 2026 show a 22% increase over the same period last year, according to industry tracker Ormax Media.

What is remarkable is the number of films that refuse moral certainty. Characters are flawed, endings ambiguous. This moral complexity suggests Indian cinema is maturing—moving away from didactic storytelling toward something more reflective of real life.

The second half of 2026 promises more ambitious releases, including big-budget spectacles from directors like SS Rajamouli and Anurag Kashyap. If the first six months are any indication, the year could go down as one of Indian cinema's finest.
