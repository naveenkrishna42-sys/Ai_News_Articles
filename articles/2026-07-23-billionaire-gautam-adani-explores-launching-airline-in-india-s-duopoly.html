Billionaire Gautam Adani explores launching airline in India's duopoly market

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Adani Group is evaluating a foray into the airline business amid potential regulatory changes.
  • India's aviation market is dominated by IndiGo and Air India, forming a duopoly.
  • The government is considering reforms to allow airport owners to also operate airlines.
  • Adani Group has written to the Airports Authority of India seeking relaxed entry norms.

Billionaire Gautam Adani is exploring a move into India's airline business, according to sources familiar with the matter. The Adani Group is weighing a launch as the central government considers lifting restrictions that currently bar airport operators from running airlines.

India's aviation market is dominated by two players — IndiGo and the Tata Group-owned Air India. Together, they control over 80% of domestic air traffic. This duopoly has led to rising fares and limited choices for flyers.

Potential regulatory shift opens door

The Union government is examining a proposal to scrap rules that prevent companies holding airport concessions from also operating airlines. The move, if implemented, would allow conglomerates like Adani to enter the sector directly.

Sources told Reuters that the Adani Group has written to the Airports Authority of India (AAI) arguing for relaxed entry norms. The group, which already runs seven airports across the country including Mumbai and Ahmedabad, sees synergies between airport management and airline operations.

The Ministry of Civil Aviation has not commented on the development. However, reports suggest the ministry is internally discussing a broad set of aviation reforms aimed at breaking the current duopoly.

Why Adani wants in

The Adani Group has transformed into a diversified infrastructure behemoth under Gautam Adani, with interests spanning ports, energy, data centres and media. An airline would complete its transportation ecosystem.

Industry analysts point out that running an airline allows an airport operator to capture more value from passenger traffic. It also provides leverage in negotiating with existing carriers over slot allocations and terminal usage fees.

The group's airport network handled over 100 million passengers in the last financial year. An in-house airline could feed traffic into these hubs, reducing dependence on third-party carriers.

Challenges in a capital-intensive business

Starting an airline in India is not easy. The sector is notorious for thin margins, high fuel costs and intense price competition. Several carriers have collapsed over the past decade, including Jet Airways and Kingfisher Airlines.

Capital requirement for a new airline runs into hundreds of crores. The Adani Group, with its deep pockets, can absorb initial losses. But it would still need to secure aircraft, crew, maintenance infrastructure and airport slots — all in short supply.

Regulatory hurdles aside, the group will also face entrenched competition. IndiGo and Air India have vast networks, loyalty programs and cost advantages built over years.

What the government plans

The BusinessLine newspaper reported that the Centre plans sweeping aviation reforms to ease airline entry. Bloomberg added that New Delhi is considering letting airport owners run airlines to curb the duopoly.

CNBC-TV18 reported that the Adani Group has already written to the AAI demanding rule changes. The group argues that the current policy restricts competition and innovation.

A formal decision from the ministry is expected in the coming months. The changes would need cabinet approval and possible amendments to the Aircraft Act.

If the rules are relaxed, other airport operators like GMR Group — which runs Delhi and Hyderabad airports — could also enter the fray. That could reshape India's aviation map entirely.

The Adani Group has not confirmed any specific timeline for a potential launch. Sources said the company is in early exploratory stages and a final decision hinges on the government's reform push.

All eyes are now on the Civil Aviation Ministry's next move. If the gates open, Adani could become the third major force in Indian skies — changing the game for millions of fliers.
