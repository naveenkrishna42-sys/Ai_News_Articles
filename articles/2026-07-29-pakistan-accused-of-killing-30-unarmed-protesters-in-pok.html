Pakistan accused of killing 30 unarmed protesters in PoK

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pakistan security forces accused of killing at least 30 unarmed protesters in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK).
  • Indian political parties including CPI(M) and PDP have condemned the killings and called for international intervention.
  • India has slammed the PoK elections as a cosmetic exercise to legitimise what it calls an illegal occupation.

Deadly crackdown in PoK sparks international outcry

Pakistan stands accused of shooting dead at least 30 unarmed protesters in Pakistan-occupied Kashmir (PoK), triggering sharp condemnation from India and several Kashmiri political groups. The incident, which took place during a crackdown on demonstrations, has reignited debate over the region's disputed status and the legitimacy of ongoing local elections.

Reports from the ground remain difficult to verify independently due to restricted media access. However, multiple sources, including local activists and cross-border media, have cited security forces opening fire on crowds protesting against alleged electoral manipulation and economic hardship.

Political reaction: CPI(M) and PDP demand accountability

The Communist Party of India (Marxist) and the Jammu and Kashmir Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) have jointly condemned the killings. Both parties have called on the international community to investigate what they describe as a gross violation of human rights.

In a statement, the CPI(M) said the firing on unarmed civilians was an act of 'state terrorism' aimed at suppressing democratic dissent. The PDP echoed these sentiments, urging the United Nations to intervene and hold the Pakistani military establishment accountable for what it termed a 'massacre'.

These reactions from Indian political parties underscore the deep sensitivity surrounding events in PoK, a territory that both India and Pakistan claim in full but control only in part.

India's position: Elections branded a 'cosmetic exercise'

New Delhi has responded sharply, dismissing the ongoing PoK elections as a 'cosmetic drive' designed to mask what it calls Pakistan's illegal occupation of the region. The Ministry of External Affairs issued a statement accusing Islamabad of systematic human rights abuses and denying the people of PoK their right to genuine self-determination.

India has consistently maintained that the entire former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir is an integral part of its territory. New Delhi argues that any political process conducted under Pakistani authority lacks legitimacy and constitutes a violation of bilateral agreements and UN resolutions.

This latest incident provides fresh ammunition for India's diplomatic offensive, as it seeks to isolate Pakistan on the international stage over its handling of the disputed region.

Khawaja Asif's defence: 'Protesters are enemies like India'

In a controversial statement that has further inflamed tensions, Pakistan's Defence Minister Khawaja Asif reportedly defended the security forces' actions. He was quoted as saying that protesters in PoK were 'enemies like India' and that the state was justified in using lethal force to maintain order.

The remarks, widely circulated by Indian media, have been interpreted as an official endorsement of the crackdown. Asif's framing of civilian dissent as an extension of the conflict with India has alarmed human rights groups, who say it encourages further repression.

Analysts note that such rhetoric risks deepening the divide between the Pakistani state and the people of PoK, many of whom have expressed growing frustration over governance failures and lack of basic amenities.

Why this matters: A flashpoint in an already volatile region

The killings come at a delicate time. The PoK elections, which Pakistan presents as evidence of democratic governance, are being held amidst allegations of widespread rigging and voter intimidation. The bloodshed threatens to delegitimise the entire process and could fuel a new wave of unrest.

For India, the incident is a propaganda opportunity to highlight what it describes as the 'true face' of Pakistani rule in Kashmir. For Pakistan, it risks international censure and further strains ties with the Kashmiri population on its side of the Line of Control.

Human rights organisations have called for an independent inquiry, but with both countries entrenched in their positions, accountability appears unlikely. The global community, already preoccupied with other crises, has yet to issue a strong collective response.

What to watch: Whether the unrest spreads beyond PoK into the Kashmir Valley, and whether the international community, particularly the UN and major powers, will press for a credible investigation. The outcome of the PoK elections and the level of voter turnout will also be a key indicator of the regime's perceived legitimacy in the region.
