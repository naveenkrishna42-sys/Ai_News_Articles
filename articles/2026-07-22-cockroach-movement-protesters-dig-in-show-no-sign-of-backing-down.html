Cockroach movement protesters dig in, show no sign of backing down

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Thousands of Cockroach movement protesters remain defiant at Delhi's Jantar Mantar after a police crackdown.
  • The movement demands the resignation of the Chief Justice of India over alleged misconduct.
  • Police used tear gas and water cannons to stop a march on Parliament, but protesters vow to stay.

The return to Jantar Mantar

Delhi's Jantar Mantar, a historic observatory that has long served as a venue for protests, is once again packed with demonstrators. Thousands of supporters of the so-called "Cockroach movement" have gathered there, showing no signs of backing down despite a heavy police crackdown earlier this week.

Protesters, many carrying brooms and wearing black armbands, chanted slogans demanding the resignation of the Chief Justice of India. The movement, which began as a small online campaign, has swelled into a sustained street protest that has drawn comparisons to previous mass agitations.

What is the Cockroach movement?

The Cockroach movement derives its name from a remark made by a senior government official who, in a leaked audio clip, referred to protesters as "cockroaches." The phrase was quickly adopted by demonstrators as a badge of defiance, turning an intended insult into a rallying cry.

The core demand of the movement is the resignation of the Chief Justice of India over allegations of judicial misconduct, including claims of nepotism and interference in case assignments. The allegations, which have been widely circulated on social media, have not been substantiated in any official inquiry. The Chief Justice has denied all wrongdoing and has refused to step down.

The protest has drawn support from a cross-section of society — lawyers, students, civil society groups, and opposition party workers. While no single political party has formally endorsed the movement, several opposition leaders have made appearances at Jantar Mantar.

Police action and the march on Parliament

The situation escalated sharply when protesters attempted to march from Jantar Mantar to Parliament, where a debate on judicial accountability was scheduled. Police blocked the route using barricades, tear gas, and water cannons, leading to scuffles and multiple arrests.

According to the Delhi Police, the march was unauthorised and violated Section 144 of the CrPC, which prohibits unlawful assembly. Protest leaders countered that the right to peacefully assemble is guaranteed under Article 19 of the Constitution. In a statement, the police said they acted to prevent a breach of public order, but critics argue the response was disproportionately harsh.

Video footage from the scene shows a lone woman standing at a petrol pump near the protest site, facing down a line of police in riot gear. That image has gone viral, becoming one of the movement's most potent symbols.

Why this matters

The Cockroach movement comes at a time of heightened scrutiny of India's judiciary. While the Supreme Court has historically enjoyed high public trust, recent controversies — including allegations of corruption in the lower judiciary and delays in case disposal — have eroded some of that confidence.

The demand for the Chief Justice's resignation is unprecedented in India's history. No Chief Justice has ever been forced out by public protests. The movement, therefore, is testing the limits of judicial accountability and the resilience of the institution's independence.

There is also a political dimension. The ruling party has largely stayed silent on the protests, though its leaders have occasionally criticised the judiciary in other contexts. Opposition parties see an opportunity to put the government on the defensive over institutional integrity. The outcome of this protest could shape the discourse around judicial reforms in the coming months.

What to watch

The immediate flashpoint remains Jantar Mantar. Protest organisers have announced a series of daily demonstrations and a potential nationwide strike if their demands are not met. The Supreme Court is expected to hear a petition filed by a lawyer challenging the police action, which could set the legal parameters for the protest.

For now, the movement shows no signs of fizzling out. Whether it can sustain momentum and translate public anger into institutional change will depend on the government's response and the judiciary's willingness to address the underlying grievances. The coming week will be crucial.
