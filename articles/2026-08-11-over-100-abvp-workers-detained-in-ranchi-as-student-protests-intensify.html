Over 100 ABVP Workers Detained in Ranchi as Student Protests Intensify

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 100 ABVP workers detained during march to Jharkhand Assembly
  • Six layers of barricades placed for 5-km march after Ranchi violence
  • MP Parimal Nathwani urges CM Hemant Soren for dialogue
  • CJP hails protest spearhead Devendra Nath Mahto; BJP questions Congress

Ranchi, August 11: Tensions ran high in Jharkhand's capital on Tuesday as over 100 ABVP workers were detained by police during a planned march to the state Assembly. The detention came amid escalating student protests over alleged police action on students, with authorities erecting six layers of barricades along the five-kilometre route.

Protest Route Sealed Off

The heavy security deployment effectively sealed off the Assembly complex, turning the city into a fortress of sorts. Police personnel, equipped with riot gear, were stationed at every major intersection, while barricades made of iron and concrete were stacked in multiple layers to prevent any breach.

Witnesses described a tense atmosphere as protesters, many of them young students, attempted to push forward but were stopped at the first barricade itself. The detentions were carried out swiftly, with police citing preventive measures to avoid a repeat of the violence that unfolded in Ranchi earlier.

How the Ranchi Violence Unfolded

The current protests trace back to a lathi charge on students that sparked outrage across the state. Reports suggest that the initial police action was triggered by a student demonstration that turned unruly, leading to injuries on both sides. The incident has since snowballed into a major political flashpoint, with opposition parties demanding accountability.

According to local reports, the administration had anticipated a large turnout and prepared accordingly. The six layers of barricades, a rare sight in Ranchi, underscored the administration's determination to prevent any escalation. Despite the heavy security, the sheer number of protesters managed to create significant disruptions in the city's traffic and daily life.

Political Reactions Pour In

The protests have drawn sharp reactions from political leaders across the spectrum. Union Minister and Rajya Sabha MP Parimal Nathwani urged Chief Minister Hemant Soren to resolve the students' demands through dialogue. In a statement, Nathwani emphasised that peaceful resolution was the only way forward and that the state government must listen to the grievances of the youth.

Meanwhile, the Central Jharkhand Party (CJP) hailed Devendra Nath Mahto, the spearhead of the Ranchi protest, calling him a symbol of student resistance. The BJP, on the other hand, has targeted the Congress, questioning its silence on the issue. In Jammu and Kashmir, the Leader of Opposition extended support to the Jharkhand students while attacking the Opposition for what he called 'double standards' in their responses to similar situations elsewhere.

Political observers note that the protests have become a rallying point for the opposition, with the BJP and other parties using the issue to corner the Hemant Soren-led government. The Congress, however, has maintained a measured response, leading to criticism from its rivals.

What Happens Next?

With over 100 detentions already made, the immediate focus remains on the safety of the detained students and the possibility of further protests. The administration has not yet confirmed the exact number of those detained or the charges they may face. Officials have indicated that the situation is under control but remain cautious about the days ahead.

The coming week will be crucial as student bodies plan to hold more demonstrations if their demands are not met. The state government faces the dual challenge of maintaining law and order while addressing the legitimate concerns of the student community. All eyes are now on Chief Minister Hemant Soren's next move, with many hoping that dialogue, rather than force, will prevail.
