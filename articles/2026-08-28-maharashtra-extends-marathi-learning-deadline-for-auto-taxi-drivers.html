Maharashtra extends Marathi learning deadline for auto, taxi drivers

📅 2026-08-28 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • 1-year extension granted for auto and taxi drivers to learn Marathi
  • Political tensions and legal challenges surrounding the requirement
  • Union minister warns against cancelling licenses for Marathi illiteracy

Government Extends Deadline Amid Political and Legal Debates

Maharashtra Chief Minister Eknath Shinde has announced a 1-year extension for auto and taxi drivers to learn Marathi, amid growing political tensions and legal challenges. The decision comes after weeks of debate over the state's requirement that drivers be proficient in the regional language.

Political Tensions and Legal Challenges

The requirement has sparked a heated political debate, with the Shiv Sena accusing the BJP-led government of trying to undermine the party's influence. The Union Minister for Road Transport and Highways, Nitin Gadkari, has warned that cancelling drivers' licenses for not knowing Marathi would be unconstitutional.

Union Minister's Warning

Union Minister Nitin Gadkari has stated that the government cannot cancel drivers' licenses based on their proficiency in Marathi. He argued that the requirement is discriminatory and violates the constitutional rights of the drivers.

Public Reaction and Support

Many drivers have expressed their support for the requirement, arguing that it is essential for maintaining the state's cultural identity. However, others have raised concerns about the practicality of the requirement, given the time constraints and the lack of resources for learning Marathi.

Broader Implications and Future Outlook

The decision has significant implications for the future of the transport sector in Maharashtra. The extension of the deadline may provide some relief to the drivers, but it also raises questions about the long-term viability of the requirement.

Impact on Transport Sector

The requirement has already had a significant impact on the transport sector, with many drivers struggling to meet the deadline. The extension of the deadline may provide some relief, but it also highlights the need for a more comprehensive approach to addressing the issue.

Cultural and Social Considerations

The requirement is also a reflection of the broader cultural and social considerations in Maharashtra. The state's government has argued that the requirement is essential for maintaining the state's cultural identity and promoting the use of Marathi in the transport sector.
