11-Year-Old Indian Student Identifies Three Preliminary Asteroids

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Atharv Sood, 11, discovered three potential asteroids.
  • The discovery was made during an international space observation campaign.
  • These detections are currently classified as preliminary.

A Young Astronomer’s Discovery

At an age when most children are focused on school projects or sports, 11-year-old Atharv Sood has turned his attention to the night sky. The student has been credited with the preliminary detection of three asteroids, a significant achievement that highlights the growing interest in space science among Indian youth.

Sood participated in a citizen science programme designed to engage students in real-world astronomical research. By analysing data sets provided through international observation campaigns, he identified patterns that led to the identification of these celestial bodies.

The Process of Detection

Identifying an asteroid requires patience and a keen eye for detail. Participants in these programmes receive images captured by professional-grade telescopes located across the globe. By comparing these images, students look for objects that move against the fixed background of distant stars.

Sood utilised specialised software to track these potential asteroids. The process involves identifying a light source that shifts its position across multiple exposures. Once a candidate is identified, the data is submitted to global databases for verification by professional astronomers.

Preliminary Status Explained

While the detections are a major personal milestone for Sood, they remain classified as preliminary. In astronomical terms, this means the objects have been spotted, but they require additional observation to confirm their orbits and physical characteristics.

Professional observatories are now tasked with performing follow-up checks. This verification process can take several weeks or even months. During this time, the data is cross-referenced with existing catalogues to ensure the objects have not been previously documented.

Encouraging Youth in Science

The success of student-led discovery programmes demonstrates how accessible space science has become. By lowering the barrier to entry, these initiatives allow children to contribute to legitimate scientific research. Experts suggest that such hands-on experiences are crucial for fostering a long-term interest in STEM fields.

Sood’s achievement has drawn attention to the effectiveness of global collaboration in science. As more students participate, the collective effort helps bridge the gap between amateur observation and professional discovery. These programmes continue to provide a platform for young minds to engage with the complexities of our solar system.

Future updates will depend on the results of the follow-up observations currently underway at professional research facilities.
