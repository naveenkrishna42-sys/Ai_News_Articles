Cauvery row: Karnataka bandh on Aug 13 sees waning support from key bodies

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vatal Nagaraj has announced a Karnataka bandh on August 13 over the Cauvery water-sharing dispute.
  • Several organisations, including the Karnataka Associated Managements of Schools (KAMS), have withdrawn their support for the shutdown.
  • Schools in Karnataka are likely to remain open on August 13, according to KAMS.
  • The bandh is expected to have a partial impact, with essential services and schools functioning normally.

Bandh call meets resistance

Kannada activist Vatal Nagaraj has called for a statewide bandh in Karnataka on August 13 to protest the Cauvery water-sharing row. But as the shutdown date nears, several key organisations have distanced themselves from the call, signalling a possible softening of support.

The Karnataka Associated Managements of Schools (KAMS) has withdrawn its earlier decision to close schools on the bandh day. This effectively means most educational institutions are likely to remain open on Thursday, a move that could significantly blunt the impact of the shutdown.

Schools to stay open

KAMS, which represents a large number of private schools across the state, issued a statement on Wednesday clarifying that schools will function as usual. The decision comes as a relief to parents and students, many of whom had been uncertain about the bandh's effect on academic schedules.

Other organisations, too, have chosen to back off from the bandh call, though the reasons vary. Some have cited logistical challenges, while others have expressed concerns about the economic disruption a full shutdown could cause in Bengaluru and other urban centres.

What will be impacted

Despite the reduced support, the bandh is expected to have some impact, particularly in rural areas and among transport operators. Auto and cab unions in certain districts have indicated they may not operate, though no official confirmation has been provided.

Essential services such as hospitals, pharmacies, and milk supply are expected to function normally. The state government has not yet issued an official statement on the bandh, but officials have indicated that they are prepared to maintain law and order.

Background of the row

The Cauvery water-sharing dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu has been a long-standing issue, with both states staking claims on the river's waters. The latest flare-up comes amid a reported shortfall in rainfall in the Cauvery basin, intensifying demands from Karnataka farmers for a larger share.

Vatal Nagaraj, a prominent pro-Kannada activist, has been at the forefront of several such agitations in the past. His bandh call on August 13 is aimed at pressuring the government to take a stronger stance in the ongoing talks with Tamil Nadu.

Mixed response from trade bodies

While some trade associations have extended support, others have remained non-committal. The Federation of Karnataka Chambers of Commerce and Industry (FKCCI) has not issued a formal statement, but sources suggest it is unlikely to back the shutdown fully.

Smaller business groups in Bengaluru's markets have said they will decide on Thursday morning based on the ground situation. This uncertainty reflects a broader trend where bandh calls in recent years have seen diminishing participation, especially in urban areas.

As the day approaches, all eyes are on the state government's response and whether it will issue any advisory to schools and offices. For now, the message from KAMS is clear: classes will go on.

The coming hours will determine whether the bandh gains any traction or fizzles out as a symbolic protest. Watch for any last-minute announcements from the government or activist groups that could alter the situation.
