Over 2 Million Indian Students Retake Medical Exam After Paper Leak

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • More than 2 million students are resitting the NEET-UG medical entrance exam.
  • The retest follows an alleged leak of the question paper before the original exam.
  • Protests have erupted across multiple states, with students demanding a fair process.
  • The government has launched an investigation and arrested several individuals.

Over 2 million students across India are sitting for a retest of the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET-UG) today, after authorities confirmed an alleged leak of the question paper. The unprecedented move has thrown the academic calendar into disarray and sparked widespread protests.

The original examination, held earlier this month, was scrapped following reports that the paper had been circulated on social media platforms hours before the test. The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) has taken over the probe.

Alleged Leak Sparks Nationwide Retest

The decision to conduct a retest was announced by the Ministry of Education late last week. Officials stated that the integrity of the examination process had been compromised, necessitating the fresh round of testing.

Students, many of whom had travelled long distances to their original exam centres, expressed frustration and anxiety. "I studied for two years for this. Now I have to go through the stress again," said a candidate waiting outside a centre in Delhi.

The leak is believed to have originated from a testing centre in Bihar. Police have arrested at least four individuals, including a middleman and a former employee of the testing agency, for their alleged involvement.

Protests Erupt Across States

In several cities, including Patna, Jaipur, and Lucknow, students have held demonstrations demanding a thorough investigation. Some protesters accused the government of failing to secure the exam process.

"This is not just about one paper. It's about the future of millions of students," said a protest leader in Patna. The demonstrations have largely remained peaceful, though traffic was disrupted in some areas.

The National Testing Agency (NTA), which conducts NEET-UG, has faced sharp criticism. Opposition parties have called for the resignation of the Education Minister, accusing the government of negligence.

Impact on Medical Admissions

The retest is expected to delay the admission process for medical colleges across the country by at least a month. The NEET-UG is the sole gateway for undergraduate medical and dental courses in India.

With over 2.2 million candidates vying for roughly 90,000 seats, the competition is fierce. Any disruption in the schedule can have a cascading effect on the academic year for aspiring doctors.

Medical education experts have warned that the leak and retest could erode trust in the examination system. "This is a major blow to the credibility of one of the country's most important exams," said a former NTA official.

What Happens Next

The retest is being held at thousands of centres nationwide, with strict security measures in place. The CBI investigation continues, and more arrests are expected.

For now, over 2 million students are once again attempting the exam, hoping that this time, the system holds.
