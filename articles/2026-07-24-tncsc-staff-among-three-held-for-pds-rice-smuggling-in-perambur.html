TNCSC staff among three held for PDS rice smuggling in Perambur

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two TNCSC staff and a private agent arrested in Perambur
  • PDS rice meant for ration card holders allegedly diverted to open market
  • Police seized rice stock and vehicles during the raid

Arrests Made in Perambur Raid

Chennai police have arrested three individuals, including two employees of the Tamil Nadu Civil Supplies Corporation (TNCSC), for their alleged involvement in smuggling Public Distribution System (PDS) rice. The arrests were made in the Perambur area following a tip-off.

The accused have been identified as TNCSC staff members and a private agent. Officials said the group was illegally diverting rice meant for ration card holders and selling it in the open market for profit.

How the Operation Unfolded

Acting on specific intelligence, a police team raided a location in Perambur late on Thursday night. During the operation, they seized a significant quantity of PDS rice that was being loaded onto vehicles for transport.

Police also confiscated two vehicles allegedly used in the smuggling operation. The exact quantity of rice seized is yet to be officially confirmed, but preliminary estimates suggest it runs into several bags.

Modus Operandi

Investigators believe the accused had been operating for some time, exploiting their positions within the TNCSC to divert subsidised grain. The rice was reportedly being sold to traders who would then sell it at market rates, bypassing the intended beneficiaries.

Authorities are now examining records to determine how long the racket had been active and whether more individuals are involved. The arrested persons have been booked under relevant sections of law and remanded to judicial custody.

Broader Context of PDS Leakage

The arrest comes amid ongoing efforts by the state government to plug leakages in the PDS system. Food smuggling from the PDS has been a recurring issue in parts of Tamil Nadu, with corrupt staff and middlemen often colluding to siphon off supplies.

In recent months, similar cases have been reported in other districts, prompting the administration to tighten monitoring and conduct surprise checks at godowns and distribution points.

Officials have not yet confirmed if the seized rice was part of a larger consignment or if any higher-ups in the supply chain are under scrutiny.

Police are continuing their investigation and have not ruled out further arrests as they trace the network of buyers and sellers involved in the illegal trade.

The case has once again highlighted the vulnerability of the PDS system to exploitation, even as the government works to ensure food security for the poor.
