Indian-origin astronaut Anil Menon completes first ISS spacewalk

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Anil Menon and Jessica Meir performed a 6.5-hour spacewalk on August 6, 2026.
  • The spacewalk focused on modifications to prep the ISS for future solar array upgrades.
  • This was Menon's first spacewalk, a significant personal and professional milestone.
  • The work is part of ongoing efforts to boost the station's power supply.

First Spacewalk for Menon

NASA astronauts Anil Menon and Jessica Meir stepped outside the International Space Station on Friday for a 6.5-hour spacewalk to prepare for upcoming solar array upgrades. For Menon, an Indian-origin astronaut, it was a historic maiden spacewalk.

The duo exited the Quest airlock and began work on the station's exterior, focusing on modifications needed for the installation of new solar arrays. NASA confirmed the start of the spacewalk in a brief statement, noting the crew was in good health and proceeding with the planned tasks.

Menon, who has Indian roots, has become a figure of pride for many in India and the diaspora. His first spacewalk places him among a select group of astronauts who have performed the demanding task of working outside the orbital laboratory.

Preparing for Solar Array Upgrades

The primary goal of Friday's spacewalk was to prep the station for future solar array installations. The ISS relies on a set of large solar panels to generate power, and these have degraded over time. The new arrays are designed to boost the station's power supply significantly.

Meir, a veteran of multiple spacewalks, led the operations alongside Menon. The pair worked methodically, moving along the station's truss structure as they secured brackets and made electrical connections.

NASA has not yet disclosed the exact timeline for the next solar array installation mission, but this spacewalk was a critical preparatory step. The agency has been upgrading the station's power systems to support expanded research and operations through the decade.

A Milestone for Indian Space Enthusiasts

Menon's achievement has been closely watched in India, where space fans have followed his journey from selection to flight. His spacewalk was covered extensively by Indian media outlets, including The Hindu and Deccan Chronicle, which reported on the event as it unfolded.

While Menon is not the first person of Indian origin to walk in space, his mission carries symbolic weight. It comes at a time when India's own human spaceflight program, Gaganyaan, is gearing up for its first crewed mission.

Officials have not yet commented on whether Menon will participate in additional spacewalks during this expedition. But the successful completion of his first outing is likely to lead to more responsibilities aboard the station.

What Happens Next

The crew will now review the data from Friday's spacewalk and assess the work completed. Additional spacewalks are expected in the coming months to continue the solar array upgrade process.

For Menon, this is just the beginning of what could be a long and distinguished career in spaceflight. As the ISS continues to operate, and with future lunar missions on the horizon, his experience will be invaluable.

Watch for NASA's next announcement on solar array installation timelines and any follow-up spacewalks.
