Typhoon Dolphin: Over 10 Lakh Evacuated as Eastern China Braces for Impact

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over 1 million people evacuated in eastern China
  • Nearly 950 flights grounded in Shanghai
  • Red alert issued as Typhoon Dolphin makes landfall
  • Heavy rain and strong winds batter coastal regions

Massive Evacuation Underway

Eastern China is reeling under the onslaught of Typhoon Dolphin, which has forced the evacuation of more than ten lakh people from vulnerable coastal areas. The tropical storm made landfall with ferocious intensity, packing heavy rain and gale-force winds that have disrupted daily life across the region.

Authorities ordered large-scale evacuations as a precautionary measure, moving residents from low-lying areas and flood-prone districts. The scale of the operation underscores the severity of the storm, which has been classified as a red alert—China's highest weather warning level.

Flight Disruptions in Shanghai

The impact on travel has been immediate and severe. Nearly 950 flights at Shanghai's airports have been grounded, leaving thousands of passengers stranded. Airlines have advised travellers to check schedules before heading to terminals, as cancellations and delays are expected to continue through the day.

Shanghai, a major global hub, has seen its aviation network grind to a halt as the storm churns along the coast. Rail services have also been affected, with several high-speed train routes suspended temporarily to ensure passenger safety.

Red Alert as Storm Intensifies

Meteorological departments have issued a red alert, warning of torrential downpours and wind speeds that could topple trees and damage infrastructure. The storm's trajectory has been closely monitored, with officials urging residents to remain indoors and avoid unnecessary travel.

Emergency response teams have been deployed to the worst-affected areas, equipped with rescue boats and relief supplies. Local governments are working around the clock to restore power and clear debris as the storm moves inland.

Regional Impact and Response

Beyond Shanghai, several provinces along the eastern seaboard have reported flooding and property damage. Agricultural fields are submerged, raising concerns about crop losses in a region that is vital to China's food supply chain. Authorities have not yet released official casualty figures, but reports suggest injuries have been limited due to the timely evacuations.

The storm has also disrupted maritime activities, with ports closed and fishing vessels docked. Coastal communities, accustomed to seasonal typhoons, are bracing for prolonged outages and supply chain interruptions.

Looking Ahead

As Typhoon Dolphin weakens gradually, the focus will shift to recovery and damage assessment. Relief operations are expected to intensify over the next 48 hours, with the government pledging support for affected families. Meteorologists are tracking a potential second wave of rain, urging continued vigilance.

For now, eastern China remains on high alert, with the full extent of the damage yet to be quantified. The coming days will reveal how the region copes with the aftermath of one of the most powerful storms to hit its shores this year.
