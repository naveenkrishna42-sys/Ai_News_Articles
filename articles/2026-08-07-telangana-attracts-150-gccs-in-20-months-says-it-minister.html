Telangana attracts 150 GCCs in 20 months, says IT Minister

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • 150 GCCs set up in Telangana in last 20 months
  • IT Minister credits policy and infrastructure for influx
  • State aims to strengthen position as global tech hub

GCCs flock to Telangana

Telangana has drawn 150 global capability centres (GCCs) over the past 20 months, IT Minister announced on Thursday. The figure underscores the state's sustained pull for multinational companies seeking to base their technology and innovation operations in India.

The Minister, speaking at an industry event in Hyderabad, said the influx reflects the state's stable policy environment, robust digital infrastructure, and skilled talent pool. He noted that the GCCs span sectors including software, engineering, analytics, and research and development.

What are GCCs and why they matter

GCCs are offshore units set up by global firms to handle functions such as IT services, product development, and business processes. They have become a key driver of employment and high-value work in Indian cities, competing with traditional IT outsourcing.

For Telangana, each new GCC typically brings hundreds of specialised jobs, boosting the local economy and cementing Hyderabad's reputation as a destination for knowledge-intensive work. Industry trackers say Hyderabad now ranks among the top three Indian cities for GCC setups, alongside Bengaluru and Pune.

Policy push and infrastructure

The Minister attributed the achievement to a mix of proactive state policies, including single-window clearances and sector-specific incentives. He also pointed to uninterrupted power, reliable connectivity, and a growing network of tech parks and innovation hubs.

State officials have been aggressively courting multinationals through roadshows and partnership agreements. The effort appears to be paying off, with the 20-month tally exceeding initial targets set by the state's IT department.

Challenges and competition

Despite the momentum, Telangana faces competition from other states offering similar incentives, and from global headwinds such as tightening tech budgets. The Minister acknowledged the need to continuously upgrade skills and infrastructure to retain the edge.

He also stressed the importance of building a pipeline of local engineering and management talent, noting that the state is working with universities and training institutes to align curricula with industry needs.

What to watch

Officials are expected to release a detailed breakdown of the 150 GCCs, including investment figures and job creation data, in the coming weeks. The state has set its sights on attracting more GCCs in emerging fields such as artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and green technology. Whether it can sustain the pace will depend on execution and global market conditions.
