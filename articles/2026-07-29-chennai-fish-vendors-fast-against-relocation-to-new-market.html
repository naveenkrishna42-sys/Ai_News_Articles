Chennai fish vendors fast against relocation to new market

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vendors at Nochikuppam started an indefinite fast on Tuesday.
  • They oppose the Chennai Corporation's relocation plan to Koyambedu market.
  • Vendors say the new site is far from their customer base and will destroy livelihoods.

Vendors begin indefinite fast at Nochikuppam

Hundreds of fish vendors at Chennai's Nochikuppam market launched an indefinite fast on Tuesday. They are protesting against the Chennai Corporation's decision to relocate them to a new facility in Koyambedu.

The vendors gathered at the market early in the morning. They sat on a dharna, refusing food and water, demanding that the civic body withdraw its relocation order.

Relocation plan sparks anger

The Corporation had recently announced plans to shift all vendors from Nochikuppam to a newly constructed fish market in Koyambedu. Officials say the move is aimed at decongesting the area and improving hygiene standards.

But the vendors argue that the new location is far from their traditional customer base. Many of them have been selling fish at Nochikuppam for decades and rely on walk-in customers from nearby residential areas.

'This will destroy our livelihoods'

M. Selvi, a vendor who has been at Nochikuppam for 25 years, said the relocation would be disastrous. “Our customers are here. If we go to Koyambedu, we will lose them. Who will travel that far to buy fish?” she asked.

Another vendor, K. Ramesh, said the new market would also mean higher rent and transport costs. “We are poor people. We cannot afford these changes,” he said.

The vendors have submitted a memorandum to the Chennai Corporation commissioner. They have demanded that the relocation plan be cancelled and that the existing market be upgraded instead.

Police deployed, situation peaceful

Police personnel were deployed at the market to prevent any untoward incident. So far, the protest has remained peaceful. No arrests have been reported.

The Corporation has not yet responded to the vendors' demands. Officials said they would study the memorandum and take a decision soon.

The fast is expected to continue until the Corporation agrees to hold talks. The vendors have said they will not back down until their demands are met.

What happens next depends on the civic body's response. If no solution is reached, the protest could escalate in the coming days.
