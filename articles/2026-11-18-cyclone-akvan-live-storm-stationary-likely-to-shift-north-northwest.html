Cyclone Akvan Live: Storm Stationary, Likely to Shift North-Northwest

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Akvan is currently stationary and is expected to drift slowly north-northwestwards, prolonging rain and rough-sea conditions.
  • Wind gusts of up to 145 kmph have been flagged for the Andhra Pradesh coast, raising risks to kutcha houses, power lines and crops.
  • Odisha has identified 2.04 lakh people for possible evacuation, signalling that district administrations are pre-positioning relief resources.

Current status: Akvan holds position over the west-central Bay

The latest bulletin on Cyclone Akvan describes the system as stationary, with a slow drift north-northwestwards expected next. A stalled or slow-moving cyclone is the more dangerous scenario for rainfall, because the same rain bands keep sitting over the same coastal belts instead of moving inland and weakening quickly.

Why the track matters more than the label

A north-northwestward drift points threat toward the north Andhra Pradesh–Odisha coast, but small changes in steering winds can shift the landfall point by tens of kilometres. That is exactly why district-level, not state-level, advisories should drive your decisions on evacuation and fishing.

Wind, rain and sea state

Reports circulating with the updates warn of gusts up to 145 kmph, which is enough to snap power feeders, flatten thatched and tin-roof structures, and bring down trees onto roads. Expect waterlogging in low-lying towns, localized flooding of rivulets, and unsafe swell conditions where waves break over harbour walls.

Official classification and timing

The precise IMD category, exact landfall window and district-wise rainfall figures were not confirmed in the updates reviewed here. Treat any specific time or category circulating on social media as unverified until the India Meteorological Department or the state disaster management authority confirms it.

State-wise preparedness

Odisha

The state government has identified 2.04 lakh people for possible evacuation, a large enough number to imply multiphase relocation using buses, trucks and school buildings as shelters. Historically, the first 24-48 hours decide whether shelters fill once or twice, so families should pre-pack medicines, documents in waterproof covers, drinking water and baby or elder-care supplies.

Andhra Pradesh

Coastal districts should assume gust damage rather than only rain damage: secure loose roofing sheets, shift boats and fishing nets ashore, and charge power banks. Farmers should move harvestable standing crops to higher ground where feasible and delay harvesting until the wind passes.

Key real-world highlights

  • Stationary cyclones produce prolonged rain; flooding risk extends well beyond the landfall hour.
  • 145 kmph gusts can uproot trees and bring down transformers; treat all fallen wires as live.
  • 2.04 lakh people flagged in Odisha indicates evacuation orders may come with short notice.
  • Fishing remains banned in rough seas; disregard of such advisories is the most common cause of marine casualties.
  • Avoid coastal roads, beaches and river mouths during high tide and storm-surge windows.

Immediate do's and don'ts for residents

Do follow the district collector's loudspeaker and SMS advisories, keep a go-bag ready, and leave early if told to move rather than waiting for the rain to peak. Do not drive through water of unknown depth, do not touch damaged electrical fittings, and do not rely on a single news source; cross-check every update against IMD bulletins and the State Disaster Management Authority.
