Sensex, Nifty Slip at Noon as Iran Conflict Drives Oil Near $96

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty both in negative territory at noon on Thursday
  • Crude oil prices near $96 per barrel as Iran conflict intensifies
  • Investors turn cautious amid geopolitical uncertainty and rising input costs

Markets Open Lower, Stay in Red

Indian equity benchmarks were trading in the red at noon on Thursday, July 23, as escalating hostilities with Iran pushed crude oil prices close to $96 a barrel. The Sensex and the Nifty both slipped into negative territory by midday, dragged down by energy, metal and banking stocks.

Traders said sentiment turned cautious after reports of fresh strikes in the Gulf region. The ongoing war has kept global oil markets on edge, with Brent crude hovering just below the $96 mark.

Oil at $96 Fans Inflation Fears

Crude oil prices have been climbing steadily over the past week, crossing $95 and now threatening the $96 level. For India, which imports over 80% of its oil needs, a sustained spike in crude prices spells trouble. It widens the trade deficit, puts pressure on the rupee and raises the cost of everything from fuel to fertilisers.

Analysts pointed out that every $10 jump in oil prices can shave off roughly 0.3-0.4% from India's GDP growth. The current surge has already led to speculation that the Reserve Bank of India may hold off on any rate cuts in its upcoming policy review.

Banking and Auto Stocks Lead Losses

Banking stocks were among the worst hit at noon, with the Nifty Bank index down nearly half a percent. HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and SBI all traded lower. Auto stocks also took a hit, as rising fuel costs could dampen demand in a sector already grappling with high input prices.

Metal stocks followed the downtrend, weighed down by fears of slower global growth due to the conflict. IT stocks, however, managed to hold steady, with some investors seeing them as a safe haven amid the volatility.

What to Watch Next

All eyes are now on the afternoon session. If crude prices touch the $100 mark, further selling pressure cannot be ruled out. Traders are also watching for any diplomatic moves that could de-escalate the situation. The weekly oil inventory data from the US is due later today and could provide some direction.

For now, caution remains the dominant theme on Dalal Street. Investors are advised to keep an eye on geopolitical developments and avoid making hasty decisions.
