Sensex Surges Over 800 Points, Nifty Reclaims 24,000 — Three Reasons Why

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex surged over 800 points in morning trade on July 27
  • Nifty reclaimed the 24,000 level for the first time in weeks
  • Three factors behind the rally: global market strength, FII inflows, and buying in banking and IT stocks

Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply higher on Monday, with the Sensex surging over 800 points and the Nifty reclaiming the psychologically important 24,000 mark. The rally was broad-based, driven by strong global cues, renewed foreign investor interest, and sectoral momentum in banking and information technology stocks.

Global Markets Provide Tailwind

Overnight gains on Wall Street and a positive close in Asian markets set the tone for Dalal Street. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq ended at record highs on Friday, buoyed by optimism around interest rate cuts from the US Federal Reserve later this year.

Asian peers such as Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng traded higher on Monday, reinforcing sentiment among Indian traders. Analysts said the absence of any negative domestic triggers allowed the market to ride the global wave.

FII Buying Returns After Weeks of Selling

Foreign institutional investors turned net buyers in the cash market after a prolonged period of selling. Provisional data from Friday showed FIIs had purchased shares worth over Rs 1,500 crore, the highest single-day inflow in more than a month.

Dealers said the reversal in FII flows was partly due to a moderation in US bond yields and a stabilising rupee. The currency traded in a narrow range against the dollar, reducing the hedging cost for overseas investors.

Banking and IT Stocks Lead the Charge

Heavyweight sectors powered the rally. The Nifty Bank index jumped nearly 2 percent, with HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank and State Bank of India contributing the most to the gains. Analysts attributed the move to expectations of healthy quarterly earnings and stable asset quality.

IT stocks also saw strong buying interest, with Infosys and TCS rising over 1.5 percent each. The sector has been under pressure in recent months due to fears of a slowdown in client spending, but Monday's bounce suggested some relief buying.

Auto and metal shares also traded higher, adding to the overall positive breadth. On the BSE, over 1,800 stocks advanced against 800 declines in early trade.

Market participants will now watch for any fresh triggers from the upcoming Union Budget and the US Federal Reserve's policy meeting later this week. The ability of the Nifty to hold above the 24,000 mark could determine the near-term direction.
