Wenger backs Infantino's World Cup sell-off reversal as 'absolutely necessary'

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Wenger endorses FIFA's U-turn on selling World Cup rights
  • Infantino's FIFA Forward Enterprise shelved after backlash
  • UEFA had served legal notice over the proposed plan

Arsène Wenger has thrown his weight behind FIFA's decision to abandon a controversial plan to sell off World Cup commercial rights, describing the reversal as "absolutely necessary" for the integrity of the sport.

The former Arsenal manager, now FIFA's chief of global football development, broke his silence on the shelved initiative known as FIFA Forward Enterprise. The plan, reportedly championed by FIFA president Gianni Infantino, would have created a private investment vehicle to sell rights to the World Cup and other tournaments — a move that drew sharp criticism from European football bodies.

A U-turn under pressure

Wenger's comments come days after UEFA confirmed it had served a legal letter to FIFA over the proposal. The European governing body argued that the plan threatened the governance structure of world football and risked concentrating too much power in private hands.

Speaking to reporters, Wenger said the reversal was not a sign of weakness but of responsiveness. "When you see the reaction, you understand that it was absolutely necessary to go back," he said. "The World Cup belongs to everyone — not to a few investors."

FIFA had reportedly been in talks with multiple investment funds to raise billions of dollars by selling future media and marketing rights. The scheme was intended to create a financial buffer for global development projects, but critics said it lacked transparency.

Why the plan unravelled

The backlash was swift. European clubs and leagues, along with several national associations, voiced concerns that FIFA was effectively mortgaging its flagship event for short-term gain. The Hindu, in an editorial, called the shelved plan a "FIFA foul" — pointing to the risks of alienating fans and member associations.

ESPN's analysis noted that the proposed structure would have given private equity partners a seat at the table for decisions that have traditionally been made by football's governing bodies. That, many argued, was a step too far.

Infantino, who had championed the plan as a way to fund grassroots football, has not publicly commented on the reversal. But sources within FIFA suggest the organisation is now looking at alternative funding models that are less disruptive.

Wenger's role in the rethink

Wenger, who has been a key figure in FIFA's technical development since 2019, appeared to be signalling that the organisation is listening to its critics. His endorsement of the U-turn could help calm tensions ahead of the next FIFA Congress.

The Frenchman has often spoken about the need to modernise football's governance. Yet he was careful to stress that change must come from within the sport's own structures — not from external financiers.

"We have to find ways to develop the game, but we must do it with the values of football," he said. "That is non-negotiable."

What lies ahead

With the plan officially off the table, attention now shifts to how FIFA intends to close its funding gap. Member associations, especially those in developing regions, had been promised increased support — a promise that now hangs in the balance.

UEFA has said it is willing to engage in dialogue, but the legal letter remains on the table. The coming months will reveal whether this U-turn marks a genuine reset in FIFA's relationship with European football — or just a tactical retreat.

For now, Wenger's words offer a rare moment of consensus. The World Cup, he insists, is not for sale. The question is whether FIFA can find a way to finance its ambitions without selling it.
