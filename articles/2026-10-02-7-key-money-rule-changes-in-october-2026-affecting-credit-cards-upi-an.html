Overview of October 2026 Financial Policy Updates

The Reserve Bank of India and major banks have announced a suite of regulatory and product revisions effective 1 October 2026. While the changes span savings, payments and lending, their direct impact on credit card holders is significant. Understanding each amendment helps consumers adjust spending habits, optimise reward earnings and avoid unexpected charges.

How the Seven Rules Influence Credit Card Users

Among the seven updates, four directly affect card pricing, reward accrual and usage limits. The repo rate adjustment influences the annual percentage rate (APR) on outstanding balances, while revisions to UPI limits and FD rates alter the cashback ecosystem tied to spends. ATM fee modifications affect card‑linked cash withdrawals, and updated eligibility criteria reshape access to premium cards.

Detailed Breakdown of the Seven Changes

RuleEffective DateKey DetailRelevance to Credit Card Users
Repo Rate Reduction1 Oct 2026Policy rate cut by 25 basis points to 5.75%Lowers APR on revolving credit; may lead banks to trim reward rates to protect margins.
UPI Merchant Payment Limit1 Oct 2026Daily cap raised from ₹1 lakh to ₹2 lakhEnables higher spend on UPI‑linked cards, boosting cashback on large ticket purchases.
Bulk FD Rate Standardisation1 Oct 2026Minimum interest for deposits >₹1 crore fixed at 6.25% p.a.Reduces variance in FD‑backed cashback offers; cards tied to bulk FD yields see steadier returns.
SBI ATM Fee Structure1 Oct 2026Free transactions for non‑SBI cards reduced from five to three per month; thereafter ₹21 per withdrawalImpacts cardholders who rely on SBI ATMs for cash advances; encourages use of own‑bank networks.
Credit Card Joining Fee Waiver for Salaried1 Oct 2026Zero joining fee for cards offered to individuals with monthly salary ≥₹50,000Lowers entry barrier for premium cards; expands access to higher reward tiers.
Lounge Access Revision1 Oct 2026Domestic lounge visits limited to 8 per quarter for cards with annual fee <₹5,000Encourages users to evaluate lounge benefits versus fee; may shift preference to higher‑fee cards.
Reward Points Expiry Policy1 Oct 2026Points earned on spends >₹2 lakh per month now expire after 24 months (previously 36)Incentivises timely redemption; affects high‑spenders who accumulate large balances.

Practical Steps for Cardholders to Adapt

First, review your card’s APR statement after the repo rate cut; if the rate drops, consider paying off high‑interest balances faster to save on interest. Second, monitor UPI‑linked spends to ensure you stay within the new ₹2 lakh daily limit while maximising cashback categories that reward UPI transactions. Third, assess whether your current card’s lounge access still matches your travel frequency; if not, consider switching to a card with a slightly higher annual fee but more lounge visits. Finally, check the expiry terms of your reward points and plan redemptions before the 24‑month window closes.

Key Takeaways for Consumers

  • Lower repo rate reduces borrowing cost but may prompt banks to adjust reward earnings.
  • Higher UPI limits enable larger spends on linked cards, increasing cashback potential.
  • Standardised bulk FD rates bring predictability to FD‑backed card offers.
  • SBI ATM fee changes make own‑bank ATM use more cost‑effective for cash withdrawals.
  • Revised eligibility and lounge rules reshape the value proposition of mid‑tier and premium cards.
