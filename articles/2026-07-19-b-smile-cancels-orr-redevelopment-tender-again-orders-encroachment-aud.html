B-SMILE Cancels ORR Redevelopment Tender Again, Orders Encroachment Audit

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • B-SMILE cancelled the ORR redevelopment tender for the second time.
  • The agency will now conduct an audit to identify potential encroachments along the stretch.
  • Previous attempts to redevelop the ORR have been stalled due to similar issues.

Second Cancellation Raises Questions

B-SMILE, the agency overseeing Bengaluru's infrastructure upgrades, has scrapped the Outer Ring Road (ORR) redevelopment tender for the second time. Officials confirmed the decision on Sunday, citing unresolved issues around land use and encroachments along the busy corridor.

The tender was originally floated earlier this year but was withdrawn after bidders raised concerns. The latest cancellation comes just weeks after the agency reopened the process with modified terms. Sources indicate that the repeated delays have frustrated contractors and commuters alike.

Audit to Map Encroachments

Instead of immediately reissuing the tender, B-SMILE has ordered a comprehensive audit of the entire ORR stretch. The audit will identify all potential encroachments — including illegal structures, hoardings, and unauthorized commercial activity — that could affect redevelopment work.

A senior official told reporters that the audit is expected to take at least two months. "We need a clear picture of what is on the ground before we invite fresh bids," the official said, speaking on condition of anonymity. The agency has not yet disclosed the firm that will conduct the audit.

Stretch Critical for Bengaluru Traffic

The ORR is one of Bengaluru's most vital road networks, carrying lakhs of vehicles daily between tech parks and residential hubs. Redevelopment plans include widening carriageways, adding pedestrian pathways, and improving drainage systems. However, encroachments along the 40-kilometre stretch have historically slowed progress.

B-SMILE's earlier attempt to redevelop the road in 2023 also ran into trouble after local traders and residents resisted eviction drives. The agency had then promised to complete the project by 2025, but that deadline has now been missed.

What Happens Next

B-SMILE is expected to release the audit findings by October this year. Only after that will it call for fresh tenders, likely with stricter clauses to prevent contractor disputes. Commuters will have to wait longer for relief on one of the city's most congested roads.
