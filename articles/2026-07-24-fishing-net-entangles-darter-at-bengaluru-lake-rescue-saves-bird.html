Fishing Net Entangles Darter at Bengaluru Lake, Rescue Saves Bird

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Darter bird was found entangled in a discarded fishing net at a lake in Bengaluru.
  • Residents and forest department officials rescued the bird, which was weak but alive.
  • The incident underscores the danger posed by litter, especially fishing nets, to aquatic birds.

A Darter bird — known for its long, snake-like neck and sharp beak — nearly lost its life this week after getting tangled in a discarded fishing net at a lake in Bengaluru. The bird was spotted by local residents who alerted the forest department.

Rescuers reached the spot and carefully cut the net away. The bird was weak and visibly stressed but alive. It was taken for observation and is expected to recover.

Discarded Net Becomes a Death Trap

The Darter, also called the snakebird, often dives underwater to catch fish. This hunting habit likely led it straight into the abandoned net. The nylon netting wrapped tightly around its body and wings, leaving it unable to fly or escape.

Such nets are often left behind by illegal anglers or washed ashore after rains. For birds and turtles, they become invisible traps. The forest department has urged citizens to report any discarded fishing gear immediately.

Litter in Lakes: A Growing Concern

Bengaluru's lakes are under constant pressure from urban waste. Plastic bags, bottles, and fishing lines are regularly found in and around these water bodies. Wildlife pays the price.

In the past year alone, multiple birds and small animals have been rescued from similar entanglements. The Darter incident is not an isolated case. Conservationists point out that even a small piece of plastic can turn fatal for a bird.

“This bird was lucky. Many are not found in time,” a local wildlife volunteer said. The rescue operation was a joint effort by residents and the forest department, but officials admit that prevention is far harder than cure.

What Happens Next

The Darter is now under observation at a wildlife care facility. If it recovers fully, it will be released back into the same lake. The forest department has said it will increase patrolling around lakes to deter illegal fishing and littering.

For now, the bird's survival is a reminder of how urban life and wildlife share a fragile boundary. One discarded net almost ended a life. The next one might not be so forgiving.
