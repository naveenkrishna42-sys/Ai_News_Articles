Closing auction adds to Nifty swings on weekly expiry day

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • New closing auction session introduces sharper Nifty swings.
  • Options volatility spikes during weekly expiry due to auction.
  • SEBI pushed through CAS despite opposition from domestic institutions.
  • Foreign investors backed the new mechanism, citing transparency.

New mechanism, new market rhythm

India's stock exchanges rolled out a new closing auction session this week, and the impact was immediate. The Nifty, already known for its intraday theatrics, swung sharply as the final minutes of trade turned into a high-stakes scramble. The weekly expiry, traditionally a day of heightened options activity, saw volatility spike further as traders adjusted positions to the new closing price discovery process.

The change, implemented by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), replaces the old method of determining the closing price, which relied on the weighted average price in the last half hour of trading. Now, a dedicated closing auction session matches buy and sell orders at a single price, a system long used in markets like the US and Japan.

Expiry day drama intensifies

Market participants say the new auction has amplified moves on expiry days, when derivatives contracts are settled. With the closing price now set by a concentrated burst of orders, rather than a gradual 30-minute average, the Nifty can lurch in either direction in the final seconds. Options traders, who typically square off positions before the close, are finding the new dynamic harder to predict.

"The auction adds a layer of uncertainty," said a derivatives trader at a Mumbai-based brokerage, requesting anonymity. "You can't just watch the last few minutes of continuous trading anymore. The real action happens in the auction, and that's where the sharp moves come from."

Foreign vs domestic: a divided house

The rollout was not without controversy. Foreign institutional investors largely backed the closing auction, arguing it improves price discovery and reduces manipulation risks. Domestic institutions, however, opposed it, citing concerns about liquidity and the potential for erratic swings, especially in less-traded stocks.

SEBI, which pressed on despite the pushback, has defended the mechanism as a step toward global best practices. The regulator has maintained that the auction will bring more transparency to the closing price, which serves as the reference for mutual fund NAVs, margin calculations, and index derivatives settlement.

What the auction changes

The closing auction session runs for a brief window after regular trading hours, during which orders are collected and matched at a single price that maximises volume. This price becomes the official closing price for the day. The system is designed to reduce the impact of large, last-minute orders that could distort prices under the old average-based method.

For index-heavy stocks like those in the Nifty 50, the auction has introduced a new layer of complexity. On days with significant news flow or expiry-related position squaring, the auction can produce outsized moves, catching unwary traders off guard.

Market participants adapt

Brokers and traders are now recalibrating their strategies. Some are placing auction-only orders to participate in the closing price discovery, while others are avoiding holding positions into the auction altogether. The volatility on weekly expiries, which has always been a feature of the Indian market, has become more pronounced.

The divergence between the Nifty and the Sensex, already a talking point in recent sessions, has also been influenced by the new mechanism. The two indices, while correlated, can react differently to the auction depending on the composition of their constituents and the orders flowing in.

Looking ahead

As the market settles into the new rhythm, the focus will be on how the auction behaves over the coming weeks, particularly during high-volatility events like quarterly earnings or global shocks. SEBI has indicated it will monitor the mechanism's performance and may tweak parameters if needed. For now, traders would do well to keep an eye on the final seconds of trade — that's where the new game is being played.
