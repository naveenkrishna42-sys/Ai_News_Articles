Hyderabad's elevated corridor: 400 trees lost, 300 saved

📅 2026-08-28 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • 400 trees felled, 300 relocated for SCB elevated corridor
  • Environmentalists demand stricter guidelines for tree felling
  • Project expected to cost ₹1,200 crore, span 5 years

Tree Felling Controversy

Hyderabad's SCB elevated corridor project has sparked controversy after reports emerged that over 400 trees were felled to make way for the infrastructure. The project, expected to cost ₹1,200 crore and span over five years, aims to improve connectivity and reduce traffic congestion in the city.

Environmentalists Speak Out

Environmentalists have raised concerns about the massive tree felling, arguing that it will have a detrimental impact on the city's ecosystem. They have called for stricter guidelines to be put in place to ensure that only necessary trees are felled and that efforts are made to relocate as many trees as possible.

Project Details and Timeline

The SCB elevated corridor project is a significant undertaking that will involve the construction of a 12-kilometer elevated corridor. The project is expected to be completed in five years, with work already underway on several sections.

Pros and Cons of the Project

  • Pros: Improved connectivity, reduced traffic congestion, modern infrastructure
  • Cons: Environmental impact, potential displacement of residents

Conclusion

The SCB elevated corridor project in Hyderabad is a significant undertaking that has the potential to improve the city's infrastructure and connectivity. However, the controversy surrounding the tree felling has raised important questions about the project's environmental impact and the need for stricter guidelines to ensure that such projects are carried out in a sustainable manner.
