BJP seeks Congress support withdrawal in Jharkhand; Rahul Gandhi hits out at police

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP demands Congress withdraw support to Hemant Soren-led government in Jharkhand
  • Rahul Gandhi slams police action against fasting student leader and job aspirants
  • Normal life hit in parts of Jharkhand as BJP observes bandh; 110 ABVP workers detained

BJP's demand and political fallout

The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) has formally asked the Congress to withdraw its support to the Hemant Soren-led government in Jharkhand, escalating the political crisis in the state. The demand comes amid widespread protests over alleged irregularities in the state's competitive examination process, which have triggered violent clashes and a sharp exchange of accusations between the ruling and opposition parties.

State BJP leaders argue that the Soren government has lost its moral authority to continue in office after the police crackdown on student protesters. They have urged the Congress, a key ally in the ruling coalition, to sever ties with the Jharkhand Mukti Morcha (JMM)-led dispensation.

The Congress, however, has not yet responded officially to the BJP's call. Political observers note that any such withdrawal would bring down the government, a scenario that seems unlikely given the Congress's national alignment with opposition unity efforts.

Rahul Gandhi's condemnation

Congress leader Rahul Gandhi has strongly condemned the police action against a fasting student leader in Jharkhand, calling it an attack on democratic rights. In a statement, Gandhi accused the state administration of using excessive force to suppress peaceful protests by job aspirants demanding transparency in recruitment exams.

He also criticised the detention of 110 Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP) workers who were marching in solidarity with the students towards the state Assembly. Gandhi's remarks have added a national dimension to the localised crisis, with the Congress leader framing the issue as a broader assault on student activism.

The BJP has dismissed Gandhi's criticism as political posturing, pointing out that similar police actions have occurred in Congress-ruled states. The war of words has intensified the already charged atmosphere in Ranchi and other districts.

Bandh and ground situation

Normal life was hit in several parts of Jharkhand on Monday as the BJP observed a statewide bandh to protest the police action against job aspirants. Shops and businesses remained shut in Ranchi, Jamshedpur, and Dhanbad, while public transport was thin on the roads.

The bandh followed a tense weekend in the capital, where protests over the examination row turned violent. Reports indicate that security forces had erected six layers of barricades along a five-kilometre stretch to prevent demonstrators from reaching the Assembly, a measure that drew sharp criticism from opposition leaders.

Police have not yet confirmed the total number of detentions across the state, but the arrest of ABVP workers has added a new flashpoint. The student body, affiliated to the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), has announced further protests if its members are not released.

What's next

The coming days will test the Soren government's ability to manage the crisis, with the BJP's bandh and the Congress's political stance keeping the pressure alive. The administration is expected to hold talks with student representatives soon, but a resolution remains elusive. All eyes are now on the Congress's formal response to the BJP's demand, which could reshape the state's political equations.
