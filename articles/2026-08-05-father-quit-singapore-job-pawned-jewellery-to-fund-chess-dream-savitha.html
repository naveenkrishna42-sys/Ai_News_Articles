Father quit Singapore job, pawned jewellery to fund chess dream: Savitha Shri's story

For most teenagers, the road to an international sporting stage is paved with academies, sponsors, and sometimes, a bit of luck. For 18-year-old chess prodigy Savitha Shri, that road was built on sacrifice — a father's career given up, and family heirlooms pawned for a few precious months of training.

On Wednesday, as the Indian contingent for the Chess Olympiad was announced, Savitha Shri's name featured prominently. But her journey to this point is far from the glossy tales of corporate backing. It's a story of quiet, relentless family conviction.

A father's gamble

Savitha's father, a software engineer, had a stable, well-paying job in Singapore. For years, the family lived comfortably, shuttling between the city-state and their hometown in Tamil Nadu. But as Savitha's rating climbed and her potential became undeniable, a crossroads emerged.

The demands of competitive chess — international tournaments, coaches, travel — were escalating. Balancing a career abroad with a daughter's rising star was becoming impossible. In a decision that stunned relatives and friends, the father resigned from his Singapore job and returned to India permanently. The family's financial anchor was gone, but their focus was singular.

Pawned gold, unshaken faith

The initial months were brutal. Without the steady income, funding Savitha's circuit became a daily struggle. The family's savings dwindled. Then came the hardest step: taking the jewellery — wedding gold, ancestral pieces — to the local pawnbroker.

“It wasn't a decision made lightly,” a family source told TIVRA News, requesting anonymity. “But they saw it as an investment in her future, not a loss. The gold was just metal; her talent was the real asset.”

The pawned ornaments funded a crucial stretch of tournaments, including international events where Savitha earned norms and rating points. Every rupee was accounted for. Every trip was a calculated risk.

The breakthrough

The sacrifices began to pay off. Savitha's performances in the past year have been stellar. She crossed the 2400 rating mark, earned her Woman Grandmaster title, and consistently outperformed higher-ranked opponents in national and Asian events.

Her aggressive style, coupled with a calm endgame, has drawn comparisons to some of India's finest. But those who know her say her real strength is an unshakeable composure, forged in the pressure of knowing what her family gave up for her.

“She doesn't talk about it much,” said a coach at her Chennai academy. “But you can see it in her focus. She plays like someone who knows the cost of every move.”

A dream realised

When the Olympiad squad was confirmed, the news didn't just celebrate a player. It validated a family's leap of faith. The father, now working in a modest role in Chennai, was reportedly emotional. The jewellery, some of it, has been redeemed. The rest, the family says, can wait.

Savitha Shri is now set to don the Indian colours at the Chess Olympiad, scheduled later this year. For her, the event is not just a competition. It is the culmination of a journey that began with a father's resignation letter and a pawnbroker's receipt.

As she prepares to board the flight, the chess board in front of her is the same one she's always played on. The pieces are the same. Only the stakes have changed.
