Sensex Crashes 748 Points Intraday; Nifty Slips Below 24,000

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex dropped 748 points intraday, Nifty settled at 23,973.
  • Broad-based selling across sectors; financials and IT hit hard.
  • Analysts cite global growth fears and FII outflows as triggers.

Indian stock markets witnessed a brutal session on Wednesday, with the benchmark Sensex plunging 748 points during intraday trade. The Nifty 50 index slipped below the crucial 24,000 mark to close at 23,973, snapping a two-day winning streak.

The sell-off was broad-based. All sectoral indices ended in the red, with banking, financial services, and information technology stocks bearing the brunt of the carnage. The BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices also fell over 1.5% each, reflecting weak investor sentiment across the board.

Why Did Markets Crash?

Market participants pointed to a cocktail of domestic and global headwinds. A sharp rise in US bond yields overnight dented risk appetite, prompting foreign institutional investors (FIIs) to pull money out of emerging markets like India.

On the domestic front, fresh concerns over inflation and a likely delay in interest rate cuts by the Reserve Bank of India added to the pressure. Traders said that the lack of any positive triggers in the near term left the market vulnerable to profit-booking at higher levels.

“The mood has turned cautious. The global macros are not supportive, and the domestic earnings season has thrown up mixed signals. Investors are choosing to reduce exposure,” said a senior analyst at a domestic brokerage.

Key Levels to Watch

The Nifty’s close below 24,000 is seen as a bearish signal by technical analysts. The next support for the index is placed at 23,800, while resistance is now at the 24,200–24,300 zone.

Volatility spiked, with the India VIX — the fear gauge — rising over 8% during the session. A higher VIX indicates that traders are bracing for more turbulence ahead.

Foreign portfolio investors offloaded shares worth over ₹1,200 crore on a provisional basis, according to exchange data. Domestic institutional investors, however, stepped in to buy, but their support was not enough to stem the slide.

What Should Investors Do?

Financial advisors recommend staying cautious in the short term. They suggest that investors with a long-term horizon should use the dips to add quality stocks in a staggered manner, but avoid chasing momentum.

“This is not a time to panic sell. But fresh buying should be selective. Focus on companies with strong balance sheets and visible earnings growth,” a fund manager noted.

The broader market action also showed that the mood is fragile. The number of stocks that declined outnumbered advances by a ratio of nearly 3:1 on the BSE.

All eyes will now be on global cues, particularly the US Federal Reserve’s policy stance and commentary from key central bankers. Any negative surprise could deepen the sell-off in the coming sessions.
