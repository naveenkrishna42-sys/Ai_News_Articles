Scammed Online? How to Unfreeze and Recover Your Money in India

Key points
  • Act within 24 hours to improve freeze and reversal chances
  • File on Cyber Crime Portal and local police for FIR and bank action
  • UPI and card transactions have distinct chargeback and reversal windows

Why Funds Get Frozen After a Scam

Money is frozen after an online scam because banks and payment networks flag suspicious movement and block the chain to prevent further loss. In Hyderabad and across India, the account that receives stolen funds is typically a mule account linked to multiple complaints. Freezing is a protective measure, not a refund.

Buyers and users need to understand that a freeze preserves evidence but does not guarantee recovery. The speed of reporting determines whether the funds are still in the scammer’s account or have moved offshore. Delays reduce the chance of reversal significantly.

First 24 Hours Action Plan

Immediate action is the single biggest factor in recovery outcomes. Keep all transaction IDs, screenshots, chat logs and wallet details ready before contacting authorities.

Stop Transactions and Secure Accounts

Immediately disable UPI auto-pay, change passwords and enable two-factor authentication on bank and wallet apps. Inform your bank’s fraud desk by phone and email to block outgoing transfers from your account. Do not attempt to chase the scammer or send more money.

File a Cyber Complaint Immediately

File a complaint on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and request an acknowledgement number. Visit the local cyber police station in Hyderabad for an FIR or e-FIR if the loss exceeds Rs 10,000 or involves impersonation. Share the acknowledgement with your bank to trigger a formal freeze request.

Official Reporting Channels and Timelines

Recovery relies on coordination between cyber police, banks and the National Payments Corporation of India. Each channel has a defined window for action.

Bank and UPI Refund Realities

Banks can raise a debit reversal request with NPCI if the complaint is lodged within the dispute window. For UPI fraud, the beneficiary bank is asked to freeze the receiving account after police intimation. Success depends on whether the funds are still available.

Card payments fall under RBI mandated chargeback rules. IMPS and NEFT transfers are harder to reverse once credited unless the recipient account is frozen by court or police order. Keep follow-up records with bank reference numbers and police complaint numbers.

Key Real World Highlights

  • Reporting within 24 hours increases the probability of a freeze before fund layering occurs
  • Police complaint copy is mandatory for banks to initiate a formal freeze and reversal process
  • UPI disputes have a short window; card disputes are more structured but slower
  • Do not share OTPs or refund links with anyone claiming to be from bank or police
  • Maintain a single file with all references to avoid duplicate complaints and delays
