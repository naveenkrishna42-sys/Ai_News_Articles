Asia Cup Trophy Row: Ex-Pakistan Batter Calls India 'Arrogant'

The Women's Asia Cup 2026 final ended with India lifting the trophy for a record eighth time, but the celebration was marred by a fresh controversy. The Indian team allegedly refused to collect the trophy from Mohsin Naqvi, the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) chairman and Pakistan's Interior Minister, who was present at the presentation ceremony. The incident has triggered a war of words, with a former Pakistan batter launching a scathing attack on India, labelling the team's behaviour as 'arrogant' and defending Naqvi's role in the ceremony.

The Incident: What Really Happened?

According to multiple reports, the Indian women's team, after defeating their opponents in the final, stayed away from the official presentation area when Naqvi was called to hand over the trophy. The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) has not issued an official statement, but sources suggest the team was instructed to avoid any political entanglement. Naqvi, who also serves as Pakistan's Interior Minister, has been a controversial figure in Indian cricketing circles due to his political affiliations. The refusal to accept the trophy from him was seen by many in Pakistan as a snub, prompting the ex-cricketer's outburst.

Ex-Pakistan Batter's Defence: 'Naqvi Is Not a Terrorist'

The former Pakistan batter, whose name has been withheld due to the sensitive nature of the remarks, argued that Naqvi was present in his capacity as PCB chairman, not as a politician. 'India's arrogance is beyond limits. They refused to take the trophy from our board chief, which is disrespectful to Pakistan cricket,' he said in a video statement. 'Naqvi is not a terrorist; he is the head of our cricket board. This is pure arrogance.' The comments have been widely circulated on social media, adding fuel to the ongoing rivalry.

Kapil Dev's Reaction: 'I Don't Care'

Legendary Indian all-rounder Kapil Dev was asked about the controversy and gave a dismissive response. 'I don't care about who gave the trophy. The team won, that's what matters,' he said. His reaction was interpreted by some as a refusal to engage with the political dimensions of the incident, while others saw it as a sign of frustration with the media's focus on non-cricketing issues. The BCCI has maintained a stoic silence, but the episode has reignited debates about the intersection of sports and politics.

India's Dominance: A Record Eighth Title

On the field, India continued their supremacy in women's cricket, clinching their eighth Asia Cup title. The team remained unbeaten throughout the tournament, with standout performances from the top order and spin bowlers. However, the trophy presentation controversy has overshadowed their achievement, with critics arguing that the team's refusal to collect the trophy reflects poorly on the Indian cricket fraternity. The Hindu, in an editorial, commented that the refusal 'reflects poorly on the Indian cricket firmament,' while Rediff opined that the 'Asia Cup Trophy Shouldn't Be Political Casualty.'

What This Means for Future India-Pakistan Cricket

The incident is likely to have repercussions for future bilateral series and tournaments. The Asian Cricket Council (ACC) may need to review protocols for trophy presentations to avoid similar controversies. For now, the focus remains on the war of words, with both sides entrenched in their positions. As the cricketing world watches, the episode serves as a reminder of the delicate balance between sport and politics in South Asia.
