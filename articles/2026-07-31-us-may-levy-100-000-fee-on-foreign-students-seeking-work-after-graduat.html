US May Levy $100,000 Fee on Foreign Students Seeking Work After Graduation

📅 2026-07-31 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Reports suggest the US is considering a $100,000 fee for international students to work after graduation.
  • The proposal could affect Indian students who form a large share of US STEM graduates.
  • Critics say the fee may deter talent, while supporters argue it funds domestic workforce training.

Indian students dreaming of a career in the United States may soon face a steep new hurdle. Reports indicate that the Trump administration is weighing a plan to charge foreign students a hefty $100,000 fee if they wish to remain in the country and work after completing their studies.

The proposal, first reported by the Wall Street Journal, has sparked alarm among thousands of Indian students enrolled in US universities. For many, the H-1B visa has long been the gateway to a lucrative American career. This new fee, if implemented, could place that dream firmly out of reach.

What the Proposal Entails

The reported plan would target international students on Optional Practical Training (OPT), a programme that allows graduates to work in the US for up to three years after finishing their degrees. Under the new rules, students would need to pay $100,000 to avail of this post-study work option.

This would be a dramatic departure from the current system, where OPT requires no such payment. The fee is reportedly being considered as part of a broader immigration overhaul aimed at curbing the number of foreign workers taking up American jobs.

Sources suggest the fee could be introduced as a one-time payment, or structured as a recurring charge for each year the graduate remains on OPT. Details remain unclear, and officials have not yet confirmed the specifics.

Impact on Indian Students

India is the second-largest source of international students in the US, with over 2 lakh students enrolled in American universities. A large majority of these students pursue STEM courses, which make them eligible for an extended 24-month OPT period.

For a typical Indian student already grappling with tuition fees of $30,000 to $60,000 per year, an additional $100,000 charge would be prohibitive. Many students rely on loans and family savings to fund their education. The new fee could force them to return home immediately after graduation, undercutting the very purpose of studying abroad.

Student visa consultants in India have already begun receiving frantic calls from anxious parents. Several have advised clients to reconsider their plans until the policy is formally announced.

Mixed Reactions in Washington

The proposal has divided opinion in the US. Proponents argue that the fee would generate substantial revenue and ensure that foreign graduates contribute fairly to the American economy. Some policymakers believe it would also free up entry-level jobs for American citizens.

Critics, however, warn that the fee would backfire. Tech companies in Silicon Valley, which rely heavily on Indian talent, have voiced concern that such a move would push skilled graduates to competing nations like Canada, the UK, and Australia. These countries have been actively courting international students with more welcoming visa policies.

Higher education institutions in the US have also opposed the plan, fearing a drop in international enrolments. Universities already face budget shortfalls, and a decline in foreign students would worsen the financial strain.

What Happens Next

The proposal is still in its early stages, and no formal legislation has been tabled. It could take months before any concrete policy emerges, and the final version may differ significantly from what has been reported.

For Indian students, the uncertainty is the hardest part. Many are now weighing alternatives, including universities in Europe and Asia that offer more affordable pathways to permanent residency. The coming months will reveal whether the US is willing to risk its standing as the world's top destination for higher education.
