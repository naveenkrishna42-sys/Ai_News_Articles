Trump asserts US control over Hormuz, Iran dismisses claims

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says US will keep control of Strait of Hormuz, citing Iran's financial weakness
  • Iranian officials dismiss Trump's remarks, saying claims don't alter ground reality
  • US reportedly weighing economic pressure and possible military action against Iran

Trump's bold claim on Hormuz

President Donald Trump has asserted that the United States has total control over the Strait of Hormuz, a vital chokepoint for global oil shipments. In a statement that has drawn sharp reactions from Tehran, Trump said, "We will keep it," adding that Iran has "no money" to challenge US dominance in the region.

The remarks came amid escalating tensions in West Asia, where the US has been increasing military presence and economic pressure on Iran. Trump's comments were reported by multiple outlets, including The Times of India and NDTV, as part of a live update on the ongoing crisis.

Economic warfare and frozen assets

Trump also indicated that the US effectively controls Iran's frozen assets, positioning himself as "their banker." This appears to be part of a broader strategy of economic coercion, with the president "low-key" betting on financial pressure to bring Iran to the negotiating table.

The US has been tightening sanctions on Iranian oil exports and banking, aiming to cut off revenue streams. Trump's latest comments suggest he believes this approach is working, but Tehran sees it differently.

Iran's defiant response

Iranian officials have rejected Trump's claims outright. A statement from Tehran said, "Claims don't change reality," dismissing the notion of total US control over the strait. Iran has long threatened to close the Strait of Hormuz in response to hostile actions, and its navy has conducted exercises in the area.

The strait is a critical artery for oil transportation, with about 20% of global petroleum consumption passing through it. Any disruption could have severe consequences for world markets.

Military options remain on the table

Trump's remarks also hinted at possible military action, as he threatened "economic or military" measures against Iran. The US has deployed additional naval assets to the region, but no specific military operation has been announced.

Analysts note that while the US enjoys superior naval power, asserting "total control" over the strait is a stretch, given international law and Iran's coastal capabilities. The situation remains fluid, with both sides trading rhetoric.

What to watch

As tensions simmer, the focus will shift to whether economic pressure can force Iran back to talks, or if the standoff escalates further. The international community is watching closely, with oil prices sensitive to any sign of conflict. The coming weeks could prove decisive.
