Sensex, Nifty50 flat in early trade; HDFC Bank extends losses

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty50 traded flat in early session
  • HDFC Bank declined for the second consecutive day
  • Broader market showed mixed cues with sectoral divergence

Markets open without direction

Indian equity benchmarks opened on a flat note on Tuesday, failing to find a clear direction in early trade. The Sensex moved in a narrow range, while the Nifty50 hovered near the 24,800 mark.

Analysts attributed the lack of momentum to cautious global cues and profit-booking after recent gains. The markets had rallied in the previous week but have since entered a consolidation phase.

HDFC Bank under pressure for second session

HDFC Bank was among the top drags on the indices, falling for the second straight session. The stock slipped nearly 1% in early deals, continuing its slide from Monday.

The private sector lender's decline weighed heavily on the banking index, which underperformed other sectoral indices. Traders said profit-booking in heavyweight banking stocks was a key reason for the flat market.

Other banking majors such as ICICI Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank also traded mixed, failing to provide support.

Sectoral trends remain mixed

Broader market indices were also subdued. The BSE Midcap index traded marginally higher, while the Smallcap index showed a slight uptick.

On the sectoral front, auto and pharma stocks saw some buying interest, while IT and metal stocks faced mild selling pressure. The Nifty Auto index rose 0.3%, led by gains in Maruti Suzuki and Mahindra & Mahindra.

FMCG stocks remained muted, with Hindustan Unilever trading flat ahead of its quarterly earnings later this week.

Global cues provide no catalyst

Asian markets were mixed on Tuesday, with Japan's Nikkei rising while China's Shanghai Composite slipped. Overnight, Wall Street ended on a cautious note as investors awaited key US inflation data due later this week.

The absence of strong global triggers left domestic traders without a clear lead. Foreign portfolio investors have been net buyers in recent sessions, but domestic institutional investors have been selling selectively.

What to watch next

Traders will now focus on the weekly expiry of Nifty derivatives and any fresh cues from the US Federal Reserve's policy stance. The market is likely to remain range-bound until a new catalyst emerges.
