Census 2027: Ladakh to Lead Independent India's First Caste Enumeration

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Census 2027 population enumeration begins in snow-bound areas of Ladakh and J&K
  • This marks India's first caste enumeration since independence
  • 16 districts in J&K declared snow-bound for census operations
  • Census phase 2 may accept half-filled forms, reports say

A Historic First in the Himalayas

In a significant move, the Census 2027 has kicked off its population enumeration phase in the snow-bound regions of Ladakh and Jammu & Kashmir. This marks the first time since independence that India will conduct a full caste enumeration as part of its decadal census.

The decision to start from the high-altitude, winter-locked areas is practical. These regions become inaccessible once winter sets in, forcing authorities to act early. The Census of India has identified 16 districts in J&K as snow-bound for this purpose.

Ladakh at the Forefront

Ladakh, with its sparse population and extreme terrain, is leading the exercise. Census officials have moved teams into the region ahead of the harsh winter months. The enumeration in these areas is expected to be completed before the weather turns completely hostile.

This is not just a routine count. For the first time, the census will collect data on caste across the country. The move follows years of demand from various social groups for authentic caste numbers to inform policy and reservation decisions.

Operational Challenges

Conducting a census in snow-bound areas comes with its own set of hurdles. Officials are using local guides and administrative machinery to reach remote hamlets. The terrain often requires trekking on foot or using specialised vehicles.

Meanwhile, a report from The Times of India suggests that the second phase of the census may accept half-filled forms. This has raised questions about data completeness, though officials have not yet confirmed the details. The first phase, currently underway, is focused on population enumeration, with the house-listing phase expected later.

Jammu & Kashmir's Unique Situation

Jammu & Kashmir presents additional complexities. The region has seen significant demographic and administrative changes in recent years. The declaration of 16 districts as snow-bound means those areas will be covered on a priority basis.

Local administration has been roped in to assist census teams. Village-level officers are coordinating with residents to ensure maximum participation. The aim is to avoid undercounting in areas where movement becomes impossible after November.

What the Caste Data Means

The inclusion of caste in the census is a politically sensitive subject. While the government has framed it as a welfare measure, opposition parties have questioned the timing and methodology. The last caste census was conducted in 1931 under British rule, making this a landmark exercise.

Social justice groups have welcomed the move, arguing that accurate data is essential for targeted development. However, experts caution that the enumeration must be handled carefully to avoid misclassification or enumeration errors. The census forms have been redesigned to capture caste details of every individual.

Looking Ahead

As the snow-bound phase proceeds, the rest of the country will be enumerated in subsequent phases. The full results of Census 2027 are expected to shape policy debates for the next decade. For now, all eyes are on Ladakh, where the first pages of this historic exercise are being written.

The coming weeks will reveal how effectively the enumeration unfolds in the challenging terrain, and whether the caste data collection meets its stated objectives without controversy.
