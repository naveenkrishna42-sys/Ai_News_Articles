Kajal Aggarwal on South cinema's shift away from 'navel obsession' in female portrayal

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Kajal Aggarwal says South cinema is no longer obsessed with female navels, marking a shift in portrayal.
  • She contrasts this with Bollywood, noting differences in how women are depicted on screen.
  • The actress also comments on punctuality in film industries and the judgment faced for bold scenes.

Kajal Aggarwal on the changing gaze of South cinema

Kajal Aggarwal has pointed out a significant shift in how South Indian cinema portrays women on screen. The actress, who has worked extensively in Tamil, Telugu, and Hindi films, said filmmakers in the South are no longer fixated on female navels. She made the observation while discussing the evolving depiction of women in regional cinema.

“They’re no longer obsessed with female navels,” Aggarwal said, drawing a clear line between the old and new approaches. Her comment comes amid a broader debate on objectification and the changing standards of storytelling in Indian cinema. The actor did not name specific films but noted that the shift reflects a maturing audience and industry.

Bollywood versus South: punctuality and portrayal

Aggarwal also contrasted work cultures in Bollywood and the South. She praised the South Indian film industry for its punctuality and discipline. “Bollywood should learn punctuality from the South,” she said, recalling that she once waited the longest for Salman Khan on a set. The remark highlights a recurring difference in production efficiency between the two industries.

The actress did not limit her observations to schedules. She also addressed how female actors are judged for performing bold scenes. Her stance on the matter has gone viral, especially in the context of the ongoing intimacy debate around her upcoming film Toxic. Aggarwal argued that the judgment is often unfair and gendered.

No fruits on my navel: a blunt reminder

In a separate interaction, Aggarwal made a pointed remark: “You are not throwing fruits on my navel.” The line was a direct reference to the older trope in South cinema where camera angles lingered on women’s midriffs. She used it to illustrate how much the industry has moved on from such clichés.

Her comments have resonated with many who see them as a sign of progress. Aggarwal did not provide specific examples of current films that avoid such objectification, but her overall message was clear. The industry, she suggested, is now more focused on storytelling and character depth than on gratuitous shots.

The judgment female actors face

Aggarwal also spoke about the double standards faced by women in the film industry. She noted that male actors rarely face the same level of scrutiny for intimate scenes. “Female actors are judged more harshly for bold scenes,” she said, adding that the conversation needs to change.

Her comments come at a time when the debate around on-screen intimacy and consent is gaining traction in Indian cinema. Aggarwal did not claim to have all the answers, but she made it clear that the industry must evolve. She emphasised that actresses should not be reduced to their bodies or the roles they choose to play.

Officials from the film industries have not yet responded to her remarks. The actress herself did not provide a timeline for when she expects these changes to fully take root. But her observations have added fuel to an ongoing discussion about gender parity and representation on screen.

What to watch next

Aggarwal is currently awaiting the release of Toxic, which has already sparked conversations around intimacy in cinema. The film’s handling of such scenes will likely be watched closely. Her comments suggest that the debate over how women are portrayed in Indian cinema is far from over.
