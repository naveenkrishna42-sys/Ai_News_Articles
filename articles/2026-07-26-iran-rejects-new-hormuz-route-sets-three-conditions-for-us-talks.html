Iran Rejects New Hormuz Route, Sets Three Conditions for US Talks

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran's Foreign Ministry rejected a proposed new shipping route in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Tehran set three conditions for fresh nuclear talks with the US.
  • The US paused military strikes for a second day as diplomatic efforts continued.

Iran Dismisses Alternative Passage, Demands Status Quo

Iran has formally rejected a proposed alternative shipping route in the Strait of Hormuz, insisting that the existing traffic management system must remain unchanged. The Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson confirmed on Sunday that there has been 'no change in the situation regarding traffic in the Strait of Hormuz.'

The rejection comes after weeks of speculation that a new sea lane could be established to ease tensions in the strategic waterway. The Strait handles about a fifth of the world's oil supply, making any disruption a major flashpoint for global energy markets.

Tehran's position is clear: the current framework governing passage through the strait is non-negotiable. The statement from the ministry effectively shut the door on any unilateral changes proposed by the US or its allies.

Three Conditions for Nuclear Negotiations

Alongside the Hormuz rejection, Iran laid out three conditions for returning to the negotiating table with the United States. While the full text of the conditions has not been officially released, Iranian officials indicated they focus on verifiable sanctions relief, guarantees against future US withdrawal from any agreement, and respect for Iran's regional security interests.

The conditions appear designed to address Tehran's core complaints from the 2015 nuclear deal, from which the US unilaterally withdrew in 2018. The move signals that Iran is unwilling to engage in talks without concrete assurances first.

Senior Iranian diplomats involved in indirect talks in Oman have described progress on managing Hormuz Strait traffic, but insist that broader negotiations cannot proceed unless Washington meets these preconditions.

US Pauses Strikes, Diplomatic Channel Opens

In a significant shift, the United States paused military strikes against Iranian targets for a second consecutive day. Reports from CBS News indicate that diplomatic talks are moving in a 'positive direction,' though no breakthrough has been announced.

The pause marks a departure from the aggressive posture the Trump administration had adopted in recent weeks. The halt in strikes has been interpreted by analysts as a gesture to allow the Oman-mediated talks to gain traction.

Both sides have confirmed that discussions are ongoing. Iran blamed the US for the Hormuz dispute but acknowledged that dialogue continues. The Al Jazeera report noted that mutual accusations have not derailed the backchannel communication.

What Comes Next

The coming days will test whether Iran's conditions are a genuine opening for negotiations or a stalling tactic. The US administration has not publicly responded to the three conditions, but the pause in strikes suggests a willingness to explore a diplomatic off-ramp.

Observers are watching the Oman talks closely. If the two sides can agree on a framework for Hormuz management, it could pave the way for broader nuclear discussions. If not, the region may see a rapid return to military escalation.
