Jharkhand crisis: BJP seeks Congress withdrawal, Rahul slams police

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP has formally asked Congress to withdraw support from the Hemant Soren-led government in Jharkhand
  • Rahul Gandhi condemned police action against fasting student leader and protesters in Ranchi
  • Normal life disrupted in parts of Jharkhand as BJP observed a bandh; 110 ABVP workers detained during a solidarity march

The political crisis in Jharkhand has sharpened after the BJP formally asked the Congress to withdraw support to the Hemant Soren-led government. The demand came amid a violent crackdown on student protesters demanding a re-examination of a state recruitment exam, which has left normal life disrupted in several districts.

The BJP's move signals a coordinated attempt to isolate the ruling coalition, even as Opposition leader Rahul Gandhi directly attacked the state administration's handling of the protests. The police action, which involved multi-layer barricades and detention of student activists, has now become the central flashpoint in the state's politics.

What triggered the confrontation

The immediate cause is the protest by job aspirants against alleged irregularities in the Jharkhand Staff Selection Commission (JSSC) examination. A student leader has been on a fast demanding a fresh exam, and the police crackdown on supporters has inflamed tensions.

On Monday, police detained 110 activists of the Akhil Bharatiya Vidyarthi Parishad (ABVP) when they attempted to march to the Assembly in solidarity with the fasting student. Reports from Ranchi describe six layers of barricades set up for a five-kilometre stretch, a measure the administration says was necessary to prevent violence but critics call an overreaction.

The BJP's bandh call on the same day saw shops shut and transport disrupted in several towns. The party has framed the police action as evidence of the Soren government's authoritarian drift, and is now using the opportunity to test the Congress's commitment to its alliance partner.

The political calculus behind the BJP's demand

The BJP's demand to the Congress is not merely a moral appeal. It is a strategic move to break the Mahagathbandhan, which includes the Congress, the Rashtriya Janata Dal, and the Left parties. By asking the Congress to pull out, the BJP is forcing a public choice — either the Congress stands with the Soren government and faces the political cost of the police action, or it walks away and triggers a collapse of the government.

Political observers note that the BJP has been probing for weaknesses in the coalition since the 2024 Lok Sabha results, where the NDA made gains in the state. The Congress, which has only 16 MLAs in the 81-member Assembly, is the second-largest partner in the government. Its withdrawal would reduce the government to a minority, potentially paving the way for fresh elections.

The timing is also significant. With Jharkhand's next Assembly election due later this year, the BJP wants to keep the focus on law-and-order failures rather than governance or welfare schemes. The bandh and the student protests give the party a ready-made narrative of a state in chaos.

Rahul Gandhi's counter-offensive

Rahul Gandhi's condemnation of the police action complicates the BJP's plan. Instead of distancing himself from the Soren government, the Congress leader has chosen to attack the administration from the left, accusing it of suppressing young voices. This allows the Congress to claim the moral high ground on civil liberties while still holding the government.

Gandhi's statement also puts pressure on the Soren government to rein in the police and address the protesters' demands. The Congress's internal dilemma is real: it cannot be seen as defending a crackdown, but it also cannot afford to destabilise an alliance that keeps the BJP out of power in the state.

The BJP, however, is unlikely to let the issue fade. It has already sought to paint Gandhi's stance as a contradiction — criticising the police action while keeping the same government in office. The coming days will test whether the Congress can maintain this balancing act or whether internal pressure will force a rethink.

Ground reality and what is at stake

Normal life has been hit in parts of Jharkhand, with schools and shops closed in several districts due to the bandh. The administration has deployed additional forces in Ranchi, and internet services have been suspended in some areas to prevent the spread of rumours, officials said without confirming details.

The fasting student leader's condition remains a concern, though no official health bulletin has been released. The student groups have vowed to continue the agitation until the government announces a fresh examination schedule or an independent inquiry into the alleged paper leak.

The economic cost of the disruption is mounting, with small traders reporting losses due to the shutdown. The political cost, however, is what the BJP is counting on. A prolonged crisis could erode the Soren government's credibility, especially among the state's large youth population, many of whom are job aspirants.

The Congress's decision in the next 48 hours will be crucial. If it chooses to stay, it must extract a concrete concession from the Soren government on the exam issue to justify its position. If it chooses to walk, it risks being blamed for handing the state to the BJP.

What to watch: Whether the Soren government announces a fresh exam date or a judicial probe to pacify protesters, and whether the Congress leadership issues any formal statement on the BJP's withdrawal demand. The next Assembly session, if it happens, will be the real test of numbers and nerves.
