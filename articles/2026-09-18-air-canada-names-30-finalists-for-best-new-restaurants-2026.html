Air Canada's 2026 Restaurant Shortlist: What's Actually at Stake

Air Canada has released the 30 finalists for its Best New Restaurants 2026 programme, continuing a culinary awards run that has become a genuine travel-planning tool for Canadians. The shortlist spans independent kitchens, chef-driven concepts, and hotel-adjacent dining rooms across major and secondary cities.

For travellers, the list is a curated itinerary. For Aeroplan members, it is a spending map. The overlap between where you eat and which card you swipe is where the real value sits.

Why a Restaurant List Matters to Cardholders

Dining is one of the largest discretionary categories in Canadian household budgets, and it is also one of the most reliably rewarded. Aeroplan co-branded cards from TD, CIBC, and American Express typically offer accelerated earn on eligible dining, which means a weekend trip built around shortlisted restaurants can double as a points-earning exercise.

The catch is category coding. A standalone bistro often codes as dining; a restaurant inside a hotel may code as lodging. That single distinction can swing earn rates by 2x or more on the same meal.

Real-World Buyer Implications

A ₹10,000 dinner billed to a 5x dining card at 1.8 cents per point returns roughly ₹900 in redemption value. The same dinner on a 1x card returns about ₹180. Over a year of shortlisted-restaurant travel, that gap compounds into a free domestic flight.

Restaurant lists also shape destination choice. A shortlist entry in a secondary city can justify a two-night stay, which pushes spend into hotel and travel categories where premium travel cards outperform.

Pros and Cons for Points Collectors

  • Pro: Dining is a stable, repeatable bonus category across most Aeroplan cards.
  • Pro: The 30-finalist list gives a concrete, low-effort way to route spend toward rewarded categories.
  • Pro: Aeroplan redemptions on Air Canada and Star Alliance partners hold steady value above 1.5 cents.
  • Con: Merchant category coding is inconsistent, especially for hotel restaurants and pop-ups.
  • Con: Premium cards with the best dining multipliers carry annual fees that need heavy spend to justify.
  • Con: Shortlist restaurants in major cities often require reservations weeks ahead, limiting spontaneity.

How to Actually Use This List

Treat the 30 finalists as a spending plan, not just a wishlist. Match each planned meal to the card with the highest dining multiplier in your wallet, and keep a backup card for venues that code as travel or entertainment.

If you are evaluating a new Aeroplan card, run the numbers against your actual annual dining spend rather than the advertised headline earn rate. A 5x card at ₹12,000 annual fee only wins if your dining volume clears roughly ₹1.5 lakh to ₹2 lakh per year.

The 2026 shortlist will narrow to a final ranking in the coming months. Until then, the finalists stand as the most useful dining-spend roadmap Canadian cardholders will get this year.
