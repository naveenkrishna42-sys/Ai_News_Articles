Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 rocket to launch diamond flower into space

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vikram-1 is India's first private space rocket, developed by Skyroot Aerospace.
  • The rocket will carry a diamond flower as a symbolic payload on its maiden commercial flight.
  • The launch marks a milestone for India's private space sector, with Skyroot aiming for a bigger prize in the global market.

Hyderabad-based Skyroot Aerospace is set to make history once again. Its Vikram-1 rocket, India's first privately built orbital launch vehicle, will carry a diamond flower into space on its maiden commercial flight.

The payload, described by the company as a 'diamond flower,' is not just a decorative item. It symbolises the growing ambitions of India's private space sector. Skyroot has not disclosed the exact nature of the diamond flower—whether it is a piece of art, a scientific instrument, or a commemorative token.

Sources close to the company said the payload is designed to test the rocket's deployment mechanisms. The mission will also validate critical systems for future commercial launches.

Vikram-1: A rocket built by ex-ISRO scientists

Skyroot Aerospace was founded by former Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) scientists. The team includes Pawan Kumar Chandana and Naga Bharath Daka, both of whom worked on ISRO's launch vehicle programmes before venturing into the private sector.

Vikram-1 is a three-stage solid-fuel rocket capable of carrying payloads of up to 480 kilograms to low Earth orbit. The rocket is named after Dr Vikram Sarabhai, the father of India's space programme.

In 2022, Skyroot successfully launched a suborbital version of the rocket, making it the first private Indian company to reach space. The upcoming orbital flight will be a significant step up.

Crashing an elite party

Skyroot's entry into the orbital launch market comes at a time when the global space industry is dominated by a handful of players. Companies like SpaceX, Rocket Lab, and Arianespace have long held the reins.

But Skyroot is aiming to disrupt this elite club. The company has positioned itself as a cost-effective alternative for small satellite launches. Its pricing, though undisclosed, is expected to be competitive with global peers.

The diamond flower payload is also a statement. Skyroot is signalling that it can handle unique, non-traditional payloads—opening up new revenue streams from art, commemorative items, and even advertising in space.

What comes next: A bigger prize

The maiden commercial flight of Vikram-1 is not just about the diamond flower. Skyroot has its eyes on a much larger prize: a share of the global small satellite launch market, valued at over $3 billion.

The company has already signed contracts with several international clients, including satellite operators from the US and Europe. A successful Vikram-1 flight will validate Skyroot's technology and open the door for more commercial orders.

Skyroot is also working on a larger rocket, Vikram-2, which will have a higher payload capacity. The company plans to eventually offer reusable rocket technology, further cutting costs.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has hailed the efforts of Skyroot. In a statement after the earlier suborbital launch, he described it as a 'historic milestone' for India's private space sector.

Challenges ahead

Despite the optimism, Skyroot faces significant challenges. The small satellite launch market is crowded, with established players like Rocket Lab and new entrants from China and Europe. Regulatory hurdles in India, though easing, still pose barriers.

Technical risks also remain. Orbital launches are notoriously difficult, and a failure could set back the company's timeline. Skyroot has not disclosed the exact launch date for Vikram-1's commercial flight, but industry insiders expect it to take place in late 2026.

The company is also in talks with Indian government agencies to launch strategic payloads, including for defence and earth observation. A successful Vikram-1 flight could accelerate those discussions.

For now, all eyes are on the diamond flower. Its journey into space will mark the beginning of a new chapter for India's private space sector. If Skyroot succeeds, it will not just be a win for the company—it will be a signal that India can compete with the world's best in space.
