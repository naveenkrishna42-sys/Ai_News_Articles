Parliament panel chief demands Zuckerberg apology over PM post row

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Parliament panel chief demands apology from Mark Zuckerberg over PM Modi video row
  • Meta, Google, X executives appear before parliamentary panel on social media regulation
  • Panel discusses foreign links and content control amid allegations of platform bias

Sharp exchange in Parliament panel

The chairperson of a parliamentary standing committee on information technology has demanded a public apology from Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, accusing him of intending to destabilise India. The demand came during a heated session where representatives from Meta, Google, and X appeared before the panel to discuss social media regulation.

The confrontation centres on the removal of a video featuring Prime Minister Narendra Modi from Meta's platforms. Committee members expressed strong displeasure over the takedown, with the chairperson asserting that Zuckerberg must apologise for what they termed an affront to India's sovereignty.

Platforms under scrutiny

Executives from the three major tech firms faced pointed questions from lawmakers about their content moderation policies and the criteria used for taking down posts. The panel sought clarity on why certain content related to Indian political figures was being removed, while similar material from other countries remained accessible.

Sources familiar with the proceedings said the discussion also touched on the influence of foreign entities on content decisions. Some members raised concerns that algorithmic choices made in Silicon Valley could have outsized consequences for democratic processes in India.

Foreign links and content control

The committee reportedly delved into the ownership structures and cross-border data flows of these platforms, pressing executives on whether external pressures shape their operations in India. Questions were raised about the transparency of takedown requests and whether Indian users receive adequate recourse when their posts are removed.

Meta's representatives defended their actions, citing community standards and legal obligations, but the panel remained unconvinced. The chairperson's demand for an apology was met with no immediate commitment from the company.

Repeated confrontations

This is not the first time the parliamentary panel has clashed with tech giants. Earlier hearings have focused on hate speech, misinformation, and the alleged misuse of platforms during election cycles. However, the current row over the PM's video has escalated tensions, with some members calling for stricter regulatory mechanisms.

The panel is expected to submit a report with recommendations to Parliament, which could include penal provisions for non-compliant platforms. Industry watchers suggest that Meta's handling of this episode may influence how future legislation is framed.

Neither Meta nor Zuckerberg has issued a formal response to the apology demand as of now. The company's legal team is said to be reviewing the committee's proceedings.

What lies ahead

The committee is likely to summon additional stakeholders, including digital rights groups and legal experts, to build a comprehensive case. With the government signalling intent to tighten oversight of social media, the coming months could see new rules mandating greater accountability for foreign platforms operating in India.
