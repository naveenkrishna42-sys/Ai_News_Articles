Samsung Rushes More Galaxy Z Fold 8s Before iPhone Fold Release

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Samsung raises Galaxy Z Fold 8 production target by 1 million units this year
  • Strong consumer demand surprises even Samsung, prompting the increase
  • Move comes ahead of anticipated iPhone Fold release, intensifying foldable competition

Samsung is ramping up production of its Galaxy Z Fold 8, adding a million more units to this year's manufacturing target. The move comes as the South Korean tech giant looks to capitalise on surging demand before Apple enters the foldable market with its much-rumoured iPhone Fold.

Industry trackers at GSMArena first reported the production increase, citing supply chain sources. The revised target suggests Samsung is bracing for a bigger sales wave than initially planned, a rare adjustment for a device that has been on the market for only a few months.

Demand Exceeds Expectations

The Galaxy Z Fold 8, launched alongside the Z Flip 8, Watch Ultra 2, and Watch 9, has apparently struck a chord with consumers. Even Samsung, a company known for its aggressive sales forecasting, did not anticipate the level of enthusiasm, according to Android Authority.

Reviews have been largely positive, with Android Police noting that the device's design has revived calls for the return of a certain older phone feature. That sentiment appears to be translating into real-world purchases, pushing Samsung to secure additional component supplies and production slots.

A Preemptive Strike Before Apple's Fold

The timing is no accident. Apple's first foldable iPhone has been the subject of intense speculation, with analysts expecting a launch within the next year. By flooding the market with more Z Fold 8 units now, Samsung aims to lock in foldable converts before Apple offers an alternative.

This is a familiar playbook. Samsung has dominated the foldable segment since the original Galaxy Fold debuted in 2019, but Apple's entry could disrupt that lead. Increasing inventory ahead of a rival's launch is a defensive move, but it also signals confidence in the Z Fold 8's appeal.

What's Driving the Surge

The Z Fold 8's success appears to stem from refinements that address earlier criticisms. Thinner bezels, a more durable hinge, and improved battery life have made the device more practical for daily use, according to early reviews. The integration with the new Watch Ultra 2 and Watch 9 may also be driving ecosystem upgrades.

Analysts point to a broader trend: foldables are no longer niche. Global shipments have grown steadily year-on-year, and Samsung's latest models are positioned at the premium end, where margins are healthier. The additional million units could translate to a noticeable bump in revenue, though Samsung has not disclosed financial projections.

Supply Chain Implications

The production increase will put pressure on Samsung's display and hinge suppliers, who are already operating at high capacity. Industry sources suggest that Samsung has secured additional panel orders from its display affiliate, Samsung Display, to meet the revised target.

There is also a knock-on effect for competitors. If Samsung is selling more foldables than expected, rivals like Google and OnePlus may need to adjust their own strategies. For now, though, the spotlight is on Samsung's ability to deliver on its new numbers without compromising quality.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact production figures, but the report has been corroborated by multiple supply chain checks. Samsung typically provides official shipment guidance during its quarterly earnings calls, so a clearer picture may emerge then.

The Road Ahead

With Apple's foldable looming, the next few quarters will be crucial. Samsung's decision to ramp up production suggests it is betting on sustained demand, but the real test will come when the iPhone Fold finally lands. If the Z Fold 8 continues to sell at this pace, Samsung may extend the production boost into next year. For now, consumers in India and elsewhere can expect better availability of the device, which was previously constrained in some markets.
