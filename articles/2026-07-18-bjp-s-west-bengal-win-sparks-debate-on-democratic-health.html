BJP's West Bengal win sparks debate on democratic health

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP wins West Bengal, ending Trinamool Congress rule after 15 years.
  • Critics allege misuse of central agencies and media bias against opposition.
  • Opposition calls for unity, but internal divisions remain a hurdle.

A decisive shift in Bengal's political map

The Bharatiya Janata Party's victory in the West Bengal assembly elections marks a turning point for the state. After 15 years under Mamata Banerjee's Trinamool Congress, the BJP has secured a clear majority. The outcome reshapes the political landscape of eastern India.

The Election Commission reported a voter turnout of 82%. Counting was largely peaceful, though some constituencies saw delays in result updates. The BJP won 198 seats in the 294-member assembly. The Trinamool Congress was reduced to 82 seats.

Allegations of institutional bias

Critics say the victory reflects more than just voter preference. Opposition leaders and civil society groups have pointed to what they describe as the weaponisation of central agencies. The Enforcement Directorate and Central Bureau of Investigation have pursued several Trinamool leaders in recent years.

Several opposition figures were arrested or summoned during the campaign period. Mamata Banerjee called the election a ' farce' and alleged that the Election Commission favoured the ruling party. The BJP has dismissed these claims as sour grapes.

Media coverage during the campaign also came under scrutiny. Some independent observers noted that several news outlets showed a clear tilt toward the BJP. The ruling party's advertising spend was significantly higher than that of its rivals.

The role of money and muscle power

The Election Commission seized cash and goods worth over Rs 500 crore during the campaign period. Most seizures were linked to the BJP and Trinamool Congress. Officials said the figure was the highest ever recorded in a state election.

Violence marred several phases of polling. At least 12 people were killed in clashes between rival party workers. The worst incidents were reported from Cooch Behar and North 24 Parganas districts. The Election Commission imposed Section 144 in several areas on voting days.

Opposition in disarray

The Congress party, once a force in Bengal, failed to win a single seat. Its vote share dropped below 4%. The Left Front, which ruled the state for 34 years until 2011, won only three seats. Their combined vote share was less than 8%.

Opposition leaders across the country have called for a united front against the BJP. But internal differences have prevented any real coordination. The Congress and the Left have been unable to agree on seat-sharing in other states. Regional parties remain wary of ceding space to the Congress.

Mamata Banerjee has said she will challenge the results in court. She also announced plans to hold a rally of opposition parties in Kolkata next month. Whether other parties will attend remains uncertain.

What comes next

The BJP will now form its first government in West Bengal. The new chief minister is expected to be sworn in within a week. The party has not yet announced its pick for the top post. Political observers are watching for any signs of a candidate who can bridge the state's regional and religious divides.

The central government's focus will now shift to the next round of assembly elections in 2027. The BJP's victory in Bengal is likely to energise its cadre in other states where it faces tough contests. For the opposition, the question remains whether they can set aside their differences to present a credible alternative.
