Chennai's Third Master Plan finalisation may take six more months: CMDA

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • CMDA indicated the Third Master Plan for Chennai could take at least six months to finalise.
  • The plan aims to guide urban growth, infrastructure, and zoning regulations for the metropolitan area.
  • Public consultations and revisions are ongoing, with officials seeking to balance development and sustainability.

CMDA provides timeline update on master plan

The Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA) has indicated that the Third Master Plan for the city may take at least six more months to be finalised. The statement comes as urban planners and civic officials continue to work through the extensive process of drafting and reviewing the document.

The master plan, which will guide land use, infrastructure development, and zoning regulations across the Chennai Metropolitan Area, has been in the works for several years. The CMDA’s latest update suggests that the final version is still some distance away.

Process involves public feedback and revisions

Officials said the delay is largely due to the need to incorporate feedback from multiple rounds of public consultations. The draft plan, which was released earlier, drew thousands of suggestions and objections from residents, industry bodies, and civic groups.

The CMDA is now in the process of reviewing these inputs and making necessary revisions. A senior official noted that the authority wants to ensure the plan is comprehensive and addresses the concerns of all stakeholders before it is submitted for final approval.

Challenges in balancing growth and sustainability

The Third Master Plan is expected to cover a range of critical issues, including housing, transportation, green spaces, and flood management. Chennai’s rapid urbanisation has put pressure on existing infrastructure, and the new plan aims to provide a roadmap for sustainable development over the next two decades.

One of the key challenges has been reconciling the demand for new housing and commercial space with the need to protect water bodies and open areas. Environmental groups have pushed for stricter regulations to prevent encroachments and ensure climate resilience.

What to watch for in the coming months

The CMDA is expected to complete the revision process in the next few months, after which the plan will be sent to the state government for final clearance. Once approved, it will replace the existing master plan, which has been in effect since 2008.

Residents and real estate developers are closely watching the final provisions, as they will determine property values, construction norms, and the overall shape of Chennai’s growth for years to come. The authority has not yet set a specific date for the plan’s release, but the six-month window provides a clearer timeline than before.
