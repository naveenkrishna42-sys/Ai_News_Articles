Jalandhar civil surgeon inspects anti-dengue drive

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Civil Surgeon Dr. J.L. Gupta conducted an anti-dengue inspection in Jalandhar.
  • He directed health teams to intensify fogging and source reduction activities.
  • Officials were told to focus on high-risk areas and involve community groups.
  • The drive aims to prevent dengue cases ahead of the monsoon season.

Inspection focuses on high-risk zones

Civil Surgeon Dr. J.L. Gupta carried out a field inspection of anti-dengue measures in Jalandhar on Sunday, reviewing the preparedness of health teams in the district. The visit covered residential areas and public spaces where stagnant water and waste accumulation have been reported.

During the inspection, Dr. Gupta directed officials to intensify fogging operations and ensure that anti-larval activity is carried out systematically in all vulnerable localities. He stressed the need for regular checks of coolers, tyres, and other breeding sites that mosquitoes thrive in.

Health teams told to step up surveillance

The civil surgeon also reviewed the functioning of rapid response teams and asked them to remain on alert for any suspected dengue cases. He instructed field staff to maintain daily records of fever cases and report unusual clusters to the district surveillance unit without delay.

Officials accompanying the civil surgeon included district health officer and other medical officers. They were told to coordinate with municipal authorities for clearing garbage and ensuring proper drainage in areas that are prone to waterlogging.

Community awareness gets priority

Dr. Gupta emphasised that prevention is the first line of defence against dengue. He asked health workers to involve resident welfare associations and school students in awareness campaigns, urging them to spread the message about eliminating mosquito breeding sites.

Local residents were advised to keep their surroundings clean and use mosquito repellents, especially during daytime hours when the Aedes mosquito is most active. The civil surgeon also recommended that people with fever should seek medical advice early rather than self-medicate.

The district administration has been ramping up its anti-dengue machinery ahead of the monsoon, with special focus on urban pockets where population density is high. Fogging machines and insecticide stock have been replenished at health centres, according to officials.

No surge in cases reported yet

Health authorities have not reported any unusual spike in dengue cases so far this season, but they remain cautious. The inspection comes as part of a routine review to ensure that preventive protocols are being followed on the ground.

Dr. Gupta's directive to intensify surveillance is expected to continue in the coming weeks, with weekly reviews planned to assess the situation. The district administration has also urged private clinics to notify suspected dengue cases to the health department for timely action.

As the monsoon approaches, Jalandhar's health machinery is on alert. The coming weeks will show whether the intensified checks and community outreach can keep dengue cases at bay.
