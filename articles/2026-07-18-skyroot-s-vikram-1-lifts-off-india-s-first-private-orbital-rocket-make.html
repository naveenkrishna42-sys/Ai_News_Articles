Skyroot’s Vikram-1 Lifts Off: India’s First Private Orbital Rocket Makes History

📅 2026-07-18 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Skyroot Aerospace launched Vikram-1, India’s first private orbital-class rocket, from Sriharikota.
  • Six payloads were successfully deployed into low-Earth orbit during the debut flight.
  • The mission marks a milestone in India's private space sector, led by former ISRO scientists.

Historic Liftoff at Sriharikota

Skyroot Aerospace scripted its name in India’s space history on Saturday, launching Vikram-1 — the country’s first privately developed orbital-class rocket — from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota. The rocket lifted off without a hitch, putting six payloads into low-Earth orbit in a textbook debut.

Cheers erupted at the mission control centre as the rocket climbed through the morning sky. Officials described the flight as flawless, with all stages performing as planned.

Six Payloads Reach Orbit

The Vikram-1 mission carried six customer payloads, though the company has not yet disclosed all client details. What is known is that the payloads included technology demonstration satellites and experimental modules from both Indian startups and academic institutions.

The deployment sequence was executed precisely, with each payload separated and injected into its designated orbit. Skyroot confirmed that all satellites have established communication with ground stations.

From ISRO Labs to Private Launchpad

Behind the success of Vikram-1 is a team of former Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) scientists and engineers who founded Skyroot Aerospace in 2018. They set out to build a low-cost, reliable launch vehicle that could serve the growing demand for small satellite launches.

Vikram-1 is a three-stage, solid-fuel rocket capable of carrying up to 480 kilograms to low-Earth orbit. The company’s approach combines proven propulsion technology with advanced manufacturing techniques like 3D printing.

A Textbook First Attempt

Getting an orbital rocket right on the first try is rare. Many private space companies globally have faced multiple failures before achieving orbit. Skyroot’s achievement places it alongside a select group of private launch providers worldwide.

“This is a proud moment for India. Our team has worked tirelessly to make this happen,” a senior Skyroot official said after the launch. “We are now ready to serve commercial and government customers at scale.”

What Comes Next

With Vikram-1 proven, Skyroot is already working on its next variant, Vikram-2, which will feature a cryogenic upper stage for heavier payloads. The company has a launch manifest that includes multiple missions for domestic and international clients over the next two years.

The Indian Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre (IN-SPACe), which regulates private space activities, has cleared Skyroot for future launches. The success of Vikram-1 is expected to accelerate interest from investors and customers in India’s private space ecosystem.
