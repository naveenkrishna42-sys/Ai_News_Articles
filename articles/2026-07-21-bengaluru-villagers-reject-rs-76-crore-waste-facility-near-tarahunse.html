Bengaluru villagers reject Rs 76 crore waste facility near Tarahunse

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Villagers near Tarahunse have rejected a proposed Rs 76 crore waste processing facility.
  • The facility was planned to handle Bengaluru's growing municipal solid waste.
  • Residents cite fears of pollution, groundwater contamination, and health risks.
  • Officials are yet to decide on the next steps after the strong opposition.

Villagers say no to Rs 76 crore waste plant

Residents of Tarahunse and surrounding villages in Bengaluru Rural district have firmly opposed a proposed Rs 76 crore waste processing facility. The project, aimed at treating the city's mounting solid waste, was met with protests and a clear message from the local community: not in our backyard.

The facility was planned by the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) as part of its long-term waste management strategy. But villagers argue they were not consulted before the decision was made.

Why the opposition?

People living near the proposed site fear the plant will pollute the air, soil, and groundwater. Many depend on borewells and open wells for drinking water. They worry that leachate from the waste treatment process could contaminate these sources.

"We have no other source of water. If the groundwater gets polluted, our families will fall sick," a local resident said. Farmers in the area are also concerned about the impact on crops and livestock.

The proposed location is close to several villages, including Tarahunse, and is surrounded by agricultural land. Residents say the plant will release foul odours and attract pests, making daily life unbearable.

No prior consultation, say locals

Village leaders claim the BBMP and the state government did not hold any public hearing before finalising the site. They say the first they heard of the project was when construction vehicles started appearing on the land.

"We are not against development. But we cannot accept a project that endangers our health without any discussion," a gram panchayat member stated. The villagers have submitted a memorandum to the deputy commissioner, demanding the project be scrapped.

The BBMP, however, maintains that the facility is essential. Bengaluru generates over 6,000 tonnes of solid waste daily, and existing processing plants are struggling to keep up. Officials argue that modern waste treatment technology can minimise pollution if operated correctly.

What happens next?

The district administration has not yet issued a formal response to the villagers' objections. The BBMP may need to look for alternative sites if the opposition remains strong. Similar protests have delayed or derailed waste projects in other parts of Karnataka in recent years.

With local resistance hardening and no resolution in sight, the Rs 76 crore plan faces an uncertain future. The coming weeks will reveal whether the government engages with the community or pushes ahead despite the opposition.
