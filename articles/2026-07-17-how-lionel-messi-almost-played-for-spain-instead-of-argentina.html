How Lionel Messi almost played for Spain instead of Argentina

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Spain's football federation had a plan to recruit a young Lionel Messi for its youth teams.
  • A single VHS tape showing Messi's talent sparked interest from Spanish clubs.
  • Argentina's football officials had to track him down via a payphone directory to secure his allegiance.

It is a detail that could have rewritten the history of two footballing nations. Long before Lionel Messi lifted a World Cup for Argentina, a quiet recruitment drive was underway in Spain. The target: a small, shy boy from Rosario, barely a teenager, who was already making defenders look foolish on pitches in Barcelona.

Recent reports from multiple Indian outlets have pieced together the story of how Messi almost ended up wearing the red of Spain instead of the blue and white stripes of Argentina. The saga involves a forgotten VHS tape, a frantic search through a telephone directory, and a plot that some have described as a near-'kidnap' by Spanish football officials.

The VHS tape that started everything

In the early 2000s, Messi was a prodigy at Newell's Old Boys in Argentina. But his family's move to Barcelona for medical treatment for his growth hormone deficiency brought him to the attention of European scouts. By 2003, a VHS tape of his performances had made its way to the offices of the Spanish Football Federation.

Officials in Madrid were impressed. They saw a player who could become a cornerstone of their national team for a generation. According to reports, they began making moves to secure his international eligibility, leveraging his residency in Spain and his family's ties to the country.

The Spanish plot and the payphone hunt

The plan was audacious. Spanish federation officials reportedly contacted Messi's family directly, offering a clear path to the youth national teams. The intention was to tie him to Spain before Argentina could act. For a family navigating a new country and financial strain, the offer must have been tempting.

But word reached Argentina's football authorities. A desperate race began. With no easy way to contact the family in Barcelona, officials from the Argentine Football Association (AFA) had to resort to a low-tech solution. Reports from India Today and The Indian Express detail how they used a payphone directory to track down Messi's residence in Catalonia. The call was made just in time.

A warning that changed history

When the AFA finally reached Messi's father, Jorge, they delivered a clear warning. If his son played even a single official match for Spain's youth side, he would be locked into representing them forever. The family, reportedly swayed by the passion of the Argentine officials and a deep-rooted national sentiment, declined Spain's offer.

The decision was not without pressure. Spanish officials had already arranged for a call-up to their Under-17 team. The Telegraph India report calls it a 'kidnap plot' in the sense that it was a calculated move to sever Messi's ties to Argentina permanently. It almost worked.

The what-if that still echoes

Had that payphone call not been made, the 2022 World Cup final might have looked very different. Messi could have been lining up alongside Xavi, Iniesta, and Sergio Ramos for Spain, perhaps adding to their 2010 World Cup triumph. Argentina would have been robbed of its most decorated son.

The story remains one of football's greatest sliding-door moments. A VHS tape, a directory, and a frantic phone call are all that separated Messi from becoming a Spanish legend. For Argentina, it is a reminder of how easily their greatest treasure was almost lost.

As Messi’s career winds down, the full weight of that moment is only now being fully appreciated. The question of what would have been will linger, but for now, Argentina can only be grateful that a payphone directory worked in their favour.
