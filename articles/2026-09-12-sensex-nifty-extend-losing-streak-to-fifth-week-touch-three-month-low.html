Sensex, Nifty Extend Losing Streak to Fifth Week, Touch Three‑Month Low

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty have fallen roughly 2% each week for five straight weeks, with the Nifty closing below 23,400.
  • Soaring Brent crude prices and heightened geopolitical risk from West Asia are the primary drag on market sentiment.
  • Investors should review sector exposure, consider defensive allocations and watch for near‑term technical rebounds amid oversold conditions.
  • Global interest rate outlook and foreign institutional flows remain key variables for the next market move.

Market Overview and Recent Performance

Indian benchmark indices have endured a fifth consecutive week of losses, pushing the Sensex and Nifty to their lowest levels in about three months. The latest session saw the Sensex slip 121 points, while the Nifty settled below the 23,400 mark. Weekly declines for both indices are estimated at near 2%, reflecting sustained selling pressure.

Sensex and Nifty Movements

The Nifty’s breach of 23,400 signals a break of short‑term support that had held since early August. On a point basis, the Sensex’s 121‑point drop translates to a modest intraday move, but the cumulative effect over five weeks has eroded roughly 10% from the recent peak. Trading volumes have remained elevated, indicating active participation rather than a passive drift.

Drivers: Crude Oil, Geopolitics, Rates

Brent crude prices have risen sharply, feeding inflation concerns and raising cost pressures on energy‑intensive sectors. Simultaneously, the escalation of conflict in West Asia has increased the risk premium for emerging‑market assets. Global interest rate expectations, particularly regarding the U.S. Federal Reserve’s policy trajectory, have added to the cautious tone, prompting foreign institutional investors to trim exposure.

Implications for Investors

For equity holders, the prolonged downtrend underscores the need to reassess portfolio risk. Defensive sectors such as FMCG, healthcare and utilities have shown relative resilience, while cyclical groups like metals, oil & gas and automobiles face headwinds from higher input costs and demand uncertainty.

Sector Exposure and Risk Management

Investors with heavy weightings in export‑oriented or commodity‑linked stocks may consider hedging strategies or shifting toward companies with strong pricing power and low leverage. Systematic rebalancing to maintain target asset allocations can help avoid overexposure to any single theme that is currently under pressure.

Outlook and Strategic Considerations

Technical indicators suggest the indices are entering oversold territory, which could trigger a short‑term bounce if any of the negative drivers ease. However, a sustained recovery will likely depend on a stabilization of crude prices, de‑escalation of geopolitical tensions and clarity on global monetary policy. Market participants should monitor weekly crude inventories, West Asia developments and central bank communications for cues on the next directional move.

  • Five‑week losing streak reflects a confluence of rising oil prices, geopolitical risk and tighter global monetary outlook.
  • Nifty below 23,400 and Sensex down 121 points in the latest session highlight near‑term weakness.
  • Defensive sectors have outperformed cyclicals; investors may tilt toward low‑leverage, pricing‑power companies.
  • Oversold conditions could produce a short‑term rebound, but a durable turnaround requires easing of cost and risk pressures.
  • Watch for foreign institutional flow trends and crude inventory data as leading indicators for market direction.
