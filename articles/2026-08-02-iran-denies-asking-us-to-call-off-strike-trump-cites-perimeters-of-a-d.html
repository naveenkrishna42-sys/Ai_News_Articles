Iran denies asking US to call off strike, Trump cites 'perimeters of a deal'

📅 2026-08-02 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iranian media denies Trump's claim that Tehran asked Washington not to strike.
  • US military reportedly learned of the cancelled strike via social media.
  • Gulf states reportedly urged Trump to hold off and pursue negotiations.

Tehran's state media has pushed back against US President Donald Trump's assertion that Iran requested Washington to abandon a planned military strike, adding another layer of confusion to an already volatile West Asian standoff. The denial came hours after Trump announced he had called off the attack, citing what he called the 'perimeters of a deal' with Iran.

Conflicting narratives emerge

Iranian outlets, quoting unnamed officials, categorically rejected the notion that Tehran had made any such plea. The official line is that Iran does not beg for its security. Washington, however, has offered no formal clarification, leaving observers to parse statements that seem to contradict each other.

Trump's own remarks have only muddied the waters. In a series of posts, he suggested that an agreement had been reached with Tehran over the framework of a potential deal, but offered no specifics. There has been no confirmation from Iranian authorities about any such understanding.

Military left in the dark?

Perhaps more troubling is a report from The Times of India indicating that the US military and Israeli officials first learnt of the strike's cancellation through social media. If true, this would mark a significant breakdown in communication between the White House and its defence establishment, raising questions about decision-making protocols during a crisis.

Neither the Pentagon nor Israeli officials have commented publicly on the report. But the suggestion that such a consequential reversal was communicated informally has sparked concern among security analysts.

Gulf states push for talks

Meanwhile, The Wall Street Journal has reported that Gulf states exerted considerable pressure on Trump to hold off on strikes and instead pursue diplomatic channels. These nations, wary of being caught in the crossfire of a US-Iran conflict, have reportedly been urging Washington to de-escalate for weeks.

Their lobbying appears to have had some effect. The decision to halt the strike, however, has left many wondering what exactly Trump means by a deal, and whether it constitutes a genuine diplomatic breakthrough or a temporary reprieve.

What is the 'deal' Trump hints at?

Al Jazeera has raised the same question that is now doing the rounds in diplomatic circles: why has Trump halted attacks, and what is the deal he is hinting at? So far, there is no clarity on the substance or scope of any proposed agreement.

Iran's leadership has remained characteristically tight-lipped, offering neither confirmation nor denial of any back-channel negotiations. The absence of an official response from Tehran only deepens the ambiguity surrounding the situation.

Regional tensions remain high

The cancellation of the strike does not signal an end to hostilities. The region remains on edge, with both sides maintaining a posture of readiness. Any miscalculation could still spiral into a wider conflict, a prospect that Gulf states and global powers alike are eager to avoid.

For now, the international community watches closely, waiting for either side to clarify what — if anything — has actually been agreed upon.

What happens next is uncertain. The coming days are likely to bring either formal negotiations or a resumption of military threats, depending on whether the 'perimeters of a deal' can be translated into concrete action.
