RBI's New SNFAs and SARFAESI Act: What Banks Need to Know

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI notifies SNFAs under SARFAESI Act for faster loan recovery
  • SNFAs include gold, silver, diamonds, and other high-value assets
  • Banks can now seize these assets without court intervention

New Asset Class for Faster Recovery

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has introduced a new category of assets called Specified Non-Financial Assets (SNFAs) under the Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest (SARFAESI) Act. This move expands the scope of assets that banks can seize and sell to recover bad loans, without going through lengthy court proceedings.

Until now, the SARFAESI Act primarily covered financial assets like loans and securities. With the inclusion of SNFAs, lenders can now directly enforce security interest over physical assets such as gold, silver, diamonds, and other high-value items pledged as collateral.

What Are Specified Non-Financial Assets?

According to the RBI notification, SNFAs are tangible assets that are not financial in nature but hold significant monetary value. The list includes precious metals like gold and silver, gemstones such as diamonds, and other specified commodities that are commonly used as collateral in lending.

This classification is expected to streamline the recovery process for banks that accept such assets as security. Previously, recovering these assets required multiple legal steps. Now, under the SARFAESI Act, banks can take possession and sell them without court approval, provided they follow due process.

Why This Matters for Banks and Borrowers

For banks, the move reduces delays in resolving non-performing assets (NPAs). The SARFAESI Act already allowed seizure of financial assets, but physical assets often fell into a legal grey area. By clearly defining SNFAs, the RBI has closed a loophole that borrowers sometimes exploited to stall recovery.

Borrowers, however, must be aware that pledging gold or diamonds now carries higher risk. Defaulting on a loan backed by SNFAs could lead to swift seizure of those assets. Industry experts say this could also encourage more disciplined repayment behaviour.

The SARFAESI Act: A Quick Refresher

The SARFAESI Act, enacted in 2002, allows banks and financial institutions to recover bad loans by taking possession of secured assets without court intervention. It was a landmark reform that gave lenders greater power to deal with defaulters.

The Act covers all secured creditors and applies to loans above a certain threshold. The new SNFAs notification is an amendment to the existing rules, widening the net of assets that can be recovered under this framework.

Implementation and Next Steps

The RBI has directed banks to update their internal policies to include SNFAs in their recovery procedures. Lenders must also ensure proper valuation and documentation of these assets before enforcing security interest.

While the notification is effective immediately, some banks may take time to fully align their processes. The central bank is expected to issue detailed guidelines on valuation standards and auction procedures for SNFAs in the coming weeks.

This development signals a more aggressive stance by regulators on bad loan recovery, especially as India grapples with rising NPAs in certain sectors. The move could set a precedent for other asset classes to be included in the future.
