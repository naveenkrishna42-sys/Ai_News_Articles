Jharkhand students' hunger strike over JPSC, JSSC exam row enters fresh phase

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Students in Ranchi have launched a hunger strike over alleged irregularities in JPSC and JSSC examinations.
  • CJP's Abhijeet Dipke has extended support to the protesting students, amplifying their demands.
  • Jharkhand CM Hemant Soren has assured that the government is serious about probing the exam irregularities.
  • Protesters say they will speak only to CM Soren, rejecting other intermediaries.

Ranchi witnesses escalating student protest

Jharkhand's capital Ranchi has become the centre of a simmering student agitation, with demonstrators now on an indefinite hunger strike. The protests, which have been building for days, centre on alleged irregularities in the JPSC and JSSC examinations. Students accuse authorities of paper leaks, manipulation of results, and a lack of transparency in the recruitment process.

The hunger strike has added a new layer of urgency to the movement. Protesters have refused to meet anyone except Chief Minister Hemant Soren, stating they will speak only to him. This direct demand has put the state government on notice, as the agitation shows no signs of abating.

Abhijeet Dipke's backing adds fuel

The protest has received a significant boost from Abhijeet Dipke, the national president of the Chhatra Jan Parishad (CJP). Dipke's open support has lent political weight to the students' cause, drawing attention beyond Jharkhand's borders. His endorsement has also raised questions about the opposition's role in mobilising student discontent ahead of elections.

Dipke's backing comes at a time when student protests across India have often become flashpoints for larger political battles. In Ranchi, however, the immediate trigger remains the exams. Students claim that the irregularities have wrecked their careers, forcing many to spend lakhs on coaching and travel, only to face an unfair selection process.

Government's response so far

Chief Minister Hemant Soren has responded with assurances, saying that the government is serious about probing the exam irregularities. He has promised that protesters will be reassured through a fair investigation. However, the students remain sceptical, pointing to past instances where such probes have dragged on without conclusive action.

The state administration has appealed for calm, but the hunger strike has intensified pressure. Protesters have reportedly said they will not relent until the CM meets them personally. The standoff has drawn parallels with similar movements in other states, where exam paper leaks and recruitment scandals have triggered widespread outrage.

What is at stake for Jharkhand's youth

For many students, these exams represent their only shot at stable government jobs. The frustration is palpable, with reports of students having to sleep on railway platforms or deliver food for Swiggy to afford exam fees and coaching. The economic burden, combined with alleged systemic failure, has pushed many to the brink.

The JPSC (Jharkhand Public Service Commission) and JSSC (Jharkhand Staff Selection Commission) exams are crucial gateways for thousands of aspirants. Any perceived unfairness in these processes threatens to alienate an entire generation of job seekers. The protests, therefore, are not just about one exam cycle but about the credibility of the state's recruitment machinery.

What happens next

The coming week is likely to be decisive. All eyes are on Chief Minister Soren's next move, as protesters insist on a personal audience. The government's ability to balance firmness with empathy will determine whether the hunger strike ends peacefully or escalates further. For now, Ranchi remains on edge, with students determined to hold the administration to its word.
