Delhi-NCR Waterlogging: Heavy Rain Paralyses Friday Commute

📅 2026-08-07 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Non-stop rain caused severe waterlogging across Delhi-NCR
  • Friday evening commute severely disrupted with massive traffic jams
  • IMD issued a red alert for the region
  • Locals used makeshift rafts to ferry schoolchildren through flooded streets

Delhi-NCR came to a grinding halt on Friday as relentless rain lashed the region, turning arterial roads into rivers and throwing the evening commute into chaos. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) issued a red alert, warning of continued heavy downpours as waterlogging crippled movement across the national capital and its satellite cities.

Commuters were left stranded for hours as traffic crawled at a snail's pace on major thoroughfares. Social media was flooded with videos and photos of submerged cars, water entering homes, and exasperated residents wading through knee-deep water.

Red Alert and Grim Forecast

The IMD's red alert signals the highest level of weather warning, indicating the potential for extremely heavy rainfall. The advisory urged residents to stay indoors and avoid unnecessary travel, but for many, the warning came too late as they were already caught in the deluge.

Forecasters predicted that the intense spell would continue, though with some respite expected later in the night. The sudden and intense nature of the rain caught both civic agencies and the public off guard, exposing the region's fragile drainage infrastructure once again.

Streets Turned Waterways

Reports from across the NCR painted a grim picture. In several areas, water levels rose rapidly, submerging two-wheelers and flooding ground-floor establishments. The underpasses, often the most vulnerable points, were completely inundated, forcing authorities to close them to traffic.

In a striking visual of the day's desperation, locals in one flooded locality were seen using a makeshift raft to ferry schoolchildren through the water. The improvised boat, likely a large tub or a wooden plank, became a lifeline for young students stranded on their way home.

Traffic Jams and Stranded Commuters

The evening rush hour turned into a nightmare as traffic jams stretched for kilometres. Key intersections and flyovers reported gridlock, with buses, cars, and auto-rickshaws barely moving. Commuters complained of a lack of cabs and app-based ride services, with many drivers refusing to venture into waterlogged routes.

"No cabs, waterlogging," read one frustrated commuter's post, encapsulating the sentiment of thousands. Many office-goers abandoned their vehicles and chose to walk, while others waited for hours at bus stops and metro stations, hoping for any mode of transport to get them home.

Civic bodies deployed pumps and teams to clear water from critical points, but the sheer volume of rainfall overwhelmed the drainage systems. The chaos was not limited to the roads; reports of delayed trains and disrupted metro services added to the commuters' woes.

Recurring Problem, Familiar Frustration

Friday's events are a stark reminder of a recurring problem. Every monsoon, Delhi-NCR struggles with waterlogging, despite repeated assurances from authorities. The situation raises serious questions about urban planning and the maintenance of stormwater drains, which are often clogged with solid waste and encroached upon by construction.

Environmentalists have long pointed to the loss of water bodies and green cover as aggravating factors. The heavy rain also highlighted the need for better real-time weather monitoring and more robust emergency response mechanisms to minimise disruption.

As the rain continued into the night, the focus shifted to the next morning. The IMD's alert remains in place, and authorities have advised residents to stay updated on traffic advisories. The coming hours will test the city's resilience once more as it grapples with the aftermath of the deluge.
