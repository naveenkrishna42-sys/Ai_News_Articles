iPhone 18 cost to rise sharply as memory chip prices soar

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • iPhone 18 BOM expected to rise sharply due to memory chip price surge
  • Rising component costs may lead to higher retail prices for the next iPhone lineup
  • Apple reportedly weighing price hikes ahead of iPhone 18 Pro launch in September

Memory chip crunch hits Apple's bottom line

The next iPhone is getting costlier to build, and that bill may soon land on your wallet. According to industry trackers, the bill of materials (BOM) for the iPhone 18 is set to jump sharply as memory chip prices continue their upward climb. The surge, driven by tightening supply and robust demand across AI-powered devices, is forcing Apple to absorb higher component costs.

Memory chips, particularly NAND flash and DRAM, have seen sustained price hikes over recent months. For Apple, which typically negotiates bulk rates, the increase marks a notable departure from the usual downward pricing curve seen in mature component markets. The company has not yet commented on the projected cost escalation.

What this means for the iPhone 18 lineup

The cost pressure is expected to hit the entire iPhone 18 range, from the base model to the Pro Max. Reports suggest the premium iPhone 18 Pro Max will feature the A20 Pro chip and a camera upgrade, but those additions come amid a backdrop of pricier memory. Analysts tracking Apple's supply chain now estimate the BOM for the upcoming devices could be significantly higher than the iPhone 17 series.

For consumers, the math is simple: if Apple's production costs go up, retail prices often follow. The company has historically avoided broad price hikes in India, preferring to adjust storage tiers or offer trade-in deals. But with component costs climbing, that strategy may be harder to sustain this cycle.

Price hike rumours swirl ahead of launch

Speculation is already building that Apple may increase iPhone 17 prices in the near term, a move that would soften the blow when the iPhone 18 arrives. Some retail watchers point to a possible price adjustment for existing models to create a wider gap before the September launch of the Pro variants. Apple has not confirmed any such plans.

The timing is tricky. Demand for the iPhone 18 Pro is said to be strong, with reports of a September launch being driven by consumer appetite. But a higher price tag could dampen that enthusiasm, particularly in price-sensitive markets like India, where import duties and local taxes already inflate costs.

India market faces double whammy

Indian buyers may feel the pinch more acutely. With the rupee's exchange rate and local levies, any global price increase gets magnified at retail. The iPhone 18 is expected to be assembled locally in some variants, which could offset some costs, but memory chips are largely imported, leaving little room for savings.

Industry insiders suggest Apple might reshuffle its storage configurations, perhaps making the base variant more attractive while nudging users toward higher tiers. That approach has worked before, but it does little to mask the underlying cost pressure.

What to watch next

All eyes are on Apple's next earnings call and any official word on pricing. Supply chain reports indicate the company is still finalising its component orders, and memory chip prices could stabilise or climb further in the coming months. For now, prospective buyers should brace for a possible price hike—either on the iPhone 17 ahead of the new launch or on the iPhone 18 itself.

The September launch will reveal the true extent of the cost impact. Until then, the rumour mill keeps spinning, and the memory chip market remains the wild card in Apple's pricing strategy.
