RBI data shows robust loan growth across key sectors

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI sectoral data shows continued strong loan growth
  • Retail and services sectors drive credit demand
  • Banks maintain cautious stance on unsecured lending

Credit momentum continues

India's banking system continues to witness robust credit growth, according to the latest sectoral data released by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). The numbers, which track lending trends across major economic segments, show that loan disbursements remain on a healthy trajectory, underscoring sustained demand from both retail and corporate borrowers.

The data, released on Friday, indicates that overall non-food credit growth has stayed in double digits, driven by a mix of festive-season consumption, working capital needs, and infrastructure spending. Banks have been expanding their lending books even as they navigate tighter liquidity conditions and evolving regulatory norms.

Retail and services lead the charge

Retail loans, particularly housing and personal loans, continue to be the primary growth engine. The RBI's numbers show that credit to the services sector has also picked up pace, aided by demand from non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and trade segments. This broad-based expansion suggests that the economic recovery is translating into higher credit off-take across the board.

Agricultural credit, too, has shown resilience, supported by seasonal sowing patterns and government-backed schemes. However, industry credit growth remains comparatively moderate, reflecting a cautious approach by lenders to certain manufacturing sub-sectors.

Unsecured loans under watch

Despite the overall strength, bankers and regulators remain alert to risks in unsecured lending categories such as credit cards and personal loans. Some lenders have tightened underwriting standards in these segments, even as demand continues to surge. The RBI has repeatedly flagged the need for vigilance in consumer credit, and the current data suggests that while growth is strong, prudence is not being abandoned.

Deposit growth lags

One area of concern, however, is deposit growth, which continues to trail credit expansion. This has led to a structural gap that banks are managing by relying more on certificates of deposit and other borrowing instruments. The resulting pressure on margins is prompting lenders to adjust interest rates on both sides of the balance sheet.

Bankers say the gap between credit and deposit growth is manageable for now, but a sustained divergence could pose challenges. Some institutions have already raised deposit rates to attract savers, while others are focusing on low-cost current and savings account (CASA) mobilisation.

Outlook remains positive

Looking ahead, the trajectory of loan growth is likely to remain firm, supported by ongoing infrastructure projects, a resilient services sector, and steady consumer demand. However, the pace could moderate as the base effect kicks in and as the RBI monitors any signs of overheating in specific loan segments.

Market participants will be watching the central bank's upcoming policy statement for cues on interest rates and liquidity management. For now, the overall credit environment appears stable, with growth and caution moving in tandem.
