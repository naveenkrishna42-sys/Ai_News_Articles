Hyderabad Police Arrest Five QNET Reps in Goa Over Pyramid Scheme Fraud

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Five QNET representatives arrested in Goa by Hyderabad Police.
  • The fraud involved a pyramid scheme worth ₹7.5 lakh.
  • Victims were targeted across multiple states in India.

Arrests Made in Goa

A team from the Hyderabad Police Cyber Crime Unit arrested five individuals in Goa on Wednesday, accusing them of running a pyramid scheme under the guise of QNET, a direct-selling company. The arrests followed a complaint filed by a victim who was duped of ₹7.5 lakh.

The suspects were taken into custody from a hotel in North Goa, where they were allegedly holding a recruitment meeting for new members. Police sources indicate that the group had been operating in the region for several months.

Modus Operandi

According to the police, the accused lured victims with promises of high returns on investments, typical of pyramid schemes. They would ask new recruits to pay an upfront fee and then recruit others to earn commissions.

The fraud came to light when a Hyderabad-based businessman, who had invested ₹7.5 lakh, realised he was not getting any returns. He approached the Hyderabad Cyber Crime Police, who then tracked the suspects to Goa.

Investigators have not yet confirmed if the accused have any links to larger QNET operations in India. QNET has faced similar allegations in the past in other states.

Legal Proceedings

The five accused have been booked under various sections of the Indian Penal Code, including cheating and criminal conspiracy. They are currently in police custody and will be produced before a local court.

Authorities are now examining their bank accounts and digital devices to trace the full extent of the fraud. They suspect that more victims may come forward as the investigation progresses.

The Hyderabad Police have urged anyone who has been defrauded by similar schemes to report it to the cyber crime helpline. The case highlights the growing challenge of online financial frauds in the country.

What Happens Next

The accused are expected to be brought to Hyderabad on a transit remand. Further arrests cannot be ruled out as the police continue to investigate the network of agents involved in the scheme.
