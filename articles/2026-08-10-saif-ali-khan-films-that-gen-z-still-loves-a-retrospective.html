Saif Ali Khan Films That Gen Z Still Loves: A Retrospective

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Saif Ali Khan's filmography spans genres and decades
  • Gen Z finds relatability in his characters and stories
  • The list includes cult classics and romantic comedies

Saif Ali Khan has been a constant in Hindi cinema for over three decades, but his appeal has found a fresh lease of life among Gen Z. Streaming platforms and social media have introduced his older films to a new generation, and they are discovering a body of work that feels surprisingly contemporary.

From the breezy charm of Dil Chahta Hai to the easygoing romance of Salaam Namaste, these films are being rediscovered, memed, and celebrated. Here is a look at five Saif Ali Khan films that continue to strike a chord with young audiences today.

The Cult of Dil Chahta Hai

When Dil Chahta Hai released in 2001, it changed the way urban Indian youth saw themselves on screen. Saif's portrayal of Sameer, the lovelorn, slightly goofy friend, was a departure from the angry young man tropes of the 90s. Gen Z sees Sameer as a relatable figure—someone who fumbles through relationships but always lands on his feet.

The film's themes of friendship, ambition, and self-discovery are as relevant now as they were two decades ago. Its soundtrack, too, has become a staple in Gen Z playlists, making the film a permanent fixture in their cultural lexicon.

Salaam Namaste and the Modern Romance

Released in 2005, Salaam Namaste was ahead of its time in many ways. It tackled live-in relationships, casual dating, and the complexities of modern love with a light touch. Saif's character, Nikhil, is a chef with commitment issues—a role that feels distinctly contemporary.

For Gen Z, who navigate relationships in an era of dating apps and blurred boundaries, Nikhil's journey from flippancy to responsibility resonates deeply. The film's setting in Melbourne and its stylish presentation also add to its timeless appeal.

The Quirky Charm of Hum Tum

Hum Tum (2004) is another Saif film that Gen Z has embraced. The story of Karan and Rhea, who meet repeatedly over the years but never quite connect, is a meditation on timing and maturity. Saif's comic timing and his character's evolution from a cartoonist with a playboy streak to a man ready for a real relationship is a satisfying arc.

The film's use of animation and its playful tone make it a unique entry in his filmography. Young viewers appreciate the film's honesty about love and its refusal to tie everything up in a neat, conventional bow.

Kal Ho Naa Ho: The Supporting Star

While Kal Ho Naa Ho (2003) is remembered for Shah Rukh Khan's iconic performance, Saif's role as Rohit is crucial. He plays the loyal friend who steps up when needed, and his character's emotional arc is one of quiet strength. Gen Z has come to appreciate Rohit's patience and sincerity, especially in an age where grand gestures often overshadow steady support.

Saif's understated performance in this film is a reminder that his range extends beyond romantic leads and comedies.

Love Aaj Kal: The Dual Narrative

In Love Aaj Kal (2009), Saif plays two characters across different eras, exploring how love changes with time. The film's parallel narratives about traditional vs. modern romance offer a rich commentary that Gen Z finds intriguing. His portrayal of both Jai and Veer shows his versatility, and the film's philosophical questions about love and ambition are still debated in online forums.

The movie's soundtrack, especially the title track, remains a favorite among young listeners, cementing the film's lasting cultural footprint.

As streaming dominates how young India consumes cinema, Saif Ali Khan's filmography is enjoying a renaissance. His characters—flawed, funny, and human—offer a counterpoint to the polished, often unrelatable heroes of today. What remains to be seen is whether his more recent work will find the same lasting connection with the next generation of viewers. For now, his older films are a comfort watch that continues to grow in relevance.
