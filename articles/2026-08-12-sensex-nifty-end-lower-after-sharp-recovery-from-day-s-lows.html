Sensex, Nifty end lower after sharp recovery from day's lows

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty ended in the red after recovering sharply from intraday lows.
  • Markets were impacted by global cues and profit booking in select heavyweights.
  • Analysts say volatility is likely to persist in the near term.

Markets close in red after volatile session

Indian equity benchmarks ended lower on Wednesday, extending their losing streak, though a sharp recovery in the final hours helped trim most of the day's losses. The Sensex closed down, while the Nifty also settled in negative territory after spending much of the session under pressure.

Traders said the initial fall was driven by weak global cues and profit booking in recent outperformers. However, buying emerged at lower levels in the last hour of trade, which pulled the indices off their lows.

What dragged the indices down

Heavy selling was seen in select index heavyweights, particularly in the financial and IT sectors. Concerns over global interest rates and mixed signals from overseas markets added to the cautious mood on Dalal Street.

Market participants also pointed to a lack of fresh domestic triggers, which left investors with little to hold onto. The broader market showed mixed trends, with midcap and smallcap stocks trading in a narrow range.

Recovery from lows offers some comfort

Despite the weak close, the sharp recovery from the day's lows was seen as a positive sign by some analysts. The Nifty, which fell sharply in early trade, managed to claw back most of the ground by the close.

"The recovery suggests that selling pressure is not aggressive and investors are using dips to accumulate quality stocks," said a market observer. However, they added that the overall trend remains cautious given global uncertainties.

Global cues and oil prices weigh

Overseas, Asian markets ended mixed, while European indices opened lower, keeping sentiment subdued. Rising crude oil prices also added to inflation worries, which could influence central bank policy moves.

Foreign institutional investors have been net sellers in recent sessions, though domestic institutions have provided some support. The rupee, too, remained under pressure against the US dollar, adding to the cautious tone.

What lies ahead

Investors will now watch global data releases and corporate earnings for direction. Any fresh triggers from the US Federal Reserve or domestic macroeconomic numbers could decide the next move for the market.

Analysts expect volatility to remain high in the short term, with the Nifty likely to find support at lower levels. Market participants are advised to stay selective and focus on stock-specific opportunities rather than the index level.
