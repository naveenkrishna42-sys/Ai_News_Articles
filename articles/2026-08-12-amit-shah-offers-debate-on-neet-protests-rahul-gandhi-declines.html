Amit Shah Offers Debate on NEET Protests, Rahul Gandhi Declines

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Amit Shah has written to the Lok Sabha Speaker offering to answer questions on the NEET student protests.
  • Shah said he is ready to discuss all issues and asked for time allocation starting today.
  • Rahul Gandhi responded that he is 'not interested' in hearing Shah, saying the 'balloon has burst'.
  • The exchange comes amid ongoing student protests over the NEET examination.

New Delhi: The political confrontation over the NEET student protests escalated on Wednesday as Union Home Minister Amit Shah offered to answer any questions in Parliament, while Congress leader Rahul Gandhi rejected the overture outright.

In a letter to the Lok Sabha Speaker, Shah requested time for a debate on the issue, saying he was ready to address all queries. Sources said the Home Minister's letter proposed that discussions begin immediately, reflecting the government's attempt to counter the opposition's offensive on the examination controversy.

Shah's Offer for Debate

Shah's communication to the Speaker was direct. He sought allocation of time 'starting today' for a discussion on the student protests. The letter underlined his willingness to respond to any question raised by members, a move seen as an effort to take the wind out of the opposition's sails.

The Home Minister's offer was not limited to NEET alone. He indicated a readiness to discuss all issues, signalling that the government was open to a broader parliamentary engagement. This comes as protests by medical aspirants have intensified in several cities, with demands for a re-test and probe into alleged irregularities.

Gandhi's Sharp Retort

Rahul Gandhi, however, was in no mood to accept the offer. Responding to Shah's proposal, the Congress MP said he did not want to listen to the Home Minister. His retort, reported by multiple news agencies, was blunt: 'The balloon has burst.'

Gandhi's dismissal suggests that the opposition views Shah's offer as a tactical move rather than a genuine attempt at dialogue. The Congress has been leading the charge against the government over the NEET paper leak allegations, demanding a joint parliamentary committee probe and the resignation of responsible ministers.

Political Significance

The exchange is more than a procedural spat. It highlights the deepening rift between the ruling BJP and the opposition alliance over handling of the crisis. While the government insists it is prepared to answer questions, the opposition questions the timing and sincerity.

For the students protesting across the country, the parliamentary back-and-forth offers little immediate relief. Their demands remain concrete: cancellation of the exam, fresh dates, and action against those involved in the leak. The government has so far resisted a full re-test, citing logistical challenges.

What Happens Next

The ball is now in the Speaker's court. Whether time is allotted for the debate and how the opposition responds to Shah's challenge will determine the next phase of this confrontation. With Parliament in session, the coming days are likely to witness more heated exchanges.

Observers will watch if the opposition accepts the debate offer under its own terms, or continues its strategy of refusal, betting that public anger is on their side. For now, the students wait, and the political rhetoric continues.
