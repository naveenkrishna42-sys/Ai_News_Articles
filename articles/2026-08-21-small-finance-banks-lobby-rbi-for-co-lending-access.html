Small Finance Banks Lobby RBI for Co-Lending Access

📅 2026-08-21 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • SFBs have formally requested the RBI to allow broader co-lending arrangements.
  • The move aims to expand credit reach to underserved segments.
  • Current regulations limit the scope of partnerships for these niche lenders.

SFBs Seek Regulatory Pivot

Small Finance Banks (SFBs) across the country have formally approached the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) with a proposal to expand their co-lending capabilities. These niche lenders are seeking a regulatory framework that allows them more flexibility in partnering with other financial institutions to deploy credit.

The representations submitted to the central bank highlight a push to integrate deeper into the co-lending model. SFBs argue that current constraints limit their ability to scale operations efficiently. By securing broader access, these banks aim to improve their asset books while maintaining their mandate of financial inclusion.

Bridging the Credit Gap

Co-lending has become a vital tool for banks to reach under-banked regions. By sharing the risk and the loan book, SFBs can extend credit to borrowers who might otherwise struggle to secure formal financing. The banks believe that updated guidelines will foster better liquidity management.

Industry experts observe that SFBs often face challenges in funding long-term loan portfolios. A formal co-lending arrangement with larger commercial banks or non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) provides a necessary buffer. This partnership model allows SFBs to utilise their local reach while leveraging the larger balance sheets of their partners.

Current Regulatory Stance

The RBI has maintained a cautious approach to co-lending to ensure systemic stability. While the central bank encourages credit flow to priority sectors, it closely monitors how risks are shared between entities. Any shift in policy would require a thorough assessment of how such partnerships impact the capital adequacy of smaller banks.

SFBs are currently navigating a competitive market where cost of funds remains a critical factor. Expanding co-lending access is seen as a strategic move to manage margins. The banks are waiting for the regulator to review their submissions and provide clarity on whether current operational boundaries will be extended.

Next Steps for the Sector

  • RBI officials are expected to review the submissions in the coming weeks.
  • Industry associations are likely to hold further consultations with the central bank.
  • Market analysts will watch for any circulars regarding revised co-lending norms.

The regulator will now evaluate the impact of these requests on retail credit portfolios before deciding on any policy amendments.
