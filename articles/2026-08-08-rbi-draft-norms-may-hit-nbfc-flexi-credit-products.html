RBI draft norms may hit NBFC flexi-credit products

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI draft rules target revolving credit facilities offered by NBFCs.
  • Flexi-credit products allow borrowers to draw and repay loans flexibly.
  • NBFCs may need to redesign products or face reduced customer uptake.

The Reserve Bank of India's draft guidelines on revolving credit facilities could slow down the growth of flexi-loan products offered by non-banking financial companies (NBFCs). The proposed curbs, aimed at tightening oversight on such credit lines, may force lenders to rethink how they structure these offerings.

What the draft says

The central bank's draft norms seek to regulate revolving credit products, where borrowers can draw funds up to a sanctioned limit and repay as per their convenience. While the exact provisions are still under review, industry watchers suggest the rules could impose stricter caps on repayment tenures and limit the flexibility that makes these products popular.

NBFCs have aggressively marketed flexi-credit as a convenient tool for managing working capital or personal expenses. A sudden shift in the regulatory framework could disrupt that pitch, making it harder for lenders to attract new customers or retain existing ones.

Impact on NBFC business models

For many NBFCs, flexi-credit products form a significant chunk of their retail loan portfolio. The draft curbs could force them to lower sanctioned limits or revise interest structures to comply, potentially squeezing margins.

Smaller lenders, in particular, may feel the heat as they lack the pricing power or operational flexibility of larger players. Some may even pull back from the segment altogether, which would reduce credit availability for borrowers who rely on such lines for short-term needs.

Borrower concerns

Customers who have come to depend on flexi-credit for emergency expenses or business cash flow could face reduced limits or stricter eligibility criteria. The proposed norms may also lead to more frequent credit assessments, adding friction to what was earlier a seamless drawdown process.

However, the RBI's move is seen as a precautionary step to prevent over-leveraging and ensure that revolving credit does not become a source of hidden debt. The regulator has not yet clarified whether existing contracts will be grandfathered or if all products must be revised immediately.

Industry response and next steps

NBFC associations and individual lenders are expected to submit feedback to the RBI during the consultation window. Many are likely to argue for a phased implementation or softer limits to avoid disrupting ongoing lending.

The final shape of the norms will depend on how the regulator weighs industry concerns against its stated goal of financial stability. Until then, lenders are advised to review their product pipelines and prepare for possible changes.

As the consultation progresses, market participants will watch for any tweaks to the draft that could ease the impact on flexi-credit volumes. The coming months are critical for NBFCs to align with the new rules while keeping their customer base intact.
