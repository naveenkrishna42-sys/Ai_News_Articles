NASA's NISAR Satellite Spots 'Hummingbird' Pattern in Antarctica Ice

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • NISAR satellite captured a 'hummingbird' pattern in East Antarctica's ice
  • The shape is formed by ice flows and bedrock features
  • Joint US-India mission provides detailed radar imagery of polar regions

Satellite Spies Bird-Like Formation in Frozen Wastes

A new image from NASA's NISAR satellite has revealed a striking 'hummingbird' pattern hidden deep within the ice of East Antarctica. The joint US-India mission, launched to monitor Earth's surface changes, captured the formation that has drawn comparisons to the tiny bird's distinctive shape.

The pattern, featured as NASA's photo of the day for July 23, 2026, shows a large bird-like structure stretching across the ice sheet. Scientists say the shape is not an illusion but a genuine geological feature formed by the movement of ice over the underlying bedrock.

How Ice Creates Art

The hummingbird pattern emerges from the complex interplay between glacial ice flows and the topography of the land beneath. As ice moves at varying speeds across different terrains, it creates visible striations and patterns on the surface.

NISAR, which stands for NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar, is uniquely equipped to see through clouds and darkness, providing clear images of even the most remote polar regions. The satellite's radar can penetrate the ice surface to map underlying structures with remarkable precision.

What the Pattern Reveals

Experts explain that the hummingbird shape is essentially a map of ice flow dynamics. The 'wings' represent faster-moving ice streams, while the 'body' corresponds to slower-moving sections where ice encounters resistance from the bedrock.

These patterns offer valuable data for climate scientists studying how Antarctic ice sheets respond to warming temperatures. Understanding ice flow helps researchers predict future sea-level rise with greater accuracy.

Joint Mission, Global Insights

NISAR is a collaborative project between NASA and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). Launched in 2024, it represents one of the most ambitious Earth observation missions ever undertaken.

The satellite scans the entire planet every 12 days, monitoring changes in forests, wetlands, ice sheets, and earthquake zones. This hummingbird image is just one example of the unexpected beauty that emerges from scientific data.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the pattern corresponds to any previously unknown subglacial lake or geological structure. Further analysis of the radar data is expected in the coming months.

Looking Ahead

NISAR will continue to map Antarctica's ice for at least three more years, providing an unprecedented record of how the continent's frozen landscapes evolve. The hummingbird pattern will be monitored for changes that could signal shifts in ice flow dynamics.
