'The Last House' Review: Moura and Lee Shine in Stifling Thriller

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Critics praise Wagner Moura and Greta Lee's performances in 'The Last House'.
  • The Netflix thriller is described as suitably stifling but not always thrilling.
  • Reviews highlight the film's claustrophobic atmosphere and character-driven tension.

A Claustrophobic Setup

Netflix's latest psychological thriller, 'The Last House', has landed with a mix of praise and reservation. Critics agree on one thing: Wagner Moura and Greta Lee bring a level of conviction that elevates a story that could easily have collapsed under its own weight.

The film, which unfolds almost entirely within a single, suffocating domestic space, has been described as a stay-at-home thriller. It leans on the chemistry between its two leads to sustain tension. Variety notes that Moura and Lee bring conviction to a suitably stifling scenario, though the film's pacing may test some viewers.

What the Critics Are Saying

The Wall Street Journal is less forgiving, calling it an unthrilling thriller on Netflix. That assessment points to a central problem: the film's atmosphere is heavy, but the suspense doesn't always land. The Hindu echoes this, observing that Moura and Lee are housebound by incuriosity, suggesting the characters' passivity mirrors the film's own hesitations.

Still, the performances are repeatedly singled out as the reason to watch. Moura, known for his intense roles, brings a simmering volatility. Lee, equally compelling, matches him with a quiet, coiled energy. Together, they make the film's slow-burn approach feel intentional, even when the plot stalls.

The Internet's Obsession with the Ending

Beyond the reviews, the film has sparked a wave of online discussion, particularly around its finale. Mashable has already published an explainer titled 'The Last House' ending, explained. Because what? That's a telling sign—the conclusion is divisive, leaving many viewers scratching their heads. It's the kind of ending that invites debate, even if it doesn't fully satisfy.

This online chatter suggests the film has found an audience, even if that audience is split. Some will appreciate the ambiguity; others may find it frustrating. Either way, it's keeping the conversation alive.

A Film of Mood, Not Action

At its core, 'The Last House' is a mood piece. It's less about what happens and more about how it feels to be trapped in that house with these characters. The direction prioritises atmosphere over plot, which works in fits and starts. The stifling quality that Variety praises is real—you can almost feel the walls closing in.

But a thriller needs more than atmosphere. It needs momentum. And that's where the film stumbles. The Hindu's point about incuriosity is sharp: the characters often seem resigned to their fate rather than actively fighting it. That choice may be deliberate, but it costs the film some of its edge.

Ultimately, 'The Last House' is a mixed bag. It's elevated by two committed performances and a palpable sense of dread. But it's also weighed down by a narrative that doesn't always know how to use its strengths.

What to Watch For

As the film settles into Netflix's catalogue, the debate around its ending will likely grow. Whether that translates into sustained viewership remains to be seen. For now, it's a film that will appeal to those who prize character over action—and who don't mind a little confusion in the final act.
