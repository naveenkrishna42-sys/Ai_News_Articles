RBI eases liquidity constraints for large foreign exchange transactions

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has relaxed the weekly access rule for Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) account (FCNR(B)) swaps above $100 million. This move is expected to ease liquidity constraints for large foreign exchange transactions, benefiting both corporates and banks.

Understanding FCNR(B) swaps and the new rules

FCNR(B) swaps are a type of foreign exchange transaction where a bank in India arranges a swap agreement with a non-resident bank. This allows Indian corporates to borrow in foreign currency without the need for a physical presence in the foreign country.

Under the new rules, FCNR(B) swaps above $100 million can now be accessed on a daily basis instead of the previous weekly limit. This change is expected to provide more flexibility and convenience to the participants in the foreign exchange market.

Impact on the foreign exchange market and corporate finance

The relaxation of the weekly access rule for FCNR(B) swaps above $100 million is expected to have a significant impact on the foreign exchange market and corporate finance. It will allow Indian corporates to access larger amounts of foreign currency more frequently, which can be used for various purposes such as debt servicing, expansion, and investment.

Moreover, this move is expected to boost the liquidity in the foreign exchange market, as it will provide more options for banks to manage their foreign exchange exposures. This, in turn, can lead to a more stable and efficient foreign exchange market.

Expert opinion and critical analysis

According to a senior banking analyst, the relaxation of the weekly access rule for FCNR(B) swaps above $100 million is a welcome move that will benefit both corporates and banks. It will provide more flexibility and convenience to the participants in the foreign exchange market, and it will also boost the liquidity in the market.

However, the analyst also cautions that the benefits of this move may not be immediately visible. It will take some time for the market to adjust to the new rules, and for the participants to take full advantage of the increased flexibility and convenience.
