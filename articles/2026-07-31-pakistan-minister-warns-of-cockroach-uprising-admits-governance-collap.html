Pakistan minister warns of 'cockroach' uprising, admits governance collapse

📅 2026-07-31 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mohsin Naqvi admitted that governance in Pakistan has collapsed.
  • He used a 'cockroaches' analogy to describe how united Gen-Z could overturn the status quo.
  • The minister warned of a potential movement similar to the recent CJP-style uprising.

Minister's startling admission

Pakistan's Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi has sent shockwaves through the political establishment with a blunt admission: governance in the country has collapsed. Speaking at an event, he painted a grim picture of the state of affairs, acknowledging that the machinery of government is no longer functioning as it should.

His comments, reported by multiple outlets including ThePrint and NDTV, have ignited a firestorm of debate. Naqvi did not mince words, using a striking analogy that has since dominated headlines and social media discussions across the subcontinent.

The 'cockroach' analogy

Naqvi likened the potential power of the country's youth to that of cockroaches. He warned that if these 'cockroaches' were to unite, they could turn everything upside down. The analogy, though unorthodox, was clearly intended to convey the sheer strength of a collective movement.

His specific reference to Gen-Z suggests a deep concern within the government about the mobilising power of young Pakistanis. The minister appeared to be cautioning that a united youth front could replicate the kind of upheaval seen in recent times, referencing what he termed a 'CJP-style uprising.'

Context of the warning

The reference to a CJP-style movement points to the mass protests led by former Chief Justice of Pakistan, which had a profound impact on the country's political landscape. Naqvi's remarks indicate that the ruling establishment is wary of a similar groundswell of public anger.

By invoking this memory, the minister is not just observing a possibility but issuing a stark warning to those in power. He seems to be saying that the current fragility could be exploited by a determined and unified population, particularly the youth who have shown their ability to organise.

Reactions and implications

The minister's comments have been met with a mixture of disbelief and criticism. Opposition leaders have seized upon his admission of collapsed governance as an indictment of the current administration's performance. Political analysts are poring over his words, trying to gauge whether this is a genuine assessment or a calculated move to justify a crackdown on dissent.

There is also a sense of irony in the analogy, as the term 'cockroach' is often used derogatorily. However, Naqvi appears to have flipped the script, suggesting that these creatures' resilience and numbers make them a formidable force when acting in concert. The message is clear: do not underestimate the power of the many.

What this means for Pakistan

For ordinary Pakistanis, this is a startling admission from a top security official. The country is already grappling with economic turmoil and political uncertainty, and such a statement from the Interior Minister does little to inspire confidence. It raises questions about the state's capacity to maintain order and deliver basic services.

The international community will also be watching closely. A collapse of governance in a nuclear-armed nation has implications far beyond its borders. The minister's words could impact investor confidence and diplomatic relations, as allies may now question the stability of the Pakistani state.

As the dust settles on this rhetorical bombshell, the focus now shifts to the government's next move. Will this be a wake-up call that prompts real reform, or a prelude to more authoritarian measures? The coming weeks will reveal whether Naqvi's warning was a genuine call for introspection or a harbinger of a more turbulent phase in Pakistan's already volatile political saga.
