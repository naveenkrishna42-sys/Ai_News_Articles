Iranian missile strikes Jordan's Aqaba Airport, state media reports

📅 2026-07-21 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iranian state media reports a missile hit Jordan's Aqaba Airport during West Asia hostilities
  • Jordan confirms it downed three Iranian missiles targeting the kingdom
  • Israel military says an Iran missile aimed at Aqaba was intercepted by Israeli and Jordanian forces

Missile strikes Aqaba Airport

An Iranian missile struck Jordan's Aqaba Airport on Monday, according to reports from Iranian state media. The strike is part of a broader escalation in the West Asia region, with multiple projectiles fired toward Jordanian territory.

Jordanian authorities have not yet confirmed the extent of damage at the airport. Officials are assessing the situation, and no casualty figures have been released so far.

Jordan intercepts three Iranian missiles

Separately, Jordan said it downed three Iranian missiles that were targeting the kingdom. The interception occurred amid heightened aerial threats over Jordanian airspace.

Details on where the intercepted missiles fell or whether they caused any damage remain unclear. Jordan's military has not elaborated on the specific locations of the interceptions.

Israel claims joint interception

The Israel military stated that an Iranian missile aimed at Aqaba was intercepted jointly by Israeli and Jordanian forces. This marks a rare instance of cooperation between the two countries in the context of the ongoing conflict.

Israeli officials have not provided further technical details about the interception. The joint operation suggests a coordinated defense mechanism against aerial threats in the region.

Broader regional tensions

The strike on Aqaba Airport is part of a wider pattern of escalation. Reports indicate that an Iranian missile also hit a U.S. troop housing facility in Jordan, according to the Wall Street Journal. Washington has not officially commented on the incident.

The developments come as the West Asia war continues to expand, drawing in multiple countries. The United Nations has called for restraint, but diplomatic efforts have yet to yield a ceasefire.

Both Jordan and Israel are on high alert. Air defense systems remain active, and civilian movements in affected areas are being restricted.

What to watch: Whether Jordan formally requests international assistance to bolster its air defenses and how the U.S. responds to the reported damage at its troop housing facility.
