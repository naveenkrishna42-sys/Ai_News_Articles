Air India turnaround a 5-10 year journey, significant progress made: Tata Sons Chairman

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tata Sons Chairman N Chandrasekaran said Air India's turnaround will take 5 to 10 years, comparing it to building great airlines over decades.
  • Air India's FY26 net loss doubled to ₹22,238 crore, reflecting heavy investment in fleet, technology, and service upgrades.
  • The airline is making 'significant progress' on operational metrics like on-time performance, customer satisfaction, and network expansion.

Tata Sons Chairman N Chandrasekaran has laid out a sober but determined timeline for Air India's revival, stating the airline's full transformation will take five to ten years. Speaking to media on Monday, Chandrasekaran said the flagship carrier was on the right path, even as its financial results showed a deepening of losses.

'Every great airline was built over decades, not quarters,' Chandrasekaran said, defending the pace of the turnaround. He stressed that the structural changes required to overhaul a legacy carrier cannot be rushed.

Losses widen as investment cycle peaks

Air India reported a net loss of ₹22,238 crore for the financial year 2025-26, more than double the previous year's loss. The sharp increase reflects the heavy capital expenditure involved in modernising a fleet that had been neglected for years under government ownership.

The airline has placed orders for hundreds of new aircraft, including wide-body planes for long-haul routes. It is also investing heavily in cabin refurbishment, IT systems, and training programmes to bring service standards up to global benchmarks.

Industry analysts say such investment cycles inevitably strain near-term finances. 'The losses were expected. The question is whether the revenue growth will catch up in the next two to three years,' a senior aviation analyst tracking the airline said.

Operational metrics show green shoots

Chandrasekaran pointed to several operational improvements that he said were 'significant progress'. On-time performance has improved noticeably across domestic and international routes. Customer satisfaction scores, which had plummeted in the final years of government control, are now trending upward.

The airline has also expanded its network, adding new destinations and increasing frequencies on key routes. 'We are seeing the results of our efforts in the data,' the chairman said, without giving specific numbers.

These improvements come as Air India faces stiff competition from IndiGo in the domestic market and from Gulf carriers like Emirates and Qatar Airways on international sectors. The airline has been working to reclaim its share of the premium travel segment, a space it once dominated.

Management backs long haul over quick fixes

Chandrasekaran's comments signal that the Tata group is prepared to absorb short-term losses in pursuit of a deeper overhaul. This approach mirrors how the group rebuilt other acquisitions, including Jaguar Land Rover and Tata Steel's European operations, both of which took years to turn around.

Air India's management has been refreshing its senior leadership, bringing in talent from rivals and from global aviation. The airline is also rationalising its workforce, offering voluntary retirement schemes to older employees while hiring younger staff trained in modern service protocols.

One of the biggest challenges remains the integration of Air India and Vistara, the joint venture with Singapore Airlines that the Tatas fully acquired last year. The merger of the two full-service carriers is expected to create operational synergies but has also created teething problems in scheduling and crew allocation.

Chandrasekaran acknowledged these issues but said the integration was on track. 'These are the messy parts of building something big,' he said. 'We will get through it.'

The airline has also been working to improve its cargo and engineering businesses, which were historically underperforming. Both units are now being positioned as separate profit centres with dedicated leadership.

Looking ahead, all eyes will be on Air India's next quarterly results, due in October. Investors and industry watchers will be looking for signs that revenue growth is starting to close the gap with costs. The airline's debt pile, while not publicly detailed in this context, remains a concern that the Tatas will need to address as the turnaround progresses.
