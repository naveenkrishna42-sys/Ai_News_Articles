Anti-Drug Activist Parvinder Jhota Shot Dead in Punjab After Confronting Chemist

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-10-13 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Parvinder Jhota was shot dead immediately after confronting a chemist in Mansa, Punjab, whom he accused of selling drugs.
  • A video recorded the activist instructing his associate to film and reportedly saying 'open fire at us' moments before the shooting.
  • The killing underscores the lethal risks for local anti-drug activists in Punjab, where pharmaceutical diversion remains a major supply route for narcotics.

Final Moments Captured on Video

Punjab anti-drug activist Parvinder Jhota was shot dead in broad daylight in Mansa district after he confronted the owner of a medical store over alleged illegal sale of drugs. A video recorded by his associate captured the tense exchange and the activist's final instructions before gunfire erupted. In the footage, Jhota is seen directing the person filming to capture evidence while reportedly challenging the chemist, with voices indicating 'open fire at us' in the critical seconds leading to the shooting.

The incident occurred on Tuesday when Jhota, known for his sustained campaign against chemists peddling contraband and prescription drugs as substitutes for harder narcotics, entered the shop to record evidence. Police have registered a case and launched an investigation. The medical store owner is reported to be among those detained in connection with the murder.

The Activist's Campaign Against Pharmaceutical Drug Diversion

Parvinder Jhota had built a reputation in the Mansa region for directly confronting pharmacies suspected of diverting psychotropic and opioid medicines into the illicit market. Punjab continues to battle a severe drug crisis where tramadol, codeine-based cough syrups, and other pharmaceutical preparations often serve as the entry point or substitute for heroin and other street drugs. Activists like Jhota have repeatedly alleged that certain chemists operate with political or police protection, making grassroots action particularly hazardous.

His approach involved surprise visits, video documentation, and public shaming of offenders, a method that yielded local results but also created powerful enemies. Several similar activists in Punjab have faced threats, intimidation, and physical attacks in recent years. Jhota's death marks one of the most brazen killings of an anti-drug campaigner in the state in recent memory.

Real-World Implications for Citizen-Led Anti-Drug Efforts

This murder exposes the vulnerability of ordinary citizens and local activists who fill the gap left by inconsistent enforcement. In Punjab, government crackdowns on synthetic drugs and opium derivatives have been announced repeatedly, yet diversion from licensed medical shops remains a documented supply channel. When activists attempt to gather evidence independently, they often operate without institutional protection or witness security.

Buyer and citizen implications are clear: communities relying on such activists for accountability face heightened risks. Families of victims like Jhota are left without the primary breadwinner while the larger battle against pharmaceutical narcotics continues. The incident is likely to deter other volunteers from direct confrontation tactics and may push more groups toward anonymous reporting or collaboration with dedicated narcotics police units.

Response from Authorities and Broader Context

Punjab Police have confirmed the registration of an FIR and stated that multiple angles, including the chemist's alleged drug peddling and direct murder charges, are being investigated. Senior officers have promised swift action. However, activists and opposition voices have questioned whether the state provides adequate safeguards for individuals who publicly challenge the drug supply network at the retail level.

The case arrives against the backdrop of Punjab's long-standing struggle with substance abuse that affects thousands of young people. Pharmaceutical drugs constitute a significant portion of the problem because they are legally manufactured, relatively easy to obtain in bulk, and harder to track than smuggled heroin. Jhota's killing is a grim reminder that the fight against this parallel economy carries mortal danger for those on the front lines.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Video evidence directly captures the confrontation and moments before the fatal shooting, strengthening the prosecution's case.
  • Parvinder Jhota's sustained activism focused specifically on medical stores diverting prescription drugs, a major but under-reported vector in Punjab's drug crisis.
  • The brazen public killing is likely to chill citizen participation in anti-drug vigilance drives across rural and semi-urban Punjab.
  • Authorities must now balance investigation speed with protection for witnesses and remaining activists in the area.
  • This incident highlights the limits of individual activism without coordinated state support and witness security mechanisms.
