Bitcoin Hovers Near $64,600 as Inflation Cools, Geopolitical Risks Cap Gains

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin is trading close to $64,600, reflecting cautious optimism.
  • Cooling US inflation data has improved market sentiment for risk assets like crypto.
  • Geopolitical risks, including tensions in the Middle East, are capping further gains.
  • Traders are watching for clearer signals before committing to larger positions.

Bitcoin is hovering near the $64,600 mark on Thursday, caught between two powerful forces: cooling inflation in the United States and simmering geopolitical tensions that are keeping a lid on any major rally.

The world's largest cryptocurrency has been trading in a narrow band over the past 24 hours, struggling to break decisively above the $65,000 resistance level. Market participants are weighing the positive impact of softer inflation data against the uncertainty from global conflict zones.

Inflation data boosts sentiment

The latest US consumer price index (CPI) report, released on Wednesday, showed inflation easing more than expected. This has fueled hopes that the Federal Reserve may soon pivot to a less aggressive monetary policy stance, which typically benefits risk assets like cryptocurrencies.

Lower inflation reduces the pressure on the Fed to keep interest rates high. When rates are high, borrowing becomes expensive, and investors often pull money out of volatile assets like Bitcoin. The cooling CPI print has provided a fresh tailwind for the crypto market.

Bitcoin briefly touched $64,800 in early Asian trading before pulling back slightly. Analysts say the market is now pricing in a higher probability of rate cuts later this year, which could drive more institutional money into digital assets.

Geopolitical risks remain a drag

Despite the positive inflation news, Bitcoin's gains have been capped by ongoing geopolitical instability. Tensions in the Middle East, particularly the conflict between Israel and Hamas, continue to weigh on investor sentiment globally. Any escalation could trigger a flight to safe-haven assets like gold and the US dollar, away from riskier bets.

Oil prices have also been volatile, adding to the uncertainty. Higher energy costs can feed into inflation, complicating the Fed's decision-making. For now, traders are adopting a wait-and-watch approach.

“The market is in a tug of war,” said one trader. “On one hand, the macro picture is improving. On the other, nobody wants to get caught offside if something blows up geopolitically.”

Technical levels to watch

From a technical perspective, the $64,000 level has emerged as key support. If Bitcoin can hold above that and break through $65,000, the next target could be $66,500. A failure to hold support could see a retest of the $63,000 zone.

Trading volumes have been moderate, suggesting that neither bulls nor bears are willing to make a decisive move. The market is waiting for a catalyst—either a clear signal from the Fed on rate cuts or a de-escalation in global tensions.

Bitcoin's price action remains tightly correlated with broader risk sentiment. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq have also posted modest gains this week, tracking the inflation data, but have not seen a breakout either.

What’s next for Bitcoin?

All eyes are now on the Fed's next policy meeting. If officials signal a readiness to cut rates, Bitcoin could see a strong rally. Conversely, if they strike a hawkish tone, the gains could evaporate quickly.

Meanwhile, geopolitical developments will remain a wildcard. Any ceasefire or de-escalation in the Middle East could remove a major headwind, while new flare-ups could push Bitcoin back toward $60,000.

For now, the market is in a holding pattern, with Bitcoin dancing near $64,600 as traders weigh the bull case against the bear case. The next big move could come from either direction.
