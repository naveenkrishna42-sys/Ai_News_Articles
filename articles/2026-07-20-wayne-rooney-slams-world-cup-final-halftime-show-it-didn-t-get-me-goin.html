Wayne Rooney slams World Cup final halftime show: 'It didn't get me going'

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Wayne Rooney called the World Cup 2026 final halftime show 'crap' and said it 'didn't get me going'.
  • The show featured major global pop stars including Madonna, BTS, Justin Bieber, and Shakira.
  • Rooney's blunt verdict has gone viral on social media, drawing mixed reactions from fans.
  • The Spain vs Argentina final in 2026 was the first to feature a full halftime entertainment spectacle.
  • The halftime break itself was reportedly the longest in World Cup final history.

Wayne Rooney has delivered a characteristically blunt verdict on the first-ever World Cup final halftime show, calling it flat and uninspiring. The former England and Manchester United striker did not hold back, telling reporters the performance 'didn't get me going'.

Rooney's comments came after the halftime entertainment during the Spain vs Argentina final in the 2026 FIFA World Cup. The show was a landmark moment — the first time a men's World Cup final had a dedicated halftime concert. But Rooney was not impressed.

'It was crap'

Speaking to media after the match, Rooney said the lineup failed to match the occasion. 'It didn't get me going at all. It was crap, to be honest,' he reportedly said. The Times of India and ESPN both quoted his blunt assessment, which quickly went viral on social media.

The halftime show featured a star-studded roster: Madonna, BTS, Justin Bieber, and Shakira. For many fans, the mix of pop royalty and K-pop superstars was a spectacle. Not for Rooney. He made clear that the energy of the performance did not match the tension of a World Cup final.

Mixed reactions from fans

Rooney's criticism has split opinion online. Some fans agreed, noting that the show felt disjointed and lacked the raw excitement of the match. Others defended the lineup, arguing that it offered something for a global audience.

The halftime break itself was the longest in World Cup final history, according to BBC. The extended interval was designed to accommodate the elaborate staging and multiple performer transitions. But for Rooney, more time did not mean more entertainment.

A new tradition under scrutiny

The 2026 final marked a turning point for FIFA, which introduced the concept of a Super Bowl-style halftime show for the first time. The move was intended to boost the tournament's global entertainment appeal. Rooney's comments suggest the experiment may not have landed with everyone.

The match itself was a tense affair, with Spain and Argentina locked in a high-stakes battle. Rooney, a veteran of many big games, likely expected the halftime show to amplify the drama. Instead, he found it a distraction.

His verdict echoes a sentiment that many purists share: football should be the main event. Whether FIFA will tweak the format for the 2030 World Cup remains to be seen. For now, the first halftime show will be remembered as much for Rooney's review as for the performances themselves.

What comes next? FIFA is expected to review feedback from the 2026 final as it plans entertainment for future tournaments. Rooney's words may carry weight, but the commercial pull of global pop stars is unlikely to fade.
