Chennai Corporation sets up panels to curb unauthorised buildings

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chennai Corporation has formed monitoring committees to check unauthorised buildings.
  • The move aims to ensure compliance with building regulations and prevent illegal constructions.
  • Committees will inspect and take action against violations across all zones.

The Greater Chennai Corporation has formed monitoring committees across all zones to crack down on unauthorised buildings in the city. The decision comes amid rising concerns over illegal constructions that flout building rules and pose safety risks.

Committees to ensure strict enforcement

The newly formed committees will be responsible for identifying and inspecting unauthorised buildings. They will also ensure that all construction activities adhere to the approved plans and norms set by the Corporation.

Officials said these panels will operate at the zonal level, with members drawn from the Corporation's engineering, planning, and revenue departments. Each committee will have the authority to issue notices, impose fines, and recommend demolition of structures that violate regulations.

Focus on high-risk areas

The monitoring committees will prioritise areas where illegal construction is rampant. Zones with a high density of unauthorised buildings, especially those near water bodies or on government land, will be under close watch.

Corporation sources indicated that the committees will also review pending cases of building violations. They will coordinate with local police and revenue officials to ensure swift action against offenders.

Background of the crackdown

The move follows several incidents where unauthorised buildings collapsed or caused accidents in Chennai. In recent years, the Corporation has faced criticism for lax enforcement of building bylaws.

Senior officials said the monitoring committees are part of a broader strategy to tighten oversight. The Corporation has also begun digitising building plan approvals to reduce scope for manual errors or corruption.

Residents in many neighbourhoods have long complained about illegal constructions that block ventilation, encroach on roads, or violate floor space index (FSI) limits. The new committees are expected to address these grievances more systematically.

Action plan for violators

The committees will follow a standard operating procedure. First, they will survey and document all buildings suspected of being unauthorised. Second, they will issue show-cause notices to owners, giving them a chance to explain or regularise the structure.

If the building cannot be regularised, the committee will recommend demolition. The Corporation will then carry out the demolition at the owner's cost. Repeat offenders may face legal action under the Tamil Nadu Town and Country Planning Act.

Officials clarified that the monitoring committees are not meant to harass genuine builders. They said the focus is on curbing large-scale illegal constructions that compromise public safety.

What happens next

The Corporation expects the committees to begin field inspections within a week. A senior official said the first round of surveys will cover all 15 zones, with a special focus on areas like Sholinganallur, Perungudi, and Madhavaram where violations are common.

Residents can also report suspected unauthorised buildings through the Corporation's helpline or online portal. The committees will be required to respond to such complaints within 15 days.

Whether this initiative succeeds in curbing illegal construction will depend on consistent enforcement and willingness to take on powerful builders. The coming months will show if the committees can deliver results or become yet another bureaucratic layer.
