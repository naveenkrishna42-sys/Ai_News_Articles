Sensex Falls 456 Pts, Nifty Below 24,600 as Financials Drag

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell 456 points, Nifty slipped below 24,600 on Aug 7.
  • Financial stocks were the main drag on the market.
  • Broader market indices also traded lower amid weak global cues.

Markets Close Lower as Banking Stocks Slump

Indian equity benchmarks ended the session on a sombre note on Friday, August 7, as selling pressure in financial stocks pulled the indices lower. The BSE Sensex tumbled 456 points, while the NSE Nifty slipped below the 24,600 mark, underscoring a risk-off mood among investors.

The decline was broad-based, but the financial sector bore the brunt of the sell-off. Banking and financial services heavyweights traded in the red throughout the day, dragging the benchmarks from their intraday highs. Analysts pointed to profit-booking after recent gains, coupled with concerns over global interest rate trends.

Financials Lead the Slide

Shares of major lenders and financial institutions were among the top losers, contributing significantly to the Sensex's fall. The Nifty Bank index also closed lower, reflecting the weakness in the sector. Market participants said the selling was largely driven by caution ahead of upcoming economic data releases and corporate earnings announcements.

While no single trigger was identified, traders noted that elevated valuations in some financial stocks prompted investors to trim positions. The broader market indices—the BSE Midcap and Smallcap—also traded lower, mirroring the sentiment on the main bourses.

Weak Global Cues Add Pressure

Sentiment was also dampened by muted cues from global markets. Asian peers traded mixed, and European indices opened on a weak note, adding to the domestic market's woes. Fears of prolonged high interest rates in major economies have kept investors on edge, limiting risk appetite across asset classes.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the cash market, according to provisional data, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) provided some support through their purchases. The rupee, meanwhile, held steady against the US dollar, though traders remain watchful of any sharp moves.

What Dragged the Sensex

The Sensex's 456-point fall marked one of the sharpest single-day declines in recent weeks. Among the top index losers were private sector banks, state-run lenders, and financial services firms. However, select IT and FMCG stocks managed to stay afloat, offering some respite to the indices.

Market breadth was negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. This suggested that the selling pressure was not confined to large-caps but had spread across the broader market.

Investors Await Fresh Triggers

With the earnings season largely behind, investors are now looking to macroeconomic indicators and global policy cues for direction. The upcoming US Federal Reserve meeting minutes and domestic inflation data are likely to be key factors in the near term.

Market participants also keep an eye on the monsoon progress and its impact on rural demand, which could influence consumer-focused sectors. Any further escalation in geopolitical tensions could also weigh on investor sentiment.

As the market consolidates, traders advise caution and suggest focusing on quality stocks with strong fundamentals. The coming sessions are expected to see volatility as investors reposition their portfolios.
