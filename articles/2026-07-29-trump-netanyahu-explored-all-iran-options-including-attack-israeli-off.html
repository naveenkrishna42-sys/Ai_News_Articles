Trump, Netanyahu Explored All Iran Options Including Attack: Israeli Official

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Israeli official says Trump and Netanyahu explored all options on Iran, including an attack
  • Discussion took place during Netanyahu's visit to Washington
  • Details emerge amid ongoing US-Israel tensions over Iran strategy

High-Level Talks on Iran Strategy

An Israeli official has confirmed that former US President Donald Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu explored all options regarding Iran, including the possibility of a military attack, during their recent meeting in Washington.

The revelation comes as Netanyahu continues his visit to the US capital, where the two leaders held extensive discussions on the Iranian nuclear programme and regional security. The official, speaking on condition of anonymity, did not provide details on whether any specific operational plans were discussed or approved.

Scope of Discussions

The talks covered a wide spectrum of potential responses to Iran's nuclear ambitions, ranging from diplomatic pressure to economic sanctions and military action. Sources familiar with the matter indicate that the leaders reviewed intelligence assessments and military scenarios.

This development underscores the shifting dynamics in US-Israel relations. Trump, who has maintained a close relationship with Netanyahu, has publicly stated that Washington is holding 'friendly talks' with Iran, even as the two allies coordinate their approach.

Context of the Visit

Netanyahu's Washington trip coincides with what some analysts have termed 'Day 150 of the US-Israel War Against Iran', though official channels have not confirmed any active conflict. The prime minister's schedule includes meetings with senior US officials and lawmakers.

The visit also comes amid reports that Trump faces a two-front challenge, with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy also converging on Washington for separate talks. This has led to comparisons with the geopolitical tensions of 1914, as world leaders scramble to address multiple crises simultaneously.

Regional Implications

Israel has long viewed Iran's nuclear programme as an existential threat, and has repeatedly stated that it reserves the right to take preemptive action. The exploration of 'all options' with the US signals a hardening of positions, even as diplomatic channels remain open.

Iran has consistently denied seeking nuclear weapons, insisting its programme is for civilian purposes. However, international inspectors have documented uranium enrichment levels that exceed limits set under the 2015 nuclear deal, which the US withdrew from in 2018 under Trump.

The official's confirmation of the wide-ranging talks is likely to raise concerns in Tehran and among European allies who have advocated for renewed diplomatic engagement.

What to Watch

Both Trump and Netanyahu are expected to address the media later this week, potentially offering more clarity on the outcomes of their discussions. The coming days will also see whether the US and Israel move to implement any concrete measures against Iran, or whether the 'friendly talks' with Tehran yield any tangible results.
