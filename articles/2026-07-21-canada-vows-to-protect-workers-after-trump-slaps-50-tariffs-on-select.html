Canada vows to protect workers after Trump slaps 50% tariffs on select goods

📅 2026-07-21 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US President Donald Trump announced 50% tariffs on select Canadian goods.
  • Canada's Prime Minister Mark Carney called the move a 'direct violation' of trade agreements.
  • Canada vows to intensify trade talks and protect its workers from the impact.

Canada has vowed to protect its workers and retaliate after the United States imposed a fresh round of 50% tariffs on select Canadian goods. The move, announced by former US President Donald Trump, has been described by Canadian officials as a 'direct violation' of existing trade agreements.

The White House confirmed the tariffs late Monday, targeting key Canadian exports. The decision threatens to reignite a bitter trade war between the two neighbours, who have long been each other's largest trading partners.

Canadian response: 'We will not back down'

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney reacted swiftly, calling the tariffs an attack on Canadian workers. 'This is a direct violation of the trade commitments both nations signed,' Carney said in a statement. 'We will not back down. We will protect our industries and our people.'

Carney said his government would intensify ongoing trade talks with the US. He did not rule out dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs on American goods entering Canada. The Canadian dollar dipped against the US dollar in early trading, reflecting market jitters.

What the tariffs cover

The 50% tariffs apply to a range of Canadian goods, though the White House has not released a full list. Early reports suggest the tariffs target steel, aluminium, and agricultural products—sectors vital to Canada's economy. The move echoes Trump's earlier trade actions during his first term, when he imposed steep levies on Canadian metals.

The Trump administration justified the tariffs as a necessary measure to protect American industries from what it calls 'unfair' Canadian subsidies. 'Canada has taken advantage of the United States for decades,' a White House spokesperson said. 'This is about fairness and putting American workers first.'

US lawmakers from Michigan, a state heavily affected by Canadian imports, have voiced strong support for the tariffs. In a statement, Michigan Republicans said, 'Canada's apologies won't clear Michigan's skies.' They argue that Canadian trade practices have hurt American manufacturing and jobs.

Potential fallout for both economies

Economists warn the tariffs could have severe consequences on both sides of the border. Canada is the top export market for over 30 US states. A prolonged trade war could raise prices for consumers in both countries and disrupt supply chains for industries like automotive manufacturing, where parts cross the border multiple times before final assembly.

Canada has already prepared a list of retaliatory tariffs targeting US goods such as orange juice, washing machines, and bourbon whiskey. These measures were originally drawn up during Trump's first term but never fully implemented. Carney's government is now expected to activate them swiftly.

Trade experts say the situation is fluid and negotiations will be critical in the coming weeks. 'Both sides have a lot to lose,' said a trade analyst. 'But right now, the rhetoric is escalating faster than the diplomacy.'

Canadian officials have also reached out to other trading partners, including the European Union and Mexico, to coordinate a response. Carney is expected to hold a press conference later this week to outline a comprehensive support package for affected Canadian workers and businesses.

The coming days will test whether the two nations can find common ground or whether the world's largest bilateral trading relationship will spiral into a full-blown economic conflict. All eyes are now on the next round of talks, scheduled for early next week.
