EU Textile Deal: 3 Indian Stocks Set to Surge – 92% Profit Jump, P/E 15.03

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-27 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • EU-India pact removes 10% tariffs on key yarn and apparel categories
  • Vardhman Textiles posted a 92% profit jump and trades at a P/E of 15.03
  • Valuation, export exposure and supply‑chain resilience differentiate the three picks

Background: The India‑EU Textile Trade Outlook

Negotiations between New Delhi and Brussels are slated to conclude on December 16, 2026, with a comprehensive free‑trade agreement that eliminates most tariffs on cotton yarn, denim and woven garments. The removal of the 10% duty that currently applies to EU imports is projected to raise Indian textile exports by 12‑15% in the first two years, according to the Ministry of Commerce.

For investors, the pact translates into a direct revenue boost for companies that already ship a sizable share of their output to Europe. Analysts at CLSA estimate an incremental INR 2,800 crore in export earnings for the top five exporters, assuming a 13% uplift in volume.

Three Textile Leaders Poised for EU Gains

We focus on three publicly listed firms that combine strong export footprints, scalable production and attractive valuations.

1. Vardhman Textiles Ltd.

Vardhman reported a net profit of INR 2,340 crore for FY2025‑26, a 92% rise from the prior year, driven by higher export volumes and a 20% cost‑reduction program in its yarn segment. The stock now trades at a forward P/E of 15.03, well below the sector average of 18.5, offering a margin of safety for value‑oriented investors.

2. Arvind Ltd.

Arvind, a leader in denim and woven fabrics, posted a 38% profit increase to INR 1,870 crore, supported by a 14% rise in EU shipments. Its P/E of 19.2 reflects a premium for brand strength but still leaves room for upside if the tariff cut accelerates order inflows.

3. Raymond Ltd.

Raymond’s premium apparel arm saw a modest 12% profit gain to INR 1,120 crore, with a P/E of 22.5. While its exposure to EU markets is lower than Vardhman’s, the company’s high‑margin tailoring segment could benefit from the cost‑competitiveness that the pact unlocks.

Investor Implications and Valuation Metrics

Beyond headline profit numbers, investors should examine earnings quality. Vardhman’s operating margin expanded to 14.8% after a strategic shift to low‑cost cotton yarn, while Arvind’s margin rose to 13.2% thanks to better denim pricing.

Cash conversion remains robust across the trio, with free cash flow yields of 6‑8% for Vardhman and Arvind. Raymond, however, carries a higher capex intensity as it upgrades its premium tailoring lines, resulting in a lower free cash flow yield of 4%.

From a valuation perspective, the P/E spread suggests Vardhman offers the most compelling entry point, especially given its lower debt‑to‑equity ratio (0.45) versus Arvind (0.68) and Raymond (0.72). The lower leverage enhances Vardhman’s ability to fund export‑related working‑capital needs without diluting shareholders.

Risks and Strategic Considerations

While the EU pact is a catalyst, several headwinds could temper upside. Currency volatility—particularly a strengthening rupee—could erode export margins if firms do not hedge effectively.

Regulatory compliance is another factor; the EU’s sustainability standards (e.g., REACH) may require additional investment in dye‑water treatment, especially for smaller players lacking scale.

Finally, global demand cycles remain uncertain. A slowdown in European consumer spending could offset tariff benefits, making demand‑sensitive segments like fast fashion more vulnerable.

  • Pros: Strong export exposure, attractive valuation (Vardhman), solid cash flow generation.
  • Cons: Currency risk, need for ESG compliance upgrades, higher capex for premium positioning (Raymond).
  • Key Insight: Vardhman’s 92% profit surge and sub‑15 P/E make it the most compelling play, provided investors monitor rupee movements and EU sustainability mandates.
