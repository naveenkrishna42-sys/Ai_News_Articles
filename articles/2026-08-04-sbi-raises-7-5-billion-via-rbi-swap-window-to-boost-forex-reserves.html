SBI Raises $7.5 Billion Via RBI Swap Window to Boost Forex Reserves

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • SBI raised $7.5 billion through RBI's swap facility
  • Move aims to bolster India's foreign exchange reserves
  • Reflects coordinated effort to stabilize rupee

Mobilising Dollars

State Bank of India (SBI), the country's largest lender, has raised $7.5 billion through the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) swap window. The transaction, executed recently, is part of a broader strategy to shore up India's foreign exchange reserves.

The swap window allows banks to exchange dollars for rupees with the central bank, providing liquidity in the forex market. This move comes amid global market volatility and a renewed focus on currency stability.

Mechanics of the Swap

Under the RBI's swap facility, banks deposit US dollars with the central bank and receive rupees in return, with an agreement to reverse the transaction at a future date. The arrangement helps manage liquidity without depleting reserves.

SBI's participation underscores its role as a key intermediary in India's financial system. The funds are expected to be used to meet domestic dollar demand, including for trade finance and corporate borrowings.

Impact on Forex Reserves

The $7.5 billion raised is likely to be added to India's foreign exchange kitty, which has seen fluctuations in recent months. As of the latest data, India's forex reserves stood at over $650 billion, providing a cushion against external shocks.

Analysts view the move as a proactive measure to preempt any sharp depreciation of the rupee. The currency has been under pressure due to global interest rate differentials and crude oil price movements.

Market Reactions

Bond yields and the rupee showed little immediate reaction, suggesting the market had priced in such interventions. Traders noted that the swap window is a routine tool used by RBI to manage forex liquidity.

Some experts argue that large-scale swaps could signal concerns about capital outflows. Foreign portfolio investors have been net sellers in Indian equities in recent weeks, adding to the pressure on the rupee.

Looking Ahead

The RBI's swap window is expected to remain active as long as global conditions warrant. SBI's move may prompt other banks to follow suit, further augmenting reserves.

Market participants will closely watch the central bank's next policy stance, scheduled for later this year. The focus will be on how the RBI balances growth support with currency stability in an uncertain global environment.
