Nike CEO Signals Fresh Layoffs Amid Operating Model Overhaul

Nike’s chief executive John Donahoe told investors that the company expects additional workforce reductions in 2027. The announcement came during a quarterly earnings call where Donahoe outlined a revised operating model designed to cut complexity and accelerate decision‑making.

Why the Operating Model Is Changing

Nike cites slowing growth in certain geographic segments and rising supply‑chain costs as drivers for the overhaul. The new model will consolidate regional offices, streamline product‑development cycles, and increase reliance on data‑driven planning. Executives say the goal is to create a more agile organization that can respond faster to consumer trends.

Projected Scale and Timeline of Job Cuts

While exact numbers have not been disclosed, internal sources suggest the layoffs could affect roughly 5‑7% of Nike’s global workforce, translating to several thousand positions. The reductions are slated to begin in the first half of 2027 and continue through the fiscal year, with a focus on corporate and administrative roles rather than retail store staff.

Historical Context: Nike’s Past Restructuring Efforts

This is not the first time Nike has trimmed its workforce. In 2020 the company cut about 2% of employees amid pandemic‑related disruptions, and in 2022 another round targeted overlapping functions after the acquisition of Céline’s digital assets. Those earlier moves delivered measurable cost savings but also prompted Nike to invest in reskilling programs for retained staff.

Implications for Stakeholders

Investors may view the layoffs as a step toward improving operating margins, which have faced pressure from higher freight costs and currency fluctuations. Analysts warn that excessive cuts could harm innovation capacity if key design or marketing talent is lost. Employees in affected regions should prepare for potential relocation offers or severance packages, while Nike’s brand teams will need to maintain morale to protect the company’s consumer‑facing image.
