Seven years on, J&K statehood delay a breach of trust, say critics

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Seven years after Article 370 was scrapped, demands for J&K statehood have intensified.
  • Opposition leaders call the continued delay a breach of trust with the people of Jammu and Kashmir.
  • The Centre defends its position, citing development and integration milestones.
  • Debate over land and job safeguards for J&K residents continues in political circles.

It has been seven years since Parliament abrogated Article 370 and split the state of Jammu and Kashmir into two Union territories. The move, announced on August 5, 2019, was billed as the dawn of a new era of integration. But as the anniversary passes, a persistent question refuses to fade: when will J&K get its statehood back?

Political parties in the opposition, along with several regional voices, have stepped up their demand. The argument is straightforward — the central government had promised statehood would be restored. Delaying it any longer, they say, is a breach of trust.

Statehood promise under scrutiny

At the time of the abrogation, Home Minister Amit Shah had assured Parliament that statehood would be granted "at the right time". That phrase has now become a point of contention. Seven years on, the Union Territory status remains, and the promise feels increasingly distant to many in the Valley.

Opposition MPs, including Syed Naseer Hussain of the Congress, have raised the issue in Parliament. The demand is not just for statehood but for constitutional safeguards on land and jobs for the region's residents. These protections, they argue, are essential to prevent demographic change and economic marginalisation.

A day of polarising narratives

August 5 has come to mean different things to different people. For the ruling dispensation, it marks the culmination of a historical correction — the end of what it calls temporary special status. For many in Kashmir, it is a day that changed their lives in ways still being understood.

The anniversary was observed across the spectrum. While the government highlighted development projects, security improvements and the end of what it terms "decades of discrimination", opposition leaders and civil society groups in the Valley held quieter, more sombre reflections on the erosion of political identity.

Debate over safeguards and rights

The Congress party has been particularly vocal. Syed Naseer Hussain's demand for constitutional safeguards for land and jobs reflects a broader anxiety. With J&K's special status gone, many fear that outsiders could buy land and compete for jobs, altering the region's demographic character.

Such fears are not unfounded. The abrogation removed the special provisions that restricted property purchase by non-residents. While the government has said it will protect the interests of the local population, no formal constitutional amendment has been made to that effect. This ambiguity has kept the issue alive.

What the government says

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has spoken of a new chapter for J&K, citing increased tourism, better infrastructure and the holding of panchayat elections. The government maintains that the region is on a path of development and that statehood will be considered once the situation is fully normalised.

Officials have not yet confirmed any timeline for the restoration of statehood. The silence from the Centre has only added to the frustration of those who see the promise as a solemn commitment, not a casual remark.

What lies ahead

As the seventh anniversary passes, the political temperature in the Valley remains high. The demand for statehood is not just a legal or administrative issue; it is a matter of trust between the people of J&K and the central government.

All eyes are now on the Supreme Court, which is hearing petitions challenging the abrogation. A ruling could shape the future of the region. Until then, the wait for statehood continues, with each passing year making the promise harder to ignore.

The next few months will show whether the government moves toward restoring statehood or continues to defer the decision. For the people of J&K, the clock is ticking.
