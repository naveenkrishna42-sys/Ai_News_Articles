Assam, Kerala, Puducherry vote in 2026 elections; test for BJP

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • Voting underway in Assam, Kerala and Puducherry for assembly elections
  • Elections seen as a test for BJP's popularity under PM Modi
  • Opposition alliances in Kerala and Puducherry pose significant challenge

Voters in Assam, Kerala and the union territory of Puducherry are heading to the polls today in what is widely seen as a major test for Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP). The elections, being held across three distinct regions, will gauge the party's electoral strength and the effectiveness of its campaign strategies.

Polling began early this morning across thousands of booths. State election commissions have made elaborate arrangements to ensure smooth voting, with security forces deployed in sensitive areas. Voter turnout was reported to be steady in the initial hours, with long queues seen at many centres.

Assam: BJP seeks to retain power amid local concerns

In Assam, the BJP is aiming to retain power after its victory in the 2021 assembly elections. The party has focused its campaign on development, infrastructure projects, and cultural identity. Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma has been the face of the BJP's outreach, holding multiple rallies across the state.

The opposition, led by the Congress and regional parties, has highlighted issues such as floods, unemployment, and land rights. The All India United Democratic Front (AIUDF) and the Bodoland People's Front are also in the fray, making the contest multi-cornered in several seats.

Kerala: Left vs Congress vs BJP in triangular fight

Kerala presents a more complex challenge for the BJP. The state has traditionally been a stronghold of the Left Democratic Front (LDF) and the Congress-led United Democratic Front (UDF). The BJP has been trying to make inroads, but has so far failed to win a significant number of seats.

The elections in Kerala are being closely watched to see if the BJP can improve its vote share. The party has invested heavily in organisational work and fielded candidates across all constituencies. The LDF, under Chief Minister Pinarayi Vijayan, is seeking a third consecutive term, while the UDF is banking on anti-incumbency and local issues to return to power.

Puducherry: A small territory with big political implications

Puducherry, the smallest of the three regions voting today, is a union territory with a unique political history. The BJP is in power here in alliance with the All India N.R. Congress (AINRC). The party is hoping to retain control, but faces a strong challenge from the Congress-DMK alliance.

Local issues such as infrastructure, tourism, and the state's finances dominate the campaign. The election in Puducherry is often seen as a bellwether for political trends in neighbouring Tamil Nadu, where assembly elections are due next year.

The results of these elections will be announced on May 2. Political analysts say the outcomes will have a significant bearing on the BJP's strategy for the 2029 general elections. A strong performance in all three states would boost the party's confidence, while setbacks could embolden the opposition.

For now, the focus is on the voters. Their choices today will shape the political narrative for months to come.
