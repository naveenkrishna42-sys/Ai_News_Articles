India Approves First Dengue Vaccine QDENGA: What You Need To Know

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Drug Controller General of India (DCGI) has approved QDENGA, the first dengue vaccine in the country.
  • QDENGA is a live-attenuated vaccine developed by Japanese pharmaceutical company Takeda.
  • It is administered as two doses, three months apart.

India has approved its first vaccine against dengue. The Drug Controller General of India (DCGI) has given the green light to QDENGA, a vaccine developed by Japanese pharmaceutical company Takeda. The announcement was made on Monday, marking a significant step in the fight against a disease that affects lakhs of Indians every year.

Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection that causes high fever, severe headache, joint pain, and, in some cases, life-threatening complications. Until now, prevention relied entirely on mosquito control and personal protection. The arrival of a vaccine changes that calculation.

How Effective Is QDENGA?

QDENGA, also known as TAK-003, is a live-attenuated vaccine. Clinical trials have shown it to be effective against all four serotypes of the dengue virus. This is crucial because infection with one serotype does not provide immunity against the others, and subsequent infections can be more severe.

Data from global trials indicate that the vaccine reduces the incidence of hospitalisation due to dengue by a significant margin. However, officials have not yet confirmed the exact efficacy rates specific to the Indian population. The DCGI clearance was based on the totality of evidence presented by Takeda.

Who Can Get The Vaccine?

The vaccine is recommended for individuals aged four years and older. This age range covers the most vulnerable groups, including school-going children who are often at high risk of exposure. It is not recommended for pregnant or breastfeeding women, as safety data for these groups is still limited.

People with compromised immune systems or those on immunosuppressive therapy should consult a doctor before taking the shot. The vaccine is not a treatment for active dengue infection. It is strictly a preventive measure.

Dosage Schedule

QDENGA is administered as two doses. The second dose is given three months after the first. This schedule is designed to ensure that the body builds a robust and lasting immune response.

Individuals who have had a previous dengue infection can also receive the vaccine. In fact, studies suggest that the vaccine may offer stronger protection in those who have already been exposed to the virus. However, no pre-vaccination screening for past infection is required.

When Will It Be Available?

The approval is the first regulatory step. The vaccine will now need to be rolled out through private and public healthcare channels. Takeda has not yet announced a specific launch date or pricing for the Indian market.

The government is expected to include QDENGA in its national immunisation programme after further evaluation of cost-effectiveness and supply chain logistics. Private hospitals and clinics may offer it sooner.

For now, the clearance is a landmark. India reports roughly one to two lakh confirmed dengue cases each year, with the actual number likely much higher. A vaccine does not replace mosquito control, but it adds a powerful tool to the public health arsenal.

Watch for further announcements from Takeda on commercial availability and from the health ministry on potential inclusion in public health programmes.
