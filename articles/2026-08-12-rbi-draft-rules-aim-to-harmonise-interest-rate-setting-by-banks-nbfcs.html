RBI Draft Rules Aim to Harmonise Interest Rate Setting by Banks, NBFCs

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI releases draft rules for harmonised interest rate determination across banks and NBFCs.
  • Proposal aims to standardise methodology and enhance transparency in rate setting.
  • Public and stakeholder comments invited before finalisation.

Uniform Approach to Rate Setting

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Wednesday released draft rules that seek to harmonise the way banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) determine interest rates on loans and deposits. The move is aimed at reducing arbitrariness and bringing greater clarity to how rates are benchmarked and communicated to customers.

Currently, lenders follow varied practices, often leading to confusion among borrowers about how their rates are calculated. The proposed framework is expected to standardise the methodology, making it easier for customers to compare products across institutions.

Key Proposals in the Draft

The draft rules outline a common approach for linking interest rates to an external benchmark, such as the repo rate, for floating rate loans. It also suggests a clear disclosure format that banks and NBFCs must follow when informing borrowers about rate changes.

For deposits, the RBI has proposed that institutions adopt a transparent and non-discriminatory policy for setting rates, ensuring that similar deposits get similar returns regardless of the customer's relationship with the lender. The central bank has also hinted at curbing practices where banks offer sweetened rates to select customers without a documented rationale.

Impact on Lenders and Borrowers

If implemented, the rules could reduce the flexibility that banks and NBFCs currently enjoy in pricing loans and deposits. Lenders may need to revise their internal systems and customer communication strategies to comply with the new norms.

For borrowers, the main benefit would be predictability. With a uniform benchmark, a home loan or auto loan from different lenders would be easier to compare, and rate revisions would follow a more predictable pattern linked to monetary policy changes.

What the RBI Says

The RBI has stated that the objective is to ensure fairness and transparency in interest rate determination. It has invited comments from stakeholders, including banks, NBFCs, consumer groups, and the general public, within a specified window before finalising the rules.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact timeline for implementing the final rules. The consultation process is expected to take at least a few weeks, after which the RBI will issue the final guidelines.

Next Steps

Industry players are likely to study the draft closely and submit their feedback, particularly on the operational challenges of shifting to a unified benchmark. The RBI's final decision will determine how quickly the harmonised regime comes into effect.

Markets will watch for any changes that could affect lending margins or deposit competition. The central bank's push for standardisation is part of a broader effort to strengthen consumer protection and financial stability.
