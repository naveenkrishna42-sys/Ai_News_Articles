60 US Senators Back Bill for 100% Tariffs on India Over Russian Oil

📅 2026-07-17 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • 60 US Senators have co-sponsored a bill imposing 100% tariffs on nations buying Russian oil.
  • India, China and three other countries are specifically targeted under the proposed legislation.
  • The bill could significantly strain India-US trade relations if enacted.

A group of 60 US Senators has thrown its weight behind a new bill that proposes 100% tariffs on India and four other nations for continuing to purchase Russian oil. The legislation, introduced in the Senate, marks the latest attempt by American lawmakers to penalise countries that have not complied with Western sanctions on Moscow following its invasion of Ukraine.

The Bill's Scope and Targets

The proposed measure specifically names India, China, and three other nations as targets. If passed, it would impose a 100% tariff on all goods imported from these countries into the United States. The bill's supporters argue that such punitive tariffs are necessary to force these nations to stop buying Russian crude oil.

Senators backing the legislation claim that current sanctions have not been effective enough. They point to India's steady imports of Russian oil, which have surged since the war began, as a primary reason for the new push. The bill would require the US President to impose the tariffs unless a country can demonstrate a significant reduction in its Russian oil purchases.

Impact on India-US Trade Ties

The development comes at a delicate time for India-US relations. Both countries have been deepening their strategic partnership, particularly in defence and technology. However, India's refusal to join the Western-led sanctions regime against Russia has been a persistent irritant for Washington.

New Delhi has repeatedly defended its position, stating that it must prioritise its energy security and source oil from the most affordable markets. Indian officials have also argued that Europe itself continued to buy Russian gas well into the conflict. The proposed 100% tariff, if enacted, would hit Indian exports ranging from pharmaceuticals to textiles, potentially disrupting thousands of jobs in both nations.

Broader Geopolitical Ramifications

The bill does not single out India alone. China, which has also ramped up Russian oil imports, faces the same penalty. The legislation reflects growing frustration in Washington over the failure of multilateral sanctions to fully isolate Moscow economically.

Analysts warn that such aggressive tariff action could backfire. They note that pushing India closer to Russia or China could undermine US strategic goals in the Indo-Pacific region. The US has been courting India as a key counterweight to China's influence, and a trade war could jeopardise that alignment.

What Happens Next

The bill now moves to the Senate floor for debate. While it enjoys bipartisan support, it still faces an uncertain path to becoming law. The White House has not yet issued a formal position on the legislation. Indian diplomats in Washington are closely monitoring the situation, though officials have not yet confirmed any formal response.

The coming weeks will determine whether this bill gathers enough momentum to pass both chambers of Congress. If it does, India may be forced to choose between cheap Russian oil and its lucrative trade relationship with the United States.
