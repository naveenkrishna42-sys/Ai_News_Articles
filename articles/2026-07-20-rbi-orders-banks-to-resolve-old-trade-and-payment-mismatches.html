RBI orders banks to resolve old trade and payment mismatches

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has instructed banks to resolve long-standing trade and payment mismatches.
  • The directive covers discrepancies in export, import, and cross-border payment records.
  • Banks must submit compliance reports within a specified timeframe.
  • The move aims to improve data accuracy and financial stability.

RBI cracks down on legacy discrepancies

The Reserve Bank of India has directed banks to clear old mismatches in trade and payment records, according to a report by The Economic Times. The central bank's order targets discrepancies that have piled up over years, especially in export, import, and cross-border payment transactions.

These mismatches, often a result of outdated systems or manual errors, have been flagged as potential risks to financial stability. The RBI has now set a deadline for banks to reconcile these records and submit compliance reports.

Scope of the directive

The directive covers a wide range of discrepancies, including those related to letters of credit, bills of entry, and foreign remittances. Banks have been asked to identify and resolve mismatches between their internal records and the data reported to the central bank.

Sources say the RBI has also warned of stricter action if banks fail to meet the deadline. The move is part of a broader push to digitize and streamline financial reporting in the country.

Why this matters

Trade and payment mismatches can lead to errors in balance of payments data and affect policy decisions. They also create compliance headaches for banks and businesses dealing with cross-border transactions.

By clearing these old records, the RBI hopes to improve the accuracy of financial data and reduce systemic risks. The central bank has been tightening its oversight of the banking sector in recent months.

What banks need to do

Banks have been given a specific timeline to complete the reconciliation process. They must identify all mismatches, correct them, and submit a detailed report to the RBI.

Failure to comply could result in penalties or other regulatory action. Industry experts say the order will require significant coordination between banks' trade finance, treasury, and compliance departments.

Impact on the sector

The directive is expected to increase the workload for banks in the short term but could lead to smoother operations in the long run. It also aligns with the RBI's focus on data integrity and transparency.

Bankers have welcomed the move, though some have expressed concerns about the tight deadline. The RBI has not yet commented on whether any extension will be granted.

As the deadline approaches, all eyes will be on banks' compliance efforts and any further guidance from the central bank.
