Sheikh Hasina to make first public appearance in two years on Wednesday

📅 2026-08-02 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sheikh Hasina will hold a press conference in India on August 5, her first public appearance since being ousted two years ago.
  • The presser comes a month before Tarique Rahman's expected visit, raising political speculation.
  • A Bangladesh minister has warned that Hasina will be arrested the moment she returns to the country.

New Delhi: Sheikh Hasina, the ousted prime minister of Bangladesh, is set to make her first public appearance in two years on Wednesday, August 5, in what will be her most significant political statement since being forced to flee Dhaka. The former premier will address a press conference in India, according to sources familiar with the matter.

The announcement has injected fresh uncertainty into the already volatile politics of Bangladesh, where Hasina's Awami League government was toppled in a popular uprising in August 2024. Since then, she has remained in India, largely out of public view, breaking her silence only through occasional written statements.

Timing that raises eyebrows

The choice of date is anything but random. August 5 marks the second anniversary of her ouster, and the presser is scheduled exactly a month before Tarique Rahman, the acting chairman of the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP), is expected to visit the country. Rahman, who leads the opposition from exile in London, has been positioning himself as a key player in the post-Hasina political order.

Political observers note that Hasina's decision to speak now could be aimed at reclaiming the narrative ahead of Rahman's arrival. It may also be a signal to her supporters within Bangladesh, who have faced a sustained crackdown since the regime change, that she is not politically finished.

"This is a calculated move," said a Delhi-based political analyst who tracks South Asian affairs, requesting anonymity. "Hasina is trying to remind both Dhaka and the international community that she remains a force to be reckoned with."

An ominous warning

The press conference comes against a backdrop of explicit threats. A senior Bangladesh government minister has publicly declared that Hasina will be arrested the moment she sets foot on Bangladeshi soil. The statement, reported by multiple news outlets, underscores the depth of animosity between the current interim administration and the exiled former leader.

Hasina herself has reportedly told confidants that she may be killed if she returns. That fear, real or perceived, has not deterred her from signaling her intent to go back. The former prime minister has repeatedly asserted her right to return and has claimed that the charges against her are politically motivated.

India's delicate balancing act

For New Delhi, Hasina's presence has become a diplomatic headache. India shares a 4,096-km border with Bangladesh, and the two countries have deep economic and security ties. The interim government in Dhaka has been pushing for Hasina's extradition, but India has so far resisted, citing legal and humanitarian considerations.

Indian foreign ministry officials have declined to comment on the specifics of Hasina's scheduled presser, saying only that "standard protocols" are being followed. However, the event is likely to be watched closely in Dhaka, where any public statement by Hasina could trigger protests or a diplomatic rebuke.

The timing also complicates matters for the interim government, which has struggled to stabilise the country amid economic woes and political unrest. A high-profile appearance by Hasina, even from Indian soil, could galvanise her loyalists and destabilise the current dispensation further.

What to watch

All eyes will be on Wednesday's press conference for the specifics: Will Hasina announce a concrete date for her return? Will she call for mass protests? Or will she strike a conciliatory tone, seeking a negotiated settlement? Her words will likely set the stage for the next phase of Bangladesh's turbulent transition.

For now, the former prime minister remains in India, her fate intertwined with the shifting calculations of two governments. The coming weeks, leading up to Rahman's expected visit, will determine whether her political comeback is a genuine possibility or a final, defiant act.
