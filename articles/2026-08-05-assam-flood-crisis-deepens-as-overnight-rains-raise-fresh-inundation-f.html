Assam flood crisis deepens as overnight rains raise fresh inundation fears

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Overnight rains in Assam have worsened the flood situation, with fresh inundation feared in several districts.
  • The death toll has climbed to 87, with more than 1.28 lakh people affected across seven districts.
  • Union minister JP Nadda visited affected regions and assured central assistance; Centre to map flood losses door-to-door for faster rehabilitation.

The flood situation in Assam turned critical on Wednesday as overnight rains raised the spectre of fresh inundation across the state. With rivers already flowing above danger marks in several areas, authorities are bracing for a second wave of flooding in districts that had just begun to recover.

Death toll rises, lakhs affected

The state's flood bulletin confirmed that the death toll has climbed to 87. More than 1.28 lakh people are now affected across seven districts, with thousands forced to take shelter in relief camps. The worst-hit areas remain in the Brahmaputra valley, where embankments are under stress after days of relentless downpour.

Officials said the overnight rains have compounded the challenge, with water levels in multiple rivers rising again. "The situation is dynamic," a disaster management official said, adding that evacuation efforts are continuing in low-lying areas.

Why Assam floods every year

The recurring devastation has brought renewed focus on why Assam faces this crisis annually. The state's geography makes it uniquely vulnerable: the Brahmaputra and its tributaries originate in the Himalayas and carry massive silt loads, causing rivers to swell rapidly during the monsoon. Deforestation and erosion have weakened the riverbanks, while encroachment on floodplains has reduced the land's natural capacity to absorb excess water.

Climate scientists note that changing rainfall patterns have made the monsoon more erratic, with intense bursts of rain over short periods. This year's floods have once again exposed the limits of existing embankments, many of which were built decades ago and are now in a state of disrepair.

Centre's response and rehabilitation push

Union minister JP Nadda visited the affected regions on Tuesday and assured full assistance from the central government. "The Centre stands with the people of Assam in this hour of crisis," he said after reviewing the situation with state officials. He announced that all necessary resources, including National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) teams and funds, would be made available without delay.

In a significant move, the government has decided to map flood losses door-to-door to speed up rehabilitation. The exercise, which will cover all affected households, aims to assess damage to homes, crops, and livestock in a systematic manner. Officials believe this will help disburse compensation faster and ensure that aid reaches those who need it most.

Ground report from affected districts

In the seven affected districts, rescue teams are navigating submerged roads to reach marooned villages. Many families have moved to higher ground, taking whatever belongings they could carry. Relief camps are functioning, but reports of shortages of drinking water and medicine have surfaced in some areas.

Local administrations have been asked to ensure uninterrupted power supply to relief camps and maintain strict vigil on embankments. The state's flood control room is monitoring the situation round-the-clock, with weather forecasts predicting more rain in the coming days.

Residents, many of whom have lost their homes for the third time in a decade, expressed frustration over the slow pace of permanent solutions. "We rebuild every year, and every year the water destroys it again," a farmer in one of the affected districts said.

The coming 48 hours will be crucial. With the monsoon still active, authorities are on high alert for further breaches in embankments. The door-to-door damage mapping is expected to be completed within a week, after which the state will submit a detailed report to the Centre for additional relief funds. For now, the priority remains saving lives and preventing another surge in the death toll.
