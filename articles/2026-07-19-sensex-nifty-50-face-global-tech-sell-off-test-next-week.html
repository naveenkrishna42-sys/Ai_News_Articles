Sensex, Nifty 50 face global tech sell-off test next week

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Global tech sell-off may drag Indian IT stocks and benchmarks lower
  • Analysts watching Q1 earnings from TCS, Infosys for sector direction
  • Foreign institutional investor flows and US Fed stance remain key triggers

Global tech rout casts shadow on Dalal Street

Indian stock markets are bracing for a volatile week ahead as a sharp sell-off in global technology stocks threatens to spill over into domestic trading. The Sensex and Nifty 50, which have been hovering near record highs, could face selling pressure in early sessions.

The trigger comes from a dramatic slide in US tech shares, led by major indices like the Nasdaq. Weak earnings guidance from some of the world's largest tech firms has reignited fears of a prolonged slowdown in the sector. Indian IT stocks, which derive a significant portion of revenue from global clients, are expected to be the most vulnerable.

IT stocks under the microscope

Investors will closely track the performance of heavyweights such as Tata Consultancy Services, Infosys, and HCL Technologies. These stocks form a substantial weight in both the Sensex and Nifty 50. Any sharp decline in these counters could pull the benchmarks lower.

Market participants are also awaiting the start of the quarterly earnings season. The first set of results from IT majors will provide crucial cues on demand trends and margin outlook. Analysts caution that any disappointment could exacerbate the sell-off.

Foreign fund flows and global cues

Foreign institutional investors have been net buyers in Indian equities over the past few weeks. But the global risk-off mood could prompt a reversal. A sustained outflow would add to the downward pressure on the market.

Beyond the tech sector, traders will also monitor the US Federal Reserve's policy stance. Any hawkish commentary from Fed officials could further dampen sentiment. On the domestic front, oil prices and the rupee's movement against the dollar will remain in focus.

Support levels and what traders expect

Technical analysts point to key support levels for the Nifty 50 around the 24,500-24,600 zone. A breach below this range could trigger further selling. The Sensex may find support near 80,800-81,000.

Options data suggests heightened volatility in the coming sessions. The India VIX, a measure of market fear, has inched up. Traders are advised to adopt cautious positions and avoid aggressive bets until clarity emerges.

Despite the near-term headwinds, some market experts believe the broader uptrend remains intact. They argue that India's strong macroeconomic fundamentals and corporate earnings growth could limit the downside. However, the immediate direction hinges on how global markets settle over the weekend.

All eyes will be on Monday's opening bell. A gap-down start looks likely, but the extent of recovery will depend on buying interest at lower levels from domestic institutional investors and retail participants.

The coming week will also see a busy economic calendar. Data on industrial production and inflation are due. Any surprise in these numbers could influence the Reserve Bank of India's future policy moves.

For now, the global tech sell-off remains the dominant narrative. Indian investors will watch for any signs of stabilization in US markets before committing fresh capital.
