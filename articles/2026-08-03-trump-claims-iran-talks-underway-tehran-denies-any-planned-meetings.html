Trump claims Iran talks underway; Tehran denies any planned meetings

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says talks with Iran are being held, calls them Iran's 'last chance'
  • Tehran denies any planned negotiations, accusing Washington of duplicity
  • Oil prices hold decline as markets weigh conflicting signals from both sides

White House claims negotiations, Tehran pushes back

US President Donald Trump has said that talks with Iran are underway, describing them as Tehran's final opportunity to reach a deal. His remarks, made on Sunday, immediately drew a sharp denial from Iranian officials, who insisted no such negotiations have been scheduled.

The conflicting statements have created confusion on the diplomatic front, with both sides trading accusations. Trump's comments came amid rising tensions over Iran's nuclear programme and its regional military activities.

'Last chance' warning

Speaking to reporters, Trump framed the purported talks as a decisive moment for Iran. He warned that failure to engage would leave Tehran facing severe consequences, though he did not specify what those might be.

His language echoed his earlier 'maximum pressure' campaign, which has crippled Iran's economy through sanctions. Trump's assertion that negotiations are Tehran's 'last chance' suggests he believes the Islamic Republic is under sufficient strain to come to the table.

Iran's denial and 'duplicity' charge

Iran's foreign ministry was quick to refute Trump's claim, stating that no talks are planned with Washington. A senior Iranian official accused the US president of 'duplicity', saying his remarks were aimed at domestic consumption rather than genuine diplomacy.

The denial has further complicated an already fraught relationship. Tehran has repeatedly said it will not negotiate under sanctions or threats, a position it has maintained since the US withdrew from the 2015 nuclear deal in 2018.

Oil markets react cautiously

Despite the war of words, global oil prices held their decline on Monday, suggesting traders are sceptical about any imminent breakthrough. Analysts noted that the market has grown accustomed to such rhetorical clashes, with little immediate impact on supply.

The muted reaction also reflects uncertainty over whether talks, if they occur, could lead to sanctions relief and increased Iranian crude exports. For now, investors appear to be waiting for concrete signals rather than presidential statements.

What to watch

Observers will be watching for any confirmation from third-party mediators, such as Oman or Qatar, which have previously facilitated US-Iran contacts. A shift in Tehran's public stance, or a new round of UN nuclear inspections, could indicate that quiet diplomacy is in fact taking place.

For now, the gap between Washington's claims and Tehran's denials leaves the situation unresolved, with the risk of miscalculation ever present.
