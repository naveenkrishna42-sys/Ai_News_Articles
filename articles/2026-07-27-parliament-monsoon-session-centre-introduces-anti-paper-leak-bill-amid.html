Parliament Monsoon Session: Centre introduces anti-paper leak Bill amid Opposition uproar

📅 2026-07-27 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Centre introduces anti-paper leak amendment Bill in Lok Sabha amid ruckus
  • Congress stages protest over alleged police brutality against NEET demonstrators
  • Rahul Gandhi demands apology from Amit Shah over use of lethal force against students

Bill tabled amid chaos

The government on Monday introduced the anti-paper leak amendment Bill in the Lok Sabha on the sixth day of the Monsoon Session. The Bill, which seeks to curb malpractices in competitive examinations, was tabled amid a massive ruckus created by Opposition members.

Opposition MPs were on their feet, shouting slogans and waving placards. The Speaker repeatedly appealed for order, but the din continued as the Bill was formally moved.

Congress protest over NEET police action

The Congress party staged a protest in Parliament accusing the government of police brutality against NEET demonstrators. Party MPs demanded a statement from Home Minister Amit Shah and an apology for what they called excessive force used against students protesting alleged irregularities in the NEET exam.

Congress leader Rahul Gandhi confronted the government in the Lok Sabha, asking Home Minister Amit Shah directly: "Did you authorise the use of lethal weapons against students?" The question drew sharp responses from treasury benches, with BJP MPs accusing the Congress of politicising a law and order matter.

The Congress has also moved adjournment and suspension of business notices in both Houses to force a discussion on the issue.

Notices filed, demand for apology

Congress MPs filed notices under Rule 267 in the Rajya Sabha and Rule 56 in the Lok Sabha, seeking suspension of scheduled business to debate the police action. The party has made it clear it will not allow the House to function unless Shah responds.

"The home minister must come to the House and apologise for the brutal lathi charge on innocent students," a Congress spokesperson said outside Parliament.

The government, however, dismissed the allegations as exaggerated and politically motivated. Sources in the Home Ministry said the police action was a proportionate response to a violent protest that turned unlawful.

Opposition unites, Treasury benches hold ground

Other Opposition parties, including the Trinamool Congress, the DMK and the Left, have extended support to the Congress demand. They have called for a joint protest in the House on Tuesday if the government does not relent.

The Treasury benches, meanwhile, have accused the Opposition of disrupting Parliament for political mileage. "They have no agenda other than to stall the country's progress," a BJP leader said, speaking on condition of anonymity.

The anti-paper leak Bill is part of a broader government push to reform examination systems after multiple leak incidents in recent years. The Bill proposes stricter penalties for offenders and a centralised monitoring mechanism.

Parliamentary affairs minister Pralhad Joshi urged all parties to cooperate for the smooth functioning of the House. "We are ready to discuss every issue under the rules. Disruption helps no one," he said.

All eyes are now on Tuesday's proceedings. The Opposition has indicated it will intensify its protest if its demands are not met. The government appears in no mood to concede.
