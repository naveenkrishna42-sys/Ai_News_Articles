Wangchuk’s Hunger Strike: Education Accountability at the Core of Jantar Mantar Protest

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Analysis Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sonam Wangchuk has listed three conditions to end his hunger strike, including a Parliament session on education accountability.
  • The protest at Jantar Mantar has drawn attention to failures in the education system, with supporters demanding government action.
  • The Delhi High Court has refused to intervene, stating the government was right in shifting Wangchuk to a hospital.

Context of the Protest

Sonam Wangchuk, the renowned education reform activist, has been on a hunger strike at Jantar Mantar in New Delhi since July 15. His protest has drawn widespread attention, with supporters from various states joining him. The core demand is accountability for failures in the education system, particularly in underserved regions like Ladakh.

Wangchuk’s conditions for ending the fast were outlined by his associate, Gitanjali Angmo. She stated that Wangchuk will end his strike if the Parliament session focuses on education accountability. This demand comes amid ongoing debates about the quality and accessibility of education in India.

Three Conditions for Ending the Fast

According to reports, Wangchuk has laid down three specific conditions. First, the government must acknowledge the systemic failures in the education system. Second, a dedicated Parliament session should be convened to discuss and address these issues. Third, concrete steps must be taken to ensure accountability at all levels of education governance.

Gitanjali Angmo confirmed that Wangchuk is willing to end the fast if these conditions are met. The protest has escalated, with Wangchuk being shifted to a hospital earlier this week due to health concerns. The Delhi High Court has refused to intervene, stating that the government was right in shifting him to a medical facility.

Government Response and Legal Angle

The government has maintained that Wangchuk’s health is a priority. Officials have not yet commented on the specific demands. The Delhi High Court’s refusal to intervene suggests that the judiciary sees no immediate legal violation in the government’s actions. However, the protest continues to put pressure on the administration.

Wangchuk’s supporters argue that the government must take the demands seriously. The education system, they say, has long been neglected, especially in rural and border areas. The protest at Jantar Mantar is a culmination of years of frustration over lack of accountability and poor infrastructure.

Why This Matters

The protest highlights a growing discontent with the state of education in India. Wangchuk’s conditions are not just about his personal fast but about a systemic overhaul. The demand for a Parliament session on education accountability is significant—it calls for legislative attention to a critical issue.

If the government agrees to the conditions, it could set a precedent for how education reforms are handled. If it does not, the protest may continue, potentially drawing more supporters. The outcome could influence public discourse on education policy in the coming months.

What to Watch

The next few days are critical. Wangchuk’s health will determine the urgency of the government’s response. If the Parliament session is called, it will be a major step. If not, the protest could escalate, with more activists joining. The Delhi High Court’s stance suggests that legal challenges may not alter the course.

Observers will watch whether the government offers a compromise or sticks to its current position. The education accountability issue is unlikely to fade away, and Wangchuk’s fast has brought it to the forefront.
