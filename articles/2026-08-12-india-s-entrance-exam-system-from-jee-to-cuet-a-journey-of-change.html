India's entrance exam system: From JEE to CUET, a journey of change

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Entrance exams in India have shifted from state-specific to national-level tests.
  • Introduction of JEE and NEET standardized engineering and medical admissions.
  • CUET represents the latest reform, aiming to reduce exam burden on students.
  • The evolution mirrors global trends towards holistic and skill-based assessment.

A System in Flux

India's entrance examination system has undergone a remarkable transformation over the decades. What began as a patchwork of state-level tests and university-specific screens has slowly morphed into a more unified, national framework. This evolution reflects not just policy changes but also shifting societal expectations from education.

The journey has been anything but linear. From the early days of subjective answer sheets to today's computer-based tests, the change has been driven by a need for fairness, efficiency, and wider access. Each reform has aimed to fix the flaws of the previous system, yet new challenges have inevitably surfaced.

From State Tests to National Standards

For much of the 20th century, admission to professional courses in India was a fragmented affair. Engineering colleges ran their own entrance tests, and medical schools followed suit. This created a logistical nightmare for students, who often had to travel across states to sit for multiple exams with different syllabi and formats.

The turning point came with the establishment of the Joint Entrance Examination (JEE) for engineering and the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET) for medical admissions. These tests consolidated what were dozens of separate exams into two major national gateways. The move promised a level playing field and reduced the financial burden on aspirants.

The Rise of Computer-Based Testing

Another significant shift was the move from pen-and-paper to computer-based testing. This change, rolled out in phases over the past decade, brought with it faster result processing and the ability to conduct tests in multiple sessions. It also allowed for more sophisticated question patterns, including adaptive testing in some cases.

However, this digital transition has not been without its critics. Concerns over digital divide and infrastructure gaps in rural areas have repeatedly surfaced. Yet, the trend towards online examination seems irreversible, especially after the pandemic forced a rapid acceleration of digital methods.

The CUET Experiment

The most recent and perhaps most ambitious reform is the Common University Entrance Test (CUET) for undergraduate admissions. Launched in 2022, CUET aims to replace the multiple entrance tests conducted by individual universities with a single national exam. This is a radical departure from the earlier practice where universities like Delhi University relied on cut-off marks from Class 12 boards.

Supporters argue that CUET reduces the emphasis on high-stakes board exams and gives students more flexibility to choose subjects. Critics, however, point to the added pressure of yet another exam and the logistical challenges of conducting such a massive test. The debate is far from settled.

What Lies Ahead

The evolution of India's entrance examination system is a story of centralisation and standardisation. From the JEE to CUET, the direction has been towards fewer, more comprehensive tests. This mirrors a global movement away from rote memorisation towards assessing analytical and application skills.

As the National Education Policy 2020 takes root, further changes are likely. The focus may shift towards aptitude-based testing and continuous assessment rather than one-day high-stakes exams. The next few years will reveal whether these reforms truly democratise access to higher education or merely relocate the pressure points.
