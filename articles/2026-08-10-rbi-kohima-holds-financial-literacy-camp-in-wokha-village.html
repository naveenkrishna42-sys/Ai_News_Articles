RBI Kohima holds financial literacy camp in Wokha village

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI Kohima conducted a financial literacy camp at Yanmhon village in Wokha district.
  • The camp aimed to educate villagers on banking, savings, and digital transactions.
  • Such initiatives are part of RBI's broader effort to boost financial inclusion in remote areas.

The Reserve Bank of India's Kohima office took its financial literacy drive to Yanmhon village in Wokha district on Monday, aiming to bring banking awareness to one of Nagaland's more remote communities. The camp was part of the central bank's ongoing outreach to deepen financial inclusion in the Northeast.

Building basic financial awareness

Residents were introduced to the fundamentals of formal banking, including the importance of saving, the role of bank accounts, and how to access credit through institutional channels. Officials from the RBI Kohima team also explained the mechanics of digital payments, a subject that remains unfamiliar to many in rural pockets of the state.

The session covered practical topics such as identifying counterfeit currency, using ATMs safely, and avoiding fraudulent schemes. The idea, according to organisers, was to equip villagers with the knowledge to make informed financial decisions without relying on informal lenders or unregulated channels.

Focus on digital and grievance redressal

A significant portion of the camp was dedicated to digital banking tools, including mobile banking apps, UPI transactions, and the precautions needed when using them. Villagers were told about the customer protection mechanisms available through the RBI's integrated ombudsman scheme, which allows them to lodge complaints against banks without excessive paperwork.

Local participants reportedly engaged actively, asking questions about loan eligibility and the documentation required to open accounts. The interactive format appeared to help bridge the gap between policy-level guidance and ground-level realities.

Part of a larger push in the Northeast

Monday's camp in Yanmhon is not an isolated exercise. The RBI has been steadily increasing its footprint in Nagaland and neighbouring states, where banking penetration lags behind the national average. Such camps are designed to complement the efforts of commercial banks and cooperative institutions that often struggle to reach sparsely populated hill districts.

Wokha district, home to the Lotha Naga community, has seen gradual improvement in banking infrastructure over the past decade, but challenges remain. Limited internet connectivity and low digital literacy continue to hamper the adoption of online services, making on-ground awareness programmes all the more critical.

What the camp means for villagers

For the residents of Yanmhon, the immediate takeaway was a clearer understanding of how to use banking services safely and effectively. Many left with pamphlets and contact details for helplines, while others gained confidence in approaching banks for small-ticket loans or savings products.

The RBI team did not announce any specific follow-up dates, but such camps are typically repeated periodically in different villages across the district. Officials have not yet confirmed whether Yanmhon will see a second session, though local community leaders have expressed interest in hosting more such programmes.

Financial literacy, after all, is not a one-time lesson but a continuous process. The camp in Yanmhon is a small but meaningful step toward ensuring that even the most remote citizens are not left out of India's formal financial system.

What happens next will depend on how quickly the villagers put their new knowledge into practice—and how soon the RBI and its partner institutions return to build on the groundwork laid on Monday.
