Telangana achieves 78.26% digitisation of SIR forms

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Telangana has digitised 78.26 per cent of SIR forms, covering vast land records.
  • The move reduces physical paperwork and speeds up land transaction processing.
  • Officials say digitisation enhances transparency and accessibility for citizens.

Land records enter digital era

Telangana has crossed a significant milestone in its land administration overhaul, achieving 78.26 per cent digitisation of SIR (Survey, Inheritance, and Registration) forms. The state government's push to modernise land records has now brought the majority of its survey and registration documents into the digital fold.

Officials familiar with the process said the digitisation covers a wide range of documents, including inheritance records, survey maps, and registration details. The effort is part of a broader initiative to create a unified, accessible database for land ownership across the state.

What are SIR forms and why they matter

SIR forms are fundamental to land administration in Telangana. They record the chain of ownership, survey numbers, and transaction history of a piece of land. Previously, these records existed only in physical registers, often leading to disputes and delays.

With digitisation, officials can now retrieve records in minutes, reducing the time taken for property registration and mutation. Citizens no longer need to queue at revenue offices for basic land information.

Progress and coverage

The 78.26 per cent figure translates to lakhs of records already converted into digital format. The remaining forms are likely in remote or conflict-prone areas where physical surveys are still underway.

Revenue department sources said the pace of digitisation has accelerated in recent months, with dedicated teams scanning and indexing old records. The state has also set up data centres to store and secure these digital files.

Impact on citizens and administration

For the common landowner, the change means faster verification of titles and smoother registration. It also reduces the scope for fraudulent transactions, as digital records are harder to tamper with.

Revenue officials in Hyderabad noted that the system has already cut down on paperwork and improved transparency. "Earlier, tracing a property's history took days. Now it takes minutes," one official said, speaking on condition of anonymity.

Challenges and next steps

The remaining 21.74 per cent of forms present their own hurdles. Some records are damaged or missing, requiring reconstruction through field visits. Others involve disputed lands where ownership is contested.

The government has not yet announced a deadline for full digitisation. However, officials have indicated that the focus is now on completing the backlog and ensuring data accuracy before scaling up further.

As Telangana pushes towards 100 per cent coverage, the success of this project could serve as a template for other states grappling with outdated land records. For now, the 78.26 per cent mark stands as a clear signal that the state is serious about modernising its land governance.

All eyes will be on how quickly the remaining records are processed and whether the digital system withstands real-world legal scrutiny in the months ahead.
