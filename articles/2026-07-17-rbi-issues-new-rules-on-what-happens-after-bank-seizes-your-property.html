RBI issues new rules on what happens after bank seizes your property

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has issued new guidelines on the procedure banks must follow after taking possession of a borrower's property.
  • The rules mandate banks to provide borrowers with a detailed valuation report and a timeline for the sale of the asset.
  • Borrowers will now have the right to inspect the property and challenge the valuation within a specified period.
  • The new framework aims to reduce arbitrariness and improve transparency in the recovery process.
  • The guidelines apply to all scheduled commercial banks and are effective from October 1, 2026.

The Reserve Bank of India has released a fresh set of rules that detail exactly what a bank must do after it takes over a borrower's property. For years, borrowers have complained about opacity and arbitrary valuations during the recovery process. The new framework aims to change that.

Clear timelines and valuation norms

Banks will now have to provide the borrower a detailed valuation report of the seized property within 15 days of taking possession. The valuation must be done by an approved valuer registered with a recognised body. If the borrower disagrees with the valuation, they can request a revaluation at their own cost within 30 days.

The lender must also inform the borrower of the proposed date of sale at least 30 days in advance. This is meant to give the borrower a realistic chance to repay the dues and reclaim the asset. Earlier, many borrowers said they were caught off guard by sudden auctions.

Right to inspect and challenge

Under the new norms, the borrower has the right to inspect the property before it is sold. This is significant because in many cases, properties deteriorate after a bank takes possession, and borrowers end up paying less than fair value. The borrower can now flag any damage or change in condition.

If the bank sells the property at a price significantly lower than the valuation, it must provide a written explanation to the borrower. The borrower can also approach the Debt Recovery Tribunal if they believe the process was unfair. The new rules give borrowers a clear legal pathway to challenge the sale.

What happens to the surplus money?

The RBI has also clarified what happens if the property sells for more than the outstanding loan. The bank must return the surplus amount to the borrower within 15 days of the sale. If there is any delay, the bank must pay interest at the rate applicable to the loan.

If the property sells for less than the loan amount, the bank can continue to recover the shortfall from the borrower through other means. But the rules now require the bank to disclose the exact amount recovered and the balance due, so borrowers are not left guessing.

New rules apply to all scheduled banks

The guidelines are applicable to all scheduled commercial banks. They come into effect from October 1, 2026. Banks have been asked to update their internal policies and recovery manuals to comply with the new framework. Non-compliance can attract penalties.

The central bank has also advised lenders to set up a dedicated grievance redressal mechanism for borrowers facing property seizure. This is expected to reduce the number of cases that end up in courts and tribunals.

What happens next? Banks will need to train their recovery officers and update systems to meet the new deadlines. Borrowers, meanwhile, should watch out for the valuation report and sale notice if their property has been taken over. The onus is now on both sides to follow the rules.
