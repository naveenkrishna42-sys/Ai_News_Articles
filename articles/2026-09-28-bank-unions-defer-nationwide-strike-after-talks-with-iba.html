Background: Why the Strike Was Called

The unions said the Indian Banks’ Association (IBA) had not responded adequately to their demands for a revised wage settlement, better pension benefits and improved working conditions, prompting the strike call.

Impact of the Strike

The three‑day nationwide strike, beginning Monday, affected over nine lakh bank employees across public and private sector banks. Core banking services such as cash withdrawals, fund transfers, and loan processing faced delays, while digital channels remained operational but experienced heightened traffic. Retail customers reported longer wait times at branches, and corporate clients noted disruptions in trade finance and clearing operations.

Detailed Analysis

Wage Demands

Unions are seeking a 15% increase in basic pay over the next three years, citing inflation and the rising cost of living. The IBA’s latest offer proposed a 9% increment, which unions deem insufficient.

Pension Benefits

Employees demand the restoration of the old pension scheme for those hired after 2010, along with a higher dearness allowance linked to the consumer price index. The IBA has offered to maintain the current contributory pension framework with a modest increase in employer contribution.

Working Conditions

Key concerns include reducing overtime, improving branch security, and implementing stricter norms for loan recovery practices. Unions also call for a uniform policy on remote work and better grievance redressal mechanisms.

Key Takeaways

  • The strike underscores growing dissatisfaction among bank employees over wage stagnation and benefit erosion.
  • Unions’ demands are significantly higher than the IBA’s current proposals, indicating a potential for prolonged negotiations.
  • Service disruptions, though limited to physical branches, highlight the sector’s reliance on a robust workforce for customer-facing operations.
  • Resolution will likely require a compromise on wage increments, pension adjustments, and working‑condition reforms to avoid further industrial action.
