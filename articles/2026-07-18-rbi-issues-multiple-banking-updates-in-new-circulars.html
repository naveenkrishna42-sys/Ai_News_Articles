RBI issues multiple banking updates in new circulars

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI released multiple circulars on lending, customer service, and digital banking
  • New norms aim to tighten compliance and protect consumer interests
  • Banks have been given timelines to implement the changes

The Reserve Bank of India has issued a fresh set of regulatory updates for the banking sector, covering areas from lending norms to customer protection and digital banking compliance. The circulars, released this week, signal the central bank's continued push for tighter oversight and operational discipline.

Revised lending norms for priority sector

One of the key updates pertains to priority sector lending (PSL) guidelines. The RBI has revised the classification and reporting requirements for certain categories, including agriculture, micro and small enterprises, and education loans.

Banks will now need to provide more granular data on their PSL portfolios. The central bank said this move aims to improve transparency and ensure that credit actually reaches the intended beneficiaries.

Lenders have been given three months to align their internal systems with the new format.

Customer service standards tightened

The RBI also issued a circular on customer grievance redressal. Banks must now acknowledge complaints within 24 hours, down from the earlier 48-hour window. Resolution timelines for common issues such as unauthorised transactions have been reduced to 14 days from 30 days.

Penalties for non-compliance have been increased. For every delayed resolution beyond the new timeline, banks will have to pay a compensation of ₹500 per day to the affected customer.

The regulator has also mandated that all public sector and private sector banks set up a dedicated ombudsman desk at their head offices.

Digital banking compliance gets a refresh

On the digital front, the RBI has clarified rules for video-based customer identification processes. The new guidelines require banks to store video recordings for at least 10 years, up from the earlier five-year norm.

Additionally, all digital onboarding workflows must now include a live liveness check. The central bank said this is to prevent identity fraud and synthetic identity theft, which have been on the rise.

Banks have been given until the next quarter to update their systems. Non-compliance will attract restrictions on new customer acquisitions via digital channels.

Capital adequacy and risk management

The RBI also released an updated framework for capital adequacy. Banks with a high concentration of unsecured retail loans will now need to maintain a higher capital buffer. The new rule applies to all scheduled commercial banks.

Risk weight on unsecured personal loans has been increased by 25 percentage points. This is expected to moderate the rapid growth in this segment, which the central bank has flagged as a potential risk area.

Banks are required to report their compliance status within 45 days.

The series of updates comes amid an environment where the RBI has been closely monitoring credit growth and asset quality. The central bank has repeatedly warned about rising retail defaults and has been tightening norms gradually.

Industry observers say these changes will increase compliance costs for banks in the short term but should improve overall system stability.

Banks are expected to start submitting their first compliance reports under the new rules by the end of the next quarter. The RBI has indicated that it will review the impact of these changes after six months and may issue further clarifications if needed.
