US Proposes Stricter Green Card Rules for Firms Sponsoring Foreign Workers

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • The proposal targets the PERM labor certification step, the gateway to employer-sponsored green cards
  • No final rule yet: a comment period, revisions, and likely litigation lie ahead
  • Employers face higher costs and audit exposure; applicants face longer queue uncertainty
  • India-born applicants already wait years under per-country caps, so delays compound quickly

What the proposal reportedly involves

The US Department of Labor has floated a proposal to tighten oversight of the PERM (Program Electronic Review Management) process, the mandatory labor certification step employers must clear before sponsoring a foreign worker for an employment-based green card. According to reports by Forbes, The Washington Post, Reuters, and others, the move signals the Trump administration's intent to make permanent hiring of foreign talent harder, framed around the message that American workers are the best in the world.

Details of the rulemaking remain limited, and no final regulation has been published. Any change would require a comment period and would almost certainly face court challenges.

How PERM works today

PERM forces an employer to prove no qualified US worker is available for the role. It is already one of the slowest links in the employment-based immigration chain.

What it means for employers

Stricter PERM rules translate into higher legal costs, longer timelines, and wider audit exposure. Compliance teams should budget for tougher recruitment documentation, repeat wage determinations, and the possibility of supervised recruitment if DOL expands audit triggers. Firms that depend on H-1B holders, especially Indian IT services companies, would feel it first because green card sponsorship is a core retention tool.

What it means for workers

For applicants, the risk is prolonged limbo. India-born green card seekers already face multi-decade waits in EB-2 and EB-3 queues because of per-country caps, so any slowdown at the certification stage pushes job switches, promotions, and family reunification further out. Avoid resignation, travel, or startup decisions based on assumed timelines until a rule is final.

Key real-world highlights

  • The proposal targets PERM labor certification, not H-1B visas directly, though the two pipelines are linked.
  • No final rule exists yet; expect a comment period, revisions, and litigation before implementation.
  • Employers should audit recruitment ads, interview records, and wage documentation now to survive tougher scrutiny.
  • Applicants should treat all processing estimates as provisional and keep immigration counsel briefed.

Reactions and what comes next

Business groups argue tighter rules will choke hiring in STEM and healthcare, while supporters say the changes protect US wages.
