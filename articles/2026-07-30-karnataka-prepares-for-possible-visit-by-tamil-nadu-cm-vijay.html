Karnataka Prepares for Possible Visit by Tamil Nadu CM Vijay

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Karnataka government is preparing for a potential visit by Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Vijay.
  • Karnataka CM has invited Vijay to inspect Cauvery dams by helicopter.
  • Central Water Commission has returned Karnataka's Mekedatu project report, flagging changes.

The Karnataka government is making arrangements for a possible visit by Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Vijay, amid ongoing discussions over the sharing of Cauvery river water. Officials in Bengaluru confirmed that protocols are being drawn up, though no official date has been announced yet.

Invitation to Inspect Dams

Karnataka Chief Minister extended an invitation to his Tamil Nadu counterpart to inspect the Cauvery dams by helicopter. The offer is seen as a gesture of transparency and an attempt to build trust between the two states that have often clashed over water sharing.

The proposed visit would allow Vijay to see the storage levels and infrastructure at key reservoirs. Sources indicate that the itinerary would likely cover the Krishna Raja Sagara and Kabini dams.

Water Release Data

The Hindu reported that Karnataka has released excess Cauvery water to Tamil Nadu over the past 10 years. This disclosure comes at a time when both states are preparing for the crucial southwest monsoon season.

Karnataka officials argue that the state has consistently met its obligations under the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal award. The data is expected to form part of the state's submission before the Cauvery Water Management Authority (CWMA).

Mekedatu Project Hurdle

The Central Water Commission (CWC) has returned Karnataka's detailed project report for the Mekedatu balancing reservoir. The CWC flagged changes in the revised plan, asking the state to address specific concerns.

Karnataka has been pushing for the Mekedatu project to store and utilise its share of Cauvery water. Tamil Nadu has opposed the project, arguing that it would affect the flow of water downstream.

DMK's Stance on CWMA

The DMK in Tamil Nadu has maintained that only the CWMA holds the power to decide on water sharing. This position aligns with the Supreme Court's order that set up the authority to oversee the implementation of the tribunal's award.

Political observers note that the DMK's stance leaves little room for bilateral negotiations outside the CWMA framework. This could complicate any goodwill generated by the proposed visit.

The stage is set for a potential meeting between the two chief ministers, but the Cauvery dispute remains legally and politically complex. All eyes will be on whether the visit materialises and what tangible outcomes it might produce.
