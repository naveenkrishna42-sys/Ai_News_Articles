The Odyssey Box Office: Christopher Nolan Breaks Non-Franchise Hollywood Record in India

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Odyssey has broken the first-day box office record in India for a non-franchise Hollywood film.
  • Christopher Nolan's latest film surpasses his own previous record set by Oppenheimer.
  • The film's success underscores Nolan's strong pull among Indian audiences and the growing appeal of premium formats.
  • The record was achieved despite limited IMAX screens and competition from local releases.

Christopher Nolan has done it again. His latest film, The Odyssey, has shattered the opening day box office record for a non-franchise Hollywood film in India, according to early estimates from industry tracker Pinkvilla. The film earned a staggering sum that eclipses the previous benchmark, which was held by Nolan's own Oppenheimer.

This is not just a win for Nolan. It signals something bigger about what Indian audiences are willing to pay for. They are choosing original storytelling over recycled superhero fare.

Nolan's New Benchmark

The record is significant because it is for a non-franchise film. Unlike Marvel or DC entries that rely on decades of brand loyalty, The Odyssey is a standalone epic. It drew crowds purely on the strength of Nolan's name and the film's buzz.

Pinkvilla reports that the film's opening day collections in India crossed the ₹20 crore mark. This is a rare feat for a Hollywood film that is not part of an established series. The previous record holder, Oppenheimer, earned around ₹14.5 crore on its first day last year.

Why Audiences Are Showing Up

Much of the credit goes to Nolan's reputation for delivering spectacle. Indian moviegoers have developed a taste for his brand of cerebral blockbusters. The director's insistence on shooting with IMAX-certified cameras has also paid off.

Despite reports that only a handful of 'real' IMAX screens in India are showing the film in its intended format, fans have flocked to those venues. The scarcity has only driven demand. Many reports suggest that tickets for IMAX shows in major metros were sold out days in advance.

The film's subject matter—a sweeping mythological tale—also resonates with an Indian audience raised on epics like the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Nolan has managed to tap into a universal story that feels both ancient and urgent.

What the Numbers Mean

The opening day haul is a clear indicator that Hollywood's non-franchise films can still command strong numbers in India. This is a market that has historically been dominated by Bollywood and regional language blockbusters.

Nolan's box office performance in India has been on a steady upward trajectory. Tenet, released during the pandemic, earned about ₹5 crore on its opening day. Oppenheimer more than doubled that. Now, The Odyssey has set a new high.

The record also comes at a time when Indian cinema is grappling with a slump in big-budget releases. The success of The Odyssey could encourage Hollywood studios to invest more aggressively in the Indian market.

The Road Ahead

While the opening day numbers are impressive, the real test will be the film's hold over the weekend. Word-of-mouth will determine whether The Odyssey can sustain its momentum. Early reviews from both critics and audiences have been overwhelmingly positive.

Industry analysts are watching the second-day figures closely. If the film shows a strong Saturday, it could set its sights on becoming the highest-grossing Hollywood non-franchise film in India, a title currently held by Oppenheimer.

The next few days will reveal whether this record is just a flash in the pan or the start of a long theatrical run.
