Hyderabad Hosts Second BRICS Anti-Corruption Meeting

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Second BRICS anti-corruption meeting begins in Hyderabad today.
  • Delegates from member nations discuss cross-border cooperation and asset recovery.
  • Meeting underscores India's role in global anti-corruption efforts.

Hyderabad is playing host to the second BRICS anti-corruption meeting, which gets underway today. The gathering brings together officials and experts from Brazil, Russia, India, China, and South Africa for discussions that will shape how member states collaborate on tackling graft.

The meeting, which is being held in the city for the first time, underscores India's growing role in steering international anti-corruption dialogue. The choice of Hyderabad as the venue also highlights the city's emergence as a hub for high-level conferences and diplomatic engagements.

Focus on Cross-Border Cooperation

At the heart of the deliberations is the need for stronger cross-border cooperation. Delegates are expected to explore ways to share intelligence and best practices in investigating and prosecuting corruption cases that span multiple jurisdictions.

Money laundering and the recovery of stolen assets are also high on the agenda. With corruption increasingly taking on a transnational character, the BRICS nations are looking to build a unified front, ensuring that illicit wealth cannot find safe haven in any member country.

India's Anti-Corruption Framework

India's own anti-corruption architecture, including the Prevention of Corruption Act and the work of agencies like the Central Bureau of Investigation and the Enforcement Directorate, is likely to be a reference point during the sessions. Officials may also discuss the use of technology and data analytics to detect fraudulent activities.

The meeting comes at a time when BRICS nations are seeking to deepen their institutional ties. While the bloc has traditionally focused on economic cooperation, governance and transparency have emerged as key pillars of its agenda in recent years.

Hyderabad as a Diplomatic Venue

Hyderabad, with its blend of historical charm and modern infrastructure, has increasingly become a preferred location for international events. The city has previously hosted global conferences, including the World Economic Forum's India Economic Summit and various UN meetings.

Local authorities have made extensive arrangements for the delegation, including security and logistical support. The meeting is expected to conclude with a set of recommendations and a roadmap for future cooperation among the member states.

Observers note that the outcomes of this meeting could have a bearing on how BRICS countries coordinate on anti-corruption at multilateral forums like the United Nations Convention against Corruption.

While the specific agenda items remain under wraps, sources suggest that sessions will also cover preventive measures, whistleblower protection, and the role of civil society in exposing corrupt practices.

The meeting is being held against the backdrop of global efforts to curb illicit financial flows, with the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) tightening norms on money laundering and terror financing. BRICS members, which account for a significant share of the world's population and GDP, are seen as critical players in this fight.

As the sessions progress, all eyes will be on the joint statement that is likely to be issued at the end. That statement, analysts say, will be a litmus test of how far the bloc is willing to go in committing to concrete actions rather than just rhetoric.

The second BRICS anti-corruption meeting in Hyderabad is more than just a diplomatic formality. It is an opportunity for member states to turn shared concerns into coordinated action. With corruption remaining a stubborn challenge across the globe, the discussions here could set the tone for how these five nations tackle the problem in the years ahead.

What happens after the meeting will be crucial. The recommendations that emerge will need to be translated into enforceable mechanisms, and the real test will be in their implementation back home. For now, Hyderabad has set the stage for what could be a pivotal moment in BRICS cooperation on governance and integrity.
