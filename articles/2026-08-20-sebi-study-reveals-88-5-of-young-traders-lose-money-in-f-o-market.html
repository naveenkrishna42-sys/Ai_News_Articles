SEBI study reveals 88.5% of young traders lose money in F&O market

📅 2026-08-20 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • 88.5% of traders under 30 incurred losses in FY26
  • Most losing traders are from low-income groups
  • FY26 saw a 20% drop in F&O participation
  • Retail traders paid Rs 25,000 crore in transaction costs despite losses
  • SEBI has not yet confirmed the reasons for the decline in participation

Young Traders Bear Brunt of F&O Losses

Nearly 9 in 10 derivatives traders under the age of 30 incurred losses in the fiscal year 2026, according to a study by the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI). The study, which analyzed data from the first half of the year, revealed that 88.5% of these traders suffered losses, with the average loss standing at Rs 1,200 per trader.

Low-Income Groups Most Affected

Most of the losing traders belonged to low-income groups, with the average monthly income of these traders being Rs 20,000. The study attributed the high number of losses to the lack of awareness and understanding of the derivatives market among young traders.

F&O Participation Falls for First Time in Four Years

The study also revealed a 20% decline in the number of traders participating in the futures and options (F&O) market in FY26. This is the first time since 2022 that the participation has fallen. SEBI officials have not yet confirmed the reasons for the decline, but analysts suggest it could be due to the recent regulatory changes and the overall economic slowdown.

Retail Traders Pay High Transaction Costs Despite Losses

Despite the losses incurred, retail traders paid a hefty amount in transaction costs. The study revealed that retail traders paid Rs 25,000 crore in transaction costs in FY26, which is a significant portion of their overall trading bill.

What Happens Next?

The study's findings are likely to prompt SEBI to take stricter measures to regulate the F&O market and protect young and low-income traders. The board may also consider introducing awareness programs and educational initiatives to help traders understand the risks and rewards of trading in derivatives. Additionally, the recent decline in participation suggests that the market may be cooling off, which could have implications for the overall economic landscape.
