Bulimba’s Oxford Street turns into local art, food, fashion hub this weekend

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oxford Street in Bulimba hosts a weekend event featuring local food, fashion, and creativity.
  • The transformation aims to highlight small businesses and artisans in the area.
  • Visitors can expect pop-up stalls, live demonstrations, and curated shopping experiences.

Bulimba's Oxford Street is set to come alive this weekend as it transforms into a bustling showcase of local food, fashion, and creativity. The event, which kicks off on Saturday, aims to draw residents and visitors alike to the Brisbane suburb's main strip.

Organisers have lined up a mix of pop-up stalls, live demonstrations, and curated shopping experiences. The focus is squarely on small businesses and independent artisans who call the area home.

What to expect on Oxford Street

Shoppers can browse clothing from emerging designers, sample artisanal cheeses and baked goods, and watch live art installations take shape. Several Oxford Street cafes and restaurants will offer special menus or tasting plates for the occasion.

Street performances and interactive workshops are also planned, giving the precinct a festival-like atmosphere. The event is free to enter, and organisers expect heavy foot traffic throughout the weekend.

Supporting local businesses

The initiative is part of a broader push to revitalise high street retail in Bulimba. Local traders have faced headwinds from online shopping and shifting consumer habits in recent years.

By turning Oxford Street into a destination for experiential shopping, the organisers hope to remind people of the value of browsing in person. Many of the pop-ups will feature products not available in major chain stores.

What's on the schedule

On Saturday morning, a local fashion collective will stage a runway show outside a converted warehouse. Later in the day, a series of cooking demonstrations using seasonal produce will take place at the street's community plaza.

Sunday is expected to be family-focused, with face painting, a mini makers' market, and a live music set from a Brisbane band. Most activities run from 9 am to 5 pm on both days.

The event has drawn interest from neighbouring suburbs, and parking is expected to be tight. Organisers recommend using public transport or ride-sharing services.

As the weekend approaches, the forecast calls for clear skies, which should help draw the crowds. Whether this becomes a recurring fixture on Brisbane's calendar will depend on how many people show up to support the local scene.
