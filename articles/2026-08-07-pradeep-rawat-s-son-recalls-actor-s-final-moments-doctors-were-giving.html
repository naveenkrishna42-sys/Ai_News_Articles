Pradeep Rawat's son recalls actor's final moments: 'Doctors were giving him CPR'

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vikramaditya Rawat describes watching doctors perform CPR on his father
  • Veteran actor Pradeep Rawat, known for Ghajini and Sarfarosh, died at 74
  • Aamir Khan, Salman Khan, and Ashutosh Gowariker attended the Mumbai funeral

Vikramaditya Rawat, son of veteran actor Pradeep Rawat, has opened up about the harrowing final moments of his father's life. The 74-year-old actor, known for his powerful screen presence in films like Ghajini and Sarfarosh, passed away recently, leaving behind a legacy that spanned over four decades.

In an emotional recollection, Vikramaditya described the scene at the hospital. "I saw doctors giving him CPR, that's when I knew," he said, his voice heavy with the weight of the memory. The suddenness of the loss has clearly left the family in shock, as they grapple with the reality of a life cut short.

A Career That Defined Indian Cinema

Pradeep Rawat was not just a face on the screen; he was a force. His portrayal of the menacing villain in Ghajini opposite Aamir Khan remains etched in the minds of millions. But his repertoire went far beyond that single role. From the gritty cop in Sarfarosh to his memorable turn in Dabangg, Rawat brought a raw intensity to every character he inhabited.

His work with some of Bollywood's biggest names made him a familiar and respected figure in the industry. Directors valued his reliability; co-stars admired his craft. Over the years, he became a staple in both mainstream and regional cinema, appearing in Tamil, Telugu, and Kannada films with equal ease.

Friends and Colleagues Bid Farewell

The funeral in Mumbai drew a who's who of the film fraternity. Aamir Khan, who shared the screen with Rawat in Ghajini, was seen paying his respects. Salman Khan, another frequent collaborator, also arrived to offer his condolences. Director Ashutosh Gowariker, who worked with the actor in Lagaan, was present as well.

The gathering was a testament to the respect Rawat commanded. In an industry often accused of fleeting loyalties, the presence of these stalwarts spoke volumes. They stood in silence, remembering a man who had contributed so much to their own journeys.

A Complicated Bond with Stardom

In the days following his death, an old interview resurfaced where Rawat spoke about his time at Salman Khan's residence. "I stayed at Salman Khan's home for 6 months," he had said, adding that he got 'too comfortable' at Galaxy Apartments. The anecdote offered a glimpse into a relationship that went beyond professional courtesy.

It painted a picture of an actor who, despite his formidable on-screen persona, was warm and personable off it. Those who knew him recall a man who could light up a room with his laughter, a stark contrast to the brooding characters he often played. This duality was part of his charm.

As news of his passing spread, tributes poured in from across the industry. Many younger actors cited him as an influence, a reminder of the depth that character actors bring to Indian cinema. The void he leaves is significant, but his body of work ensures his memory will endure.

For his son Vikramaditya, the loss is profoundly personal. The image of doctors fighting to save his father is one that will not fade easily. Yet, in sharing his story, he has given fans a human connection to the actor they admired on screen.

The family has not announced any further details about memorial services or charitable contributions in his name. The industry, meanwhile, continues to mourn one of its most dependable talents.

As the days pass, the focus will shift to preserving Rawat's cinematic legacy. Film retrospectives and social media tributes are likely to keep his memory alive for a new generation of viewers.
