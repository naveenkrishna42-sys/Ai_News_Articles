Indian education minister resigns after mass protests over exam paper leaks

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • Education minister resigned after mass protests over leaked exam papers
  • Leaks affected multiple competitive exams, sparking student anger
  • Government promises reforms and investigation into the leak network

Minister steps down amid exam paper scandal

India's education minister submitted his resignation on Saturday, bowing to weeks of intense protests by students across the country. The demonstrations erupted after question papers for several major competitive examinations were leaked online before the scheduled test dates.

The minister's office confirmed the resignation in a brief statement, citing a desire to uphold the integrity of the country's examination system. The move comes as the government scrambles to contain a crisis that has shaken public confidence in the fairness of India's high-stakes entrance exams.

Leaks triggered nationwide student anger

Students took to the streets in cities including Delhi, Lucknow, and Patna after reports emerged that question papers for engineering and medical entrance tests had been circulating on social media platforms hours before the exams. Protesters demanded accountability and a thorough investigation into the leak network.

The leaks were not an isolated incident. Over the past month, similar breaches were reported for at least three other central-level examinations, affecting lakhs of aspirants who had spent months preparing. Many students had to sit for re-exams or faced cancellations, causing significant disruption to academic calendars.

Government announces crackdown

In response to the protests, the Ministry of Education announced a high-level probe into the paper leak racket. Officials said a special task force would be constituted to identify and prosecute those responsible for the breaches. The government has also promised to digitise the entire exam administration process to prevent future leaks.

Opposition parties have criticised the ruling administration for failing to safeguard the examination system. Leaders have called for a parliamentary inquiry and demanded that the government compensate affected students. The ruling party has defended its record, pointing to previous efforts to streamline exam processes.

Impact on students and future exams

The leaks have left many students uncertain about their academic futures. Several state boards and national testing agencies have postponed or cancelled exams, forcing students to wait for revised schedules. The disruptions have also raised concerns about the mental health of young aspirants, who often face intense pressure to perform.

The government has said it will work to restore normalcy and ensure that upcoming examinations are conducted securely. It has urged students to remain patient and promised that their interests will be protected.

As the education ministry transitions to new leadership, all eyes are on the next steps. The new minister will have to address the immediate fallout and implement long-term reforms to prevent a repeat of this crisis. The coming weeks will test the government's ability to rebuild trust in one of the world's largest examination systems.
