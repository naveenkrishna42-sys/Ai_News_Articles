Houthis Claim Red Sea Tanker Attacks, Risk New Chokepoint in Iran Conflict

📅 2026-07-23 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Houthis claim attacks on Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea
  • Bab el-Mandeb strait threatened as a new chokepoint in the Iran conflict
  • US launches 12th night of strikes on Iran amid expanding crisis
  • Yemenis react with both hope and fear to the Houthi blockade on Saudi Arabia

Houthis Claim Strikes on Saudi Oil Tankers

Yemen's Houthi movement has claimed responsibility for attacking Saudi Arabian oil tankers in the Red Sea. The announcement came on Thursday, escalating a conflict that now risks disrupting one of the world's most vital maritime chokepoints.

The Houthis said the strikes targeted tankers belonging to Saudi Arabia. The group has not provided immediate evidence of the attacks. Saudi authorities have not yet confirmed the claims.

Bab el-Mandeb Strait Emerges as Flashpoint

The Red Sea attacks threaten the Bab el-Mandeb strait, a narrow waterway connecting the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden. Roughly 10% of global seaborne oil passes through this chokepoint annually.

Any sustained blockade or military action near Bab el-Mandeb could drive up oil prices and disrupt supply chains. Analysts warn that India, which imports a significant portion of its crude through this route, would be particularly vulnerable.

US Strikes on Iran Enter 12th Night

The Houthi claims come as the United States military launches its 12th consecutive night of strikes on Iran. The campaign, which began earlier this month, aims to degrade Iranian military capabilities and deter further aggression in the region.

Washington has not commented directly on the Houthi attacks. However, the US Navy maintains a significant presence in the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden to ensure freedom of navigation.

Mixed Reactions in Yemen

Inside Yemen, the Houthi blockade on Saudi Arabia has drawn a complex response. Some Yemenis express hope that the attacks will pressure Riyadh to end its years-long military intervention in their country.

Others fear the escalation will only bring more suffering to a nation already devastated by a decade of civil war. The Houthi leadership has framed the strikes as part of a broader campaign in support of Iran, their key regional ally.

India's Strategic Stakes

Indian strategic analysts have called for New Delhi to take a leading role in securing the Bab el-Mandeb strait. India relies heavily on the Red Sea route for energy imports and trade with Europe.

New Delhi has not yet issued an official response to the Houthi claims. However, the Indian Navy has been expanding its footprint in the western Indian Ocean in recent years.

What happens next depends on how Saudi Arabia and the US respond to the Houthi attacks. If confirmed, the strikes could mark a dangerous widening of the Iran war into a new maritime front.
