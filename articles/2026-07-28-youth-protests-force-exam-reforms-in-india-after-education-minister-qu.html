Youth protests force exam reforms in India after education minister quits

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigns following sustained youth protests.
  • Protests erupted over alleged irregularities in competitive exam results.
  • Government announces major reforms to the examination system.
  • Opposition criticises the government's handling of the crisis.

Minister resigns amid student unrest

India's Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigned on Monday, bowing to weeks of intense protests by students across the country. The demonstrations, which began in early July, were sparked by allegations of widespread irregularities in the results of a major competitive examination.

The protests, led primarily by young aspirants for government jobs, quickly escalated into a national movement. Students blocked highways and held sit-ins at major intersections in several state capitals, demanding accountability and a complete overhaul of the system.

Government moves to reform exams

Hours after the minister's resignation was accepted, the Cabinet announced a series of sweeping reforms to the examination process. The changes include increased transparency in result tabulation, a new independent grievance redressal body, and a review of the testing agency's protocols.

Officials said the reforms are aimed at restoring public faith in the selection process for government positions. The government has promised to implement the new measures before the next round of examinations, scheduled for early next year.

Political fallout and reactions

Mr Pradhan received a rousing welcome from his party colleagues when he entered Parliament on Monday. BJP MPs chanted slogans in his support, a gesture that drew sharp criticism from the opposition.

Congress leader Rahul Gandhi accused the ruling party of celebrating what he called the destruction of children's futures. The opposition has demanded a full parliamentary inquiry into the exam irregularities, a call the government has so far rejected.

Sources within the government indicated that the Prime Minister's Office had initially offered Mr Pradhan a change in portfolio, but the offer was rejected. The minister chose to step down entirely, taking moral responsibility for the crisis.

What the protests achieved

The protests, which at their peak drew lakhs of young participants, mark one of the most significant displays of youth-led political action in India in recent years. Analysts say the movement succeeded in forcing the government's hand where internal reviews had failed.

The new reforms include a provision for third-party audits of exam results and a dedicated helpline for students to report anomalies. The government has also promised to fast-track the appointment of a new, independent head for the testing agency.

What happens next will depend on how quickly the government implements the promised changes. Student leaders have warned they will resume protests if the reforms are not enacted in time for the next exam cycle.
