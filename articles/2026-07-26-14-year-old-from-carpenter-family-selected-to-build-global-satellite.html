14-year-old from carpenter family selected to build global satellite

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pranav Sharma, a 14-year-old from a carpenter's family in Delhi, selected to build a satellite for a global project.
  • The satellite, part of the GLOBE Program, aims to study climate change and will be launched by ISRO.
  • Pranav's project stood out among 1,500 entries for its innovative design to monitor air pollution.
  • He will work with scientists from NASA and ISRO over the next year to develop the satellite.

From a carpenter's home to the stars

A 14-year-old boy from a carpenter's family in Delhi has been selected to build a satellite as part of a global space initiative. Pranav Sharma, a Class 10 student, will now work with top scientists from NASA and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) on the project.

The selection came through the GLOBE Program, an international science and education initiative. Pranav's proposal to build a small satellite that monitors air pollution won over a panel of experts, beating over 1,500 entries from across the world.

The project that caught the world's attention

Pranav's design focuses on measuring particulate matter and greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. His satellite, once launched, will beam data to ground stations, helping scientists track pollution patterns in real time.

"I wanted to build something that helps people see what we are breathing," Pranav told India Today. His father, a carpenter, and his mother, a homemaker, have supported his passion for space since he was a child, often using scrap wood to build models of rockets and satellites.

How the selection unfolded

The GLOBE Program, in collaboration with NASA and ISRO, invited students aged 14 to 18 to submit satellite designs for a climate monitoring mission. Entries were judged on scientific merit, feasibility, and innovation.

Pranav's proposal included a low-cost design using off-the-shelf components, making it replicable for students in developing countries. The judges called it "a brilliant blend of simplicity and impact."

Over the next year, Pranav will attend workshops at ISRO's headquarters in Bengaluru and NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory in California. He will work alongside engineers to build and test the satellite, which is expected to be launched in 2027.

What this means for Indian space science

Pranav's selection is a boost for India's growing space education ecosystem. ISRO has been actively involving school students in its missions, from building small satellites to launching them aboard its rockets.

Officials say the GLOBE Program partnership will help identify and nurture young talent. "We want students to see that space is not just for scientists from big cities," an ISRO spokesperson said.

The project also highlights the role of grassroots innovation. Pranav's family does not own a computer, and he uses a shared tablet at his school to study and design his projects.

What happens next

Pranav will travel to Bengaluru next month for the first phase of the build. His satellite is expected to be completed by mid-2027 and will be launched on an ISRO Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle.

The data from his satellite will be made available to schools worldwide, giving students a chance to study climate change with real-time space observations. For now, Pranav says he just wants to finish his homework and get to work on his satellite.
