Educators meet in Mumbai to discuss primary education reforms

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two-day meet in Mumbai brings together educators from across India
  • Focus on curriculum overhaul, teacher training, and early childhood learning
  • Outcome expected to shape policy recommendations for primary education

MUMBAI: Educators from across the country gathered in Mumbai on Saturday for a two-day brainstorming session on revamping primary education. The meeting, organised by a consortium of educational institutions, aims to address persistent challenges in foundational learning.

Participants include school principals, curriculum designers, education researchers, and officials from state education boards. Discussions are centred on how to make primary schooling more effective, inclusive, and relevant to contemporary needs.

Curriculum and teaching methods under scrutiny

The first day's sessions focused on the current curriculum framework, with many educators arguing for a shift away from rote learning. There was consensus that critical thinking and problem-solving skills must be woven into early education.

Teaching methods, too, came in for review. Several speakers highlighted the need for activity-based learning and the integration of local languages in the early years, while others stressed the importance of teacher training and classroom resources.

Early childhood education takes centrestage

A significant portion of the discussions was devoted to early childhood care and education. Experts noted that the first eight years of a child's life are critical for cognitive and emotional development, yet this stage often receives inadequate attention in policy and practice.

Some participants shared models from their own states, including community-led preschool initiatives and digital tools for early assessment. These examples are expected to inform the recommendations to be drafted at the end of the meet.

Learning outcomes and assessment reform

Another key theme was the mode of assessment in primary schools. Many educators called for moving beyond annual exams towards continuous, formative evaluation that measures conceptual understanding rather than memorisation.

Concerns were also raised about learning loss, particularly among children from disadvantaged backgrounds. The pandemic's impact on foundational literacy and numeracy was referenced, with several delegates urging targeted remedial measures.

Towards a shared roadmap

The gathering is not merely an academic exercise. Organisers said the deliberations would feed into a white paper to be shared with state education departments and the union ministry. This could influence upcoming policy decisions on school education.

While no concrete proposals have been finalised yet, the mood among participants was one of cautious optimism. Many stressed that meaningful change would require sustained political will and adequate funding, not just policy tweaks.

The second day is expected to focus on implementation strategies, including teacher recruitment, infrastructure gaps, and community engagement. A draft set of recommendations is likely to be released by evening.

Observers will be watching whether the ideas generated here translate into actionable reforms. With states having primary responsibility for school education, the real test lies in how these suggestions are adapted to local contexts.
