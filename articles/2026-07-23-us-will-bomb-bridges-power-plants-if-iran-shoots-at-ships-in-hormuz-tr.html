US Will Bomb Bridges, Power Plants If Iran Shoots At Ships In Hormuz: Trump

📅 2026-07-23 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump threatens to bomb Iranian infrastructure for any attack on US ships in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Iran warns of a forceful response to any US strike, signalling escalation risks in the Gulf.
  • The threats come amid heightened tensions after Houthi actions blocked the Red Sea artery.

Trump's Warning Over Hormuz

President Donald Trump has threatened to bomb Iranian bridges and power plants if Tehran attacks American ships in the Strait of Hormuz. The warning, delivered from the White House, escalates a simmering standoff in one of the world's most critical oil transit chokepoints.

"If they shoot at our ships, we will destroy a bridge. If they shoot again, we will take out a power plant," Trump said. The statement was direct and left little room for diplomatic nuance.

Iran's Response

Tehran did not wait long to reply. Iranian officials described the US threat as a provocation. "An eye for an eye," a senior commander warned, vowing a forceful response to any American strike. The language mirrored the rising hostility between the two nations.

The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly a fifth of the world's oil supply. Any disruption there sends shockwaves through global energy markets. Both sides are now posturing for a confrontation that neither may want but both seem prepared for.

Broader Tensions in the Region

The immediate trigger for Trump's threat was not an Iranian attack in Hormuz itself. It was the Houthi blockade of the Red Sea. The Houthis, who are backed by Iran, have been disrupting shipping lanes, drawing a sharp US response.

Washington views Tehran as the puppeteer. "We hold Iran responsible for the actions of its proxies," a US official said. The threat to bomb infrastructure is a departure from previous US strategies that focused on naval patrols and sanctions.

Iran has repeatedly warned that it will not tolerate any aggression near its borders. The Strait of Hormuz is not just a trade route; it is a line of national security for Tehran. Any US strike on Iranian soil would be seen as an act of war.

What Happens Next

The situation remains volatile. No shots have been fired yet, but the rhetoric has escalated to a point where a miscalculation could trigger a broader conflict. Diplomats from third-party nations are expected to step in, attempting to de-escalate tensions behind closed doors.

For now, the world watches the waters of the Gulf. The next move from either side could define the region's stability for years to come.
