How to pick one AI subscription and cancel the rest

📅 2026-08-11 📂 AI Tips & Tools Original source ↗
Key points
  • Compare AI subscriptions by task type, not just benchmark scores.
  • Use a 7-day trial matrix to track real-world performance.
  • Cancel redundant plans and keep only the tool that fits your daily work.

Stop paying for three AI tools when one will do

If you are shelling out for Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude every month, you are probably wasting money. Android Authority's comparison of the three paid tiers found a clear winner—but that winner depends on what you actually do. Before you cancel anything, you need to audit your own usage, not just follow a reviewer's verdict.

This guide walks you through a practical method to test each tool against your real tasks, measure the results, and pick one subscription to keep. You can complete the whole process in a week, and it will save you anywhere from ₹2,000 to ₹4,000 per month depending on your current plans.

Step 1: List your top five AI tasks

Write down the five things you use AI for most often. Be specific. Instead of "writing," write "drafting client emails under 200 words." Instead of "coding," write "debugging Python scripts for data cleaning." This specificity matters because each model has different strengths.

For example, Gemini often excels at tasks tied to Google Workspace, while ChatGPT is strong at general conversation and plugins. Claude tends to handle long-form writing and analysis well. Your list will tell you which strengths you actually need.

Step 2: Run a 7-day trial matrix

Create a simple spreadsheet with columns for each task and rows for each tool. For the next seven days, use only one tool per day, rotating through Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude. Do not mix them. At the end of each day, score each tool from 1 to 5 on speed, accuracy, and ease of use.

This rotation removes bias. If you test all three on the same task in one sitting, you will overthink it. Spacing the tests over a week gives you a realistic picture of daily use.

  1. Day 1–2: Use Gemini for all five tasks. Note any friction points, like needing to switch apps or rephrase prompts.
  2. Day 3–4: Repeat the same tasks with ChatGPT. Record how many times you had to correct its output.
  3. Day 5–6: Do the same with Claude. Pay attention to response length and whether it follows formatting instructions.
  4. Day 7: Review your scores. Add up the totals for each tool across all tasks.

This is the core of the audit. Do not skip days or swap tools mid-task—you will muddy the results. If a tool fails a task entirely, note that as a zero and move on.

Step 3: Compare the costs, not just the scores

Once you have your scores, factor in what you are paying. The winner is not always the highest scorer—it is the tool that gives you the best value for your specific usage. If Claude scores 4.5 on your top task but costs ₹2,500 per month, while Gemini scores 4.0 and costs ₹1,500, the math might favour Gemini.

Make a simple table: total score, monthly price, and score per rupee. Divide the total score by the price to get a value ratio. The tool with the highest ratio is your keeper. If two tools tie, choose the one with the lower price or the one that integrates better with your existing apps.

Step 4: Cancel the losers and set a reminder

After you pick your winner, cancel the other two subscriptions immediately. Most services let you keep access until the end of the billing cycle, so you will not lose anything by cancelling early. Use that leftover time to confirm your choice with a few real projects.

Set a calendar reminder for three months from now to re-run this audit. AI models update frequently, and the winner today might not be the winner in a quarter. A quick 30-minute re-test every 90 days keeps your subscription list lean and your money where it works hardest.

When this technique works—and when it doesn't

This audit is ideal for freelancers, students, and small business owners who use AI for a defined set of tasks. If your work involves heavy experimentation or you need multiple models for different clients, keeping two subscriptions might be justified. But for most people, one paid tier is enough. Run the matrix, trust the scores, and cancel the rest.
