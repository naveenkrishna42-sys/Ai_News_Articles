Crude Posts Biggest Weekly Gain in Months as Middle East Crisis Intensifies

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Crude oil prices surged about 12% this week, marking the biggest weekly gain in months.
  • Renewed US-Iran strikes and an attack near Kuwait have reignited geopolitical risk premium.
  • Red Sea shipping threats and potential Strait of Hormuz disruption are driving supply fears.

Crude oil prices are on track for their biggest weekly gain in months, with benchmarks surging about 12% as a fresh wave of US-Iran hostilities rattles global energy markets. The rally, which pushed prices up roughly 4% on Friday alone, marks the return of a full-blown geopolitical risk premium that had been absent for much of 2026.

Brent crude futures climbed past the $85 per barrel mark during Friday's session, while West Texas Intermediate hovered near $81. The sharp upward move comes after weeks of relative calm in oil markets, when traders had largely priced out the threat of supply disruptions.

What Triggered This Week's Rally

The escalation began earlier this week when reports emerged of an attack near Kuwait's territorial waters, raising fears that the conflict could spill into the Persian Gulf's main shipping lanes. The US and Iran have exchanged renewed strikes in recent days, with each side targeting military assets in and around the Gulf region.

These developments have directly threatened the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway through which about 20% of the world's oil passes. Analysts warn that even a temporary closure could send prices spiraling well above $100 a barrel. The Red Sea, another critical chokepoint for global crude shipments, also remains under threat from Houthi-linked operations.

Market Reaction and Supply Fears

Traders have scrambled to price in the possibility of supply losses from the Middle East, which accounts for nearly a third of global oil production. The 12% weekly gain is the largest since early 2024, when a similar US-Iran confrontation briefly spiked crude prices.

India, which imports over 80% of its crude oil requirements, faces particular vulnerability. Indian refineries source a significant portion of their feedstock from the Gulf region. A sustained price rally would widen the country's import bill and could fuel domestic inflation, potentially complicating the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy stance.

Previous Calm Erased

Just two weeks ago, oil markets were trading in a narrow range, with OPEC+ production cuts and tepid global demand keeping prices steady. The sudden reversal highlights how quickly geopolitical flashpoints can reshape market dynamics.

The risk premium had been steadily eroding since mid-2025, as diplomatic channels between Washington and Tehran showed faint signs of movement. Those hopes have now evaporated. Renewed strikes between the US and Iran have jeopardised global oil supplies once again, and no immediate de-escalation is in sight.

What to Watch Next

All eyes are now on the US administration's next move. Any indication of a military response targeting Iranian oil infrastructure would likely send prices higher. Meanwhile, OPEC+ spare capacity — primarily held by Saudi Arabia and the UAE — remains the key buffer against a full-blown supply crisis. Whether the cartel chooses to unlock that capacity will determine how far this rally can run.
