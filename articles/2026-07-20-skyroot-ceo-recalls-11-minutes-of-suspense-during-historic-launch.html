Skyroot CEO recalls 11 minutes of suspense during historic launch

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Skyroot CEO recounts 11 minutes of suspense during a historic rocket launch.
  • A software glitch and lost signal added tension to the mission.
  • The launch ultimately succeeded, marking a milestone for the private space sector.

Skyroot Aerospace's CEO has detailed the nail-biting 11 minutes that defined the company's historic rocket launch. The mission faced a software glitch and a complete loss of signal. It was a test of nerve for the team on the ground.

The executive described the episode as a moment of high drama. Engineers scrambled to diagnose the problem. The glitch threatened to derail what was meant to be a landmark event for India's private space industry.

Software glitch hits critical phase

The trouble began shortly after liftoff. A software anomaly appeared in the rocket's guidance system. It caused the vehicle to deviate from its planned trajectory.

The CEO stated that the team spotted the anomaly on their screens. They had seconds to decide whether to abort or press on. The glitch was not a hardware failure but a coding issue that triggered unexpected behavior.

Mission control went silent. Engineers ran rapid diagnostics. They concluded the rocket could self-correct if given time. The call was made to stay the course.

Lost signal adds to tension

Then the signal dropped. Telemetry from the rocket vanished for several seconds. Ground stations could not confirm if the vehicle was still flying or had broken apart.

The CEO recalled that those seconds felt like hours. No data meant no way to assess the situation. The team had to rely on pre-planned contingency protocols. It was a stark reminder of the risks in spaceflight.

Signal returned as the rocket reached a higher altitude. The vehicle was back on track. The relief in the control room was palpable.

Historic milestone for private space

The launch has been hailed as a breakthrough. Skyroot became the first Indian private company to reach a significant altitude. It demonstrated indigenous capability in rocket technology.

The CEO credited the team's composure under pressure. He noted that the glitch taught valuable lessons for future missions. The company is already planning its next launch with improved software checks.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact cause of the software glitch. A full review is underway. The success, however, has boosted confidence in India's private space ecosystem.

What happens next: Skyroot will now focus on commercial satellite launches. The company aims to offer affordable access to space for small payloads. Investors and industry watchers are keen to see how it scales operations.
