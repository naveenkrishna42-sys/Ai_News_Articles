Jude Anthany Joseph reveals why he quit Ayushmann film over Mohanlal remark

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Jude Anthany Joseph walked away from an Ayushmann Khurrana project after Mohanlal was referred to as a 'secondary artist'.
  • The director said the remark deeply offended him and went against his respect for the Malayalam superstar.
  • Joseph's revelation has sparked discussions on how regional cinema icons are perceived in Bollywood productions.

Director Jude Anthany Joseph has revealed why he stepped away from a much-anticipated project starring Ayushmann Khurrana. The filmmaker, known for the blockbuster 2018, said the tipping point came when someone on the team referred to Mohanlal as a 'secondary artist'.

In a recent interview, Joseph recounted the incident without naming the film or the individuals involved. He said the comment was made during early discussions for the project, which was being planned as a Hindi-language feature. The director, who has often spoken of his deep admiration for Mohanlal, found the remark impossible to ignore.

A question of respect

Joseph said he did not take the comment lightly. For him, Mohanlal is not just a superstar but a cultural institution in Malayalam cinema. To hear the actor described in such diminutive terms, he said, felt like a dismissal of an entire industry's legacy.

“I couldn't work in an environment where such a legend is reduced to a label like that,” Joseph said, adding that creative differences are one thing, but fundamental disrespect is another. He chose to walk away rather than compromise on his values.

Regional icons, national gaze

The revelation has reignited a familiar debate in Indian cinema — how regional stars are often measured against Bollywood's yardstick. Mohanlal, with a career spanning over four decades and hundreds of films, commands a following that few can match. Yet, in certain north-centric production rooms, his stature is sometimes underplayed.

Joseph's decision, he said, was not about ego but about principle. He pointed out that Malayalam cinema has consistently produced some of the finest films in the country, and its artists deserve equal footing. The director's stance has drawn support from fans and colleagues alike, many of whom have taken to social media to applaud his integrity.

What the industry is saying

While neither Ayushmann Khurrana nor the production team has commented on the matter, the incident has become a talking point in film circles. Some have questioned whether such remarks are isolated or indicative of a deeper bias. Others have argued that the industry needs more voices like Joseph's to challenge these hierarchies.

For now, Joseph remains focused on his upcoming projects in Malayalam cinema, where he continues to find creative freedom and respect. He said he has no regrets about the decision, and that his self-respect matters more than any film, regardless of its scale or budget.

Looking ahead

As the conversation around regional representation in Bollywood grows louder, Joseph's candid admission adds a personal chapter to the discourse. Whether it prompts change in how production houses approach cross-industry collaborations remains to be seen. For the director, the line in the sand is clear: respect is non-negotiable.
