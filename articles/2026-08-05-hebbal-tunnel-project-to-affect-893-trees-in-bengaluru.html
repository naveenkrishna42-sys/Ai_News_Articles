Hebbal tunnel project to affect 893 trees in Bengaluru

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Hebbal tunnel project will affect 893 trees in Bengaluru.
  • The project is part of efforts to decongest one of the city's busiest corridors.
  • Officials have not yet specified the exact number of trees to be transplanted or felled.

The proposed tunnel project at Hebbal, one of Bengaluru's most congested junctions, is set to impact 893 trees, according to official estimates. The revelation has put the spotlight on the environmental cost of the city's infrastructure push.

Project scope and impact

The tunnel is planned to ease traffic snarls on the Hebbal flyover stretch, a critical artery connecting the city to the airport and northern suburbs. Officials say the alignment will require clearing vegetation along the corridor, though the precise breakdown of trees to be transplanted versus felled has not been made public yet.

Environmentalists have voiced concern over the scale of the impact, noting that Hebbal's green cover already faces pressure from rapid urbanisation. The number—893—represents a significant chunk of the area's tree population, many of which are mature and provide shade and habitat.

Compensatory measures under scrutiny

The civic agency overseeing the project has indicated that transplantation will be attempted where feasible. However, past efforts at relocating large trees in Bengaluru have had mixed results, with survival rates varying widely depending on species and post-transplant care.

Officials have not yet confirmed how many of the affected trees can be saved or what compensatory plantation will be undertaken. The lack of clarity has added to the unease among residents and green activists, who are demanding a detailed tree census and a transparent action plan.

Traffic vs environment debate

The tunnel is part of a broader plan to overhaul the Hebbal interchange, which handles lakhs of vehicles daily and regularly witnesses long queues during peak hours. Proponents argue that grade-separated corridors are essential to keep the city moving.

Critics, however, question whether the environmental cost is justified, pointing to alternative designs that could reduce tree loss. The project's approval process is being watched closely, with several citizen groups planning to petition for a revised alignment.

What officials say

Authorities have stated that the tree impact assessment was conducted as per regulations, and that steps will be taken to minimise damage. They have promised to release a detailed mitigation plan before work begins on the ground.

No timeline has been announced for when the project will receive final clearance or when construction is expected to start. The final decision on the tree count and relocation strategy is likely to be made in consultation with the forest department.

What to watch

In the coming weeks, the focus will be on the publication of the detailed project report and the tree-by-tree assessment. The response of the state's tree authority and any legal challenges from environmental groups will determine how quickly the tunnel moves from paper to pavement.
