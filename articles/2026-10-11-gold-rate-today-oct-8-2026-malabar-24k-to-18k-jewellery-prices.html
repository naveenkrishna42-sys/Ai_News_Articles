Gold rate today (Oct 8, 2026): what buyers should actually check

Gold prices update daily, but the figure you see in headlines is rarely the number you should base a purchase decision on—especially for jewellery. For shoppers looking at Malabar Gold & Diamonds rates for 24K, 22K, 20K, and 18K, the buyer-critical step is to compare the effective value per gram after you account for purity and any jewellery-specific add-ons.

On October 8, 2026, The Economic Times highlights “gold rate today” for these purity points from Malabar Gold & Diamonds. That’s useful for fast reference, but serious buyers should validate what the retailer is quoting: metal rate versus final making cost and taxes, and whether the listed rate is for jewellery form (with design and labour) rather than investment-grade bars.

This approach protects you from a common buying mistake: assuming that a “lower purity” jewellery rate automatically delivers a better rupee-per-gram bargain. In reality, 22K/20K/18K often carry a different making-charge structure and a different jewellery weight tolerance, which can alter the effective total cost.

How to compare 24K, 22K, 20K and 18K jewellery pricing correctly

Purity determines how much of the piece is “fine gold” and how much is alloy. While the retailer may quote a rate for each purity, comparing across purities is best done using a purity-adjusted lens: if you know the jewellery price per gram at a given karat, you can estimate the implied cost of fine gold embedded in that jewellery.

Here’s the practical workflow for buyers reviewing Malabar’s daily quotes: first, check the stated karat (24K/22K/20K/18K) and ensure the quotation is for jewellery. Second, ask for the break-up: metal value, making charges, and GST/taxes as applicable. Third, compute rupees per gram of fine gold by adjusting for purity to see whether your “cheaper-looking” option is truly cheaper after purification math.

Even without doing complex calculations, you can apply a real-world benchmark: if the gap between two purities is consistently smaller than what purity alone would explain, making charges or design weight differences are likely doing the heavy lifting. That’s the moment to request a detailed invoice comparison before you commit.

Pricing context: why jewellery rates behave differently than investment gold

Investment buyers often track spot gold because they buy near-metal value. Jewellery buyers, however, should expect additional costs that don’t scale linearly with karat. Making charges compensate for design complexity, setting, finishing, and retailer overhead—factors that can vary by model category even on the same day’s gold headline rate.

That’s why the “gold rate today” headline from a publication is only the starting point. When you shop for jewellery at Malabar (or any retailer), treat the daily gold rate as the metal-cost reference, then negotiate/compare based on the total price you’ll actually pay for the specific design.

For example, if 18K looks much cheaper than 22K on a pure karat basis, that may be true. But if the making charges for the 18K design are higher (or if the piece’s net weight differs due to design geometry), the final rupee-per-gram outcome might not be as favourable as the headline suggests.

Key insights for shoppers checking Malabar jewellery rates on Oct 8, 2026

  • Demand clarity on “rate” type: confirm whether it’s metal-only rate or the all-in jewellery price used for billing.
  • Compare like-for-like grams: ensure you’re comparing the same net weight definition (gross/less stones) when you look at different karats.
  • Use purity-adjusted reasoning: avoid choosing solely by the lower karat label; compute the effective fine-gold value trend.
  • Watch the hidden variables: making charges, labour category, and stone/setting charges can swing totals more than small daily metal changes.
  • Time your purchase around liquidity needs: if you might resell, remember that buyback terms often apply discounts versus headline rates.

Buyers who follow these steps turn daily rate pages into real decision tools. The headline “24K/22K/20K/18K gold jewellery prices” helps you track market direction, but the invoice break-up and purity-adjusted comparison is what determines whether today’s deal is genuinely value-for-money.

What to ask at the counter (fast checklist)

Before you pay, ask for the exact metal value used for the quoted karat, making charges, GST/taxes, and the net weight applicable to billing. If stones are involved, ask for stone/setting segregation so the gold component is fully auditable. This keeps your comparison objective whether you’re choosing between 24K classic pieces or more cost-optimised 18K designs.
