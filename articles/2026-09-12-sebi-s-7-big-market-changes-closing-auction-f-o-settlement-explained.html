SEBI's 7 Big Market Changes: Closing Auction, F&O Settlement Explained

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • SEBI has proposed seven changes covering the Closing Auction Session (CAS), derivatives expiry settlement and market timings, following industry feedback on the current framework.
  • Two settlement options for expiry-day closing prices are under review, a shift that directly affects how F&O positions are marked and squared off.
  • The proposals could alter closing-price discovery, liquidity concentration and intraday strategy for cash-market and derivatives traders alike.

SEBI has floated a set of seven proposed changes to the Closing Auction Session (CAS), derivatives settlement mechanics and market timings, inviting public comments before finalising the framework. The proposals follow sustained industry feedback on how closing prices are discovered and how expiry-day positions are settled. For retail traders and institutional desks alike, the fine print matters more than the headline, because closing prices feed into portfolio valuation, margin computation and index settlement.

What The Closing Auction Session Does

CAS was introduced to replace the old method of computing closing prices from the last traded price, which was prone to manipulation in the final seconds of trading. Under CAS, orders are collected in a defined window, matched, and a single equilibrium closing price is struck. That price becomes the reference for mark-to-market margins, NAV computation by mutual funds, and settlement of derivative contracts.

The regulator has been reviewing CAS after feedback that liquidity in the auction window is thinner than expected and that price discovery can diverge from the continuous market, particularly on volatile days.

The Seven Proposed Changes At A Glance

Sources indicate the proposals span auction timing, order types permitted during the session, price bands for auction orders, treatment of unfilled orders, the closing price methodology on expiry days, derivatives settlement referencing, and the overall sequencing of market close activities. Two alternative options are under review specifically for determining the closing price used in expiry settlement, according to exchange and media reports.

Why Derivatives Expiry Settlement Is The Contested Bit

Expiry-day closing prices determine the final settlement value of stock and index futures and the intrinsic value of options. If the closing price methodology changes, the payoff profile of every open position on expiry changes with it. Traders who hedge using closing prices, and brokers who compute margin shortfalls using those prices, face a direct operational impact.

The two options under review differ in how much weight they give to the auction versus the continuous session. One approach leans on the auction-derived equilibrium price; the other blends or references a broader window. Each has trade-offs between manipulation resistance and representativeness.

Market Timings And Operational Load

Any change to the sequencing of the close affects back-office cut-offs, risk-system batch runs and broker reconciliation windows. Exchanges and clearing corporations will need to re-engineer processing schedules. For members, that means testing cycles and possible changes to end-of-day reporting deadlines.

Key Real-World Implications

  • Closing price reliability: A revised CAS design aims to reduce last-minute price distortion, which protects passive funds and retail investors who transact at closing prices.
  • Expiry-day strategy: Traders must re-test hedging and squaring-off strategies once the final settlement methodology is notified.
  • Liquidity shift: Auction-window liquidity may migrate if order types or price bands change, altering bid-ask behaviour in the final minutes.
  • Operational cost: Brokers face system changes, testing and compliance costs before go-live.
  • Arbitrage impact: Cash-futures arbitrage spreads could narrow or widen depending on how the closing price is struck.

Pros And Cons Of The Proposed Framework

  • Pros: Stronger manipulation resistance, transparent auction-based discovery, and a more defensible settlement reference for derivatives.
  • Cons: Transition risk for brokers, possible thinner auction liquidity, and uncertainty for traders until the final option is chosen.

What Traders Should Watch

Track the consultation timeline and the final choice between the two expiry-settlement options. Once notified, expect exchange circulars on mock trading sessions and revised end-of-day schedules. Position sizing, margin buffers and algo logic tied to closing prices should be reviewed only after the final text is published, not on the basis of proposals.

SEBI's stated intent is a closing mechanism that is robust, fair and hard to game. Whether the seven changes achieve that without adding friction to the final minutes of trading is the question the consultation process will answer.
