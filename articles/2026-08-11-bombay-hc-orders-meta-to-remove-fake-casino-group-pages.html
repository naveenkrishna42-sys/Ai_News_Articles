Bombay HC orders Meta to remove fake casino group pages

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bombay High Court orders Meta to remove fake social media pages of a casino group
  • The court observed that the pages violated trademark rights and caused reputational damage
  • Meta has been directed to comply with the order and report back to the court

The Bombay High Court has directed Meta, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, to remove fake social media pages impersonating a casino group. The order came in response to a plea alleging that the pages were misleading users and tarnishing the group's reputation.

The petitioner, a casino group operating in India, argued that the fake pages used their name and logos without authorisation, creating confusion among customers. The court, while hearing the matter, noted that such pages amount to trademark infringement and could harm the business's goodwill.

Court's directive to Meta

Justice Manish Pitale, presiding over the bench, directed Meta to take down the identified pages within a specified timeframe. The court also asked the social media giant to ensure that no similar pages are created in the future using the casino group's trademarks.

Meta's counsel assured the court that the company would comply with the order. However, the court directed the platform to file a compliance report detailing the action taken, setting the next hearing for two weeks later.

Legal grounds and arguments

The petitioner's counsel argued that the fake pages were not only a violation of trademark laws but also amounted to passing off, a legal term used when one party misrepresents its goods or services as those of another. The counsel highlighted that the pages were used to solicit business, which could mislead customers into believing they were dealing with the official casino group.

On the other hand, Meta's legal team contended that the company had a standard procedure for handling such complaints and that the pages could be removed upon verification. However, the court was not entirely convinced and stressed the need for prompt action to prevent further misuse.

Broader implications for social media platforms

The ruling underscores the growing scrutiny on social media platforms to act against counterfeit accounts and pages. In recent years, Indian courts have increasingly held platforms accountable for content that infringes on intellectual property rights or deceives users.

Legal experts say this order could set a precedent for how similar cases are handled, especially those involving businesses that rely heavily on brand identity. The court's insistence on a compliance report indicates a proactive approach to ensure that orders are not just passed but also implemented.

The casino group, which has operations in Goa and other parts of the country, had earlier reported the fake pages to Meta, but the company did not act promptly, prompting the legal recourse.

Officials from Meta have not yet commented on the specifics of the order, but the company is expected to follow the court's directive in the coming days.

As the case progresses, all eyes will be on how Meta handles the takedown and whether it strengthens its mechanisms to address such complaints in the future. The next hearing will also determine if any further directions are needed to protect the interests of the petitioner.
