Harry Kane Cites Messi's Longevity as Inspiration for Another World Cup Bid

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Harry Kane uses Lionel Messi's career longevity as motivation for a potential 2030 World Cup run.
  • England lost the 2026 World Cup semifinal to Argentina, led by Messi.
  • Kane, at 33, believes he can emulate Messi's ability to compete at a high level into his late 30s.

England captain Harry Kane is not ready to close the book on his international career. Following England's semifinal exit at the 2026 FIFA World Cup, Kane has pointed to Lionel Messi as proof that age does not have to be a barrier to competing at the highest level.

The 33-year-old striker, who led his country through a turbulent tournament under manager Thomas Tuchel, sees a path forward that mirrors the Argentine legend's remarkable longevity.

Messi's Example Fuels Kane's Ambition

Kane, who turned 33 earlier this year, watched Messi, now 39, dismantle England's defense in the semifinal. The Argentine captain scored and assisted in a 3-1 victory that sent his team to the final against Spain. It was a painful lesson for England, but for Kane, it was also a lesson in possibility.

“You look at what Leo is doing at his age, and it gives you a lot of belief,” Kane said after the match. “He's been at the top for nearly two decades. If he can do it, why can't I?”

Kane's statement is not mere bravado. The striker has evolved his game in recent years, dropping deeper and relying less on explosive pace. At Bayern Munich, he has maintained a prolific scoring rate, suggesting his peak may extend well into his late 30s.

Tuchel's Tournament Ends in Disappointment

England's run in the tournament was marred by inconsistency and tactical confusion. Manager Thomas Tuchel, appointed to finally deliver a World Cup for England, instead delivered what some pundits have called "The Greatest English Horror." The team labored through the group stage before scraping past a lower-ranked opponent in the round of 16.

Alan Shearer, writing for the BBC, argued that Tuchel's time is not up despite the failure. “Tuchel did not deliver for England, but his time is not up,” Shearer wrote. The sentiment reflects a belief that the German coach deserves a full cycle to implement his system. Others, however, are less forgiving. The Times of India described the exit as a crushing indictment of his methods.

Kane, for his part, has not publicly criticized his manager. He has instead focused on the team's collective failure to execute on the night. “We had chances. We didn't take them. Argentina did,” he said.

Messi's Numbers Speak for Themselves

Messi's statistics at this World Cup have been otherworldly. According to data compiled by ESPN, his tournament numbers stack favorably against the all-time greats: 33 goals and 25 assists, compared to Mbappé's 33 goals and 25 assists. Against Pelé, he leads 10-4 in a combined metric of goals and assists, and against Maradona, the gap is even wider at 99-71.

These figures, while impressive, also highlight the generational talent Kane is measuring himself against. The England captain knows that matching Messi's longevity will require more than just desire. It will require discipline, adaptation, and a bit of luck.

“I feel good physically. My body feels strong,” Kane said. “I don't see why I can't play at the top level for another four or five years.”

What Comes Next for England

The immediate future for England involves a period of reflection. The failure to win the 2026 World Cup will sting, especially given the talent in the squad. Key players like Jude Bellingham and Bukayo Saka are young enough to lead the next charge, but Kane's role in that transition remains uncertain.

If he stays fit and maintains his form, he will be 37 by the time the 2030 World Cup rolls around. Messi, now 39, has shown that age is just a number. Kane is betting his legacy on that same idea.

For now, the captain is not making any promises. He is simply holding out hope. And he has the best example in the world to follow.
