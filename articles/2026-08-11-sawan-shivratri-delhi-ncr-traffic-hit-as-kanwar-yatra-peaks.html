Sawan Shivratri: Delhi-NCR traffic hit as Kanwar Yatra peaks

📅 2026-08-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Traffic diversions and road closures in Delhi-NCR due to Kanwar Yatra on Sawan Shivratri
  • Commuters face delays on NH-9, EPE, and other key routes as DME shut for the yatra
  • Pilgrim rush and slow traffic reported across the region

Kanwar Yatra disrupts Delhi-NCR traffic on Sawan Shivratri

Delhi-NCR is witnessing significant traffic disruptions today as thousands of Kanwariyas take to the roads for Sawan Shivratri. The annual Kanwar Yatra, which sees devotees carrying holy Ganga water, has prompted authorities to impose diversions and closures across key routes, leaving commuters grappling with long delays.

Reports from the ground indicate that the Delhi-Meerut Expressway (DME) has been shut for the yatra, forcing traffic onto alternative roads. This has led to severe congestion on NH-9 and the Eastern Peripheral Expressway (EPE), where vehicles are crawling at a snail's pace.

Commuters bear the brunt of the pilgrimage rush

The pilgrimage rush has made daily life a challenge for residents. Social media is abuzz with videos and posts showing bumper-to-bumper traffic on major arterial roads. Many commuters, unaware of the diversions, have found themselves stuck in long queues, missing appointments and struggling to reach workplaces.

One commuter, speaking to India Today, described the situation as "chaotic," adding that what usually takes 30 minutes now takes over two hours. The slow traffic has also affected public transport, with buses and autos delayed across the region.

Boombox trucks and noise add to the chaos

The yatra has not only disrupted movement but also amplified noise pollution. According to a Hindustan Times report, at least six trucks equipped with boomboxes passed a single point in 20 minutes, with sound levels peaking at a staggering 110 decibels. The high-decibel music, a hallmark of some Kanwar processions, has added to the discomfort of residents and commuters alike.

Authorities have not yet commented on whether noise norms are being enforced, but the sheer volume of the yatra has made it difficult to regulate.

Road closures and diversions across Delhi-NCR

Traffic police have issued advisories, but the implementation appears patchy. The DME closure has forced vehicles onto narrow service roads, creating bottlenecks. The Times of India reported that the diverted traffic has choked NH-9 and EPE, causing ripple effects on routes leading into Delhi.

Commuters are being advised to plan alternate routes and leave early. However, with the yatra expected to continue through the day, officials have cautioned that delays are likely to persist.

What lies ahead

As Sawan Shivratri celebrations peak, traffic congestion is expected to remain a challenge through the evening. Authorities are likely to review the situation as the day progresses, but for now, patience seems to be the only option for Delhi-NCR commuters. The focus will now shift to how quickly normalcy can be restored after the yatra concludes.
