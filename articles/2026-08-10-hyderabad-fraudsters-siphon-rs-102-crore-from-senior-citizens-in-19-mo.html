Hyderabad fraudsters siphon Rs 102 crore from senior citizens in 19 months

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Fraudsters siphoned Rs 102 crore from senior citizens in Hyderabad over 19 months.
  • Victims were primarily targeted through phone calls, digital payment apps, and fake investment schemes.
  • Police have launched awareness campaigns and a dedicated helpline for senior citizens.
  • Officials urge families to monitor elderly relatives' financial transactions.
  • Investigation ongoing; arrests made in some cases, but recovery remains a challenge.

Systematic Targeting of the Elderly

Hyderabad police have uncovered a massive fraud network that siphoned off Rs 102 crore from senior citizens over a 19-month period. The revelation has sent shockwaves through the city, exposing a well-organized racket that preyed on the elderly's trust and lack of digital literacy.

According to officials, the fraudsters employed a variety of tactics, including impersonating bank officials, threatening legal action, and luring victims with high-return investment schemes. Most of the transactions were executed through digital payment platforms, making it difficult for victims to trace the money.

How the Scams Operated

The scams typically began with a phone call. Posing as representatives from banks, telecom companies, or even law enforcement, the fraudsters created a sense of urgency, pressuring victims to share OTPs or transfer funds to 'safe accounts'. In some cases, fake customer care numbers were advertised online, and when senior citizens called for help, they were connected directly to the fraudsters.

One common tactic involved convincing victims that their KYC needed to be updated immediately, or their accounts would be frozen. Another involved fake lottery winnings, where victims were asked to pay a small 'processing fee' to claim a large sum. The amounts siphoned ranged from a few thousand rupees to several lakhs, with some victims losing their entire life savings.

Police Response and Challenges

Hyderabad police have registered multiple FIRs and formed special teams to investigate the cases. Several arrests have been made, but officials admit that tracing and recovering the siphoned money remains a significant challenge. The funds were often quickly moved through multiple bank accounts and converted into cryptocurrency or used to purchase gold, making them difficult to trace.

Police commissioner's office has urged senior citizens to never share OTPs or passwords with anyone, regardless of the caller's claims. They have also set up a dedicated helpline for elderly citizens to report suspicious calls or transactions. Family members are being asked to play a more active role in educating their elderly relatives about these scams.

Rising Trend Nationwide

This case is not isolated. Across India, cyber fraud targeting senior citizens has been on the rise, with fraudsters exploiting the growing use of digital payments and the isolation of many elderly people. Experts say that the elderly are particularly vulnerable because they are often less familiar with technology and more likely to trust authority figures.

The Hyderabad police's data shows that the average age of victims was over 65, with many living alone. In several cases, the fraudsters built a rapport with their victims over weeks, gaining their trust before striking. This psychological manipulation, combined with technical obfuscation, has made these scams particularly devastating.

What Needs to Be Done

While police are working to crack down on these networks, prevention is key. Banks are being urged to implement stricter verification measures for large transactions and to flag unusual activity in accounts belonging to senior citizens. Telecom regulators are also being called upon to crack down on spoofed calls that display legitimate bank numbers on caller IDs.

Civil society groups and resident welfare associations have started organizing workshops to teach senior citizens how to identify and avoid such scams. These efforts are crucial, as the fraudsters are likely to adapt their methods and continue targeting the vulnerable.

Moving forward, the focus will be on strengthening the cyber crime units, improving inter-state coordination, and creating a more robust system for freezing and recovering funds. For now, authorities are advising everyone to have regular conversations with their elderly family members about these threats, and to report any suspicious activity immediately. The fight against these fraudsters is far from over, but awareness remains the first line of defense.
