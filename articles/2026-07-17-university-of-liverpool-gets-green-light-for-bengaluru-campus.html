University of Liverpool Gets Green Light for Bengaluru Campus

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • University of Liverpool has received a letter of approval to establish a campus in Bengaluru.
  • The move is part of the UK university's strategy to expand its presence in India.
  • The campus will offer undergraduate and postgraduate programs, subject to regulatory clearances.

UK Varsity Clears Key Hurdle for India Expansion

The University of Liverpool has received a formal letter of approval from Indian authorities to open a campus in Bengaluru, marking a significant step in the institution's plans to establish a physical presence in the country. The approval, confirmed by university officials on Saturday, allows the Russell Group university to move forward with setting up a full-fledged campus in India's tech hub.

This development comes as several foreign universities seek to tap into India's growing demand for international education. The University of Liverpool's Bengaluru campus will be its first overseas campus in Asia.

What the Campus Will Offer

The proposed campus is expected to offer a range of undergraduate and postgraduate programs in fields such as engineering, data science, business, and law. The university has indicated that the curriculum will mirror its UK offerings, with a focus on research and industry connections.

Specific details about the campus location, intake capacity, and fee structure have not been disclosed yet. University representatives have stated that these details will be finalised in the coming months, pending further regulatory approvals.

Timeline and Next Steps

The letter of approval is the first major regulatory clearance required for the project. The university now needs to secure additional permissions from state and local authorities before construction can begin.

Officials have not confirmed a timeline for when the campus will become operational. However, sources suggest that the university hopes to admit its first batch of students within the next two to three academic years.

The Bengaluru campus is part of the University of Liverpool's broader strategy to increase its global footprint. The institution already has partnerships with several Indian universities and research organisations.

India's Higher Education Landscape

India has been actively encouraging foreign universities to set up campuses under the National Education Policy 2020. The policy aims to make India a global study destination and reduce the outflow of students going abroad for higher education.

The University of Liverpool joins a growing list of international institutions, including the University of Southampton and Deakin University, that have received approvals to establish campuses in India. Bengaluru, with its large student population and thriving tech ecosystem, has emerged as a preferred location for many such ventures.

The move is expected to provide Indian students with access to world-class education at a lower cost than studying abroad. It could also boost local research capabilities and create new opportunities for academic collaboration.

As the University of Liverpool navigates the remaining regulatory processes, the education sector will be watching closely for updates on course offerings, campus infrastructure, and admission timelines.
