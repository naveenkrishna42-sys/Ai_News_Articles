Bengaluru artist's solo exhibition celebrates the creative journey

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • The exhibition, titled 'It is the Journey that Matters', is now open in Bengaluru.
  • The artist emphasizes process-driven work over final results.
  • Curators say the show encourages viewers to slow down and appreciate artistic effort.

A Shift in Perspective

In a city known for its fast-paced tech culture, a new art exhibition in Bengaluru is asking visitors to pause and reconsider what truly counts in creative work. Titled 'It is the Journey that Matters', the solo show is built around a simple but profound idea: the process of making art is as significant as the finished piece.

The exhibition, curated by a local gallery, brings together works that trace the artist's evolving methods, sketches, and experiments. Rather than presenting only polished canvases, the display includes preliminary studies and unfinished pieces, offering a rare behind-the-scenes look at how ideas take shape.

Process Over Product

For the artist, whose name has not been disclosed in official materials, this show marks a deliberate departure from conventional showcases. The works on display highlight repetitive strokes, colour trials, and even errors that were later corrected. Each piece tells its own story of struggle and discovery.

Art critics in the city have noted that the exhibition arrives at a time when instant gratification often dominates public taste. By foregrounding the labour and thought behind each artwork, the show challenges visitors to slow down and engage with the medium on a deeper level.

A Personal Narrative

The exhibition also weaves in personal elements, with handwritten notes and diary-like annotations accompanying several pieces. These fragments offer glimpses into the artist's daily routine, moments of doubt, and flashes of inspiration. For many viewers, this intimate approach makes the work feel more relatable and human.

Gallery staff say footfall has been steady since the opening, with many visitors spending considerable time reading the accompanying texts. Some have described the experience as meditative, a rare quality in a city where time is often treated as a commodity.

Community Response

Local art enthusiasts have taken to social media to share their favourite pieces, with several noting that the show prompted them to reflect on their own creative habits. One visitor wrote that the exhibition "made me think about how I rush through everything, including art."

Organisers have also scheduled a series of walkthroughs and discussions, where the artist will speak about the challenges of maintaining a process-driven practice in a results-oriented world. These sessions have already drawn significant interest, with tickets for the first two events reportedly sold out.

What Lies Ahead

The exhibition is expected to run for several more weeks, and the gallery has hinted at a possible extension if the current momentum continues. There is also talk of a companion publication, which would compile the artist's notes and sketches into a book format.

As Bengaluru's art scene grows, shows like this one serve as a reminder that not everything needs to be measured by output alone. For those who attend, the takeaway may well be that the journey — with all its detours and dead ends — is where the real value resides.
