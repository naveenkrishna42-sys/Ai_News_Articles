Starmer steps down, Burnham takes over as UK Prime Minister

📅 2026-07-20 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Keir Starmer resigned as UK Prime Minister after tendering his resignation to King Charles III.
  • Andy Burnham has taken over as the new Prime Minister of the United Kingdom.
  • In his first speech, Burnham pledged to end rough sleeping across the country.

London: Keir Starmer has stepped down as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, handing over the reins to his Labour colleague Andy Burnham. The transition, which had been anticipated for months, marks a significant shift in British politics as the country prepares for a new leadership style.

Speaking outside 10 Downing Street, Starmer said, “I go with good grace and a smile on my face.” He described his tenure as one that left the nation “stronger and fairer,” before officially tendering his resignation to King Charles III.

A graceful exit

Starmer’s departure was notably devoid of rancour. He praised Burnham as a capable successor and expressed confidence in the Labour Party’s future direction. “My work is done,” Starmer declared, a phrase that quickly trended on social media.

Political analysts noted that Starmer’s tone was markedly different from the often bitter transitions seen in British politics. He thanked civil servants, his cabinet, and the public for their support during his time in office.

Starmer’s resignation came after weeks of speculation about the timing of his exit. He had previously indicated that he would not seek a second full term, making way for a new generation of leadership.

Burnham’s first address

Andy Burnham, the former Mayor of Greater Manchester, was quick to outline his priorities. In his first speech as Prime Minister, he made a bold pledge: to end rough sleeping in the United Kingdom. “No one should have to sleep on the streets in a country as wealthy as ours,” Burnham said.

The new PM also promised to address the housing crisis, improve the National Health Service, and tackle regional inequality. Burnham’s tenure as mayor was marked by a focus on social justice, and he is expected to bring that same emphasis to Downing Street.

Burnham’s rise to the top job is the culmination of a long political career. He served as Health Secretary under Gordon Brown and later became a prominent voice for the Labour Party’s left wing. His victory in the leadership contest earlier this year set the stage for this transition.

What comes next

Burnham faces a daunting in-tray. The UK continues to grapple with economic challenges, including inflation and public sector strikes. His pledge on rough sleeping will require significant policy changes and funding, though details of his plan have not yet been fully outlined.

On the international stage, Burnham will need to navigate relations with the European Union and the United States. His first foreign trip as PM is expected to be to Brussels, signalling a desire for closer post-Brexit ties.

For now, the focus is on the new cabinet. Burnham is expected to announce his ministerial team in the coming days, with several key figures from the shadow cabinet likely to retain their roles. The opposition, led by the Conservatives, has already begun to scrutinise Burnham’s early promises.

The transition marks a new chapter for British politics. Starmer’s quiet departure and Burnham’s ambitious start set the tone for what could be a transformative period. Whether Burnham delivers on his pledge to end rough sleeping will be one of the defining questions of his premiership.
