Salman Khan and the Industry’s Unseen Safety Net

📅 2026-08-26 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Salman Khan funded and oversaw the medical treatment of his long-time body double, Parvez Kazi.
  • The incident underscores the lack of institutional insurance for ancillary crew in Bollywood.
  • Stunt professionals remain the most vulnerable demographic in film production, often lacking long-term benefits.

The Cost of Stunt Work

The recent reports regarding Salman Khan facilitating medical care for his long-time body double, Parvez Kazi, following heart surgery, serve as a reminder of the personal bonds that often substitute for professional safety nets in Bollywood. While the gesture is being celebrated as a hallmark of Khan’s loyalty, it exposes a systemic issue within the film industry: the reliance on individual philanthropy rather than institutional health coverage.

Stunt performers and body doubles are the backbone of high-octane commercial cinema. Yet, their career longevity is frequently dictated by the whims of the lead actor or the production house. When a star like Khan steps in, the crisis is averted. When they do not, the financial burden of recovery often falls entirely on the worker.

Key Vulnerabilities for Stunt Performers

  • High physical strain leads to premature retirement and chronic health issues.
  • Most stunt workers operate on a per-project basis, lacking standard corporate health insurance.
  • Reliance on 'star patronage' creates an unstable dependency loop for medical emergencies.

The Industry’s Institutional Gap

While production houses have modernized their HR practices, the treatment of 'non-star' staff remains archaic. For many stunt doubles, the job involves high-risk activities that push the body to its limit for decades. Despite this, there is no standardized industry-wide provident fund or robust insurance scheme that covers long-term cardiac or occupational ailments.

Khan’s involvement is not just an act of charity; it is an informal insurance policy. However, this model is unsustainable. Relying on a lead actor’s benevolence means that the health security of a stunt professional is tethered to their relationship with a specific star, rather than their rights as a skilled worker.

Analyzing the 'Star-Patron' Culture

The culture of the 'Masiha' or savior in Bollywood often masks the absence of labor unions that can effectively bargain for basic protections. When a high-profile actor pays for surgery, the media narrative focuses on the generosity of the star. This distracts from the deeper question: why is such an intervention necessary in the first place? If the industry wants to claim it has matured into a global corporate entity, it must transition from individual charity to collective social security.

For Parvez Kazi, the support provided by Khan is undoubtedly a lifeline. Yet, for thousands of others who lack a high-profile mentor, a medical emergency of this magnitude remains a career-ending—and often life-ruining—event. True progress in the industry will not be measured by the generosity of one star, but by the implementation of a comprehensive welfare board for stunt and technical crew.
