AI boom shifts capital—Korea and Taiwan lead, Nifty trails

Markets across Asia are responding to the same underlying driver—AI-related capex expectations—but not all indices are reacting equally. Korea and Taiwan have seen sharper upside as investors price stronger demand cycles for semiconductors, foundry capacity, networking gear, and the supporting supply chain that powers AI compute.

Against that backdrop, India’s Nifty has been lagging global peers. That gap is rarely about “bad news only for India”; it usually reflects differences in sector composition, index weights, valuation starting points, and how quickly earnings revisions are arriving.

Business Standard’s report notes the divergence while pointing to the AI boom as the catalyst behind the surge in Korea and Taiwan. For Indian investors, the practical question is what this relative underperformance signals for portfolio construction over the next quarter, not just what headline percentage moves look like today.

Why AI optimism hits Korea & Taiwan harder than India

In Korea and Taiwan, AI exposure is often more direct and measurable. When AI demand rises, it quickly translates into utilization rates, pricing power, and order visibility for high-end chips, packaging capacity, and equipment. That creates a clearer earnings channel that markets can model—leading to faster valuation re-rating.

India’s market linkage to the AI cycle is real but typically more indirect. Many Indian earnings streams tied to AI—cloud consumption, IT services, data modernization—can benefit, yet timing is influenced by enterprise budgeting cycles, export orders, and currency movements. Meanwhile, if the Nifty is led by sectors whose earnings revisions are not accelerating in parallel, relative performance can lag even when the macro tone is supportive.

Another factor: foreign flows. When global investors rotate into “AI supply chain” exposure, they often gravitate toward indices with the highest immediate concentration in semiconductors and related hardware. That structural flow pattern can widen performance gaps.

What Nifty underperformance could mean for investors

Relative lag can be interpreted in multiple ways. A prolonged underperformance may mean India’s earnings momentum is not keeping pace with AI-linked global revisions. It can also mean the market is already pricing more optimistic scenarios locally, leaving less incremental upside compared with peers where valuations have more room to expand.

For investors, the key is to separate index movement from portfolio risk. If your exposure is primarily domestic large caps, you may experience muted returns even during a global AI upswing. Conversely, if you hold global or India-adjacent AI beneficiaries—such as export-heavy tech services—your performance may be more aligned with the AI cycle, but with additional currency and global-demand sensitivity.

A smart approach is to watch breadth and earnings revisions rather than chasing momentum. In periods when one region leads, it often takes 1–3 earnings cycles for the “catch-up” narrative to become evidence-backed.

Buyer guide: portfolio positioning benchmarks during AI-driven divergence

Think of this market moment as a “cycle test.” AI booms can drive strong upside, but they also introduce volatility when supply-demand expectations swing or when interest rates/FX move against risk assets. Rather than adding exposure purely on index performance, set practical benchmarks: what you expect from earnings, what drawdown you can tolerate, and which risks you are actually buying (growth, quality, or valuation expansion).

Key real-world highlights to watch next

  • If Korea/Taiwan strength is driven by earnings revisions, markets often sustain momentum until guidance disappoints.
  • Nifty’s lag may narrow if domestic earnings breadth improves, especially in sectors with improving order books or margins.
  • AI-driven rallies can front-run results; confirm through guidance, utilization, and capex commentary rather than price alone.
  • FX and rates can dominate returns for Indian investors; even “good” equity stories can underperform if global conditions tighten.

Pros and cons of the current divergence

  • Pros: AI optimism can support global risk appetite, lifting related earnings expectations and increasing long-term growth visibility.
  • Pros: Relative lag in Nifty can create valuation opportunities if it reflects slower repricing rather than deteriorating fundamentals.
  • Cons: Chasing outperforming markets (Korea/Taiwan) without confirming earnings can lead to whipsaw returns when sentiment cools.
  • Cons: India-specific lag may persist if domestic sector leadership and earnings revisions remain uneven.
  • Cons: Portfolio outcomes depend on concentration and FX exposure—divergence can be an investment risk signal, not just a timing curiosity.

Bottom line for market participants

The AI boom is delivering a tangible “winners-first” effect in markets where semiconductor and AI infrastructure exposure is concentrated. Korea and Taiwan’s surge reflects that clearer transmission mechanism from AI demand to earnings models. Nifty’s lag suggests either a sectoral mismatch, slower earnings repricing, or valuations that leave less incremental upside in the near term.

For investors, the actionable next step is to verify whether the divergence is fundamentals-driven. Use earnings revisions, valuation context, and FX/rates sensitivity as benchmarks—then adjust exposure with discipline rather than momentum.
