Trump Delayed Iran Operation Over Munitions Shortage, Report Says

📅 2026-07-26 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Report says Trump shelved major Iran operation due to low ammunition supplies.
  • Advisers had raised concerns about escalation risks and readiness.
  • Iran has paused strikes as long as US bombing halt continues.
  • Trump administration publicly cited diplomatic space for talks.

Former US President Donald Trump reportedly put a major military operation against Iran on hold because of a critical shortage of munitions, according to a new report. The revelation contradicts the administration's public explanation that the pause was intended to create room for diplomatic talks.

Munitions Shortage Cited as Key Factor

The report, published by multiple outlets, suggests that Trump's military advisors warned him that the US did not have enough precision-guided munitions to sustain a large-scale campaign against Iranian targets. The shortage forced a last-minute recalculation of the planned escalation.

Officials familiar with the discussions said the Pentagon was concerned that a prolonged conflict would quickly deplete stocks needed for other global commitments. The White House had been weighing a series of strikes in response to what it described as Iranian provocations in the Persian Gulf.

Advisers Raised Escalation Risks

Senior aides had also cautioned Trump that a direct military confrontation with Iran could spiral into a broader war. The New York Times reported that internal briefings highlighted the danger of Iranian retaliation against US assets and allies in the region.

Trump, known for his aggressive posture toward Tehran, reportedly bristled at the constraints but ultimately agreed to delay the operation. The decision marked a rare instance where logistical and strategic concerns overrode his instinct for a forceful response.

Tehran’s Conditional Pause

Iran, for its part, has signaled a willingness to de-escalate. According to Reuters, Iranian officials said they would halt retaliatory strikes as long as the US maintains its bombing pause. The Hindu confirmed that Iran has stopped what it called 'retaliatory strikes' after a lull in American attacks.

The US ambassador to the United Nations stated that the pause was meant to make space for negotiations, a framing that now appears at odds with the reported munitions crisis. Critics say the administration's public messaging was misleading.

What the Administration Said

At the time, Trump himself described the halt as a tactical move. 'We are holding fire to see if they are serious about talking,' he told reporters. No senior official publicly mentioned supply shortages as a factor.

Pentagon spokespeople declined to comment on the report, citing operational security. However, defense analysts note that the US military has repeatedly warned about the need to modernize its munitions stockpiles amid concurrent operations in Ukraine and the Indo-Pacific.

The full extent of the planned operation remains unclear. Neither the White House nor the Department of Defense has released a timeline or target list for the shelved mission.

What to watch: Whether the munitions shortfall re-emerges as a political liability for Trump, especially as he campaigns on a platform of military strength. Allies and adversaries alike will be watching to see if the US can rebuild its arsenal before the next crisis.
