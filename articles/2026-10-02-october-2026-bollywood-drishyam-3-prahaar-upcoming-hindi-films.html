October 2026 Bollywood Slate: What to Expect

As the festive season winds down, Bollywood traditionally rolls out a mix of sequels, star‑driven action and family‑oriented entertainers to capture post‑holiday audiences. Trade reports for October 2026 point to a slate headlined by the much‑anticipated third instalment of the Drishyam franchise and the action‑film Prahaar, alongside several other Hindi releases aiming for theatrical windows in the first two weeks of the month.

Major Tentative Releases

Drishyam 3

The third chapter in the Drishyam series is reported to bring back Ajay Devgn as Vijay Salgaonkar, with Tabu reprising her role as the determined IG Meera Deshmukh. Director Abhishek Pathak, who helmed the second instalment, is said to be returning to steer the narrative, which promises to deepen the moral dilemma at the heart of the story. While an exact release date has not been confirmed, industry sources suggest a mid‑October launch to avoid clashing with other major tentpoles.

Prahaar

Prahaar is described as an action‑drama centred on a covert operation, with a leading action star reportedly attached to the project. Details about the cast and director remain under wraps, but trade insiders indicate a high‑octane screenplay designed to appeal to fans of the genre. The film is slated for a late‑October release, positioning it to benefit from the post‑Diwali audience surge.

Other Notable Titles

Besides the two headliners, the October 2026 lineup reportedly includes a family‑entertainer starring a popular comic duo, a romantic thriller featuring a fresh pair of leads, and a biopic on a historical figure. These projects are said to be in various stages of post‑production, with their release dates subject to change based on censor certification and theatre availability.

Genre Trends and Audience Preferences

Analysts note a clear tilt toward sequels and franchise extensions in the October window, leveraging established audience goodwill. At the same time, there is a modest push for mid‑budget thrillers and socially relevant dramas that aim to differentiate themselves from the blockbuster fare. This mix reflects exhibitors’ desire to offer varied programming across multiplexes and single‑screen theatres during the post‑festive period.
