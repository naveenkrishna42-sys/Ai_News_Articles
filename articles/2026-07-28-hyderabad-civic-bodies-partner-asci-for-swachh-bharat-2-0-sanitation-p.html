Hyderabad Civic Bodies Partner ASCI for Swachh Bharat 2.0 Sanitation Push

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) and other civic bodies partner with Administrative Staff College of India (ASCI) for Swachh Bharat Mission 2.0.
  • ASCI to provide technical assistance, monitoring, and capacity building for sanitation workers.
  • Focus on improving waste segregation, processing, and reducing landfill dependency.

New Collaboration for Cleaner Hyderabad

Hyderabad's civic bodies are taking a structured approach to sanitation under the Swachh Bharat Mission 2.0. The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) and other local bodies have roped in the Administrative Staff College of India (ASCI) to strengthen waste management efforts.

The partnership aims to bring technical expertise and systematic monitoring to the city's cleanliness drive. Officials say this move is designed to address long-standing challenges in solid waste management.

ASCI's Role in Sanitation

ASCI will provide technical assistance and capacity-building programs for sanitation workers. The focus will be on improving waste segregation at source, streamlining collection routes, and enhancing processing facilities.

A senior GHMC official said the collaboration will help in assessing current gaps and implementing data-driven solutions. The goal is to reduce the amount of waste sent to landfills and increase recycling rates.

ASCI will also conduct training sessions for municipal staff and sanitation workers. These sessions will cover best practices in waste handling, use of protective gear, and efficient use of resources.

Monitoring and Accountability

Under the agreement, ASCI will set up a monitoring framework to track progress. This includes regular audits of waste processing plants and collection systems. The data collected will be used to make real-time adjustments to operations.

Civic bodies hope this will bring greater accountability to the sanitation workforce. Officials believe that with ASCI's expertise, the city can achieve its Swachh Bharat Mission 2.0 targets more effectively.

Challenges Ahead

Hyderabad generates over 5,000 tonnes of solid waste daily. Segregation remains a major hurdle, with mixed waste complicating processing. The new partnership aims to tackle this through community awareness drives and better infrastructure.

Residents have often complained about irregular waste collection and overflowing bins. The civic bodies say the partnership will help address these grievances by improving route planning and monitoring.

Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for visible changes. They noted that the initiative is still in its early stages and will require sustained effort.

What to Watch

The success of this collaboration will depend on how quickly training programs are rolled out and whether monitoring systems lead to tangible improvements. Over the coming months, the focus will be on pilot projects in select wards before expanding city-wide.
