India debt fundraising hits $3 billion rush after RBI move

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Indian firms raised $3 billion in debt over two weeks after RBI policy easing
  • Yields on top-rated corporate bonds fell by 30-40 basis points
  • Bankers expect further fundraising as borrowing costs remain attractive

Debt market springs to life

Indian companies have rushed to raise $3 billion through debt issuances in the past two weeks, bankers said on Thursday, after a series of Reserve Bank of India moves pushed bond yields sharply lower.

The fundraising spree, which includes both rupee-denominated bonds and foreign currency debt, marks a sharp turnaround from the cautious stance that prevailed earlier this year. Bankers said the pace of issuances is the fastest in any two-week period in recent memory.

Why yields fell

The RBI cut its key repo rate by 50 basis points last month and followed it up with measures to inject liquidity into the banking system. The actions drove yields on top-rated corporate bonds down by 30-40 basis points, making borrowing cheaper for companies.

State-owned banks and non-banking financial companies have been among the most active issuers, taking advantage of lower rates to refinance existing debt and fund new projects. At least a dozen firms have tapped the market, bankers said, with many more expected in the coming weeks.

What companies are doing

Proceeds from the debt sales are being used for a mix of purposes, including capital expenditure, working capital needs, and repayment of previous high-cost borrowings. Several firms are also looking to lock in low rates for longer tenors, bankers added.

One banker, who declined to be named, said the rush is also being driven by a fear that yields may rise again once the market fully absorbs the impact of the RBI's actions. "Companies want to strike while the iron is hot," the banker said.

Foreign investors join in

Foreign portfolio investors have also increased their purchases of Indian corporate bonds in recent weeks, attracted by the relatively high yields compared with developed markets. Data from clearing houses shows foreign holdings of rupee-denominated corporate debt have risen by nearly $1 billion since the RBI's policy announcement.

The trend is expected to continue, especially if the RBI delivers further rate cuts in its next policy review. Analysts are split on whether the central bank will act again, but most agree that the current low-yield environment is likely to persist for at least a few months.

What lies ahead

Bankers expect the fundraising momentum to continue, with another $2-3 billion in issuances likely in the next two to three weeks. The pipeline includes bonds from infrastructure companies, housing finance firms, and renewable energy developers.

However, some caution that the pace could slow if global interest rates rise or if domestic inflation picks up unexpectedly. For now, though, the debt market is enjoying a rare burst of activity that few had predicted at the start of the year.
