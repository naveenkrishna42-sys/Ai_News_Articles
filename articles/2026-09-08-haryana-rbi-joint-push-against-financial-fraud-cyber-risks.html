Haryana-RBI Joint Push Against Financial Fraud & Cyber Risks

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-08 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Haryana government partners with RBI to curb financial frauds and cyber threats through coordinated audits.
  • Banks must implement real-time transaction alerts and enhanced KYC norms for high-risk accounts.
  • Citizens urged to report suspicious activity via dedicated helplines and digital platforms.

Coordinated Crackdown on Financial Crime

The Haryana government has joined forces with the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to strengthen the fight against financial frauds and cyber threats. This collaboration comes in response to a sharp rise in digital payment frauds, identity theft, and phishing attacks targeting residents and businesses across the state.

The joint initiative focuses on real-time monitoring, strict compliance audits, and proactive citizen awareness. Both bodies have directed banks and financial institutions operating in Haryana to enhance their fraud detection systems and report anomalies within 24 hours.

This move signals a decisive shift from reactive to preventive cybersecurity, particularly as digital transactions in the state surged past 120 crore in the last financial year.

Mandatory Real-Time Alerts and Enhanced KYC

Under the new framework, all scheduled commercial banks must enable real-time SMS and app-based alerts for transactions exceeding ₹5,000. High-risk accounts flagged for suspicious activity will undergo enhanced due diligence, including video-based KYC verification and source-of-funds validation.

The RBI has also mandated quarterly penetration testing for payment gateways and core banking systems. Non-compliant institutions face penalties ranging from ₹1 lakh to ₹1 crore, depending on the severity of lapses.

Citizen Awareness and Reporting Mechanisms

To empower residents, the Haryana government has launched a multilingual awareness campaign covering rural and urban areas. Citizens are encouraged to report suspicious messages, calls, or transactions through the state’s cybercrime helpline 1930 and the national fraud reporting portal cybercrime.gov.in.

Banks are required to display QR codes at branches linking to complaint portals and fraud education resources. The RBI is also integrating its ‘Sahayata’ chatbot into mobile apps to assist users in reporting incidents instantly.

  • Proactive Monitoring: Real-time fraud detection systems now mandatory for all banks.
  • Swift Penalties: Non-compliance attracts heavy fines, incentivizing stronger security.
  • User Empowerment: Multilingual campaigns and helplines improve public participation.
  • Technology Gaps: Smaller cooperative banks may struggle with compliance costs.
  • Over-Surveillance Concerns: Enhanced KYC may inconvenience low-risk customers.

Looking Ahead: Sustained Vigilance Required

The Haryana-RBI partnership sets a precedent for state-level coordination in financial cybersecurity. However, long-term success hinges on continuous training, technology upgrades, and sustained citizen engagement. As digital adoption grows, so too must the resilience of India’s financial infrastructure.

Authorities are expected to review the effectiveness of these measures through a joint committee meeting scheduled for early 2027, with potential recommendations for nationwide implementation.
