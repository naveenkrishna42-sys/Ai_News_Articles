India’s luxury retail boom: New stores, brands and changing habits

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Luxury brands are opening standalone stores in Indian metros and tier-2 cities
  • Rising disposable incomes and digital influence are fueling demand
  • Domestic luxury labels are also gaining traction

Stores opening across metros and beyond

Luxury retail in India is expanding faster than ever. International brands that once relied on multi-brand outlets or shop-in-shop formats are now investing in standalone flagship stores. In Mumbai, Delhi, and Bengaluru, new outlets for names like Louis Vuitton, Gucci, and Dior have opened in the past year. But the growth is not limited to the top three cities.

Brands are also setting up shop in Pune, Ahmedabad, and Chandigarh. A senior executive from a global luxury group told Luxebook India that these cities now show demand levels that match metros from five years ago. The shift is driven by a growing base of high-net-worth individuals and young professionals with disposable income.

What is driving the surge

Industry insiders point to a mix of factors. Rising household incomes and a booming stock market have created new wealth. At the same time, social media and celebrity endorsements have made luxury aspirational for a wider audience. Many consumers now see branded goods as an investment rather than just an expense.

Another key trend is the rise of the Indian luxury consumer who travels frequently. Exposure to global shopping experiences has raised expectations for service and product range. Brands are responding by offering India-specific collections and tailoring store designs to local tastes. Some have introduced smaller format stores in high-footfall malls to test new markets.

Domestic labels gain ground

Indian luxury brands are also benefiting from the boom. Designers like Sabyasachi, Tarun Tahiliani, and Anamika Khanna have opened flagship stores in major cities. Their appeal lies in blending traditional craftsmanship with modern aesthetics. These brands are attracting not just local buyers but also the Indian diaspora and foreign tourists.

E-commerce platforms dedicated to luxury are helping smaller Indian labels reach customers beyond their home cities. However, offline stores remain crucial for high-ticket purchases. Customers want to see, touch, and try before they buy. This has led to a hybrid model where brands use online channels for discovery and physical stores for conversion.

Challenges remain

Despite the optimism, the luxury retail sector in India faces hurdles. High import duties and Goods and Services Tax (GST) make products more expensive than in markets like Dubai or Singapore. Real estate costs in prime locations are steep, and finding skilled staff who understand luxury service standards is not easy.

Brands also have to navigate complex regulations around foreign direct investment. Single-brand retail allows 100 percent FDI, but many global companies still prefer joint ventures to manage local compliance. Supply chain issues, especially for perishable or made-to-order items, add another layer of complexity.

The market is not immune to global economic headwinds. A slowdown in Europe or the US could affect consumer sentiment in India too. But for now, the momentum is strong. Industry estimates suggest the Indian luxury market could grow at a compound annual rate of 10-12 percent over the next five years.

What to watch next

All eyes are on the upcoming festive season, which typically drives a spike in luxury sales. Brands are expected to launch exclusive collections and host invite-only events to woo high-spenders. The performance of new stores in tier-2 cities will be a key indicator of whether the boom has staying power. If these outlets perform well, more international brands will likely follow suit in 2027.
