Two arrested in Guwahati for painting Sonam Wangchuk mural on public wall

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two individuals arrested for painting Sonam Wangchuk mural on a public wall in Guwahati
  • Police say action taken for defacing public property without permission
  • Murals appear amid Wangchuk's hunger strike over climate and education issues in Ladakh

Guwahati police have arrested two people for painting murals of climate activist Sonam Wangchuk on a public wall in the city. The arrests were made on Friday, drawing attention to a growing trend of street art supporting the Ladakh-based activist's ongoing hunger strike.

The two individuals, whose identities have not been officially disclosed, were taken into custody after local authorities spotted the murals on a wall near a busy intersection. Police officials said the action was taken under provisions related to defacing public property.

Police action and legal grounds

According to a senior police officer, the arrests were made after a complaint was lodged by the Guwahati Municipal Corporation. The murals were painted without prior permission on a government-owned wall.

"Painting on public property without authorisation is a violation of municipal laws. We have registered a case and the two individuals are being questioned," the officer said, requesting anonymity. He added that the police are also investigating whether the act was part of a coordinated campaign.

Legal experts say that under Indian law, defacing public property can attract a jail term of up to two years or a fine, or both. However, such cases often result in fines or community service, especially when first-time offenders are involved.

Sonam Wangchuk's hunger strike and support

The murals come at a time when Wangchuk is on a hunger strike in Delhi, demanding action on climate change and better educational opportunities for students in Ladakh. The 55-year-old engineer and education reformer began his fast earlier this month, drawing support from various quarters.

College students in Guwahati were reportedly behind the murals. Some of them told local media that they painted Wangchuk's portrait to show solidarity with his cause. "He is fighting for our future. We wanted to express our support in our own way," a student said, speaking on condition of anonymity.

The mural depicted Wangchuk's face with a message backing his demands. It was quickly removed by municipal authorities after the arrests.

Broader scrutiny by police

The arrests have prompted police to scrutinise other public walls in the city for similar unauthorised artwork. Officials said they are on the lookout for more such murals that may have appeared overnight.

"We are checking other areas as well. If we find more such paintings, action will be taken accordingly," the police officer said. This has raised concerns among local artists and activists who say the move could stifle peaceful expression.

Human rights groups in Assam have urged the police to handle the matter with restraint. They argue that while defacing public property is illegal, the intent behind the murals should be considered.

Previous incidents of mural-related arrests

This is not the first time that street art has led to police action in Guwahati. In 2024, three people were arrested for painting murals of a slain student leader. Those cases were later settled after the accused paid fines.

However, the current incident has a political edge, given Wangchuk's activism. The activist has been a vocal critic of the central government's policies on climate and education in Ladakh. His hunger strike has drawn support from opposition parties and civil society groups.

Guwahati, the largest city in the Northeast, has seen a rise in street art in recent years. While some murals are commissioned by the municipal corporation, unauthorised paintings remain a contentious issue.

What happens next depends on the outcome of the police investigation. The two arrested individuals are likely to be produced before a magistrate, who will decide on bail. Meanwhile, Wangchuk's hunger strike continues, and his supporters say more such acts of solidarity cannot be ruled out.
