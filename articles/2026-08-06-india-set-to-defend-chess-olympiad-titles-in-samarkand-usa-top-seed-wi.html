India Set to Defend Chess Olympiad Titles in Samarkand; USA Top Seed Without Nakamura

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Team line-ups confirmed for the 46th FIDE Chess Olympiad in Samarkand.
  • USA are the top seeds but will be without star player Hikaru Nakamura.
  • India aims to defend both the open and women's titles won in 2024.
  • Indian veteran Koneru Humpy is eager to make an impression in the title defence.

India's Double-Gold Mission in Samarkand

The 46th FIDE Chess Olympiad in Samarkand is set to begin, with India carrying the weight of a historic double-gold defence. The open and women's teams topped the standings in the previous edition, and the confirmed line-ups show a blend of youth and experience.

India's open team will rely on the core that delivered in Budapest, while the women's squad features seasoned campaigners alongside rising talents. The Chess Federation of India has named its strongest possible roster, signalling clear intent to replicate the success.

Veteran Koneru Humpy, a key figure in the women's team, has expressed her determination to make an impact. She knows the pressure of defending a title but views it as an opportunity rather than a burden.

USA's Nakamura Absence Shakes Up Seeding

The United States have been placed as the top seeds, yet their squad will be without Hikaru Nakamura. The absence of the world's top-ranked blitz player and a consistent Olympiad performer is a significant blow to their chances.

Despite this, the American team remains formidable, with a lineup that includes several 2700+ rated players. Their top-seed status reflects the depth of talent available, but questions linger over how they will cope without Nakamura's attacking flair in crucial matches.

Nakamura's decision to skip the event has not been officially explained, though speculation has been rife. Neither the player nor the US Chess Federation has issued a detailed statement, leaving fans to wonder what might have been.

Humpy's Hunger for Glory

Koneru Humpy, now in the latter stage of her career, remains a vital cog in India's women's team. She has spoken about the special feeling of competing in an Olympiad and the added motivation that comes with being defending champions.

Her experience will be crucial in guiding younger teammates through the pressure of a nine-round marathon. Humpy's calm demeanour and tactical sharpness make her a player opponents fear facing.

The women's team, led by captain and former world champion, will look to build on their recent momentum. India's depth in women's chess has grown considerably, and they are no longer seen as underdogs but as genuine favourites.

Samarkand Set for Chess Spectacle

Samarkand, the ancient Silk Road city, provides a stunning backdrop for the world's biggest team chess event. Over 190 countries are expected to participate, making it one of the largest Olympiads in history.

The venue's state-of-the-art facilities and the city's rich cultural heritage promise a memorable experience for players and fans alike. Organisers have prepared extensive logistical arrangements to handle the influx of teams, media, and spectators.

For India, the tournament is more than just a defence of titles; it is a statement of their emergence as a chess superpower. The federation has invested heavily in grassroots development, and the results are showing at the highest level.

Key Matchups to Watch

India's open team will face stiff competition from the USA, China, and Russia (competing under a neutral flag). The clash with the Americans could well decide the gold medal, provided both teams navigate the early rounds without slip-ups.

The women's section is equally competitive, with China and Ukraine posing serious threats to India's supremacy. Humpy's form and the consistency of the younger players will be decisive in the closing rounds.

With ratings and rankings so close at the top, tiebreaks could play a crucial role. Every board point will matter, and captains will need to make astute decisions on rest days and pairings.

As the first round approaches, the chess world waits to see if India can etch their name in history once more. The opening ceremony is scheduled for the day before the games begin, setting the stage for two weeks of intense battles.
