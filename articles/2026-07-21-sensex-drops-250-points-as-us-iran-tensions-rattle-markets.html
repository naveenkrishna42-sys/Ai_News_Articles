Sensex Drops 250 Points as US-Iran Tensions Rattle Markets

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 250 points in early trade, Nifty dropped below 24,200
  • HDFC Bank was among the top losers dragging the indices down
  • Media and realty stocks bucked the trend, showing gains despite the broader selloff
  • US-Iran tensions spooked global markets, impacting Indian equities

Markets Open in the Red

Indian equity benchmarks started Tuesday's session on a weak note, with the BSE Sensex falling over 250 points and the NSE Nifty slipping below the 24,200 mark. The selloff was driven by escalating geopolitical tensions between the United States and Iran, which weighed on investor sentiment globally.

The Sensex was trading near 79,450 levels, while the Nifty hovered around 24,180. Broader market indices also faced pressure, though the decline was broad-based rather than panic-driven.

HDFC Bank Leads the Losers

HDFC Bank emerged as one of the top drags on the indices, with its shares falling sharply in early trade. The banking heavyweight's decline contributed significantly to the Sensex's fall, as financial stocks generally struggled amid the risk-off mood.

Other banking and financial stocks also traded lower, though losses in the sector were not as severe. The Nifty Bank index was down in line with the broader market, reflecting cautious trading.

Sectoral Divergence: Media, Realty Shine

Despite the overall weakness, some sectors managed to buck the trend. Media stocks were among the top gainers, with the Nifty Media index rising notably. Realty stocks also held their ground, showing resilience even as most other sectors traded in the red.

Analysts attributed this divergence to stock-specific buying and a rotation of funds from beaten-down sectors. The volatility index, or VIX, was down, suggesting that the market was not in a state of extreme fear.

Global Cues Weigh on Sentiment

The US-Iran tensions were the primary catalyst for the selloff, as reports of heightened military posturing in the Middle East rattled investors. Crude oil prices edged higher on the news, adding to concerns about inflationary pressures and their impact on central bank policies.

Asian markets also traded lower, with Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng both declining. The negative global cues left little room for Indian markets to recover, though domestic fundamentals remained intact.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the previous session, adding to the selling pressure. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) provided some support, but not enough to stem the decline.

What to Watch

Traders will now focus on any further developments in the US-Iran situation and their impact on crude oil prices. The market is likely to remain volatile in the near term, with the Nifty's 24,000 level acting as a key support. Earnings reports from major companies due later this week could also influence direction.
