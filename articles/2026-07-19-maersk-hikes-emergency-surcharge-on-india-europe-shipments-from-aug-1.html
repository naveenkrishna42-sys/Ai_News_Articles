Maersk hikes emergency surcharge on India-Europe shipments from Aug 1

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Maersk will increase the emergency contingency surcharge on shipments from India to Europe effective August 1.
  • The surcharge applies to all container types on the India-Europe trade lane.
  • Rival MSC has also announced revised freight rates for India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh-Europe routes.
  • The surcharges come amid ongoing supply chain disruptions and rising operational expenses.
  • Maersk has also revised peak season surcharges across Asia, India and US trades.

New surcharge on India-Europe trade lane

Danish shipping giant Maersk has announced an increase in the emergency contingency surcharge on cargo moving from India to Europe. The revised charge will take effect from August 1, 2026.

The company cited persistent operational cost pressures and ongoing supply chain disruptions as the reasons for the hike. The surcharge applies to all container types on the India-Europe corridor.

Maersk has not yet specified the exact quantum of the increase. Industry sources expect the revision to be in line with recent market trends.

MSC follows with rate revision

Rival Mediterranean Shipping Company (MSC) has also announced new freight rates for container shipments from India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh to Europe. The move suggests a broader industry trend of rate adjustments on the key trade route.

Both carriers are responding to a volatile freight market marked by fluctuating demand and higher vessel operating costs. The Europe-India trade has seen significant volume growth over the past year.

Broader surcharge revisions by Maersk

Maersk has simultaneously revised its peak season surcharges across multiple trades involving Asia, India and the United States. The company has also adjusted contingency surcharges for Oceania–Middle East shipments.

These changes reflect the carrier's strategy to pass on rising costs to shippers while maintaining service reliability. The shipping industry continues to face challenges from port congestion and equipment shortages.

Impact on Indian exporters

Indian exporters shipping goods to Europe will face higher freight costs from next month. The surcharge hike could squeeze margins for sectors such as textiles, pharmaceuticals and engineering goods that rely on Europe as a key market.

Trade bodies have urged the government to engage with shipping lines to ensure competitive freight rates. Many exporters had already been grappling with elevated logistics costs.

What to watch

Other major carriers are expected to announce similar surcharge revisions in the coming weeks. The effectiveness of these charges in covering operational costs, and any further adjustments, will depend on how supply chain conditions evolve through the peak shipping season.
