MEA distances India from Sheikh Hasina's remarks at press meet

📅 2026-08-07 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • MEA clarifies India was not involved in organising Hasina's press event
  • Bangladesh protests over Hasina's remarks against Tarique Rahman
  • BNP MP proposes merger with Awami League as Hasina vows return

New Delhi, Aug 7: The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) on Friday said the Indian government does not endorse comments made at a press conference addressed by former Bangladesh Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina. The clarification comes amid strong reactions in Dhaka over remarks attributed to her.

Officials reiterated that India was not involved in organising or facilitating the press event. The statement follows protests in Bangladesh over Hasina's comments, particularly those directed at Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP) leader Tarique Rahman.

MEA's Position

The MEA's statement is brief but pointed. It seeks to draw a clear line between India's role as a host and any endorsement of the views expressed. "The Government of India does not endorse the comments made at the press meet," the official readout said.

This is a carefully calibrated response. New Delhi is wary of being seen as interfering in Bangladesh's internal politics, especially at a time when bilateral ties are under strain.

Context of the Press Meet

Sheikh Hasina, who has been in India since her ouster in August 2024, addressed the media recently. Her remarks included a vow to return to Dhaka and a sharp attack on Tarique Rahman, a key figure in the opposition BNP. She also spoke of her personal risk, saying, "I may be arrested. I may be sent to prison… but fear can't decide my duty to the people."

The press conference, held in New Delhi, drew immediate criticism from Dhaka. Bangladeshi officials and media interpreted her statements as provocative, given the ongoing political transition in the country.

Bangladesh's Reaction

In Dhaka, protests erupted following the broadcast of Hasina's remarks. Demonstrators gathered outside diplomatic missions, demanding that India clarify its stance. The MEA's statement is seen as an attempt to defuse the situation.

The timing is sensitive. Bangladesh is preparing for elections, and any perceived foreign interference could inflame domestic tensions. The Indian government's disavowal is aimed at reassuring Dhaka that it is not backing Hasina's political agenda.

Political Fallout

The controversy has also stirred political conversations inside Bangladesh. A BNP MP has reportedly proposed a merger with the Awami League, Hasina's party, as a way to stabilise the country. Meanwhile, Hasina has vowed to return to Dhaka, though no concrete timeline has been announced.

Analysts note that India's non-endorsement may not fully placate critics in Bangladesh. The presence of a former prime minister on Indian soil remains a point of friction. The MEA's statement, however, establishes a formal position that could be referenced in future diplomatic exchanges.

What to Watch

Observers will be watching for any further statements from Dhaka or New Delhi in the coming days. The MEA's clarification may have drawn a line, but the underlying tensions are unlikely to dissipate quickly. The next move could come from Bangladesh's interim government, which may seek a more explicit commitment from India regarding Hasina's activities.
