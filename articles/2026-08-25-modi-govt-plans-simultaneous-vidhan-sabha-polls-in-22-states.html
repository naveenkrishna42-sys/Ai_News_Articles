Modi govt plans simultaneous Vidhan Sabha polls in 22 states

📅 2026-08-25 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government aims to hold simultaneous elections for 22 NDA-ruled states and UTs
  • Critics argue plan is unconstitutional, while supporters see it as a national interest
  • Debate focuses on feasibility, impact on voters, and political implications

Government Proposes Simultaneous State Elections

The Modi government has announced plans to hold Vidhan Sabha elections for 22 NDA-ruled states and Union Territories simultaneously with the 2029 Lok Sabha polls. This proposal, known as 'One Nation, One Election' (ONOE), aims to streamline the electoral process and reduce costs. The government argues that it will save time and resources, allowing voters to exercise their franchise for both central and state governments in one go.

Constitutional and Political Debate

The proposal has sparked a heated debate among legal experts and political analysts. Critics argue that the plan is unconstitutional, as the Constitution of India does not provide for simultaneous elections at the state and national levels. They point out that Article 324 of the Constitution specifically deals with the conduct of elections to the House of the People, and there is no provision for simultaneous state elections.

Supporters of the plan, however, see it as a necessary step to address the challenges posed by the current electoral system. They argue that it will reduce the burden on voters, who often struggle to balance multiple election schedules. They also believe that it will strengthen the democratic process by ensuring that voters have a greater say in the governance of the country.

Feasibility and Impact

The feasibility of the plan is a subject of much discussion. Proponents argue that it is technically possible to conduct simultaneous elections, provided that adequate resources and infrastructure are available. They point to the success of the 2019 Lok Sabha elections, which were held in phases, as evidence of the government's ability to manage large-scale elections.

Critics, however, argue that the plan is not feasible, given the logistical challenges involved. They point out that the Election Commission of India (ECI) would need to coordinate with multiple state election commissions, which could lead to confusion and delays. They also argue that the plan could have a negative impact on voter turnout, as voters might find it difficult to participate in multiple elections at the same time.

Political Implications

The plan has significant political implications. Supporters argue that it will strengthen the government's position, as it will allow the ruling party to consolidate its support base. They also believe that it will reduce the influence of regional parties, which have often been able to influence the outcome of elections through their state governments.

Critics, however, argue that the plan could have the opposite effect. They believe that it could lead to a further centralization of power, as the government would be able to impose its will on the states. They also argue that it could lead to a decline in the quality of state governance, as state governments would be less able to respond to the needs of their constituents.

Conclusion

The 'One Nation, One Election' plan is a significant development in the Indian political landscape. It reflects the government's commitment to reforming the electoral process and strengthening the democratic process. However, it is also a contentious issue, with critics arguing that it is unconstitutional and not feasible. The outcome of the debate will have significant implications for the future of Indian democracy.
