Scotland, Netherlands slam ICC over ODI World Cup format change

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cricket Scotland and KNCB have criticised the ICC's revised ODI World Cup format for 2027.
  • The boards called the changes 'disrespectful' and said they create uncertainty for Associate nations.
  • The ICC has not yet responded to the concerns raised by the two boards.

The International Cricket Council's decision to overhaul the ODI World Cup format for 2027 has drawn sharp criticism from two of the sport's most prominent Associate nations. Cricket Scotland and the Royal Dutch Cricket Association (KNCB) have issued strongly worded statements, accusing the global governing body of sidelining developing cricket nations.

The boards say the new structure, which reportedly reduces the number of teams and alters the qualification pathway, disrupts long-term planning and undermines the growth of the game outside the traditional powerhouses. Both have demanded clarity and a rethink.

'Disrespectful' to Associates

Cricket Scotland's statement did not mince words. The governing body described the format changes as "disrespectful" to nations that have invested heavily in building competitive sides. Scottish officials pointed to their recent performances in global qualifiers as proof that Associate teams can compete at the highest level.

The KNCB echoed those sentiments, with Dutch cricket authorities arguing that the changes effectively close the door on meaningful participation for teams outside the Full Member ranks. Netherlands have been one of the most consistent Associate performers in recent years, including a notable run in the 2023 World Cup.

Uncertainty and Disrupted Planning

Both boards highlighted a practical concern: the lack of a clear, stable qualification pathway makes it nearly impossible for Associate nations to plan budgets, player development programmes, and tour schedules. "This creates uncertainty and disrupts planning," the KNCB statement read, a sentiment that has been echoed by other Associate members in private discussions.

For countries like Scotland and the Netherlands, every World Cup cycle is a multi-year project. Funding is often tied to qualification prospects, and broadcast deals or sponsorship commitments are difficult to secure when the road to the main event keeps shifting. The boards argue that the ICC's decision, made without adequate consultation, leaves them in a lurch.

A Wider Backlash

The criticism is not isolated. Several other Associate nations are believed to share similar frustrations, though few have spoken as publicly as Scotland and the Netherlands. The ICC has faced recurring questions about its commitment to growing the game globally, particularly with cricket's inclusion in the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics adding new urgency to the discussion.

At the heart of the dispute is a familiar tension: the ICC's need to protect the commercial viability of its flagship events versus the demands of fairness and inclusivity. The revised format, according to insiders, is designed to ensure more high-profile matches in the group stage, but critics argue it does so at the expense of the sport's global footprint.

What Lies Ahead

The ICC has not yet issued a formal response to the statements from Cricket Scotland and the KNCB. However, with the 2027 World Cup still more than a year away, there is time for dialogue. Both boards have indicated they will continue to press their case through official channels.

Whether the ICC bends remains to be seen. But the strong language from two of the most respected Associate boards signals a growing restiveness that the global body may not be able to ignore for long.
