Gautam Gambhir Visits Galle, Then Lawn Mower Hits Pitch Ahead of India-SL Warm-Up

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gautam Gambhir inspected the Galle pitch hours before a lawn mower damaged it.
  • India had reportedly complained about too much grass on the practice wicket.
  • Sri Lankan officials defended the surface, calling it standard preparation.
  • India built a separate simulated pitch in Colombo for practice.

Pitch Drama in Galle

Hours after India head coach Gautam Gambhir walked the Galle strip, a lawn mower struck the very surface he had been inspecting. The incident, which took place on Wednesday morning, has added a fresh twist to the build-up ahead of India's warm-up match against Sri Lanka.

The practice pitch at the Galle International Stadium now bears visible damage, though officials have yet to confirm the exact extent. What is known is that the incident occurred soon after Gambhir's visit, during which he reportedly examined the grass cover closely.

Sources indicate the Indian team management had already expressed dissatisfaction with the amount of grass left on the wicket. A Sri Lankan official, responding to the criticism, said the surface was prepared as per standard practice for a warm-up game.

India's Grass Complaints

The Indian camp's unease with the pitch was no secret. Reports suggest the team felt the grass was excessive, potentially making conditions hazardous for batting. The complaint was relayed to match officials, but the response from the Lankan side was firm.

"The Indian team said there's too much grass," the official admitted, while defending the preparation. "This is how we prepare pitches for practice matches. It's not unusual."

The exchange has turned a routine warm-up into a talking point, with both sides digging in over a surface that could influence the series ahead.

Lawn Mower Incident

The lawn mower incident has only intensified the scrutiny. The machine, operated by ground staff, reportedly veered onto the pitch during routine maintenance, leaving fresh cuts on the surface. The timing — just after Gambhir's inspection — has raised eyebrows, though no foul play has been suggested.

Ground authorities have not yet issued a statement on the damage or whether repairs will be attempted before the match. The pitch is unlikely to be replaced, but groundsmen may try to roll the affected areas.

India's Colombo Simulation

Meanwhile, in Colombo, the Indian team has taken matters into its own hands. According to reports, the management arranged for a separate practice pitch — a simulated version of a day-four Galle surface — to be built at their training facility.

To replicate the wear and tear, schoolboys were invited to bowl on the strip before the senior team used it. The unusual step underscores India's intent to prepare for the exact conditions they expect in the Test series, rather than rely on whatever is offered at the venue.

This dual-track approach — complaining about Galle while building a replica elsewhere — shows a side unwilling to leave anything to chance.

What Lies Ahead

With the warm-up match now overshadowed by pitch politics, all eyes will be on how the surface behaves once play begins. The lawn mower's damage could alter the bounce, though officials are expected to assess it closer to the start.

The bigger question is whether the controversy will spill into the Test series. India's preparation in Colombo suggests they are already planning for a turning track, regardless of what Galle offers. For now, both teams will watch the groundsmen closely — and hope the mower stays away.
