US Senate passes Russia sanctions bill with 100% tariff threat for India

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • US Senate clears Russia sanctions bill targeting oil buyers
  • Bill proposes 100% tariffs on India, China and other nations
  • India faces diplomatic and economic pressure over Russian oil imports

The US Senate has cleared a sweeping Russia sanctions bill that could impose 100% tariffs on countries that continue to purchase Russian oil. India, along with China and three other nations, finds itself in the crosshairs of this legislation, which has triggered a political storm back home.

The bill, which now moves to the House of Representatives, is being seen as a direct attempt by former president Donald Trump to leverage the Senate's authority to punish Russia while simultaneously squeezing major economies like India and China. The move has already drawn sharp reactions from Indian political circles, with AAP leader Arvind Kejriwal slamming Prime Minister Narendra Modi over the potential fallout.

What the Bill Proposes

The legislation seeks to impose a 100% tariff on imports from countries that buy Russian oil. The target list includes five nations, with India and China being the most prominent. The idea is to choke off revenue streams that Moscow uses to fund its war efforts in Ukraine.

For India, which has significantly increased its purchase of discounted Russian crude since the Ukraine conflict began, the bill poses a direct economic threat. Indian refiners have benefited from the price discount, but a 100% tariff could erase those gains and disrupt the country's energy security calculus.

Why It Matters for India

India's dependence on imported crude is well over 80%, and Russia has emerged as a top supplier in recent years. The bill, if enacted, could force Indian refiners to either swallow the tariff, pass on the cost to consumers, or scramble for alternative sources in an already tight global market.

The timing is particularly awkward. New Delhi has maintained a delicate balancing act, calling for peace in Ukraine while quietly continuing energy trade with Moscow. This bill threatens to upend that policy and could push India into a corner where it must choose between its traditional Western partnerships and its energy needs.

Political Reactions in India

The bill has already become a domestic political flashpoint. Kejriwal's attack on Modi suggests that the opposition is ready to use the issue to question the government's foreign policy and economic management. The ruling dispensation, for its part, has yet to issue a detailed response, but officials have not confirmed any immediate countermeasures.

What is clear is that this is not just an India-specific problem. The Indian Express has noted that the bill could become a global issue, affecting countries across Asia, Africa and even Europe that depend on Russian energy. The ripple effects could be felt in global fuel prices, shipping costs and supply chains.

What Happens Next

The bill now heads to the US House of Representatives, where its fate is uncertain. Even if it passes there, it would need to be signed into law, and the tariff mechanism would require further rule-making. For now, Indian diplomats are likely to engage in quiet lobbying to protect the country's interests.

The coming weeks will be crucial. Watch for how New Delhi responds officially, whether it seeks exemptions, and how the US House handles the bill. The outcome could redefine India-US relations and reshape global energy trade.
