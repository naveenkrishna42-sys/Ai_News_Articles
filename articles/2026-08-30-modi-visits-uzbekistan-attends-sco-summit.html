A Diplomatic Touchstone in Central Asia

On 29 August 2026, Prime Minister Narendra Modi arrived in Tashkent for a short but symbolically rich visit. The meeting with President Shavkat Mirziyoyev was held against a backdrop of growing Indian interest in Central Asia’s strategic corridors. The warmth of the reception underscored the long‑standing, albeit underexploited, ties between the two nations.

SCO Summit: A Platform for Regional Stability

The Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) summit brought together 12 member states, each grappling with the twin challenges of economic integration and security cooperation. Modi’s presence signaled India’s intent to play an active role in a forum that has often been dominated by China and Russia. The discussions were framed around counter‑terrorism, cyber‑security and the promotion of free trade.

Bilateral Agenda: Trade, Defence, Energy, Culture

In Tashkent, the agenda was deliberately broad. Trade talks focused on easing import‑export barriers, especially in pharmaceuticals, textiles and agricultural produce. Defence cooperation centred on joint exercises and technology transfer platforms. Energy negotiations highlighted the prospect of Uzbek gas pipelines and Indian investment in renewable projects. Cultural exchanges were also on the table, aiming to boost people‑to‑people contact through festivals and academic partnerships.

Strategic Implications: India’s Momentum in the Region

India’s outreach to Uzbekistan reflects a broader pivot towards Central Asia, an area historically seen as the land bridge between Europe and the Indian Ocean. The visit strengthens India’s position in the region’s geopolitical calculus, offering a counterweight to Chinese influence via the Belt and Road Initiative. The emphasis on counter‑terrorism aligns with India’s own internal security concerns, potentially opening avenues for intelligence sharing and joint counter‑terrorism training.

  • Pros: Expanded trade routes, enhanced defence cooperation, increased regional confidence.
  • Cons: Potential friction with China, risk of over‑commitment in a complex security environment.
  • Key Fact: The SCO summit was the first major multilateral meeting attended by an Indian head of government since 2018.

Assessing the Outcomes and Next Steps

While concrete agreements were not publicly signed, the outcomes of the visit lay in the groundwork laid for future collaboration. Subsequent steps are likely to involve detailed memoranda of understanding on trade facilitation, joint defence projects and energy partnerships. The Indian government’s decision to maintain active engagement in Central Asia is expected to yield long‑term dividends, both economically and strategically.
