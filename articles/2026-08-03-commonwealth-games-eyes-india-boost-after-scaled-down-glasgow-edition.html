Commonwealth Games eyes India boost after scaled-down Glasgow edition

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Glasgow hosted a scaled-down 2026 Commonwealth Games, with mended venues and a trimmed sports programme.
  • The Commonwealth Games flag was handed over to India, with Neeraj Chopra and PT Usha receiving it in Glasgow.
  • Ahmedabad is set to host the 2030 Games, seen as a potential Olympic audition for India.

The Commonwealth Games closed its Glasgow edition on a note of frugality, but the movement's gaze has already shifted east. With the flag formally handed over to India, the 2030 Games in Ahmedabad are being framed as a chance to restore the event's grandeur and commercial heft.

Glasgow stepped in as a late replacement host, after the original choice of Victoria, Australia fell through. The Scottish city made do with existing infrastructure, patching up venues and trimming the programme to keep costs down. It was a functional Games, but a far cry from the multi-sport spectacle that once drew global audiences.

A quiet Games, a loud signal

Organisers in Glasgow did not hide the constraints. Athletes competed in a reduced set of sports, and several events were held in temporary or renovated facilities. The approach kept the budget in check, but it also underlined the challenges facing a movement that has lost some of its lustre.

The Commonwealth Games Federation (CGF) has struggled to find hosts in recent cycles. Cities have balked at the expense, and the pandemic further dampened enthusiasm. Glasgow's willingness to host, even in a slimmed-down form, was seen as a stopgap rather than a long-term solution.

India's arrival, then, is a potential turning point. Ahmedabad's bid is backed by substantial state and private investment, and the country's appetite for large-scale sporting events is well documented.

Flag, and a message, for Ahmedabad

The closing ceremony in Glasgow featured a symbolic transfer: the Commonwealth Games flag was presented to India, with Olympic medalist Neeraj Chopra and athletics legend PT Usha on hand to receive it. The moment was brief, but it carried weight.

For Indian officials, the handover is the start of a four-year sprint. The 2030 edition is expected to be the largest yet, with a full programme of sports and a planned emphasis on accessibility and sustainability. Gujarat's minister for sports, Harsh Sanghavi, has said the Games will set new benchmarks in those areas.

The ambition goes beyond the Commonwealth itself. Several Indian sports administrators view the 2030 Games as a dry run for a future Olympic bid. Ahmedabad's infrastructure, including a sprawling new sports complex, is being built with that longer horizon in mind.

An Olympic audition?

India has never hosted the Olympics, and its attempts to secure the Games have repeatedly stalled. The Commonwealth Games offer a more attainable stage to demonstrate organisational capacity.

Ahmedabad's Narendra Modi Stadium, already the world's largest cricket arena, will anchor the event. But the city is also developing new facilities for athletics, aquatics, and indoor sports. The scale of construction is significant, and the CGF will be watching closely.

There are risks. India's track record with major events has been mixed. The 2010 Commonwealth Games in Delhi were marred by delays, corruption allegations, and infrastructure failures. Organisers have promised a different approach this time, with clearer timelines and tighter oversight.

The CGF, for its part, is banking on India's market size and enthusiasm. A successful Ahmedabad Games could revive the event's broadcast appeal and attract new sponsors. It could also silence critics who argue that the Commonwealth Games, without Britain or Australia in the lead, have lost their relevance.

What to watch

The next four years will be a test of execution. India must deliver venues on schedule, manage the logistics of a multi-sport event, and ensure that the promised accessibility and sustainability measures are not just slogans.

The eyes of the sporting world, and of the Olympic movement, will be on Ahmedabad. If India pulls it off, the Commonwealth Games may find a new lease of life. If not, the event's decline could accelerate. For now, the countdown has begun.
