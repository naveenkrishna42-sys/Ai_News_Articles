Sensex plunges 550 pts, Nifty below 24,300 as global cues weigh

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex dropped over 550 points in early trade
  • Nifty slipped below the 24,300 mark
  • Tata group stocks among the major losers

Mumbai: Indian equity benchmarks opened sharply lower on Wednesday, extending losses from the previous session, as global risk sentiment remained fragile. The BSE Sensex plunged over 550 points in early trade, while the NSE Nifty50 slipped below the 24,300 level, tracking weakness across most sectoral indices.

The sell-off was broad-based, with banking, IT, and auto stocks bearing the brunt. Tata group companies were among the prominent losers, though specific stock-wise details were not immediately available.

Global cues weigh on sentiment

Investors remained cautious amid lingering geopolitical tensions, particularly around the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. Any disruption in the region has the potential to spike crude prices, which in turn could stoke inflationary pressures and force central banks to keep interest rates higher for longer.

Asian markets traded mixed, while US futures pointed to a subdued opening. The uncertainty has prompted foreign institutional investors to adopt a wait-and-watch approach, adding to the pressure on domestic equities.

What dragged the indices down

Heavyweight stocks across sectors witnessed selling pressure. Financials, which have a significant weightage in both indices, led the decline. Private lenders and public sector banks both traded in the red, mirroring the broader market mood.

IT stocks also came under pressure amid concerns over a potential slowdown in global tech spending. Auto stocks were not spared either, with manufacturers facing headwinds from rising input costs and muted rural demand.

Market breadth remains negative

The market breadth was firmly negative, with more than three stocks declining for every advancing stock on the BSE. Midcap and smallcap indices also traded lower, though their losses were relatively contained compared to the frontline indices.

Volatility index India VIX edged higher, indicating that traders are bracing for further swings. Analysts suggest that the market could remain choppy in the near term, with the focus shifting to upcoming domestic economic data and global cues.

What to watch next

Investors will closely monitor developments around the Strait of Hormuz and any diplomatic efforts to de-escalate tensions. Additionally, crude oil price movement will be a key determinant of market direction in the coming sessions.

On the domestic front, quarterly earnings from major companies and macroeconomic data points are likely to provide further direction. Traders are advised to stay cautious and avoid taking aggressive positions until clarity emerges on the geopolitical front.
