North Bengaluru Real Estate 2026: Why the North Corridor Still Outperforms

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • North Bengaluru's rental yields of 3.5-4.2% beat the city average of roughly 3%, supported by airport, aerospace, and tech employment clusters.
  • Capital values in Hebbal, Yelahanka, and Devanahalli have stayed flat to marginally positive in 2026, even as several South Bengaluru micro-markets corrected.
  • The 2026 metro and road upgrades are largely priced in; investors buying now are betting on 2027-2030 appreciation, not a short-term flip.

North Bengaluru Holds the Line in a Cooling Market

Bengaluru's residential market entered 2026 in correction mode. Several South Bengaluru micro-markets, including parts of Kanakapura Road and Sarjapur, saw capital values slip as IT hiring slowed and investors trimmed exposure. North Bengaluru did not follow. According to local brokerages and registration data from the sub-registrar offices at Yelahanka and Devanahalli, transaction volumes in the northern corridor fell only marginally, while pricing held flat to marginally positive through the first half of 2026.

The reason is structural, not speculative. North Bengaluru's demand base is anchored by the Kempegowda International Airport, the Aerospace Park at Devanahalli, and a cluster of Global Capability Centres along Bellary Road. These are long-cycle employers that do not shed headcount as quickly as project-based IT services firms.

A 2BHK in Yelahanka that cost roughly ₹62 lakh in 2024 now trades near ₹68-72 lakh, depending on floor, facing, and builder pedigree. Rents in the same corridor rose 8-11% year-on-year, which is why yields in the north remain above the city average of roughly 3%.

Infrastructure Triggers: What Is Priced In and What Is Not

Already Priced In

The airport metro extension, the widening of Bellary Road, and the elevated corridor towards Hebbal are largely reflected in current quotes. Buyers paying ₹16,000 per sq ft in Hebbal are not buying a future metro line; they are buying today's connectivity.

Still to Play Out

The Satellite Town Ring Road (STRR) and the proposed Business Park at Devanahalli remain under construction or in approval stages. Land values within 3-5 km of the STRR alignment have risen sharply, but apartment buyers will see the benefit only after 2028. Investors with a 2-3 year horizon should underwrite these projects cautiously.

Risks Buyers Cannot Ignore

  • Water security: Several northern wards depend on tanker supply during summer. Verify Cauvery connection status and borewell depth before booking.
  • Oversupply in Devanahalli: A large pipeline of 1BHK and 2BHK units aimed at airport staff could cap rental growth if absorption slows.
  • Rental dependence: Yields look attractive, but vacancy in Thanisandra touched 12-14% in some projects during 2025. Factor in two months of vacancy annually.
  • Builder concentration: A handful of developers control most new launches in the corridor. Delayed possession remains the single biggest grievance in consumer forums.

Should You Buy Now?

For end-users with a 5-7 year horizon, North Bengaluru offers better value per rupee than Whitefield or Sarjapur at comparable amenity levels. For investors, the corridor works only if the entry price is below ₹9,000 per sq ft and the project is within 4 km of an operational employment hub. Above ₹12,000 per sq ft, the rental math stops working and the bet becomes purely capital appreciation, which is a different and riskier game.

The corridor is not immune to a broader slowdown, but its employment mix, airport linkage, and relatively affordable entry points give it more cushion than most of Bengaluru. That is the practical meaning of keeping its head above water.
