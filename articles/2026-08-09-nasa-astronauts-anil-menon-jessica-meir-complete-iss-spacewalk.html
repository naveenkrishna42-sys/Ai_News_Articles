NASA Astronauts Anil Menon, Jessica Meir Complete ISS Spacewalk

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • NASA astronauts Anil Menon and Jessica Meir completed a 6.5-hour spacewalk outside the ISS.
  • The spacewalk was to prepare the station for upcoming solar array upgrades.
  • This was Indian-origin astronaut Anil Menon's maiden spacewalk.
  • The upgrades aim to enhance the ISS power systems.

Historic Spacewalk for Indian-Origin Astronaut

NASA astronauts Anil Menon and Jessica Meir returned safely to the International Space Station (ISS) after completing a 6.5-hour spacewalk on Saturday. The primary objective was to prepare the orbital laboratory for a series of solar array upgrades designed to boost its power capabilities.

For Menon, an Indian-origin astronaut, this was a maiden spacewalk. The milestone marks a significant personal achievement and adds to the growing list of Indian-origin professionals contributing to global space exploration.

Upgrading the ISS Power Grid

The spacewalk focused on preliminary work for new solar arrays. These upgrades are critical as the station's original arrays have been in operation for over two decades and are showing signs of age-related degradation.

During the extravehicular activity (EVA), the duo worked meticulously to modify the station's truss structure. This groundwork will facilitate the installation of more efficient solar panels in subsequent missions, ensuring the ISS continues to support a wide range of scientific experiments.

Reactions and Significance

NASA officials have not yet released a detailed post-spacewalk debrief, but early indications suggest all primary tasks were accomplished. The successful completion of this EVA is a testament to the rigorous training both astronauts underwent.

Menon's participation has drawn attention in India, where space enthusiasts have been following his journey closely. His performance underscores the international nature of space exploration and the collaborative spirit that defines ISS operations.

The agency is expected to provide further updates on the condition of the new equipment and the timeline for the next spacewalk. This mission is part of a broader effort to extend the station's operational life through the end of this decade.

What Happens Next

With the prep work done, NASA will now schedule additional spacewalks to physically install the new solar arrays. Astronauts will likely conduct these EVAs over the coming months, and the upgraded power system should come online gradually.

For now, Menon and Meir will resume their other duties aboard the ISS, while engineers on the ground analyze data from Saturday's outing. The next few weeks will be crucial as the station prepares for the arrival of cargo missions carrying the new hardware.
