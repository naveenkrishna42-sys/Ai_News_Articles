Gift Nifty, Oil Surge, Gold Rally: 8 Overnight Shifts for Indian Markets

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gift Nifty indicated a weak start, trading lower by over 100 points.
  • Crude oil prices surged on fears of a widening US-Iran conflict.
  • Gold rates rallied as investors sought safe-haven assets.
  • Asian markets traded mixed, tracking Wall Street's negative close.
  • The US dollar strengthened against major currencies, impacting emerging markets.

Indian stock markets are staring at a volatile start to Tuesday's trading session. A host of global and domestic cues have shifted overnight, from escalating geopolitical tensions to surging commodity prices. Here are the eight key developments that changed the landscape for Dalal Street.

Gift Nifty Signals Weak Start

The Gift Nifty, formerly known as the SGX Nifty, was trading lower by over 100 points early Tuesday. This points to a gap-down opening for the benchmark Nifty50 on the National Stock Exchange.

Investors are bracing for a cautious session as the index tests key support levels. The decline in the futures contract mirrors weak sentiment across global markets.

Oil Prices Surge on US-Iran Tensions

Crude oil prices jumped sharply in overnight trade. The rally was triggered by heightened fears of a military confrontation between the United States and Iran.

Brent crude, the global benchmark, rose past $90 a barrel. Any sustained spike in oil prices is a negative for India, which imports over 80% of its crude requirements.

Gold Rallies as Safe-Haven Demand Returns

Gold rates moved higher as investors fled riskier assets. The yellow metal is often sought during times of geopolitical uncertainty and market turmoil.

On the Multi Commodity Exchange (MCX), gold futures were seen trading near the Rs 72,000 per 10 gram mark. Silver also gained in tandem.

Asian Markets Trade Mixed

Asian equities opened on a mixed note. Japan's Nikkei 225 and Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index were trading lower, tracking a weak session on Wall Street.

China's Shanghai Composite, however, showed resilience, offering some support to regional sentiment. The divergence reflects different reactions to the overnight cues.

Wall Street Closes in the Red

US stock indices ended Monday's session in negative territory. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell over 200 points, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq also recorded losses.

The sell-off was driven by escalating geopolitical risks and a spike in bond yields. The CBOE Volatility Index, or VIX, rose sharply, indicating increased market anxiety.

US Dollar Strengthens

The US dollar index climbed against a basket of major currencies. A stronger dollar typically puts pressure on emerging market currencies, including the Indian rupee.

A weaker rupee can increase the cost of imports, especially oil, and may prompt foreign institutional investors to pull money out of Indian equities.

FII and DII Activity

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) remained net sellers in the cash market on Monday. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) stepped in to absorb some of the selling pressure.

Market participants will closely watch the FII flow data for Tuesday. Sustained selling by foreign funds could weigh on the broader market.

Key Levels to Watch

The Nifty50 is expected to find support around the 24,400 mark. On the upside, the 24,700 level will act as a strong resistance.

Bank Nifty also faces a crucial test. Traders are advised to remain cautious and avoid taking aggressive positions until the geopolitical situation offers more clarity.

The coming sessions will likely be driven by news flow from the Middle East and the trajectory of crude oil prices. Any de-escalation in the US-Iran standoff could trigger a sharp relief rally.
