Bitcoin Hovers Near $64,000 as Trading Volume Drops, Interest Wanes

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin trades near $64,000 with reduced daily volume
  • Market sentiment shifts as traders await clearer catalysts
  • Analysts split on whether consolidation is a pause or a reversal signal

Price Stuck in a Tight Range

Bitcoin is struggling to break out of its current rut, hovering near the $64,000 mark on Saturday. The world's largest cryptocurrency has seen its trading volume taper off significantly over the past week, leaving bulls without the momentum needed to push prices higher.

Data from major exchanges shows daily turnover has dropped by roughly 30% from the levels seen earlier this month. The lull comes after a brief rally that took Bitcoin above $67,000, only for it to retreat just as quickly.

What's Behind the Cool Down?

Traders point to a lack of fresh catalysts. Regulatory news out of the United States has been quiet, and there is no major institutional buying spree to spark excitement. Retail interest, as measured by Google search trends and exchange app downloads, has also dipped.

Some analysts attribute the slowdown to profit-taking after the recent gains. Others say the market is simply waiting for a clearer signal—whether from macroeconomic data, such as the next Federal Reserve interest rate decision, or from developments in the broader crypto ecosystem.

Options Market Suggests Caution

Another telltale sign is the options market. Implied volatility has declined, and open interest in Bitcoin futures has flattened. This suggests that professional traders are not betting on a sharp move in either direction in the near term.

"The market is in a wait-and-see mode," one trader told TIVRA News. "No one wants to be caught on the wrong side if something breaks, but there's no urgency to buy either."

Is This a Pause or a Reversal?

Views are divided. Technical analysts note that Bitcoin is still holding above key support levels near $62,000. If that level holds, the consolidation could be a healthy pause before another leg up. But a break below $60,000 would likely trigger stop-losses and accelerate selling.

On the fundamental side, network activity—measured by transaction counts and active addresses—has been steady but not growing. Miners have been selling a portion of their holdings, which adds to the selling pressure. However, long-term holders continue to accumulate, suggesting confidence in the bigger picture.

For now, the price action remains indecisive. Volume is thin, and the range is narrow. Traders are watching for any news that could tip the scales.

What to watch: Bitcoin's weekly close on Sunday. If it stays above $63,000, the consolidation narrative holds. A close below that could invite more selling early next week.
