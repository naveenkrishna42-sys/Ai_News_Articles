NISAR Satellite: A Joint NASA-ISRO Mission to Map Earth in Unprecedented Detail

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • NISAR is a joint Earth-observation mission between NASA and ISRO.
  • It uses two radar systems to create high-resolution global maps every 12 days.
  • The data will help scientists monitor climate change, earthquakes, and ice melt.

What is NISAR?

NISAR, short for NASA-ISRO Synthetic Aperture Radar, is a collaborative Earth-observation satellite mission between the U.S. space agency NASA and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). The satellite is designed to provide an unprecedented, detailed view of our planet's surface, monitoring changes in land, ice, and forests.

Unlike traditional satellites that take static images, NISAR uses advanced radar technology to 'see' through clouds and darkness, allowing it to collect data day or night, in any weather. This makes it a powerful tool for tracking dynamic changes.

How It Works

The satellite carries two different synthetic aperture radar systems. One operates in the L-band, and the other in the S-band. Together, they can detect movements of the Earth's surface as small as a few centimetres.

NISAR will map the entire globe every 12 days, a frequency that is key to observing rapid changes like earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, and the melting of ice sheets. The data will be freely available to scientists worldwide.

Key Applications

The primary mission of NISAR is to study Earth's ecosystems, including forests and agricultural lands, and to measure changes in ice sheets and glaciers. This data is critical for understanding climate change impacts.

  • Monitoring deforestation and biomass changes
  • Tracking glacier and ice sheet movement
  • Predicting landslides and monitoring earthquake-prone zones
  • Assessing soil moisture and agricultural health

India's Role and Launch

ISRO is providing the spacecraft bus and the launch vehicle, while NASA contributes the two radar systems and several other key instruments. The partnership leverages the expertise of both agencies to create a mission that neither could achieve alone.

The satellite is expected to be launched from India, with officials stating it will significantly boost the country's capabilities in Earth observation. The exact launch date has not been announced, but preparations are in advanced stages.

What Happens Next

Once operational, NISAR will generate massive amounts of data – several terabytes per day. Scientists will begin processing this information to produce global maps that will be updated every 12 days, offering the most comprehensive, near-real-time view of Earth's changing surface ever achieved.
