Consumer panel fines SpiceJet ₹1 lakh for dark patterns on booking platform

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • CCPA imposed ₹1 lakh fine on SpiceJet for deploying dark patterns on its booking platform.
  • The airline used misleading design to obtain consumer consent for its loyalty programme opt-in.
  • Regulator's action draws industry criticism and highlights growing scrutiny of deceptive online practices.

CCPA slaps penalty on SpiceJet

The Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) has imposed a fine of ₹1 lakh on budget carrier SpiceJet for using what it termed 'dark patterns' on its ticket booking platform. The penalty was announced on Friday, following a complaint that the airline's interface was designed to trick customers into unknowingly signing up for its loyalty programme.

Dark patterns refer to user interface designs that deliberately make it difficult for consumers to refuse a service or easily cancel a subscription. In SpiceJet's case, the regulator found that the opt-in for the loyalty programme was presented in a way that misled users into giving consent without their full awareness.

How the dark pattern worked

According to the CCPA order, the airline's booking flow included pre-checked boxes and confusing language that nudged customers towards enrolling in the loyalty programme. The design made it cumbersome for users to opt out, often requiring multiple steps or hidden navigation to decline.

Such practices violate consumer rights under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, which prohibits unfair trade practices. The regulator noted that SpiceJet's interface failed to provide clear, unambiguous consent mechanisms, effectively coercing users into a service they did not intend to join.

Industry and legal reaction

The fine has drawn criticism from within the aviation industry, with some experts pointing out that dark patterns are not unique to SpiceJet. Many online platforms, including airlines and e-commerce sites, use similar tactics to boost sign-ups and revenue.

Legal commentators have said the CCPA's action signals a tougher stance against digital deception. The penalty, though modest, sets a precedent. It warns companies that manipulating user choices through interface design will not be tolerated.

SpiceJet has not yet issued a public statement on the fine. The airline has the option to appeal the order before the appellate authority.

Growing scrutiny of dark patterns

The CCPA has been increasingly active in policing dark patterns. Earlier this year, it issued guidelines explicitly banning such practices, including false urgency, basket sneaking, and forced action. The SpiceJet case is among the first enforcement actions under these rules.

Consumer rights groups have welcomed the move, calling it a necessary step to protect digital users. They argue that dark patterns exploit cognitive biases and leave consumers trapped in subscriptions or services they never wanted.

Regulators across the world are tightening rules on dark patterns. The European Union's Digital Services Act and India's own proposed e-commerce rules both target such deceptive design.

What to watch: Whether SpiceJet challenges the order in court, and if the CCPA will levy heavier penalties on repeat offenders. The fine of ₹1 lakh is small relative to the airline's revenue, but the regulatory signal is clear—dark patterns are now a enforcement priority in India.
