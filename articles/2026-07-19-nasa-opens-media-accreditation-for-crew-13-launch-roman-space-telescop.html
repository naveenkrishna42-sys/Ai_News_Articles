NASA Opens Media Accreditation for Crew-13 Launch, Roman Space Telescope

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Media accreditation for Crew-13 launch is open with a strict deadline.
  • The Roman Space Telescope will search for ancient black holes by observing stellar consumption.
  • Crew-13 marks another milestone in NASA's commercial crew program.

Media Accreditation Window Opens for Crew-13

NASA has issued a media advisory for the upcoming launch of Crew-13, calling it the only opportunity for media accreditation. The space agency is urging journalists to apply promptly as slots are limited.

The Crew-13 mission will carry astronauts to the International Space Station under NASA's Commercial Crew Program. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact launch date, but preparations are underway at Kennedy Space Center.

Roman Space Telescope Prepares for Deep Space Survey

Alongside the Crew-13 announcement, NASA has invited media to cover the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope. The observatory, named after NASA's first chief astronomer, is designed to study dark energy, exoplanets, and infrared astrophysics.

Scientists believe the Roman Space Telescope will find ancient black holes by watching how they consume stars. The telescope's wide-field instrument will monitor galactic centers for tidal disruption events—when a star gets too close to a black hole and is torn apart.

How the Telescope Will Spot Hidden Black Holes

Roman's ability to survey large swaths of the sky repeatedly will allow it to catch these rare events. When a black hole devours a star, it produces a bright flare that can be detected across billions of light-years.

Researchers expect Roman to discover hundreds of such events, revealing black holes that formed in the early universe. Current telescopes have only captured a handful of tidal disruption events, making Roman's survey a game-changer.

What to Expect from the Launch

The Roman Space Telescope is scheduled to launch on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket from Cape Canaveral. The mission will place the observatory at the Sun-Earth L2 Lagrange point, about 1.5 million kilometres from Earth.

NASA has not yet specified a launch date for Roman, but the telescope is in its final testing phase. The Crew-13 mission will proceed independently, with its own timeline.

Media Deadlines and Logistics

Accreditation requests for Crew-13 must be submitted by the deadline set in the advisory. International media representatives need to follow additional security protocols.

NASA will provide updates on both missions through its official channels. Journalists covering the launch should check for any last-minute changes to the schedule.

The agency is also planning a series of pre-launch briefings for the Roman Space Telescope, details of which will be announced later.

Both missions represent significant steps in NASA's exploration goals. Crew-13 continues the routine rotation of astronauts on the ISS, while Roman promises to reshape our understanding of black holes and cosmic history.

Watch for the next media advisory from NASA, which will provide specific dates and times for Crew-13 and Roman Space Telescope events.
