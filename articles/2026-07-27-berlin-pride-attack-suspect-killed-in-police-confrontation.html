Berlin Pride attack suspect killed in police confrontation

📅 2026-07-27 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • The suspect in the Berlin Pride vehicle ramming was killed in a confrontation with police.
  • Germany has classified the attack as a 'terror attack'.
  • The attack occurred near the city's annual Pride parade, causing multiple casualties.
  • Videos show attendees being evacuated from the event due to the unfolding situation.

German police have shot and killed the prime suspect in the deadly vehicle ramming that targeted attendees near Berlin's annual Pride parade. Officials confirmed the development on Sunday, bringing a swift end to a manhunt that had gripped the city.

The confrontation occurred just hours after the attack, which authorities have now formally classified as a 'terror attack'. The suspect's death marks a significant, if grim, turn in the investigation.

Attack during Pride celebrations

The incident unfolded on Saturday when a vehicle ploughed into a crowd near the route of the city's Christopher Street Day parade, a major event celebrating the LGBTQ+ community. Witnesses described scenes of panic as the vehicle struck multiple people.

Videos circulating online show parade attendees being urgently told to leave the area due to what organisers described as 'a difficult situation'. The attack left several people dead and injured, casting a dark shadow over what was meant to be a day of celebration.

Officials have not yet released the exact casualty figures, but the scale of the attack has sent shockwaves through Germany and the international community.

Police operation and suspect neutralised

Following the ramming, police launched a massive search for the driver, who had fled the scene. The operation culminated on Sunday when officers located the suspect. Details of the confrontation remain scarce, but law enforcement sources confirmed that the suspect was killed after opening fire on police.

German Interior Minister Nancy Faeser had earlier described the incident as a 'brutal attack' and vowed a full investigation. The classification of the event as a terror attack allows authorities to deploy additional resources and powers in their probe.

No group has yet claimed responsibility for the attack, and investigators are working to determine the suspect's motive and potential links to extremist networks.

City on edge

Berlin remains under a heightened security posture. Police have increased patrols at major public gatherings and transit hubs. The city's LGBTQ+ community, while reeling from the attack, has also shown resilience, with many calling for the Pride events to continue as a stand against hatred.

Chancellor Olaf Scholz condemned the attack, stating that 'violence and terror have no place in our society'. He offered condolences to the victims and their families.

The attack has reignited debates in Germany about security at public events and the threat of vehicular attacks, a tactic that has been used by extremists across Europe in recent years.

Investigators are now combing through the suspect's background, digital footprint, and any associates. They are also examining the vehicle used in the attack for any evidence that could shed light on the planning and execution of the ramming.

As Berlin absorbs the shock, the focus will now shift to the official investigation and the city's efforts to heal. Officials have urged the public to remain vigilant but not to give in to fear.
