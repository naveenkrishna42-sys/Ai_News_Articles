Ebola reaches displacement camps in DR Congo as outbreak worsens

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ebola virus has reached displacement camps in DR Congo, raising fears of rapid spread among vulnerable populations.
  • Health workers, unpaid and facing deadly risk, are threatening to abandon the Ebola response.
  • Officials suggest the virus may be mutating, complicating containment efforts.
  • WHO chief calls for a stronger, more coordinated response to the outbreak.

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has taken a dangerous turn, with the virus now reaching displacement camps. These camps, already struggling with overcrowding and limited resources, are feared to become hotspots for further transmission.

UN News confirmed the development on Thursday, raising alarms among humanitarian agencies. The camps house thousands of people displaced by ongoing conflict, making containment efforts particularly challenging.

Health workers at breaking point

Meanwhile, health workers on the frontlines are threatening to abandon the response. The reasons are stark: unpaid wages and the constant, deadly risk of infection. Many have not seen salaries for months, despite risking their lives daily.

Their potential departure would be a severe blow to an already strained effort. With fewer trained personnel, tracing contacts and isolating cases becomes nearly impossible, especially in the chaotic environment of the camps.

Concerns of mutation

Adding to the worry, officials have indicated that the virus behind this massive outbreak could be mutating. The Guardian reported that changes in the virus's genetic makeup might affect how it spreads or how severe the disease becomes, though no specifics have been confirmed yet.

Scientists are studying samples to determine if the mutations alter transmission dynamics. So far, the World Health Organization has identified the disease as caused by the Bundibugyo virus, a less common strain, but the situation remains fluid.

WHO demands stronger response

The WHO chief has called for a stronger response in Congo, urging international partners to step up. The appeal comes as the outbreak shows no signs of slowing, and the new cases in displacement camps could trigger a wider crisis.

Local authorities and aid groups are scrambling to contain the spread, but they face immense hurdles. Insecurity in the region has repeatedly disrupted vaccination campaigns and safe burial practices, allowing the virus to persist.

What to watch

The coming weeks will be critical. Whether health workers return to their posts, and whether the international community heeds the WHO's call, could decide the outbreak's trajectory. If the virus continues to spread in the camps, the death toll could climb steeply, and the risk of cross-border transmission will rise.
