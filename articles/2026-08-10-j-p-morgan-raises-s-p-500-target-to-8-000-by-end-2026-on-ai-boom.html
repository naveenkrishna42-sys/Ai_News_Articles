J.P. Morgan raises S&P 500 target to 8,000 by end-2026 on AI boom

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • J.P. Morgan raised its S&P 500 target to 8,000 for end-2026.
  • The upgrade is driven by strong earnings growth and AI-related capital expenditure.
  • Goldman Sachs separately noted share buybacks could outweigh equity supply in 2026.

Wall Street's biggest bank turns more bullish

J.P. Morgan has lifted its year-end 2026 target for the S&P 500 to 8,000, up from its previous forecast, citing stronger-than-expected earnings and the accelerating payoff from artificial intelligence investments. The new target implies a roughly 10% upside from current levels, a bold call that underscores the bank's confidence in the durability of the AI-driven market rally.

The revision, announced Monday, places J.P. Morgan among the most optimistic forecasters on Wall Street. Strategists at the bank said that corporate profitability has been resilient, and that AI-related capital expenditure is beginning to translate into tangible revenue growth for technology and other sectors.

AI capex finally paying off

For over a year, investors have debated whether the massive spending on AI infrastructure—data centres, chips, and cloud capacity—would justify the valuations of mega-cap tech stocks. J.P. Morgan's analysts now believe the answer is yes. They point to improving margins and productivity gains across industries that have adopted AI tools, which are showing up in earnings reports.

The bank's strategists noted that the earnings revision cycle has turned positive, with more companies beating estimates than missing them. This broadening of earnings strength beyond just a handful of tech giants has given the bank confidence to raise its target.

Buybacks to outpace equity supply

In a related note, Goldman Sachs strategists highlighted that share buybacks are likely to outweigh new equity supply in 2026, providing a further tailwind for stock prices. With companies continuing to generate strong cash flows, many are choosing to return capital to shareholders rather than chase expensive acquisitions.

This dynamic could tighten the supply-demand balance for equities, supporting valuations even if the Federal Reserve's rate cut path remains uncertain. Goldman's view aligns with the broader sentiment that corporate balance sheets remain healthy, despite lingering concerns about consumer spending and geopolitical tensions.

What this means for Indian markets

For Indian investors, the upgrade is a positive signal. A higher S&P 500 target typically boosts global risk appetite, which often benefits emerging markets like India. Foreign portfolio inflows into Indian equities have been volatile this year, but a sustained rally in US markets could encourage more risk-on flows.

However, analysts caution that Indian indices are not immune to a US-led correction if AI earnings disappoint. The IT sector, in particular, has a high correlation with US tech spending, and any slowdown in AI capex could hit Indian software exporters.

Risks remain on the horizon

J.P. Morgan's target is not without risks. Inflation data, geopolitical shocks, or a sudden tightening of financial conditions could derail the earnings trajectory. The bank itself acknowledged that its forecast hinges on a soft landing for the US economy, where growth slows but does not tip into recession.

Market participants will closely watch upcoming earnings from major tech companies and the Fed's policy signals in the coming months. Any deviation from the current path could force a swift reassessment of the 8,000 target.

For now, the Street's mood is cautiously optimistic. With buybacks providing a floor and AI driving the ceiling, the path to 8,000 looks plausible—but not guaranteed. Investors would do well to keep an eye on quarterly earnings and the Fed's next moves.
