Ohh My Dog review: Pankaj Tripathi shines in rare kid-friendly film

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pankaj Tripathi leads a rare kid-friendly Indian film with stray dogs as co-stars.
  • The movie offers wholesome entertainment without adult humour or excessive violence.
  • Critics praise the performances and the film's gentle, accessible tone.

A welcome shift from formula

Indian cinema has long treated children’s films as an afterthought. Most mainstream releases lean on slapstick or adult-oriented jokes, leaving families with few honest options. Ohh My Dog breaks that pattern. Directed with a light touch, it places Pankaj Tripathi and a pack of stray dogs at the centre of a story that is simple, warm, and genuinely funny.

The plot stays modest. A man’s life gets tangled with a group of street dogs, and what follows is a series of small, believable events. There are no grand set-pieces or forced twists. Instead, the film earns its laughs through character and quiet observation.

Tripathi’s natural charm

Pankaj Tripathi has built a career on playing ordinary men with extraordinary depth. Here, he anchors the film with his usual ease. His comic timing is impeccable, but he never lets the performance tip into caricature. The actor treats the dogs as equals, not props, and that respect shows on screen.

The stray dogs themselves are the real scene-stealers. They are not trained to perform tricks or mug for the camera. They simply exist, and that authenticity gives the film its heart. Watching them interact with Tripathi feels less like a scripted scene and more like a slice of life.

A film for every age

What makes Ohh My Dog rare is its refusal to talk down to children. The humour is gentle but not dumbed down. Adults will catch subtle nuances, while younger viewers will enjoy the antics of the canine cast. There is no violence, no crass jokes, and no need to fast-forward awkward moments.

The film also touches on the bond between humans and animals without getting preachy. It does not lecture about stray dog welfare or urban neglect. Instead, it shows how empathy can emerge in the most unremarkable circumstances.

Technical craft and support cast

The cinematography captures the dusty, everyday corners of a small town with warmth. The background score stays unobtrusive, letting scenes breathe. Supporting actors, though given limited screen time, complement Tripathi without overshadowing him.

If there is a flaw, it is the pacing in the middle act. A few sequences linger longer than needed, and the narrative could have been tighter. But these are minor quibbles in a film that otherwise stays focused and sincere.

Why this matters

In an industry where big-budget spectacles dominate, Ohh My Dog is a reminder that small stories can leave a lasting impression. It is the kind of film parents can watch with their kids and enjoy equally. That is a rare achievement in Indian cinema today.

The film’s success may prompt more producers to back similar projects. If audiences show up, studios might finally recognise the demand for clean, intelligent family entertainment. For now, Ohh My Dog stands as a gentle triumph — one that proves a good story, a fine actor, and a few street dogs are all you need.

What happens next depends on the box office. If word-of-mouth spreads, this could become a sleeper hit and open doors for more such films. Watch for whether multiplexes extend its run.
