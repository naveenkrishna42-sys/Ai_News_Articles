US, Japan confirm rare joint intervention to prop up yen

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • US and Japan confirm rare coordinated intervention in currency markets to support the yen.
  • Treasury Secretary Bessent reportedly tasked with buying $5-10 billion worth of yen.
  • Both nations signal readiness for further action if needed.
  • Intervention marks a historic move by the US Treasury in yen markets.

Historic move in currency markets

In a rare and significant move, the United States and Japan have confirmed a coordinated intervention to prop up the yen, signalling a united front against sustained currency weakness. The action, confirmed on Monday, marks one of the few instances where Washington has directly participated in yen-buying operations alongside Tokyo.

The intervention underscores growing concern over the yen's slide, which has put pressure on Japanese importers and added to global market volatility. By acting together, the two allies aim to inject stability into a currency market that has been heavily skewed against the Japanese unit.

Bessent's role and the scale of action

According to a Reuters photograph and exclusive reporting, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been handed a specific mandate: to buy between $5 billion and $10 billion worth of Japanese yen. This marks a historic undertaking for the US Treasury, which has rarely waded directly into foreign exchange markets to support another nation's currency.

The scale of the operation reflects the seriousness with which Washington views the yen's decline. While the exact timing and execution details remain under wraps, market participants are already dissecting the implications of such a sizeable, targeted purchase.

Signal of readiness

Both governments have signalled that this may not be a one-off move. Officials have indicated a readiness to intervene again if market conditions warrant, suggesting a sustained commitment to stabilising the yen. This forward guidance is aimed at deterring speculative bets against the currency.

The joint statement from both nations emphasised cooperation and a shared interest in orderly currency markets. Analysts note that the coordinated nature of the action amplifies its impact, making it harder for markets to dismiss as a solo effort by Tokyo alone.

Market reaction and broader implications

Financial markets have reacted with heightened attention, with traders recalibrating positions in light of the intervention. The yen saw an immediate but measured response, reflecting the market's understanding that further action could follow.

This development also carries broader implications for global monetary policy. A stronger yen could ease import costs for Japan, potentially softening inflationary pressures. For the US, the move signals a willingness to use its financial heft to support allies in times of currency stress, a posture that could influence future policy decisions.

What to watch

Investors will now closely monitor the yen's trajectory over the coming sessions. The key question is whether this intervention proves sufficient to shift sentiment or if further rounds of buying will be necessary.

With both governments vowing to remain vigilant, the coming weeks will reveal whether this rare joint action marks a turning point for the yen or merely a temporary reprieve in a longer battle.
