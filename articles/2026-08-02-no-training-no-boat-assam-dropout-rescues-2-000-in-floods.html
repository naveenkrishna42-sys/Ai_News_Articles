No Training, No Boat: Assam Dropout Rescues 2,000 in Floods

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Class 10 dropout rescued over 2,000 people in Assam floods without formal training or a motorboat.
  • Assam floods have claimed at least 85 lives, with 1.35 lakh people still affected.
  • State announces six-month loan relief for borrowers in four worst-hit districts.

In the midst of Assam's devastating floods, one man's story stands out. A Class 10 dropout, without any formal rescue training and without a motorboat, has managed to pull more than 2,000 people to safety. His name has not been officially confirmed by authorities, but local residents in the worst-hit areas describe him as a self-taught rescuer who used a traditional country boat to navigate the swollen rivers.

While the state government has deployed boats and the army has been called in, this lone man has been working tirelessly since the waters began rising. He has no life jacket, no radio, and no backup team. What he has is an intimate knowledge of the local waterways and an unshakeable resolve.

One Man, One Boat, 2,000 Lives

The rescuer, who dropped out of school after Class 10, has been ferrying people from marooned villages to higher ground. His boat, a small wooden vessel typically used for fishing, has become a lifeline for entire communities. Witnesses say he works from dawn to dusk, often skipping meals, to ensure no one is left behind.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of people he has saved, but local village councils have put the figure at over 2,000. The man reportedly refused payment from villagers, saying he was only doing what any human being would do. His identity has become a subject of curiosity, with many calling him a hero, though he remains shy of attention.

Assam's Flood Crisis Worsens

His efforts come against a grim backdrop. The flood situation in Assam has deteriorated over the past week, with 85 deaths reported so far. According to the Assam State Disaster Management Authority, around 1.35 lakh people are still affected across multiple districts. Relief camps have been set up, but many remote areas remain cut off.

In the last 24 hours alone, three more deaths were reported, adding to the mounting toll. The Brahmaputra and its tributaries continue to flow above the danger mark at several points, and the India Meteorological Department has forecast more rain in the coming days.

Government Response and Loan Relief

The state government has announced a six-month loan relief for borrowers in the four worst-hit districts. This is aimed at easing the financial burden on farmers and small business owners who have lost their livelihoods to the floods. The relief will be applicable to loans taken from banks and cooperative societies.

Meanwhile, relief teams from the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and the army have been working in the affected areas. But the scale of the disaster has stretched resources thin. Local volunteers like the Class 10 dropout have become essential, filling gaps where official machinery has struggled to reach.

Gujarat, J&K, Maharashtra Also Hit

Assam is not alone in this crisis. Monsoon fury has also struck Gujarat, Jammu and Kashmir, and Maharashtra. In Gujarat, several low-lying areas have been inundated, while J&K has witnessed flash floods and landslides. Maharashtra has reported waterlogging in parts of Mumbai and other districts.

However, Assam remains the worst-hit state in the country. With 1.78 lakh people affected in earlier estimates, the numbers have only grown. The central government has assured all possible help, but the immediate need is for more boats, more relief materials, and more people like the unnamed rescuer who are willing to risk their lives for others.

As the rains continue, the next few days will be critical. The state government is expected to release a fresh assessment of the damage soon. For now, the focus remains on rescue and relief, with the hope that the waters will recede and allow Assam to begin the long process of recovery.
