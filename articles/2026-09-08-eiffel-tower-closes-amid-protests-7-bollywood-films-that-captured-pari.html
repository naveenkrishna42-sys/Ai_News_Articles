Eiffel Tower Closure Highlights Paris's Enduring Cinematic Draw

The temporary closure of the Eiffel Tower due to local labor protests has disrupted travel plans for thousands of global tourists. For Indian travelers, the monument represents more than just a structural marvel; it is a symbol of cinematic romance. Over the past six decades, Indian filmmakers have leveraged the visual appeal of Paris to elevate their storytelling, turning the city into an active character in their narratives.

Filming in Paris has historically driven substantial outbound tourism from India. According to data from Atout France (the France Tourism Development Agency), Bollywood releases have consistently generated spikes in visa applications and travel bookings, particularly to locations showcased in high-budget productions. This analysis covers seven landmark Hindi films that brought the French capital to Indian silver screens.

Cinematic Metrics: Bollywood's Parisian Footprint

The transition from shooting on restricted sets to securing complex permits for on-location filming in Paris reflects the growing financial scale of the Indian film industry. The following table highlights the distribution platforms and key locations featured in these influential films.

1. Sangam (1964)

Directed by Raj Kapoor, Sangam was one of the earliest Indian films to feature extensive European locations. The second half of the romantic drama showcases Sundar (Raj Kapoor) and Radha (Vyjayanthimala) on their honeymoon in Paris. The film introduced Indian audiences to the Eiffel Tower and the Arc de Triomphe, establishing Paris as the ultimate destination for luxury and romance in the post-independence era.

2. An Evening in Paris (1967)

Shakti Samanta’s romantic thriller capitalized entirely on its European setting. Starring Shammi Kapoor and Sharmila Tagore, the film utilized iconic backdrops such as the River Seine and the crowded avenues of Montmartre. The high production values and vibrant cinematography by V. Gopi Krishna helped make Paris a dream destination for middle-class Indian audiences during the late 1960s.

3. London Paris New York (2012)

Directed by Anu Menon, this coming-of-age film dedicates its middle chapter entirely to Paris. The characters Lalitha (Aditi Rao Hydari) and Nikhil (Ali Zafar) spend a single night wandering through the city. The film avoids grand monuments to capture the intimate charm of Parisian alleys, local bistros, and the quiet banks of the Canal Saint-Martin, offering a contemporary, indie perspective of the city.

4. Ishkq in Paris (2013)

This romance drama, starring Preity Zinta and Rhehan Malliek, was filmed almost entirely on location in France. Under the direction of Prem Raj, the production utilized the winter aesthetic of Paris to frame a story about two strangers meeting on a train. The film features classic visual tropes, including late-night strolls under the illuminated Eiffel Tower.

5. Queen (2014)

Vikas Bahl’s critically acclaimed Queen subverted traditional romantic depictions of Paris. When Rani (Kangana Ranaut) embarks on her solo honeymoon to the city, the audience is shown a gritty, realistic view of Parisian hostels, diverse local neighborhoods, and the challenges of navigating a foreign metropolis alone. The film balances grand shots of the Eiffel Tower with authentic, street-level experiences.

6. Befikre (2016)

Aditya Chopra’s romantic comedy was shot entirely in France, with a significant portion set in Paris. Starring Ranveer Singh and Vaani Kapoor, the film serves as a virtual tour of the city, featuring high-production sequences at the Pont des Arts, the Sacré-Cœur Basilica, and the rooftops of Parisian apartments. The film benefited from tax incentives offered by the French government to promote international filmmaking.

7. Ae Dil Hai Mushkil (2016)

Directed by Karan Johar, this emotional drama features a pivotal segment set in Paris. Characters played by Ranbir Kapoor and Anushka Sharma recreate the title track of "An Evening in Paris" on the city's streets. The sequence bridges classic Bollywood nostalgia with modern European cityscapes, showcasing the enduring appeal of Paris to contemporary filmmakers.

Key Insights for Travelers and Film Enthusiasts

  • Evolving Aesthetics: Bollywood has transitioned from treating Paris as a mere backdrop to integrating the city's unique cultural landscape directly into narrative arcs.
  • Economic Driver: Film tourism remains a highly effective marketing tool, with classic and contemporary films driving consistent footfall to Parisian landmarks.
  • Operational Reality: While films showcase pristine European streets, real-world disruptions like recent public sector strikes highlight the importance of verifying monument accessibility prior to travel.
