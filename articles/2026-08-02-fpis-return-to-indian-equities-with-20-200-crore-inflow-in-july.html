FPIs return to Indian equities with ₹20,200 crore inflow in July

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • FPIs invested ₹20,200 crore in Indian equities in July, ending a four-month selloff
  • June saw heavy outflows of ₹49,340 crore as foreign investors withdrew from the market
  • Overseas sentiment improves, with analysts calling Indian equities 'probably oversold'

Foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) have staged a comeback in Indian equities, injecting ₹20,200 crore in July and snapping a four-month selling streak that had rattled markets. The reversal marks a significant shift in sentiment after overseas funds pulled out heavily through the spring.

June was particularly brutal, with FPIs withdrawing ₹49,340 crore — the sharpest monthly outflow in recent memory. That exodus had pushed benchmark indices lower and triggered concerns about sustained foreign disinvestment. Now, the tide appears to have turned.

What drove the reversal

Market watchers attribute the July inflow to a combination of factors: easing global inflation pressures, a softer dollar, and growing conviction that Indian valuations had fallen to attractive levels. One analyst quoted by The Hindu described Indian equities as "probably oversold" after months of relentless selling.

The ₹20,200 crore figure, reported across financial media including Moneycontrol and Telangana Today, represents a clean break from the trend. In dollar terms, the buying spree amounts to roughly $2.31 billion, according to Moneycontrol's calculations. That is a notable shift for a market that had grown accustomed to outflows since March.

June's exodus in context

The scale of June's outflow — nearly half a lakh crore rupees — had forced FPIs to offload positions across large-cap and mid-cap stocks. Domestic institutional investors, meanwhile, had absorbed much of that supply, cushioning the blow to indices. But the persistent selling had weighed on market breadth and kept volatility elevated.

Why did foreign investors flee in the first place? Analysts pointed to elevated US bond yields, a strengthening dollar, and geopolitical uncertainties that made emerging markets less appealing. India, despite its strong macroeconomic fundamentals, was not spared the global risk-off mood.

Signs of a durable shift?

The July inflow is being read as more than a blip. Overseas sentiment has improved markedly, with foreign investors warming up to Indian assets after weeks of caution. The Times of India noted that the buying streak has revived hopes of sustained FPI participation in the coming months.

Still, experts caution against reading too much into a single month's numbers. The global environment remains fluid — US rate decisions, oil prices, and election outcomes in key economies could all alter the calculus. But for now, the data points to a clear turnaround.

For Indian markets, the return of FPIs is a morale booster. It signals that overseas money managers see value in domestic equities after the correction. It also eases the pressure on domestic institutions, which had been the primary buyers during the selloff.

What to watch next

All eyes are now on August data to see if the inflow sustains. A second consecutive month of buying would confirm the trend and likely push indices higher. Conversely, any fresh global shock could prompt FPIs to retreat again, undoing July's gains.

Market participants will also track quarterly earnings and monsoon progress for cues on the domestic economy. If corporate profits hold up and macro stability persists, foreign investors may find it hard to stay away for long.
