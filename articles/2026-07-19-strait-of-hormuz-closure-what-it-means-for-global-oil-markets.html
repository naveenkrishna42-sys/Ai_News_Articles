Strait of Hormuz Closure: What It Means for Global Oil Markets

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Crude oil prices have not spiralled despite tensions, surprising analysts.
  • The IEA warns the crisis threatens global energy security.
  • Oil markets may be ignoring mounting risks at their own peril.

The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway between Oman and Iran, is once again in the crosshairs of global energy markets. A renewed closure, or even the credible threat of one, sends shivers through an industry that moves nearly a fifth of the world's oil through this chokepoint. But the price action tells a different story.

Despite escalating US-Iran conflict and fears of a blockade, crude oil prices have not spiralled out of control. This disconnect between geopolitical heat and market calm is puzzling many analysts. The Times of India recently asked why prices remain contained, while OilPrice.com warns that markets are ignoring mounting risks at their own peril.

The Calm Before the Storm?

The immediate answer lies in global inventories and demand expectations. Major economies have built up strategic reserves since the last major crisis. The US, for instance, holds over 700 million barrels in its Strategic Petroleum Reserve. China and India have also stockpiled heavily.

But those reserves are finite. The International Energy Agency (IEA) chief has explicitly warned that the Strait of Hormuz crisis threatens global energy security. If the closure drags on for weeks, not days, the buffer disappears. The BusinessLine report notes that time is ticking for oil reserves, suggesting a ticking clock that markets are choosing to ignore.

Supply Chain Vulnerabilities

A closure does not just stop tankers; it disrupts the entire logistics chain. Insurance premiums for vessels in the region have already spiked. Shipping companies are rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, adding weeks to transit times and millions to costs.

For India, which imports over 80% of its crude oil and a significant portion from the Gulf, the stakes are high. Any sustained disruption would force the government to dip deeper into its strategic petroleum reserves, currently housed in underground caverns at Visakhapatnam, Mangalore, and Padur. The Commerce Ministry has not yet commented on contingency plans, though officials are believed to be monitoring the situation hourly.

Why Markets Are Betting on Diplomacy

The current market calm reflects a bet that neither the US nor Iran wants a full-blown war. Tehran has used the threat of closing the Strait as leverage for decades, but has never followed through for more than a few days. The economic cost to Iran itself — which relies on the Strait for its own exports — is a powerful deterrent.

However, the OilPrice.com analysis argues this is a dangerous gamble. Geopolitical risks are binary: they either happen or they don't. When they do, the price reaction can be instant and violent. The IEA's warning should not be dismissed as routine alarmism; the agency rarely issues such direct statements without credible intelligence.

What to Watch

The coming weeks will be critical. Traders will watch for any naval skirmishes in the Persian Gulf, diplomatic signals from Washington and Tehran, and weekly inventory data from the US and IEA. If crude oil prices break above the $90 per barrel mark, it would signal that the market's patience is running out.

For now, the world is holding its breath — and its reserves.
