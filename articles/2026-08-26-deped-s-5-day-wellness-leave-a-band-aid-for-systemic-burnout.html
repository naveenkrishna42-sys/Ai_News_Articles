DepEd’s 5-Day Wellness Leave: A Band-Aid for Systemic Burnout

📅 2026-08-26 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • DepEd grants five days of mandatory wellness leave for teaching and non-teaching staff.
  • Policy focuses on mental health amid rising reports of educator exhaustion.
  • Critics argue that chronic understaffing makes the leave difficult to implement in practice.

The Policy Shift

The Department of Education (DepEd) has officially institutionalized a five-day wellness leave for its massive workforce. This directive intends to provide teachers and administrative staff a designated window to recharge, acknowledging the mounting pressure within the classroom environment. While the policy is a welcome administrative acknowledgment of mental health, its real-world effectiveness remains to be tested.

The Reality of Implementation

Granting leave is one thing; ensuring that the system can function in the absence of staff is another. In many public schools, the ratio of students to teachers is already stretched to its limit. When one teacher takes a five-day break, the workload inevitably shifts to colleagues or results in overcrowded classrooms. Without a corresponding increase in substitute teacher funding or administrative support, this policy risks becoming a logistical headache rather than a genuine benefit.

Is Five Days Enough?

A five-day window is a drop in the ocean for educators dealing with chronic fatigue. Burnout in the education sector is rarely the result of a single month of hard work; it is the cumulative effect of oversized classes, heavy clerical loads, and limited resources. A short break may alleviate immediate stress, but it does not fix the structural issues that lead to exhaustion in the first place.

  • Pros: Officially recognizes mental health as a workplace priority; provides a legal framework for rest; boosts morale in the short term.
  • Cons: Lack of coverage for absent staff; potential increase in workload for remaining teachers; does not address systemic resource shortages.

The Path Forward

The success of this wellness initiative hinges on how school principals manage the scheduling. If the leave is treated as a tick-box exercise, it will do little to improve teacher retention. To make this meaningful, the department must pair the leave policy with systemic shifts, such as reducing the non-teaching duties currently offloaded onto educators. Unless the burden of paperwork is lifted, these five days will merely be a temporary pause in an otherwise unsustainable professional cycle.

We must watch how the department handles the impact on instructional hours. If student learning outcomes suffer because teachers are away, the policy will face immediate political backlash. The challenge for DepEd is to balance the well-being of the workforce with the continuity of the academic calendar.
