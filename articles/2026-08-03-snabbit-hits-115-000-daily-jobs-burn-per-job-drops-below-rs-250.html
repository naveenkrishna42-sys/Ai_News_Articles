Snabbit hits 115,000 daily jobs, burn per job drops below Rs 250

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Snabbit achieves 115,000 daily jobs in Q1 FY27
  • Burn per job drops below Rs 250, a key efficiency milestone
  • Instant home services race heats up as Urban Company's InstaHelp also crosses 100,000 daily orders

Instant home services platform Snabbit has crossed a significant milestone, clocking over 115,000 daily jobs in the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. The company also reported that its burn per job has dropped below Rs 250, a figure that signals improving unit economics in a segment that has long struggled with profitability.

The development comes as the broader instant-help sector witnesses rapid growth. Urban Company's InstaHelp, a direct competitor, has also crossed the 100,000 daily bookings mark, according to recent reports. The race is clearly heating up, with both platforms vying for dominance in a market that is expanding faster than many anticipated.

Efficiency Gains Take Centre Stage

The drop in burn per job is particularly noteworthy. For years, instant service platforms have faced criticism for their heavy spending on customer acquisition and service delivery. A burn rate below Rs 250 per job suggests that Snabbit is finding ways to streamline operations, reduce overheads, and extract more value from each transaction.

Industry analysts point to several factors that may have contributed to this improvement. Better route optimisation for service professionals, tighter scheduling, and a growing base of repeat customers are likely playing a role. The company has not yet disclosed the exact methodology behind its burn calculation, but the trend is clear.

Competitive Landscape Intensifies

Snabbit's achievement does not exist in a vacuum. Urban Company's InstaHelp has also reported crossing 100,000 daily orders, indicating that the overall market is expanding. The two platforms are now locked in a battle for market share, with pricing emerging as a key weapon.

Recent reports suggest that househelp apps are offering services at prices as low as Rs 29 for certain tasks. This aggressive pricing strategy is aimed at attracting first-time users, but it also raises questions about sustainability. The challenge lies in balancing affordability with service quality, a tightrope that many platforms have struggled to walk.

Economic Realities Loom Large

Despite the growth, experts remain cautious. A recent analysis in Livemint questioned whether the economics of instant-help apps truly add up. While crossing the 100,000 daily order mark is impressive, the path to profitability is still unclear. The low burn per job reported by Snabbit is encouraging, but it remains to be seen if this can be sustained as the company scales further.

The sector's rapid expansion has also drawn scrutiny from investors, who are keen to see tangible returns. Venture capital funding has flowed into these platforms, but the pressure to demonstrate a viable business model is mounting. Snabbit's latest numbers offer a glimmer of hope, but the real test will come in the coming quarters.

What Lies Ahead

As the instant home services market continues to evolve, all eyes will be on how Snabbit and its rivals manage growth without bleeding cash. The company's ability to maintain or improve its burn per job will be a critical indicator of long-term viability. With competition intensifying and pricing pressures mounting, the next few quarters will be decisive.

For now, the 115,000 daily jobs mark is a testament to the demand for instant services in India. Whether the economics eventually add up remains an open question, but the momentum is undeniable. Watch this space as the battle for the home services market shifts from growth at all costs to sustainable expansion.
