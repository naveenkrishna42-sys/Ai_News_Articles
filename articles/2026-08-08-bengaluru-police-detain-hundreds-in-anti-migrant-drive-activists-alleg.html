Bengaluru police detain hundreds in anti-migrant drive; activists allege assault

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru police detained hundreds in an anti-illegal migrant drive.
  • Activists have accused police of assaulting those detained.
  • Officials have not yet issued a detailed response to the allegations.

Bengaluru police have detained hundreds of people in a large-scale drive targeting what they describe as illegal migrants, a move that has drawn sharp criticism from activists who allege that officers used excessive force, including physical assault, during the operation.

The drive, conducted across multiple parts of the city, comes as part of a broader enforcement push, but details of its legal basis or the specific criteria used to identify detainees remain unclear. Police have not yet released official figures or a formal statement explaining the scope of the operation.

Activists allege assault and harassment

Civil rights groups and local activists have condemned the drive, saying that the detentions were carried out in a heavy-handed manner. Several activists claim that individuals were rounded up without proper verification of their documents and that some were allegedly manhandled or beaten during the process.

"We are seeing a pattern of targeting vulnerable migrants, often without any prior notice or due process," said a social worker involved in the relief efforts, who spoke on condition of anonymity. The allegations have sparked protests outside a few police stations, with demonstrators demanding a probe into the conduct of the officers involved.

The police, for their part, have maintained that the operation was a routine exercise to verify the credentials of migrants, many of whom come from other states in search of work. However, they have not yet responded to the specific accusations of assault, and repeated attempts to reach senior officers for comment were unsuccessful at the time of writing.

Legal questions and migrant concerns

The drive has also raised legal questions, with experts pointing out that the term "illegal migrant" is not clearly defined under Indian law, which could leave room for arbitrary enforcement. Many of those detained are daily-wage workers, construction labourers, and domestic helps, who often lack formal documentation like Aadhaar cards or rental agreements.

"The law requires that any detention be backed by a valid reason and that the person be produced before a magistrate promptly," noted a legal aid lawyer familiar with the case. "If those procedures were not followed, the detentions could be challenged in court."

Reports suggest that some of the detained individuals have already been released after questioning, while others remain in custody. The exact number of people still held is unclear, and authorities have not disclosed how many have been formally charged or referred for further action.

Impact on migrant workers

The crackdown has created an atmosphere of fear among migrant communities in Bengaluru, many of whom rely on informal networks for jobs and housing. Some workers have reportedly gone into hiding, while others are avoiding public spaces, fearing that they could be picked up in the next round of checks.

Local NGOs have set up helplines to assist those affected, offering legal aid and help with document verification. They are also documenting cases of alleged excesses, which they plan to submit to the National Human Rights Commission if the situation demands.

The episode is reminiscent of similar drives in other cities, but the scale and swiftness of the action here have caught many off guard. It remains to be seen whether the police will issue a detailed clarification or whether the matter will escalate into a larger confrontation between the authorities and civil society groups.

As the city absorbs the fallout, all eyes are now on the police commissioner's office, which is expected to release a statement in the coming days. The next few weeks will likely determine whether this crackdown is a one-off or a sign of a longer, more contentious campaign against migrants in the city.
