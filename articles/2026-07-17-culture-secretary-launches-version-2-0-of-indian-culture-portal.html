Culture Secretary Launches Version 2.0 of Indian Culture Portal

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Version 2.0 of the Indian Culture Portal was launched by Secretary Vivek Aggarwal.
  • The portal aims to provide wider access to India's cultural and historical resources.
  • The upgrade includes new features and enhanced content for users.

New Delhi, March 5: The Ministry of Culture took a significant step forward in preserving and promoting India's heritage on Thursday. Secretary Shri Vivek Aggarwal officially launched Version 2.0 of the Indian Culture Portal.

A Digital Gateway to Heritage

The Indian Culture Portal serves as a digital repository. It collects and displays artefacts, manuscripts, and traditions from across the country. Version 2.0 builds on the original platform with improved navigation and a wider range of materials.

Officials say the upgraded portal will make it easier for students, researchers, and the general public to explore India's vast cultural landscape. The platform aggregates content from multiple sources, including museums, archives, and academic institutions.

New Features and Accessibility

The new version introduces a more intuitive search function. Users can now filter results by era, region, or medium. The portal also supports multiple Indian languages, broadening its reach.

Another key addition is a dedicated section for intangible heritage. This includes folk music, dance forms, and oral traditions that are often difficult to document. The Ministry hopes this will help preserve practices that are at risk of being lost.

The launch event was held at a cultural centre in the capital. Aggarwal highlighted the importance of technology in safeguarding heritage. He noted that the portal aligns with the government's digital India initiative.

Expanding the Digital Archive

Version 2.0 adds thousands of new entries to the database. These range from ancient palm-leaf manuscripts to contemporary art installations. The Ministry has partnered with state cultural departments to source regional content.

Users can also access high-resolution images and 3D scans of rare artefacts. This allows for virtual tours of collections that are otherwise inaccessible. The portal is free to use and does not require registration for basic browsing.

The Ministry of Culture plans to update the portal regularly. It aims to make it a comprehensive resource for Indian culture, history, and arts. Future updates may include interactive timelines and educational modules.

Reception and Impact

Cultural scholars have welcomed the launch. They see it as a step towards democratising access to heritage. The portal could become a vital tool for academic research and public education.

However, some experts caution that digital preservation must be paired with physical conservation. They argue that the portal should not replace efforts to protect original artefacts. The Ministry says both approaches are complementary.

The portal is accessible from any device with an internet connection. It is designed to be mobile-friendly, catering to India's large smartphone user base. This aligns with the government's push for digital inclusion.

Moving forward, the Ministry will focus on adding more content and refining the user experience. Officials have not yet confirmed a timeline for the next major update. But they say user feedback will guide future improvements.
