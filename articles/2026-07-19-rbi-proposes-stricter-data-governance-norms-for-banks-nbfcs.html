RBI proposes stricter data governance norms for banks, NBFCs

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI draft norms require lenders to appoint a chief data officer and set up a data governance committee.
  • Proposals mandate classification of data as critical or non-critical with specific storage and retention rules.
  • Public comments on the draft are invited until August 31, 2026.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has proposed a fresh set of data governance norms for banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs), seeking to tighten the rules around how these lenders collect, store, and manage customer data. The draft circular, released on July 16, 2026, comes amid growing concerns over data breaches and the misuse of personal financial information.

The central bank wants financial institutions to bring in more accountability and transparency. Under the proposed framework, every bank and NBFC will have to appoint a chief data officer (CDO) who will be responsible for overseeing data management practices. The CDO will report directly to the board or a board-level committee.

New roles, new responsibilities

The draft mandates the creation of a data governance committee at the board level. This committee will approve the institution's data governance policy and ensure it aligns with the RBI's guidelines. Lenders will also need to maintain a comprehensive inventory of all data assets, including customer data, transaction records, and operational data.

The rules require financial entities to classify data into two categories: critical data and non-critical data. Critical data includes personally identifiable information (PII), financial transaction data, and any information that could affect a customer's privacy or the stability of the financial system. Non-critical data covers aggregated or anonymized information used for business analytics.

Storage and retention rules tighten

The RBI has proposed clear guidelines on where and how long data should be stored. Critical data must be stored within India, and any processing or storage abroad will need prior approval from the central bank. Non-critical data can be stored abroad, but lenders must have a clear policy on data residency and access controls.

Retention periods have also been specified. Customer data must be retained for at least five years after the closure of the account or the end of the business relationship. After that, lenders must delete or anonymise the data in a way that prevents re-identification. The draft circular warns that non-compliance could invite penalties under the Banking Regulation Act and the RBI Act.

Why the move matters

The proposed norms are part of the RBI's broader push to strengthen the financial sector's digital infrastructure. In recent years, multiple data leaks have hit Indian banks and NBFCs, exposing customer details such as account numbers, phone numbers, and addresses. The central bank has flagged these incidents repeatedly in its financial stability reports.

Industry experts say the draft rules could increase compliance costs for smaller lenders. But they also note that the framework brings Indian financial data governance closer to global standards like the GDPR. The RBI has not yet specified a timeline for implementation, but the draft suggests a phased rollout over 12 to 18 months after the final circular.

What happens next

The RBI has invited comments from stakeholders, including banks, NBFCs, and industry bodies. The deadline for submissions is August 31, 2026. After reviewing the feedback, the central bank will issue the final circular, which will be binding on all regulated entities. Lenders will then have to comply within the stipulated timeline or face regulatory action.
