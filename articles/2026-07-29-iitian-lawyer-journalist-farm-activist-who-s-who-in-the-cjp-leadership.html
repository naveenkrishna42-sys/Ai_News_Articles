IITian, lawyer, journalist, farm activist: Who's who in the CJP leadership

  • The movement's leadership includes a lawyer, a journalist and a farm activist, each bringing distinct expertise.
  • Dipke has warned the Centre that the youth will change the government if it doesn't 'fall in line'.
  • He has also taken a swipe at PM Modi, suggesting he step down and become an influencer.

A four-pronged leadership

The Centre for Justice and Peace (CJP) movement, which has been grabbing headlines across the country, is not the work of a single individual. It is steered by a four-member leadership team that brings diverse backgrounds to the table: an IITian, a lawyer, a journalist and a farm activist.

Each of them has carved a distinct role in the movement, which has been demanding action on issues ranging from student rights to anti-paper leak legislation.

Abhijeet Dipke: The IITian founder

Abhijeet Dipke, the founder of CJP, is an IIT graduate. He has been the most visible face of the movement. In recent weeks, he has made strong statements directed at the central government.

“Students are not terrorists, they are the future,” Dipke said, urging the government to stop targeting protesters. He has also issued a warning: “Youth will change the government if it doesn’t fall in line.”

Dipke has not held back his criticism of Prime Minister Narendra Modi either. In a recent remark, he questioned the anti-paper leak bill and suggested that Modi should “leave the chair and become an influencer.”

The lawyer and the journalist

The legal brain of the operation is a lawyer who has been advising the movement on procedural and constitutional matters. While the lawyer’s name has not been widely publicised, sources within CJP say the individual has been handling the legal strategy for their protests and public outreach.

The journalist in the leadership team brings media and communication expertise. This person has been helping the movement craft its messaging and navigate the news cycle, ensuring that their demands reach a wider audience.

The farm activist’s role

The farm activist in the group has roots in the recent farmers’ protests that shook the country. This activist has been linking the CJP’s current demands with the larger agrarian distress that continues to simmer.

The combination of these four backgrounds — technology, law, media and farming — has given the CJP a broad appeal. It allows the movement to speak to students, legal experts, the press and rural communities all at once.

What happens next

The CJP leadership has indicated that it will continue to pressure the government on the anti-paper leak bill and other student-related issues. With Dipke’s warnings of electoral consequences, all eyes are now on how the Centre responds to this multi-pronged challenge.
