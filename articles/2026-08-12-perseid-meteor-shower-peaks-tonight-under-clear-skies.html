Perseid meteor shower peaks tonight under clear skies

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Perseid meteor shower peaks tonight with up to 100 meteors per hour
  • Perfect clear skies expected across most of India for optimal viewing
  • NASA and other agencies offer live streams for those unable to go outside

A Night of Shooting Stars

The Perseid meteor shower, one of the most anticipated celestial events of the year, reaches its peak tonight. Skywatchers across India are in for a treat as forecasters predict largely clear skies, offering ideal conditions for viewing this annual spectacle.

The shower, caused by debris from the comet Swift-Tuttle, has been active since mid-July. But tonight marks the peak, when Earth passes through the densest part of the debris stream, producing the highest number of meteors.

Under perfect conditions, observers can expect to see up to 100 meteors per hour, streaking across the night sky at speeds of nearly 60 kilometres per second.

Where and When to Look

The best viewing time is after midnight, when the constellation Perseus, the radiant point of the shower, rises high in the northeastern sky. However, meteors can appear anywhere in the sky, so find a dark spot away from city lights and let your eyes adjust for at least 20 minutes.

No special equipment is needed — just a comfortable chair, a blanket, and patience. The shower will remain active for several nights after the peak, though with fewer meteors.

NASA is hosting a live stream of the Perseids for those unable to venture outdoors. The stream, available on NASA's website and social media channels, features a camera view of the night sky from a dark-sky location.

Meanwhile, the UAE Astronomy Center has addressed media reports about four phenomena occurring on August 12. The centre clarified that while the Perseids are indeed peaking, some reported events are either misidentified or not visible from certain regions.

Khaleej Times has also published a guide on when and where to catch a glimpse of the Perseids, noting that the best views are from rural locations with minimal light pollution.

Viewing Tips for Indian Skywatchers

For those in India, the ideal time to step out is between 2 AM and dawn, when the sky is darkest and the radiant point is highest. The moon, which is in its waning crescent phase, will not interfere much with meteor visibility.

Urban dwellers may struggle to see fainter meteors due to light pollution. If possible, drive to the outskirts of the city or a nearby hill station. Even a moderate reduction in artificial light can dramatically improve the view.

Patience is key. Meteor showers often come in spurts, with lulls followed by bursts of activity. Give yourself at least an hour of observation time to catch a good number of meteors.

What to Expect in the Coming Nights

While tonight is the peak, the Perseids will continue to produce meteors for the next week or so, though in gradually decreasing numbers. The next major celestial event to watch for is the Draconid meteor shower in October, which is known for occasional outbursts.

For now, the focus remains on tonight's display. With clear skies predicted across much of the subcontinent, this could be one of the best Perseid showings in recent years. Set your alarms, find a dark spot, and look up.
