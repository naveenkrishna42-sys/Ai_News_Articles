India's Gen Z Unemployment Fuels Anti-Modi Sentiment, Report Finds

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's Gen Z expresses anger over lack of jobs and learning opportunities.
  • Unemployment among educated youth in India is a growing concern.
  • The sentiment poses a political test for Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government.

Youth Discontent Rises Across India

A recent report by The New York Times has spotlighted a growing wave of frustration among India's Generation Z. The piece, titled 'We Are Not Learning Anything', captures the anger of young Indians who feel left behind by the country's education system and job market.

This demographic, born between the late 1990s and early 2010s, forms a significant chunk of India's population. Many are graduates or post-graduates, yet they struggle to find employment that matches their qualifications.

The Education-Employment Gap

The core issue, according to the report, is a mismatch between what students learn and what the job market demands. Technical skills, digital literacy, and practical knowledge are often missing from curricula.

Young people interviewed for the report expressed a sense of betrayal. They invested years in education only to find themselves underemployed or jobless. This has fueled a perception that the system is failing them.

A Political Challenge for Modi

The Times report frames this discontent as a direct test for Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government. For over a decade, the Bharatiya Janata Party has promised economic growth and job creation.

While India's economy has expanded, employment generation has not kept pace. The youth, who were once seen as Modi's core supporters, are now voicing their anger online and in public protests.

This sentiment is particularly sharp in states with high unemployment rates, such as Uttar Pradesh and Bihar. Social media platforms are flooded with memes and posts criticizing government policies.

Government's Response Under Scrutiny

The Modi administration has launched several schemes to boost skilling and entrepreneurship. Programs like Skill India and Startup India aim to create opportunities.

However, critics argue that these initiatives have not translated into enough formal jobs. The informal sector remains the largest employer, offering low wages and no security.

Data from the Centre for Monitoring Indian Economy shows that youth unemployment remains stubbornly high, hovering around 20-25% in recent quarters. The government has disputed these figures but has not offered alternative data.

What Comes Next

The upcoming state and general elections will be a key test. Political parties are already tailoring their campaigns to address youth concerns.

For now, the anger is palpable but not yet organized into a single political movement. Whether it translates into electoral losses for the BJP remains to be seen.
