India's Most Expensive Films Ever Made: Ramayana to RRR

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ramayana is reportedly the most expensive Indian film, with a budget of around Rs 1,000 crore.
  • Kalki 2898 AD, starring Prabhas, was made on a budget of approximately Rs 600 crore.
  • SS Rajamouli's RRR, with a budget of Rs 550 crore, remains one of the biggest grossers.
  • Films like 2.0, Brahmastra, and Adipurush also feature among India's most expensive productions.

Indian cinema has always been known for its scale, but in recent years, budgets have reached unprecedented heights. From mythological epics to futuristic sci-fi, filmmakers are spending staggering sums to create spectacles that rival global productions.

The Rising Cost of Indian Cinema

With theatrical releases making a strong comeback post-pandemic, producers are betting big on visual effects, star casts, and grand sets. The result is a list of films whose budgets would have seemed unthinkable a decade ago.

Industry trackers now routinely estimate budgets in the range of Rs 300 crore to Rs 1,000 crore, a far cry from the modest costs of the 1990s and 2000s. This shift reflects both inflation and the growing ambition of Indian filmmakers.

Ramayana: The Costliest of Them All

At the top of the list is the upcoming Ramayana, starring Ranbir Kapoor and Sai Pallavi. Reports peg its budget at around Rs 1,000 crore, making it the most expensive Indian film ever made. The mythological epic, directed by Nitesh Tiwari, has been in production for several years and features elaborate visual effects.

The film's massive budget is driven by its grand scale, including extensive VFX work and high-profile cameos, with reports suggesting appearances by actors like Yash and Sunny Deol. While official figures have not been confirmed, the buzz around the project has already made it one of the most anticipated releases.

Kalki 2898 AD and RRR: Big Budgets, Big Returns

Nag Ashwin's Kalki 2898 AD, starring Prabhas, Amitabh Bachchan, and Deepika Padukone, reportedly cost around Rs 600 crore. The sci-fi epic, set in a post-apocalyptic world, combined Indian mythology with futuristic technology, and its box office performance justified the investment.

SS Rajamouli's RRR, which became a global phenomenon, was made on a budget of approximately Rs 550 crore. The period action drama, featuring Jr NTR and Ram Charan, went on to gross over Rs 1,200 crore worldwide, cementing its place as one of India's most successful films.

Other Heavyweights in the List

Shankar's 2.0, starring Rajinikanth and Akshay Kumar, was among the earlier big-budget spectacles, with a reported budget of Rs 543 crore. The film's VFX-heavy sequences were a major draw.

Brahmastra: Part One – Shiva, Ranbir Kapoor's fantasy trilogy launch, cost around Rs 410 crore. Adipurush, the controversial retelling of the Ramayana, was made on a budget of about Rs 400 crore. Other notable mentions include RRR's predecessor Baahubali 2, which was made on Rs 250 crore, and the upcoming projects like Rajinikanth's Jailer 2 and Prabhas's Spirit, which are expected to cross the Rs 500-crore mark.

What's Driving These Budgets?

Industry experts point to the rising cost of star fees, advanced visual effects, and the need to create content that works on a global streaming platform. With OTT releases now being a significant revenue stream, producers are willing to spend more to attract viewers.

However, not every big-budget film has succeeded. Adipurush, despite its massive budget, faced backlash and underperformed at the box office. This has made producers more cautious, even as they continue to push the envelope.

As Indian cinema enters an era of mega-budgets, the coming years will show whether these investments pay off. With several high-profile projects in the pipeline, the race to create the next blockbuster spectacle is only getting more intense.
