US imposes 15% tariff on solar cells, modules; India not exempt

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Additional 15% US tariff on solar cells and modules announced.
  • India not granted any carve-out or exemption in the order.
  • Measure part of broader US actions targeting Chinese solar supply chain.

The United States has slapped an additional 15% tariff on imported solar cells and modules, a move that will directly impact Indian solar manufacturers who have increasingly looked to the American market for growth. The executive order, signed by President Donald Trump, contains no specific carve-out or exemption for India, despite New Delhi's active diplomatic efforts to secure preferential treatment.

No exemption for India despite trade talks

The White House order, issued on Thursday, imposes the tariff on top of existing duties and is part of a broader protectionist push aimed at curbing Chinese dominance in renewable energy supply chains. Indian industry bodies had lobbied for an exclusion, pointing to the country's status as a strategic partner and its growing manufacturing base under the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme.

However, officials familiar with the matter said the final text of the order makes no mention of India or any other country-specific exemptions. This effectively leaves Indian solar exporters facing the full brunt of the new levy, which will apply to both crystalline silicon cells and thin-film modules.

Impact on Indian solar exporters

India exported solar cells and modules worth over $1 billion to the US in the last fiscal year, with several domestic players ramping up capacity to serve American buyers. The additional tariff will raise the cost of Indian-made panels in the US market, potentially eroding their price competitiveness against Chinese products that already face steep duties.

Industry analysts said the move could force Indian manufacturers to absorb the tariff hit or pass it on to US customers, either way affecting order volumes. Some companies may accelerate plans to set up US-based assembly facilities to bypass the import levy, though such investments take time and capital.

Broader US-China trade offensive

The solar tariff is part of a series of trade actions unveiled by the Trump administration this week, including a separate 15% tariff on polysilicon, a key raw material for solar panels and semiconductors. The measures are designed to reduce American reliance on Chinese supply chains for critical clean energy inputs, according to a White House fact sheet.

Polysilicon prices have been volatile due to oversupply from Chinese producers, and the US has now imposed both tariffs and price floors on the material. This is expected to raise costs for American solar manufacturers but also encourage domestic production of the mineral.

What India can do next

New Delhi has several options, including approaching the World Trade Organization or negotiating a bilateral agreement with Washington. The US-India trade dialogue, which was resumed earlier this year, could provide a platform for discussions on the solar tariff, though no immediate talks have been announced.

Indian solar companies are also exploring alternative markets in Europe, Southeast Asia and Africa to reduce their dependence on the US. The government's PLI scheme, which aims to boost domestic manufacturing capacity to 50 GW, could help cushion the blow by making Indian products more cost-competitive globally in the long run.

For now, the immediate impact will be felt by exporters with existing US contracts, who must decide whether to renegotiate prices or absorb the losses. The tariff takes effect within 30 days of the order's publication in the Federal Register.

The coming weeks will reveal whether New Delhi can secure any relief through diplomatic channels, or whether Indian solar firms will have to pivot their export strategies entirely.
