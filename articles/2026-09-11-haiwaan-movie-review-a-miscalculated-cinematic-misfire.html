Haiwaan Movie Review: A Miscalculated Cinematic Misfire

An Ill-Fated Collaboration on the Silver Screen

When marquee names like Akshay Kumar, Saif Ali Khan, and veteran filmmaker Priyadarshan join forces, cinematic expectations naturally soar. Unfortunately, their latest venture, Haiwaan, shatters those expectations with a narrative that feels profoundly outdated. As industry analysts at TIVRA News evaluate the commercial and artistic fallout, it is evident that this project represents a significant misstep for everyone involved.

Priyadarshan, historically celebrated for masterclass comedies and tight thrillers, relies heavily on archaic cinematic grammar here. The film attempts to straddle multiple genres—blending slapstick with supposed high-stakes drama—resulting in tonal whiplash. Audiences paying premium ticket prices in 2026 demand modern storytelling structures, yet Haiwaan traps its iconic leads in a time warp.

Performance Analysis and Star Power Deficit

Akshay Kumar and Saif Ali Khan possess undeniable on-screen chemistry, a dynamic proven by their past blockbusters. In Haiwaan, however, their performances feel markedly phoned-in, weighed down by lazy writing and uninspired dialogue delivery. Kumar's comic timing appears fatigued, while Khan struggles to find any psychological depth in a poorly sketched character arc.

Supporting characters fare little better, reduced to caricatures that rely on regressive tropes rather than genuine wit. The lack of creative synergy between the director's vision and the actors' execution drains the film of vital energy. Consequently, the runtime drags, testing the patience of even the most dedicated fans.

Critical Highlights and Commercial Implications

The financial viability of star-driven vehicles relies on strong opening weekends and positive word-of-mouth. Haiwaan currently faces a steep uphill battle on both fronts, with critical consensus pointing to a severe deficiency in script quality. Below are the key real-world takeaways regarding the film's theatrical run:

  • Dated directorial style that fails to resonate with contemporary multiplex audiences
  • Subpar writing that wastes the combined talent of Kumar and Khan
  • Weak box office momentum driven by lukewarm initial reviews
  • Missed opportunity to revitalize the classic actor-director collaboration format

Final Verdict on Haiwaan

Cinematic authorities must call out wasted potential when it hits the big screen. Haiwaan serves as a cautionary tale about relying solely on legacy star power rather than investing in robust screenplays. Both Akshay Kumar and Saif Ali Khan should have critically evaluated the script before signing on. For moviegoers, skipping this release is the most sensible option.
