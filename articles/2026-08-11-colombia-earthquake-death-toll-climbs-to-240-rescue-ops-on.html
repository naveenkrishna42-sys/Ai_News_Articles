Colombia Earthquake Death Toll Climbs To 240, Rescue Ops On

📅 2026-08-11 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Death toll from the 7.4-magnitude earthquake in Colombia reaches 240
  • Rescuers continue to search for survivors amid widespread destruction
  • International leaders, including China's Xi Jinping, express condolences

Bogota, Colombia — The death toll from the powerful 7.4-magnitude earthquake that struck Colombia has climbed to 240, according to the latest official figures. The quake, which hit on Tuesday, has left widespread devastation in its wake, with rescue teams scrambling through rubble in a desperate bid to find survivors.

Authorities have confirmed that the number of casualties is likely to rise further as search-and-rescue operations continue in the hardest-hit areas. The earthquake, one of the strongest to hit the region in recent years, has flattened buildings, disrupted power lines, and triggered landslides in mountainous zones.

Rescue Efforts Intensify Amid Challenging Conditions

Emergency crews, aided by military personnel and volunteers, are working around the clock. Heavy machinery has been deployed to clear debris, but aftershocks are hampering progress. In several towns, rescuers are using bare hands and shovels to reach trapped residents, hoping against hope to pull more people out alive.

Local hospitals are overwhelmed, with medical staff treating hundreds of injured. Field hospitals have been set up in open grounds to accommodate the influx of patients. The government has appealed for blood donations and medical supplies as the scale of the disaster becomes clearer.

International Condolences Pour In

World leaders have begun reaching out to Colombia. China's President Xi Jinping extended his condolences to Colombian President Gustavo Petro, expressing solidarity with the affected families. Similar messages have come from across Latin America and beyond, with offers of aid and technical assistance.

The United Nations and several humanitarian agencies have also pledged support. Relief materials, including food, water, and temporary shelters, are being airlifted to remote communities cut off by damaged roads.

Deadliest Quake In Latin America's Recent Memory

This earthquake is now being compared to some of the deadliest in Latin America's history. While the region has seen larger quakes, the high population density in the affected areas has amplified the impact. Experts point to fragile infrastructure and unplanned urban expansion as key factors worsening the disaster.

Colombia sits on several tectonic fault lines, making it prone to seismic activity. However, a quake of this magnitude has not been recorded in the country in decades, catching many off guard.

Survivors Struggle To Cope

Thousands of people have been left homeless, camping in parks and open fields. Many are in shock, mourning lost family members while grappling with the destruction of their homes. Local communities have organised makeshift kitchens, distributing food and water to the displaced.

Authorities have urged residents to stay away from damaged structures, warning that aftershocks could cause further collapse. Schools and public buildings have been converted into temporary shelters, though space remains scarce.

Meanwhile, the government has announced a preliminary reconstruction plan, but officials admit that the full extent of the damage is still being assessed. The economic cost is expected to run into billions of dollars, straining an already beleaguered national budget.

As the search for survivors continues, the focus is also shifting to the long road to recovery. International aid is arriving, but the logistical challenges are immense. The coming days will be critical in determining whether the death toll stabilises or continues to rise.

For now, the world watches as Colombia mourns its dead and clings to hope for the missing. The rescuers, exhausted but determined, push forward, knowing that every hour could mean the difference between life and death.
