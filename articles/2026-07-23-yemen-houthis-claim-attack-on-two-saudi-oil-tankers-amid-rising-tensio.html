Yemen Houthis claim attack on two Saudi oil tankers amid rising tensions

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Houthis claim responsibility for attacking two Saudi oil tankers.
  • Oil prices surged following the reported attacks in the Red Sea.
  • US Secretary of State Rubio says Iran is not ready to make a deal.
  • New threat risks widening conflict and putting another trade chokepoint at risk.

Houthis Claim Strikes on Saudi Vessels

Yemen's Houthi group has claimed responsibility for attacks on two Saudi oil tankers. The announcement was made on Thursday, sending ripples through global energy markets.

The group said the strikes targeted vessels in the Red Sea region. It did not provide immediate evidence of the attacks or specify the exact location or time.

Oil Markets React Sharply

Following the Houthi claim, oil prices surged in international trading. The benchmark Brent crude rose by several dollars as traders factored in potential supply disruptions from the Red Sea route.

The area is a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. Any sustained threat could force tankers to take longer, costlier routes around Africa.

The New York Times reported on the price spike, linking it directly to the attacks in the Red Sea. Analysts are watching for any signs of escalation by Saudi Arabia or its allies.

US and Iran Exchange Warnings

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio stated that Iran is not ready to make a deal. His comments came amid rising tensions following the Houthi attacks.

Former President Donald Trump said Iran will be held responsible for future Houthi attacks. The statement signals a potential hardening of the US stance toward Tehran.

CNBC reported that Rubio's remarks indicate a lack of progress in diplomatic efforts. The Houthi action could further complicate any backchannel talks.

Risk of a Wider Conflict

The Associated Press highlighted a new threat from the Houthis that could widen the ongoing conflict involving Iran. The group's ability to strike Saudi assets raises the stakes for all parties.

Another major trade chokepoint is now at risk, according to analysts. The Bab el-Mandeb strait, near Yemen, is a vital passage for oil and cargo ships.

Escalation could draw in more regional powers. The Houthis have previously targeted Saudi infrastructure with drones and missiles during the Yemen war.

There has been no immediate confirmation from Saudi authorities about the attacks. Officials have not yet released any statement on damage or casualties.

What to Watch Next

The coming days will show whether Saudi Arabia retaliates directly or seeks international action. Any disruption to Red Sea shipping could push oil prices higher, and the US response will be closely watched.
