Chennai hosts Singapenn marathon to spread cervical cancer awareness

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Singapenn marathon was held in Chennai to raise awareness about cervical cancer
  • The event aimed to promote early detection and prevention of the disease
  • Participants included survivors, healthcare workers and community members

A run for a cause

Chennai witnessed a spirited turnout on Sunday as the Singapenn marathon took to the streets, blending fitness with a critical health message. Organised to spread awareness about cervical cancer, the event saw hundreds of runners, walkers and supporters come together for a cause that remains one of the most preventable yet under-discussed cancers among women in India.

The marathon, which wound through parts of the city, was more than a test of endurance. For many participants, it was a personal statement—a chance to break the silence around a disease that claims thousands of lives every year despite being highly treatable when caught early.

Why cervical cancer needs attention

Cervical cancer is the second most common cancer among women in India, with a significant number of cases detected at advanced stages due to lack of screening and awareness. Medical experts have long stressed that regular Pap smears and HPV vaccinations can drastically reduce the risk, yet social stigma and misinformation continue to hinder progress.

Events like the Singapenn marathon serve a dual purpose: they raise funds for awareness campaigns and create a public platform where conversations about reproductive health can happen without embarrassment. Organisers said the response from the community, particularly from young women and college students, was encouraging.

Voices from the ground

Among the participants were survivors of cervical cancer, whose presence turned the marathon into a celebration of resilience. Many wore badges and carried placards with messages urging women to prioritise regular check-ups. Volunteers distributed informational pamphlets along the route, covering topics such as symptoms, risk factors and where to access free screening services in the city.

Healthcare professionals at the event offered on-the-spot counselling, answering questions that many attendees said they had never felt comfortable asking before. One volunteer noted that the marathon's informal setting made it easier for people to approach medical staff without the fear of judgement.

Community support and organisation

The event was supported by local NGOs, corporate sponsors and the Chennai police, who managed traffic and crowd control. Water stations, medical aid posts and rest areas were set up at regular intervals, ensuring a smooth experience for participants of all ages.

Organisers had earlier used social media to spread the word, and the response on the ground reflected that digital push. Families, school groups and senior citizens all took part, underscoring that cervical cancer awareness is not a women-only issue but a societal one.

What lies ahead

The Singapenn marathon is expected to become an annual fixture on Chennai's event calendar, with organisers already planning to expand its reach to other districts in Tamil Nadu. They are also in talks with public health authorities to set up more screening camps in underserved areas, where access to diagnostic facilities remains limited.

As the last runners crossed the finish line, the message of the day was clear: awareness is the first step, and action must follow. With continued community engagement and policy support, the hope is that fewer women will face a late-stage diagnosis in the years to come.
