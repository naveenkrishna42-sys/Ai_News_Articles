18-storey Bengaluru apartment building to be demolished

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • An 18-storey building in a Bengaluru apartment complex is set for demolition.
  • The decision was taken due to structural safety concerns.
  • Residents are being relocated; the timeline for demolition is yet to be finalised.

An 18-storey building in a Bengaluru apartment complex is set to be demolished. Officials confirmed the decision on Thursday, citing structural safety concerns that made the building unfit for habitation.

The building is part of a larger residential complex in the city. Authorities have not yet disclosed the name of the complex or the exact location, but sources indicate it is in the southern part of Bengaluru.

Structural concerns led to the decision

The Bengaluru civic body, along with a team of structural engineers, conducted an inspection of the building earlier this month. The report flagged serious deficiencies in the building's load-bearing capacity and foundation stability.

“The structure poses a clear risk to residents. There is no option but to bring it down,” a senior official said, speaking on condition of anonymity. The official added that the decision was taken after multiple rounds of testing and review.

Residents to be relocated

Around 100 families currently live in the building. The apartment complex management has been asked to arrange alternative accommodation for them. The civic body will oversee the relocation process to ensure no family is left without shelter.

Some residents had complained of cracks in walls and ceilings over the past year. Complaints were filed with the management, but no action was taken until the recent inspection.

Demolition timeline unclear

Officials have not yet set a date for the demolition. A detailed plan for bringing down the 18-storey structure is being prepared. Given the height of the building and its location within a dense residential area, the demolition will require careful execution.

Experts say controlled implosion is the most likely method. The complex's proximity to other buildings means debris fall and vibration will need to be tightly managed. The civic body is expected to release a timeline in the coming weeks.

This is not the first such case in Bengaluru. In recent years, several buildings have been flagged as unsafe, leading to demolition orders. The city’s rapid urbanisation and lax enforcement of building codes have been blamed for the problem.

The apartment complex management has been directed to submit a structural stability certificate for all other buildings in the complex. A failure to do so could lead to further action.

Authorities are also examining whether any lapses occurred during the original construction of the building. If negligence is found, legal action against the builder is possible.

The next few weeks will be critical as the relocation process unfolds and the demolition plan is finalised. Residents are expected to cooperate with the authorities to ensure a smooth transition.
