Delhi High Court orders takedown of 150+ URLs targeting actress Tabu

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi High Court grants interim relief to actress Tabu in personality rights plea.
  • Court directs removal of 150+ URLs hosting obscene, AI-generated content.
  • Order restrains misuse of Tabu's name and image on digital platforms.

The Delhi High Court on Monday directed the removal of over 150 online links containing derogatory and obscene content targeting veteran actress Tabu. The interim order came in response to a plea filed by the actress seeking protection of her personality rights against misuse of her name and image, particularly through AI-generated material.

Justice Navin Chawla, hearing the matter, restrained unnamed entities from creating, hosting, or distributing any content that misuses Tabu's identity. The court's directive covers a wide range of digital platforms, including social media channels, video-streaming sites, and websites hosting user-generated content.

AI-generated content under scrutiny

The case highlights the growing concern over deepfake and AI-manipulated media in India. Tabu's plea specifically flagged obscene and defamatory content that was allegedly created using artificial intelligence tools, which superimposed her face onto inappropriate material. Such content, the actress argued, not only tarnishes her reputation but also violates her right to control her own image in the digital space.

The court's order is among a series of recent rulings in India where celebrities have sought legal recourse against online misuse of their identities. The entertainment industry has been increasingly vocal about the need for stricter enforcement of personality rights, especially as AI tools become more accessible.

Platforms directed to act

The High Court directed online platforms to take down the specified URLs within a set timeframe. While the order does not name the platforms individually, it applies broadly to any intermediary hosting the offending content. Failure to comply could result in contempt proceedings, the court warned.

Legal experts note that this interim relief is a significant step in establishing a precedent for personality rights in the digital age. Unlike copyright or trademark, personality rights in India are not codified in a standalone statute, making such judicial orders crucial for celebrities seeking protection.

Tabu's legal battle

Tabu, known for her roles in films like Maqbool, Haider, and Andhadhun, has maintained a relatively private public profile despite her long career. The actress approached the court after discovering that explicit and AI-altered content featuring her likeness was circulating online, allegedly without her consent.

The court's order also restrains any third party from using her name, photographs, or voice in a manner that could be construed as endorsements or defamatory, without prior authorisation. This broader injunction goes beyond the specific URLs listed in the plea, offering Tabu comprehensive interim protection.

What lies ahead

The matter is listed for further hearing, with the court expected to consider a permanent injunction and possibly frame guidelines for intermediaries to prevent such misuse in the future. As AI-generated content becomes harder to detect, this case could push for more proactive measures by platforms.

For now, Tabu has secured a legal shield against the immediate harm caused by the offending URLs. The next hearing will determine whether the injunction becomes permanent, and whether the court will lay down broader rules for how digital platforms handle personality rights violations.
