Ebola outbreak cripples Bunia's economy and public health systems

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ebola death toll in DR Congo surpasses 2,000, with Bunia among the hardest-hit cities.
  • Bunia's economy falters as markets, transport, and cross-border trade grind to a halt.
  • WHO and Africa CDC call for urgent, community-led containment measures.
  • Health facilities in Bunia struggle with overwhelmed capacity and infection control challenges.

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo has now claimed more than 2,000 lives, with the northeastern city of Bunia bearing some of the heaviest burdens. The virus, which has spread through dense urban and rural communities, is not just a public health emergency — it is also dismantling the city's economic fabric.

Local traders report a sharp drop in customers. Markets that once hummed with activity now sit half-empty, as fear keeps people indoors. Transport routes into and out of Bunia have thinned, with many drivers refusing to carry passengers to affected zones. Cross-border trade with neighbouring Uganda, a lifeline for many households, has slowed to a trickle.

Economic fallout deepens in Bunia

Bunia's economy, already fragile, is now buckling under the weight of the outbreak. Small businesses — the backbone of the city — are recording losses that many may not survive. Shopkeepers say they have not seen such a prolonged downturn in years.

The hospitality sector is similarly hit. Guesthouses that once catered to traders and aid workers now report near-empty rooms. One hotel manager, speaking on condition of anonymity, said bookings had fallen by more than half since the outbreak intensified.

With movement restrictions in place and public gatherings discouraged, informal workers — from taxi drivers to street vendors — are struggling to make ends meet. Many have no savings to fall back on, and aid agencies warn of a looming food security crisis in the coming months.

Health system under strain

On the public health front, Bunia's hospitals and clinics are overwhelmed. Health workers are grappling with rising caseloads, dwindling supplies, and the constant risk of infection. Several medical staff have themselves fallen ill, further thinning an already stretched workforce.

Infection prevention and control measures, including safe burial practices and contact tracing, remain critical. But health officials say community mistrust continues to hamper efforts. Some residents are reluctant to seek treatment early, fearing stigma or the unknown.

The World Health Organization (WHO) and the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention (Africa CDC) have issued a joint call for urgent, community-led action. They stress that top-down measures alone will not suffice — local leaders, religious figures, and youth groups must be enlisted to rebuild trust and encourage safe practices.

Regional risk and international response

There is growing concern that the outbreak could spread beyond the DRC's borders. Bunia's proximity to Uganda and South Sudan makes cross-border transmission a real threat. Neighbouring countries have stepped up screening at points of entry, but porous borders remain a challenge.

International agencies have pledged additional support, including mobile laboratories and vaccination teams. But the response has been hampered by insecurity in parts of the region, as well as logistical hurdles in reaching remote communities.

The WHO has warned that this outbreak is on track to become the deadliest on record. The death toll has already surpassed 2,000, and the numbers continue to climb.

What lies ahead

In the coming weeks, the focus will be on scaling up community engagement and ensuring that vaccines and treatments reach those most at risk. The response will depend as much on winning hearts and minds as on medical intervention.

For Bunia, the road to recovery will be long. Even as health workers battle the virus, the city's economy will need targeted support to rebuild. The next few months will be critical in determining whether the outbreak can be contained — and whether Bunia can begin to heal.
