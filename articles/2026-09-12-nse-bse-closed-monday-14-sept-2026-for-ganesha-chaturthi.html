NSE, BSE Closed Monday 14 Sept 2026 for Ganesha Chaturthi

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • NSE and BSE will observe a full trading holiday on Monday, 14 September 2026 for Ganesha Chaturthi, with no equity, F&O or currency segment sessions.
  • Monday's holiday creates a three-day market break when combined with the preceding weekend, leaving only four full trading sessions that week.
  • Trades executed on Friday, 11 September 2026 will settle on T+1 basis, with pay-in and pay-out timelines shifting to Tuesday, 15 September 2026.
  • Commodity derivatives on MCX follow a separate evening schedule even on exchange holidays; verify segment-specific timings before placing orders.

India's two primary stock exchanges, the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and the BSE, will remain closed on Monday, 14 September 2026, in observance of Ganesha Chaturthi. No trading will take place across the equity cash, equity derivatives, currency derivatives, or interest rate derivatives segments on that day. Both exchanges confirmed the closure as part of their published 2026 trading holiday calendar.

The break follows a full trading week ending Friday, 11 September 2026, and combines with the preceding weekend to give market participants a continuous three-day gap. For retail investors and institutional desks alike, that means liquidity planning must account for a compressed week with only four active sessions starting Tuesday, 15 September 2026.

What Remains Closed and What Does Not

A trading holiday is not the same as a banking holiday, and the distinction matters for anyone managing positions over the long weekend. Clearing corporations, depositories, and participating banks operate on reduced schedules, so funds movement can lag even when the exchange is technically shut.

Commodity traders should not assume a blanket shutdown. MCX generally runs its evening session even on exchange holidays, though timings and contract availability can vary. Confirm the day's circular before placing any order.

Settlement and Fund Flow Impact

Trades executed on Friday, 11 September 2026 follow the standard T+1 settlement cycle. Because Monday is a trading holiday, the pay-in and pay-out obligations for those trades shift to Tuesday, 15 September 2026. Investors who sold shares on Friday should expect credit to reflect on Tuesday, not Monday.

This has a practical consequence for anyone planning a Systematic Investment Plan debit, a margin top-up, or an IPO application around the long weekend. Bank processing on a public holiday can delay the appearance of funds even when the transaction is initiated on time. Keep a buffer day for any time-sensitive payment.

Why This Holiday Matters for Active Traders

Weekly F&O expiry falls on a fixed weekday, and a Monday closure compresses the number of sessions available before expiry. Traders holding leveraged positions over the weekend carry three days of gap risk instead of one, including exposure to global markets that remain open on Monday.

Global cues do not pause for Indian festivals. Overnight moves in US indices, crude oil, and the dollar index will be fully priced into the Tuesday opening. Stop-loss orders placed in the system will not execute during the holiday, so positions are effectively naked against weekend news flow.

Practical Checklist Before the Long Weekend

  • Pros of the break: A pause can cool overheated intraday momentum and give traders time to reassess positions without live price pressure.
  • Cons of the break: Three days of unhedged gap risk, delayed settlement credit, and thinner liquidity on the Tuesday reopen.
  • Margin check: Top up margins before Friday close to avoid penalty charges or forced square-off on Tuesday.
  • Settlement planning: Do not schedule critical payments assuming Monday fund credit.
  • Commodity watch: Verify MCX evening session timings separately; do not treat the equity holiday as a blanket closure.
  • Global cues: Track US and Asian markets on Monday to anticipate Tuesday's opening gap.

Full-Year Context for 2026

Ganesha Chaturthi is one of a fixed set of trading holidays declared annually by NSE and BSE. The two exchanges publish their calendars in advance, and the dates rarely diverge between them. Investors planning around festivals should bookmark the official circular rather than rely on forwarded messages, since unauthorised holiday lists circulate widely and often contain errors.

For the week beginning 14 September 2026, the market operates on Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday. That is four sessions to absorb global news, process settlement backlogs, and reposition ahead of the next expiry cycle. Plan the week accordingly.
