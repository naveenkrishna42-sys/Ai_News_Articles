RBI Proposes Tighter Data Governance Norms For Banks And NBFCs

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has floated a draft circular on data governance for regulated entities.
  • The norms aim to ensure data integrity, security, and standardisation.
  • The central bank has invited comments from stakeholders by August 17.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has proposed a fresh set of data governance norms for banks and non-banking financial companies (NBFCs). The draft circular, released on Friday, seeks to tighten the way these institutions handle, store, and process customer and financial data. The central bank has invited feedback from all stakeholders until August 17.

Why The RBI Is Moving On Data Governance

Data breaches and inconsistent reporting have been persistent concerns for the financial sector. The RBI has flagged instances where entities failed to maintain accurate data or faced security lapses. The proposed norms aim to standardise data management practices across the board. Regulated entities will have to appoint a Chief Data Officer (CDO) and set up a board-level committee to oversee data governance.

The circular covers all commercial banks, urban cooperative banks, NBFCs, and payment system operators. The RBI wants these entities to classify data based on sensitivity and implement access controls accordingly. The move is part of a broader push to align Indian financial sector practices with global standards.

Key Requirements Under The Draft Norms

Entities will need to maintain a data dictionary that defines every data element they use. The dictionary must be updated regularly. Banks and NBFCs must also ensure that data is accurate, complete, and consistent across all systems. The RBI has made it clear that data quality will be a non-negotiable parameter during inspections.

The circular mandates that all data be stored within India. For cross-border data flows, entities must obtain explicit approval from the RBI. The central bank is also pushing for a standardised data format to make reporting easier. The deadline for implementing these norms will be announced after the feedback period ends.

Industry Reactions And Challenges Ahead

Banking experts have broadly welcomed the move. They say consistent data governance will reduce compliance costs in the long run. However, smaller NBFCs may face teething troubles. Many of them lack the infrastructure to implement a full-fledged data governance framework quickly. The RBI has kept the feedback window open for a month, giving industry bodies time to submit their concerns.

Some bankers have pointed out that the cost of appointing a dedicated CDO and setting up a board committee could be significant for smaller entities. The RBI may have to provide a phased implementation timeline. The central bank has not yet indicated whether it will offer any relaxation for microfinance institutions or small finance banks.

What This Means For Customers

For the average customer, the immediate impact will be minimal. But in the long run, better data governance should mean fewer errors in credit reports, faster grievance redressal, and stronger protection against data theft. The RBI has also proposed stricter penalties for non-compliance. Entities that fail to meet the norms could face restrictions on expanding their business.

The timing of the draft circular is significant. With digital payments and online lending growing rapidly, the RBI wants to ensure that data is not misused. The circular is part of a series of regulatory measures aimed at strengthening the financial system's resilience.

The RBI will now wait for feedback from banks, NBFCs, and other stakeholders. Once the final circular is issued, entities will have a set timeline to comply. The industry will be watching closely to see if the central bank offers any concessions for smaller players.
