5 90s Bollywood Horror Films for Your Weekend Watchlist

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • The 1990s produced iconic Bollywood horror films that are still remembered.
  • These films often mix horror with comedy, drama, and music.
  • Viewers can enjoy titles like 'Raat', 'Purana Mandir', and 'Darna Mana Hai'.

If you are looking for a weekend binge that mixes nostalgia with a few good scares, look no further than Bollywood's horror offerings from the 1990s. That decade produced a unique breed of horror cinema — one that was unapologetically loud, often campy, and always entertaining.

These films did not rely on subtlety. They relied on creaking doors, sudden lightning flashes, and eerie songs that stayed with you long after the credits rolled. Here are five 90s Bollywood horror films that deserve a spot on your weekend watchlist.

Raat (1992)

Directed by Ram Gopal Varma, 'Raat' remains a landmark in Indian horror cinema. The film tells the story of a family haunted by a vengeful spirit after a tragedy. Varma used atmospheric lighting and sound design instead of heavy makeup or gore.

The result is a genuinely unsettling experience. Revathi delivers a strong performance as the tormented mother. The film's climax still holds up today. It is a masterclass in building tension without overdoing it.

Purana Mandir (1984, but cult status in 90s)

Though released in 1984, 'Purana Mandir' became a staple of 90s horror viewing through television broadcasts and home video. Directed by the Ramsay brothers, it is a classic tale of a cursed family and a monstrous creature.

The film is famous for its practical effects and the iconic monster, Saamri. It is over-the-top, loud, and features a memorable song. For anyone who grew up watching horror on Doordarshan, this is essential viewing. It captures the raw, unpolished energy of 80s and 90s Bollywood horror.

Darna Mana Hai (2003, but rooted in 90s style)

While technically released in 2003, 'Darna Mana Hai' carries the spirit of 90s horror anthologies. Directed by Prawaal Raman, it presents six short stories, each with a moral twist. The film features a young ensemble cast including Saif Ali Khan and Boman Irani.

Each story is a self-contained horror fable. The film is clever, often darkly humorous, and relies on psychological scares. It feels like a bridge between the 90s campy style and the more polished horror that came later. The segment about the hitchhiker is particularly effective.

Kaun? (1999)

Another Ram Gopal Varma film, 'Kaun?' is a psychological thriller that operates almost entirely within a single house. The plot is simple: a woman is home alone when a stranger knocks on her door during a storm. The cat-and-mouse game that follows is tense and unpredictable.

Urmila Matondkar delivers a career-best performance. The film uses minimal music and relies on dialogue and silence to create dread. It is a tight, 90-minute exercise in suspense. The ending is a genuine shocker. This is horror stripped down to its essentials.

Zakhmi Aurat (1988, but a 90s video hit)

This film, though from 1988, gained massive popularity in the early 90s through video rentals and cable TV. It is a revenge drama with horror elements. The story involves a woman who returns from the dead to seek vengeance against those who wronged her.

The film is notorious for its graphic violence and strong feminist themes. It is raw, unapologetic, and features a memorable performance by Dimple Kapadia. It is not a film for the faint-hearted, but it captures the angry, rebellious energy of late 80s and early 90s Indian cinema.

These five films offer a glimpse into a distinct era of Bollywood horror. They are products of their time — rough, loud, and often bizarre. But they are also deeply entertaining. This weekend, skip the polished new releases and revisit these cult classics. You might be surprised by how well they hold up, and how much fun they still are.
