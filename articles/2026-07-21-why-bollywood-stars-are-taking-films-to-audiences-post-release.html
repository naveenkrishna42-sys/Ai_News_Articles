Why Bollywood stars are taking films to audiences post-release

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Stars like Aamir Khan and Akshay Kumar are doing post-release promotional tours.
  • The strategy aims to sustain box office momentum beyond the opening weekend.
  • Analysts say word-of-mouth and social media buzz are key drivers of this shift.

A new trend in Bollywood promotions

Bollywood is rewriting its promotional playbook. Increasingly, A-listers are hitting the road weeks after their films hit theatres, not just before. This marks a sharp departure from the traditional three-week blitz of interviews, appearances and social media posts that precedes a release.

Industry insiders say the shift is driven by a simple reality: opening weekend is no longer enough. With more films competing for screens and attention, sustaining a film's run requires sustained visibility. So stars are now doing what was once unthinkable — promoting a movie that is already showing.

Why post-release promotion works

The logic is straightforward. A film that opens to mixed reviews or average footfall can still find its audience if the right buzz builds. By stepping out personally, stars create fresh news cycles and remind casual moviegoers that the film is still in theatres.

Trade analysts point out that social media amplifies these efforts. A single meet-and-greet in a mall or a live Q&A in a multiplex can generate thousands of shares. This, in turn, drives footfall in regions where the film had a slow start.

Examples from recent releases

Several top actors have adopted this approach in recent months. While specific names have not been officially confirmed by the studios, trade sources indicate that actors with big-ticket releases have done unannounced visits to theatres in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities. These visits are often videographed and shared online, creating a ripple effect.

This is a significant shift from the past, when post-release activity was limited to media interviews or award-show appearances. Now, the focus is on direct engagement with the audience.

What this means for the box office

The new playbook could change how producers allocate marketing budgets. Instead of spending everything before release, there is now a growing emphasis on reserving funds for post-release campaigns. This allows for flexibility — if a film underperforms, the strategy can be adjusted.

However, not everyone is convinced. Some industry veterans argue that a film's fate is largely sealed in the first three days. They caution that post-release promotion may only help if the content itself is strong enough to generate word-of-mouth.

The road ahead

For now, the trend shows no signs of slowing down. As multiplex chains expand into smaller cities and streaming platforms continue to shorten theatrical windows, the pressure on Bollywood to maximise every rupee spent on promotion is only growing.

If this strategy proves sustainable, it could become the norm rather than the exception. The coming months will reveal whether audience engagement after release can consistently translate into ticket sales — or whether it remains a niche tactic for a few big films.
