Bengaluru snaps 12,774 illegal water links, 1,325 sewer lines

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • A total of 12,774 illegal water connections and 1,325 sewer lines were disconnected.
  • The drive was carried out across Bengaluru to curb water theft and sewer misuse.
  • Officials warn of strict action, including fines, against repeat offenders.

In a major crackdown on water theft, the Bengaluru civic body has disconnected 12,774 illegal water connections and 1,325 sewer lines across the city. The drive, which concluded recently, is part of a broader effort to plug revenue leaks and ensure equitable distribution of water.

Officials said the illegal connections were detected during a city-wide survey conducted over the past few months. Many of these were found in residential areas, commercial establishments, and construction sites that had tapped into the water supply network without authorisation.

What the Drive Covered

The operation targeted unauthorised taps and sewer line connections that were either not registered or bypassed metering systems. In several cases, property owners had directly connected to the main water line, avoiding payment of user charges.

According to the Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board (BWSSB), the disconnections were carried out after multiple notices were served to the defaulters. The board has also identified areas where such illegal practices were rampant, and inspections are ongoing.

Impact on Revenue and Supply

Illegal water connections not only lead to financial losses for the utility but also put additional pressure on the already strained water supply system. By removing these unauthorised links, the board aims to improve supply to legitimate consumers and reduce wastage.

Officials noted that the disconnections would help in better demand management, especially during the summer months when water scarcity is acute. The civic body has also recovered pending dues from some defaulters during the drive.

Action Against Repeat Offenders

The BWSSB has warned that strict action will be taken against those who attempt to reconnect illegal lines. Repeat offenders could face fines, criminal prosecution, and even disconnection of legitimate water supply to the property.

Citizens have been urged to report any illegal connections or water theft to the board's helpline. The civic body has also launched awareness campaigns to educate residents about the penalties of unauthorised usage.

While the exact amount of water saved has not been quantified, officials estimate that the move will significantly reduce non-revenue water in the city. Bengaluru's water supply system has been under stress, and such measures are critical for sustainability.

What's Next

The civic body plans to carry out regular inspections and audits to prevent recurrence. It is also working on streamlining the process for new connections to discourage people from opting for illegal means.

Further drives are expected in the coming months, especially in areas with a history of high pilferage. Officials are also considering the use of technology, such as smart meters, to detect anomalies in water usage.
