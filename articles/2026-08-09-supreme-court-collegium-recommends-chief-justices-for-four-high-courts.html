Supreme Court Collegium Recommends Chief Justices for Four High Courts

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Collegium recommends Justice RV Ghuge for Calcutta High Court Chief Justice
  • Justice MC Tripathi recommended for Bombay High Court Chief Justice
  • Four high courts to get new chief justices; official notification awaited

The Supreme Court Collegium has recommended the appointment of chief justices for four high courts across the country. The recommendations, made public on Sunday, include elevations of sitting judges to lead the Calcutta, Bombay, and two other high courts.

The Collegium, headed by the Chief Justice of India, has proposed Justice R V Ghuge as the next Chief Justice of the Calcutta High Court. Justice M C Tripathi has been recommended for the Bombay High Court. Names for the remaining two high courts have also been forwarded to the government for approval.

Key Recommendations

Justice R V Ghuge, currently serving as a judge in a high court, has been recommended to take charge of the Calcutta High Court. His appointment is expected to fill the vacancy arising from the retirement or transfer of the incumbent chief justice.

Justice M C Tripathi has been recommended for the Bombay High Court. The Bombay High Court, one of the oldest and busiest in the country, has been without a permanent chief justice for several months.

The Collegium has also recommended chief justices for two other high courts, though the names have not been officially disclosed yet. Legal circles suggest that the appointments are part of a broader exercise to streamline judicial administration.

Process and Timeline

The recommendations have been sent to the Ministry of Law and Justice for processing. Once cleared, the President of India will issue formal appointment orders. Sources indicate that the process may take a few weeks, given the standard protocol.

The Collegium system, which consists of the Chief Justice of India and four senior-most judges of the Supreme Court, has been the method for appointing judges to the higher judiciary. These recommendations come amid ongoing discussions about judicial appointments and vacancies in high courts.

Impact on Judicial Work

The new chief justices are expected to address pendency and administrative issues in their respective courts. For instance, the Calcutta High Court has faced criticism over case backlogs, while the Bombay High Court deals with a heavy caseload, including commercial disputes and public interest litigations.

Lawyers and court staff in these high courts have welcomed the move, hoping that permanent chief justices will bring stability. Temporary arrangements, often handled by acting chief justices, have been in place in some of these courts for months.

The government has not yet issued a formal response, but the appointments are likely to be notified after the usual scrutiny. Officials have not confirmed the exact dates for the swearing-in ceremonies.

This development is part of a series of judicial appointments aimed at filling vacancies in high courts across India. As of now, several high courts are functioning without full strength, and these elevations are seen as a step toward addressing that gap.

Observers will be watching the government's response time, as delays in clearing Collegium recommendations have previously led to friction between the judiciary and the executive. The coming weeks will reveal whether these appointments proceed smoothly.
