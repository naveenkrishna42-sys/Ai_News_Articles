Bengaluru B-to-A Khata Rebate Window Closes: Only 12% of Beneficiaries Availed

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-05 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Only 80,412 of Bengaluru's ~6.7 lakh B-Khata properties applied for the rebate-based A-Khata conversion before the September 2026 deadline.
  • Eligible owners secured up to 50% remission on improvement charges, but the capped formula made the scheme unviable for many high-value properties.
  • Digital submission via the BBMP Sahaaya portal streamlined filings, yet legacy documentation gaps stalled the bulk of pending applicants.

Low Conversion Ratio Exposes Structural Flaws in BBMP's Khata Rebate Scheme

Bengaluru's much-publicised rebate window for converting B-Khata properties into A-Khata closed on the notified cut-off with a modest tally of 80,412 applications. According to civic data reviewed by The Hindu, this figure represents only about 12% of the roughly 6.7 lakh B-Khata parcels estimated across the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) limits. The outcome confirms long-standing concerns from resident welfare associations (RWAs) and Chartered Accountants that the rebate formula, capped at 50% of improvement charges, did not adequately incentivise large property owners.

Why the Uptake Stayed Below Expectations

Property tax consultants attribute the tepid response to three structural friction points. First, the rebate ceiling meant owners of high-value commercial buildings still faced seven-figure improvement charge demands after discount. Second, the mandatory submission of legacy documents such as the DC conversion order, Katha certificates from the previous owner, and the Encumbrance Certificate (EC) dating back 30 years created logistical bottlenecks. Third, the digital-first Sahaaya portal, while convenient for routine filings, lacked a guided workflow for legacy khata transfers, forcing applicants to make multiple counter visits to the BBMP ARO office.

Practical Implications for Property Owners

For buyers and sellers in Bengaluru's secondary property market, the closed window leaves a sharp bifurcation. The 80,412 successful applicants now hold clean A-Khata titles, unlocking institutional home loan eligibility, easier registration, and lower Circle-Ready Reckoner disputes. Conversely, the remaining ~5.9 lakh B-Khata parcels will continue to attract higher property tax (up to 3x the A-Khata rate in some zones), face hurdles in obtaining building plan approvals, and command reduced liquidity in the resale market. Commercial investors holding unsold inventory in B-Khata layouts should brace for continued negotiation leverage from buyers seeking steep discounts to offset the post-rebate compliance burden.

Key Real-World Highlights for Buyers and Investors

  • Successful applicants should download and archive the A-Khata certificate PDF from the Sahaaya portal and initiate mutation in the sub-registrar records within 90 days.
  • Applicants whose files remain pending should expect BBMP scrutiny on DC conversion and agricultural-to-residential land status before approval.
  • Prospective buyers must demand the B-Khata to A-Khata conversion history during due diligence, as legacy arrears can transfer with the title.
  • Real estate brokers anticipate a short-term softening of B-Khata asking prices by 8-15% in micro-markets like Whitefield, RR Nagar, and Yelahanka where conversion uptake was lowest.
  • Lenders such as SBI and HDFC already offer revised LTV ratios for properties cleared under this window, making A-Khata titles 10-20% more loan-eligible.

What Lies Ahead for the Remaining B-Khata Stock

BBMP officials have indicated that no further rebate window is on the anvil, signalling a return to the standard improvement-charge schedule. The civic body is expected to intensify property tax drives, biometric geo-tagging of defaulting parcels, and recovery notices in wards with low conversion ratios. Industry stakeholders, including CREDAI Bengaluru, are lobbying the state government for a tiered rebate structure that links the remission percentage to property valuation, which they argue would unlock broader participation in any future amnesty. Until such a framework is notified, the 88% of B-Khata owners who missed the window must factor the full statutory penalty, plus accumulated arrears, into their 2026-27 cash flow planning.
