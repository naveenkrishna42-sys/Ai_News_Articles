5 Key Facts About Extreme Weather Events in India

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Environment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Extreme weather events are becoming more frequent and intense due to climate change.
  • Heatwaves are among the deadliest extreme weather events globally.
  • Heavy rainfall and floods are increasing in many regions, including India.
  • Droughts are becoming more severe in some parts of the world.
  • Early warning systems and preparedness can save lives and reduce damage.

Heatwaves: The Silent Killers

Heatwaves are among the deadliest extreme weather events. They do not cause the dramatic destruction of a cyclone or flood, but they claim thousands of lives each year. In India, heatwaves have become more frequent and intense over the past decade.

Experts point to rising global temperatures as the primary driver. When heat and humidity combine, the human body struggles to cool itself. This can lead to heatstroke and death, particularly among the elderly and outdoor workers.

Heavy Rainfall and Flooding on the Rise

Climate change is also altering rainfall patterns. Many regions, including large parts of India, are experiencing heavier downpours in shorter periods. This increases the risk of flash floods and urban flooding.

In 2023, several Indian cities faced record-breaking rainfall. The result was widespread damage to property and infrastructure. Scientists warn that such events will become more common without significant emission reductions.

Droughts: A Growing Threat

While some areas get too much rain, others get too little. Droughts are becoming more severe and prolonged in several parts of the world. In India, agricultural regions are particularly vulnerable.

Prolonged dry spells can lead to crop failure and water scarcity. This affects millions of farmers and their families. The economic impact of drought can be felt for years after the rains return.

Cyclones Are Getting Stronger

Warmer ocean waters provide more energy for tropical cyclones. As a result, these storms are becoming more powerful. They carry heavier rainfall and cause stronger storm surges.

India's coastline is highly exposed to cyclones. Recent storms like Cyclone Amphan and Cyclone Tauktae demonstrated the destructive potential of modern cyclones. Early warning systems have improved, but the scale of damage remains high.

Early Warning Systems Save Lives

Preparedness is the most effective tool against extreme weather. Early warning systems give people time to evacuate or take shelter. Governments and agencies like the India Meteorological Department have improved these systems significantly.

However, challenges remain. Not everyone receives warnings in time, especially in remote areas. Investment in communication infrastructure and community training is crucial. Every rupee spent on preparedness can save many lives and reduce economic losses.

Looking ahead, extreme weather events are expected to become more frequent and severe. The focus must now shift to adaptation and mitigation. Reducing greenhouse gas emissions remains the only long-term solution to stabilise the climate.
