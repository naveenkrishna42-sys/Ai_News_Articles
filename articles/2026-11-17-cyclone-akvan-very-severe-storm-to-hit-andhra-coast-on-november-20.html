Cyclone Akvan Sets Its Sights on the Andhra Coast

Cyclone Akvan, the third named system over the North Indian Ocean this season after Arnab and Murasu, is tracking towards Andhra Pradesh and is forecast to make landfall between Nellore and Tuni on November 20. The India Meteorological Department has classified the system as a very severe cyclonic storm, a category that carries sustained wind speeds of 118-166 kmph and a storm surge capable of driving seawater several kilometres inland across low-lying delta districts.

The landfall corridor is unusually wide. Nellore in the south and Tuni in Kakinada district sit roughly 400 km apart along the coast, and the IMD's track cone keeps that entire stretch inside the likely impact zone until the system's position is pinned down in subsequent bulletins.

Why the Landfall Window Decides the Evacuation Timeline

A wide corridor is harder to manage than a narrow one. District administrations must pre-position relief material, shelters and National Disaster Response Force teams across multiple revenue divisions rather than concentrating them at a single point. The IMD typically narrows the landfall window to a six-to-twelve hour band once the system crosses 15 degrees north latitude, and that narrowing is what triggers mandatory evacuation of low-lying coastal villages.

Fishermen along the coast face the most immediate risk. Sea conditions ahead of a very severe cyclonic storm turn hazardous well before the eye reaches land, so fishing bans usually come into force 48 to 72 hours in advance. Ports at Krishnapatnam and Kakinada, along with the Visakhapatnam anchorage, should expect suspension of cargo and berthing operations as swells build.

Districts on Alert and the Real-World Impact

The Godavari and Krishna deltas are the critical vulnerability. These are low-lying, densely populated agricultural belts where storm surge can breach earthen embankments and push saline water into paddy fields, damaging standing crops and contaminating drinking water sources for weeks. Prakasam and Bapatla districts carry similar exposure along their drainage channels.

Power infrastructure is the second concern. Very severe cyclonic storms routinely topple distribution poles and snap transmission lines, and restoration in rural mandals can take several days. Households should treat the forecast as a multi-day disruption rather than a single night of bad weather.
