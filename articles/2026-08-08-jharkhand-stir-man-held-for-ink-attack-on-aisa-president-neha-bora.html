Jharkhand stir: Man held for ink attack on AISA president Neha Bora

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • A man threw ink at AISA President Neha Bora during a protest in Jharkhand, accusing her of being 'anti-national'.
  • The man was detained by police following the incident.
  • The protest, now in its 15th day, continues as the government invites another faction for talks.

Ink attack during Jharkhand protest

A man was detained on Friday after he threw ink at All India Students' Association (AISA) President Neha Bora during an ongoing protest in Jharkhand. The incident occurred as the agitation over job examinations entered its 15th day.

The man reportedly accused Bora of being 'anti-national' before the attack. Police intervened quickly and took him into custody. Details of his identity or the specific trigger for the action have not been officially confirmed.

Protest enters third week

The Jharkhand student protest has now stretched into its third week, with demonstrators demanding action on issues related to recruitment exams. The agitation has drawn attention from multiple political parties and student organisations.

On the same day, the state government extended an olive branch to another faction of agitators, inviting them for talks. This comes amid a series of meetings between ministers and student representatives, though a resolution remains elusive.

Political reactions

The ink attack has sparked sharp reactions. The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) has flagged the incident as a 'drama', questioning the circumstances surrounding it. Party spokespersons have sought to link the episode to the larger political narrative in the state.

Meanwhile, supporters of AISA have condemned the attack, calling for security for their leaders. The police have assured that necessary protocols are being followed, but have not yet released a formal statement on the detained individual.

Talks continue, no end in sight

Despite multiple rounds of dialogue, the protest shows no signs of winding down. Government officials have maintained that they are open to addressing the students' grievances, but the agitators have insisted on concrete assurances.

The invitation to another faction suggests a divide among the protesters, with some groups willing to negotiate while others remain firm. This has added a layer of complexity to the ongoing standoff.

As the protest continues, authorities are bracing for further escalation. The administration has deployed additional personnel in sensitive areas, though the situation remains peaceful so far.

What happens next will depend on whether the government's latest outreach yields any breakthrough. With the agitation now past the fortnight mark, pressure is mounting on both sides to find a way out of the deadlock.
