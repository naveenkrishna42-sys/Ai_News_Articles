Amit Shah becomes Opposition's next target in Parliament after Pradhan exit

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Opposition targets Amit Shah after exit of Union Minister Dharmendra Pradhan from the political scene.
  • Congress stages protests in Parliament, demanding an apology from Shah over police action against NEET demonstrators.
  • Adjournment and suspension of business notices moved in both Houses as Monsoon Session turns stormy.

The Monsoon Session of Parliament took a sharp turn on Monday as the Opposition trained its fire on Home Minister Amit Shah, demanding an apology over what they called 'police brutality' against students protesting the NEET exam controversy.

This comes a day after the exit of Union Minister Dharmendra Pradhan from the political scene, leaving the government scrambling to manage floor coordination. The Congress and its allies have now declared Shah their next target.

Congress stages protest, demands apology

Congress MPs staged a protest inside the Parliament complex on Monday morning, carrying placards and raising slogans against the Home Minister. They accused the central government of ordering a heavy-handed police crackdown on students demonstrating peacefully over the NEET paper leak allegations.

“Amit Shah must apologise to the nation for this attack on democratic dissent,” a senior Congress leader told reporters outside the Lok Sabha chamber. The party has also moved an adjournment motion and a suspension of business notice in both Houses, seeking an immediate discussion on the issue.

Government tables anti-paper leak bill amid ruckus

Amidst the uproar, the Centre introduced the anti-paper leak amendment Bill in the Lok Sabha. The Bill aims to make cheating in public examinations a non-bailable offence and proposes a jail term of up to 10 years for those involved in organised leak rackets.

Union Education Minister tabled the legislation as Opposition MPs continued to shout slogans. Speaker Om Birla repeatedly urged members to maintain order, but the din forced a brief adjournment of the House.

Opposition strategy shifts after Pradhan exit

Political observers noted that the Opposition's aggressive posture is part of a larger strategy. With the sudden exit of Dharmendra Pradhan, a seasoned troubleshooter for the government, the Opposition sees an opportunity to corner the ruling coalition on multiple fronts.

The Congress has signalled that it will not let the NEET protest issue die down. “We will pursue this until justice is done,” a party spokesperson said. The Opposition is also likely to raise questions about the Home Minister's role in the law and order situation surrounding the student protests.

What happens next

The Monsoon Session is expected to remain stormy for the rest of the week. Both Houses may see repeated adjournments unless the government agrees to a debate on the police action against NEET demonstrators. The anti-paper leak Bill, while a key legislative agenda item, could face delays if the political logjam persists.
