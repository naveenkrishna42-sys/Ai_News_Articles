Dharmendra Pradhan’s old meeting with Sonam Wangchuk resurfaces amid hunger strike

📅 2026-07-23 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Dharmendra Pradhan's old post praising Sonam Wangchuk's passion has resurfaced online.
  • The photo was taken during a meeting in 2023, before the current protest.
  • Wangchuk is on an indefinite hunger strike demanding withdrawal of the new education policy.

A years-old social media post by Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan has gone viral. The post, from 2023, shows him with climate activist Sonam Wangchuk and Wangchuk’s wife. In the caption, Pradhan wrote that he appreciated their passion for education and innovation.

What the viral post says

The post was shared on X (formerly Twitter) on 22 July 2023. Pradhan had met Wangchuk and his wife at his residence. The minister described the meeting as inspiring and praised Wangchuk’s work in education reform in Ladakh.

The post reads: “Met Sonam Wangchuk and his wife Shabnam. Appreciate their passion for education and innovation. Their work in Ladakh is commendable.” The image shows the three of them smiling together.

Context of the resurfaced post

The post has resurfaced as Wangchuk continues an indefinite hunger strike near the Jantar Mantar area in New Delhi. He is demanding the rollback of the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020, which he says is not suited for Ladakh’s unique cultural and geographical needs.

Wangchuk has been on strike since last week. His protest has drawn support from various student and environmental groups. The government has not yet responded formally to his demands.

Some social media users are contrasting the warm 2023 meeting with the current standoff. They are questioning why the minister’s appreciation has not translated into policy changes. Others point out that the meeting was a routine courtesy call, not a policy negotiation.

Background of the activist

Sonam Wangchuk is best known for designing the Ice Stupa, an artificial glacier that helps farmers in Ladakh deal with water scarcity. He also founded the Students’ Educational and Cultural Movement of Ladakh (SECMOL), which works on alternative education models.

Wangchuk had earlier praised the NEP for some of its ideas. But he later turned critical, arguing that the policy ignores the needs of remote and hilly regions. He has been demanding a separate education board for Ladakh and the inclusion of local languages in the curriculum.

What happens next

Wangchuk has said he will continue his hunger strike until the government agrees to discuss the demands. The Ministry of Education has not issued any statement on the matter. With the viral post adding to the public pressure, all eyes are now on whether the minister will respond to the activist’s call.
