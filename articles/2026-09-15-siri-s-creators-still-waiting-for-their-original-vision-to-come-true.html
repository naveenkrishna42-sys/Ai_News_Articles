Siri's Creators Still Waiting for Their Original Vision to Come True

✍️ By TIVRA News Tech & Gadgets Desk 📅 2026-09-15 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Siri was founded in 2007 and acquired by Apple in April 2010 for a reported $200 million, with co-founders Dag Kittlaus, Adam Cheyer and Tom Gruber departing within two years.
  • The original Siri vision was an open platform that connected third-party services for tasks like bookings and payments, not a closed first-party assistant.
  • Apple's June 2024 Apple Intelligence overhaul and third-party app integrations mark the closest return yet to that founding vision.

Sixteen Years After the Acquisition, the Founders' Blueprint Remains Unbuilt

Dag Kittlaus, Adam Cheyer and Tom Gruber built Siri as an independent app in 2007, launched it on the iPhone App Store in February 2010, and sold the company to Apple just two months later for a reported $200 million. Their founding pitch was never a voice-controlled Apple assistant. It was an open marketplace of connected services — a single conversational layer that could book a table, call a cab, buy tickets and route payments through third-party partners.

Apple dismantled that architecture almost immediately after the acquisition. Third-party service integrations were stripped out, the app was rebuilt for deep iOS control, and all three founders had left the company by 2012. The assistant they imagined in 2007 is only now, in 2026, beginning to reappear inside Apple's roadmap.

The Original Siri Architecture vs. What Shipped

The founders' design treated Siri as a routing engine. A spoken request was parsed, matched to an external API, and executed by a partner service that owned the transaction. Apple's version inverted this: Siri became a first-party feature, and every capability had to be built, controlled and monetised inside Apple's walls.

Why Buyers Should Care About This History

For anyone spending Rs 80,000 to Rs 1.5 lakh on a flagship iPhone, the Siri story is not nostalgia — it is a purchasing signal. Apple's slow assistant progress is the single biggest reason Android rivals have matched or beaten it on everyday AI utility, from call screening to on-device summarisation.

The 2024 Apple Intelligence rollout and subsequent third-party app hooks — WhatsApp, Audible and Outlook among those named — are the first structural moves back toward the founders' open-services model. That matters for buyers because it determines whether Siri becomes a genuine task-completion layer or stays a query-answering novelty.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Apple paid roughly $200 million for Siri in 2010 and retired the founders' open-API model within two years.
  • Third-party service routing — the core of the original product — is only returning now through Apple Intelligence integrations.
  • Apple has never publicly credited the founders' original architecture as the template for its current AI direction.
  • Competitive pressure from Google Gemini and Samsung Galaxy AI, not internal conviction, appears to be driving the pace of change.
  • Founders have since moved on: Kittlaus to Viv (acquired by Samsung), Cheyer to Change.org and other ventures, Gruber to machine learning research.

Viv, Samsung and the Road Not Taken

Kittlaus and Cheyer founded Viv Labs in 2012 and built an assistant explicitly designed around dynamic third-party program generation — the spiritual successor to their original Siri. Samsung acquired Viv in 2016, and its technology fed into Bixby. That arc suggests the founders' vision was technically sound; the constraint was always corporate strategy, not engineering.

The Buyer's Verdict

Apple's 2026 AI push is best read as a correction, not an invention. For Indian buyers weighing an iPhone against a Galaxy or Pixel flagship, the relevant question is whether Siri's new third-party integrations ship broadly and work reliably in local contexts — UPI payments, Indian language support, and regional app coverage.

Until that happens, the founders' 2007 blueprint remains what it has always been: a superior idea that arrived inside the wrong business model.
