Two Estimates, One Clear Signal

Ajay Devgn's Drishyam 3 has entered the trade conversation with a day-one projection that would make it one of the biggest Hindi openings of its release window. Pinkvilla's box office prediction places the third instalment near the Rs 50 crore mark on opening day, while another trade-tracked estimate circulated alongside it points higher, to roughly Rs 60 crore.

The spread between Rs 50 crore and Rs 60 crore matters less than the direction: both numbers put the film in blockbuster opening territory, and both assume a near-threefold multiple across the opening weekend. For single-screen owners and multiplex chains alike, that assumption is the number that decides screen allocation, show counts and staffing in the first 72 hours.

What the Verified Certifications Say

Beyond the projections, the confirmed particulars are already locked. The film has been certified UA 16+, a rating that legally restricts entry for viewers under 16 without an adult, and carries a runtime of 2 hours 30 minutes. That runtime is long enough to support premium-format pricing but short enough to protect evening show turnover on a holiday release date of 2 October.

Why UA 16+ Is a Commercial Variable

A UA 16+ rating is not a formality. It narrows the daytime student audience at multiplexes while protecting the family-viewing slot in the evening. Exhibitors programming this title should model weekday shows around the adult-eligible audience and shift family-skewed inventory to the weekend, when the certificate's restriction has less impact on footfall.

Director's Approach to the Climax

Director Abhishek Pathak has said he read online fan theories specifically to ensure the ending of Drishyam: The Conclusion remains surprising. That is a deliberate response to the franchise's core vulnerability: a mystery series whose audience actively reverse-engineers the plot before release. Controlling spoilers is, in effect, a marketing task as much as a creative one.

Key Real-World Highlights for Buyers

  • Opening-day estimate ranges from Rs 50 crore to Rs 60 crore; treat this as a range, not a fixed number.
  • A roughly 3x weekend multiple implies an opening weekend potentially near Rs 150-180 crore if day one lands as projected.
  • The 2 hour 30 minute runtime supports four to five shows per screen per day in standard format.
  • UA 16+ restricts unaccompanied under-16 entry, trimming weekday daytime occupancy.
  • Reported promotional offers, including free pav bhaji at select venues, add footfall but should be costed against concession margins.
  • The same headline flags an update on Jailer 2, though no substantive detail on that title was included in the source material.

The Advance Booking Test

Projected numbers are only as good as the booking curve behind them. The first 48 hours of advance sales will show whether Rs 50 crore is achievable on day one or whether the figure is a hopeful read on franchise goodwill. Occupancy per show, not raw ticket volume, is the metric that tells exhibitors whether to add morning shows or hold capacity for the weekend.

For now, Drishyam 3 arrives with a certified slot, a director actively defending its twist and a trade consensus clustering around a half-hundred-crore opening. The verification point is 2 October itself.
