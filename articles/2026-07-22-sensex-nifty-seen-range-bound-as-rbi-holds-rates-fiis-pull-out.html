Sensex, Nifty Seen Range-Bound as RBI Holds Rates, FIIs Pull Out

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty likely to open flat amid lack of fresh triggers.
  • RBI's decision to hold repo rate at 6.50% keeps sentiment cautious.
  • Foreign institutional investors have pulled out over Rs 3,000 crore this week.
  • Auto and banking stocks may see some pressure; IT could gain on rupee weakness.

Indian equity benchmarks are expected to open on a cautious note on Monday, June 8, with the Sensex and Nifty likely to trade in a narrow range. The market mood remains mixed as the Reserve Bank of India's decision to keep interest rates unchanged clashes with sustained selling by foreign institutional investors.

RBI Pause Weighs on Sentiment

The central bank's Monetary Policy Committee voted to keep the repo rate at 6.50 per cent, citing concerns over sticky core inflation. This was widely expected, but it offered little fresh direction for traders. The pause has made investors wary of any near-term rate cuts, especially with the US Federal Reserve still hawkish.

Bond yields inched up after the RBI announcement, reflecting a cautious outlook on borrowing costs. Banking stocks, which typically benefit from higher margins in a rising rate environment, may see limited upside in the near term.

FII Outflows Persist

Foreign institutional investors have remained net sellers in Indian equities for the third straight session. Provisional data shows they offloaded shares worth more than Rs 3,000 crore this week. The outflow is largely attributed to the strengthening dollar and rising US bond yields, which make emerging markets like India less attractive.

Domestic institutional investors, however, have been absorbing some of this selling. Mutual funds and insurance companies have stepped in, but not enough to spark a broad rally. Analysts say the market is now in a consolidation phase.

Sectoral Cues: Auto Under Pressure, IT in Focus

Auto stocks could face headwinds after the RBI flagged rising input costs. Passenger vehicle sales data for May showed a marginal dip, adding to concerns. Two-wheeler manufacturers may also see pressure as rural demand remains patchy.

On the other hand, information technology stocks could find some support. The rupee weakened past the 83 mark against the dollar, a positive for export-heavy IT firms. TCS, Infosys, and Wipro may see mild gains at the open.

Global Cues Provide Little Support

Asian markets were mixed in early trade on Monday. Japan's Nikkei edged higher on tech gains, while China's Shanghai Composite slipped on weak factory data. US indices ended mostly flat on Friday, with the Dow and S&P 500 hovering near record highs.

Crude oil prices remained elevated above $78 a barrel, adding to inflationary concerns. This could keep the RBI cautious in its future policy actions.

Technical Levels to Watch

The Nifty 50 is likely to find support at the 22,400 level, with resistance at 22,700. The 50-day exponential moving average sits near 22,450, a key level for traders. A break below this could trigger further selling.

The Sensex may trade between 73,800 and 74,500. Volumes are expected to remain low as traders await fresh triggers from corporate earnings and global cues later this week.

Market participants will watch for the release of industrial production data later this week. Any sharp deviation from expectations could set the tone for the next directional move.
