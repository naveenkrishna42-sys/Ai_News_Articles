ISRO chief Narayanan predicts success for NASA's Artemis II mission

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • ISRO chairman V. Narayanan said NASA's Artemis II mission will be a 'grand success'.
  • He spoke at a space symposium, emphasising global collaboration.
  • Artemis II is the first crewed test flight of NASA's Orion spacecraft.
  • ISRO and NASA are already partners on several projects, including NISAR.

ISRO chief backs NASA's Artemis II

ISRO chairman V. Narayanan has voiced strong confidence in NASA's upcoming Artemis II mission, calling it a 'grand success' in the making. He made the remarks on Saturday at a space symposium in Bengaluru.

The Artemis II mission is scheduled to carry astronauts around the Moon and back. It will be the first crewed test flight of NASA's Orion spacecraft and the Space Launch System rocket.

Collaboration over competition

Narayanan stressed that space exploration is no longer a zero-sum game. 'When one agency succeeds, the entire global space community benefits,' he told the gathering.

He pointed to existing partnerships between ISRO and NASA as proof of productive collaboration. The two space agencies are jointly developing the NISAR satellite, a synthetic aperture radar mission set to map Earth's land and ice surfaces.

Artemis II: A crucial step

NASA has described Artemis II as a critical milestone in its plan to return humans to the lunar surface. The mission will test all of Orion's systems with a crew of four astronauts on board.

If successful, it will pave the way for Artemis III, which aims to land the first woman and the next man on the Moon's south pole. ISRO's own lunar ambitions include further Chandrayaan missions and potential crewed Moon landings in the 2040s.

India's space trajectory

Narayanan also reaffirmed ISRO's commitment to its own deep-space goals. The Gaganyaan programme, India's first human spaceflight effort, is moving towards a crewed orbital mission.

India plans to build an indigenous space station, the Bharatiya Antariksh Station, by 2035. Narayanan said international cooperation, including with NASA, would play a role in these projects.

The Artemis II mission is currently targeting a launch window in late 2026. NASA has not announced a specific date. ISRO and NASA officials have said they expect to deepen ties in human spaceflight and planetary science in the coming years.
