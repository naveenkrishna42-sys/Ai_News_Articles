Google's AI legal battle: Lawyers' route to challenge rivals

📅 2026-08-27 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Google's legal AI push targets OpenAI and Anthropic
  • Google expands Gemini Enterprise AI platform for law firms
  • Google Cloud launches AI agents to automate law firm workflows
  • Google partners with Morningstar, PitchBook on AI legal tool

Google's Legal AI Push

Google has reportedly told OpenAI and Anthropic that it plans to take a 'lawyers route' to challenge them. This move comes as Google continues to expand its Gemini Enterprise AI platform, targeting law firms and legal professionals.

Gemini Enterprise Expansion

Google has been actively expanding its Gemini Enterprise AI platform, which is designed to assist law firms and legal professionals. The platform aims to automate workflows and improve efficiency in legal practices.

AI Agents for Law Firms

Google Cloud has launched AI agents as part of the Gemini Enterprise platform. These agents are designed to automate various workflows in law firms, from document review to case management. The AI agents are expected to significantly reduce the time and effort required for these tasks.

Partnerships with Legal Tech Firms

Google has also partnered with Morningstar and PitchBook, two well-known legal tech firms, to develop an AI tool. This tool is designed to assist in legal research and analysis, further solidifying Google's position in the legal AI market.

Broader Implications

The legal AI market is a rapidly growing sector, with significant investment from tech giants. Google's move to challenge OpenAI and Anthropic in this space is a strategic one, aiming to solidify its position as a leader in the field.

Market Competition

The legal AI market is highly competitive, with several players vying for market share. Google's expansion of its Gemini Enterprise platform and the launch of AI agents are part of its broader strategy to dominate this market.

Impact on Legal Professionals

The introduction of AI agents and tools in the legal sector is expected to have a significant impact on legal professionals. While these tools can automate repetitive tasks and improve efficiency, they also raise concerns about job displacement and the need for reskilling.
