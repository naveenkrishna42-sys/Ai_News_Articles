Iran Claims It Destroyed Many US Jets, Caused 'Serious Damage' To Many More

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran's IRGC claims it hit US fighter jets with missiles and drones in Jordan.
  • Iran says it targeted US bases in Iraq, Jordan, Oman, and Syria.
  • The attacks come after six consecutive nights of US strikes.
  • US officials have not yet confirmed the extent of damage to their aircraft.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has claimed that its forces destroyed a number of US fighter jets and caused 'serious damage' to many more in a series of strikes on American bases across the Middle East.

The claims, reported by multiple Indian news outlets including NDTV and Deccan Herald, come amid an escalating conflict between Iran and the US-Israel alliance. Iran says it struck US jets with missiles and drones in Jordan, while also targeting American positions in Iraq, Oman and Syria.

IRGC Details of Attacks

According to Iranian state media, the IRGC said it successfully hit US fighter jets at a base in Jordan. The claims have not been independently verified. US Central Command has not yet released a formal assessment of damage to its aircraft or personnel.

The IRGC described the operation as a direct response to what it termed continued American aggression. Iran says its forces used precision-guided missiles and drones in the coordinated attacks.

Wider Conflict Escalates

These latest strikes by Iran come after six consecutive nights of US air raids against Iranian targets. The US has not confirmed the Iranian claims of damage to its jets. Military analysts point out that Iran has a history of exaggerating battlefield successes.

The attacks mark a significant widening of the conflict. Iran says it struck bases in four countries simultaneously — Jordan, Iraq, Oman and Syria. This suggests a coordinated campaign rather than isolated retaliation.

Regional Fallout

The US maintains thousands of troops across the Middle East. Jordan, a key American ally, has been drawn into the conflict as a staging ground. Iraq has repeatedly asked the US to remove its forces. Oman has traditionally played a mediating role in regional disputes.

India has called for restraint from all parties. The Ministry of External Affairs is monitoring the situation closely. Indian nationals in the region have been advised to stay in touch with the embassy.

Unverified Claims

Without independent confirmation, the true extent of damage to US military assets remains unclear. The Pentagon typically releases its own battle damage assessments hours or days after such claims are made.

Iranian media has released footage it claims shows missiles hitting American positions, but the videos cannot be geolocated or verified. The US has not commented on the specific Iranian claims about destroyed jets.

The conflict shows no signs of de-escalation. Iran has vowed to continue attacks until what it calls 'American occupation forces' leave the region. The US has warned of a decisive response to any further strikes on its personnel or equipment.

What happens next will depend on the actual damage to US military capability and Washington's appetite for a wider war. Both sides have drawn red lines, and the coming days will test whether either is willing to step back from the brink.
