New Delhi World Book Fair 2026 Set for February, Focus on Global Exchange

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • The fair runs from February 1 to 9, 2026, at Pragati Maidan, New Delhi.
  • It aims to promote reading, cultural dialogue, and global literary exchange.
  • The event will feature publishers, authors, and cultural programs.

The New Delhi World Book Fair 2026 is scheduled to return to Pragati Maidan from February 1 to 9. Organised by the National Book Trust in collaboration with the Indian Trade Promotion Organisation, the fair has been a staple of the capital's cultural calendar for decades.

This year's edition will focus on celebrating books as a bridge between cultures. The theme underscores the fair's role in fostering international exchange and dialogue through literature.

A Platform for Publishers and Readers

The fair will host hundreds of publishers from India and abroad. Major Indian publishing houses, along with international participants, are expected to showcase their latest titles across genres including fiction, non-fiction, academic works, and children's literature.

Special discounts and book launches are planned throughout the nine-day event. Organisers hope to attract bibliophiles, students, and casual readers alike. The fair typically draws lakhs of visitors, making it one of the largest literary gatherings in South Asia.

Cultural Programs and Author Interactions

Beyond book sales, the fair will feature a lineup of cultural performances, panel discussions, and meet-the-author sessions. These events aim to create a space for intellectual exchange and creative inspiration.

Children’s activities, storytelling sessions, and workshops are also on the agenda. Organisers have emphasised the importance of engaging younger audiences to build a reading culture for the future.

International Participation and Exchange

A key highlight of the 2026 edition is the focus on global literary exchange. Several countries are expected to participate with their own pavilions, showcasing translated works and regional literature. This initiative aligns with India's soft power ambitions in the cultural sphere.

The National Book Trust has not yet confirmed the guest of honour country for this year. Past editions have featured nations such as Russia, Saudi Arabia, and Bangladesh as focus countries, each bringing unique literary traditions to the fore.

Translation and multilingual literature will receive special attention. Given India's linguistic diversity, the fair serves as a platform for regional language publishers to reach a wider audience.

Logistics and Accessibility

Entry to the fair will be ticketed, with nominal fees for adults and free entry for children and students on certain days. Organisers have promised enhanced crowd management and better signage to improve visitor experience.

Pragati Maidan is well-connected by metro and bus routes. Parking facilities are limited, so visitors are encouraged to use public transport. The fairgrounds will remain open from 11 AM to 8 PM on weekdays, with extended hours on weekends.

Officials have not yet released the full schedule of events or the list of participating authors. Updates are expected on the National Book Trust's website in the coming weeks.

With the literary world watching, the New Delhi World Book Fair 2026 aims to reaffirm India's place as a hub for cultural dialogue and the written word. The focus will be on how the fair translates its theme into concrete exchanges between writers, readers, and publishers across borders.
