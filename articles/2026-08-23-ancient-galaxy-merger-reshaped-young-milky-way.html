Ancient galaxy merger reshaped young Milky Way

📅 2026-08-23 📂 Science Original source ↗

  • The merger occurred 1.8 billion years before the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus event.

Ancient Galaxy Merger Shaped Young Milky Way

Astronomers have uncovered compelling evidence of a galaxy merger that occurred 12 billion years ago, which played a significant role in shaping the young Milky Way. This discovery, published in Nature, challenges previous assumptions about the Milky Way's formation and suggests that our galaxy has been involved in more mergers than previously thought.

Evidence of a Massive Accretion Event

The evidence of this massive accretion event was found 1.8 billion years before the Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus merger, a well-known event in the Milky Way's history. The Gaia-Sausage-Enceladus merger is a significant event that occurred approximately 8-10 billion years ago, and is believed to have contributed to the formation of the Milky Way's halo and thick disk.

Hubble Space Telescope's Role

The Hubble Space Telescope played a crucial role in solving this merger mystery. The telescope's advanced imaging capabilities allowed astronomers to observe the remnants of the ancient galaxy merger in the Milky Way's halo. The observations provided detailed insights into the chemical composition and motion of the stars involved in the merger, helping to piece together the timeline of events.

Challenging Previous Assumptions

This discovery challenges the previous assumption that the Milky Way's formation was relatively quiet and undisturbed. Instead, it suggests that the Milky Way has been involved in numerous mergers and accretion events throughout its history. This new understanding of the Milky Way's formation has significant implications for our understanding of galaxy evolution and the universe as a whole.

What Happens Next?

As astronomers continue to study the Milky Way and other galaxies, they hope to uncover more evidence of ancient mergers and accretion events. This research will not only provide a more complete picture of the Milky Way's formation but also shed light on the broader processes that shape galaxies in the universe.
