Iran tracked Trump's floor, targeted aircraft at NATO summit: Report

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran had detailed knowledge of Trump's hotel floor during the NATO summit
  • Iran planned to target Trump's aircraft, prompting a security switch
  • Trump confirmed switching planes in Turkey over the Iran threat

New details have emerged about a serious security threat faced by former US President Donald Trump during a NATO summit. According to a report, Iranian operatives had precise knowledge of Trump's location, including the specific floor of his hotel, and had planned to target his aircraft.

The revelation adds another layer to an already tense period in US-Iran relations, raising questions about the extent of intelligence penetration and the measures taken to ensure the safety of the American delegation.

Threat assessment and the quiet exit

The report indicates that Iranian agents had identified the exact floor where Trump was staying. This level of detail suggests a sophisticated intelligence operation, potentially involving local sources or electronic surveillance. The information was deemed credible enough to trigger an immediate and drastic security response.

Members of Trump's cabinet remained on a decoy plane, while the president made a quiet exit, highlighting the gravity of the perceived threat. The diversion was designed to confuse any potential attackers and ensure the president's safe return.

Trump confirms the switch

Trump himself has since confirmed the change in plans. He stated, 'I go by Secret Service, military,' acknowledging the switch from his primary aircraft to an alternate one during a stopover in Turkey. This confirmation aligns with the reported threat and underscores the level of caution exercised.

The decision to swap planes was not taken lightly. Such moves are rare and are reserved for situations where intelligence suggests a direct and imminent danger. The fact that the president was moved, while others stayed behind, points to a targeted threat against him personally.

Historical precedent: Clinton's decoy

This is not the first time a US president has used a decoy plane. A report from USA Today recalls that before Trump, former President Bill Clinton also employed a decoy aircraft for safety during his tenure. That incident, which involved a similar strategy, was kept under wraps for years.

The use of decoy planes is a time-tested, albeit rarely disclosed, security measure. It highlights the lengths to which the Secret Service and military go to protect the commander-in-chief. The fact that such tactics are being discussed openly now suggests a shift in how these operations are perceived and reported.

Implications and unanswered questions

The report raises several critical questions. How did Iran obtain such precise information? Was there a mole within the delegation or the hotel staff? What specific intelligence led to the decision to switch planes? Officials have not yet confirmed the full details of the threat or the intelligence behind it.

The incident also underscores the volatile nature of US-Iran relations, which have seen numerous flashpoints over the years. The targeting of a former president's aircraft would be a significant escalation, and the fact that it was allegedly planned during a NATO summit adds an international dimension.

What to watch

As more details emerge, the focus will likely shift to how this threat was neutralised and what it means for future presidential travel. Security protocols may be reviewed, and intelligence-sharing with allies could be scrutinised. The coming weeks may reveal more about the extent of the Iranian operation and the response from US agencies.
