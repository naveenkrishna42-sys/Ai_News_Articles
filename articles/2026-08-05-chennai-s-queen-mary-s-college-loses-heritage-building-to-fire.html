Chennai's Queen Mary's College loses heritage building to fire

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A heritage building at Queen Mary's College in Chennai was destroyed in a fire.
  • The cause of the fire is not yet known; investigations are underway.
  • No casualties have been reported, officials said.
  • The loss is a blow to the city's architectural heritage.

Chennai's Queen Mary's College lost one of its heritage buildings to a fire on Tuesday, leaving a gap in the city's architectural history. The blaze, which broke out in the structure, was brought under control after several hours, but the building was largely destroyed.

No casualties have been reported, officials said, though the full extent of the damage is still being assessed. The cause of the fire is not yet known, and an investigation has been ordered.

A landmark reduced to ashes

The building was among the oldest on the college campus, an example of colonial-era architecture that has stood for over a century. Its loss has saddened students, alumni, and heritage enthusiasts alike, who saw it as a symbol of the institution's storied past.

Queen Mary's College, established in the early 20th century, is one of Chennai's premier women's colleges. Its heritage structures are a draw for those interested in the city's history, and the fire has prompted calls for better protection of such sites.

Firefighting efforts and response

Fire tenders rushed to the spot after the alarm was raised, and firefighters worked to contain the flames, preventing them from spreading to adjacent buildings. The operation took several hours, and the area was cordoned off as a precaution.

College authorities have yet to issue a detailed statement, but they have assured that the safety of students and staff is the priority. The campus remained closed for the day as a mark of mourning, and classes are expected to resume once a structural assessment is completed.

Heritage loss stings

The incident has reignited a debate about the preservation of heritage buildings in Indian cities. Many such structures, often in government or institutional use, lack adequate fire safety measures, and this fire is a stark reminder of the risks.

Architects and historians have pointed out that heritage buildings need not just maintenance but also modern safety upgrades, including fire alarms and sprinkler systems. The loss at Queen Mary's College, they say, was preventable.

What happens next

The investigation into the fire's cause is ongoing, and officials have promised a thorough probe. The college is expected to announce plans for the affected area once the damage is fully assessed.

Heritage groups are likely to push for a restoration, though the extent of the damage may make that difficult. For now, the campus mourns a piece of its history, and the city is left to reflect on how to protect what remains.
