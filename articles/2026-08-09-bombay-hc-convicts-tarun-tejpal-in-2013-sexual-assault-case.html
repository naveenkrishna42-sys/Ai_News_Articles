Bombay HC convicts Tarun Tejpal in 2013 sexual assault case

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bombay High Court reverses trial court acquittal, convicts Tarun Tejpal
  • Case relates to 2013 sexual assault of a colleague at a Goa hotel
  • Court order sets stage for fresh sentencing hearing

High Court overturns acquittal

The Bombay High Court on Thursday convicted former Tehelka editor Tarun Tejpal in the 2013 sexual assault case, setting aside a trial court's acquittal from 2021. The bench ruled that the prosecution had established its case beyond reasonable doubt, a decision that reverses years of legal back-and-forth.

The case dates back to November 2013, when a female colleague accused Tejpal of sexually assaulting her in a lift and hotel room during an event in Goa. The trial court had acquitted him in May 2021, citing contradictions in the victim's testimony. That verdict was challenged by the Goa government, and later by the victim herself.

Legal journey and arguments

During the appeal, the prosecution argued that the trial judge had overlooked key evidence, including the victim's prompt disclosure to colleagues and the hotel's CCTV footage. Tejpal's defence maintained that the allegations were fabricated, pointing to alleged inconsistencies in the complainant's account.

The High Court, however, found the prosecution's version credible. In its judgment, the bench noted that the victim's testimony was corroborated by contemporaneous records and that the acquittal had failed to appreciate the totality of circumstances. The detailed order is expected to be released in the coming days.

Context of the case

Tejpal, a high-profile journalist, stepped down as editor-in-chief of Tehelka shortly after the allegations surfaced. The case became a flashpoint for India's #MeToo movement, spurring debates on workplace safety and media accountability. The trial itself dragged on for years, with witnesses and legal challenges delaying proceedings.

The victim, who cannot be named under Indian law, has consistently maintained that Tejpal assaulted her twice on the night of the incident. She had earlier told the court that she feared for her career and safety, which is why she did not report the matter immediately.

Reactions and next steps

News of the conviction drew reactions from women's rights groups and legal experts, many of whom called it a significant moment for survivors of sexual violence. Tejpal's legal team said they are yet to receive a copy of the full judgment and will decide on an appeal to the Supreme Court after studying it.

The High Court has scheduled a hearing on Friday to decide on the quantum of sentence. Tejpal remains on bail until then, but the court may impose stricter conditions. The case now moves to the next stage, where the victim's statement on sentencing will be heard.

Observers note that the Supreme Court will be the final arbiter if Tejpal challenges the conviction. Legal experts say the appeal could hinge on the High Court's interpretation of evidence, particularly the credibility of eyewitness accounts and forensic reports.

The sentencing hearing will be watched closely, as it will set a precedent for how similar cases are treated in the higher judiciary. For now, the Bombay High Court's ruling marks a turning point in a case that has lingered for over a decade.
