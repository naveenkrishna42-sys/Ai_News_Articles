Developer Seeks Permission to Demolish Tilted Bengaluru Building

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Developer formally requests demolition of a tilted building in Bengaluru.
  • The building developed a structural lean, prompting safety fears among residents.
  • Greater Bengaluru Authority is reviewing the demolition proposal.
  • No official timeline has been provided for the authority's decision.

Demolition Request Filed for Structurally Unsound Building

The developer of a residential building in Bengaluru that developed a noticeable tilt has formally approached the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) seeking permission to raze the structure. The move comes after the building, located in a rapidly developing suburb, raised serious safety concerns among nearby residents and local authorities.

According to officials, the developer submitted a detailed demolition plan to the GBA earlier this week. The plan outlines the methodology for bringing down the multi-storey building safely, given its precarious condition. The building had been evacuated weeks ago after residents reported cracks and a visible lean.

Safety Concerns Prompt Evacuation

The building’s tilt was first noticed several weeks ago, leading to panic among residents. Local authorities quickly ordered an evacuation, and the structure has remained vacant since. Engineers who inspected the site found that the lean had worsened over time, making the building unsafe for habitation.

“The tilt was initially minor, but it became more pronounced after recent rains. We couldn’t take any chances,” a senior GBA official said. The developer has since been in talks with structural experts to determine the best course of action.

Developer’s Proposal Under Scrutiny

The developer’s application to the GBA includes a detailed assessment from a structural engineering firm, which concluded that the building cannot be stabilised and must be demolished. The firm’s report cites soil settlement issues and possible construction flaws as contributing factors.

GBA officials are now reviewing the proposal. They are expected to consult with independent engineers before granting approval. “We need to ensure that the demolition is carried out without endangering adjacent properties or public safety,” the official added.

What Happens Next

The GBA has not set a deadline for its decision. Once approved, the developer will be required to follow strict safety protocols during the demolition. Local authorities have also asked the developer to prepare a plan for debris removal and site restoration.

The incident has sparked a broader debate about construction quality in Bengaluru’s fast-growing suburbs. Residents’ groups have called for stricter oversight of building approvals and construction practices. The GBA has not commented on whether it will initiate an inquiry into the building’s original construction.

The developer’s next move hinges on the GBA’s decision. If permission is granted, demolition could begin within weeks. Until then, the tilted building remains a stark reminder of the risks posed by rapid, unregulated urban development.
