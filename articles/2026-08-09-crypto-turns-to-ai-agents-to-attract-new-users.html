Crypto turns to AI agents to attract new users

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Crypto platforms are increasingly using AI agents to assist users with trading and navigation.
  • The move is aimed at lowering entry barriers for newcomers unfamiliar with crypto.
  • Experts say AI agents could transform user experience but also pose new risks.

The cryptocurrency industry, long grappling with the challenge of onboarding newcomers, is now turning to a new tool: AI agents. These automated programs, powered by large language models, can execute trades, answer questions, and guide users through complex DeFi protocols, all in natural language.

Several major platforms have begun integrating AI agents into their interfaces, allowing users to simply type commands like "buy $100 of ETH and stake it" and have the agent handle the rest. This hands-off approach is designed to reduce the intimidation factor that has historically kept retail investors away from crypto.

Simplifying the user experience

The core pitch is simplicity. Instead of navigating multiple wallets, understanding gas fees, and memorizing contract addresses, a user can interact with an AI agent that manages the backend. This is particularly appealing to younger, tech-savvy users who are comfortable chatting with bots but wary of technical complexity.

"The goal is to make crypto feel as easy as using a banking app," said one industry analyst, noting that user onboarding remains a critical bottleneck. The number of daily active addresses has plateaued, and platforms are under pressure to show growth to investors.

From trading bots to personal assistants

While trading bots have existed for years, the new wave of AI agents is far more conversational and adaptive. They can learn a user's risk tolerance, suggest portfolio rebalancing, and even explain the rationale behind a trade in plain English. Some agents are being designed to autonomously discover yield farming opportunities across multiple chains, executing complex strategies that would be impractical for a human to do manually.

This evolution is not just about retail users. Institutional players are also exploring AI agents to monitor markets, execute large orders with minimal slippage, and handle compliance checks in real time.

Risks and regulatory concerns

However, the rise of AI agents brings new risks. If an agent is given too much autonomy, it could make costly mistakes or be exploited by malicious actors. Security researchers have already demonstrated ways to manipulate AI agents through prompt injection attacks, where hidden instructions in a transaction or webpage cause the agent to act against the user's interest.

Regulators are also paying attention. In India, where the crypto regulatory framework is still evolving, authorities have not yet issued specific guidelines for AI-driven trading. "We are watching this space closely," a source at a regulatory body said, adding that consumer protection would be a primary concern.

Industry outlook

Despite these concerns, the momentum is undeniable. Several crypto exchanges have reported that users who engage with AI agents tend to stay longer and trade more frequently. The technology is still in its early days, but the potential to lower entry barriers is attracting significant venture capital investment.

As the industry continues to search for its next wave of users, AI agents may well be the answer. But the balance between convenience and safety will be crucial. The next few months will likely see more pilots and partnerships as platforms race to integrate this nascent technology, while regulators scramble to keep pace.

For now, the crypto world is watching closely. Whether AI agents become a permanent fixture or just another passing trend depends on their ability to deliver real value without introducing new vulnerabilities.
