Political Parties Clash Over Vande Mataram Anthem

The political landscape in India is once again witnessing a heated debate over the Vande Mataram anthem, with the Congress party standing firm on its stance and the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) calling Congress the 'Muslim League'.

Congress Stands Firm

The Congress party has been vocal about its support for the Vande Mataram anthem, which is considered to be a symbol of national pride and unity. The party has been advocating for the anthem to be played in schools and public gatherings, and has been critical of the BJP government's stance on the matter.

BJP Calls Congress the 'Muslim League'

The BJP, on the other hand, has been critical of the Congress party's stance on the Vande Mataram anthem. BJP chief Nabin has called Congress the 'New Muslim League' over the party's stance, and has accused the party of trying to revive the Hindu-Muslim divide. The BJP has been advocating for the anthem to be played only in government buildings and not in public gatherings.

Amit Shah Blasts Congress

Home Minister Amit Shah has been particularly vocal about the Congress party's stance on the Vande Mataram anthem. In a recent statement, Shah has accused the Congress party of 'extreme appeasement' and has called for the party to be held accountable for its actions. Shah has also threatened to file a case against the Congress party over its stance on the anthem.

Kharge Challenges BJP Government

Congress leader Mallikarjun Kharge has also challenged the BJP government over its stance on the Vande Mataram anthem. Kharge has accused the BJP government of trying to 'hijack' the anthem and has called for the government to be held accountable for its actions. Kharge has also threatened to file a case against the BJP government over its stance on the anthem.

The debate over the Vande Mataram anthem is likely to continue, with both the Congress and BJP parties standing firm on their respective stances. The government has not yet confirmed any changes to the current laws regarding the anthem, and the debate is expected to continue in the coming weeks and months.
