Houthi strikes in Yemen as Saudi Arabia braces for attacks: What we know

📅 2026-08-07 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least two civilians killed in a Houthi strike on Marib, Yemen's government says.
  • Saudi military reports 11 civilians, including a child, injured near the Yemen border.
  • Houthi attacks have killed 58 troops in Yemen; Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey sign a defence pact.
  • Saudi Arabia is bracing for further attacks as regional tensions escalate.

Fresh Houthi strikes have hit Yemen and the Saudi border area, leaving civilians dead and wounded, as Riyadh braces for further attacks. The escalation comes amid a new defence pact signed by Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey, raising the stakes in a conflict that shows no sign of cooling.

Deadly strikes in Marib and border areas

Yemen's internationally recognised government said two civilians were killed in a Houthi strike on Marib, a key battleground province. The Saudi military separately reported that 11 civilians, including a child, were injured near the Yemen border in attacks it attributed to the Houthis.

Officials have not yet confirmed the full extent of damage, but the strikes mark one of the deadliest recent episodes in the Saudi-Yemen theatre. The Houthis have not commented on the claims.

Heavy toll on troops

The attacks have also taken a severe toll on military personnel. Reports from the region indicate that Houthi strikes have killed at least 58 troops in Yemen, with 11 civilians wounded in the assault on Saudi territory. The figures, drawn from multiple sources including Yemeni government statements and regional media, underscore the intensity of the fighting.

Casualty numbers are difficult to verify independently, and ground conditions in Marib and along the border remain fluid. Local medics say hospitals are struggling to cope with the influx of wounded.

Defence pact with Pakistan and Turkey

Amid the violence, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey have signed a Nato-style defence pact, a move that signals deeper military coordination among the three nations. The agreement, announced this week, is seen as a direct response to the heightened threat from Yemen-based attacks.

Details of the pact have not been fully disclosed, but analysts suggest it could involve joint training, intelligence sharing and rapid-response mechanisms. Turkey and Pakistan have both maintained diplomatic ties with Riyadh, but this formalises a closer security alignment.

What we know so far

  • The Houthi strike on Marib killed two civilians, according to Yemen's government.
  • Saudi military says 11 civilians, including a child, were injured near the border.
  • At least 58 troops have been killed in Yemen in recent Houthi attacks.
  • Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey have signed a Nato-style defence pact.
  • No official confirmation yet on the full casualty count or damage assessments.

Regional implications

The defence pact adds a new layer to the region's security architecture, but it also risks drawing Pakistan and Turkey deeper into a conflict that has already destabilised the Arabian Peninsula. Riyadh has long accused Tehran of arming the Houthis, a charge Iran denies.

For now, Saudi Arabia is bracing for more strikes, and its air defences remain on high alert. The coming days will show whether the defence pact translates into a coordinated response or remains a symbolic show of unity.
