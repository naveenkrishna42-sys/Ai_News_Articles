BMC finally acts on mayor's barricades

📅 2026-08-22 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • BMC removes barricades that were put up on the mayor's orders
  • Residents had flagged safety hazards and traffic bottlenecks
  • Mayor acknowledged the need for better coordination between political office and civic engineers

Decision comes after resident complaints

The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation has finally acted on barricades that were installed on the mayor's orders near a busy junction in south Mumbai. The civic body removed the barriers on Thursday after weeks of complaints from residents and commuters who said the installations created safety hazards and traffic bottlenecks.

The barricades had been put up following an initiative from the mayor's office, sources said. The exact purpose of the barriers remained unclear, but municipal officials confirmed the order originated from the mayor's camp.

Safety concerns raised

Local residents said the barricades obstructed the view of oncoming traffic at the junction. Pedestrians, particularly elderly citizens and schoolchildren, found it difficult to cross the road safely. Commuters also reported longer travel times as vehicles were forced into fewer lanes.

"The barricades were creating problems every single day," said a resident from the Fort area who asked not to be named. "We raised the issue with the BMC several times before they took notice."

Mayor acknowledges coordination gap

The mayor's office acknowledged the situation and said better coordination between the political wing and civic engineers was needed going forward. A statement from the mayor's press office said infrastructure projects should involve technical assessments before implementation.

"We are committed to working closely with the BMC to ensure such issues do not arise again," the statement said. Officials have not yet confirmed whether any formal review of the incident is planned.

BMC responds

The BMC said it acted after reviewing the matter internally. A senior civic official said barricades and other temporary installations on public roads must follow established safety norms. The official added that the department will communicate more regularly with political offices to avoid similar situations.

The removed barricades have been stored at a BMC depot. The civic body has not yet decided whether they will be redeployed elsewhere in the city.

What happens next

Watch for any formal review mechanism the BMC proposes to prevent political directives from bypassing technical checks. The mayor's office is expected to issue further guidance on how infrastructure requests from elected representatives will be processed going forward.
