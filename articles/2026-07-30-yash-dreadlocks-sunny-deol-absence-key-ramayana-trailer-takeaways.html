Yash Dreadlocks, Sunny Deol Absence: Key Ramayana Trailer Takeaways

Key points
  • Yash's Ravana appears with long dreadlocks and a menacing presence, drawing comparisons to epic fantasy villains.
  • Sunny Deol's Hanuman is notably absent from the trailer, leaving fans to speculate about his role.
  • Producer Namit Malhotra described the film's visual style as 'Avatar meets Gladiator in the world of The Lord of the Rings'.
  • Sai Pallavi's Sita is shown wielding a sword, hinting at a more action-oriented portrayal.

First Look at the Epic

The trailer for Nitesh Tiwari's Ramayana has landed, and it is already generating massive buzz. Starring Ranbir Kapoor as Rama, Yash as Ravana, and Sai Pallavi as Sita, the film promises a grand retelling of the ancient epic. Producer Namit Malhotra described the visual aesthetic as 'Avatar meets Gladiator in the world of The Lord of the Rings,' setting sky-high expectations.

The trailer offers a glimpse of a fantastical world filled with towering sets, VFX-heavy battles, and elaborate costumes. But beyond the spectacle, six major takeaways have emerged from the footage, sparking debates among fans and critics alike.

Yash's Ravana: A Terrifying Villain

Yash, known for his role in the KGF franchise, appears as the ten-headed demon king Ravana. The trailer showcases his character with long, matted dreadlocks, a towering physique, and a menacing aura. His entry scene, where he sits on a throne surrounded by darkness, has drawn comparisons to iconic fantasy villains like Sauron and Voldemort.

Fans have noted that Yash's Ravana appears more intimidating than previous portrayals. The actor's physical transformation—bulked up and adorned with intricate jewelry—suggests a performance rooted in raw power. The trailer hints at his backstory, showing glimpses of his rise as Lankesh, the lord of Lanka.

Sai Pallavi's Sword-Wielding Sita

One of the most striking moments in the trailer is Sai Pallavi's Sita holding a sword. This imagery breaks from traditional depictions of Sita as a passive figure. Instead, she appears ready to fight, hinting at a more assertive role in the narrative.

The scene has sparked discussions about how Tiwari is reinterpreting the character. Whether this is a flashback to her earlier life or a moment during the war remains unclear. But the visual is unmistakable—Sita is not just a captive; she is a warrior.

Sunny Deol's Absence Sparks Questions

Perhaps the biggest talking point is the complete absence of Sunny Deol's Hanuman. The actor was announced as part of the cast, but the trailer does not show him even in a fleeting shot. This has led to a flood of reactions on social media, with fans asking, 'Where is Hanuman?'

Some speculate that Hanuman's role is being kept under wraps for a later reveal. Others wonder if Deol's scenes are being saved for the second part of the film, which has been planned as a two-part release. The production team has not commented on the omission, leaving viewers to guess.

Visual Scale and Comparisons

The trailer's visuals are undeniably ambitious. Aerial shots of Lanka, dense forests, and battlefields are rendered with high-budget CGI. Producer Namit Malhotra's comparison to Avatar and The Lord of the Rings appears justified in the sheer scope of the sets and digital environments.

Costumes also draw attention. Ranbir Kapoor's Rama is seen in simple, earthy tones, contrasted with Yash's dark, elaborate armor. The color palette leans towards gold and crimson, evoking a mythic yet grounded feel. The action sequences, though brief, show Rama wielding a bow with precision, while Ravana's multiple arms are used to devastating effect.

Fan Reactions and Expectations

Fan response has been mixed but mostly positive. While many praised Yash's look and the film's scale, others expressed disappointment over Hanuman's absence. Online forums are buzzing with theories about Deol's role, with some suggesting he might appear in the sequel.

The trailer also drew comparisons to previous Ramayana adaptations, including the 1987 TV series and animated films. Tiwari's version is clearly aiming for a global, cinematic appeal, blending Indian mythology with Hollywood-style storytelling.

What happens next: The film is slated for a multi-part release, with the first part expected in late 2026. The makers are likely to release more teasers focusing on Hanuman and other key characters. All eyes are now on Sunny Deol's eventual reveal.
