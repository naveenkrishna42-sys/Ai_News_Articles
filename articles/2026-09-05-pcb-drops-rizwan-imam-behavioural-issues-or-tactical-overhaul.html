What Triggered the Pakistan Squad Overhaul?

Pakistan cricket plunged into administrative turmoil after the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) axed senior players Mohammad Rizwan, Imam-ul-Haq, and Salman Ali Agha following the first Test against England at Multan. The selectors, backed by head coach Mike Hesson, justified the move by citing persistent behavioural issues alongside a string of sub-par returns. The decision sent ripples through the dressing room and ignited a nationwide debate about selection transparency, dressing-room culture, and accountability norms within Pakistan's red-ball setup.

According to early PCB briefings, the trio's removal was not a knee-jerk reaction to a single failure but the culmination of long-standing feedback from support staff. While no official Performance Improvement Plan was ever made public, internal assessments flagged recurring breaches of professional protocols during training camps and match days.

Coach Mike Hesson Breaks His Silence

Newly appointed head coach Mike Hesson addressed the media at the Gaddafi Stadium, providing the most comprehensive explanation yet. Hesson clarified that selection was strictly merit-based and that behavioural concerns were evaluated alongside on-field numbers. He confirmed that Rizwan's conversion rate at number four and Imam's inconsistency against seam movement were both factors.

1. Mohammad Rizwan — The Wicketkeeper-Batter

Rizwan's axing stunned fans. Despite his reputation as one of Pakistan's most reliable batters in white-ball formats, his Test conversion rate has hovered around 32% over the last 18 months. PCB insiders reportedly flagged resistance to field-placement adjustments and a tendency to bat out of position during powerplays, contributing to the behavioural flag.

2. Imam-ul-Haq — The Top-Order Anchor

Imam's technique against lateral movement has been a long-standing concern. With a Test average below 30 against seam-heavy attacks like England's, selectors felt the left-hander was an injury risk in red-ball cricket. Reports also indicate Imam was non-committal about specific batting-order demands during recent meetings.

3. Salman Ali Agha — The All-Rounder

Agha's utility as a spin-bowling all-rounder could not offset his inconsistent shot selection. Hesson's camp reportedly wanted a more dynamic lower-order option, paving the way for changes ahead of the second Test at Rawalpindi.

External Voices: Hayden, Gillespie and the Critics

Former Australia opener Matthew Hayden called the overhaul chaotic, warning that such mid-series resets damage continuity. Former Pakistan head coach Jason Gillespie launched a scathing attack on selectors, accusing them of leaking dressing-room information and undermining team morale. A former Pakistan captain, writing for regional outlets, warned that Babar Azam could be the next target if the policy of behavioural scrutiny is applied uniformly.

What This Means for Pakistan Cricket

The PCB's bold call signals a willingness to prioritise discipline over seniority, a long-overdue reset in a system often criticised for shielding star players. For Rizwan, who turns 33 later this year, a strong domestic season could earn redemption. Imam, with his ODI credentials intact, faces an uphill battle to reclaim a red-ball slot. Agha's spin-bowling utility keeps him in contention for shorter formats.

  • Pros of the Move: Fresh accountability culture; better seam-friendly combinations; clarity on dressing-room protocols.
  • Cons of the Move: Loss of senior experience; morale shock ahead of a live series; risk of perceived political selection.
  • Key Real-World Insight: Behavioural clauses are now active selection criteria, meaning any senior player, including Babar Azam, must align with PCB's professional standards or face consequences.

The Rawalpindi Test will serve as the first public verdict on whether the axe was a strategic masterstroke or an administrative overreaction.
