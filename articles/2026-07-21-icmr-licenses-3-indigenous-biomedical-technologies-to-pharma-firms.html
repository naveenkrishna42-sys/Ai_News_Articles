ICMR Licenses 3 Indigenous Biomedical Technologies to Pharma Firms

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • ICMR has licensed three indigenous biomedical technologies to Indian pharma and vaccine manufacturers.
  • The technologies target applications in cancer and infectious disease treatment.
  • The transfers were facilitated under the PatentMitra initiative to bridge the gap between research and commercial production.

ICMR Transfers Critical Technologies to Industry

The Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR) has licensed three indigenous biomedical technologies to domestic pharmaceutical and vaccine manufacturers. The move is part of a broader push to make India self-reliant in producing advanced therapies.

Companies including Emcure and Biological E have received the technologies for commercialisation. The handover was facilitated under ICMR's PatentMitra initiative, which helps bridge the gap between publicly funded research and industrial application.

Technologies Target Cancer and Infectious Diseases

The three technologies cover areas critical to India's public health needs. Two are aimed at improving cancer treatment — one involves a novel formulation for targeted drug delivery, and the other focuses on a diagnostic platform for early detection.

The third technology addresses infectious diseases. Details about the specific pathogen or disease target have not been disclosed by ICMR. Officials said the technologies have been validated in laboratory settings and are now ready for scaling up.

PatentMitra Initiative Drives Commercialisation

ICMR launched the PatentMitra initiative to help researchers navigate patent filings and technology transfer processes. The programme has so far assisted in filing over 100 patent applications and facilitated several licensing deals.

Dr. Rajiv Bahl, ICMR Director General, said the initiative is essential for turning lab discoveries into products that reach patients. "Our goal is to ensure that indigenous research leads to affordable solutions for Indian patients," he stated.

Industry Partners Welcome the Move

Emcure and Biological E have confirmed receiving the technology licenses. Both companies have existing manufacturing capabilities for biologics and vaccines, which can be adapted for the new products.

Industry experts say the transfer will reduce dependence on imported technologies and lower the cost of therapies. The exact commercial terms of the licensing agreements have not been made public.

What Happens Next

The companies will now begin the process of scaling up production and conducting further clinical trials if required. ICMR will continue to monitor progress through the PatentMitra framework. The first commercial products from these technologies could enter the market within two to three years.
