Daayra Review: Kareena Kapoor, Prithviraj Film Soft-Soaps Vigilante Justice

A Legal Thriller That Pulls Its Punches

Daayra arrives with the kind of casting that sells tickets on name alone: Kareena Kapoor and Prithviraj Sukumaran sharing screen space in a legal drama about a vigilante who operates just outside the law. The premise promises confrontation, moral friction and courtroom fireworks. What the film delivers instead is a polished, watchable thriller that repeatedly steps back from the very questions it raises. For a genre built on uncomfortable truths, that restraint is the film's biggest weakness.

This review examines what works, what does not, and whether the film justifies a theatrical ticket at current multiplex pricing.

What Daayra Gets Right

The film's strongest asset is its lead pair. Kareena Kapoor brings a controlled intensity to a role that requires her to shift between public composure and private fury. Prithviraj Sukumaran, working in Hindi after a string of strong South Indian performances, matches her beat for beat. The dialogue-heavy confrontations between them are the film's high points.

Production values hold up well. The courtroom sets feel lived-in rather than staged, and the background score resists the urge to over-explain every emotional beat. At a time when legal dramas often drown in exposition, Daayra trusts its audience to follow the procedure.

Where the Film Stumbles

The central problem is ideological. A story about vigilante justice demands that the filmmaker take a position, or at least complicate the viewer's sympathies. Daayra does neither. The vigilante's methods are framed as regrettable but necessary, and the legal system is portrayed as either corrupt or helpless. The film wants the thrill of retribution without the discomfort of consequence.

This soft-soaping extends to the antagonist. A villain with no coherent motive beyond generic greed cannot sustain two and a half hours of tension. By the interval, the outcome feels predetermined, and the final act loses urgency.

Real-World Buyer Implications

At Rs 250-450 per ticket in metros, plus roughly Rs 400-600 for snacks and travel, a family outing to Daayra costs upwards of Rs 1,500-2,000. The film delivers enough star power and craft to justify a weekend show, but it is not the kind of film that rewards a second viewing or a premium-format ticket.

Viewers expecting the moral complexity of a strong legal drama will leave unsatisfied. Those simply wanting two strong performers in a well-mounted thriller will get their money's worth.

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Committed lead performances from Kareena Kapoor and Prithviraj Sukumaran; tight production design; courtroom scenes that avoid melodrama.
  • Cons: Avoids taking a stand on vigilante justice; weak antagonist; bloated second half; predictable final act.

Final Verdict

Daayra is a competent film that could have been a provocative one. It soft-soaps legal vigilantism at a moment when audiences are ready for harder questions. Catch it on a weekday show or wait for streaming; the performances deserve attention, but the film does not demand the big screen.
