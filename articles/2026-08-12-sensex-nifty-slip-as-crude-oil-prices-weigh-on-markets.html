Sensex, Nifty Slip as Crude Oil Prices Weigh on Markets

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty opened lower on Wednesday tracking weak global cues.
  • High crude oil prices raised concerns over inflation and fiscal deficit.
  • Investors awaited further cues from global markets and oil price movement.

Indian stock markets opened in the red on Wednesday, with the BSE Sensex and NSE Nifty both trading lower as elevated crude oil prices dampened investor sentiment. The benchmark indices slipped in early trade, reflecting worries over rising input costs and their potential impact on the domestic economy.

Crude Oil Prices Weigh on Sentiment

Brent crude remained elevated, hovering near multi-month highs, as supply concerns persisted in global markets. Higher oil prices typically translate into increased import bills for India, which meets a significant portion of its energy needs through overseas purchases. This, in turn, puts pressure on the rupee and widens the current account deficit, unnerving market participants.

Analysts said the surge in crude is a key risk factor for inflation, as it feeds into transportation and manufacturing costs. The prospect of sticky inflation could delay any potential easing by the Reserve Bank of India, adding to the caution among investors.

Broader Market Under Pressure

The selling pressure was visible across most sectors, with oil marketing companies, aviation, and auto stocks among the worst hit. These sectors are particularly sensitive to fuel prices, and any sustained rise in crude could squeeze their margins.

Midcap and smallcap stocks also traded lower, mirroring the weak trend in the benchmark indices. Market breadth was negative, with more declines than advances on the BSE, indicating broad-based selling.

Global Cues and Investor Outlook

Asian markets were mixed in early trade, while US futures pointed to a subdued opening, adding to the cautious mood. Investors are also keeping an eye on the upcoming US Federal Reserve meeting minutes, which could offer clues on the future trajectory of interest rates.

Domestic institutional investors have been net buyers in recent sessions, but foreign portfolio outflows have continued, reflecting global risk aversion. The combination of high crude prices and uncertainty over global monetary policy is keeping equity markets on edge.

Market participants are now awaiting fresh triggers, including corporate earnings and economic data, to gauge the near-term direction. The movement of crude oil prices will remain a crucial factor to watch in the coming sessions.

What to Watch

Investors will closely track oil price movements and any commentary from the RBI or the government on inflation. A sustained rise in crude could prompt further downgrades in growth forecasts, while a cooling of prices might offer some respite to the markets.
