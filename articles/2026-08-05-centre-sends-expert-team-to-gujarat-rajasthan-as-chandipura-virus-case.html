Centre sends expert team to Gujarat, Rajasthan as Chandipura virus cases rise

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Centre sends multi-institutional expert team to Gujarat and Rajasthan
  • 22 children have died of Chandipura virus in Gujarat this monsoon
  • Team to assist with containment, surveillance, and public health measures

New Delhi: The Union government has rushed a multi-institutional expert team to Gujarat and Rajasthan to help contain a rising outbreak of the Chandipura virus, which has claimed the lives of at least 22 children in Gujarat this monsoon.

The Centre's National Joint Outbreak Response Team (NJORT) has been deployed to the two states following reports of a spike in cases and fatalities, particularly among children. The move comes as public health authorities scramble to understand the spread of the virus, which has historically been associated with sporadic outbreaks in central and western India.

What is the Chandipura virus?

Chandipura virus is a vector-borne pathogen transmitted by sandflies, particularly the species Phlebotomus papatasi. It belongs to the Rhabdoviridae family, the same family as the rabies virus, though it behaves very differently.

The virus causes an acute encephalitic syndrome, often leading to rapid inflammation of the brain. The infection progresses quickly, with symptoms like sudden high fever, severe headache, vomiting, and altered consciousness appearing within hours to a day or two of exposure. In severe cases, it can lead to seizures, coma, and death.

Children are disproportionately affected. Public health experts believe this is because of their developing immune systems and their higher likelihood of exposure to sandfly habitats, which include mud walls, cattle sheds, and unhygienic surroundings common in rural and semi-urban areas.

Outbreak in Gujarat and Rajasthan

The current outbreak has been concentrated in Gujarat, where 22 children have died this monsoon. Several districts have reported cases, with the state government ramping up surveillance and vector control measures. Neighbouring Rajasthan has also reported suspected cases, prompting the Centre to expand its response.

The NJORT comprises epidemiologists, entomologists, virologists, and public health specialists drawn from multiple institutions. Their mandate includes conducting a detailed epidemiological investigation, tracing the source of infection, assessing the effectiveness of vector control measures, and guiding clinical management protocols.

The team is also expected to help state authorities launch community awareness campaigns, particularly in villages where sandfly breeding sites are common. Insecticide spraying, use of bed nets, and clearing of stagnant water are some of the key preventive steps being emphasised.

No vaccine, no specific antiviral

There is no specific antiviral treatment or vaccine for Chandipura virus. Clinical management is largely supportive, focusing on controlling fever, managing seizures, and maintaining hydration and electrolyte balance. Early hospitalisation is critical, as the virus can cause rapid neurological deterioration.

Doctors in affected areas have been advised to maintain a high index of suspicion for Chandipura virus in children presenting with acute fever and neurological symptoms, especially during the monsoon and post-monsoon period when sandfly populations peak.

The fatality rate in past outbreaks has ranged from 55 to 75 per cent, making early detection and referral all the more vital. However, the virus is not known to spread from person to person, which limits the risk of a wider urban epidemic.

Centre's response and next steps

The Union Health Ministry has been in touch with state governments, and the deployment of the NJORT is part of a broader containment strategy. The team will submit periodic reports to the ministry, and additional resources, including testing kits and logistical support, may be sent if required.

Officials have not yet confirmed the total number of cases across both states, nor have they provided a district-wise break-up. The ministry has, however, assured that all necessary support is being extended to the affected regions.

As the monsoon continues, the risk of new cases remains. The coming weeks will be crucial in determining whether the outbreak can be contained or whether it spreads further. For now, the focus remains on aggressive vector control, community awareness, and ensuring that children with symptoms reach hospitals quickly.
