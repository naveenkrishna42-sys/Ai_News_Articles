7 Indian brands championing sustainable living, beauty, and wellness

From kitchen to vanity: sustainable choices go mainstream

Indian consumers are increasingly looking for ways to reduce their environmental footprint. A new wave of homegrown brands is answering that call with products that promise both efficacy and ethics. From bamboo toothbrushes to plastic-free skincare, these seven labels are making sustainability a practical choice, not just a buzzword.

Each brand prioritises natural ingredients, minimal packaging, and transparent supply chains. For many, the journey began with a simple frustration — the lack of credible, planet-friendly options in the Indian market.

Home and lifestyle: rethinking everyday essentials

The first set of brands focuses on home and lifestyle products. One label offers biodegradable cleaning tablets that dissolve in water, eliminating plastic bottles. Another produces organic cotton towels and bed linen, sourced directly from weavers in rural India.

A third brand in this category specialises in reusable menstrual cups and cloth pads. Their products are designed to last for years, cutting down on the tonnes of sanitary waste generated every month. The company also runs awareness campaigns in schools and colleges.

Beauty and personal care: clean ingredients, clear conscience

In the beauty segment, three brands stand out for their commitment to clean formulations. One uses cold-pressed oils and plant extracts, avoiding parabens and sulphates. Another creates solid shampoo bars and conditioner bars, packaged in recycled paper.

A third beauty brand focuses on natural lip and cheek tints made from beetroot and other edible ingredients. Their entire range is vegan and cruelty-free. The products are also sold in refill pouches, encouraging customers to reuse their original containers.

Wellness and nourishment: eating and feeling good

The final brand in the list operates in the wellness space. It offers a range of organic teas, herbal infusions, and superfood powders. All ingredients are sourced from small farmers in the Himalayas, ensuring fair wages and organic certification.

Their packaging uses compostable materials, and a portion of each sale goes toward reforestation projects in Uttarakhand. The brand also provides free online consultations with nutritionists for customers who purchase subscription boxes.

These seven brands are part of a larger shift. According to industry reports, the Indian sustainable goods market has grown by over 30% in the last two years. More consumers are now willing to pay a premium for products that do not harm the planet.

What remains to be seen is whether this trend will sustain itself against the pressure of cheaper, conventional alternatives. The coming months will test the durability of these brands as they scale up and face competition from larger players entering the green space.
