Jagannath Rath Yatra 2026 begins: Full schedule, ritual timings

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Jagannath Rath Yatra 2026 begins today, July 16, in Puri, Odisha.
  • The festival involves the ceremonial journey of Lord Jagannath, Balabhadra, and Subhadra in three grand chariots.
  • Key rituals include Pahandi, Chhera Pahara, and the nine-day sojourn at the Gundicha Temple before the return journey.
  • Devotees from across India and the world gather in Puri to participate in the festivities.

Grand Chariot Procession Begins

The sacred city of Puri in Odisha is resonating with the chants of devotees as the Jagannath Rath Yatra 2026 commences today, July 16. This nine-day festival, one of the most significant events in the Hindu calendar, sees the sibling deities — Lord Jagannath, Lord Balabhadra, and Goddess Subhadra — embark on their annual journey from the Jagannath Temple to the Gundicha Temple.

Massive crowds have gathered along the Grand Road since the early hours, eager to catch a glimpse of the deities and participate in the processional rituals. The atmosphere is charged with devotion, as the rhythmic beating of drums and the blowing of conch shells fill the air.

Schedule and Ritual Timings

The day's proceedings commenced with the Mangala Alati, the first offering to the deities, followed by the ceremonial bathing and dressing of the idols. The key ritual of Pahandi, where the idols are ceremonially carried from the sanctum sanctorum to the chariots, is set to take place in the late morning hours.

Following the Pahandi, the Gajapati Maharaja of Puri, considered the first servant of Lord Jagannath, will perform the Chhera Pahara — sweeping the chariot platforms with a golden broom. This marks the official start of the chariot pulling, scheduled to begin after noon. The three towering chariots — Nandighosa, Taladhwaja, and Devadalana — will then be pulled by thousands of devotees toward the Gundicha Temple, about three kilometres away.

The journey is expected to take several hours, with the deities halting at the Mausi Maa Temple for a brief rest, a tradition observed every year. The chariots are scheduled to reach the Gundicha Temple by evening, where the deities will reside for the next eight days.

Nine-Day Sojourn and Return Journey

The deities will stay at the Gundicha Temple, their aunt's abode, for a week. During this period, the temple will host special darshans and rituals. The highlight of the festival remains the Bahuda Yatra, the return journey, scheduled for July 24.

On the return day, the deities will travel back to the Jagannath Temple in the same chariots. The festival concludes with the Suna Besha, where the deities are adorned with golden ornaments, followed by the Niladri Bije ritual, marking their re-entry into the sanctum sanctorum.

Devotees who cannot travel to Puri can participate in the celebrations at their local Jagannath temples, where smaller-scale versions of the Rath Yatra are organised. Many cities across India, including Ahmedabad, Kolkata, and Mumbai, host their own chariot processions on this day.

Security and Arrangements

The Odisha government has made extensive arrangements for the smooth conduct of the festival. Special security forces have been deployed along the route, and medical camps have been set up to assist the large number of pilgrims. The administration has also made provisions for water and food distribution along the Grand Road.

Railways have announced special trains to Puri, and the airport has seen an increase in flight frequencies to accommodate the influx of visitors. The district administration has urged devotees to cooperate with the authorities and follow the designated routes for the procession.

As the chariots roll, the festival continues to grow in scale each year, drawing devotees from across India and abroad. The procession will resume tomorrow morning from the point where it halts today, with the deities expected to reach their destination by evening. All eyes now remain on the skies, as a timely completion of the journey depends on the weather.
