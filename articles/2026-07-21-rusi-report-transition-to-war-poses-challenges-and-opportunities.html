RUSI Report: Transition to War Poses Challenges and Opportunities

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Wars & Conflicts Original source ↗
Key points
  • RUSI analysis highlights the strategic shift from peace to conflict
  • Report examines military, economic, and societal adjustments
  • Opportunities for innovation amid wartime pressures identified

Strategic Shift Under Scrutiny

The Royal United Services Institute (RUSI) has published a detailed report examining the complex process of transitioning from a peacetime posture to a war footing. The study, titled "Challenges and Opportunities of the Transition to War," analyses how nations navigate this critical period.

Defence analysts and policymakers are increasingly focused on the speed and effectiveness of such transitions. The RUSI report underscores that the gap between peace and war is no longer a clear line but a grey zone of hybrid threats and rapid escalation.

Military Readiness and Industrial Base

A central challenge identified is the readiness of armed forces. Many militaries have spent decades optimised for counter-insurgency and peacekeeping. Shifting to high-intensity conflict requires retraining, new equipment, and changes in doctrine.

Equally critical is the defence industrial base. The report notes that decades of underinvestment in munitions production and supply chains have left many nations vulnerable. Mobilising industry for war production is a slow, costly process that must begin before conflict erupts.

Economic and Societal Strains

Transitioning to war also imposes severe economic strains. Governments must reallocate budgets, impose controls, and manage inflation. The RUSI analysis points to the need for clear communication with citizens to secure public support for sacrifices.

Societal resilience is another key factor. The report argues that a nation's ability to sustain a war effort depends on the trust between the state and its people. Missteps in this area can erode morale and undermine strategic goals.

Opportunities Amid Adversity

Despite the challenges, the RUSI report identifies significant opportunities. The pressure of conflict often accelerates innovation in military technology, from drones to cyber warfare. Nations that adapt quickly can gain a decisive edge.

Moreover, the transition can force long-overdue reforms in defence procurement and command structures. The report cites historical examples where wartime necessity led to breakthroughs that shaped the post-war world.

Lessons for Policymakers

The report concludes with recommendations for policymakers. It urges nations to conduct regular transition exercises, invest in resilient supply chains, and maintain a clear-eyed assessment of their strategic environment.

RUSI emphasises that the window for preparation is shrinking. With great power competition intensifying, the ability to transition swiftly from peace to war may determine the outcome of future conflicts.

As global tensions persist, the findings from RUSI serve as a reminder that the path to war is paved with both peril and possibility. How nations prepare today will shape their security for decades to come.
