Iran's Environmental Crisis Deepens as Water Scarcity and Dust Storms Worsen

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Environment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iran is experiencing a severe environmental crisis driven by water scarcity, dust storms, and land degradation.
  • The crisis threatens millions of people, with potential for mass displacement and regional instability.
  • Experts and officials are warning that the situation is worsening due to climate change and poor resource management.

Iran is grappling with a deepening environmental crisis that experts describe as one of the most severe in the region. Widespread water scarcity, intensifying dust storms, and rapid land degradation are pushing the country toward a tipping point, with millions of people already feeling the impact.

Water Scarcity Hits Critical Levels

Decades of mismanagement, over-extraction of groundwater, and inefficient irrigation practices have drained Iran's water resources. Major rivers and wetlands, including the once vast Hamoun wetlands, have dried up, turning into sources of toxic dust.

Farmers in provinces like Khuzestan and Sistan-Baluchestan have abandoned their land as wells run dry. The government has been forced to ration water in several cities, sparking protests in recent years. Climate change has only worsened the situation, with rainfall declining by nearly 20% over the past two decades.

Dust Storms Become a Public Health Emergency

Iran now experiences some of the worst dust storms in the world. These storms, carrying fine particles from dried lake beds and degraded soil, have become more frequent and intense. Hospitals in the southwestern city of Ahvaz report a surge in respiratory ailments during storm season.

Schools and offices are often forced to shut down. The storms also damage crops, affecting food security. Officials have acknowledged that the dust problem cannot be solved within Iran alone, as many storms originate from neighboring countries like Iraq and Saudi Arabia.

Land Degradation Threatens Livelihoods

Nearly 90% of Iran's land is now classified as arid or semi-arid. Desertification is advancing rapidly, with soil erosion rates among the highest in the world. The UN has warned that Iran could lose up to 40% of its agricultural land if current trends continue.

This has triggered a slow-moving crisis in rural areas. Young people are leaving villages for cities, putting pressure on urban infrastructure. Experts warn that if the trend continues, Iran could see the displacement of tens of millions of people within a decade.

Regional and Geopolitical Dimensions

The environmental crisis is not confined to Iran's borders. Water disputes with Afghanistan over the Helmand River have escalated tensions. Dust storms from Iran's dried wetlands now affect countries as far away as India and the Gulf states.

Iranian officials have repeatedly called for regional cooperation to tackle the crisis, but political rivalries have hindered progress. The government has also blamed international sanctions for limiting its ability to import water-saving technology and modern irrigation systems.

What comes next depends on whether Iran can implement long-term water management reforms and secure regional cooperation. Without urgent action, the crisis risks becoming a humanitarian disaster with far-reaching consequences for millions across the country and beyond.
