Guru Purnima Observed Across India; PM Modi, Amit Shah Greet Nation

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Guru Purnima, a festival honouring spiritual and academic teachers, was celebrated across India.
  • Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Union Home Minister Amit Shah extended their wishes on the occasion.
  • The day is traditionally marked by offering prayers and gratitude to gurus.

Guru Purnima, the annual festival dedicated to honouring spiritual and academic teachers, was observed with traditional fervour across India on Thursday. Devotees thronged temples, ashrams and educational institutions to offer prayers and express gratitude to their gurus.

Leaders extend greetings

Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Union Home Minister Amit Shah were among the prominent leaders who extended their greetings on the occasion. In a social media post, PM Modi said the day is a reminder of the invaluable contribution of gurus in shaping lives and society. He urged people to draw inspiration from their teachings.

Amit Shah also wished the nation, emphasising the role of gurus in guiding humanity towards the path of knowledge and righteousness. Several other political leaders and public figures shared messages, underscoring the cultural and spiritual significance of the day.

Traditional observances

Guru Purnima falls on the full moon day (Purnima) in the Hindu month of Ashadha. It is traditionally a day when disciples express reverence to their gurus, who are seen as dispellers of darkness and ignorance. Many people observe a fast, participate in satsangs (spiritual discourses), and seek blessings from their teachers.

In various parts of the country, special ceremonies were held at ancient ashrams and educational centres. Students and followers offered flowers, fruits and other symbolic gifts to their mentors. Some communities also organised collective chanting and meditation sessions.

Cultural programs and community events

Several temples and cultural organisations arranged special events. Bhajans and devotional songs filled the air at many venues. In some cities, community kitchens served free meals as part of the celebrations.

The day also holds significance for Buddhists, who commemorate it as the day when Lord Buddha delivered his first sermon at Sarnath. Jain communities also observe the festival with reverence.

Significance of the guru in Indian tradition

In Indian philosophical traditions, the guru is regarded as a guide who leads a disciple from darkness to light. The word 'guru' itself is derived from Sanskrit roots meaning 'dispeller of darkness'. The festival reinforces the timeless bond between teacher and student, a relationship considered sacred in Indian culture.

Schools and colleges observed the day by organising special assemblies where students honoured their teachers. Many institutions also held online events, allowing alumni to connect and express gratitude to their former mentors.

The festival comes at a time when educators continue to play a pivotal role in the country's development. The government has been emphasising the importance of teachers under the National Education Policy 2020, which seeks to transform the learning ecosystem.

As dusk fell, the sky lit up with the glow of lamps and the chanting of mantras, bringing the day's celebrations to a peaceful close. The spirit of gratitude and reverence, however, is expected to linger in the hearts of millions who took part in the observances.

With the monsoon season underway, many regions saw light showers, which did little to dampen the enthusiasm of devotees. The coming days will see similar festivals like Nag Panchami and Raksha Bandhan, continuing the cycle of cultural celebrations that mark the Indian calendar.
