Chennai Mayor: Ban on Limited Tenders Disrupting Civic Works

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Mayor cites ban on limited tenders as cause for delays in civic projects.
  • Open tenders are seen as more time-consuming for small but urgent works.
  • State government imposed the ban to increase transparency and curb corruption.

Civic infrastructure work in Chennai is taking a hit, according to Mayor R. Priya. She said a recent ban on limited tenders has slowed down the execution of several projects across the city.

The Mayor made the remarks during a review meeting at the Ripon Building on Wednesday. She argued that the restriction is creating unnecessary bottlenecks in routine maintenance and small-scale construction works.

What the Ban Means

The state government imposed the ban on limited tenders earlier this year. The move was aimed at promoting transparency and preventing corruption in public procurement.

Limited tenders are typically used for smaller, time-sensitive projects where only a few qualified contractors are invited to bid. Open tenders, on the other hand, are advertised publicly and allow any eligible firm to participate.

Mayor Priya said that while open tenders are good for large projects, they are not always practical for urgent civic works like road repairs, drainage maintenance, and streetlight installation.

Impact on the Ground

Several ward councillors have complained that pothole filling and sewage line repairs are getting delayed. They say the open tender process takes weeks, which is too long for emergencies.

“A small drainage fix can’t wait for a month-long bidding process. We need flexibility for routine work,” one councillor said on condition of anonymity.

The Mayor echoed this sentiment, stating that the ban is “affecting the pace of development” in the city. She has urged the state government to reconsider the rule, at least for projects below a certain cost threshold.

Government's Stand

State officials have defended the ban, citing past instances of favouritism and inflated costs in limited tenders. They argue that open tenders ensure fair competition and better value for taxpayer money.

A senior official in the Municipal Administration department said the government is open to reviewing the policy, but only after a thorough assessment of its impact.

“We understand the concerns, but transparency cannot be compromised. We will look at data from the last six months and then decide,” the official said.

What Happens Next

The Greater Chennai Corporation is compiling a report on projects delayed due to the tender ban. This report will be sent to the state government for review.

A decision on whether to relax the rules for minor works is expected within the next two months.
