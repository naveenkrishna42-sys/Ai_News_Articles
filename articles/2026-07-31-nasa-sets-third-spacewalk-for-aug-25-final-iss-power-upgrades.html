NASA Sets Third Spacewalk for Aug 25, Final ISS Power Upgrades

Key points
  • NASA's third US spacewalk is set for Aug 25, 2026, with timing to be announced.
  • Astronauts Jessica Meir and Mike Menon will perform solar array work during the spacewalks.
  • The spacewalks aim to power the ISS through its final years of operation.

NASA Schedules Third Spacewalk for Aug 25

NASA has confirmed that the third of three US spacewalks will take place on August 25, 2026, from the International Space Station (ISS) in Earth orbit. The exact time of the spacewalk, designated as US EVA-98, has not been announced yet, with officials saying details will follow closer to the date.

This spacewalk follows two earlier excursions planned for August, marking a busy period of maintenance and upgrades outside the orbiting laboratory.

Astronauts Set for Solar Array Work

The spacewalks are aimed at continuing work on the station's solar power arrays. Astronauts Jessica Meir and Mike Menon are slated to head out on August 6 for the first of these spacewalks, with the duo expected to handle critical power system tasks.

Meir, a Maine native and veteran of multiple spacewalks, has been preparing for this mission, which will see her and Menon work to enhance the station's power capabilities through its remaining operational years.

Powering the Station Through Its Final Years

The solar array work is part of a broader effort to ensure the ISS remains functional until its planned decommissioning later this decade. By upgrading the power systems, NASA aims to support ongoing scientific research and maintain the station's operational readiness.

The agency has not provided a specific timeline for the station's retirement, but these spacewalks are seen as a crucial step in extending its life.

US Spacewalk Schedule and Preview

NASA is expected to hold a preview news conference ahead of the spacewalks, where officials will discuss the detailed procedures and objectives. The conference will likely clarify the timing for the August 25 excursion and any adjustments to the plan.

All three spacewalks are part of a series that began with earlier EVAs this year, reflecting the station's aging infrastructure and the need for regular maintenance.

What to Watch

As August 25 approaches, NASA will release more specifics on the spacewalk's start time and live coverage details. The success of these EVAs will be crucial for the ISS's continued operation, and any delays could impact the timeline for future missions.

Space enthusiasts and researchers will be watching closely to see how Meir and Menon perform in what could be one of the final major power upgrades for the station.
