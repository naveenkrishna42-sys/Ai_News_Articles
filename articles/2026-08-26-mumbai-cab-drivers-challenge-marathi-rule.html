Mumbai cab drivers challenge Marathi rule

  • Drivers argue the rule violates their fundamental rights and livelihoods
  • Government claims the rule is necessary to maintain order and professionalism in the industry

Auto-rickshaw drivers challenge Maharashtra government's Marathi-only rule in Bombay High Court

Mumbai's auto-rickshaw drivers have taken the Maharashtra government to the Bombay High Court, challenging a recent order mandating that all drivers must communicate with passengers in Marathi. The drivers argue that the rule violates their fundamental rights and threatens their livelihoods. The government, however, maintains that the rule is necessary to maintain order and professionalism in the industry.

Drivers argue the rule violates their fundamental rights and livelihoods

The drivers, represented by the Maharashtra Auto Rickshaw Drivers' Association, claim that the Marathi-only rule is discriminatory and violates their right to freedom of speech and expression under Article 19(1)(a) of the Indian Constitution. They also argue that the rule will lead to a significant decline in their earnings, as many passengers are not fluent in Marathi.

According to the drivers, the rule will also lead to a lack of understanding between drivers and passengers, which could result in accidents and other safety concerns. They have asked the court to quash the rule and restore the previous system, where drivers could communicate with passengers in any language.

Government claims the rule is necessary to maintain order and professionalism in the industry

The Maharashtra government, represented by the state's legal advisor, has argued that the Marathi-only rule is necessary to maintain order and professionalism in the auto-rickshaw industry. The government claims that the rule will help to standardize communication between drivers and passengers, which will improve the overall experience for passengers and make it easier for drivers to follow traffic rules and regulations.

The government has also argued that the rule is in line with the state's efforts to promote the use of Marathi as the primary language of communication in Mumbai. The government has stated that it will continue to work with the drivers' association to find a mutually acceptable solution to the issue.

Bombay High Court to hear PIL against Marathi rule for drivers

The Bombay High Court has scheduled a hearing for the Public Interest Litigation (PIL) filed by the auto-rickshaw drivers against the Marathi-only rule. The court will consider the arguments presented by both the drivers and the government and is expected to deliver its verdict in the coming weeks.

If the court upholds the rule, it will have significant implications for the auto-rickshaw industry in Mumbai. The rule could set a precedent for other cities and states in India, where similar rules are being considered to standardize communication between drivers and passengers.

Key Facts and Figures

  • The Marathi-only rule was introduced in Mumbai in June 2026
  • Mumbai has a population of over 1.2 crore people, with a significant number of residents who do not speak Marathi as their first language
  • The auto-rickshaw industry in Mumbai employs over 5 lakh drivers, who are primarily from the state's lower-income groups
  • The drivers' association has filed a PIL in the Bombay High Court, seeking to quash the rule and restore the previous system
