Karnataka SIR: 16 Districts, Mostly Backward, Show Over 12% Voters Under ASDDO

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • 16 districts in Karnataka, mostly backward, report over 12% voters under ASDDO.
  • ASDDO refers to a specific electoral category, indicating potential issues with voter registration.
  • The report highlights regional disparities in electoral rolls, prompting calls for corrective measures.

Karnataka's Electoral Roll Report Raises Red Flags

A recent report on Karnataka's Special Integrated Review (SIR) has flagged that 16 districts, a majority of them classified as backward, have over 12% of voters listed under the ASDDO category. The findings have sparked discussions among officials and civil society groups about the accuracy and inclusivity of the state's electoral rolls.

The ASDDO classification, which pertains to a specific segment of voters, has emerged as a key indicator in the review. While the exact criteria for this category remain technical, the high percentage in these districts suggests a pattern that warrants closer examination.

Backward Districts Bear the Brunt

The report identifies districts that are predominantly in the Hyderabad-Karnataka and Kalyan-Karnataka regions, known for their socio-economic challenges. These areas have historically struggled with infrastructure, literacy, and employment, which may influence voter registration and enumeration processes.

Officials familiar with the SIR data note that the 12% threshold is significant, as it exceeds the state average. However, they have not yet confirmed whether this reflects errors in the rolls or genuine demographic trends.

What Does ASDDO Mean for Voters?

The ASDDO category is used by election authorities to mark certain voters, though its implications are not widely understood by the public. Experts suggest that a high proportion of such entries could indicate issues like duplicate entries, missing documentation, or errors in data entry.

For voters in these districts, the classification could impact their ability to participate smoothly in elections. Local activists have urged the Election Commission to clarify the category and take steps to clean up the rolls before the next electoral cycle.

Official Response and Next Steps

The Karnataka Chief Electoral Officer's office has acknowledged the report but has not issued a detailed response yet. Sources indicate that a comprehensive review of the affected districts is likely, with a focus on verifying the status of voters under ASDDO.

Past exercises in other states have involved door-to-door verification and camps to correct anomalies. Similar measures may be adopted here, though timelines remain unconfirmed.

The SIR report is part of a broader effort to strengthen electoral governance in Karnataka. It comes amid growing attention on voter turnout and representation, particularly in regions that have historically been marginalised.

What to Watch

In the coming weeks, the Election Commission's response will be crucial. Observers will watch for a clear action plan, including deadlines for rectification and public communication to affected voters. The outcome could set a precedent for how such data is handled in other states.
