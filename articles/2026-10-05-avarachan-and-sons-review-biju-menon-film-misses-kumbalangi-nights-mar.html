Avarachan and Sons Review: Biju Menon Film Misses Kumbalangi Nights Mark

Key points
  • Biju Menon delivers a restrained performance that anchors the film despite uneven support.
  • Direction leans heavily on familial drama but lacks the nuanced scripting of Kumbalangi Nights.

Overview

Avarachan and Sons, released in early October 2026, arrives with the expectation of being a heartfelt family drama in the vein of the critically acclaimed Kumbalangi Nights. Starring Biju Menon in the titular role, the film attempts to explore intergenerational ties within a modest Kerala household. While the premise promises emotional depth, the execution often feels derivative, relying on familiar tropes without offering fresh insight.

Performances

Biju Menon brings his characteristic subtlety to the role of Avarachan, portraying a patriarch burdened by tradition and quiet resilience. His restrained expressions convey internal conflict effectively, especially in scenes where he confronts his sons’ differing aspirations. Supporting actors, however, vary in impact; some deliver earnest portrayals while others appear under‑utilized, leaving certain character arcs feeling underdeveloped.

Direction and Screenplay

The director, whose previous work leaned toward commercial entertainers, opts for a melodramatic approach that leans heavily on sentimental dialogues. The screenplay, while coherent, lacks the layered storytelling and authentic regional flavor that made Kumbalangi Nights a benchmark. Plot points progress predictably, and the attempt to weave social commentary feels superficial, resulting in a narrative that rarely surprises.

Technical Aspects

Cinematography captures the lush backwaters and rustic homes of Kerala with a warm palette, lending visual richness to the film. The background score, composed by a noted Malayalam musician, underscores emotional moments without becoming intrusive. Editing is generally smooth, though a few sequences linger longer than necessary, affecting pacing. Production design authentically reflects a middle‑class Malayali setting, adding credibility to the milieu.

Verdict

Avarachan and Sons is a watchable effort that benefits from Biju Menon’s steady presence and competent technical craft. Yet, it falls short of the emotional resonance and narrative ingenuity displayed by Kumbalangi Nights. For viewers seeking a sincere family drama, the film offers moments of genuine feeling; for those expecting a breakthrough in Malayalam cinema, it may feel like a missed opportunity.

  • Strong central performance by Biju Menon
  • Effective cinematography and authentic production design
  • Predictable screenplay lacking depth
  • Under‑utilized supporting cast
  • Melodramatic direction that tempers realism
