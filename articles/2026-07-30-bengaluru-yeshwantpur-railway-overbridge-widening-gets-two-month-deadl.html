Bengaluru: Yeshwantpur Railway Overbridge Widening Gets Two-Month Deadline

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has set a two-month deadline for widening the Yeshwantpur railway overbridge.
  • The project is intended to ease chronic traffic congestion on this key arterial route connecting Yeshwantpur to other parts of the city.
  • Officials have not yet disclosed specific lane expansion details or the total cost of the widening work.

The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has set a two-month deadline to widen the Yeshwantpur railway overbridge. The move is aimed at tackling the chronic traffic congestion that plagues this critical arterial stretch.

Commuters using the bridge have long complained of snarls, particularly during peak hours. The structure, which connects Yeshwantpur to other parts of the city, is a vital link for thousands of daily travellers.

Key Arterial Route Under Focus

The overbridge serves as a major connector between Yeshwantpur, Peenya Industrial Area, and central Bengaluru. Traffic jams here are routine, often stretching wait times by 20-30 minutes during rush hour.

The BBMP's decision comes after persistent demands from local residents and commuter groups. The civic body has now tasked the executing agency to complete the widening within the stipulated timeframe.

Scope of Work Not Yet Detailed

Officials have not yet disclosed the specific number of lanes to be added. The total cost of the project also remains unannounced.

The widening is expected to include structural modifications to the bridge. Engineers will need to assess load-bearing capacities and traffic flow patterns before finalising the design.

BBMP sources indicate that the deadline is tight. The agency will have to coordinate with the railways, as the overbridge spans active railway lines near Yeshwantpur station.

Expected Impact on Commuters

Once complete, the wider bridge is expected to improve vehicle throughput significantly. Motorists currently face bumper-to-bumper traffic, especially near the Yeshwantpur end where the road narrows.

Pedestrians and two-wheeler riders are also likely to benefit from clearer segregation. The current layout forces all vehicles into a single bottleneck, creating safety hazards.

Residents of nearby localities like Mathikere and Rajajinagar have welcomed the announcement. They hope the project will reduce their daily commute time by at least 15 minutes.

Monitoring and Penalties

The BBMP has warned of penalties if the two-month deadline is not met. A senior official said the agency will review progress weekly.

Any delay beyond the deadline could result in cost overruns and further inconvenience. The civic body is keen to avoid the kind of protracted delays seen in other infrastructure projects across the city.

The widening work is likely to begin within the next few days. Temporary traffic diversions are expected, though officials have not yet detailed alternative routes.

What happens next depends on how swiftly the executing agency can secure necessary approvals from the railways and commence work. All eyes are now on the two-month countdown.
