10 held for assault on engineer during Bengaluru footpath clearance

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • 10 persons arrested for assaulting a GBA engineer in Bengaluru.
  • Attack occurred during a footpath clearance drive.
  • Engineer was part of a team removing encroachments.

Assault during clearance drive

Ten people have been arrested in Bengaluru for allegedly assaulting an engineer of the Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) during a footpath clearance drive. The incident took place on Tuesday when the engineer and his team were removing encroachments along a major road in the city.

The engineer sustained injuries in the attack. Police arrived at the scene and apprehended the suspects shortly after the incident was reported.

Details of the incident

The clearance drive was part of ongoing efforts by the GBA to clear footpaths of illegal structures and vendors. Officials said the team had faced resistance from local shopkeepers and residents before the situation turned violent.

Witnesses claimed the attackers were agitated by the removal of temporary structures. The engineer was rushed to a nearby hospital for treatment. His condition is reported to be stable.

Arrests and investigation

Police have registered a case under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code. The arrested individuals have been taken into custody for questioning.

Authorities are investigating whether the assault was premeditated or a spontaneous reaction to the drive. Additional security has been deployed in the area to prevent further incidents.

Reaction from authorities

Senior GBA officials condemned the attack. They reiterated that footpath clearance drives are necessary for pedestrian safety and urban planning.

"We will not tolerate violence against our staff. Legal action will be taken against all those responsible," a spokesperson said. The police have assured a thorough investigation.

Context of footpath clearances

Footpath encroachments have been a persistent issue in Bengaluru. The GBA has been conducting regular drives to reclaim public space for pedestrians. Such operations often lead to friction with vendors and residents who depend on these spaces for livelihood.

This is not the first instance of resistance. Previous drives have also seen clashes, though arrests are less common. The latest incident has prompted calls for better crowd management during such operations.

As the investigation continues, the GBA is expected to review its clearance protocols. The focus will likely remain on balancing urban development with the concerns of those affected by the drives.
