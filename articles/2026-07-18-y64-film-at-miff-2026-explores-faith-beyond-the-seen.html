Y64 Film at MIFF 2026 Explores Faith Beyond the Seen

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Y64 – Whispers of the Unseen premiered at MIFF 2026.
  • The film explores faith, mysticism, and the unseen in Indian spirituality.
  • Festival audience and critics engaged with its philosophical themes.

Mumbai Festival Hosts Unusual Documentary

The Mumbai International Film Festival (MIFF) 2026 saw the premiere of Y64 – Whispers of the Unseen, a documentary that steps away from conventional narratives. The film, directed by an independent filmmaker, does not focus on rituals or organised religion. Instead, it looks at the spaces between — what people sense but cannot prove.

Screenings drew modest but attentive crowds. The film’s title, Y64, refers to a conceptual framework rather than a specific year. The number itself has been interpreted by some as a cipher for cycles of time or hidden knowledge.

What the Film Shows

The documentary weaves together interviews with monks, neuroscientists, and ordinary devotees. It juxtaposes ancient temple architecture with modern-day accounts of unexplained phenomena. One segment follows a group of pilgrims in the Himalayas who claim to hear sounds without a source. Another examines how certain tribal communities in central India perceive deities that have no physical form.

The director has stated that the project began as a personal exploration. "I wanted to understand why people believe in things they cannot see," they said during a post-screening Q&A. The film does not offer conclusions. It presents questions and lets the audience sit with them.

Audience Response and Critique

Initial reviews at the festival have been mixed but engaged. Some critics praised the film for its visual storytelling — long, meditative shots of empty temples and fog-covered forests. Others found it too abstract for a festival audience used to clear narratives. A few viewers walked out during the first screening, festival sources said.

"It is not a film you watch. It is a film you experience," one attendee told reporters. Another called it "pretentious" but admitted it stayed in their mind for hours after.

Broader Context of Faith in Cinema

Indian cinema has long grappled with religious themes, but mostly through mythological epics or social dramas. Y64 takes a different path. It aligns more with the slow cinema movement, where atmosphere carries meaning. The film does not name any specific god or scripture. It keeps its focus on the act of believing itself.

Scholars of religious studies who attended the festival noted that the film taps into a growing global interest in non-institutional spirituality. "People are moving away from dogma and towards personal experience," one academic said. The film seems to reflect that shift.

What Comes Next

Y64 – Whispers of the Unseen is now seeking distribution deals. The director plans to take it to other festivals in Asia and Europe. A limited theatrical release in India is expected later this year, though no dates have been confirmed.

The film may not find a wide audience. But for those who see it, it offers something rare — a quiet space to reflect on the mysteries that remain, even in an age of information.
