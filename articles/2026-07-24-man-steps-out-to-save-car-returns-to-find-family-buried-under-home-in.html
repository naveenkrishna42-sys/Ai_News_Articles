Man Steps Out to Save Car, Returns to Find Family Buried Under Home in J&K Floods

📅 2026-07-24 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Death toll in Jammu region flash floods rises to 26 after woman's body recovered in Poonch.
  • A man lost his wife and children when their house, which he called his 'Taj Mahal', collapsed while he was saving his car.
  • Jammu-Srinagar highway remained shut for a second day as landslides and boulders blocked the route.
  • Congress demanded a special relief package for flood-hit Jammu and Kashmir.

A man in Jammu and Kashmir stepped out of his home to move his car to higher ground. By the time he returned, his house had collapsed. His wife and children were buried under the rubble.

The man had built the house with his own hands. He often called it his 'Taj Mahal'. The structure could not withstand the flash floods that swept through the region. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of family members killed in the incident.

Death Toll Rises to 26

The floods have now claimed at least 26 lives across the Jammu region. The body of a woman was recovered in Poonch district, pushing the toll higher. Rescue teams are still searching for missing persons in the worst-affected areas.

Heavy rain triggered multiple landslides, blocking roads and cutting off villages. The administration has deployed additional teams to clear debris and reach stranded families. Many areas remain without power or communication links.

Highway Closed, Landslides Block Route

The Jammu-Srinagar National Highway remained shut for a second consecutive day. Giant boulders rolled onto the road at several points, making travel impossible. Videos shared on social media showed large rocks crashing down near moving vehicles.

Traffic officials said it could take days to fully clear the highway. Hundreds of vehicles are stuck on both sides of the route. The Border Roads Organisation has been pressed into service for the clearance operation.

Political Response and Demands

The Congress party has demanded a special relief package for flood-hit Jammu and Kashmir. Party leaders said the central government must release funds immediately for rescue and rehabilitation. They also criticised the state administration for being ill-prepared for the monsoon season.

Local residents have complained of slow response times. Many said they received no warning before the water levels rose. The meteorological department had issued a heavy rain alert, but the scale of the flooding caught many off guard.

The tragedy of the man losing his family while trying to save his car has become a symbol of the disaster's sudden brutality. Neighbours described him as a hardworking man who spent years building the home that became his family's tomb.

As the rain continues, authorities are urging people in low-lying areas to move to relief camps. The next 48 hours will be critical as more weather systems are expected to hit the region.
