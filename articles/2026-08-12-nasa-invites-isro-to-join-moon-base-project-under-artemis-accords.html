NASA invites ISRO to join Moon Base project under Artemis Accords

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Space Original source ↗
Key points
  • NASA formally invites ISRO to participate in the Artemis-led Moon Base initiative.
  • Invitation comes under the framework of the Artemis Accords, which India signed in 2023.
  • Move signals deepening collaboration between the two space agencies on lunar exploration.

NASA's invitation to ISRO

The United States space agency NASA has formally invited the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) to join its Moon Base project, a key component of the Artemis programme. The invitation, extended under the Artemis Accords, marks a significant step in bilateral space cooperation between India and the US.

Officials familiar with the development said the proposal was communicated through diplomatic channels earlier this week. While the specifics of India's potential role in the lunar outpost are yet to be outlined, the move is seen as a recognition of ISRO's growing capabilities in deep-space missions.

Artemis Accords and India's position

India signed the Artemis Accords in June 2023, committing to a set of principles for peaceful and transparent exploration of the Moon, Mars, and beyond. The accords, initiated by NASA, now have over 40 signatory nations. Joining the Moon Base project would make India one of the few countries to have a direct stake in the long-term human presence on the lunar surface.

ISRO has not yet issued an official response to the invitation. However, sources indicate that the Indian space agency is evaluating the technical and financial implications of participating in such a large-scale international endeavour.

What is the Moon Base project?

The Moon Base, also referred to as the Artemis Base Camp, is envisioned as a sustainable outpost near the lunar south pole. It is designed to support extended crewed missions, scientific research, and the testing of technologies needed for future Mars expeditions. The project relies heavily on international partnerships, and NASA has been actively seeking collaborators to share costs and expertise.

India's Chandrayaan-3 mission, which successfully landed near the lunar south pole in 2023, demonstrated the country's ability to operate in this challenging terrain. This achievement is believed to have strengthened ISRO's case as a valuable partner in the Moon Base initiative.

Implications for India-US space ties

The invitation comes at a time when India and the US have been expanding their space collaboration. In 2024, the two nations announced a joint mission to the International Space Station, with Indian astronauts training at NASA facilities. The Moon Base proposal, if accepted, would deepen this partnership further and position India as a key player in the next phase of lunar exploration.

Experts note that participation would not only boost India's technological prowess but also open avenues for its private space sector. Indian startups and companies involved in space technology could benefit from contracts and knowledge transfer associated with such a project.

Neither NASA nor ISRO has confirmed the timeline for a decision. The Indian government is expected to take a call after inter-ministerial consultations, given the strategic and financial stakes involved.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on ISRO's formal response and the subsequent negotiations on the scope of India's participation. The outcome could shape not just the future of lunar exploration, but also the broader strategic partnership between New Delhi and Washington in the space domain.
