Hyderabad sees spike in viral fevers, dengue-like illnesses amid wet weather

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • City hospitals see rising outpatient cases of viral fever and dengue-like symptoms
  • Continuous rains and waterlogging create breeding grounds for mosquitoes
  • Health officials advise preventive measures and early testing

Hyderabad is witnessing a sharp increase in viral fevers and dengue-like illnesses, with hospitals across the city reporting a steady stream of patients. The recent spell of wet weather, marked by intermittent rains and waterlogging, has created ideal conditions for mosquito breeding and the spread of water-borne infections.

Physicians say outpatient departments are seeing more cases of high fever, body aches, and joint pain — symptoms commonly associated with dengue and other viral infections. While most cases are mild, doctors caution that delayed diagnosis can lead to complications, especially in children and the elderly.

Hospitals on alert

Several government and private hospitals in Hyderabad have set up separate queues for fever cases to manage the inflow. "We are seeing at least 30 to 40 fever cases a day, with a significant number testing positive for dengue," a senior physician at a city hospital told Telangana Today.

Health officials have not yet confirmed an official count of dengue cases this season, but the trend is consistent with past years when post-monsoon months saw a spike. The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation (GHMC) has intensified fogging drives in residential colonies and warned residents to clear stagnant water from coolers, planters, and other containers.

Why the surge now

The ongoing wet spell, which began in late July, has left many low-lying areas waterlogged. Stagnant water serves as a breeding ground for Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, the primary vector for dengue. The intermittent nature of the rains — heavy showers followed by warm, humid days — accelerates mosquito breeding cycles.

Municipal authorities have identified several hotspots, including old city areas, and are conducting door-to-door checks for larvae. However, residents in some neighbourhoods complain that fogging is irregular and that garbage accumulation near drains is worsening the problem.

Prevention and early action

Doctors advise people to use mosquito repellents, wear full-sleeved clothing, and keep surroundings dry. They also stress the importance of early testing — a simple blood test can confirm dengue or other viral infections within a day.

"If fever persists for more than two days, especially with severe headache or pain behind the eyes, do not self-medicate. Visit a doctor immediately," a public health expert recommended. The state health department has issued advisories to private clinics to report suspected dengue cases promptly to enable timely surveillance.

What to watch

With the monsoon expected to continue for a few more weeks, officials warn that case numbers could rise further. The GHMC has promised to intensify anti-larval operations and is urging residents to cooperate. Whether this spike turns into a full-blown outbreak will depend on how quickly authorities and citizens act to eliminate mosquito breeding sites.
