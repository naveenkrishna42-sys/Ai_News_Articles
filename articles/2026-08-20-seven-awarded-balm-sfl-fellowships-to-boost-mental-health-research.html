Seven awarded BALM-SFL fellowships to boost mental health research

📅 2026-08-20 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Seven fellows selected for the prestigious mental health research program.
  • Recipients represent diverse professional backgrounds from five states.
  • The fellowship focuses on advancing mental health leadership and policy.

New cohort selected for mental health leadership

The Banyan Academy of Leadership in Mental Health (BALM) has officially announced the list of recipients for the latest edition of the BALM-SFL fellowship. Seven individuals from five states across India have been selected for this initiative.

This fellowship aims to support professionals dedicated to driving change in the mental health sector. By fostering research and leadership, the academy intends to address systemic gaps in care and advocacy.

Diverse representation across states

The selection process focused on identifying candidates with a strong track record in social sciences, public policy, and clinical practice. The seven fellows hail from five distinct states, ensuring a broad geographical perspective on mental health challenges in the country.

Bringing together experts from different regions allows for a more comprehensive understanding of local versus national trends. This diversity is expected to enrich the collaborative research projects undertaken during the fellowship period.

Focus on policy and practice

The fellowship program provides recipients with the resources needed to execute independent research projects. These projects often culminate in policy recommendations or new frameworks for community-based mental health support.

Participants will spend their term engaging with mentors and accessing the academy’s extensive network of professionals. The objective remains clear: to translate academic inquiry into tangible improvements for mental health accessibility in India.

Future initiatives and expectations

The academy continues to play a significant role in shaping the discourse around mental health leadership. By investing in these seven fellows, BALM seeks to create a sustainable pipeline of experts who can influence future government health policies.

The fellows are expected to begin their research modules immediately. Observers will be watching to see how these projects evolve and whether they result in actionable changes for mental health infrastructure in their respective home states.
