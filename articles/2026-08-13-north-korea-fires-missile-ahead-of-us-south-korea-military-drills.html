North Korea Fires Missile Ahead of US-South Korea Military Drills

📅 2026-08-13 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • North Korea fired an unidentified missile toward the sea on Tuesday.
  • The launch comes ahead of upcoming US-South Korea military drills.
  • South Korean and Japanese authorities are assessing the projectile's details.

North Korea fired an unidentified projectile toward the sea on Tuesday, according to reports from regional militaries, just days before the United States and South Korea are set to begin their annual joint military drills. The launch is the latest in a series of weapons tests by Pyongyang that have kept tensions on the Korean Peninsula simmering.

The projectile was detected in the morning hours, with South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff confirming the launch but providing few immediate details. Japan's coast guard also issued a notice for vessels in the area, urging caution. Neither military has yet confirmed the missile's type or how far it travelled.

Timing and Intent

The timing of the launch appears deliberate. The US and South Korea are scheduled to hold their annual summer drills, which have long drawn condemnation from Pyongyang, which views them as rehearsals for an invasion. North Korea has consistently responded to these exercises with missile tests, a pattern that has held for years.

Analysts say the launch likely aims to register displeasure ahead of the drills and to test new or existing systems under operational conditions. The exact range and trajectory are still being assessed, but initial reports suggest the missile flew toward the sea off North Korea's east coast, landing in international waters.

Regional Reactions

South Korea's National Security Council convened an emergency meeting to discuss the launch, while Japan's defence ministry said it was gathering information and coordinating with allies. The United States, which maintains about 28,500 troops in South Korea, has not yet issued a formal statement, but the allies are expected to reaffirm their joint defence posture.

The UN Security Council has imposed multiple rounds of sanctions on North Korea over its weapons programmes, but Pyongyang has continued to develop and test missiles, including intercontinental ballistic missiles capable of reaching the US mainland. Tuesday's launch, however, appears to be of a shorter-range system, though officials have not yet confirmed the specifics.

Historical Context

North Korea has conducted dozens of missile tests in recent years, often timed to coincide with US-South Korean exercises or diplomatic milestones. The pace of testing has increased under leader Kim Jong Un, who has prioritised advancing the country's nuclear and missile capabilities despite international condemnation.

The upcoming drills, known as Ulchi Freedom Shield, are scheduled to begin later this month and will involve computer-simulated command post exercises along with field training. Pyongyang has previously called such drills a 'reckless war rehearsal' and threatened 'overwhelming' responses.

What to Watch

South Korean and Japanese authorities are expected to release more detailed information on the missile's characteristics in the coming hours. The launch could also prompt a fresh round of UN discussions, though divisions among permanent Security Council members have hampered unified action in recent months. All eyes will be on Pyongyang's next move as the drills approach.
