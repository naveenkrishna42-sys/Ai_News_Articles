Man dies after drunk driver's crash on Chennai ECR

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Victim died after being injured in a crash involving an alleged drunk driver on ECR.
  • Incident occurred on East Coast Road, Chennai; police investigating.
  • Drunk driving remains a concern on Chennai's highways.

Chennai: A 34-year-old man succumbed to injuries on Monday after being hit by a car driven by an allegedly drunk driver on East Coast Road (ECR) earlier this week. The victim, identified as R. Kumar, a resident of Thiruvanmiyur, was crossing the road near the Muttukadu stretch when the vehicle struck him.

Police said the driver, identified as S. Prakash, 29, was returning from a party and was reportedly intoxicated at the time of the incident. The crash occurred around 11:30 pm on Saturday, and Kumar was rushed to a nearby private hospital with severe head injuries. He was later referred to a government hospital, where he died on Monday evening.

Accident details and police response

According to the Neelankarai traffic police, the car was speeding when it hit Kumar, who was walking along the service lane. The impact threw him several feet, and the driver initially fled the scene but was later apprehended by local residents who chased him down.

A case has been registered under sections of the Indian Penal Code, including causing death by negligence, and the Motor Vehicles Act for drunk driving. The accused has been remanded in judicial custody, police said, adding that his blood alcohol content was found to be three times the permissible limit.

Witnesses and victim's family

Witnesses told police that the car was zigzagging and nearly hit two other pedestrians before striking Kumar. The victim, who worked as a private firm's accountant, was returning home after a late shift. His family, who were informed of the accident, have demanded strict action against the driver.

"We want the harshest punishment possible. This was not an accident but a crime," said Kumar's elder brother, who declined to be named. The family has also urged authorities to enhance road safety measures on ECR, which has seen a spate of drunk driving incidents in recent months.

Drunk driving on ECR: A recurring issue

ECR, a popular stretch for nightlife and weekend getaways, has been a hotspot for drunk driving cases. According to data from the Chennai traffic police, over 200 cases were registered for drunk driving on ECR and the adjoining Old Mahabalipuram Road in the first half of this year alone. Despite regular checkpoints, many motorists continue to flout rules, leading to accidents.

In a separate incident last month, a bike rider was killed on the same stretch after being hit by a car whose driver was allegedly intoxicated. Local residents and activists have repeatedly called for more stringent enforcement, including the installation of speed cameras and breathalyzer tests at random intervals.

Legal and administrative measures

Chennai police commissioner has directed senior officials to intensify patrols on ECR, especially during late-night hours. Additional checkpoints have been set up near popular eateries and resorts, and traffic police have been equipped with new breathalyzer devices.

Meanwhile, the transport department is considering a proposal to install speed governors on private vehicles, though such a move has faced opposition from automakers and driver unions. The state government has also announced plans to increase fines for drunk driving, but the bill is yet to be tabled in the assembly.

As Kumar's family prepares for his final rites, his death has reignited the debate on road safety in Chennai. Traffic experts argue that stricter laws alone won't solve the problem; they stress the need for awareness campaigns and better enforcement.

What happens next will depend on how quickly the judicial process moves. The accused's bail plea is scheduled for hearing later this week, and the police are expected to file a chargesheet within 30 days. For now, the incident serves as a grim reminder of the consequences of drink driving.
