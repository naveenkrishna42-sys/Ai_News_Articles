Gift Nifty muted ahead of US-Iran talks; Vaishali Parekh picks three stocks

  • Gift Nifty edges up but signals muted opening amid unresolved US-Iran negotiations.
  • Vaishali Parekh suggests three stocks for intraday or short-term trading.
  • Markets await US inflation data and Q1 earnings for direction.

GIFT Nifty inched higher in early trade on Monday, but the gains were capped as a much-anticipated US-Iran deal remained elusive. The index, which tracks Nifty50 futures on the Singapore exchange, pointed to a cautious start for Dalal Street after last week's choppy sessions.

Investors are keeping a close watch on West Asia, where diplomatic efforts have yet to produce a breakthrough. The lack of a concrete agreement has kept oil prices volatile, adding to the uncertainty for equity markets.

Market opens flat, focus shifts to macros

Domestic indices are likely to open flat, with the Sensex and Nifty50 expected to trade in a narrow band. Analysts say the market is in a wait-and-watch mode, as traders avoid building large positions ahead of key triggers.

US inflation data due this week will be crucial for global cues. A hotter-than-expected print could alter expectations around interest rate cuts, which would have ripple effects on emerging markets like India.

Oil and geopolitical overhang

The Iran-Oman deal, which markets had hoped would ease Middle East tensions, has not materialised yet. Diplomatic channels remain active, but no timeline has been announced for a formal agreement.

Brent crude prices have stayed elevated, and any escalation in the region could push them higher. For India, a net importer of oil, rising crude prices pose a risk to inflation and the fiscal deficit.

Meanwhile, the first quarter earnings season is in full swing. Results from index heavyweights have been mixed, with some sectors showing resilience and others lagging expectations. This has kept stock-specific action lively even as the broader market consolidates.

What lies ahead for Dalal Street

This week, traders will also track domestic inflation numbers and industrial production data. The rupee's movement against the dollar and foreign institutional investor flows will add to the sentiment.

Given the multiple variables at play, analysts recommend a selective approach. The market is not in a runaway rally, but neither is it in a free fall. A breakout above recent highs could invite fresh buying, while a breakdown below support would trigger profit booking.

For now, the Street is bracing for a tentative start. The next directional cue may come from the US inflation print and any progress on the diplomatic front. Until then, patience is the watchword.
