Sensex, Nifty Fall as Red Sea Tensions Stoke Oil Supply Worries

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty dropped over 1% each in early trade on July 23, 2026.
  • Renewed attacks on commercial vessels in the Red Sea have disrupted key shipping routes.
  • Rising crude oil prices threaten to widen India's trade deficit and stoke inflation.
  • Investors shifted to safe-haven assets amid uncertainty over global supply chains.

Indian equity markets opened sharply lower on Thursday, with the BSE Sensex and NSE Nifty shedding over a percentage point each in early trade. The sell-off was triggered by escalating attacks on merchant vessels in the Red Sea, reviving fears of a sustained disruption to global oil supplies.

The Sensex fell more than 700 points in the first hour of trading, while the Nifty slipped below the 24,500 mark. Broader market indices also bled, with midcap and smallcap stocks facing the brunt of the selling pressure.

Red Sea Attacks Rattle Global Trade

Reports emerged overnight of fresh strikes on cargo ships transiting the Bab el-Mandeb strait, a critical chokepoint for oil tankers heading from the Middle East to Europe and North America. The attacks, claimed by Iran-aligned Houthi rebels, have forced several shipping companies to divert vessels around the Cape of Good Hope, adding weeks to transit times.

India imports roughly 85% of its crude oil requirements, making it acutely vulnerable to any disruption along this route. The immediate market reaction mirrored global trends, with Asian peers such as Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng also trading in the red.

Crude Prices Inch Higher

Brent crude futures rose past $86 per barrel on Thursday morning, extending gains from the previous session. Analysts warned that a sustained spike could push prices toward $90, a level that historically strains India's fiscal math.

Higher oil prices widen India's trade deficit and exert upward pressure on retail inflation, complicating the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy stance. The rupee weakened past 83.50 against the US dollar in early trade, reflecting the nervousness among forex traders.

Oil marketing companies—BPCL, HPCL, and IOC—declined between 2% and 3% on the NSE. Aviation stocks, including InterGlobe Aviation and SpiceJet, also fell sharply on fears of higher jet fuel costs.

Sectoral Sell-Off Broadens

Losses were broad-based, with banking, auto, and metal stocks leading the decline. The Nifty Bank index dropped over 1.5% as HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India all posted losses.

Auto stocks, sensitive to fuel price changes, saw Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, and Mahindra & Mahindra fall between 1% and 2%. Metal stocks tracked global weakness, with Hindalco and Tata Steel slipping over 2% each.

Only defensive sectors such as IT and pharma showed relative resilience, with the Nifty IT index trading flat. Investors rotated into safe-haven assets, sending gold prices higher in domestic markets.

What to Watch

Market participants will closely monitor diplomatic efforts to de-escalate tensions in the Red Sea. Any further disruptions to shipping could prompt the Indian government to consider tapping strategic oil reserves or engaging in bilateral talks with supplier nations. The next major trigger will be the weekly US crude inventory data due later today.
