Chennai conservancy worker becomes Instagram sensation

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Chennai conservancy worker's Instagram videos have gone viral, earning him thousands of followers.
  • His content shows the realities of street sweeping and garbage collection in the city.
  • The worker says he wants to break stereotypes and show dignity in all forms of labour.

A conservancy worker in Chennai has become an unlikely social media star, using Instagram to share the day-to-day reality of his job. His videos, filmed on a basic smartphone during his shifts, have attracted thousands of followers who say they never saw sanitation work this way before.

From streets to screens

The man, who works for the Greater Chennai Corporation, started posting short clips earlier this year. In them, he sweeps pavements, collects garbage from narrow lanes, and loads waste into municipal trucks. He does not use fancy editing or background music. The rawness of the footage is what draws people in.

One video shows him clearing a choked drain by hand under a hot sun. Another captures him smiling and waving at a resident who offers him water. The comments section is filled with messages of respect and gratitude. Many users say they will never look at a conservancy worker the same way again.

Changing perceptions

In interviews, the worker has said he did not expect the attention. He started the account to document his own life, not to become famous. But as the following grew, he realised he had a platform to change how society views sanitation work.

“People think this is a dirty job, a low job. But without us, the city would be unlivable,” he said in a recent post. His videos often include captions in Tamil and English, urging viewers to treat conservancy workers with dignity. He has also used his reach to highlight the lack of basic facilities like clean drinking water and restrooms for municipal staff.

A quiet revolution

The phenomenon is part of a broader trend where blue-collar workers in India are using social media to claim visibility. From auto drivers to vegetable vendors, many are building audiences by showing their authentic work lives. For conservancy workers, this is especially significant given the stigma attached to manual scavenging and street cleaning.

The Chennai worker’s account has also caught the attention of local officials. A corporation supervisor said they were aware of the account and appreciated the positive representation. However, no official endorsement or collaboration has been announced yet.

What the followers say

Regular viewers of the account describe it as humbling. One follower posted: “I see him working in the same heat I complain about. He never complains. It makes me want to be a better person.” Another wrote: “My children now wave at our street cleaner. This is the change we need.”

The account has not been without criticism. Some commenters have accused the worker of seeking sympathy or monetising his hardship. He has responded by saying he is not asking for charity, only respect. He does not run ads or sell products. His only request, he says, is that people stop throwing garbage on the streets.

The worker continues to post daily. His follower count is now in the tens of thousands and climbing. Whether he will turn this into a larger movement or simply keep sweeping and sharing remains to be seen. For now, he has done something rare — made a city stop and think about the person holding the broom.

What comes next is unclear. But the conversation he has started about dignity of labour in urban India is unlikely to fade anytime soon.
