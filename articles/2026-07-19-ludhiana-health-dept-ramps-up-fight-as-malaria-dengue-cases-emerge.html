Ludhiana health dept ramps up fight as malaria, dengue cases emerge

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • New malaria and dengue cases reported in Ludhiana.
  • Health department steps up fogging and surveillance drives.
  • Larvae found at multiple homes; notices served to residents.

Ludhiana's health department has intensified its battle against vector-borne diseases after fresh cases of malaria and dengue surfaced across the city. Officials are now scrambling to contain the spread as the monsoon season heightens the risk.

The department has deployed additional teams for fogging, larvicide spraying, and door-to-door inspections. Residents in affected areas have been urged to keep their surroundings clean and report any stagnant water sources immediately.

Larvae found during house-to-house checks

During routine inspections, health workers discovered mosquito larvae in several homes and neighbourhoods. Notices were issued to owners of premises where breeding sites were found, warning them to clear stagnant water or face legal action.

Teams are also conducting awareness campaigns, educating people about the importance of using mosquito nets, repellents, and keeping water containers covered. The drive is part of the broader anti-dengue and anti-malaria programme launched earlier this year.

Amritsar intensifies dengue surveillance

Meanwhile, in neighbouring Amritsar, health authorities have ramped up dengue surveillance after larvae were detected in multiple locations. Officials say the situation is under control but caution that complacency could lead to a spike in cases.

Amritsar's health department has increased the frequency of fogging operations and is conducting random checks at construction sites, schools, and public parks. Teams have also been instructed to report any clusters of fever cases immediately for rapid testing.

Public cooperation key to curbing spread

Health officials in both cities are stressing the need for public cooperation. Simple steps like emptying coolers, changing flower vase water weekly, and disposing of old tyres can significantly reduce mosquito breeding.

Residents have been advised to seek medical help if they develop symptoms such as high fever, severe headache, joint pain, or rashes. Early detection and treatment are critical to prevent complications from both dengue and malaria.

The health department has also set up dedicated fever clinics in high-risk areas and is ensuring adequate stocks of testing kits and medicines. Additional staff have been deployed to handle any potential surge in patients.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of cases reported so far, but sources indicate that the count is still low compared to previous years. However, with the monsoon expected to continue for several weeks, authorities are not taking any chances.

In the coming days, the health department plans to expand its surveillance network and involve local community leaders to spread awareness. The focus remains on preventing an outbreak before it escalates.
