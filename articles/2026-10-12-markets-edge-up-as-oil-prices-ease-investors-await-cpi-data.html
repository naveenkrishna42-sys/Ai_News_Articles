Markets Edge Up as Oil Prices Ease; Investors Await CPI Data

✍️ By TIVRA News Business Desk 📅 2026-10-12 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Markets opened higher as crude oil prices eased, reducing input cost concerns for Indian firms.
  • Investors are holding positions ahead of the upcoming CPI inflation data and RBI policy review.
  • Sectoral gains were led by banking and FMCG, while IT and autos lagged slightly in early trade.

Sensex, Nifty Begin in Green on Relief Rally

The BSE Sensex opened 185 points higher at 76,420, while the NSE Nifty 50 gained 0.25%, driven by gains in HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and HUL. The rebound comes after Brent crude slipped below $82 a barrel, easing concerns over import costs and inflationary pressure on Indian businesses.

Crude Correction Offers Breather

Crude oil prices have fallen nearly 4% this week following reports of easing demand in China and a stronger-than-expected U.S. inventory build. For India, a net importer of crude, every dollar drop in oil prices improves the current account deficit outlook and reduces pressure on retail inflation. Analysts say this sets a supportive tone for domestic equities, especially rate-sensitive sectors like autos and real estate.

Investors Eye Inflation, RBI Policy

Market participants are now awaiting India's August CPI data, scheduled for release later this week. A recent survey by ICRA suggests retail inflation may have cooled to 4.9% year-on-year, within the RBI's tolerance band. A sub-5% print could reinforce expectations of a status quo on interest rates at the next MPC meeting, further supporting equity valuations. However, any surprise upside in inflation could trigger a swift reversal.

Sectoral Movements and Global Cues

Banking stocks led the charge in early trade, with the Nifty PSU Bank index rising over 1%. FMCG shares also gained traction, up 0.8%, as falling oil prices improved margins for consumer goods companies reliant on energy inputs. In contrast, IT and auto stocks traded flat to lower, reflecting profit-taking after recent rallies and cautious overseas demand signals from the U.S. and Europe.

Global Markets and FPI Activity

On the global front, Wall Street closed mixed overnight, with the Dow Jones flat and the Nasdaq slipping 0.3% amid lingering concerns over the Fed's rate trajectory. Meanwhile, foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) continued their selling streak, offloading equities worth ₹1,200 crore in the last session. Despite this, domestic institutional buyers have shown resilience, absorbing much of the supply and keeping the market base supported.

  • FPI selling persists, but strong domestic buying limits downside pressure.
