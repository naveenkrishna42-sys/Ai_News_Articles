Ram Temple CEO applicants asked about janeu, diet in Google form

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Applicants for Ram Temple CEO post were asked about wearing janeu, diet, and drinking habits via Google form
  • The selection process has drawn criticism over lack of transparency
  • Former IPS officer and ex-Ayodhya DM among contenders; three names to be shortlisted

The selection process for the chief executive officer of the Ram Temple Trust has taken an unusual turn, with applicants being asked personal questions through a Google form. Candidates reportedly had to answer whether they wear the janeu (sacred thread), consume non-vegetarian food, or drink alcohol.

The questionnaire, part of the initial screening, has sparked debate over the criteria being used to pick the person who will manage the sprawling temple complex in Ayodhya. While the trust has not officially commented on the form, several applicants have expressed surprise at the nature of the queries.

Transparency concerns raised

One applicant, who spoke to Hindustan Times on condition of anonymity, called for greater transparency in the selection process. The candidate said the Google form, though seemingly simple, lacked clarity on how responses would be weighed against professional qualifications.

The trust began interviews for the full-time CEO post recently, with a shortlist of three names expected to emerge. The job involves overseeing daily operations, administration, and the growing network of facilities at the temple, which has become a major pilgrimage and tourist destination since its consecration in 2024.

Contenders in the fray

Among those reported to be in the running are a former IPS officer known as an 'encounter specialist' and a former district magistrate of Ayodhya. Both bring administrative and security experience, but the inclusion of lifestyle questions has shifted focus away from their professional records.

The janeu, a sacred thread worn by upper-caste Hindus, and dietary habits are seen by many as markers of personal piety. However, critics argue that such criteria could exclude capable candidates who do not conform to a narrow cultural template.

Process under scrutiny

The use of a Google form for what is a high-profile religious appointment has also raised eyebrows. Some applicants have questioned whether such a format is appropriate for evaluating leadership skills, especially for a role that requires handling crores of rupees in donations and coordinating with government agencies.

The trust, which is headed by Mahant Nritya Gopal Das, has yet to release an official statement on the selection criteria. The absence of a formal job description has led to speculation about what the trust prioritises—devotion or administrative acumen.

In the past, similar appointments in religious bodies have often leaned on personal trust and community standing. But the scale of the Ram Temple's operations, including hospitality for millions of pilgrims, demands a professional manager, observers note.

The final decision on the CEO is expected within weeks, with the trust likely to announce the shortlisted names soon. The chosen candidate will inherit a role that carries both spiritual significance and substantial administrative responsibility.

What remains to be seen is whether the trust will clarify its evaluation process or stick to its current approach. For now, applicants wait, and the debate over what makes a suitable CEO for one of India's most revered temples continues.
