Why Your CIBIL Score Matters

The CIBIL score, maintained by the Credit Information Bureau of India (CIBIL), serves as your financial identity metric in the country. Lenders use it to assess creditworthiness before extending loans, credit cards, or home financing. A higher score unlocks better interest rates, simpler approval processes, and greater access to credit products.

The 9 Habits That Work

Timeliness is the single most powerful predictor of CIBIL score movement. Even a single late payment can trigger a significant drop. Setting up automatic payments for recurring bills eliminates human error and guarantees punctuality.

3. Keep Credit Utilization Below 30%

Credit utilization—the proportion of available limit used—should stay under 30 percent. At the crore-level rupee scale, this means keeping revolving balances minimal relative to total limits. For example, a $2 lakh limit should ideally remain unused beyond ₹60,000.

Hard inquiries occur when lenders pull your credit report for a loan or credit application. Excessive short-term inquiries can depress your score. Space out applications and wait at least three months between new credit requests.

A free annual credit report from CIBIL allows you to verify accuracy, dispute errors promptly, and track progress. Watching for delays or discrepancies early prevents minor issues from becoming major score-reducing events.

Length of credit history matters. Older accounts contribute to your average age and signal long-term credit management. Closing accounts that haven’t been used recently may reduce this term significantly.

Having multiple credit types—such as installment loans alongside revolving credit—increases your credit profile breadth. However, avoid unnecessary diversity; focus first on solid performance across existing categories.
