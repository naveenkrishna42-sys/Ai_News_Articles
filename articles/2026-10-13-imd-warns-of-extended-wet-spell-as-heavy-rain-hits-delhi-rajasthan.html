IMD Warns of Extended Wet Spell as Heavy Rain Hits Delhi, Rajasthan

IMD Issues Red Alert as Rain Batters Northern India

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued a 'red' alert—its highest warning level—for heavy to extremely heavy rainfall across Delhi-NCR, eastern Rajasthan, and adjoining regions. As of Monday afternoon, Safdarjung Observatory recorded over 90mm of rainfall in 12 hours, while several areas in western Uttar Pradesh and Haryana received between 60-120mm. This late-season spell is unusual for mid-October and has raised concerns over urban flooding and traffic paralysis.

Why Is This Rainfall So Late and Intense?

Meteorologists attribute the sudden downpour to a combination of factors: a trough running along the monsoon axis, moisture-laden winds from the Bay of Bengal, and possible influence from a developing El Nino pattern in the Pacific. The IMD’s regional head for Delhi noted that such October rainfall events are becoming less predictable due to shifting climatic trends. Additionally, the monsoon withdrawal has been delayed by nearly two weeks, keeping the dynamical conditions favorable for continued precipitation.

Southern States Brace for Extended Wet Spell

Looking ahead, the IMD forecasts another 4 to 5 days of active monsoon conditions across southern India. States like Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Puducherry, and coastal Karnataka are expected to receive above-normal rainfall, with isolated pockets likely to see over 200mm in the next 72 hours. This poses challenges for paddy and banana farmers who were preparing for harvest, as waterlogging can damage standing crops and delay field operations.

Impact on Transportation and Urban Infrastructure

For daily commuters, especially in Delhi-NCR, the red alert translates to heightened risks of road congestion, submerged metro tracks, and delayed flights. The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) has advised citizens to avoid non-essential travel. Meanwhile, the National Highways Authority is on high alert, with teams deployed for emergency drainage and landslide control. In cities like Chennai and Bengaluru, where drainage systems struggle during heavy spells, residents are urged to stay indoors and avoid waterlogged streets.

Key Real-World Takeaways

  • Urban Preparedness: Residents should keep emergency kits ready and avoid traveling during peak rainfall hours.
  • Agricultural Concerns: Farmers in southern states must monitor crop stages closely to prevent waterlogging losses.
  • Transport Disruptions: Rail and flight schedules are likely to change; passengers should verify updates before heading to stations or airports.
  • Climate Pattern Shifts: Delayed monsoon withdrawal points to broader climate variability—long-term planning needs revision.

Looking Beyond the Clouds

While the immediate concern is managing the deluge, experts emphasize that erratic rainfall patterns signal deeper environmental shifts. Urban planners and disaster management authorities are now pushing for updated flood mapping and improved stormwater infrastructure. Citizens can support resilience efforts by following local advisories, minimizing waste that clogs drains, and staying informed through trusted sources like the IMD’s official portal and regional weather bulletins.
