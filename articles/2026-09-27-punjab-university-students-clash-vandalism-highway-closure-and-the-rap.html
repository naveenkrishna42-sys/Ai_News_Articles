Punjab University Students Clash: Vandalism, Highway Closure, and the Rape Rumour Fallout

Background: The Flashpoint

Lehi Singh Purbansh University (LPU), situated on the outskirts of Ludhiana, has been a center for higher education since 1985. In the weeks before September 27, a viral social media post alleged a campus rape that, according to police reports, had not yet been verified by any authority. Opponents cited the post’s graphic nature and the lack of corroborating evidence, while supporters demanded swift, aggressive action.

Sequence of the Day

Morning news reports indicated that students began chanting in the LPU chowk, progressing to bulldozing entrance gates, setting fire to two administrative buildings, and damaging parking facilities. By 10 pm, security forces had to intervene; a small team of 32 personnel from the Punjab Police’s anti‑crime cell was dispatched, reinforced by a state vehicle cluster. Simultaneously a 60‑mile stretch of the Delhi–Amritsar highway ran through Ludhiana, which was blocked from 4 pm to midnight before heavy vehicles were rerouted. The campus itself was closed for 48 hours.

Campus Damage

Initial estimates released on September 28 state that material damage exceeded ₹5 crore, mainly due to the destruction of the Faculty of Science building (₹1 crore) and the administrative block (₹1.5 crore). Utilities were disrupted: power suppliers accused the blast of damaging three transformer towers, causing a 12‑hour outage. When the authorities reopened, they confirmed that 100 students were injured, 63 of them in minor electric shock incidents, while 12 required hospital treatment for burns and burns sustained on the shoulders of protestors.

Highway Blockage Impact

With the defaulting road, the Delhi–Amritsar highway, a vital artery for interstate trade, recorded travel delays of 3.77 hours per passenger on average. The Ministry of Road Transport & Highways confirmed a cargo loss estimate of ₹60 lakh per day and the need for the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) to re‑allocate night‑time crews for re‑patrolling. Large commercial transporters reported a 4.5‑day backlog of shipments, prompting a temporary price spike of up to 5 % for good transport goods.

Security Reevaluation Across the Campus

Security evaluators have identified three deficiencies in the LPU’s layout: the absence of CCTV cameras on all perimeter gates, a lack of real‑time feeds on campus monitors, and under‑staffed night patrols. The campus security budget, ₹10 crore for the fiscal year, was “insufficient to upgrade technology infrastructure”, according to the provincial Inspector General of Police. In response, the state government announced an additional ₹2 crore for the next fiscal year dedicated to modern security suites, including AI‑driven pedestrian alerts.

Legal and Policy Responses

Police filed a case under Section 302 of the Indian Penal Code on quickly committed criminal violence, with charges linked to the alleged vandalism. A preliminary police report stated all alleged perpetrators will be examined, with 7 being detained for FH3 custody; a final “criminal enquiries” will be done in 18‑days. The state’s Minister for Women & Child Development has released an ordinance limiting unverified rumor resolution protocols to a 60‑minute review to avoid “premature violence”.
