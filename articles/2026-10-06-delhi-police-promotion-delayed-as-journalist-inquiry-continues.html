Delhi Police Promotion Delayed as Journalist Inquiry Continues

Background: Promotion Pause and the Viral Video

Army‑cadet Lieutenant Col. Vivek Bhagat, a senior ACP in the Delhi Police’s Central Bureau of Investigation, was slated for elevation to Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP) last month. The promotion, which had been recommended by higher command, was abruptly placed on hold following the filing of a Petition‑In‑Limine (PIL) in the Delhi High Court. The PIL was lodged by a Delhi‑based journalist who alleges that an AI‑generated video depicts the ACP misbehaving with a protesting woman. The video, which surfaced on social media last week, sparked nationwide debate on media ethics and law‑enforcement conduct.

The Legal Inquiry

1. Allegations of Harassment

The journalist’s complaint centres on alleged verbal and physical harassment directed at a protestor during a 2026 civic demonstration in Connaught Place. While the Punjab & Chandigarh Police continue to investigate, the Delhi Police have yet to provide a definitive record of the incident. A formal court summons has been issued to the ACP’s office in the Delhi Police Criminal Investigation Department (CID) for a cross‑examination on the matter.

2. AI‑Generated Footage in the Mix

The disputed footage used deep‑fake technology to superimpose the ACP’s face onto a recorded protest scene. The AI‑generated video is being examined by media lawyers and forensic analysts to verify authenticity. Its circulation raises legal questions about defamation, press freedom, and the use of synthetic media in litigation.

Body‑Cam Adoption and Police Accountability

Implementation Across the Force

Delhi Police installed body‑camera systems on 68 officers during a trial phase last year. Calls for mandating body‑cams during all controversial encounters now gain traction, after the current body‑cam footage revealed an engagement that may mirror the alleged incident. The High Court’s directive is pending, but activists stress that permanent body‑camera use could curb misconduct.

Impact on the DCP Promotion Review

Senior officials note that promotions are contingent on a “clean record” and a statutory review by the Police Reform Authority (PRA). The ongoing inquiry has led the PRA to hold the ACP’s promotion meeting, pending clear findings. The officer’s likelihood to secure the DCP post hinges on the investigation’s outcome and the broader narrative surrounding police conduct.

Stakeholder Perspectives

Law‑enforcement bodies argue that police should focus on public safety, while human‑rights groups insist on procedural transparency. Journalists imparting ID roll‑outs during protest coverage, in line with a recent directive, add context for confirming identities during confrontations.

Implications for Delhi Police Operations

Delhi Police officials state that the probe is “necessary and unavoidable” and that it will not substantively hinder routine policing. The provisional suspension of the DCP promotion to ACP Bhagat is, in their view, a strategic move to present due process rather than punitive action. The case sets an administrative precedent: significant promotions may stall amid litigation to avoid appearing biased.
