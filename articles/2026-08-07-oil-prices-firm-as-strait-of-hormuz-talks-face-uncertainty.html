Oil prices firm as Strait of Hormuz talks face uncertainty

📅 2026-08-07 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil prices remained steady on concerns over Strait of Hormuz plans
  • Trump says Hormuz talks 'moving along' even as Iran mulls US ban
  • Iran, US, Oman close to a deal; details emerge on no-fee reopening plan
  • Houthi attacks add to regional tensions affecting shipping routes

Oil steadies as Hormuz talks face hurdles

Oil prices held firm on Friday, buoyed by persistent concerns over the Strait of Hormuz, one of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints. Traders weighed the latest signals from Washington and Tehran, where diplomatic efforts to secure the passage remain in flux.

US President Donald Trump said talks over the strait were 'moving along', even as reports suggested Iran was considering a ban on American vessels. The mixed messages left markets cautious, with benchmark crude prices fluctuating in a narrow range through the day.

Diplomatic push for a deal

Negotiators from Iran, the United States, and Oman are said to be close to an agreement that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, according to multiple reports. The proposed deal, as outlined by The Times of India, would involve no fees or tolls for passage, a key demand from shipping nations and oil importers.

However, the exact terms remain under wraps, and officials have not yet confirmed the details. NDTV reported that each party has its own priorities: Iran seeks sanctions relief and recognition of its maritime rights, while the US wants guaranteed safe passage for its vessels and allies. Oman, a long-time mediator, aims to maintain stability in the region.

What each side wants

Iran's push for an 'America-free plan' for Hormuz, as noted by economist.com, adds another layer of complexity. Tehran appears to be exploring options that would limit US influence in the strait, even as it engages in talks. This dual-track approach has puzzled analysts and kept oil markets on edge.

For Washington, the priority is clear: preventing any disruption to global oil flows. The strait carries roughly a fifth of the world's petroleum, and any closure would have immediate repercussions on prices worldwide.

Houthi attacks escalate tensions

Adding to the volatility, attacks by Yemen's Houthi rebels have stepped up in recent weeks, targeting shipping lanes near the Red Sea and the Arabian Sea. These actions, while separate from the Hormuz talks, have raised concerns about broader regional instability.

The combination of diplomatic uncertainty and military activity has made traders wary. One analyst noted that the market is 'pricing in a modest risk premium' until there is clarity on both fronts.

Market outlook and next steps

Oil prices are likely to remain sensitive to headlines from the talks. A breakthrough could ease supply concerns and pull prices down, while a breakdown might trigger a sharp rally. For now, the market is watching for any official statement from the negotiating parties.

As the weekend approaches, all eyes are on Oman, where the next round of talks is expected to take place. Whether the parties can bridge their differences on fees, sanctions, and military presence will determine the immediate direction of crude prices.
