Flowers Replace Fish at Mumbai Market, But Stench Lingers

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sassoon Dock transformed from fish to flower market
  • Waste and odour problems persist despite change in goods
  • Local vendors and residents raise concerns over cleanliness

From Catch to Bouquets

Mumbai's historic Sassoon Dock, once a bustling fish market that defined the city's coastal character, has undergone a dramatic transformation. The familiar scent of salt and scales has been replaced by the fragrance of marigolds and roses. But ask anyone living or working nearby, and they'll tell you the change hasn't fixed everything.

Flowers have replaced fish, but the mess remains. The narrow lanes that once saw crates of pomfret and mackerel now overflow with bundles of flowers. The ground, once slick with fish scales, is now carpeted with wilted petals and discarded stems. The stench, locals insist, has merely changed its character.

Waste Woes Continue

The shift from seafood to flowers was meant to address longstanding hygiene complaints. Fish waste attracted stray dogs and flies, and the odour was notorious. But the flower trade comes with its own set of problems. Wet flowers rot quickly, especially during Mumbai's humid monsoon season. The resulting smell, while different, is equally unpleasant for those who live in the surrounding buildings.

Waste piles up daily. Municipal workers struggle to keep up with the volume of organic debris. Vendors often leave behind piles of unsold stock, which decays rapidly. Residents say the situation is as bad as it ever was.

Vendor Concerns

Many vendors who once sold fish have turned to flowers to make a living. They argue that the market's infrastructure was never designed for the new trade. Drainage clogs easily with flower waste. There are no designated spaces for composting or waste segregation.

“We are trying to adapt,” one flower vendor told Mumbai Mirror. “But the problems are the same, just with different smells.” The lack of basic amenities like clean water and proper garbage bins affects both traders and customers.

Municipal Response

The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) has taken note of the complaints. Officials say they are working on a plan to improve waste management at the dock. This includes increasing the frequency of garbage collection and providing large bins for flower waste. However, no concrete timeline has been announced.

Some local activists suggest converting the organic waste into compost or biogas. But such proposals require investment and coordination that has yet to materialise. For now, the market remains a source of livelihood for hundreds, but also a source of frustration for its neighbours.

What comes next for Sassoon Dock will depend on whether the authorities can match the market's new purpose with proper infrastructure. The flowers are here to stay, and so is the need for a cleaner solution.
