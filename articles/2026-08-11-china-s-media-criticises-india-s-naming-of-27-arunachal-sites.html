China's media criticises India's naming of 27 Arunachal sites

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • China's state media criticised India's move to name 27 places in Arunachal Pradesh.
  • India's MEA reiterated that Arunachal Pradesh is an integral part of India.
  • The naming follows China's recent objections and map-related actions.
  • Analysts say the timing reflects heightened border tensions.

China's state-controlled media has launched a sharp critique of India's decision to formally name 27 places in Arunachal Pradesh, calling it a provocative step that could further strain bilateral ties. The Indian government, in a coordinated counter to Beijing's repeated objections, announced the official designations earlier this week, asserting its administrative control over the region.

The move comes amid an ongoing war of words over the Himalayan border, with Beijing having recently issued fresh 'objections' to India's stance on the territory. New Delhi, for its part, has remained firm, with the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) reiterating that 'Arunachal is an integral part of India, and nothing can change this.'

China's media response

Chinese outlets, including state-run dailies and online portals, have framed India's naming exercise as a deliberate escalation. Editorials have accused New Delhi of 'unilaterally altering' the status quo in the region, a charge India has consistently rejected. The tone of the coverage suggests Beijing views the move as a direct challenge to its territorial claims, which extend to the entire state.

One commentary went further, warning that such actions could 'complicate' the resolution of the boundary question. However, Indian officials have dismissed these assertions, arguing that naming places within one's own territory is a sovereign right and not subject to external approval.

MEA's firm stance

On Monday, the MEA spokesperson addressed the issue in a weekly briefing, underlining that Arunachal Pradesh has always been and will remain an integral part of India. The spokesperson said that India's actions are in line with its commitment to develop border areas and improve governance, and that no amount of external protest can alter the legal and factual reality.

The naming of the 27 sites, which include villages, rivers, and other geographical features, is seen as a practical step to assert India's presence in areas that Beijing claims. Officials say the exercise was undertaken after due process, including consultations with local communities and state authorities.

Timing and strategic implications

The timing of the announcement is significant. It follows a series of Chinese map-related actions that have irked New Delhi, including the publication of new maps showing Arunachal Pradesh as part of China. India's counter-move is widely interpreted as a signal that it will not yield to pressure tactics.

Analysts note that the border dispute has seen periodic flare-ups, with the 2020 Galwan clash still fresh in memory. While diplomatic channels remain open, the rhetoric on both sides has hardened. The naming exercise, though symbolic, underscores India's resolve to maintain its position on the ground and in international forums.

The MEA has also stressed that the state of border relations will reflect the state of overall ties, a pointed remark aimed at Beijing. This suggests that India is linking progress on the border issue with broader bilateral engagement, including trade and investment.

What to watch

As both nations continue to assert their claims, the focus now shifts to the next round of military and diplomatic talks. Observers will be watching for any Chinese military movements near the Line of Actual Control (LAC) and whether Beijing will escalate its media campaign. India, for its part, is likely to maintain its firm stance, with further administrative measures possibly in the pipeline. The coming weeks will test whether the two neighbours can manage their differences without letting them spiral into a larger confrontation.
