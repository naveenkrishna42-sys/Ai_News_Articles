18 injured, 4 critical in firing along Assam-Arunachal border

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 18 people were injured, four critically, in firing along the Assam-Arunachal Pradesh border.
  • The incident occurred amid a longstanding boundary dispute between the two states.
  • Police have not yet identified the attackers or confirmed the sequence of events.

At least 18 people were injured, four of them critically, after unidentified men opened fire along the Assam-Arunachal Pradesh border in Dhemaji district on Monday. The incident has reignited tensions in a region already scarred by decades of boundary disputes between the two northeastern states.

Police said the firing took place amid an escalating clash over encroachment, though the exact trigger remains unclear. Officials have not yet confirmed the identities of the attackers or whether they belonged to any organised group. A senior police officer told reporters that additional forces have been rushed to the area to prevent further violence.

Critical condition, local hospitals overwhelmed

The injured were rushed to nearby medical facilities, with the four most critical patients referred to larger hospitals for advanced care. Local health officials said they were treating wounds consistent with gunfire, though a detailed medical bulletin is yet to be released. The Dhemaji district administration has appealed for calm as families of the injured gathered outside hospitals, anxious for news.

Eyewitness accounts, still unverified, describe a sudden volley of shots that sent villagers scattering for cover. Police have cordoned off the site and launched a search operation, but no arrests have been made so far. The district magistrate has imposed prohibitory orders in the affected area, restricting movement to avoid any flare-up.

Boundary dispute: a festering issue

The Assam-Arunachal Pradesh border has long been a flashpoint, with both states claiming ownership of certain tracts of land. The dispute dates back to the 1950s, when the North-East Frontier Agency was separated from Assam. Over the years, sporadic clashes have occurred, often over forest land and riverine territories, displacing hundreds of families.

In recent months, both state governments had initiated talks to resolve the differences, with a high-level committee examining historical records. Monday's firing, however, threatens to derail that process. Political leaders from both states have urged restraint, but neither side has yet issued a formal statement condemning the attack.

Police investigation underway

Assam Police have registered a case under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code, including attempt to murder. A special investigation team has been formed to trace the attackers, with help from central forces stationed in the region. Officials are also checking if the firing was linked to a land-grab attempt or a premeditated provocation.

  • The injured include men and women, aged between 20 and 55, according to local sources.
  • Mobile internet services in the area have been suspended as a precautionary measure.
  • Security has been heightened along the border, with additional patrols announced.

The lack of clarity over the attackers' motive has fuelled speculation. Some locals claim the firing was triggered by a dispute over bamboo groves, while others point to a recent influx of settlers. Police, however, have not confirmed any of these theories, saying the investigation is in its early stages.

What to watch next

With tensions simmering, all eyes will be on the joint meeting of officials from Assam and Arunachal Pradesh, reportedly scheduled for later this week. The focus will be on whether the two administrations can de-escalate the situation and resume dialogue, or if Monday's violence marks a dangerous new chapter in the border conflict. For now, the region remains on edge, awaiting both a medical update on the critically injured and a breakthrough in the manhunt.
