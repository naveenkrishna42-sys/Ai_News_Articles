Stocks Rise as July CPI Matches Estimates, Easing Rate Worries

📅 2026-08-13 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Stocks rose after July CPI data matched expectations
  • Inflation cooled for a second straight month in India
  • Treasury yields declined, supporting market upside
  • Investors now await further inflation cues for rate direction

Markets Climb as Inflation Data Calms Nerves

Indian equity benchmarks closed higher on Wednesday, tracking global peers, after the latest Consumer Price Index (CPI) data came in line with forecasts. The July inflation print showed a second consecutive month of cooling, easing concerns that the Reserve Bank of India might need to keep policy tighter for longer.

The BSE Sensex and NSE Nifty both ended the session with gains, led by banking, auto, and IT stocks. Broader indices also advanced, reflecting broad-based buying interest. Market participants said the data provided a sense of relief, as any upside surprise could have triggered a sharp sell-off.

CPI Cools for Second Month, But Questions Remain

India's retail inflation slowed for the second straight month in July, according to official data released after market hours. The print matched street estimates, though it remains above the central bank's medium-term target of 4 per cent. Core inflation, which strips out volatile food and fuel prices, also showed signs of easing.

Economists noted that the slowdown is welcome but not yet decisive. Food prices, a key driver of Indian inflation, have been erratic due to monsoon variability. A clearer trend will only emerge over the next couple of months, they said.

Global Cues Support Risk Appetite

Globally, the mood was buoyant as US Treasury yields fell after the US CPI report also met expectations. Lower yields typically support equity valuations, particularly for growth and technology stocks. European markets opened firm, and Asian indices closed mixed but resilient.

Investing.com noted that lower CPI and falling Treasury yields are supporting further market upside. The correlation is simple: when inflation cools, central banks have more room to cut rates, which boosts liquidity and risk appetite.

What This Means for Rate Cut Expectations

The RBI has kept the repo rate steady for several consecutive meetings, but market participants are now pricing in a higher probability of a cut later this year. The bond market reacted positively, with the 10-year benchmark yield easing. A softer inflation trajectory, if sustained, could open the door for the Monetary Policy Committee to shift its stance.

However, analysts caution that the RBI will likely wait for more data before acting. Global oil prices and the monsoon's progress remain wildcards. A spike in crude or a poor harvest could reverse the disinflation trend quickly.

Investors Eye Next Inflation Cues

With the July CPI out of the way, attention now turns to upcoming data points, including wholesale inflation and industrial production figures. Traders will also watch the US Federal Reserve's next move, as global interest rate differentials influence capital flows into emerging markets like India.

Foreign institutional investors have been net buyers in recent sessions, and sustained inflows could provide further support to domestic equities. But volatility is likely to persist as markets digest mixed signals from the global economy.

The near-term direction will hinge on whether inflation continues to cool and whether central banks signal a pivot. For now, investors are cautiously optimistic, but the path ahead remains data-dependent.
