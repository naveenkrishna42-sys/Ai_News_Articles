Supreme Court Metro Station Shut Amid Student Protests, CJI Promises Intervention

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court metro station in Delhi closed due to student protests.
  • CJI says will intervene if no solution is found by authorities.
  • Bar Association president says closure is affecting lawyers and court operations.
  • 16 metro stations closed across Delhi; DMRC cites 'further instructions'.
  • Protests linked to paper leak issue; CJP demands minister's resignation.

Station Shut as Protests Escalate

The Supreme Court metro station in the heart of Delhi was closed on Thursday as student protests turned intense. The Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) announced the closure, citing security concerns.

Commuters were seen scrambling for alternatives. The station, a key transit point for lawyers, litigants, and court staff, remained shut for hours.

CJI Breaks Silence

Chief Justice of India (CJI) took note of the disruption. He stated that he would intervene if the authorities fail to reach a solution by the end of the day.

“If no resolution is found, I will step in soon,” the CJI said. The remark came during a brief hearing where the matter was mentioned.

Bar Association Flags Crisis

The Supreme Court Bar Association president expressed concern over the shutdown. He said the closure was severely affecting the functioning of the top court.

“Lawyers cannot reach court. Cases are being delayed. This is unprecedented,” he told reporters outside the court premises.

16 Metro Stations Shut Across Delhi

The DMRC announced that 16 metro stations across the city have been closed till further instructions. The list includes several key junctions on the Yellow and Violet lines.

Commuters faced long queues at alternate stations. Many were seen walking or taking buses to reach their destinations.

Protests Over Paper Leak Issue

The protests are linked to the ongoing controversy over paper leaks in competitive examinations. The student group CJP has been leading the agitation for weeks.

In a related development, the CJP reacted to Prime Minister Modi's announcement of fast-track courts for paper leak cases. The group demanded the resignation of Union Minister Dharmendra Pradhan, calling the government's response inadequate.

“Dharmendra Pradhan must resign,” the CJP said in a statement, rejecting the fast-track court proposal as a mere distraction.

What happens next depends on the CJI's deadline. The court is expected to take up the matter if the deadlock continues. Security remains tight around the Supreme Court complex.
