US Court Halts Trump's $400-Million White House Ballroom Project

📅 2026-08-07 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US appeals court blocks construction of Trump's $400-million White House ballroom.
  • Trump calls the ruling 'horrendous' and says he will challenge it in the Supreme Court.
  • The decision paves the way for a legal battle over the project's fate.

A United States appeals court has halted construction of former President Donald Trump's $400-million White House ballroom project, a decision that clears the path for a potentially landmark Supreme Court showdown. The ruling, delivered on Friday, deals a significant blow to the ambitious renovation plan that has been a pet project of the former president.

Court Blocks Construction

The appellate court's decision effectively freezes all work on the sprawling ballroom, which was designed to be one of the most opulent additions to the White House complex in modern history. The project, estimated to cost $400 million, has been a source of contention since its inception, with critics questioning its necessity and cost.

The court's order did not elaborate on the specific legal grounds for the halt, but it signals that the judges found merit in the challenges raised against the project. Legal experts suggest that the case raises fundamental questions about presidential authority and the use of federal funds for what some see as a personal legacy project.

Trump's Response: 'Horrendous'

Reacting to the setback, Trump did not mince words. In a statement, he called the ruling "horrendous" and vowed to take the fight to the nation's highest court. "We will challenge this at the Supreme Court," he declared, promising a vigorous legal battle to revive the project.

The former president has long championed the ballroom as a centerpiece of his vision for the White House, describing it as a space for grand state functions and diplomatic receptions. Supporters argue that the project would enhance the nation's prestige, while detractors view it as an extravagant waste of taxpayer money.

Legal Implications and Next Steps

The appeals court's decision does not kill the project outright but leaves its future uncertain. The case now appears destined for the Supreme Court, where justices will likely grapple with complex constitutional questions regarding executive authority and the separation of powers.

Observers note that the timing of the ruling is critical, as it comes amid heightened political tensions. The outcome of the Supreme Court appeal could have far-reaching implications for how future presidents manage the White House's historic properties.

Neither the White House nor the Trump legal team has yet commented on the specific legal arguments they plan to present to the Supreme Court. Officials have not confirmed any timeline for the appeal.

What to Watch

All eyes now turn to the Supreme Court, which will decide whether to hear the case. If the court takes it up, a ruling could come within months, potentially setting a precedent for presidential projects and federal spending. For now, the ballroom remains an empty shell, its marble floors and crystal chandeliers waiting on a legal verdict that could reshape the White House's architectural future.
