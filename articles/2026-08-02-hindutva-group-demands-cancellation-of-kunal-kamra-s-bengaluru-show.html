Hindutva group demands cancellation of Kunal Kamra's Bengaluru show

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • A Hindutva group has formally sought cancellation of Kunal Kamra's Bengaluru performance.
  • The group has cited Kamra's past remarks in its appeal to local authorities.
  • Organisers and police have not yet issued a public response.
  • The development adds to a pattern of pressure on stand-up comedians in India.

Show under scrutiny

A Hindutva organisation has approached authorities in Bengaluru demanding the cancellation of comedian Kunal Kamra's scheduled performance in the city. The group has cited the comedian's past remarks as grounds for its appeal, though the specific comments have not been detailed publicly.

Kamra, known for his sharp political satire, has frequently drawn the ire of right-wing outfits. His shows in other cities have previously faced protests and police complaints, though several have gone ahead without major disruption.

Appeal to authorities

The group's letter, addressed to local police and district officials, argues that the performance could disturb public order. It has urged the administration to intervene and stop the event before it takes place.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether they will act on the demand. Organisers of the show have also remained silent, leaving fans uncertain about the event's status.

Track record of controversy

Kamra has been at the centre of multiple flashpoints over the past few years. His routines targeting politicians and religious figures have led to legal notices and physical altercations at venues.

In 2020, a video of him confronting a journalist on a flight led to a temporary ban from flying with certain airlines. More recently, his remarks on state leaders have triggered complaints in Maharashtra and Gujarat.

Free speech vs. offence

The fresh demand in Bengaluru reignites the debate over comedy's limits in India. Artists and civil liberties groups have argued that such pressure tactics amount to censorship, while proponents of the complaints say satire should not cross into hate speech.

Stand-up comedians across the country have faced similar campaigns, with some shows cancelled under threat of violence. Legal experts note that police typically assess whether a performance could incite unrest before taking action.

What's next

Bengaluru police are expected to respond to the group's representation in the coming days. The show's organisers may also issue a statement clarifying their position.

For now, ticket holders are watching closely. Whether the performance proceeds will likely depend on the administration's reading of the law and the group's ability to mobilise protests.
