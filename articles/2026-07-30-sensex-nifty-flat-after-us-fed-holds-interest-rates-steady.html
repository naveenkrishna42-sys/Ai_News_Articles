Sensex, Nifty Flat After US Fed Holds Interest Rates Steady

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty opened flat on July 30 following the US Fed's decision to hold rates.
  • The Fed's move was widely expected by markets and did not trigger major volatility.
  • Investors are now watching for cues from the RBI's upcoming monetary policy meeting.

Markets Open Flat After Fed Decision

Indian equity benchmarks opened on a flat note on Thursday, July 30, as the US Federal Reserve's decision to leave interest rates unchanged was largely priced in by the markets. The BSE Sensex hovered near its previous close, while the NSE Nifty 50 stayed in a narrow range, reflecting cautious investor sentiment.

The Fed's announcement, made late Wednesday, kept the federal funds rate at its current level. While the move was widely anticipated, it did not provide any fresh directional trigger for Indian markets, which have been trading in a tight band over the past few sessions.

Rate Status Quo Expected, No Surprises

The US central bank's decision to hold rates came amid mixed economic data and persistent inflation concerns. Market participants had already factored in a pause, so the reaction on Dalal Street remained muted during the opening hour.

“The Fed’s hold was within expectations. The real cue for Indian markets will come from domestic factors, including the RBI’s stance and corporate earnings,” said a Mumbai-based dealer. The dollar index remained stable, and bond yields moved marginally, offering little direction to foreign portfolio investors.

Sectoral Action Mixed

On the sectoral front, buying was seen in select IT and pharma stocks, while banking and auto shares traded with a negative bias. Broader market indices also showed a lack of conviction, with the midcap and smallcap indices moving in sync with the benchmarks.

Market breadth was slightly negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. The overall volume was lower compared to the previous session, indicating that most traders were waiting for a clearer signal before taking fresh positions.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) were net sellers in the cash market on Wednesday, while domestic institutional investors (DIIs) provided some support. The trend continued on Thursday, with FIIs staying on the sidelines.

What Lies Ahead for Indian Markets

With the Fed decision out of the way, the focus now shifts to the Reserve Bank of India’s monetary policy meeting scheduled for next week. Analysts expect the RBI to maintain its hawkish stance, though a rate cut is not entirely ruled out if inflation moderates further.

The next major trigger for the market will be the monthly auto sales data and the progress of the monsoon. Both factors are closely watched for their impact on rural demand and corporate earnings.
