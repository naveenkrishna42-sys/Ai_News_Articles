OpenAI Enters Hardware with $230 Codex Micro Keypad for AI Control

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • OpenAI has launched Codex Micro, a $230 physical keypad for controlling AI agents
  • The device is a limited-edition desktop keypad, marking OpenAI's first hardware product
  • Codex Micro is designed for use with Codex, OpenAI's AI coding assistant

OpenAI Steps Into Hardware

OpenAI has launched Codex Micro, a $230 physical controller designed for managing AI agents. The limited-edition desktop keypad is the company's first hardware product, marking a significant shift from its software-only origins.

The device is a mini-keyboard tailored for Codex, OpenAI's AI coding assistant. Users can press physical keys to trigger AI actions, moving away from screen-based interfaces.

Design and Functionality

Codex Micro is a compact keypad with a minimalist design. It connects to a computer via USB or Bluetooth, offering a tactile way to interact with AI agents.

Each key can be programmed to execute specific commands, such as generating code snippets, running scripts, or querying the AI. The device aims to streamline workflows for developers and power users who rely on Codex.

Pricing and Availability

At $230, Codex Micro is priced as a premium accessory. OpenAI has not disclosed production numbers, but the company is calling it a limited-edition product.

Orders are open on OpenAI's website, with shipping expected to begin in the coming weeks. The company has not confirmed whether this is a one-off experiment or the start of a broader hardware push.

Market Reaction

The launch has drawn mixed reactions. Some see it as a gimmick, while others view it as a natural step for OpenAI to control its AI ecosystem.

TechCrunch noted the device's similarity to a mini-keyboard, while Engadget called it a 'desktop keypad for AI agents.' The Times of India highlighted Sam Altman's role in the launch.

What Comes Next

OpenAI has not announced plans for additional hardware. The success of Codex Micro will likely determine whether the company expands into physical products or sticks to software.

For now, the keypad offers a novel way to control AI agents—one that could either become a staple for developers or a collector's item.
