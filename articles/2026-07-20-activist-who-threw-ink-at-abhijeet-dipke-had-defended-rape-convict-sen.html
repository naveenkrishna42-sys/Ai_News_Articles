Activist who threw ink at Abhijeet Dipke had defended rape convict Sengar

📅 2026-07-20 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Barkha Trehan threw an ink-like liquid at CJP activist Abhijeet Dipke at Jantar Mantar
  • Trehan is a men's rights activist who had earlier voiced support for rape-accused MLA Kuldeep Singh Sengar
  • Dipke ended his fast after a request from climate activist Sonam Wangchuk and joined a march to Parliament

Ink attack at Jantar Mantar

A woman hurled an ink-like liquid at activist Abhijeet Dipke during a protest at Jantar Mantar in Delhi on Saturday. Dipke, a key figure in the 'Chalo Sansad' march led by the Campaign for Justice and Peace (CJP), was taken by surprise as the liquid splattered across his face and clothes.

The attacker was identified as Barkha Trehan. She was immediately detained by police personnel present at the scene. Video clips of the incident spread rapidly on social media, drawing sharp reactions from political and activist circles.

Who is Barkha Trehan?

Barkha Trehan describes herself as a men's rights activist. Reports indicate she has previously been vocal in her support for Kuldeep Singh Sengar, the former BJP MLA convicted of raping a minor in Unnao. Sengar is serving a life sentence for the crime that sparked nationwide outrage.

Trehan had participated in online campaigns and public statements questioning the conviction, aligning with groups that argue men are often falsely accused in sexual assault cases. Her presence at the Jantar Mantar protest appears to have been motivated by her opposition to Dipke's activism.

Dipke ends fast, joins march

Abhijeet Dipke had been on an indefinite fast demanding justice for various issues, including police reforms and accountability in sexual assault cases. The fast was called off on Saturday after climate activist Sonam Wangchuk intervened and requested him to end it.

Following the request, Dipke broke his fast and joined the 'Chalo Sansad' march towards Parliament. He later called for a night-long vigil ahead of the march, urging supporters to stay put and amplify their demands. The CJP has been organising a series of protests, drawing participants from across the country.

Police have registered a case against Trehan under relevant sections for the attack. Investigations are ongoing, and officials have not yet confirmed whether she will face additional charges linked to her past statements.

Political reactions

The incident has triggered a debate about the nature of protests and the line between dissent and disruption. Some activists have condemned the attack, saying it undermines the legitimacy of peaceful demonstrations. Others have questioned the police response, noting that Trehan was not immediately arrested after the incident.

Meanwhile, supporters of Dipke have called for a thorough inquiry into Trehan's background and her links to organisations that advocate for accused rapists. The CJP has reiterated its commitment to non-violent protest.

What comes next for Trehan will depend on the police investigation. Dipke and the CJP are expected to continue their push for parliamentary action on their demands.
