Total solar eclipse Aug 12, 2026: Six days to totality, Spain village sees double sunset

Key points

  • A small Spanish village will experience two sunsets in one evening due to the eclipse's geometry.
  • The same night will feature a meteor shower, creating a rare celestial double bill.
  • Astronomers and tourists are converging on the village for the unique viewing opportunity.

Countdown to totality

Six days remain before the Moon's shadow sweeps across Earth on August 12, 2026, plunging parts of the globe into an eerie midday darkness. This total solar eclipse is shaping up to be one of the most-watched celestial events of the decade, and skywatchers are already tracking weather forecasts and mapping out viewing spots.

The path of totality will cut across several regions, with Spain emerging as a prime destination for eclipse chasers. In particular, a tiny Spanish village has captured global attention for a quirk of geography that promises not one, but two sunsets in a single evening.

The village where darkness falls twice

Perched on a hillside, this small settlement is positioned precisely at the edge of the eclipse's shadow path. As the Moon covers the Sun, observers on one side of the village will see darkness descend, only for the light to return moments later as the shadow passes. The effect: two distinct periods of twilight, or what locals are calling a double sunset.

Astronomers explain that the village's location near the umbra's boundary creates this unusual phenomenon. The shadow's edge will graze the area, causing the sky to darken, brighten, and darken again within minutes. For residents, it is a once-in-a-lifetime spectacle, and for scientists, a rare chance to study the eclipse's edge effects.

A night of sky-borne wonders

Adding to the drama, the same night will host a meteor shower, with streaks of light expected to crisscross the post-eclipse sky. This cosmic coincidence has turned the village into an impromptu festival ground, with reports of tourists booking every available room and local authorities preparing for an influx of visitors.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of expected visitors, but anecdotal accounts suggest thousands may gather on nearby hills and fields. The village, normally quiet and unassuming, is bracing for a night of chaos and wonder, with streetlights to be switched off during the eclipse to enhance viewing conditions.

Scientific and cultural significance

For astronomers, the August 12 eclipse offers a chance to study the Sun's corona, the outer atmosphere normally hidden by daylight. The double sunset effect, while a tourist draw, also provides a natural experiment in how light and shadow interact with terrain.

Locally, the event has taken on a festive character. Schools are planning early dismissals, and community groups are organising viewing parties. The meteor shower, which peaks around the same time, adds an unpredictable element, reminding everyone that the sky rarely follows a script.

As the countdown continues, weather remains the wildcard. Forecasters are monitoring cloud cover, and eclipse chasers know that a single patch of clouds could ruin the view. Yet, even a partially obscured eclipse promises a memorable evening in this corner of Spain.

What to watch next

Over the next six days, expect updates on visibility, cloud forecasts, and live streams from the village. For those unable to travel, online broadcasts will capture the double sunset and the meteor shower, offering a front-row seat to this rare celestial double bill.
