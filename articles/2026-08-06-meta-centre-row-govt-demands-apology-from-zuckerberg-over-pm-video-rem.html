Meta-Centre Row: Govt Demands Apology From Zuckerberg Over PM Video Removal

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government sources indicate they want a personal apology from Mark Zuckerberg over platform errors.
  • Meta has apologised for restricting the PM's video and for ads related to child sexual abuse material.
  • Parliamentary panel chairperson says Zuckerberg should apologise; officials to be summoned again.

New Delhi: The confrontation between the Indian government and Meta intensified on Wednesday, with government sources indicating that they expect a personal apology from Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg over what they describe as 'errors in operating the platform'. The demand comes amid a widening political row over the removal of a video posted by Prime Minister Narendra Modi.

Meta has already issued an apology for restricting the PM's video, but the government is insisting on a more direct mea culpa from Zuckerberg himself. The development marks the latest escalation in a standoff that has seen parliamentary panels, government summons, and now the world's most famous tech executive dragged into the centre of the controversy.

Government's Stance Hardens

Government sources, speaking on condition of anonymity, said that the apology from Meta's Indian arm is not sufficient. They want Zuckerberg to personally acknowledge the lapses, framing it as a matter of accountability rather than a mere regulatory formality.

The demand follows a series of meetings and communications between Meta officials and government representatives. The company has been under scrutiny not just for the PM video removal but also for the discovery of advertisements related to child sexual abuse material (CSAM) on its platforms, which has further inflamed the situation.

Meta's Apology and the Parliamentary Panel

Meta, in its official response, has expressed regret over the 'error in restricting the PM's video'. The company has also apologised for the CSAM ads, calling them a violation of its policies. However, these apologies have not cooled the political temperature.

The chairperson of the parliamentary panel, which has been examining the issue, has publicly stated that Zuckerberg should apologise. The panel's stance is clear: corporate apologies from regional offices are not enough when the platform's CEO remains silent.

Officials from Meta are expected to be summoned again by the parliamentary committee, marking another round of what has become a high-stakes tussle between the Indian state and the social media giant.

What Triggered the Row

The controversy began when a video posted by Prime Minister Modi was briefly removed or restricted on Meta's platforms, including Facebook and Instagram. The government viewed this as an affront, especially given the PM's massive online following and the platform's role in public discourse.

Subsequent reports revealed that the same period saw the circulation of CSAM ads, which Meta claimed were promptly removed. But for the government, the combination of these incidents points to systemic failures in Meta's content moderation and ad review processes.

Meta has maintained that the removal of the PM's video was a technical error, not a deliberate act. Yet, the government appears unwilling to accept this explanation at face value, pushing for a higher level of accountability.

The Stakes for Big Tech

This row is being watched closely by other tech companies operating in India, one of the world's largest internet markets. The government's tough stance signals that it expects platforms to be more responsive to Indian regulatory and political sensitivities.

For Zuckerberg, the demand for a personal apology is a test of how he handles a sovereign government's ire. While Meta has faced similar pressures in other countries, India's scale and strategic importance make this a significant challenge.

The parliamentary panel's decision to summon officials again suggests that the issue is far from resolved. The government is likely to keep the pressure on, particularly with elections in some states expected in the coming months, making the row politically charged.

As the standoff continues, all eyes are on Zuckerberg's next move. Whether he offers a direct apology or chooses to let his regional team manage the fallout remains to be seen, but the government's demand is unambiguous.

Watch for further developments as the parliamentary panel reconvenes, and for whether Meta's global leadership will bow to the government's insistence on a personal apology from its CEO.
