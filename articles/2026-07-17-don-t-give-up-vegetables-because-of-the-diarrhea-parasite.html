Don’t give up vegetables because of the diarrhea parasite

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cyclosporiasis cases rising in the US with ‘explosive’ diarrhea symptoms.
  • Vegetables are the suspected source, but experts advise washing, not eliminating them.
  • Michigan health officials have identified a ‘potential source’ of the outbreak.

A parasite causing ‘explosive’ diarrhea is spreading in the United States. Cases of cyclosporiasis have climbed steadily in recent weeks, prompting health alerts from multiple states. The culprit appears to be fresh vegetables.

But experts have a clear message for consumers: do not stop eating vegetables. The solution is not elimination but vigilance.

What is cyclosporiasis?

Cyclosporiasis is an intestinal infection caused by the microscopic parasite Cyclospora cayetanensis. It is not transmitted from person to person. Instead, people contract it by consuming food or water contaminated with the parasite.

Symptoms include watery diarrhea, often described as ‘explosive,’ along with stomach cramps, nausea, and fatigue. The illness can last from a few days to several weeks if untreated. Health officials have not yet confirmed the total number of cases, but the map of affected regions continues to expand.

Why vegetables are the suspect

Previous cyclosporiasis outbreaks in the US have been linked to imported fresh produce, especially cilantro, basil, and salad mixes. The current outbreak appears to follow a similar pattern.

Michigan health officials said they have identified a ‘potential source’ of the parasite, though they have not publicly named a specific product or supplier. The US Food and Drug Administration is investigating. The parasite thrives in warm, humid conditions and can survive on produce after harvest if washing is inadequate.

How to stay safe without swearing off salads

Public health experts are clear: vegetables remain a vital part of a healthy diet. The risk of infection is low if you follow basic food safety steps.

Wash all fresh produce thoroughly under running water before eating. Scrub firm-skinned vegetables like carrots and cucumbers with a brush. There is no need to buy special washes — plain water is effective. Cooking kills the parasite, so thoroughly cooked vegetables carry no risk. For raw salads, the key is washing just before eating, not before storing.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) also recommends avoiding produce that is bruised or damaged, as the parasite can hide in crevices. If you are immunocompromised or pregnant, consider cooking vegetables until they are soft.

It is not just the US

Cyclosporiasis is more common in tropical and subtropical regions. Travellers to countries where the parasite is endemic are at higher risk. In India, cases are reported but not typically linked to large outbreaks. However, the current US outbreak has drawn attention because of the wide geographic spread and the severity of symptoms reported.

Indian health authorities have not issued any specific advisory related to this outbreak. But given the global nature of food supply chains, similar precautions apply.

The bottom line is simple: do not abandon vegetables. Wash them, cook them when possible, and stay informed. The outbreak is still being investigated, and officials expect to issue more specific guidance once the source is confirmed.

What to watch: The FDA and CDC are expected to release updated case counts and possibly a product recall notice in the coming days. Consumers should monitor local health department alerts for their region.
