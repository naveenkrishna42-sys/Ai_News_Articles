China evacuates over one million as Typhoon Dolphin hits

📅 2026-08-10 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Over one million people evacuated in eastern China as Typhoon Dolphin makes landfall.
  • Shanghai records unprecedented rainfall, with nearly 950 flights grounded.
  • Authorities warn of crop damage and further flooding risks.

Mass evacuation ordered as storm strikes

China has evacuated more than one million people from its eastern coastal regions as Typhoon Dolphin slammed ashore, bringing ferocious winds and record rainfall. The storm, which made landfall on Monday, has disrupted daily life across a densely populated swath of the country, with Shanghai bearing the brunt of the deluge.

State media reported that authorities moved residents from vulnerable low-lying areas ahead of the typhoon's arrival, prioritising safety over economic disruption. The scale of the evacuation underscores the severity of the weather system, which has been tracked for days by meteorological agencies.

Shanghai records unprecedented rainfall

The financial hub of Shanghai has been hit particularly hard, with officials describing the rainfall as record-breaking. Streets have turned into rivers in some neighbourhoods, and emergency services are working around the clock to pump out water and clear blocked drains.

Nearly 950 flights from Shanghai's two main airports have been grounded, stranding thousands of travellers. Rail services in the region have also been suspended or reduced, and schools have been closed for the day. Commuters faced chaotic conditions as public transport struggled to operate under the onslaught of rain and wind.

Storm's path and agricultural concerns

Typhoon Dolphin, classified as a tropical storm by some international agencies at landfall, is moving northwards along the coast. The storm's trajectory has raised alarms for agricultural regions, with analysts warning of significant damage to standing crops. Rice paddies and vegetable fields across Zhejiang and Jiangsu provinces are at risk from flooding and wind damage.

Local governments have mobilised teams to reinforce river embankments and clear drainage channels, hoping to mitigate the impact. The agriculture ministry has urged farmers to harvest mature crops quickly and secure greenhouses, though time is running short as the storm continues to churn.

Flights, transport, and daily life disrupted

Beyond aviation, ferry services along the coast have been halted, and highways have seen speed restrictions. Port operations, including in Shanghai's busy container terminals, have been suspended, affecting supply chains that ripple across global markets.

Residents have been advised to stay indoors, with many heeding the warning. Supermarkets reported a rush on essentials like bottled water and instant noodles as people prepared for extended outages. Power cuts have been reported in several districts, though utility crews are on standby to restore services once winds subside.

Official response and relief efforts

The national disaster relief agency has deployed teams to the worst-affected areas, with rescue boats and pumps on standby. Authorities have not yet confirmed any casualties, but the full extent of the damage is expected to become clearer only after the storm passes.

In a statement, the Shanghai municipal government said it is prioritising the safety of residents and urged calm. Evacuation centres have been set up in schools and community halls, with food and bedding provided for those displaced.

As the typhoon weakens inland, the focus will shift to recovery and assessing the economic toll. Insurance firms are bracing for a surge in claims, while rebuilding efforts are likely to take weeks in the worst-hit areas.

The coming days will reveal how quickly China can restore normalcy, and whether the agricultural losses will push up food prices. Meteorologists are also monitoring the possibility of additional storms in the region as the typhoon season peaks.
