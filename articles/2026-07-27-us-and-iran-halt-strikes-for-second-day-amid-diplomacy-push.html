US and Iran Halt Strikes for Second Day Amid Diplomacy Push

📅 2026-07-27 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • The US has paused strikes on Iran for two consecutive nights, the first such break in two weeks.
  • Iran has halted its retaliatory strikes in response, creating a potential opening for diplomatic talks.
  • VP Vance and CIA Director Caine reportedly raised concerns about the escalation of the conflict.
  • Houthis attacked a Saudi oil site amid the broader tensions in the region.

For the first time in two weeks, the United States and Iran have both stepped back from active hostilities, pausing strikes for a second consecutive day. The unexpected lull has raised cautious hopes for a diplomatic off-ramp in a conflict that has gripped West Asia.

Washington Hits Pause

US military operations against Iranian positions were halted for the second night running on Saturday. This marks the longest break in American strikes since the campaign intensified earlier this month.

Officials in Washington have not officially confirmed the reason for the pause. However, sources familiar with internal discussions told Al Jazeera that Vice President JD Vance and CIA Director John Caine had both voiced concerns about the widening scope of the war.

The two officials reportedly warned that the escalating cycle of strikes was dragging the US deeper into a conflict with no clear exit strategy. Their reservations are believed to have influenced the decision to hold fire.

Tehran Responds in Kind

Iran, which had been launching retaliatory strikes against US assets and allies in the region, also halted its operations on Saturday. The Islamic Republic's mission to the United Nations issued a statement saying Tehran was giving space for talks, provided the US demonstrated a genuine commitment to de-escalation.

The decision represents a significant shift. Just days ago, both nations were locked in a back-and-forth of air raids and missile attacks that threatened to pull in other regional powers.

Houthi Attack Complicates the Picture

Even as the main adversaries paused, a separate incident underscored the fragility of the situation. Yemen's Houthi movement, which is backed by Iran, launched an attack on a Saudi oil facility on Saturday.

The attack did not cause major casualties but disrupted production briefly. Saudi authorities condemned the strike and called for restraint. The incident serves as a reminder that proxies and allies could yet drag the US and Iran back into direct confrontation.

CNN reported that this was the first time in two weeks that the US had deliberately held back from striking Iran following a Houthi attack. In previous instances, Washington had responded with immediate force against Iranian targets believed to be supporting the Houthis.

What Comes Next

The diplomatic window remains narrow. Both sides have signalled a willingness to talk, but no formal negotiations have been announced. The coming days will test whether the pause is a prelude to peace or simply a tactical breather before the next round of strikes.
