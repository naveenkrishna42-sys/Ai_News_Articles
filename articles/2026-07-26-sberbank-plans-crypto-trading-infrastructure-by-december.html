Sberbank plans crypto trading infrastructure by December

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sberbank, Russia's largest bank, plans to roll out crypto trading infrastructure by December.
  • The move targets institutional clients and aims to comply with Russia's developing digital asset laws.
  • This positions Sberbank among major global banks exploring crypto services amid regulatory shifts.

Russia's largest bank, Sberbank, has announced plans to launch cryptocurrency trading infrastructure by December. The move marks a significant step for the state-controlled lender as it positions itself within the country's evolving digital asset landscape.

The bank intends to offer crypto trading services primarily to institutional clients. This approach aligns with Sberbank's existing focus on serving corporate and large-scale investors, though retail access may be considered later depending on regulatory developments.

Russia's crypto regulatory backdrop

Russia has been gradually shaping its stance on digital assets. While the government has maintained a wary view of cryptocurrencies as a means to bypass sanctions and finance illicit activities, it has simultaneously moved to legalise and regulate certain crypto operations under strict conditions.

A key law, the Digital Financial Assets Act, came into force in 2021, providing a legal framework for digital currencies but banning their use as a means of payment for goods and services. Sberbank's planned infrastructure would need to operate within these boundaries, focusing on trading rather than payment usage.

Additionally, Russia's central bank has historically advocated for a complete ban on crypto. However, recent signals suggest a softening. The central bank has indicated support for regulated crypto trading for certain qualified investors, particularly as Western sanctions have pushed Russia to explore alternative financial channels.

Sberbank's crypto journey so far

Sberbank has not been a stranger to blockchain technology. It has already launched a platform for digital financial assets, which are tokenised versions of traditional securities. This platform, based on the bank's proprietary blockchain, has been operational since early 2022.

The bank has also participated in experiments with the Bank of Russia's digital ruble project. The digital ruble, a central bank digital currency, is currently in pilot testing across several Russian cities. Sberbank's foray into crypto trading is a separate but complementary initiative, targeting non-sovereign digital assets.

Details on the specific cryptocurrencies Sberbank plans to support remain scarce. Bitcoin and Ethereum are likely candidates given their global liquidity. The bank has not confirmed whether it will custody assets directly or partner with existing crypto exchanges.

Implications for the broader market

Sberbank's entry into crypto trading could lend legitimacy to the asset class in Russia. As the country's largest bank by assets, its move may encourage other financial institutions to follow suit. This could expand the domestic crypto market, which has remained relatively fragmented and driven largely by peer-to-peer trading.

However, the infrastructure rollout is subject to final regulatory approvals. The Bank of Russia and other authorities retain the power to impose restrictions. Sberbank will need to ensure compliance with anti-money laundering and know-your-customer norms, which have become stricter globally.

Observers will watch how the bank navigates the tension between providing crypto services and adhering to sanctions. Western countries have targeted Russia's financial system, and crypto trading could raise compliance red flags for international partners.

The December timeline is ambitious. Sberbank has yet to announce specific partners or technology providers. The bank's existing blockchain expertise may help it move quickly, but building a robust and compliant trading platform from scratch in a few months is a tall order.

What comes next hinges on regulatory clarity. If the Bank of Russia finalises rules for crypto trading by qualified investors before year-end, Sberbank's infrastructure could be operational on schedule. If not, the launch may slip into early 2027.
