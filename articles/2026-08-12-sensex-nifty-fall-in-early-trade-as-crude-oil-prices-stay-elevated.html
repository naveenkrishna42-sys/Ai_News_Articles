Sensex, Nifty fall in early trade as crude oil prices stay elevated

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 550 points in early trade, Nifty slipped below 24,300.
  • Elevated crude oil prices, with Brent near $90, weighed on market sentiment.
  • Asian markets traded mixed amid geopolitical tensions around the Strait of Hormuz.

MUMBAI: Indian equity benchmarks opened lower on Tuesday, extending the previous session's losses, as elevated crude oil prices and geopolitical concerns dampened investor risk appetite. The BSE Sensex dropped over 550 points in early trade, while the Nifty 50 slipped below the 24,300 mark, tracking weakness across most sectoral indices.

Rising oil prices, with Brent crude hovering near $90 a barrel, triggered selling pressure in banking, auto, and FMCG stocks. Higher crude costs threaten to widen India's trade deficit and stoke inflationary pressures, a worry that has kept investors cautious in recent sessions.

Oil prices and global cues weigh

Brent crude's climb to the $90 level has become a key overhang for domestic markets. India imports roughly 85% of its crude oil requirements, making it particularly vulnerable to price spikes. A sustained rally in oil could prompt the Reserve Bank of India to keep interest rates higher for longer, a scenario that markets are pricing in.

Asian peers traded mixed, with Chinese and Hong Kong indices showing mild gains while Japanese and South Korean markets edged lower. Sentiment in the region remained fragile amid the ongoing deadlock around the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global oil shipments. Any escalation there could push crude prices even higher, analysts said.

Hormuz standoff adds to uncertainty

The Strait of Hormuz, through which about a fifth of global oil consumption passes, has become the focal point of geopolitical tension. The standoff has kept traders on edge, with any disruption to shipping lanes seen as a direct threat to global energy supplies. Indian markets, already sensitive to foreign fund flows, are feeling the heat as overseas investors adopt a wait-and-watch approach.

Gold extended its gains for a second straight day, reflecting safe-haven demand amid the uncertainty. Investors are rotating into bullion and other defensive assets, a trend that typically accelerates when equity volatility rises.

Sectoral trends and market breadth

Early trade saw broad-based selling, with the Nifty Bank index and auto stocks among the worst hit. IT shares, however, showed some resilience, helped by a weaker rupee that boosts export competitiveness. Midcap and smallcap indices also traded lower, though the declines were less severe than the frontline benchmarks.

Market breadth was negative, with roughly two stocks declining for every one advancing on the BSE. Traders pointed to the lack of fresh domestic triggers, leaving global cues and oil prices to dictate the day's direction. The rupee's movement will also be watched closely, as a sharp depreciation could add to imported inflation concerns.

Analysts noted that the market's ability to hold above key support levels will hinge on crude price action and any de-escalation in the Hormuz situation. Until then, volatility is likely to remain the norm, with investors advised to stay selective.

What lies ahead

Investors will track afternoon developments on the geopolitical front and any statement from oil-producing nations. A cooling of crude prices could trigger a rebound, while further escalation may extend the slide. The coming sessions are expected to remain data-driven, with global oil inventories and US Federal Reserve commentary also on the radar.
