Delhi court acquits Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh in wrestlers' harassment case

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Delhi court acquits Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh in wrestlers' sexual harassment case
  • Court terms allegations political, false and fabricated
  • Case had sparked nationwide protests by top wrestlers in 2023

Court's Verdict

A Delhi court on Sunday acquitted former Wrestling Federation of India (WFI) chief Brij Bhushan Sharan Singh in the sexual harassment case filed by six women wrestlers. The court observed that the allegations were political, false and fabricated, and part of a conspiracy against the politician.

The verdict brings a legal closure to a case that had rocked Indian sports and triggered months of protests at Jantar Mantar, with top wrestlers demanding action against Singh.

Political Conspiracy Angle

The court's judgment rested heavily on what it described as a 'politically-motivated conspiracy' behind the accusations. It noted inconsistencies in the complainants' statements and questioned the timing and manner in which the allegations were made.

Observers say the ruling has reopened debates about the credibility of sexual harassment claims, especially when they involve powerful figures. The court's language — calling the case 'false and fabricated' — has drawn sharp reactions from women's rights groups and legal experts.

Background of the Case

The case dates back to 2023, when multiple wrestlers, including Olympic medallists, accused Singh of sexual harassment spanning years. The allegations led to public protests, with wrestlers threatening to return their medals. The Delhi Police filed a chargesheet against Singh in June 2023, but the court has now dismissed the charges.

Singh, a six-time Member of Parliament from the BJP, had consistently denied all allegations, calling them a conspiracy hatched by rivals to tarnish his image. His lawyers argued that the complainants had changed their statements and lacked corroborative evidence.

Reactions and Implications

The verdict has been welcomed by Singh's supporters, who say he was falsely implicated. However, the wrestlers have expressed disappointment and indicated they may approach a higher court. Legal experts point out that the acquittal could set a precedent, making it harder for future complainants in similar cases to be believed.

The ruling also raises questions about the handling of sexual harassment cases involving public figures in India. Some commentators have noted that the trial focused on the credibility of the complainants rather than the alleged incidents, with one article asking, "Why did you choose your career over your body?"

What Happens Next

The wrestlers have the option to appeal the verdict in the Delhi High Court. Meanwhile, the case has already had a lasting impact on Indian wrestling, leading to reforms in the WFI and increased scrutiny of sports administration. The coming weeks will show whether the legal battle continues or ends here.
