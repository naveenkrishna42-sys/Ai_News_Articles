Madras HC Orders Private University to Furnish Rs 3.3 Crore Bank Guarantee

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Madras High Court has directed a private university to furnish a bank guarantee of Rs 3.3 crore.
  • The order was passed in connection with a pending case involving the university's financial obligations.
  • The guarantee is intended to protect the interests of students and employees amid the legal dispute.

Court Order in Ongoing Dispute

The Madras High Court has directed a private university to furnish a bank guarantee of Rs 3.3 crore as part of an ongoing legal matter. The order was issued by a single-judge bench on Friday, in response to a petition filed against the university.

The court's directive comes amid allegations of financial irregularities and non-payment of dues to staff members. The university has been asked to submit the guarantee within four weeks to ensure compliance with the court's earlier orders.

Background of the Case

The dispute traces back to a series of complaints raised by faculty and administrative staff regarding unpaid salaries and other benefits. The petitioners had approached the court seeking intervention to recover the dues.

During the hearing, the university's counsel argued that the institution was facing financial difficulties. However, the court noted that such difficulties could not be used to evade statutory obligations towards employees and students.

Bank Guarantee as Interim Measure

The bank guarantee is intended to serve as a security deposit, ensuring that funds are available to meet the university's liabilities. The court observed that this would protect the interests of all stakeholders while the main petition is pending.

Justice M. Sundar, who presided over the case, remarked that the guarantee would act as a safeguard against any potential default. The university has been directed to provide details of the guarantee to the court and the petitioners within the stipulated time.

Legal Implications for Private Institutions

The ruling has significant implications for private universities across Tamil Nadu, particularly those facing similar disputes. Legal experts say the order reinforces the principle that educational institutions must adhere to financial transparency and accountability.

The court has also listed the matter for further hearing after eight weeks, allowing time for the guarantee to be furnished and for both parties to file their responses. The university has not yet issued an official statement on the order.

Observers are now watching whether the university will comply with the court's directive or seek a modification of the order in a higher bench. The case is expected to be closely monitored by educational bodies and employee unions.

In the meantime, the court has made it clear that any failure to furnish the guarantee could result in contempt proceedings against the university's management.
