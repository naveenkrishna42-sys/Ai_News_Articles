Cockroach Infestation Threatens Azad Maidan in Mumbai

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Cockroaches have overrun Azad Maidan, a popular public ground in Mumbai.
  • The infestation has alarmed local residents and visitors due to health risks.
  • Municipal authorities have not yet confirmed a timeline for disinfestation measures.

A massive cockroach infestation has taken over Azad Maidan, one of Mumbai's most prominent open spaces. The problem has become so severe that visitors and nearby residents are raising alarm over hygiene and health risks.

The Scale of the Problem

Azad Maidan, located in South Mumbai, has long been a venue for cricket matches, political rallies, and public gatherings. But now, it is being described as a breeding ground for cockroaches. Reports suggest the insects are visible in large numbers across the ground, especially near food stalls and garbage piles.

Locals have complained to the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) multiple times. They say the infestation has been building up over the last few weeks, but no concrete action has been taken so far. The situation has worsened with the onset of the monsoon, which provides ideal conditions for cockroach breeding.

Health Concerns

Cockroaches are known carriers of pathogens that can cause diseases such as dysentery, typhoid, and allergies. With Azad Maidan being frequented by children and families, the health risk has become a major worry. Dr. Meera Joshi, a local physician, told TIVRA News that residents have reported increased cases of respiratory issues and skin rashes in the area.

Many residents have stopped visiting the maidan altogether. Some are now demanding that the BMC declare the area a public health emergency and carry out immediate fumigation.

Civic Response

The BMC has not yet issued an official statement on the infestation. Sources within the corporation said that a team has been sent to inspect the site, but no timeline for cleanup has been announced. This delay has frustrated local activists, who accuse the civic body of negligence.

"Azad Maidan is not just a playground—it's a lung space for South Mumbai," said Rohan Desai, a resident and member of a local welfare group. "If the BMC cannot keep it clean, they need to answer to the public."

Other areas near the maidan, including Churchgate and Kala Ghoda, have also reported a rise in cockroach sightings, leading to fears that the infestation could spread further into residential and commercial zones.

For now, Azad Maidan remains open to the public, but the stench and sight of cockroaches have driven most people away. The maidan's regular morning walkers and evening cricketers are now shifting to alternative grounds nearby.

The next few days will be critical as the BMC faces mounting pressure to act. Whether the corporation will launch a swift disinfestation drive or allow the problem to fester remains to be seen.
