Delhi Metro Shutdowns and Security Measures Ahead of Supreme Court March

Delhi authorities on October 12 implemented unprecedented restrictions at six metro stations along with citywide traffic curbs as Opposition parties prepared to march toward the Supreme Court. The action follows mounting tensions over recent appointments within the Election Commission, with protesters demanding judicial intervention. The Hindu first reported the station closures, later confirmed by multiple news agencies including Reuters and The Times of India.

Stations Affected and Transit Disruptions

The closed stations include key hubs across central and north Delhi, disrupting daily commuter movement during peak hours. Passengers were forced to disembark at adjacent stations or seek alternative transport options. Officials cited law and order concerns as the primary rationale for the closures, though no formal curfew was announced. The Delhi Traffic Police also erected barriers on major arterial roads leading to the Supreme Court and Parliament Street.

Opposition Rally and Legal Demands

Rahul Gandhi and other Opposition leaders called for a "March for Democracy" targeting the Supreme Court, echoing concerns raised by senior advocates like Menaka Guruswamy regarding alleged mass detentions and constitutional violations. Protesters specifically demand a review of the appointment process for election officials, arguing that recent decisions compromise the autonomy of the poll body. The rally coincides with ongoing public debates about transparency in electoral governance and judicial oversight mechanisms.

Security Response and Constitutional Implications

Delhi Police deployed additional forces near protest sites, citing intelligence inputs about potential disruptions. While government sources defended the measures as routine precautionary steps, civil society groups expressed concern over restrictions on peaceful assembly. Legal experts note that while temporary transit limitations are legally permissible under exceptional circumstances, prolonged shutdowns could raise questions about proportionality under Article 19 of the Constitution. The situation remains fluid as crowds gather near Ramlila Maidan and India Gate.

Real-Time Updates and Public Reaction

Social media reports indicate mixed responses from residents, with some commuters criticizing the inconvenience while others support the security precautions. Schools and offices in affected zones reportedly adjusted schedules to accommodate transport disruptions. Meanwhile, the Supreme Court has not yet issued any formal directive regarding the planned protest march.
