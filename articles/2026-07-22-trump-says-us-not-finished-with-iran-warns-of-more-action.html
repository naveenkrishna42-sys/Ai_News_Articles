Trump says US 'not finished' with Iran, warns of more action

📅 2026-07-22 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Donald Trump said the US is 'not finished' with Iran, suggesting ongoing pressure.
  • The statement comes amid heightened tensions between Washington and Tehran.
  • No specific actions or timeline were announced by Trump.

Former United States President Donald Trump has declared that America is 'not finished' with Iran, signalling that further measures against Tehran could be on the horizon. The statement, made public on Wednesday, adds a fresh layer of uncertainty to an already volatile relationship between the two nations.

Trump did not elaborate on what specific steps the US might take next. His comments come at a time when diplomatic channels remain strained, and both sides have engaged in a series of escalatory moves over the past several months.

Ongoing tensions with Tehran

The former president's remarks are the latest in a long line of confrontational exchanges between Washington and Tehran. Under Trump's previous administration, the US withdrew from the 2015 nuclear deal and reimposed crippling sanctions on Iran, which it has maintained and even tightened since then.

Iran, in response, has accelerated its nuclear programme and repeatedly violated the terms of the agreement. The situation has drawn concern from European allies and international observers, who fear a full-blown crisis if diplomatic efforts fail.

No specifics on next steps

While Trump's statement suggests a continued hardline approach, he offered no concrete details. 'We are not finished with Iran,' he was quoted as saying, but declined to mention whether new sanctions, military posturing, or diplomatic initiatives were being considered.

Analysts point out that this ambiguity could be intentional, keeping Tehran guessing about US intentions. However, it also risks further inflaming tensions, as Iran has warned that any aggressive move would be met with a firm response.

Regional and global implications

The US-Iran standoff has ripple effects across the Middle East. Countries like Saudi Arabia, Israel, and the United Arab Emirates are closely watching the developments, as any escalation could destabilise the region further.

Oil markets have also been sensitive to the rhetoric. Prices have fluctuated in recent weeks on fears of supply disruptions, especially if the Strait of Hormuz becomes a flashpoint again.

Meanwhile, European nations have been trying to salvage the nuclear deal through backchannel diplomacy. Their efforts have seen limited success, as Iran demands relief from sanctions before returning to full compliance.

What to watch

All eyes are now on whether the US will announce any concrete measures in the coming days. Trump's remarks could be a precursor to new sanctions or a diplomatic push. For now, the world waits to see if words will translate into action.
