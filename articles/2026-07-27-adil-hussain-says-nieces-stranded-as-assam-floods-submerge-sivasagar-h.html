Adil Hussain says nieces stranded as Assam floods submerge Sivasagar homes

📅 2026-07-27 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Adil Hussain said his nieces are stranded in Sivasagar, their homes submerged by floodwater.
  • Assam flood situation is improving but two more deaths pushed the toll to 68, over 5 lakh people affected.
  • Congress chief targeted Amit Shah over his 'flood-free Assam' promise; Rahul Gandhi called for scientific prevention.

Assam floods: Adil Hussain speaks out

National Award-winning actor Adil Hussain has revealed that his nieces are stranded in Sivasagar district as devastating floods continue to batter Assam. In a statement, he said their homes are completely submerged under floodwater.

The actor, known for his roles in films like 'English Vinglish' and 'Life of Pi', expressed deep concern for his family members. He urged authorities to expedite rescue and relief operations in the affected areas.

Death toll rises, over 5 lakh affected

The flood situation in Assam is showing signs of improvement, but the damage remains extensive. Two more deaths were reported on Monday, pushing the official toll to 68. Over 5 lakh people across multiple districts have been affected, according to the Assam State Disaster Management Authority.

Thousands have taken shelter in relief camps as water levels slowly recede in some areas. However, many regions, including Sivasagar, remain cut off, with residents facing shortages of food and clean drinking water.

Political blame game intensifies

The disaster has triggered sharp political exchanges. Congress chief Mallikarjun Kharge targeted Union Home Minister Amit Shah over his earlier promise of making Assam 'flood-free'. Kharge accused the BJP-led government of failing to deliver on that commitment.

Rahul Gandhi, during a visit to the affected regions, called for a robust system that prioritises scientific prevention over reactive measures. He argued that long-term solutions, including better river management and afforestation, are urgently needed.

The ruling BJP has defended its record, pointing to ongoing relief efforts and central assistance. Officials have not yet commented on the specific allegations regarding the 'flood-free' pledge.

Ground reports and relief challenges

On the ground, survivors describe the floods as the worst in years. A BBC report quoted one resident saying, 'It took everything from us.' Many have lost their homes, livestock and belongings.

Relief teams are struggling to reach marooned villages due to damaged roads and bridges. The Indian Army and National Disaster Response Force have been deployed for rescue operations. Medical camps have been set up to prevent outbreaks of waterborne diseases.

Officials say the situation is improving gradually, but warn that more heavy rain could trigger fresh flooding. The Brahmaputra river continues to flow above the danger mark in several locations.

What to watch

All eyes are now on the weather forecast and the government's next steps. The Assam Assembly is expected to discuss the flood crisis in the coming session, with opposition parties demanding a detailed statement on relief and rehabilitation plans.
