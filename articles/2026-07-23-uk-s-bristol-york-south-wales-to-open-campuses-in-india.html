UK’s Bristol, York, South Wales to open campuses in India

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • University of Bristol, University of York and University of South Wales to establish campuses in India.
  • The move follows the UGC's 2025 guidelines allowing foreign universities to set up campuses in India.
  • The campuses are expected to offer a range of undergraduate and postgraduate programmes.

Three prominent British universities — the University of Bristol, the University of York and the University of South Wales — have announced plans to set up campuses in India. The decision marks a significant step in the internationalisation of higher education in the country, following the University Grants Commission’s (UGC) new regulations that came into effect earlier this year.

New campuses under UGC norms

The universities are moving under the UGC’s 2025 guidelines for setting up campuses of foreign higher educational institutions in India. These norms permit top-ranked global universities to establish physical campuses and award degrees recognised in India and abroad.

The University of Bristol, a member of the Russell Group, is one of the UK’s leading research-intensive institutions. The University of York is also a Russell Group member, known for its strengths in the humanities and sciences. The University of South Wales is among the largest universities in the UK, with strong vocational and professional programmes.

What programmes are expected

While specific details on courses, locations and timelines have not been officially released, the campuses are expected to offer a range of undergraduate and postgraduate programmes. The universities are likely to focus on fields such as engineering, business, computer science and the social sciences, aligning with India’s growing demand for globally competitive education.

The UGC has mandated that foreign universities must ensure parity in curriculum, faculty qualifications and assessment standards with their home campuses. The degrees awarded will be considered equivalent to degrees from Indian universities under the UGC Act.

India’s push for foreign universities

The move is part of India’s broader National Education Policy 2020, which encourages top foreign institutions to enter the country. The government has argued that this will reduce the need for Indian students to study abroad, saving foreign exchange and retaining talent.

Several other international universities, including Australia’s Deakin University and the UK’s University of Southampton, have already announced plans to set up campuses in India. The University of Southampton had earlier signed a memorandum of understanding with the Gujarat government to establish a campus in GIFT City.

The UGC’s 2025 guidelines require foreign universities to be among the top 500 in global rankings or have a proven track record of academic excellence. They must also ensure that 30% of faculty are from abroad, with the rest being Indian or international.

Challenges and next steps

Setting up a campus in India involves navigating complex regulatory approvals, land acquisition and infrastructure development. The universities will need to partner with Indian state governments or private entities to secure land and necessary clearances.

Industry experts have pointed out that tuition fees at these campuses are expected to be lower than studying abroad but higher than most Indian private universities. Scholarships and financial aid may be offered to attract a diverse student body.

The three universities have not yet announced specific timelines for when their campuses will open. Applications for faculty and administrative staff are expected to be released in the coming months.

What to watch: More foreign universities are likely to follow, especially from the UK, Australia and the US, as India’s regulatory framework becomes more welcoming. The success of these campuses will depend on their ability to maintain academic quality while adapting to local needs.
