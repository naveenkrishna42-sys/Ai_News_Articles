Punjab Police Bust ISI-Backed Terror Modules, 9 Held Over Jantar Mantar Plot

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nine people arrested, including four juveniles, in two separate terror modules.
  • The modules were allegedly backed by Pakistan's ISI and planned an attack at Jantar Mantar in Delhi.
  • Punjab Police say the arrests have disrupted cross-border terror activities.
  • Further investigations are underway to identify other links in the network.

Major Crackdown on Cross-Border Terror Network

In a significant counter-terror operation, Punjab Police have busted two terror modules allegedly backed by Pakistan's Inter-Services Intelligence (ISI), arresting nine individuals, including four minors. The modules were reportedly planning an attack at Jantar Mantar in Delhi, a popular protest site in the national capital.

The arrests, announced on Wednesday, mark one of the largest such crackdowns in the state in recent months. Police said the modules were operating across the border, with handlers in Pakistan directing local operatives.

Details of the Arrests

According to officials, the nine arrested include four juveniles, whose identities have not been disclosed. The arrests were made following a coordinated operation based on intelligence inputs. Punjab Police have not yet confirmed the specific locations where the arrests took place, but sources suggest multiple districts were involved.

The police have recovered material that indicates the modules were in the advanced stages of planning an attack. While the exact nature of the planned attack has not been fully disclosed, the targeting of Jantar Mantar suggests an intent to cause maximum public impact.

ISI Link and Cross-Border Operations

Punjab Police have stated that the modules were directly linked to ISI handlers based in Pakistan. This is not the first time such a link has been alleged; the state has repeatedly flagged cross-border terror infiltration attempts.

"The modules were being directed from across the border," a senior police officer said, requesting anonymity. "Their handlers were providing instructions and logistical support. We have disrupted a major network."

The involvement of minors in such modules has raised concerns about radicalisation of youth. Police are investigating how the juveniles were recruited and what roles they were assigned.

Recoveries and Evidence

While the police have not released a detailed list of recovered items, they have confirmed that incriminating material was seized during the operation. This includes electronic devices, which are being examined for communication with handlers in Pakistan.

Forensic teams are also analysing the material to establish the exact scope of the planned attack. Officials have indicated that more arrests cannot be ruled out as the investigation progresses.

Political and Security Implications

The bust comes at a time when security agencies across India have been on high alert for terror activities. Punjab, being a border state, has been a focal point for such operations. The state police have been working in coordination with central agencies to track cross-border modules.

This development is likely to be raised in political circles, with opposition parties questioning the government's preparedness. However, the police have maintained that they are taking all necessary steps to ensure public safety.

"We are committed to neutralising any threat to the nation," the officer added. "Operations like this are a testament to our vigilance."

What Happens Next

The investigation is ongoing, with police likely to conduct further interrogations to uncover the full extent of the network. Authorities are also expected to brief central security agencies on the findings. The public has been urged to remain alert, though officials have assured that there is no immediate threat following the arrests.
