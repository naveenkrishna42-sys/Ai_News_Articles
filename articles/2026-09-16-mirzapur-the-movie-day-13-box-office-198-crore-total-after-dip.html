Mirzapur The Movie Box Office Day 13: ₹198 Crore and Counting

The big-screen adaptation of Amazon Prime Video's flagship crime series has crossed the ₹198 crore net mark in India by the end of its thirteenth day, according to trade tracking. The number arrives after a visible second-week dip, the standard pattern for a mass-appeal release that opened to a huge festive weekend.

Pankaj Tripathi returns as Kaleen Bhaiya, Ali Fazal reprises Guddu Pandit, and Divyenndu's Munna Tripathi remains the franchise's chaotic engine. That casting continuity is the film's biggest commercial asset: it converts a streaming fanbase of tens of millions into paying theatregoers.

Day-Wise Collection Snapshot

Trade estimates place the opening weekend well above ₹70 crore net, driven by a strong single-screen footprint in Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra. The second Friday and Monday drops confirm that the initial rush has cooled, though weekday holds are still respectable for a non-holiday window.

What the Dip Actually Means for Buyers and Exhibitors

A second-week fall is not a failure signal by itself. What matters is the ratio between the opening weekend and the first full week. Mirzapur The Movie is holding better in tier-2 and tier-3 centres, where ticket prices are lower but occupancy is stickier. Multiplex chains in metros are seeing faster erosion, largely because the fanbase that queued on day one has already watched it.

For exhibitors, the practical question is screen allocation in week three. If the film retains even 35–40% of its second-weekend footfalls, it justifies prime evening shows over newer mid-budget releases. If it falls below 25%, distributors will push for a faster shift to smaller auditoriums.

Budget Maths and Value Justification

At a reported all-in cost of ₹120–140 crore, including print and advertising, a ₹198 crore India net haul already clears the theatrical hurdle. Add overseas gross, satellite, music and digital rights, and the film is comfortably profitable before its streaming premiere. That is a rare position for a Hindi gangland drama without a holiday-heavy release calendar.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Franchise pull works: Existing series viewers converted into opening-weekend footfalls, giving the film a front-loaded but high base.
  • Heartland dominance: UP, Bihar and MP circuits are outperforming metro multiplexes, a reversal of the usual urban-first trend.
  • Star value intact: Pankaj Tripathi's Kaleen Bhaiya remains the single most quoted character in Hindi crime fiction, and the marketing leaned on it.
  • Dip is normal, not alarming: A 60–70% second-week drop is standard for event-style releases with strong opening weekends.
  • Week three is decisive: Screen retention and weekday occupancy will determine whether the film targets ₹230 crore or stalls near ₹210 crore.

Verdict: A Profitable Hit With a Shorter Tail

Mirzapur The Movie has done what most adaptations fail to do — it converted a streaming brand into a theatrical event. The ₹198 crore day-13 figure is strong, and the cost structure means the film is already in the green. The trade's focus now shifts to whether it can add another ₹25–35 crore before the streaming window opens.

For audiences, the calculus is simple: the film is still playing widely in Hindi belts, and ticket prices in tier-2 cities make it an easy weekend pick. For the industry, the result strengthens the case for more big-screen extensions of successful Indian series, provided the casting and scale hold up.
