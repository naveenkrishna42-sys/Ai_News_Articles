HDFC Bank vs SBI: Gap Narrows as State Bank Posts Record Q1 Profit

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • SBI reported a standalone net profit of ₹21,121 crore for Q1 FY27, up 10.2% year-on-year.
  • The bank's NPAs hit a two-decade low, reflecting improved asset quality.
  • SBI may raise up to USD 10 billion in FCNR deposits by September end, according to Chairman CS Setty.

The numbers are out, and they tell a story of quiet resilience. State Bank of India (SBI), the country's largest lender, posted a standalone net profit of ₹21,121 crore for the first quarter of the current fiscal, a 10.2% jump from the year-ago period. The growth came on the back of higher interest income, even as the bank continued to clean up its books.

For years, the narrative in Indian banking has been about HDFC Bank's dominance—its efficiency, its profitability, its sheer consistency. But with SBI now reporting record earnings and asset quality at a two-decade best, the gap between the king and the challenger is narrowing. The question is no longer whether SBI can compete, but how long before it overtakes.

A Record Quarter, Built on Interest Income

SBI's Q1 performance was driven by robust net interest income, which grew as the bank leveraged its massive deposit base. The ₹21,121-crore profit is a record for the lender, surpassing its previous best. The bank's net interest margin, a key metric for profitability, held steady even as the cost of deposits rose across the industry.

What's more, the bank's asset quality has improved significantly. Gross non-performing assets (NPAs) fell to a two-decade low, a sign that the worst of the bad-loan cycle is firmly behind it. This is not just a cyclical recovery; it reflects structural changes in how the bank underwrites and monitors credit.

The Challenger's Playbook

SBI's rise is not accidental. The bank has been aggressively expanding its digital footprint, rationalising its branch network, and focusing on retail and SME lending. Its sheer scale—over 500 million customers—gives it a cost advantage that few rivals can match.

Chairman CS Setty has also signalled ambition beyond domestic shores. The bank may mop up USD 10 billion in Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) deposits by the end of September, a move that would bolster its foreign currency kitty and support overseas lending. This is a calculated push to tap into the diaspora's savings, and it comes at a time when global rates are still elevated.

Analysts Take Notice

Brokerages are beginning to re-rate the stock. Motilal Oswal Financial Services has set a target price of ₹1,370 for SBI, citing improving return ratios and a strong deposit franchise. The bank's loan growth has outpaced the industry average, and its subsidiary businesses—from insurance to asset management—are adding value.

Investors, too, are paying attention. SBI's stock has been on an upward trajectory, though it still trades at a discount to HDFC Bank on price-to-book multiple. That valuation gap, many argue, is the opportunity.

HDFC Bank's Moat

HDFC Bank, for its part, remains the most profitable private lender in the country. Its return on assets and return on equity are still superior to SBI's, and its customer service and technology stack are widely regarded as best-in-class. The merger with its parent, HDFC Ltd, has given it a massive mortgage book and a larger balance sheet.

But the bank has faced its own challenges—deposit growth has lagged loan growth, and the integration has thrown up some operational hiccups. Its net profit, while healthy, has not grown at the pace SBI has managed in recent quarters.

The Gap Is Closing

In absolute terms, HDFC Bank still leads on net profit. But the margin of difference has shrunk. In Q1 FY27, SBI's profit was roughly 70% of HDFC Bank's, compared to about 60% a few years ago. At this pace, the gap could close entirely within the next couple of years.

What works in SBI's favour is its low cost of funds. As a public sector bank with a huge current and savings account (CASA) ratio, it can price loans more aggressively. It also has a larger branch network, which helps in mobilising deposits in semi-urban and rural areas—a segment that is growing faster than metros.

What to Watch Next

The next few quarters will be crucial. SBI's ability to sustain double-digit profit growth, manage its slippages, and execute on its digital strategy will determine whether it can truly dethrone HDFC Bank. Watch for the bank's commentary on loan growth guidance and any update on the FCNR deposit mobilisation plan.

For now, the king is still standing. But the challenger is no longer just knocking at the door—it's already inside the house.
