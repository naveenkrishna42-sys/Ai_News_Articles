Delhi High Court Seeks Explanation Over Activist's Detention

The Delhi High Court on Monday expressed strong disapproval over the alleged illegal confinement of activist Nadeem Khan, questioning how Delhi Police could enter his residence and detain him for nearly eight hours without proper legal authorization. The court was responding to a plea challenging the circumstances under which Khan was held ahead of a planned protest.

Court Questions Entry Without Warrant

During the hearing, the bench questioned the basis on which police officers entered Khan's house, asking pointedly, 'How can you enter his house?' The court emphasized that such actions require due process and proper warrants, especially when involving residential premises. The judges noted that mere suspicion or inquiry cannot justify forced entry without following established legal protocols.

Police Claim of Inquiry Visit

In their submission before the court, Delhi Police argued that their visit to Khan's residence was solely for making inquiries related to the upcoming protest. However, the court remained unconvinced, pointing out that even routine inquiries must respect privacy rights and follow procedural safeguards. The police further stated that no formal arrest was made during the visit, but the court questioned whether prolonged detention without formal charges constitutes unlawful confinement.

Timeline and Context of Detention

According to reports, Khan was allegedly held at his residence for approximately eight hours on October 11, a day before a scheduled protest. His legal representatives argued that this amounted to preventive detention aimed at preventing his participation in the demonstration. The court has now sought a detailed report from the Delhi Police regarding the exact sequence of events, the legal provisions invoked, and the justification for the extended questioning at his home.

Court Directs Detailed Inquiry Report

The Delhi High Court has directed the Delhi Police to submit a comprehensive report within a specified timeframe, outlining the reasons for the visit, the duration of detention, and the legal backing for their actions. The court warned that failure to provide satisfactory explanations could result in disciplinary action. This case has reignited debates over the balance between maintaining public order and protecting civil liberties, particularly in the context of protests and dissent.

  • Legal Precedent: The court's intervention reinforces the requirement of warrants for residential entries, even during investigations.
  • Civil Liberties: The case highlights concerns over preventive detentions targeting activists ahead of public demonstrations.
  • Accountability: The directive for a detailed report underscores judicial oversight over police conduct in matters involving personal liberty.
