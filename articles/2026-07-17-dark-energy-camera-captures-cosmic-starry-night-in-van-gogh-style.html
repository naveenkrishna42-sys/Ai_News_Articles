Dark Energy Camera captures cosmic 'Starry Night' in Van Gogh style

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Dark Energy Camera captured a 570-megapixel image of a star-filled region in the Milky Way.
  • The image's swirling patterns of stars, nebulae, and dust clouds bear a striking resemblance to Van Gogh's 'The Starry Night'.
  • The image was released by the team behind the Dark Energy Survey, showcasing the camera's ability to capture fine details in the cosmos.

Cosmic Canvas: A 570-Megapixel Masterpiece

The night sky has always inspired artists, but now a telescope has returned the favor. The Dark Energy Camera (DECam) — a 570-megapixel instrument mounted on the Víctor M. Blanco 4-meter Telescope in Chile — has captured a celestial scene that looks like it was painted by Vincent van Gogh.

The image, released this week by the Dark Energy Survey collaboration, shows a dense region of the Milky Way. Swirling clouds of interstellar gas and dust, dotted with bright stars, form patterns that echo the iconic brushstrokes of The Starry Night.

Officials at the National Science Foundation's Cerro Tololo Inter-American Observatory, which hosts the telescope, described the image as a "cosmic redux" of the 1889 masterpiece.

How the Camera Captured the Scene

DECam was originally built for the Dark Energy Survey, a project that mapped hundreds of millions of galaxies to understand why the universe's expansion is accelerating. But the camera also spends time on other observations, including this one of the Milky Way's galactic plane.

The image combines multiple exposures taken through different filters. These filters isolate light from specific elements — hydrogen, oxygen, and sulfur — which glow in distinct colors. The result is a vivid, layered portrait of a stellar nursery, where new stars are born inside dense clouds of gas.

According to the team, the swirling appearance comes from magnetic fields and stellar winds sculpting the material. The bright stars scattered across the frame are both young and old, adding sparkle to the scene.

Art Meets Astrophysics

The comparison to Van Gogh is not just a poetic one. The painting's iconic swirls have long been compared to turbulent flows in physics — from whirlpools to star-forming clouds. In this case, the image's blue and yellow hues, along with the dynamic patterns, create a near-perfect visual echo.

Dr. Alfredo Zenteno, a staff scientist at the observatory, said in a statement that the image "reminds us that the universe is not just a place of scientific inquiry, but also of profound beauty."

The image has been circulating widely on social media, with users calling it a "cosmic van Gogh" and a "starry night redux." It was also featured on Newswise and Phys.org, drawing attention from both astronomers and art lovers.

DECam's 570-megapixel resolution allows it to capture fine details across a wide field of view — about 2.2 degrees across, or four times the diameter of the full moon. This makes it ideal for such sweeping portraits of the Milky Way.

The camera continues to operate, supporting new surveys and public outreach images. The team plans to release more such cosmic art pieces in the coming months.

For now, the image stands as a reminder that science and art often share the same canvas — the night sky.
