Silent Atherosclerosis: A Hidden Epidemic

New imaging studies presented at the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) 2026 conference have revealed a widespread and previously overlooked burden of silent atherosclerosis in young adults. The findings suggest that one in every 13 adults under the age of 30 may have silent artery plaques, a condition that can lead to serious cardiovascular events if left undetected.

What is Silent Atherosclerosis?

Silent atherosclerosis refers to the presence of artery plaques that do not cause symptoms but can rupture or cause blockages, leading to heart attacks, strokes, or other cardiovascular diseases. Unlike traditional atherosclerosis, which often presents with symptoms like chest pain, silent atherosclerosis is asymptomatic, making it difficult to detect and treat.

Key Findings from the ESC 2026 Conference

The REACT study, presented at ESC 2026, mapped the prevalence of silent atherosclerosis across the adult lifespan. The study found that silent artery plaques may appear as early as the third decade of life and can remain undetected by standard risk assessment tools. This is a significant finding because it challenges the traditional view that atherosclerosis is a disease of the elderly.

Why This Matters

The discovery of widespread silent atherosclerosis in young adults has profound implications for public health and medical practice. It suggests that cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk assessment and prevention strategies need to be revised to include early detection and intervention. The current focus on traditional risk factors like high cholesterol and hypertension may not be sufficient to identify and manage silent atherosclerosis.

Expert Opinion and Critical Analysis

Dr. Rajesh Verma, a cardiologist at Apollo Hospitals, commented on the findings: 'This study highlights the need for a paradigm shift in how we approach cardiovascular health. Silent atherosclerosis can be a silent killer, and early detection is crucial.' However, Dr. Verma also noted that the study's findings are based on imaging data and may not reflect the clinical significance of these plaques. Further research is needed to understand the long-term implications of silent atherosclerosis in young adults.
