Nifty, Sensex Fall for Second Day as F&O Expiry, Selling in HDFC Bank, RIL Weigh

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Nifty fell below the 23,800 mark, with Sensex declining more than 400 points
  • HDFC Bank and Reliance Industries were the top drags on both indices
  • Monthly futures and options expiry added to the selling pressure in the market

Indian stock markets continued their slide on Tuesday, with both headline indices extending losses for the second straight session. The sell-off gathered pace as the monthly futures and options expiry approached, and heavyweight stocks like HDFC Bank and Reliance Industries bore the brunt of selling.

The Nifty 50 slipped below the 23,800 mark in afternoon trade, while the BSE Sensex shed over 400 points. Traders cited a lack of fresh triggers and caution ahead of the weekly F&O expiry as key reasons for the downturn.

Heavyweights Lead the Fall

HDFC Bank and Reliance Industries emerged as the top losers among Nifty stocks, accounting for a significant chunk of the index's decline. HDFC Bank shares dropped over 2%, while RIL fell more than 1.5%. The two stocks together have a combined weight of over 20% on the Nifty, amplifying their impact on the benchmark.

Other major contributors to the fall included ICICI Bank, Infosys, and Larsen & Toubro. The broader market also felt the heat, with the BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices trading in the red.

F&O Expiry Sparks Volatility

The monthly derivatives expiry, scheduled for Thursday, kept traders on edge. Rollover activity from the July series to August contracts added to the volatility, with many participants choosing to square off positions rather than carry them forward.

Analysts pointed out that the market had been trading in a narrow range over the past few sessions, and the expiry often acts as a catalyst for sharp moves. Open interest data showed heavy buildup of call options at the 23,800 and 24,000 strikes, indicating resistance levels.

Global Cues Remain Mixed

Overseas markets provided no clear direction. Asian peers traded mixed, with Japan's Nikkei edging higher while China's Shanghai Composite slipped. US futures were flat ahead of key earnings reports later this week.

Foreign portfolio investors have been net sellers in recent sessions, adding to domestic pressure. Provisional data from exchanges showed FPIs offloaded equities worth over Rs 1,200 crore on Monday. Domestic institutional investors, however, have been net buyers, providing some cushion.

Sectorally, the Nifty Bank index fell over 1%, led by declines in HDFC Bank and Axis Bank. The Nifty IT index also slipped, weighed down by Infosys and TCS. On the flip side, the Nifty Pharma index managed to stay afloat, supported by gains in Sun Pharma and Dr Reddy's.

Volatility index India VIX edged up, reflecting heightened anxiety among traders. The gauge, often called the fear index, rose 3% to hover around the 14-mark.

Market breadth was negative, with roughly two declining stocks for every advancing one on the BSE. This broad-based selling indicated that the weakness was not limited to a few stocks.

Technically, the Nifty has slipped below its 50-day moving average, a key support level. If the index fails to hold above 23,700, analysts expect further downside towards the 23,500 zone. The next few sessions, especially the F&O expiry, will be crucial in determining the short-term trend.

Traders are now eyeing the US Federal Reserve's policy meeting next week for cues on interest rates. Any hawkish surprise could exacerbate selling in emerging markets like India.
