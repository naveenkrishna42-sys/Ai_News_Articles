SC Slams UP Cop Over Hate Crime Probe Evasion

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court rebukes UP investigating officer for evading court in hate crime case
  • Court questions delay and lack of transparency in investigation
  • Case highlights concerns over handling of hate crimes in Uttar Pradesh

SC Takes Exception to Officer's Absence

The Supreme Court on Tuesday pulled up the Uttar Pradesh government over the conduct of an investigating officer in a hate crime case, asking why the officer was "playing hide and seek" with the court. The bench expressed displeasure over the officer's repeated failure to appear before the court, which has stalled the proceedings.

The remarks came during a hearing on a plea related to the case, where the court sought an explanation from the state. The officer's absence has raised questions about the seriousness with which hate crime complaints are being handled in the state.

Court's Sharp Rebuke

"Why is the investigating officer playing hide and seek with the court?" the bench asked, directing the state to ensure the officer's presence at the next hearing. The court's observation underscores its growing impatience with procedural delays that often plague sensitive cases.

The bench also noted that the officer's non-appearance was hampering the court's ability to monitor the investigation. It warned that continued evasion would invite strict action, including possible contempt proceedings.

Concerns Over Hate Crime Probe

The case pertains to an alleged hate crime, a category of offences that has drawn heightened judicial scrutiny in recent years. The court's intervention signals its intent to keep a tight leash on investigations that involve communal overtones, where delays can exacerbate tensions.

Legal experts say the court's message is clear: investigating officers cannot treat court summons casually, especially in cases with broader societal implications. The state has been asked to file a status report and ensure compliance.

What's at Stake

The Supreme Court's tough stance reflects a broader judicial push for accountability in hate crime probes. Victims and petitioners often face long waits and procedural hurdles, and the court appears determined to cut through them.

The Uttar Pradesh government has not yet issued an official response to the court's remarks. It remains to be seen how the state will address the court's concerns in its next submission.

This is not the first time the apex court has expressed dissatisfaction with the pace of hate crime investigations. Earlier rulings have emphasised the need for swift and impartial probes to maintain public confidence in the justice system.

The Road Ahead

The next hearing is expected to take place in the coming weeks, where the investigating officer's presence will be mandatory. The court has made it clear that any further absence would be viewed seriously.

Observers will be watching how the Uttar Pradesh police respond to this judicial directive, as it could set a precedent for handling similar cases across the country.
