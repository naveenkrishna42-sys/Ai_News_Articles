BRICS Trade Surge Boosts Indian Export Opportunities Amid Growing Deficit

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-11 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Intra‑BRICS trade grew 18% YoY, lifting India’s import share to 42% of its total imports.
  • India’s trade deficit with BRICS widened to ₹2.8 lakh crore, prompting a shift toward higher‑value exports.
  • Policy reforms and logistics upgrades are essential for Indian firms to capture the emerging market upside.

Overview of the Recent BRICS Trade Surge

Data from the Ministry of Commerce shows that intra‑BRICS trade jumped 18% in the 2025‑26 fiscal year, marking the steepest rise since the bloc’s inception. India’s share of BRICS‑origin imports climbed to 42%, while its exports to fellow members linger at 22%.

Growth metrics and what they mean

The surge reflects deeper integration of supply chains in sectors such as fertilizers, electronics and automotive components. However, the asymmetry in export participation signals a structural imbalance that could affect India’s balance of payments if left unchecked.

Implications for Indian Importers and Exporters

Business leaders are recalibrating sourcing strategies to leverage lower‑cost inputs from Russia and Brazil, while seeking to upgrade product value for markets like China and South Africa.

Pricing benchmarks and supply‑chain shifts

Fertiliser prices, for instance, fell 9% year‑on‑year as Russian urea entered the Indian market under new credit‑linked contracts. Indian agro‑industries can now benchmark raw‑material costs against a ₹4,500‑per‑tonne baseline, a noticeable dip from the ₹5,200 level recorded in 2024.

On the export side, Indian pharmaceutical firms are targeting South African generic drug tenders, where average contract values hover around USD 2 million per batch. Achieving these contracts requires compliance with South Africa’s GMP standards, prompting Indian firms to invest an estimated ₹150 crore in certification upgrades.

  • Pros: Access to cheaper inputs, diversified market outlets, and potential to negotiate better credit terms.
  • Cons: Enlarged trade deficit, dependency on volatile commodity prices, and heightened competition from Chinese firms within the bloc.

Policy Backdrop and Strategic Considerations

The Indian government has responded with a mix of tariff adjustments and logistics incentives. A provisional 5% reduction in import duties for BRICS‑origin raw materials aims to offset the widening deficit, while a ₹2,000 crore fund supports export‑oriented SMEs.

Sanctions, deficits and future outlook

Despite Western sanctions on Russia, trade flows have remained robust due to parallel financial channels and the BRICS Development Bank’s credit facilities. Analysts project that if India can raise its export share to 30% within two years, the deficit could shrink by roughly ₹900 crore, bringing the net balance to a more sustainable ₹1.9 lakh crore.

Strategic Takeaways for Indian Buyers

Enterprises should benchmark input costs against the new Russian pricing, renegotiate supplier contracts, and explore joint‑venture models that embed value‑addition locally. Simultaneously, exporters must align product standards with partner‑country regulations to capture the upside of rising demand.

Overall, the BRICS trade upswing offers a tangible growth corridor, but only firms that adapt procurement and compliance frameworks will translate the macro trend into bottom‑line profit.
