Bitcoin rises to $64,300 but Fed decision holds the key

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin touched $64,300, recovering from recent lows near $63,000.
  • The bounce came despite a sell-off in AI and chip stocks that dragged global markets.
  • Over $510 million in crypto liquidations were recorded as volatility spiked.
  • Traders are waiting for the Fed's rate decision for the next major directional move.

Bitcoin climbed back to $64,300 during Wednesday's trading session, staging a modest recovery after slipping near the $63,000 mark earlier this week. The move higher came even as a broad sell-off in AI and chip stocks rattled global equity markets. Crypto traders, however, appeared to shrug off the weakness in tech shares, focusing instead on the US Federal Reserve's upcoming interest rate decision.

Bitcoin bounces despite tech-led pressure

The world's largest digital asset rose roughly 2% from its intra-week low, trading hands at $64,300 at the time of writing. Analysts attributed the bounce to short-term buying by traders who viewed the recent dip as an entry point. The recovery lacked conviction, though, with volumes remaining below the 20-day average.

On the equities side, the Nasdaq Composite fell sharply as chipmakers like Nvidia and AMD lost ground. The sell-off was triggered by disappointing earnings guidance from a major semiconductor firm. Despite this, Bitcoin's correlation with tech stocks has weakened in recent weeks, allowing it to hold its ground better than in previous episodes.

Liquidations cross $510 million as volatility spikes

The recent price swings triggered significant forced selling across crypto derivatives markets. Data from Coinglass showed that over $510 million worth of leveraged positions were liquidated in the past 24 hours. Long positions accounted for the majority of the liquidations, as traders who had bet on a sustained rally were caught off guard by the sudden dip below $63,000.

The liquidation wave was concentrated on Binance and OKX, with Bitcoin and Ethereum futures bearing the brunt of the losses. Open interest in Bitcoin futures also dropped, signaling that some speculative froth had been flushed out of the market. Analysts said this could set the stage for a more measured recovery if the Fed delivers a dovish surprise.

Fed decision in focus: What traders are watching

The main event for global markets today is the US Federal Reserve's interest rate announcement. The central bank is widely expected to hold rates steady at 5.25% to 5.50%, but traders are laser-focused on the accompanying statement and Chair Jerome Powell's press conference. Any hint of a rate cut in September could ignite a risk-on rally, lifting Bitcoin along with equities.

On the flip side, a hawkish tone or a surprise rate hike would likely send Bitcoin back toward the $60,000 support level. The CME FedWatch Tool currently shows a 65% probability of a cut in September, but that could shift rapidly depending on Powell's language. Crypto market participants have been conditioned to trade the Fed's narrative, and today is no different.

ETF outflows add to uncertainty

Adding to the cautious mood, spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) in the US recorded net outflows for the third consecutive day. Data from SoSoValue showed that the 11 approved ETFs saw a combined net outflow of $120 million on Tuesday, led by Grayscale's GBTC and Fidelity's FBTC. The outflows suggest that institutional demand has cooled off after a strong first half of the year.

Despite the outflows, some analysts argue that the ETF channel remains a net positive for Bitcoin over the medium term. The key question is whether the outflows are a temporary profit-taking event or a sign of deeper bearish sentiment. The answer may become clearer once the Fed's policy path is confirmed.

For now, Bitcoin is stuck in a familiar pattern — rallying into resistance near $65,000 and finding support around $60,000. The next breakout, one way or the other, will likely depend on whether the Fed signals an imminent pivot to looser monetary policy. Until then, traders are playing a waiting game.
