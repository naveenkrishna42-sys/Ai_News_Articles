Karnataka bans sale of gutka, pan masala with tobacco for one year

📅 2026-08-12 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • One-year ban on sale, distribution of gutka and pan masala containing tobacco or nicotine in Karnataka
  • Order covers manufacture, storage, and transport of these products
  • CAMPCO urges government to protect arecanut farmers and traders from fallout

In a significant move aimed at curbing tobacco use, the Karnataka government has banned the sale, distribution, and manufacture of gutka and pan masala containing tobacco or nicotine for a period of one year. The order, issued on Wednesday, comes into effect immediately and applies across the state.

The prohibition extends beyond retail sales to include storage and transport of these products. Officials said the decision was taken to address public health concerns, though the full text of the order has not yet been made public.

Scope of the ban

The ban covers all forms of gutka and pan masala that contain tobacco or nicotine in any quantity. This includes products that are sold in sachets, tins, or loose form. The state government has invoked its powers under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006, to enforce the restriction.

Authorities have been directed to conduct regular inspections of shops, wholesale markets, and godowns to ensure compliance. Violators could face penalties under the Act, including fines and imprisonment, though specific figures have not been disclosed.

Reaction from trade bodies

The move has drawn immediate reaction from the Central Arecanut and Cocoa Marketing and Processing Cooperative (CAMPCO), which has urged the government to ensure that the ban does not disrupt the arecanut trade. Arecanut, a key ingredient in many pan masala products, is a major cash crop in Karnataka, particularly in the coastal districts of Dakshina Kannada and Udupi.

CAMPCO representatives have sought a meeting with state officials to discuss safeguards for farmers and traders who depend on the arecanut economy. They argue that the ban should target only the harmful additives, not the legitimate uses of arecanut in other products.

Public health rationale

Health experts have long pointed to the dangers of gutka and pan masala, which are linked to oral cancer and other serious illnesses. The ban is seen as a step toward reducing the prevalence of tobacco use in the state, particularly among younger populations.

However, enforcement will be a key challenge. Previous bans in other states have faced difficulties due to the widespread availability of these products and the grey market. The Karnataka government has said it will work with district administrations to ensure strict implementation.

What lies ahead

Over the next few weeks, the state is expected to release detailed guidelines for enforcement and for handling seized products. CAMPCO and other trade bodies are likely to press for clarity on how the ban will affect arecanut exports and domestic supply chains.

All eyes will be on how effectively the ban is implemented and whether it leads to a measurable dip in tobacco consumption. The state government has indicated it will review the order's impact before deciding on any extension beyond the one-year period.
