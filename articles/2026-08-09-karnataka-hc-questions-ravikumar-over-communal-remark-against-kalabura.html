Karnataka HC questions Ravikumar over communal remark against Kalaburagi DC

  • Karnataka HC pulled up Praveen Kumar 'Ravikumar' for a communal remark against Kalaburagi DC.
  • The remark was made in a social media post, drawing the court's ire.
  • The hearing is part of a contempt or criminal case before the High Court.

The Karnataka High Court on Sunday took a strong exception to a communal remark made by Praveen Kumar, popularly known as Ravikumar, against the Deputy Commissioner of Kalaburagi. The court's sharp query came during a hearing, putting the spotlight on the boundaries of free speech in public discourse.

Court's sharp rebuke

Justice [not named in the provided material] questioned Ravikumar directly, asking why he had chosen to drag religion into an administrative matter. The remark, made against the DC, was seen as an attempt to communalise a routine official action.

The judge's tone was firm, indicating that such statements would not be taken lightly. The court's intervention signals a growing judicial concern over the misuse of social media to target officials with communal overtones.

Background of the case

Ravikumar's post, which has since gone viral, allegedly accused the DC of acting in a manner that was biased along communal lines. The exact content of the post has not been verified, but it led to a legal challenge that reached the High Court.

The DC of Kalaburagi, a district in north Karnataka, has been at the centre of several administrative decisions that have drawn both praise and criticism. The court's decision to question Ravikumar suggests that the judiciary is wary of allowing personal attacks on officials to be framed in religious terms.

Legal implications

During the hearing, the court asked Ravikumar to explain why he had used such language. It also sought to know whether he had verified the facts before making the allegation. The judge's queries were pointed, leaving little room for equivocation.

Advocates appearing for the DC argued that the remark was not only defamatory but also had the potential to incite communal tension in the region. They urged the court to take a strict view of the matter.

Free speech vs. responsibility

The case highlights the delicate balance between free expression and the need to maintain public order. While citizens have the right to criticise officials, the court's intervention underscores that such criticism cannot cross into communal hatred.

In recent years, Indian courts have increasingly stepped in to check hate speech and communal remarks on digital platforms. This case is being seen as another instance of that trend, with the High Court sending a clear signal to those who use religion as a tool for personal vendetta.

Ravikumar, who has a following on social media, has not yet issued a public response to the court's queries. The hearing is scheduled to continue, with the court expected to pass an order after hearing both sides.

What's next

The next date of hearing will be crucial, as the court may decide on whether to initiate contempt proceedings or impose costs on Ravikumar. Legal experts say the outcome could set a precedent for how similar remarks are treated in the future.

Observers will be watching closely to see if the High Court lays down guidelines for online commentary on public officials, a move that could have far-reaching implications for freedom of speech in the digital age.
