Honor Sets Aug 12 China Launch for Robot Phone

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Honor's Robot Phone launches in China on August 12.
  • The device boasts a 200MP titanium gimbal camera.
  • AI-powered automation redefines smartphone interaction.
  • Early hands-on reports call the design radical and ambitious.

Chinese smartphone maker Honor has confirmed that its much-anticipated Robot Phone will go on sale in China on August 12. The announcement, first reported by TechNode, ends months of speculation about the device that the company has teased as a radical departure from conventional handsets.

The Robot Phone is not a foldable or a rollable. Instead, Honor has focused on automation and camera technology. At the heart of the device is a 200MP titanium gimbal camera — a hardware setup that promises stabilisation previously reserved for professional video rigs.

A Gimbal Built for Vloggers

The gimbal camera is the headline feature. The titanium construction keeps the module lightweight yet durable. The stabilisation system is designed to let users shoot smooth video while walking, running, or even cycling without needing an external gimbal.

WIRED, which got early hands-on time with the device, called it a "radical reinvention" of the smartphone for the robot age. CNET, which also played with the phone, described the experience as "wild". The camera system, they say, feels like having a professional stabiliser built into the phone.

AI-Driven Automation

Beyond the camera, the Robot Phone leans heavily into artificial intelligence. The device is said to automate routine tasks — from sorting notifications to managing app permissions — without constant user input. Honor has positioned this as a step toward a more autonomous smartphone.

The company has not yet revealed full specifications, including the processor or battery capacity. However, early reports suggest the phone runs on a flagship-tier chipset with dedicated AI acceleration.

China First, Global Later?

The August 12 launch is for the Chinese market. Honor has not confirmed a global release date. Given the company's recent strategy, a phased rollout is likely, with international availability possibly following later in the year or early 2027.

Pricing remains unannounced. With a titanium gimbal camera and advanced AI features, the Robot Phone is expected to sit in the premium segment, competing with the likes of Samsung's Galaxy Ultra series and Apple's Pro models.

Honor has been on a roll since its split from Huawei. The company has consistently pushed camera innovation, and the Robot Phone appears to be its most ambitious statement yet. The titanium gimbal, in particular, could set a new benchmark for mobile videography.

What Critics Are Saying

Early reactions from tech journalists who handled the device are largely positive. The gimbal mechanism is reportedly smooth and responsive. The AI features, while still in early software form, show promise in reducing the friction of daily phone use.

Some reviewers have noted that the device feels "different" — a compliment in a market flooded with iterative updates. The Robot Phone may not be for everyone, but it is clearly not trying to be.

Honor is betting that creators and early adopters will embrace the new form factor. The company's confidence is evident in the launch timeline — August 12 is just days away, and the device is already generating significant buzz.

The Bigger Picture

The smartphone industry has been criticised for stagnation. Honor's Robot Phone challenges that narrative. By focusing on automation and camera hardware, the company is attempting to carve a niche that others have ignored.

Whether the Robot Phone becomes a mainstream hit or a niche curiosity remains to be seen. The launch will be closely watched by competitors and consumers alike.

As the August 12 date approaches, expect more details to emerge — including pricing, full specs, and possibly a global launch window. For now, all eyes are on Honor's bold experiment in the world's largest smartphone market.
