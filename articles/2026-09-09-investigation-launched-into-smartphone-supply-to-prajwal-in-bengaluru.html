Formal Inquiry Ordered After Contraband Discovery

Bengaluru Central Prison Officials Confirm Seizure

In a development that has raised questions about security lapses within Karnataka’s prison system, authorities at Bengaluru Central Prison have initiated a formal investigation into the recovery of a smartphone allegedly used by former MP Prajwal Revanna during his judicial custody. The device was reportedly found during a routine inspection, triggering a high-level review of entry points and surveillance protocols.

Prison sources indicate that the probe will examine potential breaches in frisking procedures, visitor access controls, and staff deployment patterns. Senior officials from the Department of Prisons have been tasked with submitting a detailed report within two weeks, including recommendations to prevent future incidents.

Background on Prajwal Revanna’s Incarceration

Prajwal Revanna, a former Lok Sabha member from Bengaluru Central, was placed under judicial custody earlier this year following allegations related to misconduct. He has been lodged in the high-security wing of Bengaluru Central Prison, where movement and communication restrictions are typically stringent.

The presence of a functioning smartphone inside such a facility is considered a serious breach, as these devices can be used to coordinate external influence, leak sensitive information, or tamper with evidence. Prison rules strictly prohibit inmates from possessing mobile phones, and violations often result in disciplinary action.

Implications for Prison Security Infrastructure

Tightening Contraband Control Measures

This incident underscores persistent vulnerabilities in managing contraband within Indian prisons, particularly in urban centers like Bengaluru where political figures are housed. Experts suggest deploying advanced screening technologies such as body scanners, RF signal jammers, and biometric visitor verification systems to strengthen perimeter control.

Additionally, regular audits of prison staff rotations and unexplained leave patterns may help identify insider facilitation. Several states, including Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu, have already implemented zero-tolerance policies for unauthorized items, backed by surprise inspections and enhanced CCTV coverage.

Public and Political Reactions

Opposition leaders in Karnataka have demanded a transparent, independent probe, citing concerns over political interference given Revanna’s status. Meanwhile, the state government has assured full cooperation with the investigation, emphasizing that no individual inside or outside the prison premises will be shielded from accountability.

Legal experts note that if evidence emerges of deliberate collusion among prison personnel, charges under Section 269 of the IPC (public nuisance) and relevant service conduct rules could be invoked. The outcome of this probe is expected to set precedent for handling similar cases involving VIP inmates.
