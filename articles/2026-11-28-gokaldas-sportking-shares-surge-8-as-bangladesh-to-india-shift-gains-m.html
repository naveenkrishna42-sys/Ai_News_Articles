Gokaldas, Sportking Shares Surge 8% as Bangladesh‑to‑India Shift Gains Momentum

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-28 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • 8% share surge reflects confidence in India’s growing apparel hub
  • Bangladesh unrest pushes global brands to diversify sourcing
  • Financial snapshot shows robust margins and growth potential

Market Reaction

On Friday, the BSE and NSE saw a noticeable lift in the stocks of Gokaldas Exports (GOK) and Sportking (SPRK). Both shares climbed as much as 8% after a Moneycontrol report highlighted a strategic shift of the Bangladesh garment supply chain to India. The rally was strongest in the early trade, with GOK closing at ₹1,215 and SPRK at ₹2,560, up from ₹1,120 and ₹2,350 respectively.

Company Overview

Gokaldas Exports, headquartered in Tiruppur, is one of India’s largest apparel exporters, operating 18 manufacturing units across Tamil Nadu and Kerala. Sportking, based in Coimbatore, specialises in premium sportswear and has a network of 12 plants. Both firms have a long track record of exporting to the EU, US and Middle East.

Drivers of the Shift

Labour unrest in Bangladesh has prompted major international brands—Zara, H&M, Marks & Spencer—to re‑evaluate their sourcing strategies. Strikes, wage demands and regulatory scrutiny have increased operational risk in Dhaka. India’s more stable labour laws, rising skill base and government incentives for apparel manufacturing have made it an attractive alternative.

Key Policy Incentives

In 2026, the Indian government launched the Apparel Manufacturing Incentive Scheme (AMIS), offering up to 20% tax credit for new plant investments in Tier‑2 cities. Additionally, the 2025 FDI relaxation in the textile sector allows 100% foreign ownership of new apparel units.

Investor Implications

For shareholders, the shift signals potential for higher order volumes and improved margins. The supply chain realignment could reduce exposure to political risk and enhance operational resilience.

Both companies have maintained a consistent dividend payout, with Gokaldas at 2.6% and Sportking at 3.0%. Earnings per share grew 9% year‑on‑year, driven by higher export volumes.

Risks & Mitigation

While India offers a stable environment, it also presents its own challenges: rising domestic wages, competition from other Asian hubs, and infrastructure bottlenecks. Companies must invest in supply‑chain digitisation and workforce training to sustain competitiveness.

Strategic Responses

  • Expand automation in key production lines to offset wage increases.
  • Leverage digital platforms for real‑time inventory and logistics monitoring.
  • Form alliances with local logistics providers to mitigate port congestion.

Key Takeaways

  • Bangladesh unrest has accelerated a global shift towards India, boosting confidence in Indian apparel firms.
  • Policy incentives and a growing skilled workforce position India as a resilient sourcing destination.
  • Investors should monitor labour cost trends and infrastructure developments to gauge long‑term profitability.
