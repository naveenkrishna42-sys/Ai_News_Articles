Market Opens Lower Despite Energy Relief

The BSE Sensex opened Monday's session trading nearly 350 points lower, undercutting early optimism from a tentative Russia-Ukraine ceasefire announcement that pushed crude oil prices down. While the truce talks helped ease energy inflation fears, domestic investors remained cautious amid mixed global cues and profit-booking across heavyweight sectors.

Analysts say the drop reflects a correction phase rather than a trend reversal. "The ceasefire is positive for input costs, but macro headwinds like sticky core inflation and global rate uncertainty continue to weigh on sentiment," said a senior market participant familiar with fund flows.

Banks and IT indices were the primary drag, with HDFC Bank and Infosys each falling over 1.2% in morning trade. Auto and FMCG stocks showed resilience, supported by rural demand optimism and festive season expectations. The Indian rupee traded flat against the US dollar at 83.40, while 10-year government bond yields held around 6.5%.

Global Cues and Inflation Outlook

Wall Street closed mixed Friday, with the Nasdaq edging up on tech earnings while the Dow Jones slipped on rate concerns. European markets opened lower Monday, tracking cautious sentiment. For India, the key concern remains whether easing oil prices will translate into meaningful disinflation, giving the RBI room to pause rate hikes.

Investor Takeaways and Near-Term Outlook

For investors, the 350-point drop offers limited cause for alarm. The market is digesting a complex mix of geopolitical relief, global monetary tightening, and domestic macro data. Traders are likely to focus on this week's IIP numbers and CPI print for directional cues.

Meanwhile, foreign institutional investors have remained net sellers so far this month, while domestic mutual funds continue to support the market through systematic inflows. Volatility is expected to stay elevated until global rate trajectories clarify.
