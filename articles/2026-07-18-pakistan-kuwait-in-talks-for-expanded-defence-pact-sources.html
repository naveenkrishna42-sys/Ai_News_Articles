Pakistan, Kuwait in Talks for Expanded Defence Pact: Sources

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pakistan and Kuwait are in discussions for an expanded defence pact, according to sources.
  • The talks come amid heightened US-Iran tensions and concerns over Kuwait's air defence capabilities.
  • Kuwait is looking to strengthen its military posture, similar to Saudi Arabia's approach in the region.

Pakistan and Kuwait are exploring a major expansion of their defence ties, with discussions underway for a new partnership agreement, two sources familiar with the matter told Reuters.

The talks, confirmed by diplomatic and security sources, signal a deepening of military cooperation between the two nations. While details remain under wraps, the potential pact is expected to cover training, joint exercises, and defence equipment collaboration.

Regional Tensions Drive the Talks

The discussions come at a time of heightened volatility in the Middle East. Tensions between the US and Iran have put Gulf nations on edge, and Kuwait, in particular, is reassessing its defence needs.

Reports from regional media indicate that Kuwait's air defences have been strained during recent conflicts involving Iran. This has pushed the country to seek stronger partnerships, with Pakistan emerging as a key ally.

Pakistan's military, with its combat-proven capabilities and experience in counterterrorism operations, offers a reliable option for Kuwait. The country's air force and army are well-regarded in the Gulf, and Islamabad has a history of defence cooperation with several Arab states.

Kuwait's Saudi-Style Security Question

Analysts point out that Kuwait is now facing what some call a 'Saudi-style security question' — how to build a robust defence network without relying entirely on Western powers. Saudi Arabia has long invested in its own military capacity while also hosting foreign bases.

Kuwait, by contrast, has a smaller military footprint. The potential pact with Pakistan could help it bridge capability gaps, particularly in air defence and missile protection. The move mirrors similar discussions other Gulf states have had with Pakistan in recent years.

Pakistan's military strength is a key factor. With an estimated 640,000 active personnel and a well-developed arms industry, it is one of the largest and most capable forces in the Islamic world. Its expertise in air defence systems and pilot training is especially relevant for Kuwait.

What the Pact Could Include

The proposed agreement is still under negotiation, and officials have not yet confirmed its scope. However, sources indicate it could cover several areas:

  • Joint military exercises and training programmes for Kuwaiti personnel.
  • Technical assistance for air defence systems.
  • Potential sale or transfer of defence equipment.
  • Intelligence sharing on regional threats.

Both governments have remained tight-lipped about the talks. Kuwait's defence ministry has not issued a formal statement, and Pakistani officials have declined to comment on the record.

The development has drawn attention from other Gulf states, who are watching to see how the partnership evolves. Any formal agreement would likely strengthen Pakistan's position as a security partner in the region, a role it has played for decades in Saudi Arabia and the UAE.

What happens next depends on the pace of negotiations. Both sides are expected to hold further rounds of talks in the coming weeks, with a possible framework agreement later this year.
