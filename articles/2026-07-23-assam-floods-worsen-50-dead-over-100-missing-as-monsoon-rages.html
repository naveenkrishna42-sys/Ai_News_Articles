Assam Floods Worsen: 50 Dead, Over 100 Missing as Monsoon Rages

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • At least 50 people have died in Assam due to severe flooding, with over 100 still missing.
  • The state government has asked all departments to expedite support to affected communities.
  • The monsoon has also battered Himalayan states, with over 120 killed and thousands displaced across the region.

Death Toll Climbs Amid Relentless Rains

The flood situation in Assam has turned increasingly dire, with officials confirming that the death toll has risen to 50. Over 100 people remain missing as rescue teams struggle to reach isolated villages amid swollen rivers and continuous downpour.

According to reports, 10 more bodies were recovered in the last 24 hours, pushing the earlier count of 41 up to the current figure. The worst-affected districts are in the central and northern parts of the state, where the Brahmaputra and its tributaries have burst their banks.

State Machinery Mobilised for Relief

Assam's Chief Secretary held a high-level review meeting on Wednesday, directing all departments to expedite support to those stranded. The focus is on providing food, drinking water, and medical aid to the thousands living in temporary shelters and relief camps.

The National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) has been deployed in multiple locations, conducting boat rescues and air-dropping supplies. However, officials admit that the scale of the disaster is stretching available resources thin.

Regional Impact and Wider Monsoon Toll

The devastation in Assam is part of a larger monsoon tragedy unfolding across several Himalayan states and the northeast. Preliminary estimates from various state governments suggest that over 120 people have died in the region, with many more displaced from their homes.

Roads and bridges have been washed away in multiple areas, cutting off entire communities. Landslides triggered by the heavy rains have compounded the crisis, burying homes and blocking key highways.

Rescue Operations Hampered by Weather

Incessant rainfall and strong currents are making rescue operations slow and dangerous. Many of the missing are feared trapped under debris or swept away by floodwaters. Local fishermen and volunteers have joined forces with government teams, using country boats to navigate the submerged villages.

Authorities have not yet released a district-wise breakdown of casualties or a complete list of the missing. The state government has urged citizens to cooperate with evacuation orders and move to safer ground.

What Happens Next

The India Meteorological Department has forecast more heavy rain for Assam and neighbouring states over the next 48 hours. With river levels already above the danger mark, the risk of further flooding and landslides remains high. The state administration is bracing for a prolonged crisis, and relief efforts are expected to intensify in the coming days as weather conditions allow.
