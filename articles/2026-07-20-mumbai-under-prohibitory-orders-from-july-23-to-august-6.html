Mumbai under prohibitory orders from July 23 to August 6

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Prohibitory orders under Section 144 CrPC will be in effect in Mumbai from July 23 to August 6.
  • The order bans assembly of five or more people, carrying of weapons, and processions without permission.
  • Police cite the likelihood of public order disturbance as the reason for the preventive measure.

Mumbai Police impose Section 144 for two weeks

Mumbai will be under prohibitory orders for 15 days starting July 23. The Mumbai Police issued the order under Section 144 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC), effective until August 6. The directive bans the assembly of five or more people in any public place across the city.

Police officials stated the decision was taken in anticipation of potential public order disturbances. While the exact triggers for the order were not specified in the official release, sources indicate the move is precautionary.

What the order prohibits

Under the prohibitory order, no person or group can organise a procession, rally, or public gathering without prior written permission from the police. Carrying firearms, swords, sticks, or any sharp objects in public is also banned. Any violation will attract legal action under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code.

The order applies to the entire geographical area of Mumbai. Essential services and government functions are expected to remain exempted from the restrictions.

Reason cited by police

The police cited the "likelihood of public order disturbance" as the primary reason for invoking the draconian powers under Section 144. The order is preventive in nature and aims to maintain peace and communal harmony in the city.

Mumbai has seen a few protests and counter-protests in recent weeks over local issues. However, authorities have not linked the order to any specific event or group.

Public reaction and compliance

Commuters and shopkeepers in areas like Dadar, Andheri, and south Mumbai expressed surprise at the sudden move. Many said they were unaware of the order until media reports appeared. Local traders' associations said they would cooperate but sought clarity on whether routine business activities would be affected.

The police have urged citizens to report any suspicious activity or potential violations. Additional forces have been deployed in sensitive neighbourhoods as a precaution.

What happens next

The prohibitory order will remain in force until August 6 unless revoked earlier. The police will review the situation periodically and may extend or relax restrictions based on the ground reality. Residents are advised to check official police notifications before planning any large gatherings or events during this period.
