CWG 2026: Glasgow hands over to Ahmedabad as India eyes 2030

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Glasgow officially handed over the Commonwealth Games baton to Ahmedabad for the 2030 edition.
  • Boxer Jaismine Lamboria was named India's flag-bearer for the closing ceremony.
  • Australia's cyclists wrapped up a dominant medal haul on the final day.
  • India's contingent finished Day 10 with several podium finishes, capping a successful meet.

Glasgow's Final Curtain

The 2026 Commonwealth Games drew to a close in Glasgow on Sunday, with a ceremony that blended Scottish tradition with a distinct Indian flavour. The official handover of the baton to Ahmedabad, hosts of the 2030 Games, marked a symbolic shift in the Commonwealth sporting calendar.

Athletes from 72 nations and territories walked into the stadium for the last time, their medals glinting under the floodlights. For India, the moment carried extra weight — the country now prepares to host the Games for the first time since 2010.

Jaismine Lamboria Leads India's Flag

Boxer Jaismine Lamboria, who secured a medal earlier in the competition, was named India's flag-bearer for the closing ceremony. The choice underscored the rise of women's boxing in the country, with Lamboria's gritty performances in Glasgow drawing praise from fans and officials alike.

Her selection came as no surprise to those tracking the contingent's progress. Indian boxers collectively delivered one of their best overseas campaigns, with multiple podium finishes across weight categories.

Australia's Cycling Dominance

On the track, Australia's cyclists completed a mammoth medal haul, finishing the Games as the undisputed kings of the velodrome. Their final-day gold in the men's sprint and a silver in the women's keirin capped a week of relentless speed and tactical precision.

The Australian team's tally in cycling alone rivalled the total medal counts of several smaller nations. Their depth across road and track events set a benchmark that other countries, including India, will study closely ahead of Ahmedabad 2030.

India's Day 10 Highlights

India's athletes added to their medal count on the penultimate day, with strong showings in wrestling, athletics, and badminton. The women's 4x400m relay team clinched a bronze in a season-best time, while the men's hockey team wrapped up their campaign with a commanding victory in the bronze-medal match.

Youngsters in the squad, many making their CWG debut, gained invaluable experience in Glasgow. Team officials expressed satisfaction with the overall performance, though they stopped short of declaring a final tally, pending official confirmation from the Indian Olympic Association.

What's Next for India

With the baton now in Indian hands, attention shifts to Ahmedabad's preparations for 2030. The organising committee has already outlined plans for new venues and infrastructure, though timelines remain tentative.

For the athletes, the road to 2030 begins now. Selection trials, training camps, and international exposure tours are expected to be announced in the coming months. The Commonwealth Games may have ended in Glasgow, but for India, the countdown has just begun.
