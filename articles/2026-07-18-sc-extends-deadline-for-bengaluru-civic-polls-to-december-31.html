SC extends deadline for Bengaluru civic polls to December 31

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court extends election deadline for five Bengaluru city corporations until December 31.
  • The extension applies to BBMP and other civic bodies in the city.
  • Ongoing delimitation and reservation issues cited as reasons for delay.

New deadline for Bengaluru civic polls

The Supreme Court on Friday extended the deadline for holding elections in five Bengaluru city corporations until December 31. The order comes as a breather for the state government, which had sought more time to complete the electoral process.

The five civic bodies include the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) and four other city corporations. The court had earlier set a deadline, but unresolved issues around delimitation and reservation of wards prompted the extension.

Why the delay?

Officials cite the ongoing delimitation exercise as a key reason for the delay. Redrawing ward boundaries and finalising reservation for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes have taken longer than expected.

The state government argued that rushing the process could lead to legal challenges later. The Supreme Court accepted this reasoning and granted the additional time.

What happens next

The Karnataka State Election Commission will now work towards completing the election process by the new deadline. The commission has already begun preparatory work, including updating voter lists and identifying polling stations.

Political parties in Bengaluru have been waiting for the polls, with many candidates already campaigning informally. The extended deadline gives them more time to strategise.

The court has directed the state to ensure that elections are held without further delay. Any additional extension is unlikely, officials said.

The Bengaluru civic polls are crucial for the city's administration, as the elected bodies oversee urban planning, infrastructure, and public services. The current administrators have been managing affairs for months without an elected council.

Residents have raised concerns about pending development projects and service delivery, which new councils could address. The December deadline offers a clear timeline for when voters can expect to choose their representatives.

All eyes are now on the State Election Commission to see if it can meet the revised schedule.
