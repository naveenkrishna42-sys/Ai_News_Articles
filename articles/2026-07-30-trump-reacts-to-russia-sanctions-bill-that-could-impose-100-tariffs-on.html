Trump reacts to Russia sanctions bill that could impose 100% tariffs on India

📅 2026-07-30 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump called sweeping Russia sanctions bill 'not necessary' but did not reject it.
  • Bill could impose 100% tariffs on India, China for Russian oil imports.
  • US Senate fast-tracked the legislation in an 86-12 vote, named after Senator Lindsey Graham.

Trump's measured response to sanctions legislation

US President Donald Trump has weighed in on a fast-tracked Senate bill that could slap 100% tariffs on India for importing Russian oil. Calling the legislation 'not necessary,' Trump stopped short of outright opposing it, leaving room for ambiguity.

The bill, passed by the Senate in an 86-12 vote, is named after Senator Lindsey Graham. It targets countries that continue to buy Russian crude oil, with India and China squarely in its crosshairs. Trump's remark came as the legislation moves toward a final vote.

What the bill proposes

The proposed law would allow the US to impose tariffs of up to 100% on goods from nations deemed to be 'significant' importers of Russian oil. India has emerged as a major buyer of discounted Russian crude since the Ukraine war began in 2022.

Senators backing the bill argue that countries like India and China are undermining Western sanctions. 'They are the main culprits,' one US Senator said, referring to the two Asian giants. The legislation is being fast-tracked as a tribute to Senator Graham, a longtime hawk on Russia.

India's delicate balancing act

New Delhi has defended its oil purchases as a matter of energy security, not a political endorsement of Moscow. India buys Russian crude at a discount, helping it manage inflation and keep fuel prices in check for its 1.4 billion citizens.

However, the US move threatens to strain bilateral ties. India has sought to maintain a strategic partnership with Washington while preserving its traditional ties with Russia. The tariffs could hit Indian exports of pharmaceuticals, textiles, and engineering goods.

Officials have not yet confirmed how New Delhi plans to respond. Diplomatic channels remain open, with Indian envoys in Washington reportedly seeking clarifications.

What comes next

The bill now heads to the House of Representatives. If passed, it will land on Trump's desk for his signature. His 'not necessary' remark suggests he may not veto it, but he could still pressure lawmakers to soften the language.

For India, the window to lobby against the tariffs is narrowing. The coming weeks will show whether diplomacy can defuse what could become a major trade confrontation.
