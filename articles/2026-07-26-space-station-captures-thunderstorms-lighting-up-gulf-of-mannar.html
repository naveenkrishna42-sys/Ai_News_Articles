Space Station captures thunderstorms lighting up Gulf of Mannar

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Astronauts on the ISS captured thunderstorms over the Gulf of Mannar, with lightning illuminating the night sky.
  • The images show the Indian coastline and Sri Lanka's northern tip clearly visible from orbit.
  • Scientists are studying the imagery to understand storm dynamics in the region.

Astronauts aboard the International Space Station have captured a rare and striking view of thunderstorms lighting up the Gulf of Mannar. The images, released by NASA this week, show bright flashes of lightning over the sea, with the coastlines of India and Sri Lanka clearly visible below.

The photographs were taken during a night pass over the Indian subcontinent. The Gulf of Mannar, a shallow bay between the two countries, is known for its intense convective weather during the monsoon season.

Lightning over the Palk Strait

The images show a series of powerful thunderstorms stretching across the Gulf of Mannar and the Palk Strait. Lightning bolts appear as jagged white lines against the dark blue of the ocean. The city lights of Tamil Nadu's southern coast and Sri Lanka's Jaffna Peninsula are also visible, giving a sense of scale to the storm system.

Meteorologists say such storms are common in July, when the southwest monsoon drives moisture-laden winds over the warm waters of the bay. What makes these images special is the vantage point from space, offering a synoptic view of the weather system.

How the images were captured

The ISS orbits Earth at an altitude of about 400 kilometres. Astronauts use handheld cameras with high-sensitivity sensors to photograph lightning and other transient phenomena. The images are part of a long-running NASA programme to document Earth's weather from orbit.

Scientists at the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) have expressed interest in the data. The imagery can help validate satellite-based lightning detection systems and improve short-term forecasts for coastal communities.

What the images reveal

The photographs show multiple lightning strokes in quick succession, illuminating large patches of cloud. The Gulf of Mannar is a hotspot for thunderstorm activity during the monsoon, and the ISS pass happened to coincide with a particularly active night.

Researchers note that the brightness of the lightning suggests strong updrafts within the storms. Such storms can produce heavy rain, gusty winds, and occasional waterspouts. Fishermen in the region often monitor weather warnings before heading out to sea.

India's coastline from space

The images also highlight the dense network of lights along India's southern coast. Cities like Thoothukudi, Rameswaram, and Kanniyakumari glow brightly, contrasting with the darker ocean and the storm clouds. Sri Lanka's northern tip, including the city of Jaffna, is also clearly outlined.

This is not the first time the ISS has captured dramatic weather over India. Previous images have shown cyclones, dust storms, and the Himalayan snow cover. Each image adds to a growing archive used by climate scientists worldwide.

The Indian government has invested in its own lightning detection network, with over 200 sensors across the country. Data from the ISS can serve as an additional calibration tool for these ground-based systems.

No official statement has been issued by the Indian Space Research Organisation regarding the images. However, space enthusiasts and meteorologists have shared the photographs widely on social media, calling them a spectacular reminder of the planet's dynamic atmosphere.

The ISS crew continues to document Earth's weather patterns. More such images are expected as the monsoon season progresses over the Indian Ocean. Scientists will analyse the latest visuals to better understand the behaviour of thunderstorms in the Gulf of Mannar.
