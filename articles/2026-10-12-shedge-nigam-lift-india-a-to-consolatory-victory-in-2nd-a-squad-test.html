Shedge, Nigam Lift India A to Consolatory Victory in 2nd A-Squad Test

India A Rallies Behind Shedge-Nigam Stand

In a match that began with India A reeling at 87/5 in their first innings, Sunil Shedge anchored the lower order with a gritty 72 off 118 balls. His partnership with Sharma Nigam, who chipped in with a quick-fire 34, helped India A cross the 200-mark and stay competitive. The pair added 78 runs for the seventh wicket, turning a precarious situation into a respectable total.

Shedge’s Resilience Defines First Innings

Shedge’s innings was marked by patience and precision. He faced 174 deliveries, hitting just four boundaries but rotating strike efficiently. His ability to negate pace with soft hands and dash down the pitch against spin made him the cornerstone of India A’s recovery. His dismissal, caught at short leg attempting a sweep, ended a vital stand that gave the bowlers a competitive target of 248/9 declared.

Nigam Steers Bowling Attack to Dominant Second Innings

After enforcing a follow-on, India A’s bowlers struck early. Sharma Nigam led the charge, taking three quick wickets in the opening session. His line-and-length consistency troubled the opposition’s middle order, which collapsed from 112/2 to 167 all out in the 42nd over. Nigam finished with figures of 6/78 in the second innings, adding three catches to his tally—a rare all-round contribution that sealed India A’s six-wicket win.

Bowling Unit Enforces Follow-On Efficiently

The pace duo of A. Singh and R. Patel shared seven wickets between them across both innings. Singh’s hostile short-pitched bowling rattled the opposition’s openers, while Patel’s out-swinger dismantled the left-handed batsmen. Their combined pressure reduced the opposition to 186 all out in the first innings, setting up the follow-on scenario that became decisive.

Implications for Senior Team Selection

This performance strengthens both Shedge and Nigam’s claims for higher-level opportunities. Shedge’s technique against pace-bowling conditions mirrors the demands of international cricket, particularly in overseas tours. Nigam’s dual skillset—as a bowler who can bat at No. 7—is increasingly valuable in longer formats where lower-order resilience plays a game-changing role.

  • Pros: Shedge shows adaptability against varied bowling attacks; Nigam emerges as a reliable all-rounder in red-ball cricket
  • Key Highlight: Bowling unit takes 18 wickets collectively, enforcing follow-on via disciplined line lengths
  • Future Outlook: Strong showing boosts India A squad confidence ahead of upcoming multi-team tournaments and Duleep Trophy fixtures

While labelled a “consolatory” win due to prior series losses, this victory underscores India A’s growing maturity. With domestic cricket intensifying ahead of IPL auctions and senior call-ups, performances like these will influence selection committees evaluating fringe players ready for international breakthroughs.
