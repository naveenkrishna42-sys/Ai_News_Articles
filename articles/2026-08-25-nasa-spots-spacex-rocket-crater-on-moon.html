NASA spots SpaceX rocket crater on Moon

📅 2026-08-25 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • NASA's LRO captured images of a new lunar crater from SpaceX's Falcon 9 upper stage impact
  • The crater is about 10 meters wide and confirms SpaceX's intentional lunar impact mission
  • This marks the first time NASA has observed a human-made object crashing into the Moon

NASA Confirms SpaceX Moon Impact

NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) has captured images of a new lunar crater created by the impact of SpaceX's Falcon 9 upper stage. This marks the first time NASA has observed a human-made object intentionally crashing into the Moon, confirming SpaceX's mission to test lunar impact technology.

Crater Details and Analysis

The newly formed crater is approximately 10 meters wide, located in the lunar highlands near the Moon's south pole. The impact occurred on August 15, 2026, when SpaceX intentionally directed the Falcon 9 upper stage to crash into the Moon's surface.

Why This Matters

This event is significant for several reasons. First, it demonstrates SpaceX's capability to precisely control the trajectory of an upper stage to impact the Moon. Second, it provides valuable data for future lunar missions, particularly for understanding the effects of human-made objects on the lunar surface. Third, it confirms the feasibility of using lunar impacts as a method to test space debris mitigation techniques.

Expert Opinions

Dr. Sarah Noble, a planetary scientist at NASA, stated, "This impact event is a significant step forward in our understanding of lunar geology and the effects of human activities on the Moon. It also highlights the importance of international cooperation in space exploration."

Broader Implications

The impact of the Falcon 9 upper stage on the Moon has broader implications for space debris management and lunar exploration. It provides a unique opportunity to study the lunar regolith and the effects of high-velocity impacts on the Moon's surface. Additionally, it sets a precedent for future missions that may involve controlled impacts on the Moon or other celestial bodies.
