Aamir Khan Says 3 Idiots Not Based on Sonam Wangchuk: 'Chatur Is Incorrect'

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Aamir Khan stated the character Phunsukh Wangdu in 3 Idiots is not based on Sonam Wangchuk, calling the connection 'incorrect'.
  • The actor expressed concern over Wangchuk's health during a recent interaction.
  • Wangchuk had earlier said he didn't take legal action against the film to avoid being seen as seeking money.

Bollywood star Aamir Khan has categorically denied that his character in the 2009 blockbuster 3 Idiots was inspired by real-life education reformer Sonam Wangchuk. The clarification comes months after Wangchuk himself publicly distanced from the iconic role.

Speaking at a recent event, Khan dismissed the long-standing belief among fans that the film's genius innovator Phunsukh Wangdu was a fictionalised version of the Ladakh-based engineer and educator.

No Basis for the Claim

"Chatur is incorrect," Khan said, referencing the film's famously pedantic character. "The character of Phunsukh Wangdu in 3 Idiots is not based on Sonam Wangchuk. That connection has been made by people, but it is not true."

The actor's statement contradicts a popular narrative that has persisted for over a decade. Many viewers had assumed the film's climax — where the protagonist is revealed to be a reclusive inventor running a progressive school in Ladakh — was a direct homage to Wangchuk's work at the Students' Educational and Cultural Movement of Ladakh (SECMOL).

Wangchuk's Previous Stance

Sonam Wangchuk had earlier addressed the rumour in an interview, stating plainly: "I am not Phunsukh Wangdu from Aamir Khan's 3 Idiots." The educator revealed that he chose not to take legal action against the filmmakers despite the widespread association.

"I didn't want people to think I was trying to get money or fame from the film," Wangchuk had said. His decision was rooted in a desire to avoid being seen as opportunistic, even though the character's name and backstory closely mirrored his own public image.

The film, directed by Rajkumar Hirani and based on Chetan Bhagat's novel Five Point Someone, was a massive commercial and critical success. It won several Filmfare Awards and was India's official entry to the Oscars that year.

Concern Over Health

During the same interaction, Aamir Khan also expressed worry about Wangchuk's well-being. The educator has been at the centre of protests in Ladakh demanding statehood and constitutional safeguards for the region under the Sixth Schedule.

Khan, who has previously supported social causes, said he was concerned about the toll the ongoing agitation might be taking on Wangchuk's health. "I have heard about his health issues. I hope he takes care of himself," the actor added, without elaborating on specific details.

Wangchuk has been on a hunger strike at times during the protest, drawing attention to the demands of the Leh Apex Body (LAB) and the Kargil Democratic Alliance (KDA). The groups have been pressing for the inclusion of Ladakh in the Sixth Schedule of the Constitution, which would grant greater autonomy to tribal areas.

Officials have not yet confirmed any recent deterioration in Wangchuk's medical condition, but Khan's remarks have reignited public interest in the activist's welfare.

What Lies Ahead

With the actor now formally severing the fictional link to the real-life educator, the debate over artistic inspiration versus factual basis is likely to continue. Meanwhile, Wangchuk's health remains a point of concern as his protest enters its next phase.
