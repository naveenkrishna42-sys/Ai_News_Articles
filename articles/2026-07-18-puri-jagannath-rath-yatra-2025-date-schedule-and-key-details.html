Puri Jagannath Rath Yatra 2025: Date, Schedule, and Key Details

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Puri Jagannath Rath Yatra is scheduled for July 2025, with the exact date to be confirmed by temple authorities.
  • The festival involves the chariot procession of Lord Jagannath, Lord Balabhadra, and Goddess Subhadra to the Gundicha Temple.
  • Key rituals include the Snana Purnima (bathing ceremony) and the return journey, Bahuda Yatra.

The Puri Jagannath Rath Yatra, one of India's most awaited religious events, is set to take place in July 2025. The exact date, however, is yet to be officially announced by the Shree Jagannath Temple Administration (SJTA). Historically, the festival occurs on the second day of the bright fortnight of the Hindu month of Ashadha.

Last year, the yatra was held on July 7. Devotees across the country and abroad are already planning their visits to the coastal town of Odisha to witness the grand chariot procession.

What is the Rath Yatra?

The Rath Yatra, or Chariot Festival, is an annual event where the deities Lord Jagannath, Lord Balabhadra, and Goddess Subhadra are taken out of the Jagannath Temple in a grand procession. They travel to the Gundicha Temple, about three kilometres away, on massive wooden chariots.

Thousands of devotees pull the ropes attached to the chariots, a ritual believed to bring blessings. The festival symbolises the deities' visit to their aunt's home and is one of the few Hindu festivals where deities are publicly displayed.

Key Dates and Schedule for 2025

The SJTA typically releases the detailed schedule a few months before the event. The festival's preparatory rituals begin weeks in advance. The main events include:

  • Snana Purnima: The bathing ceremony, which usually falls in June. The deities are bathed with 108 pots of water.
  • Anavasara: A 15-day period after the bath when the deities are kept away from public view for recuperation.
  • Netrotsava: The painting of the deities' eyes, which marks their readiness for the yatra.
  • Rath Yatra: The main chariot procession to Gundicha Temple.
  • Bahuda Yatra: The return journey after a stay of nine days at Gundicha Temple.

The exact dates for 2025 will be confirmed after the astrological calculations by the temple's traditional priests.

What Devotees Can Expect

Puri transforms into a sea of humanity during the yatra. The town sees lakhs of pilgrims, with dedicated arrangements for crowd management and security. The Odisha government, along with the railway and local police, typically deploys special trains and additional personnel.

Devotees can also participate in the pulling of the chariot ropes, though it is advised to follow the designated lanes and safety protocols. The chariots — Nandighosha for Lord Jagannath, Taladhwaja for Lord Balabhadra, and Devadalana for Goddess Subhadra — are newly built each year from specific wood.

Significance of the Festival

The Rath Yatra is not just a local event. It has deep cultural and spiritual significance across India and abroad. It is believed that anyone who sees the deities on the chariots attains salvation. The festival also inspired the English word 'juggernaut', derived from Lord Jagannath's chariot.

For many, it is a time of community bonding, devotion, and celebration. The yatra also draws international tourists and researchers interested in India's living traditions.

Official updates on the 2025 schedule will be posted on the SJTA website and through major news outlets closer to the event. Devotees are advised to book accommodations well in advance, as Puri sees a massive spike in visitors.
