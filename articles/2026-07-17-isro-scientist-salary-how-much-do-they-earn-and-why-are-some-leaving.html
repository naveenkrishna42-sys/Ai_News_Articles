ISRO scientist salary: How much do they earn and why are some leaving?

📅 2026-07-17 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • ISRO scientists' salaries range from Rs 56,100 to Rs 2,18,200 per month depending on grade and experience.
  • Eligibility requires a B.E./B.Tech or M.Sc. with high GATE/JEST scores; selection through written test and interview.
  • Reports of 100+ resignations in recent months prompt government to issue a memo to retain key personnel.
  • Higher pay in private sector and international agencies, better recognition, and opportunities drive the exodus.

ISRO scientist salary: What does the pay scale look like?

An ISRO scientist's salary is determined by the Central Pay Commission (CPC) pay matrix. For a Scientist/Engineer 'SC' (entry-level), the basic pay starts at Rs 56,100 per month. With allowances, the gross pay can be around Rs 80,000-90,000.

For Scientist 'SD' (after 4-5 years), the basic pay rises to Rs 67,700. Higher grades like 'SF' and 'SG' can earn up to Rs 2,18,200 basic pay. These figures include Dearness Allowance (DA) and House Rent Allowance (HRA), which vary by location.

Senior scientists at the Outstanding Scientist or Distinguished Scientist level can have a basic pay exceeding Rs 2,00,000. However, these numbers are modest compared to private aerospace firms or international space agencies.

Eligibility: How does one become an ISRO scientist?

To become an ISRO scientist, a candidate needs a B.E./B.Tech in aerospace, electronics, mechanical, or computer science with at least 65% marks. An M.Sc. in physics or mathematics is also accepted. A valid GATE score is mandatory for most recruitments.

Selection involves a written test followed by a technical interview. For research positions, a JEST (Joint Entrance Screening Test) score is required. ISRO also recruits through campus placements from IITs and NITs.

Age limit is generally 28-35 years, with relaxations for reserved categories. Experience in relevant fields is preferred for lateral entry.

Why are scientists leaving? The resignation reports explained

Recent reports suggest at least 100 scientists have quit ISRO in the past few months, including from key missions like Gaganyaan. The Department of Space has issued a memo to stop the exodus, asking for reasons and offering retention measures.

Union Science Minister, downplaying the resignations, said: 'Many come and go' — but sources indicate a brain drain to private space startups and global firms. Higher pay, better recognition, and faster career growth are cited as reasons.

Private companies like SpaceX, Blue Origin, and Indian startups offer salaries 2-3 times higher. ISRO's hierarchical structure and slower promotions also push talent away.

What ISRO is doing to retain talent

The Department of Space is considering faster promotions, better allowances, and project-specific bonuses. A memo was sent to all centres to identify critical positions and offer incentives.

ISRO Chairman has urged scientists to stay for national pride, but the pay gap remains a challenge. The government is also looking at allowing ISRO scientists to work on private projects for extra income.

Meanwhile, the Gaganyaan mission, India's first human spaceflight, is at risk of delays if more senior scientists leave. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of resignations.

What happens next: The government is expected to announce a new pay package for space scientists within the next quarter. For now, ISRO continues to hire fresh graduates while trying to hold on to its experienced workforce.
