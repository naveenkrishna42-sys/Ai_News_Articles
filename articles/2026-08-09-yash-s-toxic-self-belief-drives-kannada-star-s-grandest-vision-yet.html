Yash's 'Toxic': Self-belief drives Kannada star's grandest vision yet

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Yash credits self-belief for taking on 'Toxic', his most ambitious project
  • The film marks his first pan-India release post 'KGF' stardom
  • Trailer launched with lead cast including Kiara Advani and Huma Qureshi

Bengaluru: For Yash, the road from a Kannada-language star to a nationwide phenomenon has been paved with one stubborn conviction: trust your own instinct. That self-belief, he says, is what drove him to greenlight 'Toxic: A Fairytale for Grown-Ups' — a project he calls his biggest ambition yet.

The film, directed by National Award-winning filmmaker Geetu Mohandas, marks Yash's first major release since the 'KGF' franchise turned him into a pan-India sensation. At the trailer launch in Mumbai, the actor made it clear that the film is not a safe sequel but a deliberate leap into uncharted territory.

'I had to back myself'

Speaking to reporters, Yash acknowledged the weight of expectations that follows a blockbuster like 'KGF'. But he insisted that fear was never part of the equation. "If I don't believe in myself, no one else will," he said, explaining why he chose a dark, stylised action drama over a more conventional mass entertainer.

The actor, known for his intense screen presence, said the script demanded a level of emotional and physical commitment he had not attempted before. "This is not just about action or style. It is about a story that stays with you," he added, without revealing plot specifics.

A fairy tale with sharp edges

The film's title — 'A Fairytale for Grown-Ups' — hints at a narrative that subverts the genre. While the trailer showcases high-octane fight sequences and moody cinematography, it also suggests a layered father-son conflict. Yash plays dual roles, a feuding father and his son, a choice that required him to switch between contrasting personalities.

Geetu Mohandas, known for her work in 'Moothon' and 'The Last Film Show', brings an arthouse sensibility to a big-budget spectacle. The combination has generated curiosity, though early reviews of the trailer have been mixed. Some critics have called the promotional strategy "ragebait" and questioned whether the film's style overpowers its substance.

Stardom versus story

The trailer's reception highlights a broader debate in Indian cinema: can a star's aura carry a film that challenges mainstream conventions? Yash seems unbothered by the chatter. His focus remains on the theatrical experience, which he believes will silence doubters.

The film also features Kiara Advani, whose all-white corset outfit at the trailer launch reportedly cost Rs 5.7 lakh, making headlines of its own. Huma Qureshi, another key cast member, said she felt "honoured" to be part of the project, citing its unconventional script and Mohandas's direction as reasons.

What to watch next

'Toxic' is slated for a worldwide theatrical release later this year. The film's box-office performance will be closely watched, not just for its numbers but for what it signals about the durability of star-driven, genre-bending cinema in India's evolving market.
