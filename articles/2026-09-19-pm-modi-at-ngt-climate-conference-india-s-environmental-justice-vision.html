PM Modi Inaugurates NGT Climate Conference: A Defining Moment for Environmental Justice

Prime Minister Narendra Modi inaugurated the National Green Tribunal's (NGT) international climate conference on Saturday, delivering a sharp critique of global climate inequality while championing India's rapid strides in renewable energy and sustainable development. The event, which brought together legal experts, environmentalists, and policymakers, served as a platform for India to assert its leadership in climate justice and to push back against what it perceives as unfair lecturing by developed nations.

Modi's address underscored a fundamental imbalance: developed countries, which have historically contributed the most to global carbon emissions, continue to lecture developing nations on environmental responsibility. He pointed out that the per capita carbon emissions of developed nations are six times higher than those of developing countries, a statistic that underscores the principle of common but differentiated responsibilities.

Climate Inequality: The Uncomfortable Truth

The Prime Minister's remarks highlighted a growing frustration among developing nations over the failure of wealthy countries to meet their climate finance commitments. While developed nations pledge billions, actual disbursements fall short, leaving countries like India to fund their own green transitions. Modi's assertion that "those who lecture on environment go to court to stop nuclear work" was a direct jibe at activists and opposition parties who oppose nuclear energy projects despite their low-carbon benefits.

India's stance is clear: it will not compromise its developmental aspirations for climate goals set by others. Instead, it is pursuing a path of sustainable growth that balances economic progress with environmental stewardship. The NGT conference thus became a stage to reiterate India's commitment to climate action, but on its own terms.

India's Renewable Energy Revolution

India's renewable energy sector has seen unprecedented growth. As of 2026, the country's installed renewable capacity has crossed 200 GW, with solar and wind leading the charge. The government's target of 500 GW by 2030 is ambitious but achievable, given the policy push and falling tariffs. Modi highlighted youth-led innovation and startups as key drivers of this transition.

Nuclear energy, often sidelined in global debates, remains a critical component of India's clean energy mix. The Prime Minister defended nuclear power as a safe, reliable, and clean source, criticising those who oppose it while claiming to champion the environment.

Supreme Court as a Banyan Tree of Environmental Justice

The Supreme Court of India has long been a torchbearer of environmental jurisprudence, evolving principles like the polluter pays principle, the precautionary principle, and public trust doctrine. Modi likened the apex court to a banyan tree—its roots deep in India's civilisational ethos of worshipping nature, and its branches extending to protect the environment for future generations.

This metaphor resonates with India's cultural heritage, where rivers, trees, and animals are revered. The judiciary's proactive stance has led to landmark judgments on air pollution, waste management, and forest conservation. The NGT, established in 2010, has further strengthened this framework by providing speedy environmental justice.

Real-World Implications for Businesses and Citizens

  • Policy Stability: India's renewable push offers long-term investment certainty for solar and wind projects.
  • Nuclear Energy Revival: Expect renewed momentum for nuclear power projects despite local opposition.
  • Climate Justice Advocacy: India will continue to demand climate finance and technology transfer from developed nations.
  • Judicial Activism: The Supreme Court and NGT will remain pivotal in enforcing environmental norms.
  • Youth Engagement: Startups and young innovators will drive clean tech solutions.

The Road Ahead: Balancing Growth and Sustainability

India's approach to climate change is pragmatic and rooted in equity. The Prime Minister's speech at the NGT conference signals that India will not be a passive recipient of global climate diktats but an active shaper of the narrative. With a strong judicial framework, ambitious renewable targets, and a focus on innovation, India is poised to lead by example.

However, challenges remain: financing gaps, land acquisition issues, and the need for grid modernisation. The government's focus on domestic manufacturing under the PLI scheme and green hydrogen missions aims to address some of these. As the world watches, India's model of sustainable development could offer a blueprint for other developing nations.

The NGT conference concluded with a call for collective action, but with a clear message: climate justice demands fairness, and India will continue to champion it.
