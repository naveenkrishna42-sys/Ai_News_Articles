Glasgow 2026 ends, Commonwealth Games flag handed to Ahmedabad

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games concluded with a formal handover ceremony.
  • The Commonwealth Games flag and baton were officially passed to Ahmedabad.
  • Jaismine Lamboria was named India's flag-bearer for the closing ceremony.
  • Ahmedabad's 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam' theme was showcased for the 2030 Games.

A Night of Farewell and Promise in Glasgow

The 2026 Commonwealth Games drew to a close in Glasgow on Sunday night, with a ceremony that blended Scottish tradition with a bold preview of India's return to the sporting stage. The official handover of the Commonwealth Games flag and baton to Ahmedabad, the host city for the 2030 edition, marked the emotional centrepiece of the evening.

Athletes from across the Commonwealth marched into the stadium one last time, their medals gleaming under the floodlights. The atmosphere was one of celebration, but also of anticipation — the baton now travels to a city that promises a distinctly Indian spectacle in four years' time.

Jaismine Lamboria Leads India's Contingent

India's presence at the closing ceremony was led by gold medallist boxer Jaismine Lamboria, who was named the country's flag-bearer. Her selection was a nod to the new generation of Indian athletes who delivered standout performances in Glasgow, and she carried the tricolour with visible pride.

The Indian contingent, which recorded its best-ever medal haul at a Commonwealth Games, received a warm reception from the crowd. For many athletes, the night was a final chance to soak in the atmosphere before attention shifts to the next cycle of competition.

The Baton Passes to Ahmedabad

The highlight of the ceremony was the formal transfer of the Commonwealth Games flag and baton. Neeraj Chopra and athletics legend PT Usha were seen receiving the flag, a symbolic moment that bridged two generations of Indian sport. The baton, now bound for Ahmedabad, will travel across India in the coming years as part of the traditional relay.

Ahmedabad's presentation for 2030 was built around the theme 'Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam' — the world is one family. The segment showcased Gujarat's rich cultural heritage, from traditional dance forms to architectural marvels like the Statue of Unity, offering a glimpse of what the world can expect in 2030.

A New Chapter for India

The handover is significant for India, which last hosted the Commonwealth Games in 2010 in New Delhi. Ahmedabad's selection as the 2030 host was confirmed after a competitive bidding process, and the city has already begun infrastructure preparations, including upgrades to the Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel Sports Enclave.

Organisers in Gujarat have promised a compact, athlete-friendly Games, with a focus on sustainability and legacy. The state government has been working closely with the Commonwealth Games Federation to ensure that the 2030 edition leaves a lasting impact on the region's sporting ecosystem.

What to Watch Next

With Glasgow now in the rearview mirror, the spotlight moves to the official launch of the Ahmedabad Games mascot and logo, expected later this year. The baton relay, which will traverse the length and breadth of India, is set to begin in early 2027, generating grassroots excitement ahead of the main event.
