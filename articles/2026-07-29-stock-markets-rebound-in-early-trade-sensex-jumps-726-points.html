Stock Markets Rebound in Early Trade; Sensex Jumps 726 Points

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex jumped 726 points in early trade on Wednesday.
  • Markets rebounded after recent losses, tracking global cues.
  • All sectoral indices were in the green, led by banking and IT stocks.

Markets Open Strong After Recent Slump

Indian stock markets staged a sharp recovery on Wednesday, with the BSE Sensex jumping 726 points in early trade. The rebound came after a period of selling pressure that had dragged indices lower in the previous sessions.

The 30-share benchmark was trading at 81,234 points shortly after the opening bell. The broader Nifty 50 also climbed over 200 points to breach the 24,700 level.

Investor sentiment improved tracking positive cues from global markets. Asian peers traded higher overnight, while US stock futures also pointed to a firm start.

Banking and IT Stocks Lead the Rally

All sectoral indices were trading in the green, with banking, IT, and metal stocks leading the charge. The Nifty Bank index surged nearly 2 percent, driven by gains in HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India.

IT stocks also saw strong buying interest, with Infosys and TCS rising over 1.5 percent each. Metal stocks joined the rally on hopes of improved demand from China.

Auto and pharma stocks also gained, adding to the broad-based buying momentum. Market breadth was positive, with more than two stocks advancing for every one that declined on the BSE.

Foreign and Domestic Buying Picks Up

Provisional data from Tuesday's session had shown that foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) turned net buyers after days of selling. Domestic institutional investors (DIIs) also remained net buyers, supporting the market.

Analysts said the rebound was partly technical, as the market found support at key levels. However, they cautioned that volatility could continue due to global economic uncertainties and corporate earnings announcements.

Global Cues and Oil Prices Support Sentiment

Global markets provided a tailwind, with Asian indices like Japan's Nikkei and Hong Kong's Hang Seng trading higher. US markets ended in positive territory overnight, with the Dow Jones gaining over 300 points.

Crude oil prices also eased slightly, providing relief to import-dependent economies like India. The benchmark Brent crude was trading around $82 per barrel, down from recent highs.

The rupee opened stronger against the US dollar, reflecting improved risk appetite. The currency was trading at 83.45 to the dollar, up 12 paise from the previous close.

What to Watch

Investors will now focus on the monthly F&O expiry later this week and corporate earnings from major companies. The market's ability to hold on to gains will depend on sustained buying from foreign investors and global trends.
