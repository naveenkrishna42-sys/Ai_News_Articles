World IVF Day 2026: Expert explains how to choose between IVF and IUI

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Dr. Manjunath C S busts 7 common IVF myths that may be delaying parenthood.
  • Experts highlight personalised care and advanced tech as key to overcoming recurrent IVF failures.
  • Despite a growing market, IVF continues to pose financial and emotional challenges for many couples.

Two paths, one goal

On World IVF Day 2026, the conversation around fertility treatments has shifted from confusion to clarity — but only for those who have access to the right information. For millions of couples trying to conceive, the first big question remains: should we try IUI or go straight to IVF?

Experts say the answer depends on factors like age, cause of infertility, and how long the couple has been trying. Intrauterine insemination (IUI) is less invasive and cheaper, while in-vitro fertilisation (IVF) offers higher success rates but comes with a heavier financial and emotional burden.

Dr. Manjunath C S, a senior fertility specialist, explains that many couples waste precious time and money on treatments that are not suited to their specific condition. He stresses that a thorough diagnostic workup before starting any treatment is non-negotiable.

The myth that stops couples from starting

One of the most persistent myths Dr. Manjunath encounters is that IVF is always the last resort. In reality, for some conditions like blocked fallopian tubes or severe male factor infertility, IVF may be the only viable option from the start. Delaying it in favour of repeated IUI cycles can cost couples years.

Another common misconception is that IVF always leads to multiple births or that the procedure itself causes cancer. Dr. Manjunath busts these myths with data, explaining that modern techniques allow for single embryo transfers and that large studies have found no direct link between IVF and cancer.

He also addresses the fear that IVF babies are less healthy. Thousands of healthy children born through IVF across India every year prove otherwise, he says.

When IVF fails — hope is not lost

Recurrent IVF failure is one of the most emotionally draining experiences for any couple. But experts speaking at a recent panel in Bengaluru emphasised that failure is not the end of the road. Personalised care and advances in technology are now offering new hope.

Pre-implantation genetic testing (PGT), endometrial receptivity assays, and time-lapse embryo imaging are helping clinicians identify why a particular embryo failed to implant. These tools allow for tailored protocols in the next cycle rather than a blind repeat of the same process.

Dr. Manjunath notes that many couples who had given up after three or four failed cycles have gone on to have successful pregnancies after a thorough investigation and a customised plan. The key, he says, is to not treat every patient with a cookie-cutter approach.

The growing market and the lingering challenge

India's fertility market is expanding rapidly, with more clinics, better technology, and increased awareness. Yet IVF remains a challenge for many — not because of a lack of options, but because of cost and access. A single IVF cycle can cost anywhere between Rs 1 lakh and Rs 2.5 lakh, and multiple cycles are often needed.

Insurance coverage for IVF is still patchy, and many couples end up exhausting their savings or taking on debt. The emotional toll is equally heavy, with anxiety and depression common among those undergoing treatment.

Experts argue that the real challenge is not medical but systemic — making fertility care affordable and accessible to the middle class and rural populations. Without that, the growing market will only serve those who can already afford it.

What happens next depends on policy changes and greater public awareness. As the field evolves, the message from doctors is clear: get the right diagnosis first, choose the right treatment second, and never lose hope — but don't wait too long either.
