RSS chief Bhagwat defends India's 'Vishwaguru' role in global unity

📅 2026-08-31 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • RSS chief Bhagwat emphasizes India's role as a 'Vishwaguru' in global unity
  • He draws criticism for RSS's 'exclusionary' image in the US
  • Bhagwat asserts India's legacy of service, not superpower status

RSS Chief Emphasizes Unity in Diversity

Mohan Bhagwat, the chief of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), recently emphasized India's role as a 'Vishwaguru' (world teacher) in a global context, asserting that Bharat's destiny is to make the world realize unity in diversity. This statement was made during an event in New York, where Bhagwat addressed a diverse audience, highlighting the need for harmony and understanding among different cultures and religions.

Asserting India's Legacy of Service

In Toronto, Bhagwat further emphasized India's legacy of service, stating that India has 'conquered none, converted nobody'. He argued that India's role in the world is not to pursue superpower status but to serve humanity through its rich cultural and spiritual heritage. This assertion has drawn both praise and criticism, with some viewing it as a noble stance and others seeing it as a form of cultural imperialism.

Criticism Over RSS's 'Exclusionary' Image

Bhagwat's emphasis on unity in diversity has drawn criticism in the US, where his remarks were seen as an attempt to counter the perception of the RSS as an 'exclusionary' organization. The Opposition in the US has criticized the RSS for its perceived exclusionary practices, arguing that it promotes a narrow, Hindu-centric view of the world. This criticism has raised questions about the RSS's ability to promote true unity in diversity in a global context.

Broader Implications and Expert Analysis

The statements by Bhagwat have broader implications for India's soft power and its role in the global community. As India continues to assert its global influence, it is crucial for the country to balance its cultural and religious diversity with its desire to promote a unified global community. The RSS's role in this process is significant, and its ability to navigate the complexities of global diplomacy will be closely watched.

Expert analysts have noted that Bhagwat's statements reflect a growing trend in India, where the country is increasingly seen as a model of cultural and religious harmony. However, the RSS's perceived 'exclusionary' image in the US poses a significant challenge to this narrative. As India continues to engage with the global community, it will be crucial for the country to address these concerns and promote a more inclusive and diverse vision of its global role.
