GRAP-3 Revoked: Delhi-NCR AQI at 204, Stages 1 & 2 Remain

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-17 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • GRAP Stage 3 restrictions, including non-essential construction and specific vehicle bans, have been revoked across Delhi-NCR.
  • The Air Quality Index (AQI) for Delhi has improved to 204, categorizing the air quality as 'Poor' but a significant step down from 'Very Poor' or 'Severe'.
  • GRAP Stage 1 and Stage 2 measures remain actively enforced, requiring continued adherence to basic pollution control norms.
  • While offering relief to some sectors, the CAQM emphasizes ongoing vigilance and adherence to traffic management directives to prevent further deterioration.

GRAP-3 Revocation Signals Cautious Improvement in Delhi-NCR Air Quality

NEW DELHI – The Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM) has announced the immediate revocation of Stage 3 of the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) across the Delhi-National Capital Region (NCR). This decision follows a sustained improvement in the region's air quality, with Delhi's average Air Quality Index (AQI) registering at 204, moving from the 'Very Poor' to the 'Poor' category. While offering partial relief, the CAQM has underscored that GRAP Stages 1 and 2 will remain in effect, emphasizing the need for continued vigilance against air pollution.

The lifting of GRAP-3 measures brings a significant easing for various sectors, particularly construction and demolition activities, which faced stringent restrictions. However, the underlying air quality remains a concern, necessitating adherence to the foundational pollution control protocols outlined in the initial GRAP stages.

Understanding the Implications of GRAP-3 Revocation

The revocation of GRAP Stage 3 signifies a crucial shift in the region's battle against severe air pollution. Under Stage 3, numerous activities, including specific construction projects, brick kilns, hot mix plants, and certain categories of industrial operations, were halted. Furthermore, restrictions on BS-III petrol and BS-IV diesel light motor vehicles were enforced.

The current AQI of 204, while in the 'Poor' category, represents a substantial improvement from the 'Severe' or 'Very Poor' levels that triggered GRAP-3. This improvement allows for a calibrated resumption of economic activities that were previously curtailed, providing much-needed relief to daily wage earners and businesses reliant on these sectors. The CAQM's decision is based on comprehensive meteorological forecasts and air quality models, aiming to balance environmental protection with socio-economic considerations.

What GRAP Stages Mean for Citizens and Businesses

Understanding the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP) is crucial for residents and businesses in Delhi-NCR. It's a set of emergency measures implemented incrementally based on the severity of air pollution.

Persistent Challenges: GRAP Stages 1 & 2 Remain Active

Despite the relief from GRAP-3, it is critical for residents and authorities to recognize that Stages 1 and 2 remain fully operational. This means that fundamental measures aimed at controlling pollution sources are still actively enforced. Stage 1 mandates a complete ban on firecrackers, strict adherence to dust control norms at construction sites, regular mechanical street sweeping, and a prohibition on garbage burning. These are year-round measures intensified during periods of poor air quality.

Stage 2, triggered by 'Very Poor' air quality, entails further restrictions. These include increased parking fees to discourage private vehicle use, intensified efforts to promote public transport, and a ban on the use of coal and firewood in hotels, restaurants, and open eateries. Furthermore, the use of diesel generator sets, unless for essential services, remains under strict regulation. The continued enforcement of these stages highlights that while the immediate crisis has eased, the air quality is far from optimal, demanding sustained collective action.

The Road Ahead: Sustained Efforts and Long-Term Solutions

The CAQM's directive to address severe traffic congestion, even as GRAP-3 is lifted, underscores a critical area of concern. Vehicular emissions remain a dominant contributor to Delhi-NCR's air pollution. While the region has seen a positive trend with no 'severe' AQI days for Delhi during November 1-15 – a first since 2015 – this progress is fragile.

Long-term solutions require a multi-faceted approach, including accelerating the transition to cleaner fuels, enhancing public transportation infrastructure, promoting electric vehicles, and stringent enforcement against industrial emissions and stubble burning in neighboring states. The revocation of GRAP-3 offers a temporary reprieve, but the overarching goal must be to achieve and maintain 'Good' or 'Satisfactory' air quality consistently, transforming emergency responses into sustainable environmental governance.
