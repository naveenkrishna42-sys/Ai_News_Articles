Google Pay Flex SBI Card: A Lifetime-Free Credit Card Built for UPI Users

The Google Pay Flex SBI Card has officially launched in India as a co-branded lifetime-free (LTF) credit card, jointly issued by Google Pay and State Bank of India. Unlike most entry-level credit cards that charge ₹500 to ₹1,000 as annual fees, the Flex Card carries a ₹0 joining fee and ₹0 annual fee for the entire lifetime of the cardholder. This positions it directly against popular no-fee rivals such as the HDFC MoneyBack+ and the SBI SimplyCLICK.

The card is targeted at users who already rely on Google Pay for UPI transactions, bill payments, and merchant spends. By extending a credit line into the Google Pay ecosystem, the partnership aims to convert habitual UPI users into revolving credit customers without forcing them to switch apps or merchants.

Reward Structure and Real-World Earning Potential

The headline benefit of the Google Pay Flex SBI Card is its 3% cashback on three high-frequency online categories: Google Play purchases (apps, in-app subscriptions, movies on Google Play), dining spends (including Swiggy, Zomato, and POS transactions at restaurants), and movie tickets booked through partner platforms. All other retail purchases, both online and offline, earn a flat 1% cashback.

For a user spending ₹40,000 per month, with ₹15,000 directed toward the 3% category and ₹25,000 toward general spends, the projected annual cashback works out to ₹8,400. Add the ₹500 welcome voucher, and the first-year value crosses ₹8,900 without paying any annual fee. That is a meaningfully better return profile than the SBI SimplyCLICK, which offers 5% on partner merchants but only 0.5% on other spends.

Fees, Surcharges, and Hidden Costs

The Flex Card avoids the typical credit card fine-print traps, but buyers should still examine three cost heads. First, the 3.5% foreign currency markup makes the card a poor choice for international travel; co-branded cards from Axis and HDFC offer 1% to 2% markups. Second, finance charges on revolving credit are steep at 3.5% per month (around 42% annually), so the card should be paid in full every month. Third, cash advance fees of 2.5% (minimum ₹500) apply from the day of withdrawal, so ATM withdrawals are best avoided.

Fuel users get a 1% surcharge waiver capped at ₹100 per cycle, which translates to effective savings on fuel spends up to ₹10,000 monthly. Users exceeding that threshold receive no incremental waiver.

Who Should Apply and Who Should Skip

  • Pros: Lifetime free with zero hidden annual fee clauses; high 3% cashback on Google Play, dining, and movies; seamless reward credit directly into the Google Pay wallet; ₹500 welcome voucher softens the cold-start earnings period; strong acceptance on RuPay/Visa networks depending on the variant issued.
  • Cons: 3.5% forex markup rules out international travel use; 1% base cashback trails competitors offering 1.5% to 2% on all spends; no lounge access or premium travel perks; minimum income requirement of ₹25,000 per month excludes a large segment of entry-level salaried users.

For users whose monthly credit card swipe pattern is dominated by food delivery, OTT subscriptions on Google Play, and weekend movie outings, the Google Pay Flex SBI Card delivers a tangible cashback lift over generic 1% reward cards. For frequent flyers or users seeking lounge benefits, premium cards remain the better fit.
