Sensex plunges over 400 points, Nifty below 24,150 as crude oil surge and Trump tariff hit pharma stocks

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex fell over 400 points, Nifty slipped below 24,150.
  • Crude oil prices surged, adding to inflation and fiscal concerns.
  • Pharma stocks came under heavy selling pressure after Trump announced new tariffs.
  • Investors turned risk-averse, with most sectoral indices ending in the red.

Indian equity benchmarks took a sharp knock on Wednesday, with the Sensex tumbling over 400 points and the Nifty sliding below the crucial 24,150 mark. A surge in global crude oil prices and a fresh tariff salvo from former US President Donald Trump triggered a broad-based selloff, particularly hammering pharmaceutical stocks.

The BSE Sensex closed at 79,842, down 423 points, while the NSE Nifty ended the session at 24,132, losing 134 points. Market breadth was decisively negative, with more than two stocks declining for every one that advanced on the BSE.

Crude surge jolts markets

Brent crude futures jumped over 3% to trade above $86 a barrel during Indian trading hours. The spike was driven by supply concerns after reports of production disruptions in key OPEC nations and renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

Rising crude oil prices are a double-edged sword for India. The country imports nearly 85% of its oil needs. A sustained surge widens the current account deficit, stokes imported inflation, and puts pressure on the rupee. Markets factored in the risk of the Reserve Bank of India holding interest rates higher for longer.

Trump tariff rattles pharma pack

The worst hit were pharmaceutical stocks. The Nifty Pharma index crashed over 3%, its biggest single-day fall in months. Major drugmakers like Sun Pharma, Dr Reddy's, Cipla, and Lupin lost between 2.5% and 5%.

The trigger was a fresh announcement from Donald Trump, who said he would impose a 25% tariff on select pharmaceutical imports if elected. While the proposal is not yet policy, markets abhor uncertainty. Indian pharma companies derive a significant chunk of their revenue from the US market, making them vulnerable to any protectionist move.

Broader market under pressure

The selling was not limited to pharma. Auto, metal, and realty stocks also declined, dragged down by the negative sentiment and fears of higher input costs. The BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices fell 1.2% and 1.5%, respectively, as retail investors trimmed positions.

Foreign portfolio investors were net sellers in the cash market, offloading shares worth over ₹1,200 crore, provisional data showed. Domestic institutional buyers provided some support but could not stem the tide.

Market participants are now watching crude oil prices and any further clarity on Trump's trade policies. The volatility index, India VIX, rose 8% to 14.6, indicating heightened anxiety among traders.

Technical analysts noted that the Nifty has immediate support at 24,000. A close below that level could trigger further profit-booking. On the upside, 24,350–24,400 is now a stiff resistance zone.

With global headwinds strengthening and an uncertain political backdrop in the US, Indian markets are likely to remain choppy in the near term. All eyes will be on the upcoming US Federal Reserve policy meeting and any fresh cues on oil supply dynamics.
