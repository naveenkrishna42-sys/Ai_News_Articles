Oil Prices Slide as US Signals Possible Iran Strait Deal

📅 2026-08-04 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil futures dropped following Bessent's comments on a potential Iran deal.
  • A deal could reopen the Strait of Hormuz with 'freedom of movement'.
  • Qatar signaled progress toward a short-term agreement between Washington and Tehran.

Oil futures tumbled on Tuesday after US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent suggested a deal with Iran to reopen the strategic Strait of Hormuz could be struck as early as Wednesday. Speaking to reporters, Bessent said the two sides might reach an agreement 'today or tomorrow' that would ensure 'freedom of movement' through the waterway, a critical chokepoint for global crude shipments.

The remarks injected fresh optimism into markets that have been on edge for weeks due to escalating tensions in the Middle East. Brent crude and West Texas Intermediate both declined sharply in early trading, building on losses from the previous session as traders weighed the prospect of de-escalation.

Hormuz in Focus

The Strait of Hormuz, situated between Iran and Oman, handles roughly a fifth of the world's oil consumption. Any disruption there has outsized effects on global energy prices, and recent clashes between US and Iranian forces had raised fears of a prolonged closure.

Bessent's comments came amid a flurry of diplomatic activity. Qatar, which has maintained channels with both Washington and Tehran, signaled progress toward a short-term arrangement. According to reports, Qatari mediators have been shuttling between the two sides to narrow differences on maritime security and sanctions relief.

Market Reaction

The oil market's response was immediate but cautious. Analysts noted that while a deal would ease supply concerns, implementation remains untested. 'Traders are pricing in the possibility, not the certainty,' said one energy strategist, pointing to lingering distrust between the parties.

Futures for September delivery fell by more than 3 percent at one point, before paring losses. The dip also dragged down energy stocks on major indices, though broader markets showed resilience.

Diplomatic Push

Diplomatic sources indicate that the proposed deal would focus narrowly on reopening the strait, with 'freedom of movement' guaranteed for commercial vessels. It is unclear whether the agreement would address other contentious issues, such as Iran's nuclear programme or its regional proxies.

Qatar's role has been pivotal, leveraging its ties with Tehran and its security relationship with Washington. The Gulf state has hosted several rounds of indirect talks, and its foreign ministry expressed optimism that a breakthrough was within reach.

What's Next

All eyes are now on Tehran and Washington as the self-imposed deadline approaches. If a deal materialises, oil prices could see further downside, but any collapse in talks would likely trigger a sharp rebound.

For now, the market watches the clock. Bessent's 'today or tomorrow' timeline gives little room for delay, and the coming hours will determine whether the strait stays open or becomes a flashpoint again.
