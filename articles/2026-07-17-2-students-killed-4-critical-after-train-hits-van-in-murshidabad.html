2 students killed, 4 critical after train hits van in Murshidabad

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two students died and four others are critical after a train hit their van at an unmanned crossing.
  • The accident occurred in West Bengal's Murshidabad district on Friday morning.
  • Bengal CM has promised 'strictest possible action' and ordered an investigation.

Deadly morning at unmanned crossing

A speeding train ploughed into a school van at an unmanned level crossing in West Bengal's Murshidabad district on Friday morning, killing two students and leaving four others critically injured. The incident took place near the Salar police station area around 7 am, when the van was crossing the tracks on its way to pick up children for school.

Local residents rushed to the spot after hearing the crash and pulled out the trapped children from the mangled vehicle. The injured were taken to a nearby hospital, where doctors said two of them were brought dead. Four others remain in critical condition and have been referred to a larger medical facility.

Details of the victims

Police have identified the deceased as Class 5 and Class 7 students of a local private school. The van driver is among the critically injured. Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of children inside the van at the time of the accident, but preliminary reports suggest around six to seven students were onboard.

The train, which was on its regular route, reportedly did not stop after the collision and continued onward. Railway authorities have launched a probe into whether the crossing had functional warning signals or gates at the time of the mishap.

Political response and probe

West Bengal Chief Minister, reacting to the tragedy, promised the 'strictest possible action' against those responsible. She announced an ex-gratia payment of Rs 2 lakh each to the families of the deceased and Rs 50,000 to the injured. The state government has ordered a magisterial inquiry into the incident.

Opposition leaders have blamed poor infrastructure at unmanned crossings for the recurring accidents. Local residents claimed the crossing has been a safety hazard for years, with frequent near-misses involving school vehicles. The railway ministry has yet to issue a detailed statement on the condition of the crossing.

What happens next

The district administration has sealed off the accident site for forensic examination. A special investigation team will submit its report within a week. The school has been closed for the day as a mark of mourning, and counseling sessions have been arranged for traumatized students.
