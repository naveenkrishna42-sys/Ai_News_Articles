Uber gets interim relief from Karnataka HC on Gig Workers Act

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Uber has filed a petition in the Karnataka High Court against the Gig Workers Act.
  • The court has granted interim protection, preventing immediate enforcement of the Act's provisions.
  • The case is part of a broader legal challenge by platform companies to state-level gig worker regulations.

Ride-hailing major Uber has approached the Karnataka High Court challenging the state's Gig Workers Act, and has secured interim protection against its enforcement. The move is the latest salvo in a widening legal tussle between platform companies and state governments over the rights and benefits of gig workers.

The petition, filed this week, argues that provisions of the Act overreach and conflict with existing central laws. While the court has not yet issued a final ruling, the interim protection means Uber cannot be compelled to comply with the Act's requirements until the case is disposed of.

What the Act mandates

The Karnataka Gig Workers Act, passed earlier this year, seeks to regulate the working conditions of app-based drivers, delivery partners, and other platform workers. It mandates social security contributions from aggregators, grievance redressal mechanisms, and transparency in algorithmic decisions that affect workers' pay and assignments.

The law is among the first of its kind in India, and has been closely watched by other states drafting similar legislation. Industry bodies have criticised it as compliance-heavy and legally ambiguous, while worker unions argue it does not go far enough.

Uber's legal challenge

In its petition, Uber is believed to have contended that the Act encroaches on the central government's exclusive jurisdiction over contracts and industrial disputes. The company has also raised concerns about the financial burden of the social security fund, which requires aggregators to contribute a percentage of their earnings.

The Karnataka High Court's interim order, while not a final verdict, provides immediate relief to Uber. The company can continue its operations in the state without the threat of penalties for non-compliance, at least until the next hearing. Officials from Uber have not yet commented on the development.

Context and implications

The case comes amid a broader push by state governments to bring platform workers under social security nets. Several states, including Rajasthan and Maharashtra, have either passed or are considering similar laws. The central government, too, has been working on a national framework for gig workers, but it remains a work in progress.

For Uber, the stakes are high. The company relies heavily on gig workers for its ride-hailing and food delivery businesses in Bengaluru, one of its largest markets in India. Any disruption to its operations could hit its bottom line.

For workers, the outcome of this case could set a precedent. If the court upholds the Act, other states may follow Karnataka's lead. If it strikes it down, it could stall similar efforts across the country.

What happens next

The Karnataka High Court is expected to hear the case in the coming weeks. Meanwhile, other ride-hailing and delivery companies are likely to watch closely, with some possibly filing their own petitions. The central government's draft gig worker policy, once finalised, could also influence the proceedings. For now, Uber has won breathing room, but the fight over gig worker rights is far from over.
