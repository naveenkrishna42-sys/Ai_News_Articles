Overview of Friday’s Market Sentiment

Indian equities enter Friday with a mixed backdrop. Global risk appetite has steadied after the US Federal Reserve hinted at a slower pace of rate hikes, while domestic data on inflation and fiscal deficit remain under the scanner. The consensus among floor traders points to a tentative range‑bound session, but technical triggers could spark volatility.

Sensex and Nifty: Technical and Macro Outlook

Technical Levels to Watch

The Sensex currently trades around 71,050, flirting with a short‑term resistance at 71,200. A break above this zone could open the path to 71,600, the 20‑day moving average. On the downside, the 70,800 level, supported by the 50‑day moving average, serves as a safety net.

The Nifty sits near 22,970, with 23,000 acting as a psychological ceiling. Support is anchored at 22,800, coinciding with the 200‑day moving average. Traders should monitor the 22,950–23,000 band for early signs of a breakout.

Macro Catalysts

Key domestic drivers include the RBI’s upcoming monetary policy meeting and the release of the latest GDP growth estimate, projected at 6.8% YoY. A dovish stance from the RBI could buoy equities, while any surprise in inflation data may trigger short‑term pull‑backs.

Internationally, the US non‑farm payrolls report on Thursday is expected to be modest, keeping the dollar index stable. A stable dollar reduces pressure on the rupee, indirectly supporting foreign institutional inflows.

Asian Benchmarks: Kospi, Taiwan, Nikkei

Regional Trends Impacting India

South Korean Kospi closed at 2,650, holding above its 20‑day average, signaling resilience in technology exports. Taiwan’s Weighted Index steadied at 15,420 after a brief correction, while Japan’s Nikkei 225 edged up to 38,150, buoyed by a weaker yen.

These Asian cues matter for India because export‑linked sectors—IT, pharmaceuticals, and auto components—track regional sentiment. A sustained rally in the Kospi and Nikkei often translates into higher foreign fund participation in Indian growth stocks.

Practical Implications for Traders and Investors

Retail investors should align position sizing with the identified support‑resistance zones. For those eyeing short‑term trades, a breakout above Sensex 71,200 or Nifty 23,000 could justify a bullish entry with a tight stop at the nearest support.

Long‑term investors may use the Asian benchmark trends as a macro filter. If Kospi and Nikkei maintain upward momentum, allocating a modest portion to high‑quality Indian export‑oriented equities could enhance portfolio diversification.

Key Real‑World Highlights

  • Sensex resistance at 71,200; Nifty ceiling at 23,000
  • RBI policy decision and GDP estimate are pivotal domestic catalysts
  • US payroll data likely to keep global risk sentiment stable
  • Positive Kospi and Nikkei trends support Indian export stocks
