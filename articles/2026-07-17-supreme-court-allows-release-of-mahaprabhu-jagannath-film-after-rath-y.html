Supreme Court Allows Release of 'Mahaprabhu Jagannath' Film After Rath Yatra

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court allows release of animated film 'Mahaprabhu Jagannath' after Puri Rath Yatra.
  • Court says animation cannot erode devotion, overturning Orissa High Court's ban.
  • Producers had moved the apex court against the High Court's order blocking the film's release.

The Supreme Court on Friday allowed the release of the animated film 'Mahaprabhu Jagannath', but with a condition: it can hit the screens only after the Puri Rath Yatra concludes. The decision came as a relief to the film's producers, who had challenged an Orissa High Court order that banned its release.

A bench headed by the Chief Justice observed that animation, as a medium, cannot erode devotion. The court's remark came during the hearing of a plea filed by the film's makers against the High Court's decision to block the movie over concerns that it might hurt religious sentiments.

Court's Reasoning on Animation and Devotion

The Supreme Court noted that the film is an animated feature and not a live-action portrayal. The bench stated that animation, by its very nature, does not undermine the sanctity of religious figures or practices. "Animation cannot erode devotion," the court observed, signalling that the medium should not be a ground for a blanket ban.

The court also took into account that the film is based on the life and times of Lord Jagannath, a deity deeply revered in Odisha and across India. The judges seemed to lean towards allowing creative expression, provided it does not disrupt public order or religious events.

Background of the Controversy

The dispute began when the Orissa High Court imposed a ban on the movie, citing potential law and order issues. Petitioners had argued that the film's release before the Rath Yatra could offend devotees and trigger unrest. The High Court had agreed with these concerns and stopped the release.

In response, the producers approached the Supreme Court, seeking an urgent hearing. They contended that the film had been cleared by the Central Board of Film Certification and that the ban was an overreach. The makers also emphasised that the film was a respectful tribute to Lord Jagannath, not a distortion of religious narratives.

What Happens Next

The Supreme Court's order allows the film to be released after the Rath Yatra, which is scheduled to take place in Puri. The producers are expected to announce a new release date shortly. The court has directed that all stakeholders be informed of the decision.

Observers believe this ruling could set a precedent for how courts balance religious sentiments with artistic freedom in India. The case also highlights the growing debate around the depiction of religious figures in animated films.

For now, devotees can look forward to watching the animated portrayal of Lord Jagannath after the grand chariot festival concludes. The film's release will be closely watched, both for its content and the legal implications it carries.
