Trump announces Hamas disarmament deal, Israeli support uncertain

📅 2026-07-31 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump announces agreement for Hamas's complete disarmament in Gaza
  • Hamas confirms the deal, calling it a step toward lasting peace
  • Israeli support for the plan remains uncertain, raising questions about implementation

Trump claims breakthrough in Gaza disarmament

US President Donald Trump has announced an agreement for the complete disarmament of Hamas in Gaza, calling it a "historic" deal and a "monumental step toward lasting peace." The announcement, made on Thursday, comes after what the White House described as intense negotiations involving regional stakeholders.

Hamas has confirmed the agreement, marking a rare public acknowledgment of a deal with the United States. However, no specifics have been released about the timeline, mechanism, or verification process for the disarmament.

Board of Peace agrees on terms

The deal was brokered under the auspices of the Board of Peace, a multilateral body that has been mediating between the parties. According to a joint statement, the board has agreed on a framework that would see all Hamas military wings dismantled and their arms handed over to a neutral authority.

Neither the White House nor the Board of Peace has detailed who will oversee the disarmament process, nor what sanctions would apply if Hamas fails to comply. Officials have not yet confirmed the role of international monitors, though sources suggest the United Nations may be involved in verification.

Israeli government's stance remains unclear

While Trump described the deal as a triumph, Israeli officials have not formally endorsed it. The Prime Minister's office has not issued a public statement, and analysts note that any plan requiring Hamas to retain any political role in Gaza could face stiff opposition in Israel's governing coalition.

Earlier reports indicated that Israeli military chiefs were briefed on the contours of the deal but stopped short of signing off. This uncertainty raises questions about whether the agreement can be implemented on the ground, given that Israel controls Gaza's borders and airspace.

Hamas confirms, but details remain vague

A Hamas spokesperson confirmed the agreement in a brief statement, calling it a "step toward ending the suffering of our people." The statement did not mention any conditions, such as a permanent ceasefire or the lifting of the blockade, which have been longstanding demands from the group.

This vagueness has led to skepticism among experts. Many point out that previous ceasefire agreements have collapsed due to lack of trust and enforcement mechanisms. The current deal, if it holds, would mark the first time Hamas has agreed to full disarmament since it took control of Gaza in 2007.

Meanwhile, Palestinian civil society groups have expressed cautious optimism, though they demand clarity on reconstruction efforts and the return of displaced families. Humanitarian agencies are waiting to see if the deal includes provisions for aid access and rebuilding of Gaza's devastated infrastructure.

What to watch next

Over the coming days, all eyes will be on Israeli Prime Minister's response and whether the Israeli cabinet formally backs the plan. Also crucial is the release of a detailed implementation roadmap, which the Board of Peace has promised to publish within a week. If Israel withholds support, the deal could unravel before it begins, leaving the region in a familiar state of limbo.
