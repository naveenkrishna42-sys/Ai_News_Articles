IDFC FIRST Bank Eliminates Forex Markup: What You Need to Know

IDFC FIRST Bank has announced zero forex markup on international card spends, a move that directly challenges the long-standing 2-3.5% fee levied by most Indian banks. For frequent travellers, this translates into significant savings on every swipe abroad. The offer applies to both debit and credit cards, but the devil lies in the details—eligibility, reward caps, and ancillary charges can make or break the deal.

Technical Specifications and Pricing Context

Below is a snapshot of the key metrics based on verified data from IDFC FIRST Bank's official disclosures and industry benchmarks. Note that while the markup is zero, other charges like ATM withdrawal fees and dynamic currency conversion may still apply.

Specification / MetricVerified Details
Forex Markup0% (down from 2-3.5% industry standard)
Reward Rate on International SpendsUp to 10X reward points (capped at 1,000 points per month)
Annual Fee (Credit Card)₹1,000 + GST (waived on spends above ₹2 lakh per year)
ATM Withdrawal Fee Abroad₹150 per transaction + applicable bank charges
EligibilitySalaried individuals with minimum income of ₹25,000 per month
Card NetworkVisa and Mastercard

These figures are current as of September 2026. The zero markup is not a promotional gimmick—it is a permanent feature, according to the bank's press release. However, the reward cap means high spenders may not maximise returns beyond a point.

Real-World Savings: A Buyer's Benchmark

Consider a typical international trip where you spend $2,000 (approximately ₹1.66 lakh at ₹83 per dollar). With a standard card charging 3% markup, you would pay an extra ₹4,980. With IDFC FIRST Bank's zero markup, that cost vanishes. Add the reward points—up to 1,000 points per month, worth roughly ₹250-₹500 depending on redemption—and the effective savings climb further. For a family of four travelling twice a year, the annual savings can exceed ₹20,000.

Yet, the card is not without caveats. ATM withdrawals abroad still attract a ₹150 fee plus the overseas bank's charges, which can erode savings if you rely heavily on cash. Also, the annual fee of ₹1,000 is waived only if you spend ₹2 lakh or more annually—a threshold that occasional travellers may not meet.

How It Stacks Up Against Competitors

IDFC FIRST Bank is not alone in this space. Niyo Global, Fi Money, and several travel-focused credit cards from HDFC and Axis also offer zero or low forex markups. Niyo, for instance, provides zero markup on debit cards but charges a joining fee. Fi Money offers zero markup with a subscription model. IDFC's advantage lies in its reward structure and the backing of a full-service bank, which means better customer support and integration with savings accounts.

However, competitors like the HDFC Infinia and Axis Atlas offer higher reward rates and lounge access, albeit with a 2% markup. For pure forex savings, IDFC FIRST Bank is a strong contender, but if you value perks like airport lounges and concierge services, you might need to weigh the trade-offs.

Pros and Cons: Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pros:
    • Zero forex markup saves 2-3.5% on every international transaction.
    • Up to 10X reward points on overseas spends, adding further value.
    • No annual fee if you spend ₹2 lakh or more per year.
    • Backed by a reputable Indian bank with a growing global acceptance network.
  • Cons:
    • ATM withdrawals abroad incur a ₹150 fee per transaction.
    • Reward points capped at 1,000 per month, limiting high spenders.
    • Annual fee of ₹1,000 applies if spend threshold not met.
    • No airport lounge access or travel insurance, unlike premium competitors.

Verdict: Who Should Get This Card?

If you are a frequent international traveller—whether for business or leisure—and spend at least ₹2 lakh annually on your card, the IDFC FIRST Bank zero forex markup card is a no-brainer. The savings on markup alone can run into thousands of rupees per trip. However, if you are an occasional traveller who rarely crosses the ₹2 lakh spend mark, the annual fee and limited perks may make it less attractive than a no-fee debit card from Niyo or Fi. As always, read the fine print and compare total costs, not just the headline markup.
