Kerala SRTC bus crash on Bengaluru-Mysuru Highway kills 2, injures 25

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Two people died and 25 were injured in the crash near the highway.
  • The bus belonged to Kerala SRTC and was en route when it hit a signboard.
  • Injured were shifted to hospitals; police have registered a case.

Bengaluru, Aug 8: Two persons were killed and 25 others injured when a Kerala State Road Transport Corporation (KSRTC) bus rammed into a large signboard on the Bengaluru-Mysuru Highway on Saturday morning. The accident took place near Bidadi, roughly 30 km from the city, around 9:30 am.

The bus, which was heading towards Mysuru from Bengaluru, veered off the left side of the road and struck the metal structure. The impact crumpled the front portion of the vehicle, trapping the driver and a front-row passenger. Both were declared dead at the scene by emergency responders.

Local police and fire services reached the spot within minutes. Twenty-five passengers, including the bus conductor, sustained injuries ranging from minor cuts to fractures. They were shifted to a government hospital in Ramanagara and a private facility in Bengaluru for treatment. Hospital sources said most were out of danger.

Cause under investigation

Preliminary inquiries suggest the driver may have lost control while trying to overtake a vehicle. However, officials have not yet confirmed whether brake failure, speeding, or driver fatigue played a role. The bus had left the KSRTC depot in Thiruvananthapuram the previous night and was on a long-distance route.

Police have registered a case under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code for causing death by negligence. The vehicle has been seized and sent for mechanical inspection. A detailed forensic team has been called in to examine the spot marks and the bus's black box data, if any.

Road safety concerns resurface

The Bengaluru-Mysuru Highway, a six-lane expressway, has seen multiple accidents since its upgrade. In the past year, at least 15 fatalities have been reported on this stretch, mostly due to high-speed collisions and improper lane changes. The signboard that was hit is one of several large gantries installed for route guidance.

Local residents and commuter groups have repeatedly demanded better signage design and crash barriers around such structures. A senior police officer said the department would review the placement of signboards and consider adding protective guardrails.

KSRTC has announced an ex gratia payment of Rs 5 lakh to the families of the deceased and Rs 1 lakh to the seriously injured. The corporation has also ordered a departmental inquiry into the incident and suspended the driver's licence pending investigation.

Passengers onboard told reporters that the bus was moving at high speed just before the crash. One injured passenger, who declined to be named, said, "I felt a sudden jerk, and then everything turned upside down. People were screaming for help." Authorities are yet to release the full list of victims.

Traffic and rescue operations

The accident caused a brief traffic snarl on the highway, with police diverting vehicles for about two hours. Cranes were used to clear the wreckage, and the road was restored to normal by afternoon. The injured are being monitored, and doctors say one passenger is in critical condition.

This is the second major bus accident on this corridor in three months. In May, a private bus had overturned near Ramanagara, injuring 12 people. The recurring incidents have prompted the transport department to consider stricter enforcement of speed limits and mandatory rest breaks for drivers on long routes.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the driver possessed a valid fitness certificate for the vehicle. The KSRTC regional manager is expected to issue a detailed statement later today.

As investigations continue, the focus now shifts to preventing such tragedies. The transport department is likely to hold a review meeting with highway authorities next week to discuss safety upgrades, including better signage and rumble strips at accident-prone zones.
