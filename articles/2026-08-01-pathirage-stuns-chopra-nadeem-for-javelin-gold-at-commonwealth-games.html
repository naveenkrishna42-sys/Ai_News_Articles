Pathirage stuns Chopra, Nadeem for javelin gold at Commonwealth Games

📅 2026-08-01 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rumesh Pathirage won the men's javelin gold at the Commonwealth Games, defeating Neeraj Chopra and Arshad Nadeem.
  • Neeraj Chopra claimed silver, while India's Yashvir Singh secured bronze.
  • Chopra had earlier qualified for the final ahead of Nadeem, setting up a high-profile showdown.

An upset for the ages in the javelin final

Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage produced the performance of his life to clinch the men's javelin gold at the Commonwealth Games, edging out two of the sport's biggest names. The 26-year-old, better known in his cricket-mad homeland for his love of the game, threw a season's best to finish atop the podium.

India's Neeraj Chopra, the reigning Olympic and world champion, had to settle for silver. Pakistan's Arshad Nadeem, the defending Commonwealth champion and Tokyo Olympics silver medallist, finished off the podium, a result few had predicted.

Chopra's silver, Yashvir's breakthrough bronze

Chopra's throw was enough for second place, but it was not the gold he had targeted. The Indian star had looked in ominous form during qualification, topping the standings ahead of Nadeem. But in the final, Pathirage's early rhythm proved decisive.

India's Yashvir Singh emerged as the surprise package, securing bronze. The young thrower later credited his own self-talk for keeping him calm under pressure, a mental approach that helped him deliver when it mattered most.

From cricket fan to champion

Pathirage's victory is a landmark for Sri Lankan athletics. Known more for producing world-class cricketers than javelin throwers, the island nation now has a Commonwealth champion in the sport. Reports suggest Pathirage's cricket obsession kept him relaxed before the final, a quirky detail that endeared him to fans.

The final was billed as a two-way duel between Chopra and Nadeem, given their recent rivalry. Instead, it was the unheralded Sri Lankan who stole the show, throwing with consistency that neither of the favourites could match on the day.

What this means for the rivalry

For Chopra, the silver adds to an already glittering career, but it also signals that the gap at the top is narrowing. Nadeem's failure to medal will raise questions about his form, especially after he was beaten in qualification by Chopra.

For Indian athletics, Yashvir's bronze offers hope of a deeper bench in the javelin, an event that has become a national obsession since Chopra's Olympic glory. The result also underscores the growing competitiveness of the discipline across South Asia.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact distances of the throws, but the outcome has already sparked celebrations in Colombo and muted disappointment in New Delhi and Lahore.

Looking ahead

All eyes now shift to the world championships next year, where Chopra will seek to reassert his dominance. Pathirage, meanwhile, will aim to prove this was no flash in the pan. The javelin rivalry in Asia just got a new, unexpected chapter.
