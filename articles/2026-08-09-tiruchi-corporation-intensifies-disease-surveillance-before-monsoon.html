Tiruchi Corporation intensifies disease surveillance before monsoon

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Enhanced monitoring for dengue, malaria, and other vector-borne diseases
  • Fogging and larvicide operations intensified in vulnerable areas
  • Public awareness drives on water storage and sanitation
  • Health workers trained for rapid response to suspected cases

Pre-monsoon preparedness

With the southwest monsoon expected to arrive in the coming weeks, the Tiruchi City Corporation has intensified its disease surveillance network across the city. The move is aimed at preventing outbreaks of vector-borne illnesses such as dengue, malaria, and chikungunya, which typically spike during the rainy season.

Corporation officials said the enhanced surveillance includes daily monitoring of fever cases reported at government and private hospitals, as well as door-to-door checks in high-risk neighbourhoods. The data is being compiled centrally to identify emerging clusters at the earliest.

Fogging and source reduction

Fogging operations have been stepped up in residential areas, markets, and near water bodies, where mosquito breeding is most common. In addition, field staff are conducting regular larvicide application in stagnant water collections, including drains, abandoned containers, and construction sites.

Residents have been urged to clear water from coolers, flower pots, and other household items that can become breeding grounds. The corporation has also begun distributing pamphlets and using public address systems in local markets to spread awareness about preventive measures.

Health worker deployment

Special teams comprising sanitary workers and health inspectors have been deployed across all wards. These teams are tasked with identifying potential breeding sites and reporting them for immediate action. Health workers have also been trained to recognise symptoms of vector-borne diseases and to guide residents to nearby health facilities for early testing and treatment.

The corporation's control room is functioning round the clock to receive complaints related to stagnant water or mosquito nuisance. Officials said that swift action is being taken on every complaint received.

Community involvement

Resident welfare associations and school students are being roped in to support the cleanliness drive. The corporation has requested that community groups organise weekly cleaning drives in their localities, particularly focusing on vacant plots and common areas.

Past outbreaks in the region have been linked to improper solid waste management and waterlogging during heavy rain. The current effort is designed to reduce such risks before the monsoon peaks.

Officials have not yet confirmed the exact number of cases reported this season, but they are closely tracking the trends. The corporation is also coordinating with the district health department to ensure adequate stock of testing kits and medicines at all primary health centres.

While the surveillance measures are being implemented citywide, special attention is being given to areas that experienced higher mosquito density in previous years. Regular review meetings are being held to assess the effectiveness of the ongoing operations.

Moving forward, the corporation plans to use the monsoon period to test its rapid response mechanisms, with an aim to bring down vector-borne disease incidence by at least 30% compared to last year.
