Chennai Police Unveil Strict Vinayaka Chaturthi Guidelines for Safer Festivities

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-09 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Only low‑decibel crackers allowed between 6 pm and 10 pm.
  • Heavy fines and seizure powers for violations.
  • Clear protocols for temple processions and public gatherings.

Overview of the New Guidelines

The Chennai Metropolitan Police issued a 12‑point directive on September 9, 2026 to curb firecracker‑related injuries and noise complaints during Vinayaka Chaturthi. The rules aim to protect residents while preserving core religious rituals.

Authorities consulted temple committees, fire‑safety experts, and resident welfare associations before finalising the list. Enforcement will begin on the first day of the festival and continue until the final immersion ceremony.

Key Restrictions and Allowances

Only crackers classified as "Category II" by the Bureau of Indian Standards are permitted. These produce noise levels below 75 dB and are deemed safe for urban settings.

All firecracker sales must cease after 5 pm on the day before the festival, and any remaining stock must be surrendered to the police.

Temple processions may use electronic sound systems but must keep volume under 80 dB. Organisers are required to submit crowd‑management plans at least 48 hours before the event.

Enforcement and Penalties

Police will deploy 25 mobile units across the city, each equipped with sound meters and seizure kits. Spot checks will focus on high‑density neighborhoods such as T. Nagar and Mylapore.

Violators face a tiered penalty structure: first‑time offenders receive a warning and a ₹5,000 fine; repeat offenses attract confiscation of all crackers and a fine up to ₹50,000. In cases of injury or property damage, criminal charges under the Explosives Act will be filed.

Practical Implications for Residents and Vendors

Homeowners must inform neighbours about the 6 pm–10 pm window and avoid loud music after 10 pm to stay within the noise curfew. Vendors need to register with the local police station and display the approved product list prominently.

Many residents appreciate the reduced noise, especially senior citizens and school‑going children. However, some traditionalists argue that the restrictions dilute the festive spirit.

Pros & Cons

  • Pros: Lower incidence of firecracker injuries; quieter evenings improve sleep quality for families.
  • Pros: Clear legal framework reduces arbitrary police action.
  • Cons: Limited cracker variety may disappoint long‑time celebrants.
  • Cons: Additional paperwork for vendors could raise operational costs.
