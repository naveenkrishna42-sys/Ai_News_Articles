US-Iran conflict: Air raid sirens in Bahrain; fire at Kuwait power, water plant after Iran strike

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Air raid sirens were heard across Bahrain following an Iranian strike.
  • A fire broke out at a Kuwaiti power and water desalination plant, injuring firefighters.
  • Iran's attack has damaged critical desalination infrastructure, exposing water vulnerabilities.

Air raid sirens in Bahrain after Iran strike

Air raid sirens blared across Bahrain on Saturday as the conflict between the US and Iran escalated. Residents reported hearing the wail of sirens in multiple locations, though no immediate confirmation of incoming projectiles has been made. The Bahraini government has not yet issued a statement on the nature of the alert.

The sirens came shortly after Iran launched a strike that hit targets in Kuwait. The attack has raised fears of a broader regional war, with Bahrain hosting the US Navy's Fifth Fleet.

Fire at Kuwait power and water desalination plant

In Kuwait, an Iranian strike triggered a fire at a major power and water desalination plant. Firefighters were injured while battling the blaze, though officials have not released the exact number of casualties. The facility is critical for supplying fresh water to the arid region.

The Kuwaiti oil firm reported that its facility was damaged and some personnel were wounded by what it described as 'repeated Iranian attacks'. The company did not specify the extent of the damage or the number of injured.

Water desalination plants hit as Hormuz battle intensifies

The strike on the desalination plant in Kuwait is part of a broader Iranian campaign targeting water infrastructure. A similar facility in Iran itself was also damaged as the battle for the Strait of Hormuz intensifies. The strait is a vital chokepoint for global oil shipments, and the fighting has disrupted supply chains.

Analysts say the attack exposes the water vulnerability of the dry Middle East. Desalination plants are lifelines for many Gulf nations, and any disruption can lead to severe shortages. The Iranian strike has now put this critical infrastructure in the crosshairs of the conflict.

Casualties and damage remain unconfirmed

Authorities in Kuwait and Bahrain have not yet confirmed the full scale of casualties or property damage. The injured firefighters are receiving medical treatment, but their conditions are unknown. The Kuwaiti government is expected to release a detailed assessment in the coming hours.

International agencies are monitoring the situation closely. The attack marks a significant escalation, as it directly targets civilian infrastructure in a third country. The US and its allies have condemned the strike, but no military response has been announced so far.

As the conflict unfolds, the region braces for further strikes. The battle for the Strait of Hormuz shows no signs of abating, and the potential for more attacks on water and power plants remains high. What happens next will depend on whether diplomatic channels can reopen or if the fighting spreads further.
