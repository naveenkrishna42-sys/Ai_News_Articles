Midcaps, smallcaps fall for second week but beat benchmarks

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Midcap and smallcap indices extended losses for a second consecutive week but outperformed benchmark indices.
  • Nifty and Sensex fell over 2.3% on a weekly basis due to global inflation fears and US trade tariff concerns.
  • Sensex faced resistance at 76,300 as market pressure persisted amid West Asia tensions and Gulf war ripples.

Broader markets show resilience despite weekly decline

Midcap and smallcap stocks continued their losing streak for a second straight week, but managed to outperform the benchmark Nifty and Sensex. The broader market indices fell less sharply than their large-cap counterparts, offering some relief to investors in these segments.

The Nifty and Sensex each dropped over 2.3% on a weekly basis, dragged down by renewed global inflation fears and lingering uncertainty over US trade tariffs. Market participants remained cautious as geopolitical tensions in West Asia added to the selling pressure.

Sensex struggles at key resistance level

The Sensex faced stiff resistance at the 76,300 level, failing to hold gains amid persistent selling by foreign institutional investors. Analysts noted that the index has been unable to sustain above this mark in recent sessions, indicating weak sentiment.

The Nifty also closed below the crucial 23,800 level, a threshold that traders have been watching closely. The breach came as reports of Gulf war ripples rattled global markets, prompting risk-off behaviour among domestic investors.

Global factors weigh on domestic sentiment

Global inflation fears remained the primary driver of the sell-off, with investors worried that central banks might keep interest rates higher for longer. The US trade tariff stance under the new administration added to the uncertainty, hitting export-oriented sectors.

West Asia tensions further clouded the outlook, with crude oil prices edging up on supply disruption fears. This raised concerns about India's import bill and fiscal deficit, putting additional pressure on the rupee and bond yields.

Midcaps and smallcaps show relative strength

Despite the overall decline, midcap and smallcap indices fell less than the benchmarks, continuing a trend seen in recent weeks. Analysts attribute this outperformance to domestic institutional buying and a preference for relatively cheaper valuations in these segments.

Sectorally, IT and banking stocks were among the worst hit, while some pockets of manufacturing and consumer goods held up better. Market breadth remained negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE and NSE.

Investors are now watching for cues from the upcoming US Federal Reserve meeting and domestic inflation data due later this week. Any surprise on the policy front could trigger a sharp move in either direction.

Technically, the Nifty has support near 23,500, while the Sensex could test 75,800 if selling pressure persists. A recovery above 76,300 is needed for the Sensex to regain momentum.
