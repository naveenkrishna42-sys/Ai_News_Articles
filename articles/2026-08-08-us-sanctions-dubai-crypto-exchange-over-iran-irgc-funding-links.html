US sanctions Dubai crypto exchange over Iran, IRGC funding links

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • US Treasury imposes sanctions on Dubai-based crypto exchange over alleged Iran sanctions evasion.
  • Action targets networks allegedly funneling funds to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).
  • Sanctions follow recent US strikes on Iranian targets and broader crackdown on shadow banking.
  • Dubai exchange and six entities, one individual placed on US blacklist.

Washington targets digital asset network

The United States has imposed sanctions on a Dubai-based cryptocurrency exchange, accusing it of helping Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) bypass international financial restrictions. The move, announced on Friday, adds to a widening US campaign against Tehran's use of digital currencies and shadow banking systems.

The US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) placed the exchange and several affiliated entities on its blacklist, alleging they formed part of a network that moved funds for the IRGC. According to officials, the exchange processed transactions that allowed the IRGC to access the global crypto market, circumventing US and international sanctions.

Six entities, one individual blacklisted

In total, the Treasury named six entities and one individual in the latest action. The designations freeze any US-held assets and bar American citizens and companies from doing business with them. The names of the designated parties have not been fully disclosed, but the Dubai exchange is the central target.

The sanctions come after a Reuters report detailed how the exchange allegedly facilitated trades linked to Iranian military units. US officials have not yet confirmed the full scope of the transactions or the volume of funds involved, but they described the network as a key financial conduit for the IRGC.

Fresh crackdown after Hormuz strikes

This action follows recent US military strikes on Iranian targets in the Strait of Hormuz, which have heightened tensions in the region. Washington has increasingly focused on Tehran's use of cryptocurrencies and informal money transfer systems to evade sanctions, which have crippled Iran's economy.

In recent months, the US has targeted several Iran-linked crypto exchanges and shadow banking networks, according to reports from Arab News and other outlets. The Treasury has argued that such digital asset platforms enable Iran to finance proxy groups and its missile program, including the IRGC's Quds Force.

Dubai's role under scrutiny

The choice of Dubai as the exchange's base highlights the emirate's growing role as a global crypto hub, but also its vulnerability to sanctions evasion. The UAE has often walked a tightrope between maintaining its reputation as a financial center and avoiding friction with Washington.

This is not the first time Dubai-based firms have been accused of links to Iranian networks. The latest sanctions are likely to put pressure on UAE regulators to tighten oversight of crypto firms operating in the emirate, though no official statement has been issued from Dubai authorities so far.

Wider implications for crypto industry

The action sends a clear signal to crypto exchanges worldwide that they must enforce robust compliance measures or face US penalties. Industry experts note that digital assets, once seen as a sanctions-proof haven, are now under intensified scrutiny from Washington.

The Treasury's move also underscores a broader strategy to disrupt Iran's financial lifelines, including through the use of the so-called shadow banking system. These networks often involve multiple intermediaries, making it harder for regulators to trace funds.

For India, which has significant trade ties with the UAE and a large diaspora in Dubai, the sanctions could have ripple effects on remittance flows and crypto transactions involving Indian nationals. The Reserve Bank of India has repeatedly warned against the risks of unregulated crypto exchanges.

Observers say the sanctions may deter other exchanges from operating in grey areas, but they may also push Iran to adopt even more opaque methods. The US Treasury has vowed to continue targeting such networks, but it has not disclosed whether further designations are planned.

Officials in Washington have said the investigation into the Dubai exchange is ongoing, and more details may emerge in the coming weeks.

Going forward, the focus will be on how Dubai responds to this scrutiny and whether other Gulf states follow suit in tightening their crypto regulations. The success of these sanctions will depend on international cooperation, especially with the UAE, which has yet to publicly comment on the matter.
