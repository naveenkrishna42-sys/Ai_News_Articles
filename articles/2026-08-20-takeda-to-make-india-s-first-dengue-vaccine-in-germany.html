Takeda to make India's first dengue vaccine in Germany

📅 2026-08-20 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Takeda's dengue vaccine is India's first approved vaccine for the disease.
  • Initial manufacturing will take place at Takeda's facility in Germany.
  • Vaccine is expected to be available in India by the end of the year.

Takeda's Dengue Vaccine Gets India Approval

Takeda Pharmaceutical Company Limited has announced that its dengue vaccine, Qdenga, has received approval from India's Central Drugs Standard Control Organization (CDSCO). This marks the first authorization of a dengue vaccine in India, a significant milestone in the country's fight against the mosquito-borne disease.

Manufacturing to Begin in Germany

The initial supply of the dengue vaccine will be manufactured at Takeda's facility in Germany. The company has not yet disclosed the exact quantity of the vaccine that will be produced in Germany, but officials have confirmed that the vaccine will be available in India by the end of the year.

Vaccine's Efficacy and Safety

The efficacy and safety of the Qdenga vaccine have been well-documented in clinical trials. The vaccine has shown to be effective in preventing dengue fever and severe dengue, with a high level of safety. The vaccine is administered in two doses, with the second dose given six months after the first.

Availability and Distribution

Once the vaccine becomes available in India, it will be distributed through the country's public health system. The government has not yet announced any specific plans for the distribution of the vaccine, but officials have expressed confidence in the vaccine's potential to significantly reduce the burden of dengue fever in India.

Future of Dengue Vaccination in India

The approval of Takeda's dengue vaccine in India opens up new avenues for dengue vaccination in the country. With the vaccine's proven efficacy and safety, it is expected to play a crucial role in the country's efforts to control and eliminate dengue fever. The successful launch of the vaccine in India could also pave the way for the approval and distribution of other dengue vaccines in the future.
