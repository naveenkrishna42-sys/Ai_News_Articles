  • Elections in wartime could 'destroy' Ukraine, says Zelensky

Zelensky warns of Ukraine's destruction if wartime elections held

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has strongly rejected the idea of holding elections in wartime, warning that it could lead to chaos and division. Speaking to the media, Zelensky stated that such elections would be 'like a tsunami' and could 'split Ukraine' into factions.

Elections in wartime could 'destroy' Ukraine, says Zelensky

Zelensky's remarks come as Ukraine continues to fight against Russian aggression. He emphasized that holding elections in the midst of a war would risk destroying the country's unity and stability. Zelensky has been a vocal advocate for Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity, and he has warned that any political maneuvering during wartime could have severe consequences.

Ousted defence minister plans US trip to raise project funding

Meanwhile, Ukraine's ousted defence minister, Oleksii Reznikov, is set to travel to the United States to raise funding for a new project. Reznikov, who was sacked from his position earlier this year, is expected to meet with potential investors and donors in the US to secure financial support for his initiatives.

Zelensky should be asked about government corruption, sacked minister tells BBC

A former Ukrainian minister has called on Zelensky to be questioned about government corruption. The minister, who was sacked from his position earlier this year, has urged the media to investigate and expose any wrongdoing within the government. This comes as Zelensky has faced criticism from various quarters for his handling of the country's affairs.
