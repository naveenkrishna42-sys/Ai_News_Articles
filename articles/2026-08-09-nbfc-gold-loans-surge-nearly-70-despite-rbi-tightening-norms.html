NBFC gold loans surge nearly 70% despite RBI tightening norms

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Gold loan portfolios of NBFCs are expanding at nearly 70% year-on-year.
  • Rising gold prices and quicker disbursal processes are attracting borrowers.
  • RBI has tightened loan-to-value ratios and monitoring, but demand remains strong.

Gold loans extended by non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) are growing at a scorching pace, with portfolios expanding by nearly 70% year-on-year. This surge comes even as the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has tightened norms around loan-to-value ratios and gold collateral valuation. The trend underscores a paradox: stricter rules have not cooled borrower appetite, but have instead pushed lenders to innovate within the framework.

The growth is driven largely by a sharp rally in gold prices, which has increased the borrowing capacity of households against their jewellery. For many small borrowers, especially in semi-urban and rural areas, gold loans remain the fastest route to credit — often disbursed within minutes, without lengthy income documentation. NBFCs, with their aggressive branch networks and digital onboarding, have capitalised on this demand.

Why NBFCs are outpacing banks

Banks have traditionally dominated the gold loan market, but NBFCs are now gaining ground. Their ability to offer higher loan-to-value ratios — up to 90% for certain borrowers — and quicker turnaround times gives them an edge. Public sector banks, hampered by procedural delays and stricter internal checks, have struggled to match this agility.

NBFCs are also leveraging technology to assess gold purity and value on the spot, reducing human discretion and fraud risk. This has allowed them to scale operations without proportionally increasing compliance costs. The result is a leaner, faster model that appeals to borrowers who need cash urgently for agricultural expenses, school fees, or medical emergencies.

RBI's tightening and the industry response

The RBI has, over the past year, raised concerns about aggressive lending practices and the risk of asset-liability mismatches. It has directed NBFCs to review their gold loan appraisal systems and ensure that auction processes for defaulted collateral are transparent. Some lenders have been asked to strengthen their internal audit mechanisms and board oversight.

Despite these measures, the growth trajectory remains robust. Industry analysts point out that the regulatory push has largely weeded out weaker players, leaving larger, well-capitalised NBFCs to capture more market share. These firms have responded by tightening their own credit appraisal standards, but without compromising on speed — a balance that has kept borrowers coming back.

Borrower dynamics and risks

The typical borrower is not a speculator but a household in need of short-term liquidity. Unlike unsecured personal loans, gold loans are backed by an asset that has appreciated significantly, reducing credit risk for lenders. However, a sudden correction in gold prices could expose lenders to losses if loan-to-value ratios are breached.

Regulators are watching this exposure closely. The RBI has already mandated that NBFCs mark-to-market their gold collateral daily and maintain a minimum buffer. Lenders have also been advised to avoid lending against hallmarked gold only, ensuring that even non-hallmarked jewellery is accurately valued.

What lies ahead

The coming quarters will test whether this growth is sustainable. If gold prices stabilise or decline, the pace of loan growth may moderate. Lenders may also face higher compliance costs as the RBI tightens supervision further. For now, the gold loan segment remains a bright spot for NBFCs, but the sector's resilience will depend on how well it navigates the delicate balance between growth and prudence.

As the festive season approaches, demand for gold loans is expected to remain firm. The key metric to watch is whether the RBI introduces any further curbs, and how quickly NBFCs adapt their models to stay within the regulatory perimeter.
