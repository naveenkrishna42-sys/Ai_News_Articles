Sensex, Nifty Close Higher on July 15 as Financials Offset Oil, Iran Jitters

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty erased early rally but closed with small gains on July 15.
  • Financial stocks, led by HDFC Bank, provided support amid oil price spike and Iran tension.
  • Geopolitical concerns and crude rise capped upside for broader market.

Indian benchmark indices ended Wednesday's session with modest gains, after surrendering a sharp early rally. The Sensex and Nifty both ticked up, as buying in financial stocks helped offset pressure from rising crude oil prices and escalating geopolitical tensions involving Iran.

Traders said early optimism faded quickly as global cues turned cautious. Oil prices climbed on supply fears linked to the Iran situation, which weighed on sentiment across emerging markets. Domestic investors, however, found some refuge in banking and financial counters.

Banking stocks lead the charge

Financials, especially HDFC Bank, were the main drivers behind the day's positive close. The Nifty Bank index posted gains, with HDFC Bank contributing significantly to the Nifty 50's advance. Analysts attributed the strength to expectations of steady loan growth and adequate capital buffers.

Other private lenders also saw buying interest. The sector's resilience stood out against a backdrop of global uncertainty, helping the broader market avoid a deeper slide.

Oil and Iran overhang

Crude oil prices firmed up during the session, reacting to fresh developments in the Middle East. Tensions around Iran have kept traders on edge, with any escalation threatening supply routes. For India, a major oil importer, higher crude prices raise concerns about inflation and the fiscal deficit.

Energy stocks, including Reliance Industries, faced selling pressure as the oil spike hit margins. The Nifty Energy index ended lower, reflecting the sector's sensitivity to crude movements.

Broader market mixed

The broader market showed a mixed picture. Mid-cap and small-cap indices traded with small changes, as investors remained selective. Auto and metal stocks saw profit booking, while IT shares managed modest gains in line with the headline indices.

Market breadth was slightly negative, with more stocks declining than advancing on the BSE. Analysts noted that volumes were lower than average, suggesting that institutional investors were cautious ahead of more global cues.

Foreign portfolio investors were net sellers in the cash market, while domestic institutions bought on dips, providing some support.

On the macro front, no major domestic triggers emerged during the day. Focus remains on the upcoming quarterly earnings season, with several companies set to report in the coming weeks.

Looking ahead, traders will watch global oil prices and any fresh news from the Middle East. The direction of foreign flows and the progress of the monsoon will also influence near-term sentiment. For now, the market appears to be consolidating, with the financial sector acting as a key anchor.
