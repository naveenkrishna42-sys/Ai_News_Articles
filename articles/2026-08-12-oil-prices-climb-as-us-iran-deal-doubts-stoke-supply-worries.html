Oil prices climb as US-Iran deal doubts stoke supply worries

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Oil prices rose amid doubts over a US-Iran nuclear deal.
  • Fresh ship attacks in the Red Sea and Gulf of Oman stoked supply worries.
  • Traders eye Strait of Hormuz reopening prospects with caution.
  • Volatility expected in crude futures as Middle East tensions persist.

Oil prices edged higher on Wednesday as fresh doubts emerged over a potential US-Iran nuclear deal, reigniting concerns about supply disruptions from the Middle East. Brent crude futures and West Texas Intermediate (WTI) both advanced in early trading, reflecting market jitters over the latest developments in the region.

The uptick came after reports of separate ship attacks in West Asia, including incidents near the Red Sea and the Gulf of Oman. These attacks have dented hopes for a swift reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of global oil consumption passes.

Deal doubts rattle traders

Market sentiment has swung sharply in recent weeks as negotiations between Washington and Tehran remain clouded by uncertainty. While diplomatic channels have not officially closed, traders are increasingly sceptical that a comprehensive agreement will materialise soon, given the complex geopolitical stakes involved.

Analysts note that even the prospect of a deal had previously pressured prices, as it raised expectations of increased Iranian crude exports. Now, with that scenario looking less likely, the market is recalibrating supply forecasts.

Attacks heighten supply fears

The reported attacks on vessels have added a fresh layer of risk to an already tense environment. Shipping companies have been forced to reroute or delay journeys, which could tighten near-term supply even if overall inventories remain comfortable.

According to reports from Al Jazeera and CNBC, the incidents have specifically raised concerns about the safety of transit routes leading to the Strait of Hormuz. Any prolonged disruption there would have immediate implications for global energy prices, given the sheer volume of crude and liquefied natural gas shipped through the waterway.

Volatility expected to persist

The Wall Street Journal noted that oil traders are bracing for continued volatility amid Middle East developments. Price swings have become routine, with futures reacting sharply to headlines about diplomatic progress or setbacks.

For Indian refiners and consumers, the trend is worrying. India imports over 80 per cent of its crude oil needs, and any sustained spike in global prices could stoke domestic inflation. The rupee's exchange rate against the dollar will also come under watch, as a weaker currency typically amplifies the cost of imports.

While the government has not yet commented on the latest price movement, officials are likely monitoring the situation closely. In past episodes, New Delhi has leaned on diversified sourcing from Russia, the US and West Africa to cushion the impact of Middle East disruptions.

What to watch next

Market participants will now focus on any official statements from Washington or Tehran about the status of negotiations. Also on the radar are weekly US inventory data and shipping advisories from maritime agencies operating in the region.

In the near term, expect oil prices to remain hostage to headline risks. A confirmed deal could trigger a sharp sell-off, while any escalation in attacks might push prices higher. For now, caution is the operative word.
