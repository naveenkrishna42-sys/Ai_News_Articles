Delhi Police submits details of facial recognition technology used during CJP protest to Supreme Court

The Delhi Police has submitted details of the facial recognition technology used during the recent protest against the Citizenship (Amendment) Act to the Supreme Court. The submission comes in response to a petition filed by a group of activists seeking information on the use of such technology.

Facial recognition technology used to identify and track protesters

The facial recognition technology was used to identify and track protesters during the protest. The technology was deployed by the Delhi Police in collaboration with a private company. The company's software was used to scan the faces of protesters and match them against a database of known faces.

Delhi Police claims the technology was used to ensure public safety and prevent violence

The Delhi Police has claimed that the facial recognition technology was used to ensure public safety and prevent violence during the protest. The police have stated that the technology was used to identify and detain individuals who were found to be involved in violent activities. The police have also stated that the technology was used to track the movement of protesters and prevent them from entering restricted areas.

Activists raise concerns over privacy and misuse of facial recognition technology

Activists have raised concerns over the privacy and misuse of facial recognition technology. The activists have stated that the technology can be used to track and monitor individuals without their consent. The activists have also stated that the technology can be used to target and harass individuals based on their political beliefs.

Supreme Court to hear petition on facial recognition technology

The Supreme Court is set to hear a petition on the use of facial recognition technology by the Delhi Police. The petition seeks information on the use of the technology and the privacy implications of such use. The Supreme Court is expected to issue a ruling on the matter in the coming weeks.
