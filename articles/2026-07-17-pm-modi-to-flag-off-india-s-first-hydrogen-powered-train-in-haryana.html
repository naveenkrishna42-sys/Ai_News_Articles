PM Modi to flag off India’s first hydrogen-powered train in Haryana

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Prime Minister Modi to launch India's first hydrogen-powered train in Haryana.
  • The train emits only water vapour, aiming to reduce carbon emissions on rail routes.
  • Indian Railways targets net-zero carbon emissions by 2030, with hydrogen trains as a key component.
  • The initiative aligns with the National Hydrogen Mission to boost green hydrogen production.

Historic launch in Haryana

Prime Minister Narendra Modi will flag off India's first hydrogen-powered train in Haryana on Thursday, officials said. The train, developed by Indian Railways, runs on hydrogen fuel cells and emits only water vapour. This marks a significant leap in the country's push for green mobility.

The event is scheduled to take place at a railway station in Haryana, with senior railway officials and state government representatives present. The train will initially operate on a designated route to demonstrate its viability before expansion to other corridors.

How hydrogen trains work

Hydrogen-powered trains use fuel cells that combine hydrogen and oxygen to generate electricity, with water vapour as the only byproduct. This eliminates harmful emissions like carbon dioxide and nitrogen oxides, which are common in diesel engines.

Indian Railways has been testing the train for several months. The system includes onboard hydrogen storage tanks and a fuel cell stack that powers the traction motors. The train is designed for non-electrified routes, offering a cleaner alternative to diesel locomotives.

Part of a larger green push

The launch is part of Indian Railways' ambitious plan to achieve net-zero carbon emissions by 2030. The organisation currently runs over 60,000 kilometres of track, with a significant portion still dependent on diesel traction. Hydrogen trains are seen as a key solution for these routes.

The government has allocated funds for developing hydrogen fuel infrastructure, including production, storage, and refuelling stations. The National Hydrogen Mission, launched in 2021, aims to make India a global hub for green hydrogen production.

Challenges ahead

Experts note that hydrogen trains face hurdles, including high production costs and limited refuelling infrastructure. Green hydrogen, produced using renewable energy, remains expensive compared to diesel. However, falling renewable energy prices and government subsidies are expected to bridge the gap over time.

Indian Railways is also exploring partnerships with private firms to scale up hydrogen technology. The initial rollout is modest, but officials say it paves the way for larger deployments.

What to watch

The success of this pilot will determine the pace of hydrogen train adoption across India. If the trial meets performance and cost targets, more routes could be converted in the next few years. The global rail industry is watching closely, as India's scale could drive down costs for hydrogen technology worldwide.
