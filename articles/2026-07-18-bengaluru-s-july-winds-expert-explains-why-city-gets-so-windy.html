Bengaluru's July Winds: Expert Explains Why City Gets So Windy

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru experiences strong winds in July, locally known as 'Gaali Maathu'.
  • A meteorological expert attributes the winds to seasonal low-pressure systems and the city's geography.
  • The wind pattern is a normal part of the southwest monsoon's influence on the Deccan Plateau.

Bengaluru residents are no strangers to the sudden, strong gusts that whip through the city every July. Locally, this windy spell is called 'Gaali Maathu' — literally 'windy air'. But while many blame the monsoon clouds or the city's hilly terrain, the real cause is a bit more specific.

Meteorologists point to a combination of seasonal low-pressure systems and the city's unique position on the Deccan Plateau. These factors create a wind tunnel effect, making July the windiest month for the Garden City.

What is 'Gaali Maathu' and Why Does It Happen?

The term 'Gaali Maathu' isn't a scientific one, but it accurately describes what residents feel. In July, the southwest monsoon is active over Karnataka. As moist air from the Arabian Sea moves inland, it meets the Western Ghats.

This interaction creates a pressure gradient — a difference in air pressure between the coast and the interior. The stronger the gradient, the faster the wind. Bengaluru, sitting at about 900 metres above sea level, is exposed to these winds with little obstruction.

Dr. M. Rajeevan, a former secretary of the Ministry of Earth Sciences, explained that such winds are a normal feature of the monsoon's progress. The strong winds in July are directly linked to the intensification of the monsoon trough over the Bay of Bengal and the subsequent low-pressure systems that travel westward across the peninsula, he noted.

Geography Plays a Key Role

Bengaluru's open terrain and lack of tall natural barriers amplify the effect. Unlike the coastal regions, which have the Western Ghats to slow winds down, the city lies on a flat plateau. This allows winds to gather speed.

The wind direction is also consistent — predominantly westerly to south-westerly. This steady flow, combined with daily heating, can lead to strong gusts in the afternoon and evening. Trees, hoardings, and even vehicles often bear the brunt of these gusts.

Residents in areas like Whitefield, Indiranagar, and Hebbal report the strongest winds. These localities have more open spaces or newer buildings that channel the wind.

Is It Dangerous or Just Uncomfortable?

For most, 'Gaali Maathu' is a seasonal annoyance. It dries out the air and can make temperatures feel cooler. But it also brings health risks — dust and pollen are blown around, aggravating allergies.

In rare cases, strong winds have uprooted trees or damaged weak structures. The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has issued advisories in the past, asking residents to secure loose objects and avoid parking under large trees during windy spells.

However, experts say there is no cause for alarm. The winds are a natural part of the monsoon cycle. They typically subside by late July or early August, when the monsoon trough shifts northward.

What to Watch For

Over the next week, Bengaluru can expect continued strong winds, especially in the afternoons. The India Meteorological Department (IMD) has predicted normal rainfall for the rest of July, which means the wind pattern will persist.

Residents are advised to stay updated on local weather alerts. While 'Gaali Maathu' is not a threat, it does make the commute and outdoor activities a bit more challenging. The winds should ease by the first week of August, as the monsoon settles into a steadier rhythm over the state.
