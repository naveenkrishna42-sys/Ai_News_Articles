France's historic double gold at Badminton Worlds

📅 2026-08-23 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • France wins men's singles and mixed doubles gold at Badminton World Championships
  • Thomas Gicquel becomes first Frenchman to win singles world title
  • Delphine Delrue and Ronan Labar win mixed doubles gold
  • Historic day for French badminton, setting new benchmarks

France's Historic Double Gold

France made history at the Badminton World Championships, winning both the men's singles and mixed doubles titles. Thomas Gicquel, a 28-year-old from Lille, became the first Frenchman to win the singles world title, defeating Japan's Kento Momota in a thrilling final. The mixed doubles gold went to Delphine Delrue and Ronan Labar, who triumphed over their Japanese opponents.

Key Moments and Statistics

Gicquel's victory was a testament to his relentless training and strategic gameplay. He overcame a slow start to secure a 21-17, 15-21, 21-19 win, showcasing his resilience and skill. Delrue and Labar, a pair with a combined experience of over 20 years, displayed exceptional teamwork and coordination, winning 21-15, 21-16.

Broader Impact and Analysis

This double gold victory is a significant milestone for French badminton. It not only adds to the country's rich sporting heritage but also sets new benchmarks for future generations. The success of Gicquel and Delrue-Labar pairs reflects the growing strength of French badminton on the global stage.

Expert Opinions

Badminton analyst and former player, Sanjay Singh, commented on the significance of the victory: "This is a historic moment for French badminton. Gicquel's victory is a testament to his hard work and dedication. The mixed doubles win by Delrue and Labar is a testament to their experience and teamwork."
