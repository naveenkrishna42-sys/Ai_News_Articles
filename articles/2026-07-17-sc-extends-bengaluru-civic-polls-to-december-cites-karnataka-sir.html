SC Extends Bengaluru Civic Polls to December, Cites Karnataka SIR

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court extends Bengaluru civic polls to December
  • Delay attributed to Karnataka State Industrial Report (SIR)
  • Election schedule remains uncertain until further notification

Supreme Court Orders Extension of Bengaluru Civic Polls

The Supreme Court on Friday extended the deadline for Bengaluru civic body elections to December, citing the pendency of the Karnataka State Industrial Report (SIR). The bench, in its interim order, directed the State Election Commission not to proceed with the polls until further notice.

The ruling came in response to a petition filed by the Karnataka government, which argued that the delimitation of wards could not be finalised without the SIR. The report, yet to be submitted, is crucial for determining the industrial zones that affect the electoral boundaries.

What Is the Karnataka SIR?

The Karnataka State Industrial Report is a comprehensive document that maps industrial areas across the state. Its completion is mandatory for redrawing ward boundaries in urban local bodies, including Bengaluru's Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP).

Government sources said the report is still under preparation and will take at least a few more months. The court accepted this submission, noting that elections without proper delimitation could lead to legal complications later.

Political Reactions and Voter Impact

Opposition parties criticised the delay, accusing the state government of using the SIR as a pretext to postpone elections. The Congress said the move was aimed at avoiding a potential defeat. The ruling Bharatiya Janata Party countered that the extension was legally sound and necessary for fair representation.

For Bengaluru's residents, the extension means the city will remain without an elected council for several more months. The BBMP has been under an administrator since the previous council's term ended. Civic services, including waste management and road maintenance, have faced criticism during this period.

What the Court Order Says

The Supreme Court bench, comprising Justices Surya Kant and Dipankar Datta, directed the State Election Commission to wait for the SIR before announcing any schedule. It also asked the government to expedite the report and submit a timeline within four weeks.

“Elections must be held, but not at the cost of proper delimitation. The SIR is a prerequisite,” the court observed. The matter will be listed for further hearing in October, with a final election date expected in December.

The court made it clear that no further extensions will be granted beyond December. It warned the state against any unnecessary delays.

What Happens Next

The Karnataka government now has until October to complete the SIR. Once submitted, the State Election Commission will draft a new electoral roll and announce the poll schedule. Voters in Bengaluru can expect elections to take place in the first half of December, barring any unforeseen legal challenges.
