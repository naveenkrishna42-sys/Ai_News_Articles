College Board expands SAT, PSAT access to students in 180 countries

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Education Original source ↗
Key points
  • College Board extends SAT and PSAT availability to students across 180 countries.
  • The initiative aims to democratize access to higher education opportunities globally.
  • New digital formats and flexible testing options are part of the expansion strategy.

Global reach of SAT and PSAT exams widens

The College Board, the organisation behind the SAT and PSAT exams, has announced a significant expansion of its testing programmes. Students in 180 countries will now have access to these standardised tests, which are widely used for college admissions in the United States and increasingly in other parts of the world.

This move is part of a broader effort to level the playing field for students from diverse backgrounds. By making the exams available in more locations, the College Board hopes to reduce barriers that have historically kept talented students from applying to universities abroad.

Digital transformation and flexible testing

A key component of this expansion is the shift to a digital format for the SAT. The new digital SAT is shorter and provides more time per question, which officials say should reduce test-day anxiety. PSAT exams will also move online, allowing for faster score reporting and more adaptive testing.

Students can now take the SAT on school days or at designated test centres, offering greater flexibility. The College Board has also introduced tools like free practice resources and personalised study plans through its partnership with Khan Academy, aiming to support students regardless of their economic background.

Impact on Indian students and education

For Indian students, this expansion is particularly relevant. With more test centres and digital access, the opportunity to study abroad could become more attainable. Many Indian applicants already take the SAT for US universities, and the increased availability may encourage more to consider international education.

Education experts in India note that the move aligns with the growing trend of globalisation in higher education. As universities worldwide seek diverse student bodies, standardised tests like the SAT serve as a common benchmark. However, critics argue that such tests can still favour students with access to coaching and resources.

What this means for future applicants

The College Board has not yet confirmed exact timelines for the full rollout in all 180 countries. But officials have indicated that the digital format will be phased in over the coming academic years. Students are advised to check local test centre availability and registration deadlines.

As the landscape of college admissions evolves, this expansion could reshape how students from India and other nations prepare for higher education. The next few years will show whether increased access translates into greater diversity on campuses worldwide.
