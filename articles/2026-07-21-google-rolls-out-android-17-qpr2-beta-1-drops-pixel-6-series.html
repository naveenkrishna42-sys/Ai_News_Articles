Google Rolls Out Android 17 QPR2 Beta 1, Drops Pixel 6 Series

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • Android 17 QPR2 Beta 1 is now available for Pixel devices
  • Pixel 6 and Pixel 6 Pro are no longer supported in this beta
  • The update fixes Bluetooth connectivity problems and Gemini app crashes
  • A new taskbar feature improves multitasking on foldable Pixel devices

Beta Release and Device Support Changes

Google has released Android 17 QPR2 Beta 1 for its Pixel lineup. The update, which arrived on Monday, brings the latest round of platform refinements ahead of a stable release expected later this year.

Notably, the Pixel 6 and Pixel 6 Pro have been dropped from the beta program. This move comes ahead of the official end-of-life status for these devices. Users on older Pixel hardware will no longer receive beta builds, though they will continue to get standard security patches for a limited period.

Bug Fixes Take Center Stage

This beta release focuses heavily on stability. Reports from early testers indicate that Google has addressed persistent Bluetooth connectivity issues that plagued earlier versions of Android 17. Pairing with headphones and car systems is reportedly more reliable now.

Another major fix targets the Gemini app. Users had reported frequent crashes when using the AI assistant on the previous beta. Google has rolled out an under-the-hood fix that appears to resolve these instability problems.

Taskbar Improvement for Foldables

Android 17 QPR2 Beta 1 introduces a refined taskbar experience for foldable devices like the Pixel Fold. The new trick allows users to quickly switch between recent apps without closing the current one.

Early hands-on impressions suggest this change solves a major frustration with multitasking on larger screens. The taskbar now intelligently adapts to the user's workflow, making it easier to drag and drop content between apps.

What to Expect Next

The beta is currently available for Pixel 7 and newer devices, including the Pixel Fold and Pixel Tablet. Google is expected to release a few more beta iterations before the final QPR2 update rolls out to all supported devices.

Developers and early adopters can sideload the update or join the Android Beta Program to get the build over the air. Users should expect some bugs as Google continues to polish the software.

With the Pixel 6 series now out of the beta loop, the focus is squarely on newer hardware. Google will likely announce the stable Android 17 QPR2 update in the coming months.
