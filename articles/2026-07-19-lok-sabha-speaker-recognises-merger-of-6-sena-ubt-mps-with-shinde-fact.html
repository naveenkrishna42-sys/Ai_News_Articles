Lok Sabha Speaker Recognises Merger of 6 Sena UBT MPs With Shinde Faction

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Six Shiv Sena (UBT) MPs merged with Shinde faction, recognised by Lok Sabha Speaker.
  • Decision is a major political blow to Uddhav Thackeray's leadership.
  • Speaker kept pending decision on TMC rebel faction MPs' merger request.

In a significant political development, Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla has formally approved the merger of six Shiv Sena (UBT) MPs with the Eknath Shinde-led Shiv Sena faction. The decision, announced on Saturday, deals a severe blow to former Maharashtra Chief Minister Uddhav Thackeray's standing in the Lok Sabha.

The six MPs who have switched allegiance include those who had earlier rebelled against the Thackeray leadership and sided with Shinde during the split in the party last year. Their merger has now been officially recognised under the anti-defection law, effectively reducing the strength of the Shiv Sena (UBT) in the lower house.

Speaker's Order Clears Path for Shinde Faction

Speaker Birla's order, issued after a detailed review of the merger petition, confirms that the six MPs have voluntarily merged with the Shinde faction. This move is seen as a validation of the Shinde camp's claim to being the original Shiv Sena.

The recognition of the merger strengthens the ruling NDA's numbers in the Lok Sabha. The six MPs will now sit with the Shinde faction, bolstering its parliamentary presence. Sources say the Speaker's decision was based on the representation made by the MPs and the faction's leadership.

Setback for Uddhav Thackeray

For Uddhav Thackeray, the development is a major political setback. It not only reduces his party's numerical strength in Parliament but also undermines his claim to be the rightful heir to the Shiv Sena legacy. The Thackeray camp had contested the merger, arguing that the six MPs had been suspended and thus could not legally merge.

However, the Speaker's ruling has gone against that argument. Political analysts note that this decision could have a cascading effect on the factional feud within the Shiv Sena, potentially encouraging more MPs to switch sides.

TMC Rebels Get Separate Seating

In a related but separate decision, the Speaker also allotted separate seating to the rebel faction of the Trinamool Congress (TMC) MPs in the Lok Sabha. However, unlike the Shiv Sena case, the Speaker kept the merger petition of the TMC rebels pending.

The TMC rebels, who have been at odds with the party leadership in West Bengal, had sought formal recognition of their faction. The Speaker's decision to only grant them separate seating, without approving their merger, indicates a cautious approach. It leaves the door open for further proceedings while avoiding an immediate escalation with the TMC high command.

What Lies Ahead

The Speaker's rulings are likely to trigger fresh political manoeuvring in the run-up to the next general election. The Shinde faction will leverage this recognition to consolidate its position, while the Thackeray camp may explore legal remedies.

Observers are now watching how the TMC leadership responds to the separate seating arrangement and whether the rebel MPs will push for a formal merger in the coming weeks.
