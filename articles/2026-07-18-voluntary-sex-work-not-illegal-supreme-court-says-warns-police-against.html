Voluntary sex work not illegal, Supreme Court says; warns police against harassment

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court declares voluntary sex work is not illegal under Indian law.
  • Police directed to stop harassing consenting adults involved in sex work.
  • Ruling emphasizes sex workers' rights and dignity, warns against moral policing.

Supreme Court clarifies law on sex work

The Supreme Court on Monday ruled that voluntary sex work is not illegal in India and directed police authorities across the country to stop harassing consenting adults engaged in the profession. The judgment came in response to a petition seeking protection for sex workers from police abuse and moral policing.

The bench stated that consensual sex between adults, including in the context of sex work, is not a criminal offence. The court emphasised that sex workers have the same rights as any other citizen under the Constitution.

Police told to stop harassment

The court issued clear instructions to law enforcement agencies. Police cannot raid brothels or harass sex workers based on assumptions or moral judgment. The ruling makes it clear that only those who force others into sex work or exploit minors can be prosecuted under existing laws.

The judgment noted that sex workers are often treated as criminals by the police, leading to arbitrary arrests and violence. The court warned that any officer found harassing consenting adults will face consequences.

Rights and dignity upheld

The Supreme Court underlined that sex workers have the right to live with dignity. They cannot be denied access to healthcare, housing, or legal aid simply because of their profession. The court also directed state governments to ensure that sex workers are not evicted from their homes or denied public services.

The bench observed that criminalising sex work only pushes the profession underground and makes workers more vulnerable to exploitation and violence. The court urged lawmakers to consider a more rights-based approach to sex work regulation.

What the ruling means

This judgment does not legalise brothels or pimping, which remain illegal under the Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act. However, it draws a clear line between voluntary sex work and trafficking or coercion. Consenting adults cannot be treated as criminals for engaging in sex work.

Lawyers representing sex worker groups welcomed the ruling, saying it will reduce police brutality and protect the basic rights of an estimated 15 lakh sex workers in India. The court has asked the central and state governments to frame guidelines within three months to ensure the judgment is implemented properly.

Activists have long argued that sex workers face constant harassment from police, who often demand bribes or threaten arrest. This ruling is expected to change that dynamic.

Watch for implementation

The real test now is how police forces and local authorities respond to the court's directive. The Supreme Court has made its position clear, but enforcement on the ground often lags behind judicial pronouncements. Civil society groups will be watching closely for any violations of the new order.
