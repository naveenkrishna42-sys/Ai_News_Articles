5 Temples in India to Visit for a Unique Holi Celebration

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Five temples offer distinct Holi experiences blending devotion with local culture.
  • Vrindavan's Banke Bihari Temple is famous for its week-long Phoolwali Holi.
  • Udaipur's Jagdish Temple features a royal procession and traditional rituals.

Holi, the festival of colours, is celebrated across India with unmatched fervour. While the streets erupt in a riot of gulal and water balloons, many temples offer a more traditional and spiritually immersive experience. For those looking to combine faith with festivity, these five temples present unique ways to witness Holi.

Banke Bihari Temple, Vrindavan

Vrindavan is synonymous with Holi. The Banke Bihari Temple draws thousands for its week-long celebrations. The highlight is Phoolwali Holi, where priests shower devotees with flower petals instead of powder. The temple resonates with bhajans and the air fills with fragrance. It is a sensory spectacle that stays with visitors long after the colours fade.

Dwarkadhish Temple, Dwarka

In Gujarat, the Dwarkadhish Temple celebrates Holi as a royal affair. The temple premises are decorated, and a grand procession carries the idol of Lord Krishna. Devotees sing traditional garba songs and offer sweets. The celebration here is less about chaos and more about community prayer. It offers a glimpse into the maritime heritage of the region.

Jagdish Temple, Udaipur

Udaipur's Jagdish Temple adds a royal touch to Holi. The temple, located near the City Palace, hosts a traditional puja followed by the burning of Holika. The evening sees a spectacular fireworks display. The Maharana of Udaipur presides over the event, making it a blend of devotion and regal pageantry. Visitors can witness age-old rituals that date back centuries.

Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple, Srirangam

In Tamil Nadu, the Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple celebrates Holi with a distinct southern flavour. The festival is observed as Panguni Uthiram, marking the divine wedding of Lord Ranganatha. Colours are used sparingly, with focus on temple car processions and classical music performances. It is a serene alternative to the boisterous celebrations up north.

Iskcon Temple, Delhi

The Iskcon Temple in Delhi holds a special Holi event that attracts families and tourists. The celebrations include chanting, dance performances, and a colour ceremony. The temple premises are kept clean, and the use of organic colours is encouraged. It is a controlled yet joyful environment, ideal for those who prefer a structured experience. The event often features cultural programmes in the evening.

Each of these temples offers a distinct lens through which to experience Holi. Whether it is the floral showers of Vrindavan or the royal procession of Udaipur, the festival takes on a deeper meaning. Travelers should check local schedules as dates may vary. The coming weeks will see these temples become hubs of devotion and colour, drawing pilgrims from across the country.
