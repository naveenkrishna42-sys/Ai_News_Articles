Cloned number plates on the rise: Hyderabad police on alert

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Rising incidents of cloned number plates in Hyderabad linked to criminal activities.
  • Police advise vehicle owners to verify documents and report suspicious vehicles.
  • Authorities plan increased use of technology and checkpoints to curb the menace.

Hyderabad is witnessing a worrying surge in the use of cloned number plates, with vehicles bearing duplicate registration details increasingly being linked to criminal activities. The practice, which involves affixing a stolen or replicated number plate onto a different vehicle, has become a preferred tool for those looking to evade law enforcement and commit offences with a cloak of anonymity.

According to police sources, the cloned plates are often used on two-wheelers and cars that are then involved in chain snatching, thefts, and even more serious crimes. The original owner of the number plate is left to deal with the fallout, including wrongful challans and police inquiries, while the actual offender remains unidentified.

How the racket operates

The modus operandi is deceptively simple. Criminals either note down a vehicle's number from the road or obtain it from a database, and then get a duplicate plate fabricated at an unauthorised workshop. In some cases, the cloning is done with a similar make and model of vehicle, making the disguise even more effective for traffic cameras and manual checks.

Officials point out that the lack of mandatory holograms or secure features on older plates makes duplication easy. Even the newer high-security registration plates, which come with a chromium-based hologram, are not entirely foolproof, as some fabricators have found ways to replicate them with varying degrees of accuracy.

Victims left in the lurch

For the law-abiding citizen, discovering that their number has been cloned can be a harrowing experience. One resident who wished to remain anonymous told reporters that he received a speeding challan from a city he had never visited, and it took weeks of running around to get it quashed. Such cases are becoming increasingly common, and the police have advised vehicle owners to keep a record of their vehicle's photographs and documents.

The Telangana transport department has also urged owners to verify their vehicle details periodically on the Vahan portal. Any discrepancy in the registration number, engine number, or chassis number should be immediately reported to the authorities, they said.

Police response and technology

The Hyderabad police have ramped up their efforts to tackle the issue. Special teams have been deployed at key checkpoints, and there is a growing reliance on automatic number plate recognition (ANPR) cameras installed across the city. These cameras can flag a vehicle if the plate does not match the registration database, though their effectiveness depends on the quality of the image and the angle of capture.

Sources indicate that the police are also cross-checking vehicles seized during crime investigations for cloned plates. In several recent arrests, the accused were found to be using plates that belonged to unsuspecting citizens, leading to a push for stricter verification at the time of vehicle purchase and during annual fitness tests.

What vehicle owners can do

Experts suggest that owners should avoid sharing their registration details casually and should be wary of parking in unmonitored areas where criminals might photograph their plates. Installing a tamper-proof screw set for the number plate is a simple but effective deterrent, they add.

If a vehicle owner receives a challan or a notice for an offence they did not commit, the police advise reporting it immediately with proof of the vehicle's location. A formal complaint can be lodged, and the matter is then investigated to determine if cloning has occurred.

The transport department has also indicated that it will soon mandate the use of high-security plates for all vehicles, including older ones, in a phased manner. This move is expected to make cloning significantly harder, though officials concede that technology alone cannot solve the problem.

Going forward, the focus will be on increasing public awareness and ensuring that fabricators of fake plates are prosecuted under the Motor Vehicles Act. The police have promised regular drives against such units, and citizens are encouraged to report any suspicious activity related to vehicle documentation.
