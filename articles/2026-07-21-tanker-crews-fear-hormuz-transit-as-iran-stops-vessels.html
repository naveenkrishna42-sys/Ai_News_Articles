Tanker Crews Fear Hormuz Transit as Iran Stops Vessels

📅 2026-07-21 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Iranian forces have stopped several tankers attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Reports of a 'massive fire' on tankers emerged as the US launched its 10th night of strikes against Iran.
  • The standoff threatens to choke a critical chokepoint for global crude oil shipments.

Hormuz Transit Becomes a Gamble

The Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway that carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, has turned into a high-risk zone. Iran has stopped multiple vessels attempting to pass through, according to reports emerging on Monday.

Tanker crews now fear the crossing. The Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) reported a 'massive fire' on tankers as the United States launched its 10th consecutive night of airstrikes against Iranian positions. The situation on the water remains fluid.

Iran's Warnings Turn into Action

Tehran had issued warnings to shipping companies in recent days. Now, it appears to be enforcing them. Iranian authorities said two ships were involved in an 'accident' after they defied warnings to stay clear of the strait.

The details of that incident remain murky. Independent verification is difficult, as communication with vessels in the area has been patchy. The Deccan Herald reported that Iran claims the ships defied explicit orders.

US Strikes Fail to Break the Standoff

The American military campaign, now in its tenth day, has not achieved its stated goal of keeping the strait open. Washington has bombed coastal defense sites and radar installations, but Iranian patrol boats and fast-attack craft continue to operate.

India Today noted that US strikes have failed to break the standoff. Iran's ability to harass shipping appears undiminished. Each night of bombing is followed by another day of confrontation in the water.

Global Oil Markets on Edge

The disruption is already being felt in oil markets. Traders are pricing in a risk premium that shows no signs of easing. A prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz could send crude prices soaring past levels not seen in years.

India, which imports a significant portion of its crude from the Gulf, is watching closely. New Delhi has not issued a formal statement on the tanker seizures, but officials are believed to be in touch with multiple governments to secure safe passage for Indian-flagged vessels.

The coming hours will be critical. If Iran continues to stop tankers, the US may be forced to escalate its naval response, pushing the conflict into a direct maritime confrontation. For now, the strait remains a dangerous passage no ship wants to attempt.
