Trump signals Hormuz deal soon as Iran-Oman diplomacy intensifies

📅 2026-08-05 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump hints at a possible agreement on the Strait of Hormuz in the near future.
  • Iran and Oman are entering a new phase of diplomatic engagement.
  • Houthis claim a missile strike on a Saudi oil tanker off Yanbu, escalating regional tensions.
  • Oil prices fall as markets react to potential diplomatic progress.

Diplomatic Signals on Hormuz

Washington and Tehran appear to be inching toward a deal over the Strait of Hormuz, with US President Donald Trump signalling that an agreement could be reached soon. Speaking to reporters, Trump underscored the urgency, warning that Iran would be 'hit very hard' if the crucial waterway was not reopened promptly. His remarks came as oil prices slipped, reflecting cautious optimism in global markets.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which nearly a fifth of the world's oil passes, has been a flashpoint in the ongoing West Asia conflict. Any disruption to shipping lanes there carries immediate consequences for energy prices and global supply chains. Trump's latest comments suggest a shift toward negotiation, though he stopped short of outlining specific terms.

Iran-Oman Engagement Deepens

Meanwhile, Iran and Oman have entered a new phase of diplomatic engagement, according to reports. The two nations have historically maintained channels of communication, even during periods of heightened tension. This renewed interaction is being viewed as a potential conduit for broader talks, with Oman often playing the role of mediator in regional disputes.

Details of the discussions remain sparse, but sources indicate that Iran has put forward specific demands. These include inbound control of the strait and outbound oversight, a proposal that could reshape how the waterway is managed. Whether these terms are acceptable to the United States and its allies is unclear, but the very fact of dialogue marks a departure from recent hostilities.

Houthi Strike Claims Add Complexity

Adding a layer of complexity, Yemen's Houthi rebels have claimed a missile strike on a Saudi oil tanker off the port of Yanbu. The attack, if confirmed, would represent a significant escalation, targeting Saudi infrastructure amid the broader conflict. The Houthis, who have been engaged in a separate war with Saudi Arabia, have previously launched projectiles at vessels in the Red Sea and the Arabian Sea.

There has been no immediate confirmation from Saudi authorities or the tanker's operator. The incident underscores the fragility of maritime security in the region, even as diplomatic efforts gather pace. It also raises questions about the Houthis' role in any potential settlement, as they are not party to the US-Iran talks.

Market Reactions and Oil Prices

The prospect of a deal has already had an impact on oil markets. Prices have fallen as traders factor in the possibility of a reopening of the strait and a resumption of normal flows. However, analysts caution that the situation remains fluid, with any breakdown in talks likely to trigger a sharp reversal.

For India, a major importer of crude from the region, the stakes are high. A stable Hormuz would ease supply concerns and help contain inflation, which has been a persistent challenge. The government has been closely monitoring the situation, though it has not issued any public statement on the latest developments.

What Lies Ahead

As negotiations continue, the coming days will be critical. The Trump administration has not set a public deadline, but the rhetoric suggests a sense of urgency. Iran, for its part, appears willing to engage, though its demands for control and oversight could prove contentious.

Observers will be watching for any concrete framework that emerges from the Iran-Oman channel, as well as for reactions from Gulf states and other stakeholders. The Houthi strike, if verified, could complicate matters, potentially derailing progress. For now, the world waits to see whether diplomacy can prevail over brinkmanship in the waters of the Gulf.
