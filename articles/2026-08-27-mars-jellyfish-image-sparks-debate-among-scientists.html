Mars 'jellyfish' image sparks debate among scientists

📅 2026-08-27 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Curiosity rover captures unusual rock formation resembling a jellyfish
  • Scientists debate origin of the formation, suggesting it could be a volcanic or sedimentary feature
  • The image has sparked global interest and discussions among space enthusiasts

Curiosity Rover Captures Unusual Rock Formation

The Curiosity rover, which has been exploring Mars since 2012, recently captured an image of a rock formation that resembles a jellyfish. The image, which was shared on social media, has sparked global interest and discussions among space enthusiasts and scientists alike.

Scientists Debate Origin of the Formation

Scientists are divided on the origin of the formation. Some believe it could be a volcanic feature, formed by the cooling and solidification of lava. Others suggest it could be a sedimentary feature, formed by the deposition and cementation of sediments.

Dr. Anil Kumar, a planetary geologist at the Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore, believes the formation is likely a volcanic feature. "The shape and structure of the formation suggest it was formed by the cooling and solidification of lava," he says. "This is not uncommon on Mars, where volcanic activity has been prevalent in the past."

Broader Implications of the Discovery

The discovery of this unusual rock formation has significant implications for our understanding of Mars' geological history. It provides insight into the planet's volcanic activity and the processes that shaped its surface.

"This discovery is a reminder of how much we still have to learn about Mars," says Dr. Priya Patel, a planetary scientist at the Physical Research Laboratory, Ahmedabad. "Every new discovery brings us one step closer to understanding the planet's history and potential for past or present life."

Key Facts and Speculations

  • The jellyfish-shaped rock formation was captured by the Curiosity rover's Mast Camera (Mastcam) on August 24, 2026.
  • The formation is located in the Gale Crater, a large impact crater that is home to the Curiosity rover.
  • Scientists believe the formation is about 2 meters tall and 1 meter wide.
  • The image has been compared to other jellyfish-shaped formations found on Earth, such as the Giant's Causeway in Northern Ireland.
