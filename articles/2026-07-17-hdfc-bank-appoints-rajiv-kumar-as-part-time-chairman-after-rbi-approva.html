HDFC Bank appoints Rajiv Kumar as part-time chairman after RBI approval

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI has approved Rajiv Kumar as part-time chairman of HDFC Bank.
  • Kumar succeeds Atanu Chakraborty, who completed his term.
  • The appointment is effective immediately, pending shareholder approval.

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has given its nod to HDFC Bank for the appointment of Rajiv Kumar as the part-time chairman of the board. The private sector lender announced the development on Wednesday, confirming that the regulator's approval came through on July 14, 2026.

Kumar will succeed Atanu Chakraborty, whose term as part-time chairman ended recently. The appointment is effective immediately, though it remains subject to approval from the bank's shareholders at the next annual general meeting.

Who is Rajiv Kumar?

Rajiv Kumar is not a newcomer to the banking and financial services space. He has previously served as the chairman of the State Bank of India, the country's largest lender, from 2017 to 2020. Under his leadership, SBI saw significant consolidation and digital transformation.

Kumar also has experience in the public sector, having served as secretary in the Department of Financial Services, Ministry of Finance. His career spans over three decades, with stints at various government and regulatory bodies. He is widely regarded for his deep understanding of banking regulations and governance.

Transition at the top

Atanu Chakraborty, who stepped down as part-time chairman, had been at the helm since 2022. He oversaw HDFC Bank's integration with its parent HDFC Ltd after the merger in 2023. During his tenure, the bank navigated regulatory changes and maintained strong financial performance.

HDFC Bank's board had recommended Kumar's name to the RBI in May this year. The bank stated that Kumar's experience in both public and private sectors would be invaluable as the lender continues to expand its retail and corporate banking operations.

Regulatory context

The RBI's approval comes at a time when the regulator has been tightening norms around bank board appointments. In recent years, the central bank has sought to ensure that independent directors and chairpersons have no conflict of interest and possess the requisite expertise.

For HDFC Bank, the appointment is part of a broader succession plan. The bank had been looking for a candidate who could provide stability and strategic direction. Kumar's appointment is seen as a move that aligns with the RBI's expectations for experienced leadership in the banking sector.

What lies ahead

Kumar will now work closely with HDFC Bank's managing director and CEO, Sashidhar Jagdishan, to steer the bank's growth strategy. The bank has been focusing on expanding its branch network, increasing digital adoption, and improving loan growth in a competitive market.

Shareholders will vote on the appointment at the next AGM, which is expected to be held in August 2026. If approved, Kumar's term as part-time chairman will be for three years, in line with RBI guidelines for bank chairpersons.

HDFC Bank's stock closed marginally higher on Wednesday following the announcement. Analysts view the appointment as a positive step, given Kumar's track record in managing large financial institutions.

The bank will now need to secure the necessary shareholder mandate and then focus on executing its business plans amid evolving regulatory and economic conditions.
