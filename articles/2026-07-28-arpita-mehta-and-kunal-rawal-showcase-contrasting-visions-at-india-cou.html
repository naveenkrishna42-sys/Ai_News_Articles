Arpita Mehta and Kunal Rawal Showcase Contrasting Visions at India Couture Week 2026

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Arpita Mehta and Kunal Rawal, a married couple in the fashion industry, showcased separate collections at ICW 2026.
  • Mehta's collection focused on Indian textiles and feminine silhouettes, while Rawal's line emphasized modern, tailored menswear.
  • The event also featured other notable designers, including Rimzim Dadu and Falguni Shane Peacock.

Mumbai, July 28: India Couture Week 2026 has been a stage for bold statements, but perhaps the most intriguing dynamic this season is the creative divergence of fashion power couple Arpita Mehta and Kunal Rawal. While they may be husband and wife in their personal lives, their latest collections tell two very different stories.

Arpita Mehta: A Celebration of Indian Textiles

Mehta's showcase was a deep dive into the richness of Indian craftsmanship. She presented a line that married traditional textiles with contemporary silhouettes, a signature move that has made her a favourite among bridal shoppers.

The collection featured intricate hand-embroidery on lightweight fabrics like organza and silk. Models walked the runway in lehengas and anarkalis that shimmered under the lights, but the designs felt breathable rather than heavy. Mehta focused on earthy tones punctuated by pops of jewel colours — emerald, ruby, and sapphire.

“It’s about making the woman feel powerful yet comfortable,” a description from the show notes suggested. The garments carried a distinct feminine energy, with flowing dupattas and delicate gota patti work that caught the eye without overwhelming the drape.

Kunal Rawal: Modern Silhouettes for the New Age Groom

Across the venue, Kunal Rawal presented a starkly different vision. Known for his sharp tailoring and modern menswear, Rawal’s collection leaned into structured, minimalist forms. He reimagined the classic sherwani with asymmetric cuts and sleek, unlined jackets.

Rawal used a muted palette of charcoal, ivory, and dusty rose. The detailing was subtle — hidden pockets, contrast stitching, and hand-finished buttonholes. His models walked with a quiet confidence, reflecting a shift in how Indian men approach wedding wear.

“The groom today wants to look polished, not fussy,” the show indicated. Rawal’s designs appealed to the man who values fit and fabric over heavy embellishment. The collection included linen bandhgalas and tailored waistcoats that could easily transition from the wedding mandap to the reception.

A Week of Contrasts and Craftsmanship

India Couture Week 2026 has been a festival of contrasts. While Mehta and Rawal offered their distinct takes, other designers also made their mark. Rimzim Dadu turned mother-of-pearl into sculptural couture, a feat of engineering as much as design. Her pieces looked like armour made of light, with shimmering sequins and metallic threads woven into structured gowns.

On the opening night, Falguni Shane Peacock returned to the runway with Le Grand Voyage. The duo, known for their theatrical presentations, sent models down in opulent, jewel-encrusted ensembles that referenced global travel motifs. It was a return to maximalism after a season of understated looks.

Another standout was the label ROSEROOM, which debuted a collection titled ‘Softly, Strong’. The brand redefined feminine fashion with sharp tailoring softened by sheer fabrics and delicate lace. It was a commentary on duality — strength and vulnerability existing in the same garment.

As the week continues, the spotlight remains on how Indian couturiers are balancing tradition with innovation. For Mehta and Rawal, their contrasting visions serve as a reminder that in fashion — as in marriage — harmony doesn’t mean sameness.

What to watch: The next few days will see emerging designers present their interpretations of Indian couture, with buyers and stylists closely watching for trends that will define the upcoming wedding season.
