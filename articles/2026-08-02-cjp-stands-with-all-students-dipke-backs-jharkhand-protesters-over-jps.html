CJP stands with all students: Dipke backs Jharkhand protesters over JPSC, JSSC exam row

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Abhijeet Dipke of the CJP has extended support to students protesting in Ranchi over alleged JPSC and JSSC exam irregularities.
  • Protesters are demanding a CBI investigation into the examination process, which they say has eroded trust.
  • The protests have drawn comparisons to the Jantar Mantar demonstrations, reflecting growing public anger.
  • Officials have not yet confirmed details of the alleged irregularities or announced any formal inquiry.

Ranchi is witnessing a growing tide of student unrest as aspirants of the Jharkhand Public Service Commission (JPSC) and Jharkhand Staff Selection Commission (JSSC) exams continue their sit-in, alleging serious irregularities in the recruitment process. The protests, which have been ongoing for days, have now drawn political support from an unexpected quarter — Abhijeet Dipke of the CJP.

Dipke, in a statement on Sunday, said, "CJP stands with all students." His remarks come as hundreds of young men and women camp at designated protest sites in the state capital, demanding a Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) probe into the conduct of the examinations. The students argue that paper leaks, mismanagement, and opaque evaluation methods have made a mockery of their efforts.

What triggered the protests

The agitation began after allegations surfaced that question papers for certain JPSC and JSSC examinations were leaked in advance. Candidates who had prepared for months, often at great personal and financial cost, found themselves competing against those who allegedly had access to the papers beforehand. The scale of the alleged leak, say protesters, is so vast that it has rendered the entire selection process suspect.

Students have also accused the examining bodies of failing to provide transparency in the evaluation process. Several candidates claim that answer sheets were not shown to them despite repeated requests, and that marks awarded in some cases do not match their expectations. This, they say, has left thousands of qualified aspirants in the lurch.

Political reactions and support

Dipke's endorsement is significant because it marks one of the first major political voices to openly back the protesters. His message, "CJP stands with all students," was shared widely on social media, with many student groups welcoming the support. However, political observers note that the backing could also be a double-edged sword, as it might polarise the movement along party lines.

The state government has so far remained tight-lipped on the specific allegations. Officials have not yet confirmed the details of the irregularities or announced any formal inquiry, though sources indicate that a preliminary internal review may have been initiated. The lack of a clear official response has only fuelled the protesters' resolve.

Echoes of Jantar Mantar

The Ranchi protests have drawn comparisons to the landmark Jantar Mantar demonstrations in Delhi, where students from various states have gathered over the years to press for justice in recruitment exams. The comparison is not lost on the protesters, many of whom carry placards referencing the Delhi site. "This is our Jantar Mantar moment," said one demonstrator, speaking on condition of anonymity.

The mood at the protest site remains tense but peaceful. Students have vowed to continue their sit-in until their demands are met, which include a CBI investigation, re-conduct of affected examinations, and action against those responsible for the leak. They have also called for greater transparency in future recruitment drives.

As the standoff enters its second week, the pressure is mounting on the Jharkhand administration to respond. The state's dream of a robust government recruitment system now hangs in the balance, with thousands of young careers caught in the crossfire.

The coming days will be crucial. All eyes are now on whether the state government will concede to the demand for a CBI probe or attempt to defuse the situation with its own inquiry. Either way, the protesters have made it clear that they will not disperse until they see credible action.
