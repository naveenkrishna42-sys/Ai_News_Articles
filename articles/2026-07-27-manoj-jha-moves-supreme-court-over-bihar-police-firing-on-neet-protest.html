Manoj Jha Moves Supreme Court Over Bihar Police Firing on NEET Protesters

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Manoj Jha filed a petition in the Supreme Court over police firing on NEET protesters in Bihar.
  • The petition seeks registration of FIRs against the police officials involved.
  • The Supreme Court remarked that lathicharge cannot be justified just because there is an agitation.
  • The court reiterated that the right to peaceful protest is constitutionally protected and may frame guidelines.
  • The incident has sparked a debate on police conduct during student protests in Bihar.

Petition Filed Over Bihar Police Action

Rajya Sabha MP Manoj Jha has moved the Supreme Court, seeking action against Bihar Police officials for opening fire on NEET aspirants protesting in the state. The petition demands the registration of FIRs against the officers involved in the incident.

The development comes amid growing outrage over the police's handling of student demonstrations. Jha's legal challenge focuses on the alleged excessive force used to disperse the crowd.

Supreme Court's Strong Remarks on Protest Rights

During the hearing, the Supreme Court made a significant observation. “Can't lathicharge just because there is agitation,” the bench remarked, underscoring that mere protest does not warrant violent police action.

The court also noted that the right to peaceful protest is constitutionally protected. It suggested that it may consider framing guidelines to prevent similar excesses in the future, signaling a potential shift in how such cases are adjudicated.

CJI Weighs In on Police Conduct

Chief Justice Surya Kant, part of the bench, reinforced the message. “Peaceful protests are constitutionally protected, and mere agitation cannot justify lathi-charge,” he stated. The remarks have been widely reported as a rebuke to the Bihar Police's tactics.

The court's stance aligns with previous judgments that upheld the citizens' right to assemble peacefully. However, the specific context of student protests over NEET exam irregularities adds a layer of urgency to the proceedings.

What the Petition Seeks

Manoj Jha's petition specifically calls for a court-monitored investigation into the firing incident. It seeks the registration of criminal cases against the police personnel responsible, arguing that their actions were disproportionate.

The MP has also urged the court to ensure that such force is not used against future agitations. The plea highlights the need for accountability when law enforcement exceeds its mandate.

Broader Implications for Police Guidelines

The Supreme Court has hinted at framing comprehensive guidelines for police conduct during protests. This could have far-reaching consequences for how law enforcement agencies across the country handle demonstrations.

Legal experts say the court's intervention may lead to a clearer demarcation between permissible crowd control and unlawful violence. The matter is expected to be heard again soon, with the court seeking responses from the Bihar government.

What Happens Next

The Supreme Court is likely to issue a formal notice to the Bihar government in the coming days. The case will test the balance between maintaining public order and protecting fundamental rights, with all eyes on the court's eventual directive.
