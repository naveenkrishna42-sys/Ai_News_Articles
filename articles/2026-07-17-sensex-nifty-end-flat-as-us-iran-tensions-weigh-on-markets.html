Sensex, Nifty End Flat as US-Iran Tensions Weigh on Markets

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sensex and Nifty closed flat on Thursday amid renewed US-Iran tensions.
  • Geopolitical concerns outweighed positive domestic cues.
  • Broader markets outperformed benchmarks, with midcap and smallcap indices rising.
  • Investors remained cautious ahead of global cues and oil price movements.

Indian benchmark equity indices ended Thursday's trading session on a flat note as renewed geopolitical tensions between the United States and Iran dampened investor sentiment. The BSE Sensex closed marginally lower while the Nifty 50 settled near its previous close, reflecting cautious trading through the day.

Markets Open Flat, Stay Range-Bound

The Sensex opened slightly higher but quickly gave up gains as reports of heightened US-Iran tensions emerged. The index oscillated in a narrow band before closing with a negligible change. The Nifty 50 followed a similar trajectory, ending just above the 23,800 mark.

Market breadth was mixed, with advances and declines evenly split on the BSE. Sectors directly sensitive to crude oil prices, such as aviation and paint stocks, saw some pressure.

Geopolitical Jitters Offset Domestic Optimism

Renewed hostilities between the US and Iran raised concerns about potential supply disruptions in the Middle East, a key oil-producing region. This pushed global crude oil prices higher, weighing on sentiment for import-dependent economies like India.

Domestically, positive cues from quarterly earnings reports and steady foreign portfolio investment inflows provided some support. However, the geopolitical overhang prevented any sustained rally.

Broader Markets Show Resilience

While the headline indices remained flat, broader markets performed better. The BSE Midcap and Smallcap indices ended with gains of up to 0.5 percent, indicating that retail and high-net-worth investors continued to pick stocks selectively.

Analysts said the divergence between benchmarks and broader indices suggested that institutional investors were taking a cautious stance while domestic participants remained optimistic about medium-term prospects.

Investors Eye Oil Prices and Global Cues

Crude oil prices will be a key trigger for the markets in the coming sessions. Any escalation in the US-Iran conflict could push oil prices higher, impacting India's trade deficit and inflation outlook.

Global markets also remained in a wait-and-watch mode, with US and European indices trading mixed. The US dollar strengthened against major currencies, adding to the uncertainty for emerging markets.

Traders now await further developments on the geopolitical front and the next batch of quarterly corporate earnings.

Market participants will closely monitor the movement of crude oil prices and any diplomatic efforts to de-escalate tensions between the US and Iran. A resolution could trigger a relief rally, while further escalation may lead to increased volatility in the near term.
