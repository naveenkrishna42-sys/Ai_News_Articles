Four rare celestial events to coincide on 12 August

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • A partial solar eclipse and the peak of the Perseid meteor shower fall on the same day, 12 August.
  • Two other rare celestial events—a planetary conjunction and a supermoon—are also expected the same day.
  • Astronomers advise viewing away from city lights for the meteor shower, while eclipse viewing requires proper eye protection.

A Rare Celestial Alignment

Skywatchers in India are in for a treat on 12 August, when four rare celestial events coincide in a single night. A partial solar eclipse, the peak of the Perseid meteor shower, a planetary conjunction, and a supermoon will all occur within hours of each other. Astronomers call it a once-in-a-decade alignment.

The partial solar eclipse will be visible across northern and central India, starting around 4:30 pm IST. The moon will cover up to 60% of the sun's disk at maximum, depending on the location. The eclipse will end just before sunset, making it a dramatic twilight event.

The Perseid Meteor Shower Peaks

Later that night, the Perseid meteor shower will reach its peak. Known for its bright, fast meteors, the shower can produce up to 100 meteors per hour under ideal conditions. But this year, the supermoon will wash out fainter meteors, so observers may see only the brightest ones—perhaps 20 to 30 per hour.

"The supermoon will be a problem for meteor watchers," says Dr. Anjali Sharma, an astrophysicist at the Nehru Planetarium in New Delhi. "But the brighter Perseids will still put on a show, especially after midnight when the radiant is high." She advises viewers to find a dark spot, away from city lights, and to let their eyes adapt for at least 20 minutes.

Conjunction and Supermoon

Adding to the spectacle, Jupiter and Venus will appear in a close conjunction in the western sky after sunset on 12 August. The two planets will be just half a degree apart—about the width of a full moon. They will be visible to the naked eye, appearing as two brilliant points of light.

The supermoon, meanwhile, will rise in the east just as the sun sets. This will be the closest full moon of the year, appearing about 14% larger and 30% brighter than a typical full moon. The combination of a supermoon and a solar eclipse on the same day is extremely rare; the last such occurrence was in 2018, and the next won't happen until 2036.

How to Watch Safely

For the solar eclipse, experts stress that looking directly at the sun is dangerous. Special eclipse glasses or solar filters are essential. Regular sunglasses are not enough. During the meteor shower, no equipment is needed—just a reclining chair, a blanket, and patience.

Several observatories and science centres across India are planning public viewing events. The Nehru Planetarium in New Delhi will host a live stream of the eclipse and a night-long meteor watch. Similar events are planned in Mumbai, Bengaluru, and Kolkata, with telescopes set up for safe solar viewing.

Cloud cover could be a spoiler in some regions. The India Meteorological Department will issue a forecast closer to the date. If skies are clear, this will be a night to remember.

For those who miss it, the next partial solar eclipse visible from India occurs in 2027, and the next Perseid supermoon combination is not expected until 2036. So mark your calendars—12 August promises to be a spectacular celestial show.
