Himachal High Court: Filming a video is not a crime, junks FIR against man

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Himachal Pradesh High Court ruled that filming a video is not a crime.
  • Court quashed FIR against a man who recorded a brawl at a police post.
  • The judgment reinforces citizens' right to record incidents in public spaces.

Court quashes FIR over filming at police post

The Himachal Pradesh High Court has ruled that simply recording a video is not a crime. The court quashed an FIR filed against a man who filmed a brawl that broke out at a police post. The judgment underscores the limits of police power and the rights of citizens to document events in public spaces.

The case originated from an incident where the petitioner recorded a physical altercation involving police personnel at a police post. The police registered an FIR against the man under various sections, including those related to obstructing public servants and causing mischief.

Recording is not an offence, court says

Justice Sushil Kukreja, who heard the petition, observed that the act of filming a video, by itself, does not constitute a criminal offence. The court noted that the petitioner was merely a bystander who captured the incident on his phone. He did not participate in the brawl or incite violence.

The bench held that the FIR was an abuse of the legal process. It stated that the police cannot file cases against individuals solely for recording events in a public place. The court directed the state to quash the FIR and all subsequent proceedings against the man.

Citizens' right to record upheld

Legal experts say the ruling is significant. It reinforces the principle that citizens have the right to record the actions of public officials, including the police, as long as they do not interfere with official duties. The court's decision aligns with earlier judgments by the Supreme Court that protect the right to film in public spaces under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution, which guarantees freedom of speech and expression.

The High Court also noted that the police cannot claim immunity from being recorded. If a public servant is performing duties in a public place, a citizen has the right to document that activity. The court warned that filing frivolous FIRs against people for exercising this right amounts to harassment.

No evidence of obstruction

The prosecution had argued that the man's act of filming obstructed the police in their duty. The court rejected this argument, finding no evidence that the petitioner had physically blocked or interfered with the police. The mere presence of a camera or phone does not constitute obstruction, the bench said.

The judgment also criticised the police for overreacting. The court observed that instead of dealing with the brawl, the police chose to target the person who was recording it. This approach, the court said, is contrary to the principles of justice and fairness.

The Himachal Pradesh government has not announced whether it will appeal the decision. The petitioner's lawyer welcomed the order, calling it a victory for transparency and accountability.

What happens next

With the FIR quashed, the man is free from prosecution. The case sets a precedent for similar matters in Himachal Pradesh. It is likely to be cited in future cases where police file complaints against people for recording their actions.
