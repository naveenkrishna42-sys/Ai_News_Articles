Pakistan Hockey Team Refuses India Travel for Asian Champions Trophy 2026

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-16 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pakistan has formally asked the Asian Hockey Federation to shift its ACT 2026 matches from Mohali and Jalandhar to a neutral venue.
  • The Hero Men's Asian Champions Trophy 2026 is scheduled from October 27 to November 5, with Hockey India as host.
  • A withdrawal would hand India a major competitive advantage but weaken the tournament's credibility and broadcast value.

Pakistan's national hockey team has informed the Asian Hockey Federation (AHF) that it will not travel to India for the Hero Men's Asian Champions Trophy (ACT) 2026, scheduled from October 27 to November 5 in Mohali and Jalandhar. The Pakistan Hockey Federation (PHF) has formally requested that its matches be moved to a neutral venue, citing what it calls "sensitive" bilateral ties between the two countries. The demand, framed under the slogan "Keep Sports Free From Politics", puts the AHF and Hockey India in a tight spot with barely weeks left before the opening whistle.

What Pakistan Has Actually Demanded

The PHF's position is specific: it is not asking for a full tournament relocation, but for its own fixtures to be staged outside India. That distinction matters. A partial shift would force the AHF to either run a hybrid event across two countries or restructure the draw entirely. Neither option is cheap, and both carry logistical risk for a six-nation tournament with a compressed 10-day window.

Why This Is More Than a Scheduling Dispute

India and Pakistan are the two most commercially significant hockey markets in Asia. Their head-to-head fixture is the single biggest draw on the ACT calendar, routinely commanding the largest broadcast audience and the highest ticket demand. Losing that match doesn't just hurt the gate receipts at Mohali's Olympian Balwant Singh Stadium or Jalandhar's Surjit Hockey Stadium — it undermines the tournament's entire sponsorship proposition.

For Indian hockey fans, the practical loss is straightforward. An ACT without Pakistan is a diminished product: fewer high-intensity contests, weaker ranking points on offer, and a title race that becomes materially easier for the host nation. That cuts both ways. A trophy won without facing Pakistan carries less weight in the eyes of selectors, sponsors, and the FIH world rankings committee.

Financial and Competitive Fallout

Hockey India has invested heavily in pitch preparation, team accommodation, and broadcast infrastructure across two venues. A late withdrawal triggers partial refunds, renegotiated media rights, and reputational damage with the AHF — the same body that awards future hosting rights. For Pakistan, skipping the event means forfeiting FIH ranking points and match practice ahead of the 2026 World Cup cycle, a serious cost for a programme rebuilding after years of financial strain.

  • Pro (Pakistan's stance): Avoids a politically charged travel decision and protects player welfare from external pressure.
  • Con: Forfeits ranking points and competitive exposure in a critical World Cup preparation year.
  • Pro (India as host): Retains hosting rights, broadcast control, and home advantage.
  • Con: A weakened field reduces the ACT's prestige and sponsorship value.
  • Risk to AHF: A precedent where participating nations can dictate venue changes on non-sporting grounds.

The Neutral Venue Question

Realistically, a neutral venue means Oman, Malaysia, or the UAE — all with adequate turf facilities and recent FIH event experience. But relocating even a handful of matches means new visas, fresh broadcast contracts, and renegotiated hotel blocks, all inside a four-to-six week window. The AHF has historically avoided partial relocations because they fracture the integrity of a single round-robin format.

What Buyers and Fans Should Watch

For ticket holders and streaming subscribers, the key date is the AHF's official response. Until that arrives, ACT 2026 remains scheduled as planned in Punjab. Fans who booked travel to Mohali or Jalandhar should hold off on cancellations — the tournament itself is not in doubt, only Pakistan's participation. Broadcasters, meanwhile, are quietly modelling two scenarios: a full six-team field, and a five-team event with a revised final-four structure.

The standoff is a test of whether Asian hockey's governing body can enforce a calendar it has already ratified. Whichever way the AHF rules, the outcome will shape how future hosts — and future participants — treat multi-nation events in the region.
