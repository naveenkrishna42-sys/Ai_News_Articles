Sensex, Nifty fall on Friday: Key reasons behind the market decline

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Markets Original source ↗
Key points
  • Markets opened lower and remained under pressure through the session
  • Banking, financial, and IT stocks led the decline
  • Weak global cues and rising crude oil prices weighed on sentiment

Markets open lower, extend losses through session

Indian equity benchmarks Sensex and Nifty fell sharply on Friday, extending losses from the previous session. The BSE Sensex dropped over 800 points during intraday trade, while the NSE Nifty slipped below the 24,500 mark before recovering some ground.

The sell-off was broad-based, with investors trimming positions across sectors. Market breadth turned negative, with more than two stocks declining for every one advancing on the BSE.

Banking and IT stocks lead the decline

Banking and financial stocks were the biggest drag on the indices. Heavyweights like HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, and State Bank of India traded in the red, pulling the Nifty Bank index down by over 1.5 percent.

IT stocks also came under pressure, with Infosys, TCS, and Wipro witnessing selling. The Nifty IT index fell nearly 2 percent, reflecting concerns over global demand and currency fluctuations.

Global cues and crude oil weigh on sentiment

Weak global cues added to the pessimism. Asian markets traded lower, tracking overnight losses on Wall Street, where tech stocks led a sell-off. Investors remained cautious ahead of key US inflation data due later this month.

Rising crude oil prices further dampened sentiment. Brent crude traded above $85 a barrel, raising concerns about inflationary pressures and their impact on India's fiscal deficit and current account balance.

What analysts are watching

Market participants said volatility is likely to persist in the near term as global factors remain uncertain. The rupee's movement against the dollar and foreign institutional investor flows will be closely monitored.

Domestic institutional investors have been providing some support, but sustained FII selling has kept the market under pressure. Analysts suggest that any positive surprise on the inflation front could trigger a rebound.

Meanwhile, the broader market also witnessed profit booking, with midcap and smallcap indices falling in line with the benchmarks. Sectors like auto and FMCG managed to limit losses, offering some resilience.

Investors are now looking ahead to the upcoming corporate earnings season and the Reserve Bank of India's policy stance for further direction. The market's ability to hold key support levels will be crucial in the coming sessions.
