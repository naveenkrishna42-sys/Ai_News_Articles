Cristiano Ronaldo Instagram like sparks FIFA bias row after Argentina final loss

📅 2026-07-21 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ronaldo's account liked a post claiming FIFA wanted Argentina to win the World Cup.
  • The like came just before Argentina's final defeat to Spain.
  • The post has reignited corruption allegations against football's governing body.

Social media stir

Cristiano Ronaldo's Instagram account has liked a post accusing FIFA of manipulating the World Cup for Argentina. The post, which claimed that FIFA 'wanted to give the World Cup to Messi,' was liked just before Argentina's final defeat to Spain.

The move has reignited debate about bias in international football. Fans and critics have pointed to the timing as significant. The post itself directly calls FIFA's integrity into question.

Context of the claim

The allegation comes from a social media user who suggested that FIFA actively helped Argentina reach the final. No evidence has been provided to support this claim. FIFA has not issued an official response to the post or Ronaldo's interaction with it.

Argentina lost the final to Spain by a 3-1 margin. The defeat ended their reign as world champions. The match itself was free of major controversial refereeing decisions.

Ronaldo's silence speaks volumes

Ronaldo has not commented publicly on the like. His representatives have also not clarified whether the interaction was intentional. The Portuguese star has a history of intense rivalry with Lionel Messi, which adds fuel to the fire.

This is not the first time a public figure's social media activity has sparked controversy. However, Ronaldo's stature makes this incident particularly significant. Fans are divided—some see it as a simple mistake, others as a deliberate statement.

Previous allegations against FIFA

FIFA has faced corruption allegations for decades. The 2015 scandal involving top officials still casts a long shadow. While the current administration has promised reform, scepticism remains high among fans and media.

The organisation has consistently denied any favouritism. A former World Cup official told the BBC, 'I promise you there is no favouritism.' Yet, incidents like this keep the debate alive.

What happens next

Ronaldo is expected to break his silence in the coming days. FIFA may also issue a formal statement. For now, the football world watches and waits for clarity on one of its biggest stars' most provocative social media moves.
