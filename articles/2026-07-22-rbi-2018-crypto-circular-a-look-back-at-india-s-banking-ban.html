RBI 2018 Crypto Circular: A Look Back at India's Banking Ban

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • The RBI circular, issued on April 6, 2018, barred regulated entities from providing services to crypto businesses.
  • The ban was challenged in the Supreme Court, which quashed the circular on March 4, 2020, citing proportionality issues.
  • The circular had a chilling effect on Indian crypto exchanges, many of which shut down or moved overseas.

In April 2018, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) issued a circular that sent shockwaves through the country's fledgling cryptocurrency industry. The directive prohibited all banks and regulated financial institutions from dealing in or providing services to any individual or business entity handling virtual currencies.

The circular, titled 'Prohibition on Dealing in Virtual Currencies', effectively cut off the banking lifeline for crypto exchanges and startups. Without access to the formal banking system, these companies could no longer process deposits or withdrawals in Indian rupees.

The Immediate Fallout

Within weeks of the circular, major Indian crypto exchanges faced a liquidity crisis. Users struggled to convert their digital assets back into fiat currency. Several platforms, including Zebpay and Koinex, suspended operations or relocated to more crypto-friendly jurisdictions.

The RBI's stance was clear: it viewed virtual currencies as a threat to financial stability and consumer protection. The central bank cited risks related to money laundering, terrorist financing, and the volatility of unregulated assets.

Legal Challenge and Supreme Court Verdict

The crypto industry did not take the ban lying down. The Internet and Mobile Association of India (IAMAI) filed a writ petition in the Supreme Court, arguing that the RBI's circular was unconstitutional and disproportionate.

After months of hearings, the Supreme Court delivered a landmark verdict on March 4, 2020. The bench quashed the RBI circular, ruling that the ban was not proportionate to the stated objectives. The court noted that since cryptocurrencies were not illegal, the central bank could not deny access to banking services.

The ruling was a major victory for the crypto community. Exchanges reopened, and trading volumes surged. However, the regulatory uncertainty did not disappear entirely.

Legacy and Ongoing Uncertainty

The 2018 circular remains a cautionary tale. It demonstrated how quickly regulatory action can reshape an entire industry. While the Supreme Court struck down the banking ban, the government has since proposed new legislation to create a more comprehensive framework for digital assets.

As of mid-2026, India still lacks a dedicated crypto law. The RBI continues to express concerns, but the banking channels remain open. The episode has left a lasting impression on investors and entrepreneurs, who now watch regulatory signals more closely than ever.

What comes next? The government is expected to introduce a new bill that could classify certain cryptocurrencies as assets while banning others. The industry waits for clarity, hoping the lessons of 2018 inform a balanced approach.
