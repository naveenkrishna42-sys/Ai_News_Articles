Houthis Strike Saudi Oil Site, US Pauses Iran Bombing for First Time in Two Weeks

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Houthi rebels attacked a Saudi oil facility, escalating regional tensions.
  • The US paused consecutive nightly strikes on Iran for the first time in two weeks.
  • White House advisers, including Vice President Vance and General Caine, reportedly raised concerns about the trajectory of the conflict.

Houthi rebels launched an attack on a Saudi oil facility on Saturday, marking a significant escalation in the regional conflict. The strike came on the same day the United States paused its nightly bombing campaign against Iran for the first time in fourteen days.

The White House has not confirmed the extent of damage at the Saudi site. Officials in Riyadh have not issued a formal statement on the incident. The attack underscores how the war between Israel, the US, and Iran is drawing in other regional actors.

US Pauses Strikes, Advisers Voice Concerns

American warplanes did not conduct bombing runs over Iran on Saturday night. It was the first break in a relentless two-week campaign that had seen near-daily US air strikes against Iranian military positions.

Sources told CNN and The New York Times that Vice President JD Vance and General John Caine, a senior military adviser, raised concerns inside the White House about the escalating war. The advisers reportedly warned that the current trajectory risked a wider, uncontrollable conflict in the Middle East.

President Donald Trump has given space for talks, according to reports from The Hindu. Tehran has also halted its retaliatory strikes, Al Jazeera reported, suggesting a fragile de-escalation may be taking hold.

Houthi Attack Complicates Ceasefire Push

The Houthi attack on the Saudi oil site threatens to unravel any momentum toward a truce. The Iran-backed group has repeatedly targeted Saudi energy infrastructure during the conflict, but Saturday's strike was one of the most brazen in weeks.

Regional analysts have long warned that the Houthis possess drones and missiles capable of striking deep inside Saudi Arabia. The group has framed its attacks as solidarity with Iran, which is facing a multi-front war with the US and Israel.

Saudi Arabia has tried to stay on the sidelines of the conflict. The attack may force Riyadh to reconsider its position, though the kingdom has not signalled any immediate military response.

Iran Halts Retaliation, Trump Offers Talks

Iranian officials confirmed on Sunday that they had paused their own retaliatory strikes against US and Israeli targets. The move aligns with Trump's stated preference for negotiations over continued bombardment.

Tehran's decision to hold fire is a significant shift. Just days ago, Iran had launched barrages of missiles and drones at American bases in the region. The pause in tit-for-tat attacks has raised cautious hopes for a diplomatic off-ramp.

Neither Washington nor Tehran has set a date for formal talks. The Houthi attack may harden positions on both sides before any negotiation can begin.

The coming days will test whether the pause in strikes holds. The Houthi assault on Saudi oil infrastructure could provoke a new cycle of retaliation, derailing the fragile calm that has emerged after two weeks of intense warfare.
