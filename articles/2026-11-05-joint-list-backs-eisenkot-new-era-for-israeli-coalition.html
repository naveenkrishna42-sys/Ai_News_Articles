Joint List Endorses Eisenkot: A New Political Calculus

In a significant development reshaping Israel's post-election landscape, the Joint List, an alliance of Arab-majority parties, has announced its unequivocal support for Gadi Eisenkot to form the nation's next government. This endorsement, coming after intense coalition negotiations following the November 2026 elections, marks a pivotal moment, potentially ushering in a new era of political alliances and influence within the Knesset.

Historically, Arab-majority parties have often been sidelined in government formation, rarely joining governing coalitions or explicitly endorsing a prime ministerial candidate. The Joint List's decision to back Eisenkot signals a strategic shift, positioning these parties as crucial kingmakers rather than peripheral players. This move directly challenges conventional wisdom and complicates the path for other contenders, most notably former Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

The Joint List's Strategic Leverage

A Shift in Arab Political Influence

The Joint List's endorsement of Gadi Eisenkot is not merely a procedural step; it represents a profound evolution in Israeli politics. For decades, Arab parties have navigated a complex political identity, balancing their representation of Palestinian citizens of Israel with the broader national political discourse. Their decision to actively support a mainstream Zionist candidate for prime minister underscores a pragmatic approach aimed at maximizing their legislative influence and addressing the needs of their constituents.

This strategic pivot transforms the 'Bibi or Tibi' narrative – a past framing of Israeli politics as a choice between Netanyahu and the Joint List's Ahmed Tibi – into one where Arab parties are indispensable to coalition building. Their votes are now critical for any candidate attempting to secure the 61-seat majority required in the 120-member Knesset. This newfound leverage could translate into greater bargaining power on issues ranging from civil rights and budget allocations to the peace process.

Eisenkot's Path to Power

For Gadi Eisenkot, the Joint List's backing provides a crucial numerical advantage. While specific seat counts from the recent election are still being finalized, securing the Joint List's support significantly strengthens his mandate to be tasked with forming a government. This endorsement could provide the necessary buffer against a fragmented opposition and allow him to build a broader, more stable coalition across the political spectrum.

The challenge for Eisenkot will be to bridge the ideological gaps between the Joint List and other potential coalition partners, particularly centrist and right-leaning factions. His ability to navigate these diverse interests while maintaining the Joint List's support will be key to forming a functional government and avoiding further political deadlock.

Challenges for Netanyahu and the Right-Wing Bloc

The Tightening Grip on Coalition Prospects

The Joint List's explicit support for Eisenkot places Benjamin Netanyahu in a considerably more precarious position. Netanyahu, who has long relied on a solid right-wing and religious bloc, now faces an uphill battle to muster a governing majority without any potential tacit or explicit support from Arab parties, which is unlikely given their stance. This development forces him to either convince reluctant right-wing factions to join his camp or risk being relegated to the opposition.

The traditional right-wing bloc may find it challenging to form a government independently, especially if some of its members are unwilling to make concessions necessary for a broader alliance. The political calculations are further complicated by internal divisions and the reluctance of some parties to sit with Netanyahu, regardless of the Joint List's actions.

Palestinian Voter Impact

The outcomes of the November 2026 election, particularly the robust turnout and consolidated support for Arab-majority parties, have directly impacted Netanyahu's political strategy. Palestinian citizens of Israel, often feeling disenfranchised, have demonstrated their collective power at the ballot box. Their votes have not only bolstered the Joint List's representation but also sent a clear message that their political agency can no longer be overlooked in the national discourse.

This increased engagement and strategic voting have compelled mainstream parties to acknowledge the necessity of engaging with Arab political leaders. The Joint List's endorsement of Eisenkot is a direct consequence of this evolving voter dynamic, reflecting a desire to actively shape the country's future rather than passively observe from the sidelines.

  • Arab-majority parties solidify their role as essential 'kingmakers' in Israeli parliamentary politics.
  • This event underscores a growing trend of political engagement and influence from Palestinian citizens of Israel.
