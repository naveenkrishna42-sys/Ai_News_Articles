Auto-Sweep Fixed Deposits: How They Work, What They Cost

What Is an Auto-Sweep Fixed Deposit?

An auto-sweep fixed deposit (FD) is a hybrid product that links your savings account to a fixed deposit. When your balance exceeds a pre-set threshold, the excess is automatically swept into an FD, earning higher interest. When you need funds, the FD is partially or fully liquidated to cover the shortfall. This gives you the liquidity of a savings account with the returns of an FD.

Banks like ICICI, HDFC, SBI, and Axis offer variants. The sweep-in and sweep-out happen in multiples of a fixed amount (e.g., ₹10,000), and the FD tenure is typically one year, renewable automatically.

How Does It Work?

You set a threshold—say ₹25,000. Any balance above this is swept into an FD at the end of the day. If you spend ₹15,000, the bank breaks ₹15,000 from the FD to cover it, and the remaining FD continues. Interest on the swept portion accrues at the FD rate, while the threshold balance earns savings account interest.

The sweep-out is usually instantaneous, but some banks process it at specific intervals. The FD is broken in units, so you don’t lose interest on the entire amount—only on the portion withdrawn.

What Does It Cost?

Auto-sweep FDs are not free. Most banks charge a penalty for premature withdrawal of the swept FD, typically 0.5% to 1% on the withdrawn amount. Some banks waive it if the withdrawal is for emergencies, but that’s discretionary. Additionally, if you fall below the threshold, you may incur charges for non-maintenance of minimum balance.

Pros and Cons

  • Pros: Higher returns than a savings account; instant liquidity; no need to manually manage FDs; ideal for emergency funds.
  • Cons: Penalty on premature withdrawals; interest rate may be lower than a dedicated FD; complexity in tracking; not suitable for long-term goals.

Is It Worth It?

For salaried individuals with fluctuating balances, auto-sweep FDs can generate an extra ₹2,000–₹5,000 annually on a ₹5 lakh average surplus, compared to a savings account. However, if you can lock funds for 1–2 years, a dedicated FD or a debt fund may yield better post-tax returns. Use auto-sweep for parking funds you might need within 3–6 months.
