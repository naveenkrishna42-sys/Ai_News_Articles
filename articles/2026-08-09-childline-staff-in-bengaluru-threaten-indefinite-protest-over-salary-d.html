Childline Staff in Bengaluru Threaten Indefinite Protest Over Salary Delay

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Staff at Childline's Bengaluru unit have threatened an indefinite protest over unpaid salaries.
  • The protest stems from months of delayed wages, affecting the helpline's operations.
  • Authorities have not yet responded to the staff's demands.

Protest Looms Over Unpaid Wages

Staff at Childline's Bengaluru chapter have threatened to launch an indefinite protest over the continued delay in salary payments. The employees, who handle the city's child helpline services, say they have not received their wages on time for several months, pushing them to the brink.

The threat comes after multiple requests to the management reportedly went unheard. Workers now say they are left with no choice but to take to the streets if their dues are not cleared immediately.

Helpline Services at Risk

Childline, a 24x7 emergency helpline for children in distress, relies on its staff to respond to calls, coordinate rescues, and provide counselling. With salaries pending, the staff argue that their ability to serve vulnerable children is being severely compromised.

"We are being forced to work without pay," one staff member said on condition of anonymity. "How long can we continue like this?" The delay has reportedly caused financial hardship for many employees, some of whom are contract workers with no other source of income.

Management's Silence Fuels Anger

The staff claim that the management has not provided a clear timeline for releasing the pending salaries. This lack of communication has only deepened the frustration, with some employees already stopping work in protest.

The indefinite protest, if it materialises, could disrupt Childline's operations in Bengaluru, leaving thousands of children without access to emergency support. The helpline receives hundreds of calls each month, many involving cases of abuse, trafficking, and abandonment.

Past Grievances Resurface

This is not the first time Childline staff have complained about salary delays. Similar issues were reported in previous years, but the problem appears to persist. Workers now say they will not back down until the management addresses their demands in writing.

They are also seeking a formal commitment to ensure timely payments in the future. The protest, they say, is not just about money but about dignity and respect for the work they do.

What Happens Next

As of now, the management has not issued a public statement on the matter. The staff have given a deadline for the payment of dues, after which they plan to begin the indefinite protest.

If the impasse continues, the situation could escalate, potentially drawing the attention of the labour department and child welfare authorities. All eyes are now on the management to see if it will act before the deadline expires.
