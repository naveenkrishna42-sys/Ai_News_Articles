Overnight Russian airstrikes kill 14 in Kyiv, damage warehouses

📅 2026-08-05 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Overnight Russian missile and drone barrage kills at least 14 in Kyiv, with warehouses damaged and fires breaking out.
  • Ukrainian officials warn that a shortage of interceptor missiles is costing lives as attacks intensify.
  • President Zelenskyy renews calls for more air-defence systems from allies.
  • Reports of the death toll vary between 14 and 17, with authorities yet to give a final figure.

KYIV — Russian forces launched a heavy overnight barrage of missiles and drones at the Ukrainian capital, killing at least 14 people and setting warehouses ablaze, authorities said on Wednesday. The attack, which lasted several hours, underscored the intensifying strain on Ukraine's air-defence network as the war enters a new phase of attrition.

Emergency crews worked through the night to douse fires that broke out in industrial districts on the city's outskirts. Thick black smoke rose over the affected areas as residents sheltered in metro stations and underground passages. Debris from intercepted missiles rained down on residential blocks, shattering windows and littering streets with shrapnel.

Death toll varies as rescue operations continue

Kyiv's military administration confirmed at least 14 fatalities, though some reports put the number higher. The Hindu and The Guardian cited figures of 15 and 17 killed respectively, while the Wall Street Journal reported 17 dead. Officials have not yet released a final casualty count, and rescue teams were still combing through damaged structures at midday.

Warehouses storing grain and consumer goods were among the structures hit, contributing to the large fires. The blasts also damaged power lines, leaving parts of the capital without electricity for several hours. Ukraine's state emergency service said firefighters had contained the main fires by morning, but warned that secondary blazes could still flare up.

Interceptor shortage becomes critical

The attack came as Ukrainian officials repeated a stark warning: the country is running dangerously low on the interceptor missiles needed to shoot down incoming Russian projectiles. In a statement after the strike, the Ukrainian air force said that a lack of munitions had forced it to prioritise targets, leaving some drones and missiles to hit their mark.

"Every gap in coverage costs lives," a senior defence official told reporters, speaking on condition of anonymity. The official did not specify how many interceptors remain, but acknowledged that stockpiles have dwindled to critically low levels. Western allies have pledged additional systems, but deliveries have been slow and patchy.

Zelenskyy renews appeal for air defences

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy used the attack to again press Ukraine's partners for more air-defence systems. In his nightly address, he said that "every day of delay means more deaths" and called for immediate shipments of Patriot and other advanced interceptors. He did not name specific countries, but his message was clearly aimed at Washington and European capitals.

Ukraine has repeatedly asked for at least seven more Patriot batteries to cover its major cities. The United States and Germany have each pledged additional systems in recent months, but deliveries have been incremental. Meanwhile, Russia has adapted its tactics, launching waves of cheap drones to exhaust Ukrainian defences before sending in cruise missiles.

What to watch next

The coming weeks will test whether Ukraine's allies can accelerate deliveries of interceptors before winter, when Russia historically steps up strikes on the energy grid. Kyiv's ability to protect its cities will hinge on those shipments, and on whether new Western aid packages clear parliamentary hurdles. For now, the people of Kyiv are left to count the cost of a night that brought the war back to their doorsteps.
