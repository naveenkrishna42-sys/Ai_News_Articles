Air India captain's confirmatory drug test positive for marijuana

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Confirmatory drug test of Air India captain positive for marijuana, source says
  • Test follows a rare flight-control event causing 300-foot altitude drop
  • AAIB to investigate the incident; government confirms probe

Confirmatory test confirms initial finding

The Air India captain at the centre of last week's Phuket-Delhi flight incident has tested positive for marijuana in a confirmatory drug test, a source familiar with the matter said on Tuesday. The test result corroborates the initial screening that had flagged the presence of the substance.

The development comes days after the flight, operating as AI 176, experienced a rare flight-control event that caused a sudden 300-foot drop in altitude. The aircraft, carrying over 200 passengers and crew, was en route from Phuket to Delhi when the incident occurred.

Air India has not yet issued an official statement on the confirmatory test result. The airline had earlier said it was cooperating with authorities and had taken the captain off flying duties pending investigation.

Rare flight-control event under scrutiny

The incident, which unfolded mid-flight, has drawn attention to the aircraft's flight-control systems. The Hindu reported that the event was "rare" and triggered the sharp descent before the crew regained control.

Sources told Reuters that the captain's preliminary test had already returned positive, but the confirmatory test, which is more accurate, has now validated the initial finding. The co-pilot's test results have not been disclosed so far.

Aviation experts say a positive confirmatory test for a psychoactive substance in a pilot is a serious breach of safety norms. The Directorate General of Civil Aviation (DGCA) has strict rules prohibiting crew members from consuming any such substances.

Government orders AAIB probe

The government has now mandated the Aircraft Accident Investigation Board (AAIB) to probe the incident. ThePrint reported that the government confirmed the AAIB would investigate the circumstances surrounding the flight-control event and the drug test.

The AAIB's mandate will include examining the sequence of events that led to the altitude drop, the crew's actions, and any contributing factors, including the captain's medical and toxicological status. The probe is expected to take several weeks.

The captain, whose identity has not been released, remains suspended. The DGCA had earlier issued a show-cause notice to the pilot, but the confirmatory test result is likely to escalate disciplinary action.

Safety protocols and airline response

Air India has maintained that it follows standard operating procedures for crew health and fitness. In a brief statement last week, the airline said it was "committed to the highest safety standards" and was extending full cooperation to investigators.

This incident has reignited concerns about pilot substance abuse and the adequacy of random drug testing in Indian aviation. While the DGCA conducts periodic checks, the case has prompted questions about the frequency and effectiveness of such screenings.

A senior pilot, speaking on condition of anonymity, said that while such cases are rare, they highlight the need for a more robust monitoring system. "The system relies heavily on self-reporting and random checks," he said, adding that the industry needs to address mental health and substance abuse issues more openly.

The flight-control event itself is also being examined separately. Aviation regulators will look at whether the aircraft's systems malfunctioned or if there was any procedural lapse by the crew. The Boeing aircraft involved in the incident has been grounded for inspection.

Passengers on the flight have been contacted by Air India, though the airline has not disclosed the number of those who have sought compensation. The airline has offered refunds and vouchers to affected travellers.

As the AAIB begins its work, the focus will be on determining the exact cause of the altitude drop and whether the captain's condition played a role. The findings could lead to stricter regulations and enhanced screening protocols for cockpit crews.

The DGCA is expected to take a final decision on the captain's licence after reviewing the AAIB report and the confirmatory test results. Industry observers say this case could set a precedent for how Indian aviation handles substance abuse among flight crew.

For now, the captain remains grounded, and the aircraft stays out of service. The investigation will determine whether this was an isolated lapse or a symptom of a deeper issue in pilot oversight.
