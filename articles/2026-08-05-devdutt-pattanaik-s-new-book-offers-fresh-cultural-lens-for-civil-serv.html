Devdutt Pattanaik's new book offers fresh cultural lens for civil service aspirants

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • Pattanaik's book presents a new approach to understanding culture for UPSC aspirants.
  • It integrates mythological narratives with administrative ethics and governance.
  • The book aims to bridge traditional knowledge with modern bureaucratic challenges.

Mythologist and author Devdutt Pattanaik has come out with a new book aimed at civil service aspirants, promising what he calls “an entirely new approach to culture.” The book, which seeks to rethink how culture is studied and applied in governance, is already generating interest among UPSC candidates and public administration enthusiasts.

Redefining culture for administration

Pattanaik, known for his interpretations of Indian mythology and its relevance to modern life, argues that culture is often misunderstood in the context of civil services. For aspirants, culture is usually reduced to static facts — festivals, monuments, and traditions — but the author contends it is a dynamic force that shapes decision-making, public policy, and leadership.

“It is an entirely new approach to culture,” Pattanaik said, explaining that the book moves beyond rote learning. It encourages readers to see culture as a living framework that influences how societies function and how administrators can connect with the people they serve.

Mythology meets governance

The book draws on mythological stories and symbols to illustrate ethical dilemmas, conflict resolution, and the art of communication. Pattanaik uses familiar narratives from the Ramayana, Mahabharata, and regional folklore to demonstrate how ancient wisdom can inform contemporary bureaucratic practice.

For instance, he explores how the concept of dharma can guide officers in making fair decisions under pressure, or how the idea of loka (the world) helps in understanding community dynamics. The approach is practical, not academic, making it accessible to aspirants who may not have a background in the humanities.

Why this matters for aspirants

Civil service exams in India have long emphasized culture as part of the syllabus, but Pattanaik's book attempts to reframe it as a tool for empathy and effective governance. The author believes that an officer who understands cultural nuances is better equipped to handle ground-level realities, from caste dynamics to regional sensitivities.

“The exam tests memory; the job tests wisdom,” Pattanaik noted in a recent interview. The book, he says, is designed to bridge that gap, offering aspirants a way to internalize cultural knowledge rather than merely memorize it.

Early responses from readers suggest that the book is being welcomed for its fresh perspective. Some aspirants have taken to social media to say that it has changed how they approach the subject, making it more engaging and relevant.

A larger conversation

The release also feeds into a broader discussion about the role of culture in India's development model. Scholars and administrators have often debated whether Western management theories are sufficient for Indian public systems, and Pattanaik's work adds a distinctly Indian voice to that conversation.

His previous books, including Jaya and Myth = Mithya, have already made him a popular figure among young readers. With this new offering, he extends his reach to a demographic that is often focused on competitive success, reminding them that culture is not a subject but a lens.

The book is now available in leading bookstores and online platforms. Pattanaik is expected to hold a series of webinars and workshops in the coming months to discuss its themes with aspirants and educators.

As the civil services examination cycle picks up pace, this book could well become a companion for many — not just to clear a test, but to understand the society they will one day serve.
