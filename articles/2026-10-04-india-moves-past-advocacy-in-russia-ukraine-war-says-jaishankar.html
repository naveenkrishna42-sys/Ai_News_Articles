India’s Dual Path: Advocacy Meets Implementation

Foreign Minister P. S. Jaishankar declared India has "gone beyond advocacy" amid Russia’s war against Ukraine. The ministry’s brief reflects a shift from public condemnation to a more nuanced engagement, balancing global diplomatic expectations with national strategic interests. India’s foreign policy now includes tangible actions—energy procurement, labour agreements, and mediation efforts—rather than solely rhetorical opposition.

1. Balancing Moral Stance and National Interest

India’s public statements criticize Russia for infringing on sovereignty, yet its economic ties remain robust. The policy is guided by the principle of "non‑alignment with war parties," letting India collaborate without sanctioning outright. This duality allows India to leverage its indispensable technology and defence cooperation with Russia while keeping a conduit open for humanitarian and diplomatic dialogue.

Energy Policy: Russia’s Crude in India’s Portfolio

India’s energy mix has continued to rely on Russian crude, a fact highlighted in Jaishankar’s briefing to the national energy panel. Even with global sanctions, India argues that substituting Russian oil would cripple its power sector and raise costs for consumers. Import volumes are forecasted to remain around 1.6 million barrels per day, representing roughly 12% of total fuel imports as of 2026.

2. Economic Impact of the Crude Trade

Removing Russian crude would entail a 15–20% hike in fuel costs, aggravating inflation. On the other hand, diversification toward U.S. and Middle Eastern suppliers could reduce political friction but at a higher price tag. India’s long‑term strategy focuses on maintaining, not eliminating, this relationship, ensuring a predictable supply as the global energy transition progresses.

Human Capital: 70,000 Skilled Workers in Moscow

The state‑backed programme to deploy 70 000 Indian professionals in Russia showcases a significant workforce alignment. The initiative covers fields such as information technology, aerospace, and energy engineering. It acts as both a commercial safeguard for Indian firms and a soft‑power tool, reinforcing India’s presence in the Moscow economy.
