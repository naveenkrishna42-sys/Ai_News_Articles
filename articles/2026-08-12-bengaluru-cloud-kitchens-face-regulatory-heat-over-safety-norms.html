Bengaluru cloud kitchens face regulatory heat over safety norms

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bengaluru authorities have launched a crackdown on cloud kitchens over safety and hygiene violations.
  • Several outlets face action for operating without proper licences and flouting zoning rules.
  • The city is reviewing regulations to balance growth of the delivery-only food sector with compliance.

Bengaluru's booming cloud kitchen sector, which thrives on app-based food delivery, is now under the scanner. Civic authorities have begun inspecting these delivery-only kitchens following a spate of complaints about hygiene, safety and illegal operations.

Officials say many cloud kitchens operate from residential premises or commercial spaces not zoned for food preparation. This has led to friction with neighbours and raised concerns about fire safety and waste disposal.

Licence and zoning violations

The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) has identified several outlets running without the mandatory food safety licence or the required trade certificate. In some cases, kitchens were found operating in spaces that were never approved for commercial cooking.

Under the current rules, a food business must obtain a licence from the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) and comply with local zoning laws. But the rapid proliferation of cloud kitchens has outpaced enforcement, leaving grey areas.

Residents in several neighbourhoods have lodged complaints about fumes, noise and late-night activity from these kitchens. Many such operations are tucked inside apartment complexes or standalone buildings in mixed-use areas.

Health and safety risks

Inspectors have flagged inadequate ventilation, improper storage of raw materials and lack of pest control measures in some kitchens. Fire safety, too, has emerged as a major concern, with several outlets lacking extinguishers or clear exits.

One official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the focus is not to shut down the industry but to ensure minimum standards. "We want compliance, not confrontation," the official said, adding that repeated violators could face fines or closure.

The food delivery boom, accelerated by the pandemic, has made cloud kitchens a lucrative business. However, the informal nature of many operations has made oversight difficult.

Industry response

Restaurant associations have acknowledged the need for clearer rules. They argue that the current regulatory framework, designed for traditional eateries, does not fit the cloud kitchen model.

Some operators have begun voluntarily upgrading their facilities and seeking proper permits. But smaller players, often run by first-time entrepreneurs, struggle with the paperwork and costs involved.

Experts point out that a streamlined approval process, with a single-window clearance, could help. They also suggest that the BBMP issue specific guidelines for cloud kitchens, addressing everything from kitchen size to waste management.

What lies ahead

The BBMP is expected to complete its survey of cloud kitchens in the coming weeks. Based on the findings, it may revise local bylaws and introduce a dedicated licensing category.

Until then, operators face uncertainty. Those with valid licences and proper infrastructure are likely to benefit as the city tightens its grip, while others may find themselves pushed out of business. The coming months will show whether Bengaluru can strike a balance between innovation and accountability.
