Trump Announces Landmark Hamas Disarmament Deal in Gaza

📅 2026-08-01 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump calls the agreement 'historic' and says it covers 'complete disarmament' of Hamas.
  • Israeli officials insist troops won't pull back without 'genuine' disarmament, raising doubts.
  • Gaza residents describe the moment as 'longest nightmare' possibly ending, but details remain unclear.

Trump Declares a 'Historic' Breakthrough

Former US President Donald Trump has announced what he calls a 'historic' agreement that would secure the 'complete disarmament' of Hamas in Gaza. The announcement, made on Thursday, was brief on specifics but heavy on symbolism, with Trump framing it as a decisive step toward ending the conflict that has ravaged the enclave for years.

The deal, as described, would see Hamas hand over its weapons and renounce armed resistance in exchange for a full Israeli military withdrawal. Trump's language suggested a sweeping finality, but the announcement left many questions unanswered, particularly around verification and enforcement.

Israel's Cautious Response

Within hours, Israeli officials pushed back with a clear caveat. A senior Israeli official told the BBC that troops would not pull back from Gaza without 'genuine' disarmament by Hamas. The official stressed that a signed document alone would not be enough to trigger a withdrawal.

This public hedging underscores a deep mistrust between the parties. Israel has long argued that any ceasefire must be verifiable and that Hamas has a history of rearming after previous agreements. The phrase 'complete disarmament' is being treated in Jerusalem with visible scepticism, and military commanders are reportedly seeking a robust inspection mechanism before any redeployment begins.

What Constitutes 'Genuine' Disarmament?

The Israeli condition raises a practical question: how will disarmament be measured? Hamas's arsenal includes rockets, mortars, and underground manufacturing facilities, many of which are hidden across Gaza's dense urban landscape. Aerial surveillance and on-ground inspections would be needed, but who would conduct them remains unclear.

Neither the Trump team nor Hamas has released a detailed timeline or a list of weapons to be surrendered. Palestinian factions have historically refused to disarm as a matter of principle, making this announcement a significant rhetorical shift if it proves accurate. Yet the lack of concrete terms suggests the deal may be more of a framework than a finalised treaty.

Voices From Gaza: 'The Longest Nightmare'

Inside Gaza, the news has been met with a mix of exhaustion and tentative hope. Residents, many of whom have lost homes and loved ones over the past year, described the announcement as the possible end of what one person called 'the longest nightmare'. Street-level reactions captured by reporters show people gathering around radios and phones, trying to parse the headlines.

But the mood is not uniformly optimistic. Some civilians worry that a poorly executed deal could lead to a new round of violence, or that Israel's conditions might delay any meaningful change. For many, the immediate concern is humanitarian: access to food, water, and medical supplies remains critical, and no political announcement has yet altered the ground reality.

International Reactions and Unanswered Questions

International observers have welcomed the announcement but with caution. Regional powers, including Egypt and Qatar, which have mediated previous ceasefires, have not yet issued formal statements. The United Nations has not commented on the specifics of the disarmament plan.

There is also no clarity on who will guarantee the agreement. Trump's role, while prominent in the announcement, is not that of a sitting president, and his ability to influence events on the ground remains untested. Israeli officials have made clear that their military decisions will be driven by security assessments, not political declarations.

What Happens Next

The next 48 hours will be critical. Israeli security cabinet meetings are expected to discuss the framework, and Hamas leadership may offer its own clarification on the scope of disarmament. Whether the 'historic' label holds will depend on tangible steps, not just statements. For now, the world watches to see if this deal moves from announcement to action.
