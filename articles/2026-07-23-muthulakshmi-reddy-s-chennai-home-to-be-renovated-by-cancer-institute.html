Muthulakshmi Reddy's Chennai home to be renovated by Cancer Institute

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Cancer Institute (WIA) in Chennai will renovate Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddy's former residence.
  • Dr. Reddy was a pioneering physician, social reformer, and the first woman legislator in India.
  • The house, located in the city, will be restored as a heritage site to honour her contributions.

Preserving a Pioneer's Legacy

The Cancer Institute (WIA) in Chennai has taken up the renovation of the former home of Dr. Muthulakshmi Reddy, one of India's first female physicians and a prominent social reformer. The house, located in the heart of the city, had fallen into disrepair over the years.

Dr. Reddy was a trailblazer in multiple fields. She was the first woman to graduate from Madras Medical College, the first woman legislator in India, and a fierce advocate for women's rights and public health. She also founded the Cancer Institute in 1954.

Restoring a Historic Structure

The renovation project aims to restore the building to its original condition. Officials at the Cancer Institute said the work will focus on structural repairs, restoring original architectural features, and creating a small museum dedicated to Dr. Reddy's life and work.

The house, which served as her residence for several decades, holds immense historical and sentimental value. It was here that she planned many of her landmark initiatives in healthcare and social welfare.

A Museum in the Making

Plans for the renovated building include a dedicated space to display photographs, personal belongings, and documents related to Dr. Reddy's career. The museum will also highlight her role in the abolition of the devadasi system and her efforts to improve medical education for women.

The Cancer Institute has not yet disclosed the total cost of the renovation or a timeline for completion. However, officials stated that the work will be carried out with minimal disruption to the surrounding neighbourhood.

Honouring a Visionary

Dr. Reddy's contributions to medicine and society remain deeply respected in Tamil Nadu. The renovation of her home is seen as a fitting tribute to a woman who dedicated her life to public service.

The institute hopes the restored house will serve as an inspiration for future generations, particularly young women aspiring to careers in science and medicine. It will also stand as a reminder of the city's rich historical legacy.

The project is expected to be completed within the next year.
