Understanding RBI’s Revised Bulk FD Framework

The Reserve Bank of India periodically updates the definition of bulk term deposits to reflect evolving market conditions and to ensure that large deposits are monitored for systemic risk. Effective October 1, 2026, the RBI has issued a circular that revises the threshold at which a fixed deposit is considered ‘bulk’. This change influences how banks price, report, and manage such deposits.

For investors, the revision means that a larger pool of fixed deposits will now fall under the regular deposit category, subject to standard interest‑rate policies and disclosure requirements. Understanding the nuance behind the threshold shift is essential for assessing the true cost and return of large‑value FDs.

Key Changes Effective October 1, 2026

The RBI’s circular modifies three core aspects: the bulk‑deposit threshold, the interest‑rate flexibility afforded to banks, and the reporting obligations for bulk FD holders. While the central bank does not prescribe a specific rate for bulk FDs, it sets an overall ceiling that banks must not exceed, preserving depositor protection.

These adjustments aim to balance depositor safety with banks’ need to manage large‑ticket funds efficiently. The relaxed rate‑setting freedom enables banks to respond to liquidity pressures, while the higher threshold reduces regulatory burden on mid‑size deposits.

Impact on Individual and Institutional Investors

Individual investors who previously booked FDs just above ₹15 lakh now find their deposits classified as regular, potentially qualifying for standard promotional rates offered to retail customers. Institutional investors, such as corporations and trusts, will see a larger share of their term‑deposit book fall under the bulk category, giving them access to negotiated rates but also subjecting them to the new penalty structure.

From a liquidity perspective, the revised premature‑withdrawal penalty may affect cash‑flow planning for investors who rely on breaking FDs before maturity. The slab‑linked penalty encourages alignment of deposit tenor with actual funding needs, discouraging premature exits.

Practical Considerations for FD Investors

Interest Rate Flexibility

Banks can now offer differentiated rates on bulk FDs based on tenure, deposit amount, and the bank’s own cost of funds. Investors should compare offers across multiple banks, focusing on the effective annual yield after accounting for any premium or discount tied to the deposit size.

It is advisable to request a written rate‑sheet that clearly states the applicable rate, the minimum deposit to avail that rate, and any conditions for rate revision during the tenure.

Premature Withdrawal and Loan Against FD

The new penalty structure means that breaking a two‑year bulk FD after six months may attract a different charge than breaking a five‑year deposit after the same period. Investors should verify the penalty table before committing funds, especially if the deposit is earmarked for contingent expenses.
