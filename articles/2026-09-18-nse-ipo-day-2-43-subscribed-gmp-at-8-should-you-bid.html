NSE IPO Day 2: 43% Subscribed, GMP at 8% — Should You Bid?

✍️ By TIVRA News Markets & Finance Desk 📅 2026-09-18 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • NSE's Rs 22,569-crore IPO crossed full subscription on the back of strong institutional demand after a modest 43% Day 1 covered book.
  • Grey market premium hovered near 8% on Day 2, implying a listing gain of roughly Rs 100-120 per share on the Rs 1,400-1,500 band — far short of the 30-35% GMP seen in smaller issues.
  • At the upper band, NSE trades at a premium valuation to listed peers, so analysts largely favour a long-term holding stance over a listing-pop trade.

The National Stock Exchange's Rs 22,569-crore initial public offering entered its second day of bidding with its book fully subscribed, aided by a sharp jump in qualified institutional buyer (QIB) demand on Day 1. Retail participation, however, stayed measured — the non-institutional and retail portions were still short of full cover when the QIB leg closed the gap. Grey market premium (GMP) held at roughly 8% over the issue band, a modest signal compared with the 25-35% premiums quoted for smaller issues like Hero Motors and Jindal Supreme in the same window.

Day 1 Subscription and Day 2 Momentum

Day 1 closed with the issue subscribed about 43%, driven mainly by anchor-backed institutions. The full subscription mark was breached once QIB bids scaled up on the morning of Day 2. Employee and shareholder quotas moved faster than the retail category, a pattern typical of large, well-known issuers where institutional conviction — not retail frenzy — sets the tone.

For buyers, the takeaway is structural: this is a heavily institution-led book. Retail allotment odds improve only if the retail category stays under-subscribed, but a late surge in HNI bids can flip that equation on the final day.

What Brokerages Are Saying

Brokerage notes broadly converge on two themes. First, NSE is a monopoly-plus asset — its dominance in equity derivatives, index licensing and colocation services gives it pricing power few financial platforms match. Second, that dominance is also the central risk: any regulatory intervention on derivatives volumes, transaction charges or market share caps directly hits revenue.

Most houses have assigned a subscribe rating for the long term rather than for listing gains. Valuation at the upper band prices in years of growth, leaving limited margin of safety for investors chasing a quick flip. A minority view flags the Forbes India argument that market dominance can become a liability if regulators push for a more balanced exchange ecosystem that benefits rivals such as BSE.

  • Pros: Unmatched liquidity franchise, asset-light model, high operating margins, index and data licensing moat, strong institutional backing.
  • Cons: Rich valuation at upper band, regulatory concentration risk, modest 8% GMP, revenue tied to derivatives volumes that policy can disrupt.
  • Read-through: A strong NSE listing could lift sentiment for BSE and other capital-market plays, even as it competes with them.

Buyer Strategy and Value Justification

At an 8% GMP, the expected listing pop of Rs 100-120 per share barely covers the risk of a flat debut. Allottees looking for a trade should plan an exit near listing; investors with a three-to-five-year horizon can hold, provided they accept that NSE's earnings are cyclical to trading volumes.

Apply with realistic expectations. Avoid borrowing to fund HNI bids at this premium, and size the application to your existing equity exposure — a single large-cap exchange stock should not dominate a portfolio. The final-day subscription trend, particularly in the retail category, remains the single most useful signal for allotment odds.
