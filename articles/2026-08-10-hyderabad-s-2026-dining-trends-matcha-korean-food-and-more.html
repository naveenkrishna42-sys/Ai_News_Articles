Hyderabad's 2026 dining trends: matcha, Korean food and more

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Matcha-based drinks and desserts are a major trend in Hyderabad's cafés.
  • Korean cuisine, including bibimbap and Korean fried chicken, is gaining popularity.
  • Experiential dining and fusion menus are reshaping the city's restaurant scene.

Hyderabad's culinary calendar for 2026 is taking shape, and the city's palate is clearly travelling. From the quiet rise of matcha to the bold flavours of Korean street food, restaurant menus are reflecting a more globalised, adventurous diner.

The shift is visible across neighbourhoods, from Jubilee Hills to Madhapur, where new eateries are betting on ingredients and dishes that were niche just a few years ago. Telangana Today's early survey of the year's trends points to a dining scene that is less about the familiar biryani-and-burgers split and more about specific, story-driven cuisines.

Matcha moves beyond the latte

Matcha has shed its status as a mere Instagram prop. In 2026, the powdered green tea is appearing in cold brews, soft serves, and even savoury applications. Cafés across Hyderabad are reporting steady demand for matcha-based desserts, with some outlets struggling to keep up with supply.

What started as a niche order among health-conscious customers has now become a menu staple. Several coffee chains have introduced dedicated matcha sections, and independent bakeries are experimenting with matcha-infused cakes and croissants. The trend shows no sign of peaking, with suppliers noting a sharp increase in bulk orders from local businesses.

Korean food finds a faithful audience

Korean cuisine has moved from a curiosity to a mainstay. Bibimbap bowls, kimchi jjigae, and Korean fried chicken are appearing on menus beyond dedicated Korean restaurants. Food courts and delivery apps now list Korean options alongside North Indian and Chinese staples.

The appeal, restaurateurs say, is the balance of heat, umami, and texture. Korean dishes offer a sensory punch that pairs well with Hyderabad's love for bold spices. However, the trend is not just about authenticity; fusion versions, such as Korean tacos and kimchi fried rice, are also drawing crowds, especially among younger diners.

Experiential dining takes centre stage

Diners in Hyderabad are no longer satisfied with just a meal; they want a show. The rise of chef's table experiences, interactive cooking stations, and themed pop-ups is reshaping how restaurants design their spaces and menus. Multi-course tasting menus with beverage pairings are booking out weeks in advance.

This shift is pushing chefs to be more inventive. Seasonal menus, foraged ingredients, and open-kitchen formats are becoming selling points. Some restaurants are even offering DIY components, where diners assemble their own dishes under guidance, adding an element of participation that was previously rare.

Fusion and regional revival

While global cuisines are expanding, there is also a parallel movement towards regional Indian food. Chefs are revisiting Telangana's own culinary heritage, presenting millet-based dishes and traditional pickles in contemporary formats. This dual trend—global and hyper-local—is defining the city's dining identity in 2026.

Fusion is also getting smarter. Rather than random mashups, chefs are pairing flavours with clear logic, such as matcha with pistachio, or Korean gochujang with local vegetables. The result is a menu that feels both new and familiar, appealing to a wide range of tastes.

As the year progresses, the test will be whether these trends sustain momentum or fizzle out. Early signs suggest that matcha and Korean food are here to stay, but the next few months will determine which variations become permanent fixtures. For now, Hyderabad's diners have plenty to explore, and restaurants are racing to keep up.
