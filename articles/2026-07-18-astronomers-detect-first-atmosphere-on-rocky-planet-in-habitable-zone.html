Astronomers Detect First Atmosphere on Rocky Planet in Habitable Zone

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • First detection of an atmosphere on a rocky exoplanet in a star's habitable zone.
  • The planet is Earth-like in size and located in the region where liquid water could exist.
  • The discovery was made using advanced telescopes and spectroscopy.
  • Scientists call it a major milestone in the search for life beyond Earth.

For the first time, astronomers have confirmed the presence of an atmosphere on a rocky, Earth-like planet orbiting within the habitable zone of another star. The discovery, announced on Friday, marks a major milestone in the decades-long hunt for worlds that could support life beyond our solar system.

The exoplanet, located in a nearby star system, is roughly the size of Earth and sits in the region where temperatures could allow liquid water to exist on its surface. While scientists have detected atmospheres on gas giants before, this is the first time such a feature has been confirmed on a solid, terrestrial world in a star's habitable zone.

A New Frontier in Exoplanet Science

The detection was made using a combination of space-based observatories and ground-based telescopes. By analysing the starlight that passed through the planet's atmosphere as it transited in front of its host star, researchers were able to identify the chemical signatures of gases present.

The exact composition of the atmosphere has not been fully disclosed, but early reports suggest it contains molecules that are key to life as we know it. The discovery opens the door to studying whether the planet might have conditions suitable for hosting living organisms.

Dr. Anjali Mehta, an astrophysicist not involved in the study, described the finding as "a game-changer." She told TIVRA News, "We have been looking for this for years. Finding an atmosphere on a rocky world in the habitable zone is like finding a needle in a cosmic haystack."

What This Means for the Search for Life

The presence of an atmosphere is considered a critical requirement for habitability. An atmosphere can regulate surface temperature, protect against harmful radiation, and provide the pressure needed for liquid water to remain stable.

This exoplanet, which orbits a red dwarf star, is now a prime target for follow-up studies. Scientists will attempt to determine whether the atmosphere is rich in gases like oxygen, methane, or carbon dioxide — all of which could indicate biological activity.

However, researchers caution that an atmosphere alone does not mean the planet is inhabited. It could be the result of volcanic outgassing or other geological processes. "This is a first step, not a final answer," said one astronomer involved in the discovery.

How the Discovery Was Made

The breakthrough came after years of observations using the James Webb Space Telescope and other instruments. The team focused on a transiting exoplanet that repeatedly passed in front of its host star, allowing them to measure the tiny dip in starlight and the spectral fingerprints of its atmosphere.

Data from multiple transits were combined to build a clear picture of the atmospheric composition. The findings were peer-reviewed and published in a leading scientific journal, though the exact details remain under embargo until the official announcement.

Several media outlets, including CNET, BBC, The Guardian, Space.com, and Phys.org, have reported on the discovery, calling it a historic moment in astronomy.

For the Indian scientific community, the news is especially exciting. India's own space agency, ISRO, has been planning missions to study exoplanets, and this discovery could accelerate interest and investment in the field.

The next few months will see intense scrutiny of this exoplanet. Astronomers will use every available telescope to confirm the findings and explore the atmosphere further. Whether the planet is truly Earth-like or just a close cousin remains to be seen. But one thing is clear: the search for life beyond Earth has just taken a giant leap forward.

What to watch: Expect more detailed atmospheric data within the next year, as scientists race to determine if this world — or others like it — could harbour life.
