Man dies after drunk driver's crash on Chennai ECR

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Victim, 32, died at Rajiv Gandhi Government General Hospital
  • Drunk driver in SUV hit victim's car near Uthandi on ECR
  • Accused arrested under IPC sections; investigation ongoing

Fatal Crash on East Coast Road

A 32-year-old man died in Chennai on Monday after a drunk driver rammed his car on the East Coast Road (ECR), according to police. The victim, a resident of Thiruvanmiyur, was critically injured in the collision near Uthandi around 10:30 pm on Sunday and was rushed to Rajiv Gandhi Government General Hospital, where he succumbed early Monday.

The accused, identified as a 28-year-old businessman from the city, was driving an SUV in an inebriated state, police said. He was taken into custody shortly after the incident and later released on bail, as the offence is bailable.

How the Accident Unfolded

Preliminary investigations suggest the SUV, travelling at high speed, rear-ended the victim's car at a signal near Uthandi. The impact crushed the rear of the car, trapping the driver inside. Fire and rescue personnel had to cut open the vehicle to extricate him.

The victim was conscious when pulled out but had severe internal injuries. He was stabilised at a nearby private hospital before being shifted to the government hospital for advanced care. Doctors said he suffered multiple fractures and a head injury.

Police Action and Legal Proceedings

The Neelankarai police registered a case under sections of the Indian Penal Code, including 279 (rash driving) and 337 (causing hurt by act endangering life). Following the victim's death, the section was upgraded to 304A (causing death by negligence).

Police said the accused's blood sample has been sent for analysis to confirm alcohol levels. A senior officer told reporters that the driver had a history of traffic violations, though this could not be independently verified.

Rising Concerns Over Drunk Driving on ECR

The ECR, a popular stretch for weekend outings, has seen multiple accidents involving speeding and drunk drivers. Local residents have repeatedly demanded better enforcement, especially at night. Traffic police have announced increased patrolling and random breathalyser checks on the stretch.

Meanwhile, the victim's family has appealed for stricter punishment for drunk driving. "He was returning home after a family dinner. One irresponsible act took his life," a relative said, requesting anonymity.

Experts say that while Tamil Nadu has stringent laws against drunk driving, conviction rates remain low, and many offenders escape with minor fines. Social activists have called for mandatory counselling and license suspension for repeat offenders.

What Next

The police are awaiting the forensic report, which will determine the exact blood alcohol content of the accused at the time of the crash. The case is likely to be heard in a local court soon, where the accused will face trial for causing death by negligence.
