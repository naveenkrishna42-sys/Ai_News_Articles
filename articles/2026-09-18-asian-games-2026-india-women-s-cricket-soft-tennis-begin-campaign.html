Asian Games 2026: India Women's Cricket & Soft Tennis Begin Campaign

  • Coach Amol Muzumdar calls the Asiad a 'teaser' for the Olympics, signalling a long-term development focus.
  • Soft tennis offers India a realistic multi-medal pathway given strong recent Asian circuit performances.

India's medal ambitions at the Asian Games 2026 shift into a higher gear on September 18 with two headline campaigns getting underway in Japan: the women's cricket team's gold defence and the soft tennis squad's opening rounds. Both disciplines carry genuine podium potential, but for very different reasons — one protects an established crown, the other hunts an emerging opportunity.

Women's Cricket: Defending Gold Without Complacency

India enter the cricket competition as defending champions, having won gold at the 2022 edition in Hangzhou. Captain Harmanpreet Kaur leads a squad blending power-hitting top-order batters with a spin-heavy attack suited to Japanese conditions. Head coach Amol Muzumdar has publicly framed the tournament as a "teaser" for the Olympics — a signal that the team management views this as a development checkpoint rather than a final destination.

Former captain Anjum Chopra has backed Harmanpreet to rediscover her batting rhythm during the competition, a view echoed across Indian cricket circles. The compact T20 format used at the Asian Games rewards quick adaptation: teams play knockout-style fixtures with little recovery time, making toss decisions, death bowling and fielding efficiency disproportionately decisive.

What Buyers of Cricket Content Should Watch

For fans planning subscriptions around the Games, the cricket window is short and intense. Streaming passes purchased for the Asiad deliver better value if bundled with the full multi-sport package rather than cricket-only add-ons, since India's medal events cluster across hockey, athletics and shooting in the same fortnight.

Soft Tennis: India's Quiet Medal Pathway

Soft tennis — played with softer rubber balls and lighter rackets than lawn tennis — has been an Asian Games fixture since 1994. India's squad has steadily improved on the Asian circuit, with doubles pairings showing consistency against Southeast Asian opponents. The sport offers four medal events across men's, women's and mixed doubles categories, giving India multiple entry points to the podium.

Why Soft Tennis Deserves Attention

Unlike cricket, soft tennis is not an Olympic sport, so the Asian Games represents the highest available stage. That makes it a priority event for the athletes and for national federation funding cycles. Expect aggressive net play and long baseline rallies; matches can stretch past 90 minutes in the Japanese heat and humidity.

Real-World Highlights and Watch Points

  • Gold defence pressure: India carry favourite status in women's cricket, which raises expectation but also tactical scrutiny on team selection.
  • Coaching narrative: Muzumdar's "Olympics teaser" framing suggests experimentation with combinations ahead of future global events.
  • Soft tennis upside: Multiple doubles categories give India repeated medal shots from a single squad.
  • Conditions matter: Japanese September heat and humidity will test fitness in both sports, especially in longer soft tennis matches.
  • Viewing economics: Multi-sport passes offer better per-event value than single-discipline subscriptions during the Games fortnight.

What This Means for Indian Sport

The September 18 slate captures India's dual sporting identity: a cricket powerhouse defending a crown it is expected to win, and a developing multi-sport nation quietly building depth in niche disciplines. Soft tennis medals, if they arrive, would carry outsized significance for federation funding and athlete visibility. Cricket gold, by contrast, would simply confirm the status quo — which is precisely why the manner of victory will be scrutinised as closely as the result itself.

Both campaigns run through the coming days in Japan. For Indian fans, the practical takeaway is straightforward: check official schedules for exact start times, as cricket fixtures and soft tennis draws are subject to change based on weather and tournament progression.
