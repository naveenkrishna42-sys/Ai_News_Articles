BCCI set to review team India support staff after Sri Lanka Tests

📅 2026-08-03 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • BCCI to conduct a formal review of India's performance in the UK tour in August.
  • Injuries and excuses have triggered scrutiny of support staff and management.
  • Gautam Gambhir and Shreyas Iyer face BCCI scrutiny after the Zimbabwe tour.

The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI) is gearing up for another round of introspection. After a forgettable UK tour and a fresh spate of injuries, the team's backroom staff is once again in the crosshairs. A formal review of the senior men's team's performance in England is expected in August, and sources indicate that more changes could be on the anvil.

Review after review

This is not the first time the board has called for a post-mortem. The pattern is familiar: a high-profile series loss, a flood of criticism, and then a quiet reshuffle behind the scenes. The upcoming review, however, comes with added urgency. The Indian team's recent showings have not just been about losses; they have been about how those losses were framed.

Critics have pointed out that the team's problems go beyond the playing XI. The support staff, including coaches, trainers, and physios, have faced questions over fitness management and tactical preparedness. The BCCI's August review is expected to scrutinise these roles closely.

Injuries and excuses

The injury crisis has been a recurring theme. Reports suggest that the BCCI is particularly concerned about the mounting number of players breaking down during tours. The Sri Lanka Tests were meant to be a reset, but they have instead brought the team's fragility back into focus.

One commentator put it bluntly: India didn't lose to Ireland and England. It lost to its own excuses. That sentiment appears to be gaining traction within the board as well. The review is likely to address not just the results, but the narrative around them.

Gambhir and Iyer under the lens

Head coach Gautam Gambhir and batter Shreyas Iyer are expected to face specific scrutiny. Gambhir's coaching methods and man-management have been questioned after the Zimbabwe tour, while Iyer's fitness and consistency have come under the scanner. Both are likely to be part of the review discussions.

The BCCI has not yet confirmed the exact scope of the review or who will be called in for talks. Officials have remained tight-lipped, but insiders suggest that the board is looking at a broader overhaul rather than a targeted fix.

What could change

Possible changes include new appointments in the support staff, revised fitness protocols, and a stricter rotation policy for players. The board may also look at how injuries are managed during overseas tours, a sore point in recent months.

There is also talk of separating the coaching roles for different formats. The current setup, where a single coach oversees all formats, has been criticised for being stretched too thin. A split could be on the cards, though nothing has been finalised.

Looking ahead

The Sri Lanka series was supposed to provide clarity. Instead, it has raised more questions. The BCCI's August meeting will be crucial in determining the direction for the next cycle of international cricket.

For now, the focus remains on the review. What comes out of it could reshape the team's support structure for years to come. The board is expected to announce its decisions before the next major assignment, with fans and experts alike watching closely.
