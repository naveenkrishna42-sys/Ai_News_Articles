Chennai's Regional Biriyanis: A Complete Guide

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chennai offers a variety of regional biriyani styles, including Ambur, Dindigul, and Chettinad.
  • Each style has unique cooking methods, spice blends, and meat choices.
  • Key restaurants and local spots serve authentic versions of these biriyanis.

Chennai's love affair with biriyani is no secret. But for the uninitiated, the city's biriyani scene is a maze of regional variations, each with its own loyal following. From the fiery notes of Ambur to the subtle aromas of Dindigul, the choices can be overwhelming. This guide breaks down the key styles and where to find them.

The Ambur Biriyani: A Star in Its Own Right

Ambur biriyani, originating from the town of Ambur in Vellore district, is arguably the most popular style in Chennai. It is known for its distinct use of jeera samba rice, which is small-grained and aromatic. The meat, usually chicken or mutton, is marinated in a blend of spices and curd before being slow-cooked with the rice in a sealed pot.

The defining feature is the generous use of mint and coriander, giving it a fresh, green hue. It is typically served with a brinjal curry and raita. For an authentic experience, head to Ambur Star Biriyani in Triplicane or the popular branches of Thalappakatti, which also serves a variant.

Dindigul Biriyani: The Thalappakatti Legacy

Dindigul biriyani, made famous by the chain Thalappakatti, is another heavyweight. It uses a different rice variety—usually seeraga samba, which is slightly longer than jeera samba. The meat is marinated for hours with a secret spice mix that includes anardana (dried pomegranate seeds), lending a subtle tanginess.

The cooking method involves a dum process where the pot is sealed with dough. The result is a drier, fluffier rice compared to Ambur biriyani. The meat is fall-off-the-bone tender. You can find this style at Thalappakatti outlets across the city, but the original branch in Dindigul remains the benchmark.

Chettinad Biriyani: Spice from the Heartland

Chettinad biriyani, hailing from the Chettinad region, is for those who crave heat. It is characterised by a fiery masala paste made with black pepper, fennel, and star anise. The meat, often mutton, is cooked separately with the masala before being layered with rice and baked.

The spice level is intense but balanced by the richness of coconut milk, which is sometimes added. The biriyani is typically served with a tangy onion raita and a spicy kuzhambu (gravy). For an authentic taste, visit The Chettinad House in Mylapore or the popular Karaikudi restaurant chain.

Other Regional Variations Worth Trying

Beyond the big three, Chennai offers other regional styles. The Lucknowi biriyani, available at places like Dilli 6, is heavier on ghee and saffron, with a more delicate spice profile. The Kolkata biriyani, with its subtle sweetness from potatoes and eggs, can be found at Arsalan.

For a unique twist, some restaurants serve a Malabar biriyani, which uses short-grained rice and a coconut-based masala. These styles are less common but offer a fascinating glimpse into India's diverse biriyani traditions.

Where to Start Your Biriyani Trail

If you are new to Chennai's biriyani, start with Ambur Star for a classic experience. Then move to Thalappakatti for Dindigul style, and end with Chettinad House for a spicy finish. Many local eateries also serve their own versions, so don't hesitate to explore.

Prices range from around Rs 150 to Rs 400 per plate, depending on the meat and restaurant. Most places offer takeaway, but eating fresh at the outlet is recommended for the best flavour.

Chennai's biriyani scene continues to evolve, with new restaurants and fusion versions appearing regularly. The city's love for this dish shows no sign of waning, making it an exciting time for food lovers to explore.
