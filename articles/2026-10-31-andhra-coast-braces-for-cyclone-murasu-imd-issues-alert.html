Andhra Pradesh On High Alert: Cyclone Murasu Looms After Arnab

The Andhra Pradesh coast is on high alert as the India Meteorological Department (IMD) has issued a fresh warning regarding Cyclone Murasu, projected to make landfall around November 2, 2026. This development places significant pressure on coastal communities and disaster management agencies, coming hot on the heels of Cyclone Arnab, which recently impacted the region. The impending storm, currently a well-marked low-pressure system in the Bay of Bengal, is undergoing rapid intensification, necessitating immediate and robust preparedness measures.

IMD's latest bulletin confirms the low-pressure system, originating over the Andaman Sea, has consolidated and is moving west-northwestwards. Meteorological models indicate a strong likelihood of it intensifying into a cyclonic storm over the central Bay of Bengal before approaching the Andhra coast. This rapid succession of cyclonic events underscores the heightened vulnerability of India's eastern seaboard and the critical importance of advanced weather forecasting.

Understanding the Meteorological Progression

A low-pressure system forms when warm, moist air rises, creating an area of lower atmospheric pressure. Over warm ocean waters, such as the Bay of Bengal, this system can draw in more moisture and energy, leading to further intensification. As the system strengthens, it can evolve from a depression to a deep depression, and eventually into a cyclonic storm characterized by strong winds rotating around a central eye.

IMD's categorisation of these systems is crucial for guiding public and governmental response. The current trajectory and intensification rate suggest Murasu could reach speeds of up to 100 kmph at landfall, classifying it as a severe cyclonic storm. Continuous monitoring via satellite imagery, radar, and ocean buoys allows meteorologists to refine predictions regarding its path, intensity, and potential impact zones, providing a vital window for preparedness.

Expected Impact and Coastal Vulnerability

The Andhra Pradesh coast, with its extensive coastline and densely populated areas, is particularly susceptible to cyclonic impacts. Cyclone Murasu is expected to bring heavy to very heavy rainfall, which could trigger flash floods, disrupt transportation networks, and inundate low-lying areas. The projected wind speeds of up to 100 kmph pose a significant threat to temporary structures, power lines, and agricultural crops, particularly those nearing harvest.

Beyond wind and rain, the potential for storm surge is a major concern. A storm surge, driven by strong winds pushing water towards the shore, can cause extensive coastal flooding, erosion, and saltwater intrusion into freshwater sources and agricultural lands. Fishing communities are advised to suspend operations immediately, and port authorities are preparing for disruptions. The economic repercussions, especially for agriculture and fisheries, could be substantial, affecting livelihoods across several districts.

Preparedness and Response Protocols

In anticipation of Cyclone Murasu, state disaster management authorities, in coordination with the National Disaster Response Force (NDRF) and State Disaster Response Force (SDRF), have initiated extensive preparedness measures. These include identifying evacuation routes, setting up temporary relief shelters, and deploying rescue teams to vulnerable areas. Public advisories are being disseminated through various channels, urging residents to stay informed, secure their homes, and follow official guidance.

Communication channels are being reinforced to ensure timely updates and emergency alerts reach all affected populations. Power utilities are pre-positioning equipment for rapid restoration efforts, and healthcare facilities are preparing for potential emergencies. The experience gained from managing previous cyclones, including Arnab, is proving invaluable in streamlining response efforts and mitigating the potential for widespread damage and loss of life.
