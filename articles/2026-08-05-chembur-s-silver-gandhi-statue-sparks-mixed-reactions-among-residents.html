Chembur's silver Gandhi statue sparks mixed reactions among residents

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • New silver-hued Gandhi statue installed in Chembur, Mumbai
  • Residents divided over artistic representation and choice of colour
  • Civic officials defend statue, say it was commissioned by local body
  • Debate highlights evolving public art discourse in Mumbai

MUMBAI: A newly installed statue of Mahatma Gandhi in Chembur has become the talk of the neighbourhood, but not all of it is flattering. The statue, painted a bright silver, has drawn both admiration and sharp criticism from residents, with some calling it a fresh tribute to the Father of the Nation and others dismissing it as gaudy and disrespectful.

The statue, located at a busy junction in Chembur, was unveiled earlier this week by the local municipal ward office. It depicts Gandhi in his signature walking pose, staff in hand, but the metallic silver finish has set it apart from the conventional bronze or white marble statues seen across the city.

Mixed reactions from locals

Residents who spoke to TIVRA News expressed divergent views. "It's eye-catching, yes, but is this how we remember Bapu? He was a man of simplicity. Silver feels flashy," said a retired schoolteacher who lives near the junction. Another resident, a college student, disagreed: "It's different. It makes you stop and look. Maybe that's the point — to get young people interested in history."

Several passers-by were seen taking photographs of the statue, with some even posing for selfies. However, a group of senior citizens gathered at a nearby park said they found the colour choice "odd" and "out of place" in a residential area. "It looks more like a film prop than a memorial," one of them remarked.

Civic officials defend the artwork

Officials from the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) ward office, however, defended the statue, stating that it was commissioned as part of a local beautification drive. A ward officer, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the silver finish was a deliberate artistic choice to make the statue stand out and attract attention. "The idea was to create a landmark that people notice, especially the youth," he said.

The officer added that the statue was funded through the ward's development budget and had been approved by the committee overseeing public art projects. He said no formal complaints had been received so far, though the ward office was open to feedback.

A broader debate on public art

The Chembur statue is not an isolated case. In recent years, Mumbai has seen several experimental public artworks, from murals to installations, sparking similar debates. Art critics point out that public statues often become sites of contestation, where aesthetics and sentiment collide.

"Gandhi's imagery is deeply symbolic. Any deviation from the familiar can feel jarring," said a city-based art historian, who did not wish to be named. "But public art is also about dialogue. Whether it succeeds or fails depends on how the community receives it."

The statue has also become a talking point on social media, with some users posting pictures and memes. While some praised the "modern touch", others questioned whether the money could have been better spent on civic amenities.

The debate is unlikely to end soon. The ward office has said it will consider residents' feedback and may hold a meeting to discuss the statue's future. For now, the silver Gandhi stands at the Chembur junction, drawing stares, selfies and strong opinions in equal measure.

What remains to be seen is whether the statue will become a beloved neighbourhood landmark or a cautionary tale in Mumbai's ongoing experiment with public art.
