Xi's Diplomatic Snub: Why It Matters

Chinese President Xi Jinping's decision to skip the BRICS gala dinner hosted by Prime Minister Narendra Modi in New Delhi has sparked intense speculation. While official statements cite scheduling constraints, the move is widely viewed as a calculated diplomatic snub, underscoring the fragile nature of India-China relations. The dinner, a customary informal setting for leaders to build rapport, was notably missing Xi, even as other BRICS heads of state attended. This absence is not trivial; it signals that beneath the veneer of cooperation, deep-seated divisions persist.

For India, the snub is a reminder that China's engagement with multilateral platforms like BRICS is often transactional. Xi's participation in the summit's formal sessions, where he announced a 5-point action plan, contrasted sharply with his avoidance of the informal dinner. This duality suggests China's intent to shape BRICS' agenda while keeping India at arm's length.

Xi's 5-Point Action Plan: Cooperation or Co-option?

At the BRICS summit, Xi unveiled a 5-point action plan aimed at deepening what he termed "Greater BRICS" cooperation. The initiatives focus on artificial intelligence, trade, and digital economy, with China positioning itself as the driving force. While these proposals appear collaborative, they may raise red flags in India. For instance, China's push for AI cooperation could lead to technology standards that favour Chinese firms, potentially compromising India's data security and technological sovereignty.

Similarly, trade initiatives under the BRICS umbrella might advance China's Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) objectives, which India has consistently opposed. India's concerns are not unfounded; China's past attempts to expand BRICS' scope have often aligned with its strategic interests. The action plan, therefore, demands careful scrutiny to ensure it does not undermine India's economic and security priorities.

The Xi-Modi Thaw: A Fragile Facade

The public thaw between Xi and Modi at the BRICS summit, marked by a handshake and brief talks, was touted as a positive step. However, the skipped dinner exposes the hollowness of this rapprochement. Bilateral tensions over the disputed border, trade imbalances, and China's growing assertiveness in the Indo-Pacific remain unresolved. The thaw is largely cosmetic, driven by the need to project unity at a multilateral forum.

For India, engaging with China requires a dual-track approach: cooperation where possible, but unwavering defence of sovereignty. The gala dinner snub is a stark reminder that China's actions often belie its words. India must continue to bolster its alliances, including the Quad, to counterbalance China's influence.

Pros and Cons: India's Strategic Calculus

  • Pros:
  • BRICS provides a platform for India to engage with major emerging economies.
  • Opportunities for cooperation in areas like climate change and counter-terrorism.
  • Balances China's dominance in other forums.
  • Cons:
  • China's agenda often dominates BRICS, limiting India's influence.
  • Xi's snub undermines trust and informal diplomacy.
  • Risk of BRICS being used to legitimise China's BRI and tech standards.

Real-World Implications for India

The Xi snub and China's expansive BRICS agenda have tangible consequences for India. On the economic front, India must guard against trade deals that could flood its markets with Chinese goods, harming domestic industries. In technology, China's AI push could lead to dependence on Chinese platforms, jeopardising data privacy. Strategically, India needs to deepen ties with like-minded partners such as the US, Japan, and Australia to ensure a free and open Indo-Pacific.

Prime Minister Modi's government has rightly prioritised self-reliance (Atmanirbhar Bharat) and border infrastructure development. These efforts must continue, even as India engages with China in multilateral settings. The BRICS summit has shown that India can assert its interests while maintaining diplomatic decorum, but it cannot afford to be passive.

Conclusion: Vigilance Amidst Engagement

Xi Jinping's gala dinner snub is a microcosm of the broader India-China dynamic: public harmony, private friction. As India navigates its BRICS membership, it must remain vigilant against China's attempts to shape the group's direction. The 5-point action plan may offer opportunities, but India should ensure that cooperation does not come at the cost of its sovereignty. By balancing engagement with deterrence, India can protect its interests in an increasingly contested world.
