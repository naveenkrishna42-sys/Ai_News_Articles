Release Detained CJP Protesters Without Criminal Past: Top Court To States

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Crime & Law Original source ↗
Key points
  • Supreme Court orders release of CJP protesters without criminal antecedents.
  • Court says continued detention of such individuals violates fundamental rights.
  • States directed to identify and free eligible detainees within 48 hours.

The Supreme Court on Tuesday directed all state governments to immediately release protesters detained during the recent CJP (Communist Party of India) agitation who have no criminal background. The bench observed that holding such individuals in custody is “illegal and unconstitutional.”

Court's Strong Observations

A division bench of Justices Hima Kohli and Ahsanuddin Amanullah made the remarks while hearing a batch of petitions challenging the prolonged detention of participants in the CJP-led stir. The court noted that the right to peaceful protest is a fundamental right under Article 19(1)(b) of the Constitution.

“The state cannot label all protesters as criminals. Those without any criminal antecedents must be set free immediately,” the bench said. The court warned that continuing to detain such persons would amount to “executive overreach.”

States Directed to Act Fast

The top court directed all state governments to identify and release eligible detainees within 48 hours. It asked authorities to submit an affidavit detailing the steps taken and the number of people set free.

“We are not shutting our eyes to law and order. Those with criminal records can be dealt with under the law. But the rest cannot be held hostage,” the bench added. The order comes amid reports that over 2,000 people are still in custody across several states following the CJP protests.

Background of the Protests

The CJP agitation, which began in early July, saw thousands taking to the streets demanding agrarian reforms and better wages for farm labour. The protests turned violent in some places, leading to clashes with police and mass arrests.

However, rights groups and opposition parties have alleged that the state machinery “indiscriminately” picked up peaceful demonstrators and held them without charge. The Supreme Court’s intervention follows multiple habeas corpus petitions filed by families of the detained.

What Happens Next

The court has posted the matter for further hearing next week to monitor compliance. It has also sought a report from the National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) on the conditions in detention centres where protesters are being held.

States are now scrambling to comply with the order, with several already forming committees to verify the criminal records of detainees. The final count of those to be released will become clear only after the 48-hour deadline expires.
