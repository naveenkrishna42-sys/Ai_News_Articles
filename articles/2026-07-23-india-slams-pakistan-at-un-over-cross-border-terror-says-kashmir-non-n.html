India Slams Pakistan at UN Over Cross-Border Terror, Says Kashmir Non-Negotiable

📅 2026-07-23 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's UN envoy Harish condemned Pakistan for cross-border terrorism at the UN Security Council.
  • Envoy stated that Jammu and Kashmir is an integral and inalienable part of India.
  • Pakistan's attempts to raise the Kashmir issue at the UN were firmly rejected by India.
  • Pakistan also sought Canada's support on the Indus Waters Treaty but was rebuffed.

India Delivers Sharp Rebuke at UN

India delivered a blistering response to Pakistan at the United Nations on Wednesday, telling Islamabad to 'put its own house in order' instead of lecturing others. The strong remarks came during a UN Security Council session where Pakistan attempted to raise the issue of Jammu and Kashmir.

India's Permanent Representative to the UN, Ambassador Harish, did not mince words. He categorically stated that Jammu and Kashmir is an integral and inalienable part of India. 'There is no dispute. There is no room for discussion,' he told the council.

Direct Message to Pakistan

The Indian envoy accused Pakistan of being the epicentre of cross-border terrorism. He pointed out that Islamabad's track record on human rights and democracy is far from exemplary. 'Pakistan should look inward and focus on its own problems before making baseless allegations against India,' Harish said.

He reminded the international community that terrorist groups operating from Pakistani soil continue to threaten regional peace. 'The world has seen how terrorism is used as a state policy by certain countries. India will not remain silent,' he added.

Pakistan's Indus Waters Treaty Gambit Fails

In a related development, Pakistan's diplomatic push on another front also hit a dead end. Islamabad had sought Canada's support for the restoration of the Indus Waters Treaty with India. However, Ottawa gave a firm rebuff to the Pakistani request.

The Indus Waters Treaty, brokered by the World Bank in 1960, governs the sharing of river waters between the two neighbours. India has consistently maintained that the treaty remains in force and that any issues must be resolved through bilateral mechanisms.

Canada's refusal to back Pakistan's position is seen as a diplomatic setback for Islamabad. It also underscores the growing isolation of Pakistan on issues it tries to internationalise against India.

Kashmir: A Settled Matter

India's position on Kashmir remains unchanged and unambiguous. Ambassador Harish reminded the UN that the region's status was settled after the abrogation of Article 370 in August 2019. 'Kashmir is, was, and will always be an integral part of India,' he asserted.

Pakistan's repeated attempts to raise the matter at international forums have drawn sharp criticism from New Delhi. India has consistently advised Pakistan to stop exporting terror and focus on the welfare of its own people, especially those in the restive Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa provinces.

The UN session saw several member states backing India's stance, with some calling for concrete action against cross-border terrorism.

What to Watch Next

India is expected to continue its diplomatic offensive against Pakistan at upcoming multilateral meetings, including the UN General Assembly session later this year. Meanwhile, the Indus Waters Treaty issue is likely to see further bilateral talks, with India insisting on strict adherence to existing mechanisms.
