CWG 2026: New approach behind India's track & field surge

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's track & field medals at CWG 2026 reflect a new training and support system.
  • IOA chief PT Usha credits 'Nari Shakti' for the women's gold rush.
  • Officials call it the best performance outside India, with sustained progress expected.
  • The approach focuses on scientific coaching, infrastructure, and athlete welfare.

India's track and field contingent at the 2026 Commonwealth Games has turned heads, and officials insist this is no flash in the pan. With a medal haul that ranks among the country's best on foreign soil, the performance points to a deliberate overhaul in how athletes are trained, supported, and prepared for international competition. The results, they say, are the product of a system built over years, not a lucky streak.

A shift in strategy

The change is visible in the numbers. India's athletics squad has logged multiple podium finishes across sprints, jumps, and throws, a spread that suggests depth rather than dependence on a single star. Behind this lies a restructured approach: scientific training protocols, foreign exposure trips, and a sharper focus on recovery and nutrition.

Coaches and federation insiders describe a move away from ad-hoc preparation. Athletes now follow periodised training cycles, with physios and biomechanics experts attached to the core group. This, they say, has reduced injuries and improved consistency in peak seasons.

'Nari Shakti' leads the charge

Indian Olympic Association chief PT Usha has been quick to credit the women athletes, coining the term 'Nari Shakti' to describe their dominance. Women's events have contributed a significant share of the track and field medals, with gold-winning performances that have inspired a generation back home.

Usha, herself a former sprint icon, has emphasised that this is a collective triumph. She pointed to grassroots talent identification programmes that have begun to bear fruit, particularly in states with strong athletics cultures. The federation's push to scout rural talent and provide them with residential coaching is now showing returns.

Best outside India

IOA officials and Athletics Federation of India (AFI) leaders have described the CWG 2026 showing as the best ever on foreign soil. Sumariwalla, a key official, noted that the progress has been continual, with each major championship since the last Tokyo Olympics yielding more finalists and medalists.

The improvement is not just in medals. Several Indian athletes have set personal bests and national records during the Games, a sign that the system is enabling peak performance when it matters. The federation's decision to hold high-altitude camps and send relay teams for overseas competitions has paid off in close finishes.

Beyond the podium

What excites officials more is the pipeline. Young athletes in under-20 and under-23 categories have clocked times and distances that would have been unthinkable a decade ago. The federation has also invested in better track facilities in multiple cities, reducing the reliance on a single training hub.

Corporate sponsorships and the government's Target Olympic Podium Scheme have provided financial stability, allowing athletes to train full-time without worrying about day jobs. Mental conditioning, once a neglected area, is now a standard part of the support staff.

The challenge ahead is sustainability. Experts caution that one good Games does not guarantee long-term dominance, and the competition at the Asian Games and Olympics will be stiffer. However, the structural changes suggest that India's athletics rise is built on foundations that can endure.

As the CWG 2026 draws to a close, the focus shifts to the next major events. The federation has already announced plans to review performances, tweak training modules, and expand the talent pool further. With the system in place, Indian track and field may no longer be an occasional surprise but a consistent contender on the world stage.
