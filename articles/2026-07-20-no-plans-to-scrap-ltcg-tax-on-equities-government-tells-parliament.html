No Plans to Scrap LTCG Tax on Equities, Government Tells Parliament

📅 2026-07-20 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government says no proposal to scrap LTCG tax on equities currently.
  • Clarification came in response to a query in Lok Sabha.
  • LTCG tax on equity over Rs 1 lakh was reintroduced in 2018.

The government has no active plan to remove the long-term capital gains (LTCG) tax on equities, the finance ministry told Parliament on Monday. The clarification comes amid recurring speculation in financial markets that the tax could be scrapped to boost investor sentiment.

In a written reply to the Lok Sabha, the ministry confirmed that there is currently no proposal under consideration to amend the provisions related to LTCG tax on listed equity shares. The statement puts to rest rumours that had been circulating in trading circles for weeks.

Tax on Equity Gains Over Rs 1 Lakh

Under current rules, gains exceeding Rs 1 lakh from the sale of listed equity shares or equity-oriented mutual funds held for more than 12 months are taxed at 10 per cent. This tax was reintroduced in the 2018 Union Budget after a gap of over a decade, during which long-term equity gains were completely exempt.

The government had then argued that the tax was necessary to broaden the revenue base and ensure that wealthy investors contributed their fair share. Since its reintroduction, the LTCG tax has remained a point of debate among market participants and tax experts.

Industry Calls for Review

Several industry bodies and investment advisors have periodically urged the government to reconsider the tax, claiming it discourages long-term savings in equities. They argue that removing it could channel more household savings into the capital markets and support economic growth.

However, the finance ministry's latest response indicates no immediate move in that direction. Officials have not elaborated on whether the matter might be revisited in future budgets.

Market Impact and Outlook

The clarification is unlikely to trigger a sharp reaction in the markets, as analysts say the LTCG tax was never expected to be withdrawn in the near term. The government's focus remains on fiscal consolidation and meeting revenue targets, making any tax concession on capital gains a low priority for now.

Investors and market watchers will now look to the next full budget for any signals on tax policy changes. For the moment, the status quo remains firmly in place.
