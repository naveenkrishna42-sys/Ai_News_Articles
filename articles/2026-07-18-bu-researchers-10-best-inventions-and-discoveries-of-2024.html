BU Researchers' 10 Best Inventions and Discoveries of 2024

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Inventions Original source ↗
Key points
  • BU researchers made breakthroughs in health, tech, and sustainability in 2024.
  • List includes innovations like a lung cancer vaccine and plastic-degrading enzymes.
  • Each project highlights BU's focus on real-world impact and interdisciplinary work.

Boston University's research magazine, The Brink, has released its annual list of the 10 most inspiring inventions and discoveries from BU researchers in 2024. The roundup covers a wide range of fields, from medicine and engineering to environmental science and artificial intelligence.

Health innovations lead the pack

Several entries focus on healthcare breakthroughs. A team at BU developed a vaccine candidate for lung cancer, targeting a protein found in many tumors. Early trials in mice showed promise, with researchers now seeking funding for human trials.

Another project involved a wearable device that monitors blood sugar levels through sweat, eliminating the need for finger pricks. The sensor is designed to be cheap and disposable, potentially helping millions of diabetics in India and beyond.

Tech and sustainability solutions

In the tech space, BU engineers created a new type of battery that uses seawater as an electrolyte. The battery is non-flammable and can be recharged thousands of times, offering a safer alternative for grid storage.

Environmental breakthroughs include a plastic-eating enzyme discovered by BU biologists. The enzyme breaks down PET plastic into its original components, which can then be recycled into new plastic. The team says the process works at room temperature, making it energy-efficient.

AI and social impact

BU computer scientists developed an AI tool that can detect early signs of depression from social media posts. The tool analyzes language patterns and has been tested on data from Reddit and Twitter. Researchers stress the tool is not meant to replace doctors but could help flag at-risk individuals.

Another project uses satellite imagery and machine learning to map illegal fishing in real time. The system has already been deployed in Southeast Asia and is being expanded to other regions.

Other notable mentions

The list includes a gene-editing technique that corrects mutations in human cells with higher precision than CRISPR. A team of BU physicists also demonstrated a new method for quantum computing that reduces errors by 90%.

A low-cost water filter made from rice husks, developed by BU engineers, can remove arsenic and lead. Tests show it works for over 100 litres before needing replacement.

Rounding out the list is a project on urban farming: a vertical garden system that uses AI to optimize light and water for each plant. The system has been installed in several Boston schools and community centres.

BU officials said the list reflects the university's commitment to research that tackles real-world problems. "These projects show how curiosity-driven science can lead to practical solutions," a spokesperson said.

What to watch: Several of these projects are moving toward commercialisation or field trials in 2025. BU plans to announce partnerships with Indian research institutes for the water filter and battery projects later this year.
