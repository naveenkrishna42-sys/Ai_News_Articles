Trump warns Iran: US will destroy bridge or power plant for each attack in Strait of Hormuz

📅 2026-07-23 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Trump says US will destroy a bridge or power plant for each Iranian attack in the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Oil prices rise after Marco Rubio says Iran is not serious about a deal.
  • Rubio says Iran keeps reneging on deals while 'paying the price'.

Former US President Donald Trump has issued a stark warning to Iran, stating that the United States will destroy a bridge or a power plant for each attack carried out by Iranian forces in the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz. The comments come amid escalating tensions in West Asia, with global condemnation of threats posed by Houthi rebels and ongoing concerns over maritime security.

Trump's warning: Infrastructure as a target

Speaking to reporters, Trump said the US would respond to any Iranian aggression in the Strait of Hormuz by targeting critical infrastructure. "For every attack they make, we'll take out a bridge or a power plant," he said. The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway between the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, through which about a fifth of the world's oil passes.

The warning signals a potential escalation in the US approach to Iran, moving beyond naval skirmishes to direct strikes on civilian infrastructure. Analysts say such a strategy could widen the conflict and draw in regional powers.

Oil prices rise as diplomacy stalls

Oil prices have climbed following comments from US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who said Iran is not serious about reaching a nuclear deal. Rubio told reporters that Tehran is "begging" for an agreement but is unwilling to make the necessary concessions. "Iran keeps reneging on deals while paying the price," Rubio said, without elaborating on specific penalties.

The rise in crude prices reflects market anxiety over potential supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz. Traders are bracing for possible blockades or attacks on tankers, which could send prices higher. The US and its allies have increased naval patrols in the region, but the situation remains volatile.

Global condemnation of Houthi threats

The warning from Trump comes against a backdrop of international criticism of Houthi rebels in Yemen, who have threatened shipping in the Red Sea and the Arabian Sea. The Houthis, backed by Iran, have launched drones and missiles at commercial vessels, forcing some shipping companies to reroute cargo around Africa.

The United Nations and several Western nations have condemned the Houthi attacks, calling them a violation of international law. However, efforts to broker a ceasefire in Yemen have so far failed, with the Houthis demanding an end to the Saudi-led blockade as a precondition for talks.

Rubio: Iran 'begging' but not ready

Rubio's assessment of Iran's negotiating posture has added to the uncertainty. He said Iran's leadership is under domestic pressure but is not prepared to make a deal that would satisfy US demands. "They are begging for a deal, but they are not ready to make one," Rubio said, accusing Tehran of using talks as a stalling tactic.

Iran has denied the allegations, insisting it is serious about negotiations. However, the US has maintained that any agreement must include strict limits on Iran's nuclear programme and its ballistic missile development. Talks in Vienna have been suspended for months, with no new date set for their resumption.

What happens next will depend on whether Iran tests Trump's warning. Any attack in the Strait of Hormuz could trigger a swift and severe response, potentially plunging the region into a broader conflict. For now, the world watches and waits.
