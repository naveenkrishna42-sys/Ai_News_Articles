Anurag Thakur files privilege notice against Rahul Gandhi over Lok Sabha speech

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • BJP MP Anurag Thakur filed a privilege notice against Rahul Gandhi.
  • The notice stems from remarks Gandhi made in the Lok Sabha regarding Amit Shah.
  • Gandhi had called for the removal of Amit Shah over alleged police brutality on student protesters.

Privilege notice filed in Lok Sabha

Bharatiya Janata Party MP Anurag Thakur has moved a privilege notice against Congress leader Rahul Gandhi in the Lok Sabha. The notice accuses Gandhi of making false and deliberate misstatements during his speech in the House.

Thakur, a former Union minister, alleged that Gandhi's remarks were aimed at misleading the Parliament and lowering its dignity. The notice was submitted to the Speaker's office on Thursday.

Remarks that sparked the move

The privilege notice comes days after Rahul Gandhi, during a debate in the Lok Sabha, demanded that Prime Minister Narendra Modi sack Home Minister Amit Shah. Gandhi linked his demand to incidents of alleged police brutality against student protesters in recent weeks.

Gandhi had also accused Amit Shah of being absent from Parliament, claiming it “reeks of guilt”. He further attacked the RSS over its role in the handling of protests related to the NEET exam controversy.

Opposition sets agenda

Political observers noted that Gandhi's speech had effectively set the opposition's agenda in Parliament. By focusing on the home minister and the RSS, the Congress leader attempted to corner the government on law and order and student rights.

The opposition has been pressing for a discussion on the handling of student protests, with several parties demanding Shah's statement in the House. The privilege notice is seen by many as a countermove by the BJP to turn the tables on Gandhi.

What happens next

The Lok Sabha Speaker will now examine the privilege notice and decide whether to refer it to the Privileges Committee. If admitted, Gandhi could be asked to explain his remarks or face a formal inquiry.

Both the ruling party and the opposition are expected to continue their sparring over the issue in the coming days. The House proceedings are likely to see heated exchanges as the matter unfolds.
