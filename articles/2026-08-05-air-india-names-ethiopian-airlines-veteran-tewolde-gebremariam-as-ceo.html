Air India Names Ethiopian Airlines Veteran Tewolde Gebremariam as CEO

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tewolde Gebremariam appointed Air India CEO
  • Succeeds Campbell Wilson
  • Former Ethiopian Airlines chief brings global aviation experience

Air India has appointed Tewolde Gebremariam as its new chief executive officer, succeeding Campbell Wilson, who is stepping down after a tenure marked by the airline's ambitious revival under the Tata Group. The announcement, made on Wednesday, ends weeks of speculation about the leadership transition at the national carrier.

A veteran at the helm

Gebremariam is no stranger to the complexities of running a major airline. He spent over a decade leading Ethiopian Airlines, transforming it into one of Africa's most successful and profitable carriers. His track record includes expanding the airline's network across five continents and steering it through turbulent periods in global aviation.

The appointment signals Air India's intent to leverage his deep operational expertise and international connections as it seeks to strengthen its position in the competitive global market. Gebremariam's experience in building a hub-and-spoke model at Addis Ababa could prove valuable for Air India's own hub strategy at Delhi and Mumbai.

The Wilson era ends

Campbell Wilson's exit comes after a little over two years at the helm. He was brought in by the Tata Group in 2022, tasked with overhauling an airline that had been saddled with years of losses and operational inefficiencies. During his tenure, Wilson focused on modernising the fleet, improving on-time performance, and revamping customer service—efforts that began to show results, though the journey was far from complete.

The exact reasons for Wilson's departure have not been officially detailed, but industry insiders suggest that the board may have wanted a different leadership style as the airline enters a new phase of consolidation and expansion. Wilson is expected to remain with the group in an advisory capacity for a brief period to ensure a smooth handover.

Challenges ahead for the new CEO

Gebremariam inherits an airline that is in a much better shape than the one Wilson took over, but significant hurdles remain. Air India is in the middle of a massive fleet modernisation programme, with orders for hundreds of new aircraft from Airbus and Boeing. Managing these deliveries and integrating them into the existing network will be a key priority.

There is also the question of the merger with Vistara, the joint venture with Singapore Airlines, which is expected to be completed in the coming months. The integration of the two airlines' operations, staff, and systems is a complex process that could define the carrier's trajectory for years to come. Gebremariam's experience in managing growth and operational efficiency will be tested on this front.

Moreover, the airline continues to face stiff competition from both full-service carriers like IndiGo and international players on long-haul routes. The new CEO will need to strike a balance between aggressive expansion and financial prudence, especially as the aviation industry contends with fluctuating fuel prices and supply chain disruptions.

What to watch

Industry observers will be watching how quickly Gebremariam settles into the role and whether he brings in his own team to support the transition. The Tata Group has not indicated any immediate changes to the top management structure, but that could evolve in the coming months. Also, the airline's response to the ongoing Boeing delivery delays and its plans for the Vistara merger will be early indicators of his approach.

As Air India enters this new chapter, the stakes are high. The carrier is not just a business; it is a symbol of India's global aspirations. Gebremariam's success will depend on his ability to build on the foundations laid by his predecessor while charting a course that is distinctly his own.
