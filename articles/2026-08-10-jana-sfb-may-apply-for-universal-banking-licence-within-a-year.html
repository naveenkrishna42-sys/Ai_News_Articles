Jana SFB may apply for universal banking licence within a year

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Jana Small Finance Bank may apply for universal banking licence within a year.
  • The move follows RBI's draft guidelines allowing SFBs to convert to universal banks.
  • The bank will evaluate capital requirements and business model before applying.

Jana Small Finance Bank is considering applying for a universal banking licence within the next year, a top official said, as the lender looks to expand its scope of operations and tap into a broader customer base.

The announcement comes on the back of the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) draft guidelines that permit small finance banks (SFBs) to transition into universal banks, provided they meet certain eligibility criteria. The proposed framework is seen as a major step in the gradual liberalisation of India's banking sector.

What the RBI has proposed

Under the draft norms, SFBs with a minimum net worth of Rs 1,000 crore and a satisfactory track record of at least five years can apply for a universal banking licence. The central bank has also set out a phased roadmap for compliance with priority sector lending and other regulatory requirements.

Jana SFB, which began operations in 2018, has been steadily growing its balance sheet and branch network. The bank's management believes it is well-positioned to meet the eligibility norms, though a final decision will depend on a detailed internal review.

What a universal licence would mean for Jana

A universal banking licence would allow Jana to offer a wider range of products, including current accounts, trade finance, and foreign exchange services, which are currently restricted for SFBs. It would also enable the bank to lend to larger corporate clients, diversifying its asset book beyond the microfinance and small-business segments.

The transition, however, would require the bank to maintain higher capital adequacy ratios and comply with stricter regulatory norms. Analysts say the move could improve profitability in the long run, but would also bring increased competition from established private and public sector banks.

Timeline and next steps

According to the official, the bank is likely to take a final call on the application within the next 12 months. The decision will hinge on the final guidelines issued by the RBI, which are expected after the consultation process is complete.

“We are evaluating the implications of the draft framework on our business model, capital requirements, and technology investments,” the official said, adding that the bank is also in talks with potential investors to shore up its capital base ahead of any licence application.

Jana SFB is not alone in this pursuit. Several other small finance banks, including AU SFB and Equitas SFB, have indicated interest in upgrading to universal banking licences, setting the stage for a wave of consolidation and expansion in India's banking sector.

The final guidelines from the RBI are expected within the next few months, and the central bank's stance on the pace of such transitions will be closely watched by the industry.

As the regulatory framework takes shape, Jana SFB's next move will depend on how quickly it can align its operations with the stringent requirements of a universal bank. The coming year will be crucial for the lender as it navigates this transition, with the final decision likely to reshape its growth trajectory.
