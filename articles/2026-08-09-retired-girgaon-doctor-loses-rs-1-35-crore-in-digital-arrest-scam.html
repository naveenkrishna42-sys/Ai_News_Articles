Retired Girgaon doctor loses Rs 1.35 crore in digital arrest scam

📅 2026-08-09 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Retired Girgaon doctor lost Rs 1.35 crore in digital arrest scam
  • Fraudsters posed as law enforcement and kept victim on video call
  • Mumbai cyber police have registered a case and launched probe

A retired doctor from Girgaon has lost Rs 1.35 crore to a 'digital arrest' scam, the latest instance of a fraud that continues to target elderly citizens across Mumbai. The victim, in his 70s, was allegedly kept under virtual confinement for days by callers posing as law enforcement officials, police said on Saturday.

The doctor, who practised in Girgaon for decades before retiring, received a call earlier this week from someone claiming to be a police officer. The caller told him that a parcel sent in his name had been intercepted with illegal items, and that he was now part of a money-laundering investigation.

Fake officials, real fear

The fraudsters, police said, used a video-calling app to show the doctor a fake police station backdrop, complete with officers in uniform. They threatened him with arrest and seizure of his property unless he cooperated with the 'investigation'.

Over the next few days, the doctor was instructed to stay on the video call at all times, a tactic that cyber experts say is designed to isolate the victim from family and friends. He was told to transfer funds to what he was assured were official government accounts for verification.

In total, the doctor transferred Rs 1.35 crore across multiple transactions, exhausting his savings and retirement corpus. It was only after the callers stopped responding that he realised he had been cheated and approached the police.

Case registered, probe underway

The Mumbai cyber police have registered a case under relevant sections of the Indian Penal Code and the Information Technology Act. Officials have not yet confirmed whether any arrests have been made or if the money trail has been traced.

Investigators are now looking into the phone numbers and bank accounts used in the fraud, which are often opened with forged documents. Similar cases have been reported across the city in recent months, with fraudsters increasingly using the 'digital arrest' ruse to target senior citizens who are less familiar with such tactics.

What is a digital arrest?

In a digital arrest scam, fraudsters impersonate police, customs, or other authorities and claim the victim is under investigation. They then force the victim to stay on a video call, effectively placing them under 'digital arrest', while demanding money to avoid legal action.

Police have repeatedly warned that no legitimate agency conducts arrests or investigations over video calls. They have urged citizens to hang up immediately if they receive such calls and to report them to the cyber helpline at 1930.

A pattern of targeting the elderly

This is not an isolated case. Earlier this year, a retired professor in Bandra lost Rs 80 lakh in a similar fraud, and a senior citizen in Dadar was duped of Rs 50 lakh after receiving a fake notice from a customs officer. In most instances, the victims are educated but not well-versed in cyber fraud tactics.

Cyber crime experts say the emotional manipulation is key. The fraudsters create a sense of urgency and fear, making it difficult for victims to think clearly. They also instruct victims not to tell anyone, including family, about the 'investigation'.

The Girgaon doctor's case highlights how even retired professionals with decades of experience can fall prey to such schemes. Police have urged families to talk to elderly relatives about these scams and to encourage them to verify any such calls with local authorities before acting.

As the probe continues, the focus will be on tracing the money and identifying the gang behind the calls. Investigators are also working with banks to freeze accounts where the funds were parked, though officials admit that recovering the amount may take time.

For now, the doctor is left to grapple with the loss of a lifetime of savings. The case is a stark reminder that in the digital age, a uniform on a screen is no proof of authority.
