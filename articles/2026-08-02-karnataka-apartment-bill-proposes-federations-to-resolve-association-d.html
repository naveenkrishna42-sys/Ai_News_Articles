Karnataka apartment Bill proposes federations to resolve association disputes

📅 2026-08-02 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bill proposes federations of resident welfare associations to resolve disputes
  • Aimed at large apartment complexes with multiple associations
  • Karnataka government yet to finalise the draft legislation

Proposal aims to streamline governance in large complexes

The Karnataka government's proposed apartment Bill includes a significant provision to create federations of resident welfare associations (RWAs) within large housing complexes. This move is designed to tackle recurring disputes that arise when multiple associations operate independently in a single layout or project.

Officials familiar with the draft say the federations would act as an umbrella body to coordinate common issues such as maintenance, security, and civic amenities. The aim is to reduce friction among associations that often have overlapping jurisdiction and conflicting interests.

Why the need for federations?

In many Bengaluru apartment complexes, especially those with multiple towers or phases, separate RWAs are formed for each block or section. This has led to disagreements over shared infrastructure, common area maintenance, and even election of office bearers.

Residents have frequently complained about the lack of a unified voice when dealing with builders, municipal authorities, and utility providers. The proposed federations would provide a single representative body, potentially simplifying decision-making and dispute resolution.

What the Bill entails

The draft Bill, which is yet to be tabled in the state legislature, outlines a structure for forming federations with elected representatives from each member association. It also proposes clear rules for their registration, powers, and responsibilities.

Legal experts point out that the provision could reduce the burden on courts and tribunals, which are often flooded with cases of association disputes. However, some residents' groups have expressed concerns about the potential for the federations to override the autonomy of individual associations.

Stakeholder reactions

While the government has not released the full text of the Bill, preliminary discussions suggest that it has received mixed feedback. RWA leaders in the city have welcomed the idea but want clarity on the voting rights and dispute resolution mechanisms.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the Bill will be introduced in the upcoming session of the Karnataka legislature. A consultation process with stakeholders is expected before the final draft is prepared.

What to watch next

All eyes will be on the state government's next steps as it fine-tunes the Bill. If passed, the legislation could set a precedent for other states grappling with similar issues in multi-association apartment complexes.
