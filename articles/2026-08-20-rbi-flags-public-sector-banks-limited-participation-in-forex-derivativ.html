RBI flags public sector banks' limited participation in forex derivatives market

📅 2026-08-20 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI Deputy Governor flags underparticipation by public sector banks in forex derivatives
  • Forex derivatives help corporates and banks hedge currency risk
  • Participation gap raises concerns about risk management readiness
  • Regulatory push expected to encourage broader derivatives adoption

RBI flags participation gap in currency markets

The Reserve Bank of India has drawn attention to public sector banks' limited engagement with the foreign exchange derivatives market, a gap that could affect their ability to manage currency risk effectively.

Speaking on the issue, RBI Deputy Governor Jain noted that state-owned banks have been underrepresented in derivatives trading compared to their private sector peers. The observation comes at a time when currency market volatility has made hedging tools increasingly relevant for banks and their corporate clients.

Understanding the derivatives gap

Forex derivatives are financial instruments that allow banks and their customers to hedge against adverse currency movements. These include forward contracts, options, and swaps that lock in exchange rates for future transactions.

Industry experts suggest that public sector banks have historically relied more on traditional lending models. Their participation in complex derivative products has remained lower, partly due to risk aversion and partly due to legacy systems that were not designed for sophisticated treasury operations.

Private banks, with their more agile technology platforms and aggressive treasury desks, have captured a larger share of the derivatives market. This divergence has widened over the past few years as global capital flows and rupee volatility increased.

Implications for risk management

The underparticipation raises practical concerns. When public sector banks lack depth in derivatives expertise, their ability to offer currency hedging solutions to exporters, importers, and other corporate clients becomes limited. This can leave Indian businesses more exposed to exchange rate fluctuations.

Additionally, a shallow derivatives market reduces liquidity and price discovery, making it harder for all participants to get competitive rates. The central bank has long encouraged wider adoption of hedging mechanisms to strengthen the overall financial system's resilience.

Regulatory push expected

Officials have indicated that the RBI may look at measures to encourage greater participation. Training initiatives, simplified product structures, and technology upgrades could feature in any future push. The goal would be to bring public sector banks closer to the level of sophistication already present in private sector institutions.

Some analysts suggest that mandatory hedging requirements for certain categories of foreign currency exposure could also drive volume. However, no formal announcements have been made regarding specific policy changes.

Market outlook

The forex derivatives market in India has grown steadily, supported by liberalised capital account policies and increased cross-border trade. As the economy integrates further with global financial systems, the need for robust hedging mechanisms will only increase.

Stakeholders will watch for any formal guidance from the RBI on steps to bridge the participation gap. Whether through capacity building programmes or regulatory incentives, bringing public sector banks up to speed in derivatives trading is emerging as a priority for the central bank.
