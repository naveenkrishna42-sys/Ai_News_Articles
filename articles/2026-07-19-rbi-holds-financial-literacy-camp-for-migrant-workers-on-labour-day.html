RBI Holds Financial Literacy Camp for Migrant Workers on Labour Day

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • RBI conducted a financial literacy camp for migrant workers on International Labour Day.
  • Topics included basic banking, digital payments, and government social security schemes.
  • The initiative aims to promote financial inclusion among vulnerable worker groups.

Camp Held on Labour Day

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) organised a financial literacy camp for migrant workers on International Labour Day, observed on 1 May. The camp was part of the central bank's ongoing efforts to extend banking and financial services to underserved sections of the population.

Migrant workers, who often lack access to formal banking channels, were the focus of the initiative. The camp aimed to educate them on the basics of savings, credit, and digital transactions.

Key Topics Covered

Participants were briefed on how to open a bank account, use mobile banking apps, and make digital payments safely. The sessions also covered the importance of linking Aadhaar with bank accounts and using the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) for daily transactions.

Financial counsellors explained the benefits of government social security schemes, including the Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY), Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY), and Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY). These schemes offer insurance cover and pension benefits at low premiums.

Addressing Common Challenges

Migrant workers face unique financial hurdles, such as lack of proper identification documents, frequent relocation, and limited digital literacy. The camp addressed these issues by providing step-by-step guidance on documentation and account operation.

RBI officials demonstrated how to check account balances, transfer money, and avoid common frauds like phishing and unauthorised SIM swaps. Pamphlets in multiple languages were distributed to reinforce the learning.

Partnerships and Outreach

The camp was conducted in collaboration with local banks and non-profit organisations working with migrant communities. Similar camps are planned in industrial clusters and construction sites across major cities.

The initiative aligns with the government's financial inclusion agenda, which aims to bring every adult into the formal banking system. RBI has been running financial literacy programmes for various groups, including women, farmers, and small business owners.

Response from Participants

Many workers said they had never visited a bank before and were unaware of government schemes available to them. Several opened bank accounts on the spot with the help of camp volunteers.

One participant, a construction worker from Bihar, said he used to send money home through informal channels. After the camp, he plans to use UPI for remittances.

RBI has not yet disclosed the exact number of workers who attended or the total number of accounts opened during the camp.

Additional camps are expected to be held in the coming months, particularly in states with high migrant worker populations such as Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, and Karnataka. The central bank is also working on a digital literacy module tailored for low-literacy users.
