Modi Puts ASEAN at the Core of Act East Policy Ahead of Philippines Visit

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Desk 📅 2026-11-16 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • PM Modi's departure statement frames ASEAN as the central pillar of India's Act East Policy, elevating the 12-year-old framework ahead of the Philippines summit.
  • India-ASEAN goods trade is worth roughly $131 billion, but a widening deficit and a stalled FTA review remain the hard commercial reality behind the diplomacy.
  • Maritime security, freedom of navigation in the South China Sea and the slow-moving India-Myanmar-Thailand Trilateral Highway will shape the summit's deliverables.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi has placed India's relationship with the Association of Southeast Asian Nations at the centre of the Act East Policy, framing the 10-nation bloc as the strategic and economic anchor of New Delhi's eastern engagement. In his departure statement ahead of a visit to the Philippines, the Prime Minister linked the Act East framework directly to ASEAN, signalling that the outreach launched in 2014 is now being measured less by ceremony and more by trade, connectivity and security deliverables.

Why the Philippines Summit Carries Extra Weight

The Philippines holds the ASEAN chairmanship in 2026, which makes this summit cycle unusually consequential for India. Manila's priorities — maritime security, supply-chain resilience and digital economy rules — overlap closely with Indian interests, giving officials a workable agenda rather than a communiqué exercise.

India's institutional ties with the bloc run deep. Sectoral dialogue began in 1992, full dialogue partner status followed in 1995, and the relationship was elevated to a strategic partnership in 2012. The Act East Policy, unveiled in 2014 as a sharper successor to the 1991 Look East Policy, is the operating framework that converts those tiers into projects.

The Trade Arithmetic Behind the Diplomacy

Commerce remains the weakest link in an otherwise expanding partnership. ASEAN is among India's largest trading partners as a bloc, yet the balance sheet is lopsided: imports outpace exports, and Indian exporters complain that tariff elimination under the goods agreement has not been matched by market access for services and professionals.

The immediate test is whether the FTA review produces credible outcomes on rules of origin and non-tariff barriers. For Indian manufacturers of auto components, pharmaceuticals and textiles, a rebalanced agreement is worth more than any joint statement.

Maritime Security and the South China Sea

New Delhi has consistently backed freedom of navigation, unimpeded lawful commerce and the peaceful resolution of disputes in the South China Sea. That position aligns India with several ASEAN claimant states without drawing it into the dispute itself. Expect the summit to push defence-industrial cooperation, white-shipping coordination and maritime domain awareness funding rather than any formal security bloc.

Connectivity: The Slow Lane

Physical links have lagged rhetoric. The Trilateral Highway has missed repeated deadlines, and the Kaladan corridor is only partially operational. Both projects matter commercially because they shorten transit to Southeast Asian markets and reduce dependence on congested sea routes, but financing gaps and instability in Myanmar have held them back. India's north-eastern states have the most to gain from any acceleration.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Pro: ASEAN offers Indian firms a $3 trillion-plus market and a plug into regional electronics and automotive supply chains.
  • Pro: The Philippines chairmanship gives India a like-minded partner on maritime norms and defence procurement.
  • Pro: Active FTA review creates leverage to fix services mobility and non-tariff barriers that Indian exporters have flagged for years.
  • Con: India runs a persistent trade deficit with the bloc, with limited progress on reciprocal market access.
  • Con: Connectivity projects remain delayed, undercutting the core promise of Act East.
  • Con: ASEAN's consensus-driven style limits how far security language can go, especially on Myanmar.

What to Watch After the Summit

Three signals will reveal whether this visit moves the needle: a dated roadmap for the FTA review, fresh disbursements from the ASEAN–India Fund into digital and green projects, and any financing commitment that restarts construction on the Trilateral Highway. Without those, the ASEAN-first framing stays a diplomatic posture rather than an economic strategy.
