TNUHDB flats show serious structural defects within four years

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Flats in a TNUHDB complex developed structural defects within four years of completion.
  • Residents blame poor construction quality and substandard materials for the damage.
  • TNUHDB officials have not yet responded to the complaints or announced an inspection.

Residents raise alarm over cracks in new TNUHDB flats

Just four years after moving in, residents of a Tamil Nadu Urban Habitat Development Board (TNUHDB) housing complex in Chennai are facing a nightmare. Large cracks have appeared on walls and ceilings, and some structural beams show visible signs of stress. Residents say the problems started within two years of occupation and have worsened rapidly.

Families living in the complex fear for their safety, especially during the monsoon season when water seeps through the cracks. Many have lodged complaints with the TNUHDB office but say they have received no satisfactory response.

Residents blame poor construction quality

Local residents allege that the builder used substandard materials and cut corners during construction. “The plaster is crumbling, and we can see the steel rods rusting. This is not safe for our children,” said a resident who did not wish to be named.

Several families have had to spend their own money on temporary repairs, but the structural issues remain. Residents have formed a committee and plan to approach the Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority (CMDA) for an independent structural audit.

TNUHDB yet to respond

TNUHDB officials have not yet issued a statement on the matter. The board is responsible for affordable housing projects across the state, and this is not the first time quality concerns have been raised. In previous instances, the board has promised stricter oversight, but residents say little has changed on the ground.

An engineer who inspected the building on behalf of the residents said the defects appeared to be due to inadequate curing and poor-quality concrete. He recommended immediate retrofitting to prevent further deterioration.

What residents are demanding

  • A thorough structural audit by an independent agency.
  • Immediate repair work at TNUHDB's cost.
  • Compensation for damages incurred by residents.
  • Transparency in the contractor selection process.

The issue has also caught the attention of local politicians, who have promised to raise the matter in the Chennai Corporation council. However, no formal action has been taken yet.

With the monsoon season approaching, residents are anxious. They want the authorities to act before the damage becomes irreversible. The coming weeks will show whether TNUHDB will step in or leave families to fend for themselves.
