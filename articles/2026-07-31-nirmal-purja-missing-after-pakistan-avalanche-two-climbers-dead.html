Nirmal Purja Missing After Pakistan Avalanche; Two Climbers Dead

📅 2026-07-31 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • Avalanche struck Broad Peak in Pakistan, leaving two climbers dead and six missing.
  • Renowned Nepalese mountaineer Nirmal Purja is among the missing.
  • Search operations are ongoing, with four bodies located so far per some sources.

Avalanche Hits Broad Peak

A deadly avalanche has struck Broad Peak in Pakistan, leaving at least two climbers dead and six others missing, including renowned Nepalese mountaineer Nirmal Purja. The incident occurred on the 8,051-metre peak in the Karakoram range, a favourite among experienced mountaineers aiming for the world's highest summits.

Search teams have recovered two bodies so far, with sources indicating that four bodies have been located in total. The discrepancy in figures reflects the chaos and difficulty of operating at such extreme altitudes, where rescue efforts are often hampered by weather and terrain.

Nirmal Purja Among Missing

Purja, widely known as Nimsdai, shot to global fame in 2019 when he climbed all 14 of the world's 8,000-metre peaks in just over six months, a record that stunned the climbing community. His feats have been documented in films and books, making him one of the most recognisable figures in modern mountaineering.

His presence on Broad Peak was part of an expedition, though details of the team's composition and objective have not been officially confirmed. The Nepalese government has not yet issued a statement, and family members are awaiting updates from Pakistani authorities.

Search Efforts Continue

Rescue operations are underway, with helicopters scouring the mountain's upper slopes where the avalanche struck. However, the extreme altitude and unpredictable weather have slowed progress, and officials have not confirmed the condition of those still missing.

Broad Peak, the 12th highest mountain in the world, is known for its treacherous final ridge and frequent avalanches. The region, part of Pakistan-administered Gilgit-Baltistan, has seen several fatal incidents in recent years, though the climbing season remains popular among international expeditions.

Mountaineering Community in Shock

The news has sent ripples through the global climbing fraternity, which holds Purja in high regard for his daring summits and rescue efforts. Fellow climbers have taken to social media to express concern, though no official statements have been released by his team or sponsors.

Local authorities in Pakistan are coordinating with international agencies to track the missing climbers. The exact number of those caught in the avalanche remains unclear, with some reports suggesting up to 10 climbers were affected.

What Happens Next

As the search enters its critical phase, the focus remains on locating survivors in the avalanche debris. The outcome will hinge on weather conditions over the next 48 hours, and officials are expected to provide further updates once rescue teams return from the mountain.
