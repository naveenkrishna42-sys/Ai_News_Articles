Jamshedpur Hosts First Literature Festival of East Singhbhum at Gopal Maidan

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Culture & Arts Original source ↗
Key points
  • The festival is the first of its kind in the East Singhbhum district, held at Gopal Maidan in Jamshedpur.
  • The event brought together authors, poets, and literary enthusiasts for discussions, readings, and cultural performances.
  • Organizers aim to promote reading habits and showcase local writers and regional languages.

A New Chapter for Jamshedpur

Jamshedpur rang with the sound of poetry, debate, and applause this week as Gopal Maidan hosted East Singhbhum’s first-ever literature festival. The event, simply called the East Singhbhum Literature Festival 2026, marked a milestone for the steel city’s cultural calendar.

Until now, literary gatherings in the region were small, often confined to college campuses or private clubs. This festival brought them into the open, under the winter sun at Gopal Maidan, a central public ground known for sports and political rallies.

Authors, Poets, and Open Mics

The two-day festival featured a mix of established authors and emerging voices. Sessions included panel discussions on contemporary Indian fiction, poetry readings in Hindi, English, and regional dialects, and workshops for aspiring writers.

Organizers said the idea was to create a platform where people could meet authors face-to-face. “People in Jamshedpur read a lot, but they rarely get to interact with writers,” one volunteer told The Avenue Mail. The open mic segments, held in the evenings, saw participation from school students and senior citizens alike.

Spotlight on Regional Literature

A significant portion of the festival was dedicated to literature in local languages, including Santhali and Odia. A small bookstall set up by a local publisher sold out of poetry collections by midday on the first day.

Organizers said they deliberately kept the scale modest to ensure quality interactions. There were no corporate sponsorships. Instead, the festival was funded by a group of local bibliophiles and supported by the district administration, which provided the venue and security.

Why Gopal Maidan Matters

Choosing Gopal Maidan for the venue was a deliberate move. The ground has long been a symbol of public gathering in Jamshedpur. By holding a literature festival there, organisers hoped to signal that literary events are not just for elite auditoriums but for everyone.

The turnout, estimated at over 3,000 visitors across two days, seemed to prove the point. Families with children, college students, and elderly couples walked through the rows of stalls and listened to speakers under temporary shamianas.

What Comes Next

The festival ended with a promise from the organisers to make it an annual event. They are already planning for 2027, with hopes to bring in more publishers and perhaps a literary award for writers from the region.

For now, Jamshedpur has taken its first step towards building a literary identity that matches its industrial one. Whether the momentum holds will depend on how many of the new readers and writers stay engaged through the year.
