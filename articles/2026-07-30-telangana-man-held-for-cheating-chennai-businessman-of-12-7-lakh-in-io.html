Telangana man held for cheating Chennai businessman of ₹12.7 lakh in IOC tender fraud

📅 2026-07-30 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Accused arrested from Telangana by Chennai cybercrime police.
  • Victim duped of ₹12.7 lakh in a fake Indian Oil Corporation tender scam.
  • Fraudsters posed as IOC officials to extract money for contract fees.

The Chennai cybercrime police have arrested a man from Telangana for allegedly duping a city-based businessman of ₹12.7 lakh in a tender fraud. The accused posed as officials from the Indian Oil Corporation (IOC) and promised a lucrative fuel supply contract.

The victim, a Chennai resident, was lured through a fake tender notice. He transferred the money over several weeks, believing he was securing a legitimate business deal.

How the scam unfolded

According to police, the fraud began with an unsolicited call. The caller claimed to be an IOC manager and offered the businessman a contract to supply fuel to retail outlets in Telangana.

The victim was asked to pay processing fees, registration charges, and security deposits. Each payment was made to different bank accounts controlled by the accused.

When the contract failed to materialise and the caller stopped answering, the businessman realised he had been cheated. He filed a complaint with the Chennai cybercrime police in June.

Investigation and arrest

Police tracked the money trail to multiple accounts linked to the accused, a 32-year-old resident of Karimnagar district in Telangana. The cybercrime team arrested him earlier this week from his hometown.

Officials said the accused was part of a larger network that targeted small and medium business owners. He has been remanded to judicial custody.

Investigators are now probing whether the same gang cheated others. They are examining seized mobile phones and bank records to identify accomplices.

Growing threat of tender frauds

Police say tender scams are rising as fraudsters exploit business owners eager for government contracts. They often impersonate officials from public sector companies like IOC, BPCL, or HPCL.

The modus operandi remains similar: fake tender notices, convincing documentation, and repeated demands for payments. Victims rarely recover their money.

Authorities urge business owners to verify tender details directly with the issuing authority. They also advise against making payments to personal bank accounts.

The Chennai police have launched a public awareness campaign on social media. They are asking people to report suspicious calls to the cybercrime helpline.

What to watch: The accused may be brought to Chennai for further questioning. Police are also looking for other members of the gang who remain at large.
