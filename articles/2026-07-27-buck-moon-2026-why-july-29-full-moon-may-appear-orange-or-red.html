Buck Moon 2026: Why July 29 Full Moon May Appear Orange or Red

📅 2026-07-27 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • The Buck Moon will be visible on July 29, 2026, at its fullest in the night sky.
  • The moon may appear orange or red due to low altitude and atmospheric particles scattering blue light.
  • The Delta Aquariid meteor shower peaks on July 30-31, offering a dual celestial event.

What Is the Buck Moon?

The full moon in July is traditionally called the Buck Moon. The name comes from Native American and colonial American folklore, referring to the time when male deer, or bucks, grow new antlers. It is also known as the Thunder Moon, as July often brings frequent thunderstorms.

This year, the Buck Moon will reach its peak fullness on July 29, 2026. Skywatchers in India can see it at its brightest around 10:36 PM IST, though the exact timing depends on local weather conditions.

Will the Moon Look Orange or Red?

Many observers expect the Buck Moon to appear orange or red. This is not a permanent colour change. The moon itself remains grey. The colour shift happens because of its position in the sky.

When the moon is low on the horizon, its light passes through more of Earth's atmosphere. Dust, pollution, and water vapour scatter shorter blue wavelengths, leaving longer red and orange wavelengths to reach your eyes. This is the same reason sunsets look red.

If the sky is clear, the moon may appear a pale orange near the horizon. If there is smoke from wildfires or dust from dry regions, the effect can be stronger, making it look deep red.

How to See the Buck Moon

No special equipment is needed. Find an open area with a clear view of the eastern horizon just after sunset. The moon will rise there. Binoculars or a small telescope can enhance the view of craters and shadows.

In cities, light pollution may wash out some of the colour. For the best experience, head to a darker location away from streetlights. The colour effect is strongest just after moonrise, when the moon is still low.

A Double Celestial Treat

This Buck Moon comes with an extra event. The Delta Aquariid meteor shower will peak on July 30-31, just a day after the full moon. The bright moonlight may wash out fainter meteors, but the brightest ones will still be visible.

The Delta Aquariids produce about 15 to 20 meteors per hour at their peak. They are known for their fast, bright streaks. The best viewing time is after midnight, away from city lights.

What to Watch For

Weather forecasts are not yet available for July 29. Cloud cover could block the view in some regions. Keep an eye on local updates closer to the date.

If you miss the Buck Moon, the next full moon on August 28, 2026, will be the Sturgeon Moon. It will not have the same low-horizon colour effect in most of India.
