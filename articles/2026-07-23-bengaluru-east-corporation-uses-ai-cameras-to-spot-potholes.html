Bengaluru East Corporation uses AI cameras to spot potholes

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • AI cameras mounted on municipal vehicles scan roads for potholes in real time.
  • System geotags each pothole and sends alerts to repair crews automatically.
  • Officials hope to reduce response time from weeks to under 48 hours.

AI cameras mounted on municipal vehicles

Bengaluru East City Corporation has launched a new artificial intelligence system to detect potholes on city roads. The technology uses high-resolution cameras fitted on municipal vehicles that scan the road surface as the vehicles move.

The system analyses images in real time to identify cracks, depressions and full-blown potholes. Once a pothole is spotted, the AI software geotags its exact location and logs the severity of the damage.

Automatic alerts to repair crews

After detection, the system automatically sends a notification to the nearest road repair team. This eliminates the need for residents to file complaints or for officials to manually survey roads.

Corporation officials said the goal is to cut the time between spotting a pothole and repairing it from weeks to under 48 hours. The system also maintains a digital record of all repairs carried out, which helps in monitoring contractor performance.

Pilot phase and expansion plans

The AI deployment is currently in a pilot phase covering major arterial roads in the east zone. Five corporation vehicles have been fitted with the camera units so far.

If the pilot succeeds, the corporation plans to expand the system to all its wards by early next year. Officials are also exploring the use of drones to scan roads in areas where vehicle access is limited.

Residents welcome the move

Local resident welfare associations have welcomed the initiative. Many said manual reporting of potholes often led to delays and ignored complaints.

A senior corporation official said the AI system will also help in planning long-term road maintenance. The data collected will show which stretches develop potholes repeatedly, allowing engineers to redesign those sections.

The corporation has not yet disclosed the total cost of the AI system. Officials said the investment will be recovered through reduced repair delays and better road quality.

Residents can still report potholes through the existing helpline numbers. The corporation has said both channels — manual and AI — will operate in parallel during the pilot phase.

The system is expected to be fully operational within six months. If it performs as planned, other city corporations in Bengaluru may adopt similar AI-driven road monitoring.
