Tamil Nadu to Move Resolution Against Delimitation After All-Party Meet

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tamil Nadu will pass a resolution against the Delimitation Bill after an all-party meeting chaired by CM Vijay.
  • Only 21 of 57 Tamil Nadu MPs attended the meet; DMK and AIADMK stayed away.
  • The resolution seeks a freeze on the number of Lok Sabha seats for the state.
  • AIADMK defended its absence, citing political reasons, as the delimitation debate escalates.

Resolution Against Delimitation

The Tamil Nadu government will move a resolution in the state assembly opposing the proposed Delimitation Bill, following an all-party meeting chaired by Chief Minister Vijay. The decision came after deliberations with attending MPs and party representatives, though the turnout was notably thin.

Only 21 of the state's 57 Lok Sabha MPs participated in the meeting, which concluded with a consensus to formally register the state's objection. The resolution is expected to seek a freeze on the number of parliamentary seats allocated to Tamil Nadu, a move aimed at protecting the state's political weight.

DMK and AIADMK Boycott

The meeting, however, was marked by the absence of two major political forces—the DMK and the AIADMK. Both parties chose to boycott the proceedings, leaving the ruling party to steer the discussions with a reduced representation.

The AIADMK has since defended its decision to stay away, saying it had its own reasons for not attending. Party leaders indicated that their absence should not be read as support for the delimitation exercise, but rather as a separate political strategy to oppose it.

What the Boycott Means

Political observers note that the boycott could dilute the perceived unity of Tamil Nadu's opposition to delimitation. With the DMK and AIADMK absent, the all-party meet's output may carry less weight in negotiations with the central government.

Yet, the state government has pressed ahead, framing the resolution as a collective stance of those who did participate. The chief minister's office has stressed that the move reflects the state's concerns over fair representation.

Escalating Delimitation Debate

The delimitation issue has been simmering for months, with southern states fearing a loss of parliamentary seats relative to northern regions with higher population growth. Tamil Nadu has been among the most vocal in demanding a freeze on the current seat allocation.

Saturday's meeting was intended to forge a unified front, but the boycott has exposed fissures within the state's political spectrum. The DMK's absence is particularly significant, given its historical role in championing federalism and state rights.

Government's Next Step

The state cabinet is likely to formalise the resolution text in the coming days, after which it will be tabled in the assembly. A debate is expected, with all parties given a chance to voice their positions.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the resolution will be binding or merely symbolic, but it is expected to be sent to the central government and the Election Commission for consideration. The state has also hinted at legal action if the delimitation is pushed through without addressing its concerns.

What to Watch

The coming weeks will reveal whether the resolution garners support from the boycotting parties or remains a standalone move. All eyes will be on the assembly session, where the debate could set the tone for Tamil Nadu's broader political strategy ahead of the next general elections.
