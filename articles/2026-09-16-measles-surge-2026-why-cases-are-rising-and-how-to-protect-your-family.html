Measles Is Back on the Rise — And the Numbers Are Not Subtle

Global measles surveillance reports through 2026 show a sustained upward trend in confirmed cases, reversing a decade-long decline. The World from PRX flagged the resurgence, and the underlying data from national health agencies and WHO regional offices points to one dominant driver: falling vaccination coverage.

Measles is among the most contagious viruses known to medicine — one infected person can infect 12 to 18 susceptible people in an unvaccinated community. That reproductive number (R0) is why a 95% two-dose coverage rate is the accepted threshold for herd immunity. Multiple countries are now sitting below 90%.

What the Coverage Gap Looks Like in Practice

India's Universal Immunisation Programme provides two free measles-rubella doses — at 9–12 months and 16–24 months. Despite this, NFHS-5 data showed only about 95% of children received the first dose and roughly 88% the second, with wide variation across states. Urban slums and remote districts consistently lag.

The consequence is predictable. When a single case enters a low-coverage cluster — a school, a construction site dormitory, a pilgrimage route — attack rates can exceed 90% among the unvaccinated. Adults born between the late 1960s and 1990s who missed natural infection or a second dose are also vulnerable.

Real-World Buyer and Family Implications

For parents in India, the practical question is not whether to vaccinate but where and when. Private paediatric clinics charge between Rs 800 and Rs 2,500 per MMR dose depending on city and brand, putting a two-child, two-dose schedule at roughly Rs 3,200 to Rs 10,000. Government centres eliminate that cost but may involve queues and fixed session days.

Adults planning international travel to outbreak-affected regions should verify their own immunity. A single documented dose, or birth before 1957, is generally accepted as presumptive immunity in most guidelines, but a second dose is advisable if records are unclear.

Key Real-World Highlights

  • Two MMR doses deliver roughly 97% protection — among the best efficacy figures of any routine vaccine.
  • Coverage below 95% allows sustained community transmission even in otherwise well-served areas.
  • Private MMR costs in India range from Rs 800 to Rs 2,500 per dose; government supply is free.
  • Adults with unclear vaccination history should not assume immunity — a titre test or booster resolves it.
  • Outbreak response costs — isolation, contact tracing, school closures — dwarf the price of a single vaccine dose.

Why This Surge Demands Attention Now

Measles is a reliable canary. Because it spreads so efficiently, rising case counts signal that routine immunisation systems are under strain well before other vaccine-preventable diseases show up. Catch-up campaigns, mop-up rounds in low-coverage districts, and clear communication about the safety of the MMR vaccine are the standard playbook.

The economic argument is straightforward. Treating one measles pneumonia case in a private Indian hospital can run into several lakh rupees, against a vaccine that costs a fraction of that. From a pure value-for-money standpoint, the choice is not close.
