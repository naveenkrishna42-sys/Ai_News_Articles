7 Bollywood Films That Captured Student Politics on Screen

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bollywood has frequently used student politics as a backdrop for storytelling.
  • Films like Raanjhanaa, Yuva, and Haasil examine power, idealism, and violence on campus.
  • These movies reflect real tensions between student factions in Indian universities.

A Recurring Theme on the Big Screen

Student politics has long been a source of gripping drama in Bollywood. From the idealism of youth to the ruthless pursuit of power, campus life offers a rich setting for conflict. A recent list compiled by News18 highlights seven films that have explored this world with varying degrees of depth.

These movies don't just use student politics as a backdrop. They often mirror real tensions on Indian campuses, where student unions wield considerable influence. The films range from romantic dramas to intense political thrillers.

Raanjhanaa (2013)

Set in Varanasi, this film follows a love story entangled with local politics. The protagonist, played by Dhanush, gets drawn into student activism after a personal tragedy. The movie shows how campus rivalries can escalate into larger communal conflicts.

The portrayal of Benaras Hindu University's student politics added authenticity. It highlighted the clash between established political families and rising new voices.

Yuva (2004)

Mani Ratnam's ensemble film weaves together three stories, one of which is rooted in student politics. The character played by Ajay Devgn represents a cynical young man who enters politics for personal gain. His journey shows the moral compromises often made in the pursuit of power.

The film examines how student leaders are groomed by larger political parties. It remains a sharp commentary on the system that recruits young activists.

Haasil (2003)

This film starring Jimmy Shergill and Irrfan Khan is often cited as one of the most realistic portrayals of campus politics. It deals with the ruthless competition for student union positions in Allahabad University. The movie does not romanticise the violence or the manipulation involved.

Haasil shows how students are used as pawns by senior politicians. It remains a cult favourite for its unflinching look at the dark side of student elections.

Other Notable Mentions

The list also includes films like Gulaal, which explores student politics in a law college in Rajasthan. It captures the rise of a charismatic but dangerous leader. Jolly LLB touches on the subject tangentially through its courtroom drama.

Student of the Year and Mujhse Dosti Karoge are lighter takes on campus life, but they still reflect social hierarchies within educational institutions. These films, while not as intense, show that student politics is a versatile theme.

Why These Stories Resonate

Bollywood's fascination with student politics likely stems from its dramatic potential. The campus is a microcosm of larger society, where ideologies clash and ambitions are forged. Many of these films were inspired by real events or figures.

Directors have used this setting to critique the politicisation of education. They have also shown how young people can be both idealistic and vulnerable to manipulation. The best of these films leave the audience questioning the system.

For viewers, these movies offer a glimpse into a world that is both familiar and alien. They remind us that the battles fought on campus often shape the leaders of tomorrow.

As student politics continues to make headlines in India, Bollywood will likely return to this well of material. The next film to capture the grit and glamour of campus power struggles may already be in the works.
