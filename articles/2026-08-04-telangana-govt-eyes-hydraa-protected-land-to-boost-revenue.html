Telangana govt eyes HYDRAA-protected land to boost revenue

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Telangana government explores revenue generation from HYDRAA-protected land.
  • Move raises concerns over environmental impact and legal hurdles.
  • Officials yet to confirm specific parcels or timelines.

The Congress-led Telangana government, grappling with a cash crunch, is now looking at HYDRAA-protected land as a potential source of revenue. The Hyderabad Disaster Response and Asset Monitoring Agency (HYDRAA) was set up to safeguard water bodies, parks, and other public assets from encroachment. But with the state's finances under strain, the administration is exploring ways to monetise these very assets.

What HYDRAA protects

HYDRAA, established by the previous BRS government, has been tasked with identifying and clearing encroachments on lakes, stormwater drains, and other natural buffers across Hyderabad. Its mandate includes restoring water bodies and preventing illegal constructions. The agency's list of protected assets runs into thousands of acres, much of it prime land in and around the city.

Now, the current administration is examining whether portions of this land can be leased or developed for revenue. Sources indicate that the government is weighing options such as commercial leasing, setting up solar parks, or creating public-private partnerships on select parcels. However, no final decision has been made public yet.

Legal and environmental hurdles

Any move to use HYDRAA-protected land for revenue generation is likely to face stiff legal challenges. Environmental activists have already warned that tampering with these areas could worsen flooding in Hyderabad, which has seen devastating inundation in recent years. The Telangana High Court has previously issued orders protecting water bodies and open spaces, and any deviation may require judicial approval.

Moreover, HYDRAA was created with a specific mandate, and modifying its charter might need legislation. Political observers note that the Congress government, which came to power with promises of transparency and environmental protection, could face criticism if it appears to backtrack on these commitments.

Revenue pressure mounts

The state's finances have been under pressure due to rising expenditure on welfare schemes and loan waivers promised during elections. With GST compensation from the Centre tapering off, Telangana is looking for new revenue streams. The government has already raised taxes on liquor and stamp duties, but these measures may not be enough.

Officials familiar with the matter say that the land monetisation plan is still at a preliminary stage. A committee has been formed to study the feasibility, and it is expected to submit a report in the coming weeks. The report is likely to identify specific parcels that could be developed without harming the environment.

What could be on the table

While no list has been published, areas around the city's outskirts, where HYDRAA has cleared encroachments, are being considered. These include buffer zones around lakes like Himayat Sagar and Osmansagar, which supply drinking water to Hyderabad. Building on these zones would require relaxing existing regulations, which could be a tough sell.

Another option being explored is using the land for renewable energy projects. Solar panels could be installed on large tracts that are currently lying vacant after encroachments were removed. This would generate clean energy and revenue, but transmission infrastructure and grid connectivity remain challenges.

Environmentalists argue that the primary purpose of HYDRAA land is ecological, not commercial. They point out that lake beds and catchment areas are prone to flooding and building on them could be disastrous. The government, however, maintains that any project will be environmentally sustainable and will not compromise the original intent of HYDRAA.

Next steps

The feasibility committee's report is expected to be the first concrete step. If it recommends specific projects, the government will need to bring in a legislative amendment or seek court clearance. Meanwhile, opposition parties are likely to use the issue to attack the Congress government, accusing it of selling off public assets.

For now, the administration is tight-lipped about the details. A final call is expected only after the report is submitted and reviewed. The coming weeks will show whether the government can balance its revenue needs with the imperative to protect Hyderabad's fragile ecology.
