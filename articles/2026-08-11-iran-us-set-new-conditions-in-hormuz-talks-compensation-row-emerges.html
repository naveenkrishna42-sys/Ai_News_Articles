Iran, US set new conditions in Hormuz talks; compensation row emerges

📅 2026-08-11 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US President Trump has demanded Iran pay compensation for decades of US soldier deaths.
  • Iran has reportedly attempted to send a 'war bill' to the US, prompting a reciprocal demand from Washington.
  • Separate maritime attacks on a cargo ship and tanker have escalated tensions in the Red Sea and Gulf of Oman.
  • Trump told Axios that the US is 'low-keying' its approach with Iran, indicating a cautious diplomatic stance.

New conditions emerge in Hormuz talks

Negotiations over the Strait of Hormuz have taken a fresh turn, with both Iran and the United States introducing new conditions that could reshape the diplomatic landscape. The talks, which were already fraught with complexity, now face additional hurdles as each side seeks to press its demands.

According to reports, the discussions have moved beyond the narrow focus on maritime security to broader, more contentious issues. The introduction of these conditions signals a hardening of positions, even as both nations appear willing to remain at the negotiating table.

Trump's compensation demand

US President Donald Trump has reportedly demanded that Iran pay compensation for the deaths of American soldiers over the past several decades. The demand, which was first reported by The Guardian, has added a new layer of friction to the already delicate talks.

This comes after Iran allegedly attempted to send a 'war bill' to the US, a move that was met with a reciprocal demand from Washington. The Deccan Herald reported that Trump sent a bill back, insisting that Tehran compensate the US for its military losses.

'Low-keying' approach

In a separate interaction with Axios, Trump described the US approach as 'low-keying' its dealings with Iran. This suggests a deliberate strategy to downplay expectations and avoid inflaming an already volatile situation.

The comment reflects a cautious tone from Washington, even as it pushes for concessions. Analysts will be watching closely to see whether this approach yields any tangible progress or merely prolongs the stalemate.

Escalation in regional waters

Meanwhile, the region has witnessed a fresh escalation in maritime attacks. A cargo ship and a tanker were struck in separate incidents, as the Red Sea and the Gulf of Oman see heightened activity. The Times of India's live updates have tracked these developments closely.

The attacks underscore the fragile security environment in these critical waterways, which are vital for global oil shipments. While no group has yet claimed responsibility, the incidents are likely to complicate the ongoing negotiations.

What the talks hinge on

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most strategically important chokepoints, and the talks are seen as a crucial effort to prevent a broader conflict. The new conditions introduced by both sides, however, suggest that a quick resolution is unlikely.

Observers note that the compensation demand, in particular, could be a non-starter for Tehran, which has long argued against such claims. The reciprocal nature of the bills exchanged indicates a tit-for-tat dynamic that could derail the process.

Next steps to watch

As the talks continue, the focus will be on whether the two sides can move past these contentious demands. The maritime attacks add an urgent backdrop, raising the stakes for a diplomatic breakthrough.

For now, both capitals seem to be testing each other's resolve, with the 'low-key' approach from Washington suggesting a willingness to keep channels open. The coming weeks will reveal whether this strategy can lead to de-escalation or if the new conditions will push the region closer to the brink.
