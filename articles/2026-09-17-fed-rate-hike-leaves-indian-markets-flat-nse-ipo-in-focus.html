Fed Hikes Again, But Dalal Street Barely Blinks

The U.S. Federal Reserve delivered yet another rate hike overnight, yet Indian equities opened marginally higher on Thursday. The Nifty 50 and Sensex managed gains of under half a percent in early trade, signalling that the domestic market has largely priced in the Fed's tightening cycle. For Indian investors, the bigger story isn't Washington—it's the National Stock Exchange (NSE) IPO, which is now the single most anticipated event on Dalal Street.

Foreign institutional investors (FIIs) have been net buyers in recent sessions, but the pace has slowed. The rupee's stability near 83-84 against the dollar has provided some comfort, even as the yield on the 10-year U.S. Treasury hovers above 4.5%. The real question: will the NSE IPO suck liquidity out of the secondary market, or will it bring fresh capital and deepen India's equity culture?

What the Fed Rate Hike Means for Indian Markets

A U.S. rate hike typically pressures emerging markets by strengthening the dollar and prompting FII outflows. This time, the impact has been muted because the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has already front-loaded its own tightening and inflation has moderated to within its tolerance band. The RBI's repo rate has stayed unchanged for three consecutive policy meetings, giving domestic borrowers some relief.

However, rate-sensitive sectors like real estate and auto have not rallied uniformly. Banks are better placed, with net interest margins holding up, but a sustained high-rate environment in the U.S. could cap valuation expansion for Indian equities. Investors should watch the Fed's forward guidance more than the hike itself.

NSE IPO: The Elephant in the Room

The NSE IPO is expected to be one of India's largest ever, with a valuation potentially exceeding ₹3 lakh crore. The exchange has already filed its draft red herring prospectus (DRHP) with SEBI, and the issue is likely to include both fresh capital and an offer for sale by existing shareholders. For retail investors, the key question is pricing—will the IPO leave enough on the table?

NSE's dominance in the derivatives segment, which accounts for over 90% of its revenue, is a double-edged sword. Regulatory changes to derivative rules could impact volumes and margins. Still, the exchange's brand, technology infrastructure, and near-monopoly in equity derivatives make it a compelling long-term bet.

Real-World Buyer Implications

For retail investors, the NSE IPO offers a rare chance to own a piece of India's market infrastructure. But the pricing must be reasonable. At a valuation of ₹3.5 lakh crore, NSE would trade at a premium to global peers like Hong Kong Exchanges and Clearing (HKEX) and Singapore Exchange (SGX). Investors should compare the price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio with listed peers before applying.

In the secondary market, the Fed hike is a non-event for now. Focus on domestic-facing sectors—banks, capital goods, and consumption—that are less sensitive to global rates. Avoid over-leveraged exporters and IT stocks, which face currency and demand headwinds.

  • Pros: NSE IPO provides exposure to a monopoly-like business with high margins; Fed hike largely priced in; FII flows stable.
  • Cons: NSE valuation may be expensive; regulatory risks in derivatives; global rates could stay higher for longer, capping market upside.
  • Key Insight: The NSE IPO could divert liquidity from the secondary market, but it also signals confidence in India's capital markets.

What to Watch Next

Keep an eye on the NSE IPO price band and the Fed's next meeting. If the Fed signals a pause, Indian markets could rally. If not, expect continued consolidation. For now, the Nifty's support at 19,500 and resistance at 20,200 are the levels to track.
