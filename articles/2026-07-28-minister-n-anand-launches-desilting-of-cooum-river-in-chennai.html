Minister N Anand launches desilting of Cooum River in Chennai

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tamil Nadu Minister N Anand launched desilting works on the Cooum River in Chennai.
  • The project aims to improve water flow and reduce flooding during the monsoon season.
  • Desilting is part of a larger effort to rejuvenate the heavily polluted river.

CHENNAI: Tamil Nadu Minister for Municipal Administration and Rural Development, N Anand, on Tuesday launched desilting works on the heavily silted Cooum River. The operation began near the river's mouth at the Napier Bridge stretch.

Project aims to ease monsoon flooding

The desilting drive is expected to deepen the riverbed and improve water flow. Officials say this will reduce the risk of waterlogging in low-lying areas during the northeast monsoon, which typically hits Chennai between October and December.

Minister Anand personally inspected the initial phase of the work. He directed officials to complete the desilting on schedule, stating that the government is committed to restoring the river's carrying capacity. The Chennai River Restoration Trust is overseeing the execution.

Decades of neglect and pollution

The Cooum has suffered from unchecked sewage dumping and encroachment for decades. Its banks are lined with illegal structures, and the water is often black with industrial and domestic waste. Past revival attempts have largely failed to make a lasting impact.

Environmental activists have repeatedly called for a comprehensive cleanup. They argue that desilting alone is insufficient without strict enforcement against pollution sources. The minister acknowledged these concerns but said the current focus is on flood mitigation.

Phased approach to river rejuvenation

Authorities have divided the desilting work into multiple phases. The first phase targets the most clogged stretches from the estuary to the Koyambedu market area. Heavy machinery, including excavators and dredgers, is being used to remove silt and solid waste.

The removed material will be dumped at designated sites outside the city. Officials have not yet given a timeline for the entire project, but they expect the first phase to be completed before the monsoon peak.

Residents of neighbourhoods along the river have welcomed the move. Many say they have suffered waterlogging every rainy season for years. A local trader in Chintadripet told reporters that he hopes the work is done properly this time.

The state government has allocated funds under the Chennai Mega City Development Mission for this project. The total cost has not been disclosed. The desilting is being carried out in coordination with the Water Resources Department and the Greater Chennai Corporation.

Minister Anand also visited the site later in the day to review progress. He was accompanied by senior officials from the municipal administration and the corporation. He reiterated that the government is serious about cleaning the Cooum and making it navigable again, at least in parts.

The next phase of desilting will extend to the river's upper reaches, including areas near Ambattur and Avadi. Authorities are also mapping encroachments along the banks for removal.

Chennai has seen rapid urbanisation, which has worsened the Cooum's condition. Storm drains empty directly into the river, carrying solid waste and sewage. The desilting is seen as a stopgap measure until a long-term sewage treatment plan is implemented.

Work is expected to continue round the clock in key stretches to meet the pre-monsoon deadline. The minister has asked officials to ensure minimal disruption to traffic and daily life.
