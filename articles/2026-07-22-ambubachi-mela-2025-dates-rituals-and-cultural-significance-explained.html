Ambubachi Mela 2025: Dates, rituals, and cultural significance explained

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ambubachi Mela 2025 begins on June 27 and lasts four days at the Kamakhya Temple in Assam.
  • The festival celebrates the annual menstruation of Goddess Kamakhya and the temple remains closed for three days.
  • Hundreds of thousands of devotees and sadhus gather for rituals and the reopening on the fourth day.

The Ambubachi Mela, one of Hinduism's most significant and distinct festivals, has commenced at the Kamakhya Temple in Guwahati, Assam. Starting on June 27, 2025, the four-day event draws hundreds of thousands of devotees, sadhus, and tourists from across India and abroad.

This annual gathering is not just a religious congregation but a deep-rooted cultural event that celebrates the goddess's fertility and the power of womanhood.

What is the Ambubachi Mela?

The Ambubachi Mela marks the annual menstruation period of Goddess Kamakhya, a revered Shakti Peetha. According to legend, the goddess's womb and genitalia fell at this spot after Lord Shiva carried her burnt body. The temple is closed for three days during this period, as tradition holds that the goddess is undergoing her menstrual cycle.

During these three days, devotees abstain from performing any puja or agricultural work. The temple premises are considered impure, and entry is restricted. The main rituals resume on the fourth day, known as 'Snan' or 'Bath', when the temple reopens for the public.

Dates and key rituals for 2025

This year, the Ambubachi Mela began on June 27 and will conclude on June 30. The core ritual involves the temple being sealed for three days. On the fourth day, the doors open after a ceremonial bath of the goddess's idol and the holy stone (yoni).

Devotees then gather to receive 'prasad' and seek blessings. The festival is also marked by the presence of naked ascetics (Naga sadhus) from various akharas, who perform rituals and offer prayers. The air fills with chants and the scent of incense.

Cultural and social significance

Beyond its religious roots, the Ambubachi Mela carries a powerful social message. It openly celebrates menstruation, a natural biological process often stigmatised in many societies. The festival normalises the concept, highlighting the goddess's fertility and the sacredness of womanhood.

For local communities, the mela is a major economic driver. Thousands of stalls selling handicrafts, food, and religious items line the route to the temple. The state government makes extensive arrangements for security, sanitation, and crowd management, given the massive turnout.

This year, authorities have implemented additional measures to ensure smooth darshan and prevent any untoward incidents. Devotees are advised to plan their visit considering the rush and limited facilities on the hilltop.

The Ambubachi Mela remains a testament to India's diverse spiritual fabric, where age-old traditions and contemporary logistics meet. As the temple gates reopen on June 30, the faithful will once again seek the goddess's blessings, marking another chapter in this timeless festival.
