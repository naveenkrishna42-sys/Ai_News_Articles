IIT Delhi PM visit: Security tight, students told how low to bow

📅 2026-08-08 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Security measures during PM Modi's IIT Delhi visit caused disruption
  • Students were reportedly told how low to bow their heads during the event
  • PM Modi expressed disappointment over few girl medal winners

New Delhi: The Prime Minister's 'outreach' visit to IIT Delhi on Friday brought the campus to a standstill, with security arrangements so elaborate that students were reportedly given specific instructions on how low to bow their heads in the Prime Minister's presence.

The visit, aimed at engaging with young innovators, instead triggered a debate on protocol versus practicality, as the institute struggled to balance the demands of a high-profile political event with academic routine.

Security grid and student instructions

According to sources, the security blanket thrown over the campus was unprecedented. Students were frisked multiple times, and movement within several academic blocks was restricted for hours. But it was the reported instruction on bowing that raised eyebrows.

Students were allegedly told the exact angle at which they should bow their heads when greeting the Prime Minister, a directive that many found both amusing and unsettling. Neither the institute nor the Prime Minister's Office has officially commented on the specifics, but the anecdote has become the talk of the campus.

PM's message: Learn, seek solutions

During his address, PM Modi urged students to embrace change. "In a rapidly changing world, those who learn win," he said, emphasising the need for continuous upskilling. He also encouraged the Gen-Z audience to "have the courage to seek solutions" to the country's pressing challenges.

The PM's interaction was part of a broader outreach effort, with Union Minister Pralhad Joshi adding that India's youth are "not merely competing with the world, but leading it." The event was streamed live across multiple platforms, drawing attention to the government's focus on innovation and education.

Disappointment over medal winners

However, the PM's visit was not without its moments of candid critique. In an unscripted remark, he expressed disappointment that only "one or two" girls were among the IIT Delhi medal winners. "Should have been more," he said, sparking a conversation about gender disparity in STEM fields at the country's top engineering institutes.

His observation was met with mixed reactions. While some applauded his attention to the issue, others pointed out that systemic changes, not just remarks, are needed to address the underlying causes of underrepresentation.

Campus life disrupted

The security-driven disruptions left many students frustrated. Classes were rescheduled, and access to labs and libraries was curtailed. For a campus that prides itself on intellectual freedom, the sight of metal detectors and barricades was a stark reminder of the logistical realities of hosting a VVIP.

Students took to social media to share their experiences, with some joking about the bowing instructions while others questioned the necessity of such extreme measures. The institute administration has yet to issue a formal response to the grievances.

As the dust settles, the focus now shifts to whether the government will follow up on the PM's remarks with concrete policy initiatives to boost female representation in IITs and ease the security burden on academic institutions during future visits.
