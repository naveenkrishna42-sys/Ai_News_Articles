West Indies pacer Justin Greaves creates Test history with five-wicket maiden

📅 2026-07-28 📂 Sports Original source ↗
Key points
  • Justin Greaves took five wickets without conceding a run, a first in Test cricket history.
  • The feat occurred during the 1st Test between West Indies and Pakistan on the Pakistan tour of West Indies in 2026.
  • Greaves' historic spell is being hailed as an unprecedented bowling achievement in the longest format of the game.

An unprecedented feat in Test cricket

West Indies pacer Justin Greaves has etched his name into the record books with a spell that has never been seen before in Test cricket. During the first Test against Pakistan on the 2026 Pakistan tour of the West Indies, Greaves became the first bowler in the history of the format to claim a five-wicket maiden.

The milestone means that Greaves took five wickets while not conceding a single run off his bowling in that over. It is a feat that had not been achieved in over 140 years of Test cricket, leaving fans and experts searching for superlatives.

A spell for the ages

Greaves, known for his raw pace and ability to generate bounce, was in devastating form. The West Indian quick bowled a maiden over that saw five Pakistani batsmen walk back to the pavilion, their stumps disturbed or edges held, without a single run being added to the scoreboard.

The achievement is even more remarkable given the context of the match. Against a strong Pakistan batting lineup, Greaves' spell shifted the momentum firmly in the West Indies' favour. Cricket boards and former players took to social media to congratulate the pacer, with the International Cricket Council (ICC) also acknowledging the historic moment.

What makes a five-wicket maiden so rare?

In cricket, a maiden over is one where no runs are scored. A five-wicket haul in an innings is a celebrated achievement for any bowler. Combining the two — taking five wickets in a single over without conceding a run — has been considered nearly impossible. Bowlers have come close, but no one had achieved the perfect over until Greaves.

The record highlights both Greaves' skill and the vulnerability of the Pakistani batting order on that day. While conditions may have assisted the pacers, the execution required to take five wickets in six balls is extraordinary.

Reactions pour in

News of the record spread quickly across cricket-mad nations. In India, where the sport is a religion, the feat was covered extensively by outlets such as The Times of India and The New Indian Express. Cricbuzz described it as 'a fifer to remember,' while Al Jazeera called it a 'historic first for West Indies pacer.'

Former West Indies greats and current players alike hailed the achievement as a sign of the region's rich fast-bowling tradition continuing. The Pakistan camp, meanwhile, was left to ponder what went wrong as their batting card showed a remarkable collapse.

What comes next

With this record, Justin Greaves has not only secured his place in cricket history but has also set a new benchmark for bowlers around the world. The Test match is still ongoing, and all eyes will be on whether Greaves can add to his tally. The cricketing world will be watching closely to see if any bowler can ever match this remarkable feat.
