JSW Steel Q1 Profit Tumbles 72% on Weak Demand, Margin Improves

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • JSW Steel's consolidated net profit dropped 71.9% year-on-year to ₹1,320 crore in Q1 FY26.
  • Revenue from operations fell 8.3% to ₹38,470 crore, impacted by lower steel prices.
  • EBITDA margin improved marginally to 19.2% from 18.1% a year ago, aided by cost controls.

Profit Takes a Hit

JSW Steel reported a sharp 71.9% decline in consolidated net profit for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. The figure stood at ₹1,320 crore, down from ₹4,700 crore in the same period last year. The drop comes amid subdued global demand and falling steel prices.

Revenue from operations fell 8.3% to ₹38,470 crore. The company attributed the top-line pressure to lower realisations across key product segments. Market conditions remain challenging, with oversupply in major export destinations.

Margins Show a Silver Lining

Despite the profit plunge, JSW Steel managed to expand its EBITDA margin slightly. The margin rose to 19.2% in Q1 FY26, compared to 18.1% in the year-ago quarter. This was driven by cost-saving measures and operational efficiencies.

EBITDA for the quarter stood at ₹7,380 crore, against ₹7,200 crore last year. The company said it benefited from lower input costs and better product mix. However, analysts caution that margin expansion may be temporary if demand does not recover.

Volume and Export Performance

JSW Steel reported crude steel production of 6.3 million tonnes in Q1, a 2% year-on-year decline. Sales volume also slipped to 5.8 million tonnes. Exports were hit particularly hard, falling 35% on weak overseas demand.

Domestic sales, however, showed some resilience, rising 3% sequentially. The company is banking on infrastructure spending and government capex to shore up local demand. But the broader picture remains muted.

Outlook and Pressure Points

Going forward, JSW Steel faces headwinds from global trade tensions and cheap imports from China. The company has called for policy support to protect domestic steelmakers. It is also focusing on reducing debt, which stood at ₹72,000 crore at the end of Q1.

Analysts expect the steel major to report a similar performance in the coming quarters unless demand picks up. The management has not provided any specific guidance for Q2. All eyes will be on the government's upcoming infrastructure announcements and any anti-dumping measures.
