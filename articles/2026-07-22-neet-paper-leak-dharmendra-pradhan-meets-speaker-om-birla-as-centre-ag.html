NEET paper leak: Dharmendra Pradhan meets Speaker Om Birla as Centre agrees to Lok Sabha debate

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Dharmendra Pradhan met Speaker Om Birla to finalise the NEET debate in Lok Sabha
  • Government ready for a detailed discussion but says street protests won't help
  • Opposition insists on Pradhan's resignation before agreeing to the debate
  • Rajya Sabha Rule 267 also invoked by Opposition to seek discussion on NEET and police actions

Government relents on NEET debate

Union Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan met Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla on Wednesday as the government agreed to a discussion on the NEET paper leak issue in the Lower House. The meeting came amid intense political pressure over the alleged breach of exam integrity.

The government has now signalled readiness for a detailed debate. Sources indicated that the discussion could take place later this week. The decision marks a shift from earlier reluctance to allow a dedicated slot on the floor.

Opposition digs in over resignation demand

Opposition parties have refused to let the matter rest at a simple debate. They are demanding that Pradhan resign before any discussion takes place. Leaders from the Congress and other parties argued that accountability must precede dialogue.

“We want Dharmendra Pradhan’s resignation first. Only then will a discussion be meaningful,” a senior Opposition leader said. The government has outright rejected this pre-condition, with Union Minister Kiren Rijiju accusing the Congress of setting conditions to scuttle the debate.

Government criticises street protests

Union ministers have also taken aim at protests by medical aspirants in several cities. The government described the demonstrations as counterproductive. “Street protests will not help. The matter will be resolved through discussion in Parliament,” a government spokesperson said.

The Centre has maintained that it is committed to a thorough inquiry. It has also urged students to return to their studies, promising that strict action will be taken against those responsible for the leak.

Rajya Sabha Rule 267 invoked

In the Upper House, the Opposition has invoked Rule 267 of Rajya Sabha procedures to demand an immediate discussion on the NEET paper leak. The rule allows suspension of listed business to take up a matter of urgent public importance.

Opposition MPs are also seeking a debate on alleged police brutality during protests over the exam controversy. The government has yet to respond to this specific demand. Parliamentary sources indicated that the Treasury Benches are likely to resist any move that disrupts scheduled legislative business.

What happens next

The Lok Sabha debate on NEET is expected to be scheduled once the Speaker and the government finalise the time allocation. The Opposition will have to decide whether to participate under the current terms or continue pressing for Pradhan's resignation. The outcome of this standoff will determine whether the Monsoon Session can proceed without further disruption.
