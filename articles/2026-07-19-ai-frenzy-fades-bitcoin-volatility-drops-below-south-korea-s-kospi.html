AI Frenzy Fades, Bitcoin Volatility Drops Below South Korea's Kospi

📅 2026-07-19 📂 Crypto Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bitcoin volatility has dropped below that of South Korea's Kospi index.
  • The decline in AI-related market enthusiasm is a key factor behind reduced crypto volatility.
  • Traders are moving away from speculative tech bets, impacting Bitcoin's price action.

Crypto Markets Calm Down

Bitcoin's wild price swings have settled down so much that the cryptocurrency is now less volatile than South Korea's benchmark Kospi stock index. This is a rare shift for an asset class known for its dramatic daily moves.

The change comes as the global frenzy around artificial intelligence stocks loses steam. Investors who were piling into AI-related bets are now pulling back, and that cooling effect is spilling over into crypto markets.

AI Hype Fades, Volatility Drops

For months, the AI rally drove speculative capital into both tech stocks and cryptocurrencies. Bitcoin often moved in tandem with high-growth names like Nvidia and other AI darlings. But as earnings growth slows and regulatory scrutiny around AI intensifies, that momentum is fading.

Traders are rotating out of risk-on assets. This shift has compressed Bitcoin's price range. The crypto king is now posting smaller daily moves than the Kospi, a major Asian stock index that itself has seen reduced turbulence recently.

What the Numbers Show

Volatility metrics for Bitcoin have fallen to multi-month lows. The Kospi index, meanwhile, has been swinging more than usual due to domestic political uncertainty and global trade tensions. The result is a rare inversion: a famously volatile asset is now calmer than a stock market.

Market analysts point to lower trading volumes on crypto exchanges as a contributing factor. Without large directional bets, Bitcoin is drifting in a narrow band between $27,000 and $30,000 in recent sessions.

Trader Sentiment Shifts

Retail and institutional traders alike are showing less appetite for leveraged crypto positions. Open interest in Bitcoin futures has declined, and funding rates remain neutral. This suggests that neither bulls nor bears are confident enough to push prices aggressively.

The absence of a clear catalyst—whether regulatory clarity or a major adoption event—is keeping markets rangebound. The AI frenzy provided a narrative; without it, crypto is searching for a new story.

What Comes Next

With the AI trade unwinding, Bitcoin's next move may depend on macroeconomic signals. The US Federal Reserve's interest rate decisions and inflation data could inject fresh volatility. For now, traders are watching whether Bitcoin can hold its current support levels or if a broader risk-off move will drag it lower.
