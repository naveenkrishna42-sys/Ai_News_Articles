Rahul Bhatia calls Adani airline plan massive conflict of interest

📅 2026-07-23 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • IndiGo's Rahul Bhatia says Adani Group's airline plans pose a massive conflict of interest due to its airport ownership.
  • Both IndiGo and Air India have opposed the move, fearing unfair advantage and market distortion.
  • The government is considering reforms to let airport operators run airlines, potentially breaking the IndiGo-Air India duopoly.

Rahul Bhatia, co-founder of InterGlobe Aviation that runs IndiGo, has strongly opposed the Adani Group's reported plans to enter the airline business. He described the move as a “massive conflict of interest,” given that Adani already operates seven airports across India.

IndiGo and Air India, the two dominant carriers in the country, have both voiced opposition to the proposal. The resistance comes as the government explores sweeping aviation reforms that could allow airport owners to launch their own airlines.

Conflict of interest claims

Bhatia argued that allowing an airport operator to also run an airline would create an uneven playing field. An airport owner could potentially favour its own airline with better slots, lower charges, or preferential access to infrastructure.

“This is not about competition. This is about a massive conflict of interest that will distort the market,” Bhatia said during a recent industry interaction. He added that such a structure would undermine the principle of fair access that independent airlines rely on.

Adani Group has not officially confirmed its airline venture plans. However, multiple reports indicate that the conglomerate is weighing an entry into the sector, which is currently dominated by IndiGo and Air India.

Government mulls reform

The Centre is examining a proposal to ease entry norms for airlines. Sources indicate that policymakers are considering allowing airport operators—such as Adani and GMR—to run their own airlines. The idea is to break the current duopoly and increase competition.

Currently, IndiGo holds roughly 60% of the domestic market share, while Air India, backed by the Tata Group, commands around 28%. Together, they control nearly nine out of every ten seats sold in the country.

Officials believe new entrants could lower fares and improve service quality. But critics warn that letting airport operators into the airline business could concentrate too much power in a few hands.

Industry divided

Air India has also expressed concerns. The airline's management has privately argued that airport operators already enjoy significant control over costs and schedules. Giving them an airline licence would amount to vertical integration without adequate regulatory safeguards.

IndiGo has publicly stated that any reform must ensure a level playing field. The airline has urged the government to mandate strict separation between airport operations and airline services if the policy is adopted.

The debate comes at a time when Indian aviation is recovering strongly from the pandemic. Domestic passenger traffic has surpassed pre-Covid levels, and airlines are adding capacity to meet demand.

Adani Group, which also has interests in ports, energy, and media, has been expanding its aviation footprint. It currently manages airports in Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Lucknow, Jaipur, Guwahati, Thiruvananthapuram, and Mangaluru. Adding an airline would make it a fully integrated player in the sector.

Regulatory clarity is expected in the coming months. The government is likely to release a discussion paper on aviation reforms before finalising any changes. All eyes will be on how the Centre balances the need for competition with the risk of conflicts of interest.
