India plans tax cuts to boost foreign investment, report says

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Business Original source ↗
Key points
  • Government proposes easing tax relief conditions for offshore funds
  • Tax breaks for contract manufacturing could be extended, benefiting Apple
  • Changes aim to boost electronics manufacturing and foreign investment

The Indian government is preparing a series of tax cuts and regulatory relaxations aimed at attracting greater foreign investment, according to a Bloomberg report. The proposed measures include easing tax relief conditions for offshore funds and extending tax breaks for contract manufacturing, a move that could benefit companies like Apple.

Offshore fund tax relief

The government is looking to simplify the tax regime for foreign portfolio investors. Currently, offshore funds must meet certain conditions to claim tax exemptions. The proposed changes would make it easier for these funds to qualify, potentially drawing more international capital into Indian markets.

This comes as part of a broader effort to position India as a preferred investment destination amid global supply chain shifts. The report suggests that the government is keen to remove hurdles that have historically deterred foreign institutional investors.

Boost for electronics and Apple

In a separate but related move, the government is proposing to extend tax breaks for contract manufacturing. This is seen as a direct boost for electronics manufacturers, including Apple's contract partners in India. The extension would likely reduce the cost burden on companies assembling devices locally, encouraging more production within the country.

Apple has been ramping up its manufacturing footprint in India in recent years, with partners like Foxconn and Wistron expanding operations. The proposed tax incentives could accelerate this trend, helping India capture a larger share of global electronics manufacturing.

Legislative route

The changes are expected to be introduced through the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026. This omnibus legislation is designed to streamline multiple tax provisions and align them with the government's investment promotion goals. The Bill is currently under preparation and is likely to be tabled in the upcoming parliamentary session.

While the exact provisions are yet to be made public, the Bill is expected to address several long-standing demands from industry bodies. These include clarity on tax treatment for foreign funds and incentives for export-oriented manufacturing units.

What this means for investors

For foreign investors, the proposed changes signal a more welcoming stance from New Delhi. Easing tax conditions could lower the effective cost of investing in India, making it more competitive against other emerging markets. Analysts believe this could lead to increased inflows into Indian equities and debt markets over the medium term.

However, the actual impact will depend on the final wording of the legislation. Tax experts caution that the devil is often in the details, and the proposed relaxations may come with new compliance requirements. Investors will be watching closely as the Bill moves through the legislative process.

The government has not yet officially confirmed the details of the Bloomberg report. Finance ministry officials have declined to comment on the matter, saying only that tax policy changes are routinely considered as part of the budgetary cycle.

The coming weeks will be crucial as the government finalises the Bill's provisions and decides on the parliamentary timetable. If passed, the amendments could mark a significant shift in India's approach to foreign investment, with implications for manufacturing, capital markets, and trade policy.
