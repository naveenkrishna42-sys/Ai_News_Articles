Bank Deposit Rates Rise in June, Lending Rates Edge Up: RBI Data

📅 2026-07-31 📂 Banking Original source ↗
Key points
  • Fresh deposit rates increased in June, reflecting tighter liquidity conditions.
  • Lending rates also inched up, impacting borrowers across segments.
  • RBI's data indicates a gradual transmission of monetary policy actions.

MUMBAI: Banks in India raised rates on fresh deposits in June, while lending rates also moved up, according to data released by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) on Friday. The central bank's monthly bulletin showed a clear uptick in the cost of funds for lenders, even as credit demand remains robust.

The increase in deposit rates comes after months of intense competition among banks to attract savers, as credit growth has outpaced deposit mobilisation. This trend has forced lenders to offer higher returns on term deposits, particularly for tenors of one to three years, which are most preferred by depositors.

Fresh Deposit Rates Climb

Data on fresh rupee deposits showed a noticeable rise in the weighted average domestic term deposit rate (WADTDR) for new deposits during June. Banks have been revising their deposit rate cards upward, especially for bulk deposits, as they look to shore up their liability franchises.

The move is a reversal from the earlier phase of surplus liquidity, when deposit rates were at multi-year lows. Now, with systemic liquidity in deficit and credit demand accelerating, banks are passing on the benefit to depositors. This is a welcome shift for households, who have seen real returns on savings erode over the past few years.

Lending Rates Edge Higher

On the lending side, the weighted average lending rate (WALR) on fresh rupee loans also inched up in June. This is in line with the transmission of the RBI's monetary policy stance, though the pass-through has been gradual and uneven across banks.

For existing borrowers, the impact is felt through the marginal cost of funds-based lending rate (MCLR) revisions, which have been moving up in tandem. New borrowers, however, are more directly exposed to the fresh lending rates, which are now reflecting the higher cost of funds for banks.

The RBI's data underscores that the transmission of policy rate changes to the broader economy is underway, but with a lag. Banks, while keen to protect margins, are also mindful of the need to keep credit flowing to productive sectors.

What This Means for Borrowers and Savers

For depositors, the rise in fresh deposit rates is good news, offering better returns on new fixed deposits. However, existing deposits will continue to earn older, lower rates until they mature.

For borrowers, the edge higher in lending rates implies a modest increase in equated monthly instalments (EMIs) for those with floating-rate loans. Home loan and auto loan customers, in particular, may see their monthly outgo go up, though the increase is likely to be small given the gradual nature of the move.

The RBI's data also showed that the spread between deposit and lending rates remains under pressure, as banks compete for deposits. This could squeeze net interest margins in the coming quarters, unless credit growth remains strong enough to compensate.

Outlook

Bankers and analysts will be watching the July data for signs of whether this trend continues. With the festival season approaching and credit demand expected to pick up further, deposit rates are likely to stay firm. The RBI's monetary policy committee, which meets next month, will also factor in these developments while charting its course.

For now, the message from the data is clear: the era of cheap money is over, and both savers and borrowers need to brace for a period of gradually rising rates.
