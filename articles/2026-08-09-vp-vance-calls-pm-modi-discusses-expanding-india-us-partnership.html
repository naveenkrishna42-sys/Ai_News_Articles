VP Vance calls PM Modi, discusses expanding India-US partnership

📅 2026-08-09 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • US Vice-President JD Vance telephoned Prime Minister Narendra Modi to discuss bilateral ties.
  • The call came a day after the US Senate cleared a bill proposing 100% tariffs on certain imports.
  • Vance also congratulated Second Lady Usha Vance on the birth of the couple's son.
  • Both leaders discussed ways to expand the India-US partnership across multiple sectors.

US Vice-President JD Vance placed a telephone call to Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Saturday, in a conversation that centred on expanding the bilateral partnership between Washington and New Delhi.

The call comes at a sensitive moment in trade relations, just a day after the US Senate cleared a bill proposing 100% tariffs on imports from certain countries. While officials have not yet disclosed the specific agenda in detail, the timing has drawn attention in diplomatic circles.

Focus on partnership expansion

According to a readout from the Prime Minister's Office, the two leaders discussed ways to broaden the India-US partnership across trade, technology, defence and people-to-people ties. Both sides described the conversation as warm and constructive.

The discussion also touched upon regional and global developments, though specific issues were not elaborated in the official statement. The call signals continuity in the high-level engagement that has marked India-US relations in recent years.

Personal congratulations

During the call, PM Modi congratulated Vice-President Vance on the birth of his son with Second Lady Usha Vance. The couple welcomed their third child earlier this year. Modi conveyed his best wishes to the family, a gesture that added a personal dimension to the official exchange.

Usha Vance, who is of Indian origin, has often been a talking point in India-US diplomatic circles. Her familial roots in India have been seen as a soft link between the two democracies.

Context of the tariff bill

The conversation took place against the backdrop of the US Senate's approval of a bill proposing 100% tariffs on imports from specific countries. The legislation, cleared on Friday, has been part of a broader trade debate in Washington.

Indian officials have not yet issued a detailed response to the tariff bill. However, the fact that the Vice-President chose to call New Delhi the very next day is being interpreted by analysts as an attempt to reassure a key partner.

Trade between India and the US has grown steadily, with bilateral goods and services trade crossing the $190-billion mark in recent years. Both countries have also been working on resolving outstanding WTO disputes and expanding cooperation in critical minerals and semiconductors.

Broader diplomatic signals

The call also comes amid discussions in the US Senate on a Russia sanctions bill, which has implications for India's energy imports. New Delhi has maintained a careful balancing act, continuing to buy Russian crude while deepening strategic ties with Washington.

Observers note that the frequency of high-level calls between the two countries reflects a maturing relationship that can handle differences on specific issues without derailing the overall trajectory.

Neither side has indicated when the next in-person meeting between Modi and Vance might take place, but diplomatic channels remain active.

As the US moves closer to presidential elections next year, India will be watching closely to see if the momentum in bilateral ties is sustained. For now, the telephone call is a signal that both capitals are keen to keep the partnership on an upward curve.
