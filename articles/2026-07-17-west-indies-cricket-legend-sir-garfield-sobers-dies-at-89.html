West Indies cricket legend Sir Garfield Sobers dies at 89

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Breaking News Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sir Garfield Sobers, the iconic West Indies all-rounder, has died at 89.
  • Widely hailed as cricket's original superstar, Sobers set records that stood for decades.
  • His legacy includes the famous six sixes in an over and unmatched all-round excellence.

Sir Garfield Sobers, widely regarded as the greatest all-rounder cricket has ever seen, passed away on Friday at the age of 89. The West Indies legend's death was confirmed by the International Cricket Council (ICC), which released a statement remembering his unmatched legacy in the game.

Sobers died in his native Barbados. No official cause of death has been released by his family or the ICC.

The original superstar of world cricket

Born in Bridgetown, Barbados, in 1936, Sobers made his Test debut for the West Indies in 1954 at just 17. Over the next two decades, he turned the sport on its head. He was a batsman of sublime grace, a bowler who could operate at pace or spin, and a fielder of breathtaking athleticism.

By the time he retired in 1974, Sobers had amassed 8,032 Test runs at an average of 57.78, with 26 centuries. He also took 235 wickets with his left-arm seam and spin bowling. In 1958, at age 22, he set the world record for the highest individual Test score — 365 not out against Pakistan in Kingston. That record stood for 36 years.

The six sixes that became folklore

In 1968, playing for Nottinghamshire against Glamorgan, Sobers became the first batsman in first-class cricket to hit six sixes in a single over. The feat, achieved off the bowling of Malcolm Nash, remains one of the most celebrated moments in the game's history. It epitomised his audacity and skill.

Sobers was knighted in 1975 for his services to cricket. He was also named one of the five Wisden Cricketers of the Century in 2000, alongside Sir Don Bradman, Sir Jack Hobbs, Shane Warne and Sir Vivian Richards. For many, he was the most complete cricketer to have played the game.

A life lived for the sport

After retiring, Sobers remained a revered figure in world cricket. He served as a mentor and ambassador for the West Indies and the ICC. He was a constant presence at major events, offering wisdom to generations of players who followed him.

His passing has drawn tributes from across the cricketing world. The West Indies Cricket Board called him "the embodiment of Caribbean genius." The ICC described him as "a player who redefined what was possible on a cricket field." Social media was flooded with messages from fans and former players, all acknowledging his singular place in the sport.

What comes next

Funeral arrangements for Sir Garfield Sobers are expected to be announced in the coming days. Barbados is likely to hold a state funeral for its most famous son. The cricketing world will pause to honour a man who was not just a player, but the very definition of greatness in sport.
