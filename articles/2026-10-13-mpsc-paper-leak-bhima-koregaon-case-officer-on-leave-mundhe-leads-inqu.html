High-Level Inquiry Committee Formed After MPSC Paper Leak

The Maharashtra government on Sunday placed Additional Director General of Police (MPSC) Dharmendra Bhimanwar on leave, hours after the state cabinet approved the constitution of a high-level committee to investigate the alleged leak of the Maharashtra Public Service Commission (MPSC) preliminary examination question paper. The move comes amid mounting pressure from student organisations and opposition parties demanding a thorough probe into the recruitment scandal.

Committee Mandate and Leadership

The five-member inquiry committee, headed by retired Bombay High Court Chief Justice Naresh Patil, will be assisted by Tukaram Mundhe as its secretary. Mundhe, a senior IAS officer and former Maharashtra CEO, was entrusted with the responsibility due to his administrative experience and credibility. The committee’s scope includes examining the role of current and former chairpersons and members of the MPSC, as well as identifying systemic lapses that led to the breach.

Student Protests Withdrawn After Assurance

Following the government’s action, the Maharashtra State Students’ Federation and other youth groups announced the withdrawal of their 10-day-long agitation. The protests, which had gathered momentum across Mumbai, Pune, and Nagpur, were called off after the protesting bodies received written assurance that the inquiry would be impartial and time-bound. Congress leader Rahul Gandhi expressed support for the students, terming the development a “big victory for youth seeking merit-based recruitment.”

  • Transparency Push: The appointment of a retired high court chief justice signals political intent to insulate the probe.
  • Administrative Continuity: Mundhe’s role ensures coordination between the committee and state machinery.
  • Broader Accountability: Review of past MPSC tenures may uncover long-standing structural issues.
  • Political Sensitivity: Bhimanwar’s leave indicates potential influence of political pressure on bureaucratic decisions.

Timeline and Next Steps

The committee has been directed to submit its report within 60 days. Sources within the state personnel department indicated that the panel may also recommend corrective actions, including changes to the MPSC examination protocol and enhanced cybersecurity measures. The Siddaram Kumar committee, constituted earlier this month, had recommended a detailed review of the MPSC’s operational framework. Its findings formed the basis for the cabinet’s decision to initiate a fresh inquiry.

Civil society groups have welcomed the move but cautioned that implementation of recommendations will be critical. The episode has once again highlighted the vulnerabilities in India’s public service recruitment ecosystem, where lakhs of aspirants compete annually for limited government positions.
