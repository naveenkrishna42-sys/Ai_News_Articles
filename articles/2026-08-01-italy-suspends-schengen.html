Italy Suspends Schengen

📅 2026-08-01 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Italy suspends Schengen agreement with Spain
  • Migrant crisis in Ceuta causes diplomatic tension
  • PM Meloni calls the move an 'extraordinary measure'

Italy has suspended the Schengen agreement with Spain, citing the recent migrant crisis in Ceuta as the reason. The move has been described as an 'extraordinary measure' by Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni.

Background of the Crisis

The migrant crisis in Ceuta, a Spanish territory in North Africa, has been escalating in recent days. Thousands of migrants have attempted to cross the border from Morocco, resulting in a significant influx of people into the territory. The situation has put a strain on the resources and infrastructure of Ceuta, leading to concerns about the welfare of both the migrants and the local population.

International Response

The response to the crisis from European Union countries has been mixed, with some countries being accused of being 'selfish' in their approach. Spain has been critical of the response from some EU nations, stating that they are not doing enough to help manage the crisis. The situation has led to diplomatic tensions between Spain and other EU countries, including Italy.

Some of the migrants who crossed into Ceuta have reported poor treatment and a lack of resources, with many opting to return to Morocco the day after entering Spain. The humanitarian situation in Ceuta remains a concern, with reports of inadequate food and shelter for the migrants.

Humanitarian Toll

The crisis has also had a significant humanitarian toll, with at least 67 people reported dead in Ceuta. The exact circumstances of the deaths are not yet clear, but officials have confirmed that the number of fatalities is likely to rise as more information becomes available.

Future Implications

The suspension of the Schengen agreement between Italy and Spain is likely to have significant implications for migration policy in the European Union. The move may prompt other EU countries to re-evaluate their own migration policies and consider similar measures to manage the flow of migrants into their territories.
