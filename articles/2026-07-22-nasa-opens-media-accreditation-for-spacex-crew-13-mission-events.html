NASA Opens Media Accreditation for SpaceX Crew-13 Mission Events

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Science Original source ↗
Key points
  • Media accreditation is open for Crew-13 mission events in Houston.
  • NASA will hold briefings and activities ahead of the SpaceX launch.
  • Crew-13 will transport astronauts to the International Space Station.

Media Accreditation Open for Houston Events

NASA has invited journalists to apply for media accreditation to cover events related to the SpaceX Crew-13 mission. The activities will take place in Houston, Texas, ahead of the launch to the International Space Station (ISS).

The space agency issued a media advisory on Tuesday, July 21, 2026, detailing the process for reporters seeking access to briefings and other mission-related events. The advisory marks the start of formal preparations for public coverage of the flight.

Briefings and Launch Preparations

NASA will host a series of briefings for accredited media, providing details on the Crew-13 mission objectives, the crew, and the SpaceX Dragon spacecraft. These sessions are standard procedure before any crewed launch.

The briefings will cover technical aspects of the mission, including the launch schedule, docking procedures, and experiments planned for the ISS. Media will also have opportunities to interview astronauts and mission managers.

Crew-13 Mission Details

Crew-13 is part of NASA's Commercial Crew Program, which relies on SpaceX to ferry astronauts to and from the space station. The mission will carry four crew members to the ISS for a science expedition.

While specific crew names and exact launch dates are yet to be confirmed in the advisory, the mission continues the regular rotation of astronauts that began with Crew-1 in 2020. Each mission typically lasts about six months.

Application Process for Journalists

Journalists interested in covering the Houston events must submit accreditation requests through NASA's media portal. The agency sets deadlines and provides credentials for access to NASA facilities and briefings.

NASA advises media to apply early, as slots may be limited. International journalists are also eligible but must comply with additional visa and security requirements.

Looking Ahead

NASA is expected to release further details on the Crew-13 crew and launch timeline in the coming weeks. The mission will mark another milestone in public-private spaceflight collaborations.
