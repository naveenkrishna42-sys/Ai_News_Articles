Bengaluru's SIR North, South achieve 100% form digitisation

📅 2026-08-04 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • SIR North and South Bengaluru zones have digitised all forms
  • Move aims to streamline citizen services and reduce paperwork
  • Officials say this is a step towards a fully digital administrative system

Administrative milestone for Bengaluru's SIR zones

The South Interior Revenue (SIR) zones of North and South Bengaluru have achieved full digitisation of all their forms, officials confirmed on Tuesday. The development marks a significant shift in how the city's revenue department handles citizen-facing paperwork.

According to the Bengaluru district administration, every form used by these two zones—ranging from property records to caste and income certificates—has been converted into a digital format. The process is now complete, with the digital versions available for applicants through official channels.

This move is expected to cut down on physical visits to government offices and reduce the time taken to process applications. Officials say the digitisation will also help in better tracking and transparency of pending requests.

What led to the change

The initiative is part of a broader push by the state government to modernise revenue administration. Over the past year, several Bengaluru zones have been gradually shifting to online submissions, but SIR North and South are the first to achieve complete coverage.

A senior official from the revenue department said the transition was completed after months of groundwork, including training staff and updating backend systems. "We wanted to ensure that no form is left behind, and now citizens can access every service without having to rely on paper," the official noted.

The digitisation also includes forms that were previously only available in physical format at village offices and taluk centres. These have now been uploaded to the department's portal, with clear instructions for applicants.

Impact on citizens

For residents, the change means faster processing of applications and fewer errors. Earlier, incomplete or incorrect forms often led to repeated visits. With digital submission, the system validates entries before they are accepted, reducing such glitches.

Local activists have welcomed the move but are watching how implementation pans out. "Digitisation is good, but the real test is whether the backend works smoothly. If servers crash or there is no support for those who are not tech-savvy, the benefit could be lost," said a resident welfare association member in north Bengaluru.

Authorities have assured that help desks and support staff are available at all SIR offices for those who need assistance with online submissions.

What happens next

The focus now shifts to other zones in Bengaluru, which are expected to follow suit in the coming months. The revenue department has indicated that a district-wide rollout is on the cards, with the SIR North and South experience serving as a template.

Officials are also working on integrating these forms with the state's common service centres and mobile apps, so that citizens can apply from anywhere. The next few months will show how smoothly this expansion proceeds and whether the digital-first approach delivers on its promise of efficiency.
