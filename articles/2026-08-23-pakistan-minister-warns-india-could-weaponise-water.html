Pakistan minister warns India could weaponise water

Pakistan minister warns India could weaponise water

Pakistan's Minister for Climate Change has warned that India could use water as a weapon in the ongoing dispute over the Indus Waters Treaty. The treaty, which governs the sharing of water between India and Pakistan, has been in abeyance since 2019.

Indus Waters Treaty remains in abeyance

The Indus Waters Treaty, signed in 1960, has been in abeyance since 2019 due to the ongoing dispute between India and Pakistan. The treaty has been a source of tension between the two countries, with each side accusing the other of violating its provisions.

Ex-Punjab official proposes law to abrogate treaty

An ex-Punjab Special Chief Secretary has proposed a law to abrogate the Indus Waters Treaty. The law, which has not yet been implemented, would allow India to unilaterally terminate the treaty if it is violated by Pakistan. The proposal has been met with criticism from Pakistan, which sees it as a violation of international law.

Ratle Hydroelectric Project raises concerns

The Ratle Hydroelectric Project, located in the Chenab River basin, has raised concerns about the transparency of its design and data. The project, which is being developed by the Indian government, has been a source of tension between India and Pakistan. Pakistan has accused India of violating the Indus Waters Treaty by diverting water from the Chenab River for the project.
