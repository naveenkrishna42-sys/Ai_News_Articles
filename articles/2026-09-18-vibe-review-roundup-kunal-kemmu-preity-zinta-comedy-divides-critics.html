'Vibe' Review Roundup: A Comedy That Splits Critics and Crowds

Kunal Kemmu's spy comedy 'Vibe', headlined by Preity Zinta, has landed in theatres with a sharply divided reception. Critics at The Hindu and Telegraph India acknowledge the film's comic energy but flag a sprawling runtime and a second half that drifts. Audiences on X, meanwhile, have pushed a far warmer verdict, with 'popcorn entertainer' emerging as the dominant phrase. The gap between professional and public response is now the film's central story.

Shilpa Shetty and Raj Kundra's appearance in the promotional cycle added mainstream entertainment-page traction, but the film's commercial fate will rest on word of mouth rather than star cameos.

What the Critics Actually Said

The Hindu's review describes 'Vibe' as a "rudderless, overlong comedy" in which Kemmu, making his writing-directing debut, aims for stars but struggles to hold the narrative together. Telegraph India is kinder on the humour, calling it a spy comedy with "plenty of laughs" that nonetheless loses its way. Both reviews converge on the same structural complaint: the film's middle stretch sags under a runtime that outlasts its premise.

That is a meaningful signal for buyers of tickets this weekend. A comedy that opens strong and fades is a different proposition from one that builds — and the critical consensus here points to the former.

Audience Verdict on X

Fan reaction tells a different story. The Times of India's audience review roundup notes viewers calling 'Vibe' a "popcorn entertainer", with praise centred on the humour and the leads' chemistry. One widely shared X post went further, labelling it the "best comedy film of this year". Such superlatives rarely survive a second week, but they do drive opening-weekend footfalls.

Pros and Cons for Ticket Buyers

  • Pro: Kemmu and Zinta share easy comic timing, and the spy-comedy premise delivers consistent laughs in the first half.
  • Pro: Strong opening-weekend audience energy makes it a viable group watch, especially for viewers wanting a light, low-stakes outing.
  • Pro: Zinta's return to mainstream comedy is a genuine draw for audiences who grew up on her 2000s work.
  • Con: An overlong runtime with a meandering second half, flagged independently by two major dailies.
  • Con: A first-time director's structural wobble means the film's tone shifts unevenly between parody and plot.
  • Con: Critical and audience scores diverge sharply — expect the online hype to cool once the weekend crowd weighs in.

The Commercial Picture

For a mid-budget comedy, the first three days decide the trajectory. Positive X chatter and 'popcorn entertainer' framing typically convert into steady weekday occupancy, provided the film holds screens past Monday. If the second-half criticism hardens into a common complaint among paying viewers, the drop-off will be steep — a pattern familiar from several recent Hindi comedies that opened on hype and faded by week two.

Kemmu's dual role as actor and director also invites a specific kind of scrutiny. Debut directors who also headline their own films often struggle to separate performance from pacing in the edit, and both major reviews hint at exactly that problem. His next project will be judged against how 'Vibe' performs beyond its opening weekend, not against its trailer.

What to Expect

Viewers wanting a breezy, joke-forward outing with two likeable leads will find 'Vibe' serviceable. Those expecting a tightly plotted spy comedy should temper expectations — the critics' shared complaint about a lost second half is specific and repeated. The film's real test is whether the audience goodwill on X outlasts the reviews it is currently contradicting.
