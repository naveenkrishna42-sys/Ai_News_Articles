GBA Records 1,40,277 Tonnes of Waste Cleared in August 2026

The Greater Bengaluru Authority (GBA) has reported clearing 1,40,277 tonnes of municipal solid waste during August 2026. The figure covers door-to-door collection, street sweeping, and bulk waste operations carried out across the erstwhile Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) limits and the seven surrounding urban local bodies that now fall under the unified GBA framework.

For residents tracking civic performance, the headline number translates into a daily clearance average of roughly 4,525 tonnes. At an estimated city population of 1.45 crore, that works out to about 312 grams of waste handled per capita each day, which sits in line with Tier-1 Indian urban averages of 300 to 450 grams.

What the Tonnage Actually Includes

GBA officials have clarified that the consolidated figure aggregates wet, dry, domestic hazardous, construction and demolition debris, and sanitary waste lifted through authorised vehicles. However, the public dashboard does not yet disaggregate the share of legacy BBMP wards versus the merged municipalities of Dasarahalli, Mahadevapura, Bommanahalli, Yelahanka, Rajarajeshwari Nagar, Kengeri, and Byatarayanapura.

This is a critical gap for civic auditors, because the 198 wards of the former BBMP historically generated close to 65 percent of the city's waste, while the newer ULBs are still scaling up formal collection infrastructure.

Operational Context and Contractor Workload

Bengaluru's solid waste management value chain is dominated by zonal contracts awarded to firms such as Sai Environment, KCDC, and a handful of joint ventures. Each zone typically deploys 350 to 500 vehicles, including auto-tippers, compactor lorries, and skip loaders. At 4,525 tonnes daily, the fleet would need to complete more than 8,000 trips assuming an average compactor payload of five tonnes, a logistical stretch that has historically pushed contractors to engage subcontractors for last-mile collection.

The August clearance, if verified against weighbridge data at the nine legacy landfills and processing sites, suggests that the GBA's primary contractors retained near-full deployment even during monsoon disruptions, when leachate volumes and road damage typically reduce daily throughput by 10 to 15 percent.

What Citizens and Buyers Should Track

For residents, the practical takeaway is reliability of doorstep collection rather than headline tonnage. Bengaluru households and gated communities should look for consistency of timing, segregation enforcement, and zero-tolerance on mixed loads that attract fines up to ₹10,000 under Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016.

For bulk waste generators, including commercial establishments, hotels, and IT campuses, the figure underscores the importance of onboarding with authorised aggregators. The city's bulk waste processing capacity at facilities such as Terra Green, Hasiru Dala, and the KCDC plants remains underutilised, creating an opportunity for private players.

Pros and Cons of the August Performance

  • Clearance volume crossed 1.40 lakh tonnes, indicating that zonal contractors maintained near-full deployment despite monsoon.
  • Daily per-capita figure of 312 grams aligns with national Tier-1 averages, suggesting baseline collection efficiency.
  • Public reporting now extends beyond BBMP wards to all seven merged ULBs under the GBA.
  • No published breakdown of wet versus dry waste, limiting visibility on segregation outcomes.
  • No data on landfill diversion rate, processing efficiency, or compost output for August 2026.
  • Weighted verification at landfills and processing plants has not been disclosed alongside the headline number.

Outlook and Verification Questions

The GBA's centralisation of waste reporting is a step towards transparency, but the September 5 disclosure leaves key questions unanswered. Civic groups such as the Solid Waste Management Round Table and Hasiru Dala will likely demand granular data on source segregation, scientific processing compliance, and the share of legacy BBMP tonnes versus merged-ULB tonnes.

Until that data is released, the 1,40,277-tonne figure should be read as a clearance number, not a sustainability outcome. Residents and bulk generators should continue to demand segregated lifting and verified weighbridge reports before assessing whether Bengaluru's waste system is truly meeting its 100 percent door-to-door coverage mandate.
