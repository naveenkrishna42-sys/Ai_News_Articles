Three IPOs, One Signal: Primary Market Appetite Is Back

The Indian primary market is heating up again. Grey market premiums (GMPs) for three upcoming mainboard issues — NSE, Hero Motors and Jindal Supreme — have climbed as high as 29% ahead of their subscription windows this week. The activity marks a sharp reversal from the slump that followed several muted listings earlier this year. For retail and HNI investors, the GMP trend is the first real-time indicator of how aggressively these issues may be bid.

GMP is not an official price. It reflects unregulated, off-market deals where traders wager on the likely listing price. It can swing wildly within days, especially once subscription data and anchor allocations are disclosed.

NSE IPO: India's Largest Bourse Lists Its Own Shares

The National Stock Exchange issue carries unusual significance. NSE is India's largest exchange by turnover, and its own listing has been anticipated for years. Reports indicate that major shareholders including SBI, GIC and Bank of Baroda have trimmed portions of their stake-sale plans — a signal that long-term holders expect further value appreciation. The IPO is structured as an offer for sale, meaning the company itself raises no fresh capital; proceeds go to selling shareholders.

One question dominating market chatter: why can't NSE trade on its own platform after listing? As a listed company, NSE shares will trade on rival exchanges, since an exchange cannot list itself. That is standard global practice and not a red flag, but investors should understand the mechanics before bidding.

Hero Motors and Jindal Supreme: What the Numbers Say

Hero Motors brings a recognised brand from the Hero Group ecosystem into the capital markets, riding on India's two-wheeler and mobility demand. Jindal Supreme adds to the steel and infrastructure-linked pipeline. Both issues are benefiting from improved sentiment and expectations of healthy listing pops. Key Real-World Highlights

  • GMP of up to 29% suggests strong listing-day expectations, but premiums can evaporate if subscription demand disappoints.
  • NSE's OFS structure means listing gains accrue to selling shareholders, not the exchange's balance sheet.
  • Reduced stake-sale plans by SBI, GIC and Bank of Baroda signal confidence in post-listing valuation.
  • Hero Motors offers brand familiarity; Jindal Supreme offers sector exposure to steel and infrastructure.
  • Anchor investor allocations and final subscription figures will be the true test of demand.

Should You Bid? Valuation and Risk Check

GMP is a sentiment gauge, not a valuation tool. Investors should compare each issue's price-to-earnings ratio against listed peers. NSE, as a market infrastructure business, typically commands premium valuations due to stable transaction-fee revenue. Hero Motors will be judged on margins, EV transition plans and competitive intensity against TVS, Bajaj and Ola Electric. Jindal Supreme's prospects hinge on steel demand cycles and infrastructure spending.

Apply with a clear exit strategy. If listing gains meet your target, book profits; if the stock slips below issue price, assess whether the business thesis still holds rather than averaging blindly. The week's subscription data — especially HNI and QIB portions — will reveal whether the 29% GMP is justified or merely hype.

Bottom Line for Investors

The return of strong GMPs across NSE, Hero Motors and Jindal Supreme indicates that primary market confidence is rebuilding. NSE is the headline act, but its OFS structure and self-listing mechanics require careful reading. Hero Motors and Jindal Supreme offer sector-specific bets with their own risk profiles. Track the subscription numbers daily, and treat GMP as a directional hint — never as a guarantee.
