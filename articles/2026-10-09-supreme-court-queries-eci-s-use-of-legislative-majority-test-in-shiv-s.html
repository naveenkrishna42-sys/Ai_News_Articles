Supreme Court Queries ECI’s Use of Legislative Majority Test in Shiv Sena Symbol Row

Background of the Shiv Sena Symbol Dispute

The Shiv Sena split in mid‑2022 produced two rival factions: the Uddhav Thackeray‑led group and the Eknath Shinde‑led faction. After the Election Commission of India (ECI) recognised the Shinde faction as the legitimate party and allotted it the official bow‑and‑arrow symbol, the Thackeray camp moved the Supreme Court, alleging that the decision violated the party’s constitution and the anti‑defection law.

Supreme Court’s Scrutiny of the Legislative Majority Test

During hearings, a bench led by Justice S. A. Nazeer questioned the ECI’s reliance on a ‘legislative majority’ test to decide symbol ownership. The Court asked why the commission treated the number of MLAs supporting a faction as the decisive factor, ignoring internal party procedures, constitutional submissions, and the timing of the split. The justices highlighted that the test appeared to conflate electoral strength with organizational legitimacy.

ECI’s Rationale and Legal Challenges

The Election Commission defended its approach by citing paragraph 15 of the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, which permits consideration of the strength of legislative wings when rival claims arise. The ECI argued that, in the absence of a clear party constitution submitted by the Thackeray faction, the legislative majority provided an objective benchmark. Critics, however, pointed out that the Thackeray side had submitted a revised constitution shortly before the split, a fact the ECI allegedly overlooked.

Implications for Party Law and Electoral Politics

Legal scholars warn that endorsing a legislative majority test could encourage parties to prioritize defections over internal democratic processes, potentially weakening the anti‑defection law’s purpose of ensuring stability. Conversely, a strict adherence to internal party documents might lead to frequent litigation whenever a faction splits. The pending judgment may thus set a precedent on how the ECI balances organizational integrity with electoral realities in future symbol disputes.

Key Takeaways

  • The Supreme Court’s questioning signals a possible shift away from purely numeric criteria in symbol allocation.
  • If the Court rejects the legislative majority test, parties may need to strengthen internal governance to avoid electoral disadvantages.
  • The decision could influence how the ECI handles similar splits in regional parties across India.
  • Observers expect clearer guidelines that combine legislative strength with constitutional adherence.
  • The outcome will affect not only the Shiv Sena but also the broader framework of party recognition in Indian democracy.
