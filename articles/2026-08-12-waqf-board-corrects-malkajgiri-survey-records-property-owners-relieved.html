Waqf Board corrects Malkajgiri survey records, property owners relieved

📅 2026-08-12 📂 Hyderabad Original source ↗
Key points
  • Waqf Board initiates correction of survey records in Malkajgiri
  • Property owners in the area had faced uncertainty over land titles
  • Move expected to resolve long-pending disputes and ease documentation

Property owners in Malkajgiri can breathe a little easier now that the Telangana State Waqf Board has begun the process of correcting survey records in the area. The move comes after years of complaints from residents who found their land titles entangled in discrepancies within the board's documentation.

Long-pending issue addressed

For several years, owners of plots and buildings in parts of Malkajgiri have struggled with mismatched survey numbers and unclear boundaries. These errors, they said, hampered everything from obtaining bank loans to registering sale deeds. Many feared their properties could be claimed as Waqf land due to the faulty records.

The Waqf Board's decision to rectify the survey data is being seen as a significant step toward removing that uncertainty. Officials have indicated that the correction process will be carried out systematically, with surveys being re-verified on the ground before records are updated.

How the correction process works

According to sources, the board has initiated a field-level verification exercise. Revenue officials and Waqf Board surveyors are jointly inspecting the disputed parcels to match physical boundaries with existing records. Once the verification is complete, corrected entries will be incorporated into the official land records.

Property owners have been asked to cooperate with the survey teams and produce relevant documents, such as sale deeds and occupancy certificates, to expedite the process. The board has also set up a grievance redressal mechanism for those who wish to raise objections during the correction window.

Relief for residents and buyers

Residents of Malkajgiri, a densely populated suburb on the city's northern fringe, have welcomed the initiative. Many had been living with the fear that their homes or commercial establishments could be declared Waqf property, a worry that had depressed property values and made transactions difficult.

Real estate agents in the area say the correction will likely boost buyer confidence. With clean survey records, owners can finally proceed with registrations and mutations without legal hurdles. The move is also expected to reduce disputes between neighbours over boundary lines, which often stem from outdated or erroneous government maps.

Background of the dispute

The issue dates back to colonial-era survey maps that were never updated after urban expansion. Over the decades, as Malkajgiri grew, the original survey numbers became increasingly out of sync with actual ground conditions. The Waqf Board's records, in some cases, overlapped with private holdings, creating a tangled web of claims.

Previous attempts to resolve the matter had stalled due to procedural delays and lack of coordination between departments. This time, the board appears to be acting on a directive from higher authorities, though specific orders have not been made public yet.

What remains to be done

While the initiation of corrections is a positive sign, officials caution that the full process could take several months. The board needs to complete physical surveys, cross-check with revenue department data, and publish revised lists for public scrutiny before finalising the records.

Meanwhile, property owners are advised to stay vigilant and track the progress of their specific cases. Those with unresolved objections may need to approach the board's local office with supporting evidence. The coming weeks will show whether this administrative push translates into lasting clarity for Malkajgiri's property market.
