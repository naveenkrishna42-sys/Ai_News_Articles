Trump pauses Iran strikes after 13 days; munitions shortage questioned

📅 2026-07-26 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • US President Donald Trump has paused military strikes on Iran after 13 consecutive days of operations.
  • Reports from multiple sources question whether a shortage of precision-guided munitions forced the pause.
  • Iran has agreed to halt its attacks as long as the US maintains the pause, according to a Reuters report.
  • A US official told NDTV the pause is intended to give peace talks 'some space'.

Thirteen Days of Strikes Come to a Halt

US President Donald Trump has paused military strikes against Iran after 13 days of continuous operations, a decision that has sparked intense debate over its motivations. The halt, first reported by India Today, follows a sustained campaign that began on July 13 and marked one of the longest uninterrupted bombing runs by the US in the region in recent years.

The New York Times reported that Trump held off on a major war escalation against Iran after advisers raised concerns. The pause comes as both sides consider the possibility of a negotiated settlement to the conflict, which has seen exchanges of missiles and drone attacks across the Middle East.

Munitions Shortage or Strategic Pause?

One of the most persistent questions surrounding the pause is whether the US military is running low on precision-guided munitions. Reports from several news outlets, including India Today, have hinted at logistical strains after nearly two weeks of high-tempo operations. The Pentagon has not officially commented on stockpile levels, but former military officials have pointed out that sustained bombing campaigns can deplete expensive guided bombs and missiles faster than they can be replenished.

Critics argue that a shortage would explain why the White House suddenly shifted course after projecting an aggressive posture. Supporters of the administration, however, maintain that the pause is purely diplomatic. A US official told NDTV that Trump paused the strikes to give peace talks 'some space'. The official did not elaborate on the specific nature of the ongoing negotiations.

Iran's Conditional Response

Iran has responded with its own conditional offer. According to a Reuters report, an Iranian source said Tehran will halt attacks as long as the US maintains its pause. This marks a potential de-escalation after weeks of tit-for-tat violence that raised fears of a broader regional war. The Iranian government has not made any public statements beyond the anonymous source, though state media has reported positively on the development.

The Guardian confirmed that the US has paused attacks for a second day, with peace talks continuing behind the scenes. No details have emerged about the location or participants of these talks, but officials from several Gulf nations are believed to be acting as intermediaries.

What Led to the Conflict?

The 13-day bombing campaign was triggered by a series of attacks on US assets in the region, which Washington blamed on Iran-backed militias. The US retaliated with airstrikes on Iranian military positions in Iraq and Syria, and later on Iranian soil. Iran responded with missile strikes on US bases in Iraq, leading to a rapid escalation that neither side appeared to anticipate fully.

Casualty figures remain unclear. The Pentagon has not released a count of civilian or military deaths from the strikes, and Iran has provided only partial reports. Independent verification has been difficult due to restricted access in conflict zones.

What Happens Next

All eyes are now on the peace talks. If the pause holds, it could mark the beginning of a broader diplomatic process. But if either side perceives the other as using the pause to resupply or reposition, the fragile calm could collapse quickly. The coming days will reveal whether this is a genuine step toward peace or just a temporary breather before another round of fire.
