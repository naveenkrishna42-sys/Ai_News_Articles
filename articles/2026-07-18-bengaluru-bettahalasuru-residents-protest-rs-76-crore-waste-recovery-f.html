Bengaluru: Bettahalasuru residents protest Rs 76-crore waste recovery facility

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Residents of Bettahalasuru are protesting a Rs 76-crore waste recovery facility proposed in their area.
  • The protest stems from fears of health hazards, pollution, and property devaluation.
  • Officials have not confirmed the timeline for the project or addressed resident concerns.

Residents of Bettahalasuru, a village on the outskirts of Bengaluru, have taken to the streets to protest a proposed Rs 76-crore waste recovery facility. The project, which aims to process solid waste from the city, has sparked fears of health risks and environmental damage among locals.

What is the protest about?

The facility, planned on a 20-acre plot, is intended to handle up to 500 tonnes of waste daily. But residents argue that the location is too close to residential areas, schools, and water bodies. They worry that the plant will release toxic fumes, contaminate groundwater, and lower property values.

"We are not against development, but this facility will ruin our lives," said a local resident who participated in the protest. The demonstrators blocked a major road near the site on Friday, demanding that the government scrap the project.

Why are residents concerned?

Health concerns are at the forefront. Residents recall past incidents where waste processing plants in other parts of Bengaluru led to respiratory issues and foul odours. The Bettahalasuru area already grapples with poor waste management, and locals fear the new facility will worsen the situation.

Environmental activists have also voiced support for the protest. They point out that the site is near a lake, which could be polluted by leachate from the plant. The Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP), which is backing the project, has not yet released an environmental impact assessment report.

What is the government saying?

Officials from BBMP have defended the project, calling it essential to tackle Bengaluru's mounting waste crisis. The city generates over 4,000 tonnes of solid waste daily, and existing facilities are stretched thin. The proposed plant, they claim, will use advanced technology to minimise pollution.

But residents remain unconvinced. They have demanded a public hearing and a detailed study before any construction begins. The protest is expected to continue until the government addresses their demands.

What happens next?

Talks between resident groups and BBMP officials are scheduled for next week. The outcome of these discussions will determine whether the project moves forward or faces further delays. For now, the residents of Bettahalasuru are standing firm, hoping their voices will be heard over the roar of bulldozers.
