Freelance journalist Shaurya Thapa says most Bollywood films now praise the government

📅 2026-07-29 📂 Movies Original source ↗
Key points
  • Shaurya Thapa, a freelance film journalist and content creator, says most Bollywood films now praise the government.
  • He notes a visible shift from earlier cinema that often critiqued those in power.
  • Thapa's comments come amid ongoing debate about censorship and creative freedom in the Indian film industry.

Freelance film journalist and content creator Shaurya Thapa has made a pointed observation about the current state of Bollywood. In a recent interview with Mid-Day, Thapa stated that most mainstream Hindi films today openly praise the government. This marks a significant departure from earlier decades when Bollywood often took a critical stance toward those in power.

Shift in narrative tone

Thapa, who has been covering the industry for years, said the change is hard to miss. He pointed out that films now routinely feature dialogues and plot points that glorify the ruling establishment. This trend, he argued, reflects a broader realignment of the film industry's relationship with political authority.

The journalist did not name specific movies or governments. But his comment adds to a growing chorus of voices questioning whether Bollywood is exercising true creative freedom. Some filmmakers have defended the shift, saying it mirrors public sentiment. Others fear it signals a chilling effect on dissent.

Context of the debate

Thapa's remarks come at a time when the Indian film industry is under intense scrutiny. Several high-profile films in recent years have been accused of carrying a pro-government message. Critics argue this is partly driven by the need to avoid trouble with censors or online mobs.

Supporters of the trend say cinema has always reflected the mood of the nation. They argue that if the public supports the government, films will naturally reflect that. Thapa, however, sees it differently. He believes the change is more about self-censorship than organic storytelling.

What this means for Bollywood

For decades, Bollywood produced films that questioned authority. From political dramas to social satires, the industry often punched up. Thapa's observation suggests that era may be fading. He noted that even films not directly about politics now include lines or scenes that seem designed to please the government.

This has implications for the kind of stories being told. If writers and directors feel constrained, audiences may see fewer films that challenge the status quo. Thapa did not offer a solution, but his comment serves as a warning about the state of creative expression in India's most popular film industry.

Reactions and silence

The interview has sparked discussion on social media. Some users agreed with Thapa, sharing examples of recent films they felt were overly deferential. Others dismissed his view as an overgeneralisation. The journalist has not responded to the backlash. He remains focused on his work as a freelance critic and content creator.

No major filmmaker or studio has publicly commented on Thapa's statement. This silence itself is telling, according to some observers. It suggests that even acknowledging the trend is considered risky in the current climate.

What happens next will depend on whether more voices in the industry speak up. For now, Thapa's observation stands as a sharp, unsentimental look at where Bollywood stands today. The audience, as always, will decide if they want to watch films that praise power or question it.
