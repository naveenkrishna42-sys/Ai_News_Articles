Skyroot Aerospace to launch India's first private orbital rocket Vikram-1 on July 18

📅 2026-07-17 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Vikram-1, developed by Skyroot Aerospace, will be India's first privately built orbital-class rocket to launch.
  • The rocket will carry micro-sculptures of Indian icons and a special message from Prime Minister Narendra Modi.
  • The launch is scheduled for July 18, marking a milestone for India's private space sector.

Historic Launch for India's Private Space Sector

Skyroot Aerospace is set to script history on July 18 with the launch of Vikram-1, India's first privately developed orbital-class rocket. The mission marks a significant leap for the country's private space industry, which has been gaining momentum since the sector was opened to private players in 2020.

Vikram-1 is a three-stage, solid-fuel rocket capable of placing small satellites into low Earth orbit. The launch is scheduled from the Satish Dhawan Space Centre in Sriharikota, the same facility used by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) for its missions.

Payload: Micro-Sculptures of Indian Icons and PM Modi's Message

The rocket will carry a unique payload — micro-sculptures of iconic Indian figures, including Mahatma Gandhi, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, and other national heroes. These miniature artworks, etched onto a small plaque, are designed to inspire a new generation of space enthusiasts.

In addition, Vikram-1 will also carry a special message from Prime Minister Narendra Modi, the contents of which have not been disclosed. Skyroot Aerospace officials said the message reflects the government's support for private enterprise in space exploration.

Skyroot's Journey: From Startup to Orbital Launch

Hyderabad-based Skyroot Aerospace was founded in 2018 by former ISRO scientists. The company previously launched a sub-orbital test vehicle, Vikram-S, in November 2022, which successfully reached an altitude of 89.5 km. That mission made Skyroot the first private Indian company to launch a rocket into space.

Vikram-1 is significantly more advanced, designed to deliver payloads of up to 480 kg to a 500 km orbit. The rocket uses carbon composite structures and 3D-printed components, technologies that Skyroot says reduce costs and production time. The launch vehicle is named after Dr. Vikram Sarabhai, the father of India's space program.

Support from ISRO and Government Policies

The Indian National Space Promotion and Authorization Centre (IN-SPACe), the nodal agency for private space activities, has facilitated the launch. ISRO has provided technical support and access to its launch facilities. The government's 2020 space sector reforms allowed private companies to build and launch rockets, a domain previously reserved for ISRO.

Skyroot has raised over $90 million in funding from investors including GIC, the sovereign wealth fund of Singapore, and others. The company plans to offer commercial launch services at competitive rates, targeting the growing global market for small satellite launches.

What This Means for India's Space Economy

India's space economy is estimated to be worth $8 billion, with projections to reach $40 billion by 2040. The success of Vikram-1 could spur further investment in the sector, encouraging other startups like Agnikul Cosmos and Bellatrix Aerospace to accelerate their plans. Experts say private participation is crucial for India to capture a larger share of the global space market, which is expected to exceed $1 trillion in the coming decades.

Skyroot's achievement also positions India alongside the United States, where companies like SpaceX and Rocket Lab have pioneered private spaceflight. However, Vikram-1 is entirely designed and manufactured in India, with indigenous supply chains.

The launch on July 18 will be closely watched by the global space community. Weather permitting, the rocket will lift off in the morning hours. Skyroot has not yet announced a live-streaming plan, but details are expected closer to the date. If successful, Vikram-1 will not only carry Indian icons into orbit but also a nation's ambition to become a hub for affordable space access.
