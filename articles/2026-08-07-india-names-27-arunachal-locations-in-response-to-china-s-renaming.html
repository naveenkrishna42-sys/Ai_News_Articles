India names 27 Arunachal locations in response to China's renaming

📅 2026-08-07 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • India has officially named 27 locations in Arunachal Pradesh.
  • The move is a direct response to China's repeated renaming attempts.
  • The locations are now part of India's national maps.

In a firm rejoinder to China's persistent attempts to rename places in Arunachal Pradesh, India has officially assigned names to 27 locations in the state. The decision, announced on Friday, marks a significant step in India's efforts to assert its territorial claims and counter Beijing's 'map war' in the region.

The 27 locations, spread across the state, have now been formally added to India's national maps. This move is seen as a strategic response to China's repeated renaming of places in the area it claims as 'Zangnan' or South Tibet. The Indian government's decision underscores its resolve to protect the integrity of its borders and its administrative control over Arunachal Pradesh.

Strategic Response to China's Map Offensive

China has, over the years, engaged in a systematic campaign to rename several locations in Arunachal Pradesh, a practice New Delhi has consistently rejected. The latest Indian action is a direct counter to this, ensuring that the official names used by India are firmly established in cartographic and administrative records.

Officials familiar with the development said the naming exercise was a result of extensive deliberation and consultation. The names chosen reflect local traditions and geographical features, reinforcing the cultural and historical ties of these areas with India. While the full list of names has not been made public, the locations are believed to include areas of strategic importance along the Line of Actual Control (LAC).

Asserting Sovereignty Through Cartography

The inclusion of these 27 locations in national maps is a clear message from New Delhi. It signals that India will not remain passive to China's unilateral actions. By officially naming these places, India is strengthening its legal and administrative claims, which are crucial in any future diplomatic engagements or negotiations.

This is not the first time India has taken such a step. In recent years, the government has renamed several places in the region to reflect Indian perspectives, such as the naming of the 'Depsang Plains' and other strategic areas. The current move, however, is more comprehensive and targeted, covering a wider set of locations across the state.

Local and Diplomatic Reactions

The announcement has been welcomed by various quarters, including local communities and political leaders in Arunachal Pradesh. Many see this as a reaffirmation of the state's integral place within the Indian Union. The state has long been a point of contention, with China claiming the entire area, a stance India firmly rejects.

Diplomatic observers note that the timing of the announcement is significant. It comes amid ongoing talks between the two countries on border disputes, though no immediate official reaction from China has been reported. The Indian government has maintained that such actions are within its sovereign rights and are not aimed at escalating tensions but at clarifying its position.

The move also aligns with India's broader policy of strengthening infrastructure and administrative presence in border areas. Over the past few years, the government has invested heavily in roads, bridges, and other facilities in Arunachal Pradesh, ensuring that these regions are well-connected and integrated.

What to Watch Next

As India solidifies its cartographic claims, the onus is now on China to respond. New Delhi is likely to continue its efforts to map and name more locations in the region, reinforcing its position. The coming weeks may see further diplomatic exchanges, but India's resolve to protect its territory remains clear and unwavering.
