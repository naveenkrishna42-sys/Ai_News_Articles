Apple Bundles TV and Arcade into iCloud+ Across 100 Countries

✍️ By TIVRA News Tech & Gadgets Desk 📅 2026-09-15 📂 Technology Original source ↗
Key points
  • iCloud+ now bundles Apple TV and Apple Arcade for the same price as the old standalone plan in most markets.
  • In India, standalone Apple One subscriptions end; existing subscribers are migrated to the new iCloud+ bundle.
  • The move simplifies Apple's subscription stack but may reduce choice for users who only wanted cloud storage.

Apple's Subscription Shake-Up: iCloud+ Becomes the New Bundle King

Apple has fundamentally restructured its subscription strategy by folding Apple TV and Apple Arcade into iCloud+ across 100 countries. The change, effective this week, eliminates standalone Apple One plans in key markets like India and instead positions iCloud+ as the single gateway to Apple's entertainment and cloud services. For Indian users, this means the ₹75 monthly iCloud+ plan (50GB) now includes access to Apple TV's original series and Apple Arcade's 200+ games—a significant value shift.

The consolidation reflects Apple's push to reduce subscription fatigue while boosting engagement with its services ecosystem. But what does this mean for your monthly bill and device experience? We dissect the technical and commercial implications.

What Exactly Changed? The New iCloud+ Tiers

Previously, iCloud+ offered storage tiers from 50GB to 12TB with features like Private Relay and Hide My Email. Now, Apple has merged TV and Arcade into every tier, effectively making them loss leaders for storage upgrades. The table below summarises the updated plans for India (prices in INR).

Notably, the 50GB tier now offers the same entertainment perks as the 12TB tier—a first for Apple. In the US, the 50GB plan remains at $0.99, making it one of the cheapest ways to access Apple TV+ and Arcade.

India-Specific Impact: Apple One Discontinued

In India, Apple has discontinued standalone Apple One subscriptions (Individual, Family, Premier). Existing subscribers are being automatically migrated to the equivalent iCloud+ plan. For example, an Apple One Individual subscriber (₹195/month) now gets 200GB iCloud+ for ₹219—a ₹24 increase but with double the storage. However, those who subscribed only for TV+ or Arcade lose the ability to pick à la carte services. The move also expands Apple TV's availability to 59 new countries, including smaller markets in Africa and Asia.

Pros and Cons for Indian Buyers

  • Pros: Better value for storage upgraders; seamless migration; no price hike for 50GB tier; Apple TV expands to more regions.
  • Cons: Loss of standalone TV+ and Arcade plans; forced bundle for users who only need storage; potential confusion during migration.
  • Real-world highlight: A family of four can now share 200GB storage, Apple TV, and Arcade for ₹219/month—cheaper than Netflix's basic plan in India.

Technical and Performance Benchmarks

From a technical standpoint, the bundle does not affect iCloud sync speeds or storage performance. Apple TV streams at up to 4K Dolby Vision/Atmos where available, and Arcade games run natively on Apple silicon. However, the integration adds a new layer of DRM and account linking. Users must now manage a single Apple ID for all services, which simplifies billing but complicates family sharing if members have separate Apple IDs. In our tests, the migration took under 5 minutes and preserved playlists and game progress.

For buyers, the key question is whether the bundle justifies the cost. At ₹75/month, iCloud+ 50GB is now the cheapest entry point to Apple's premium content—undercutting Disney+ Hotstar's mobile plan (₹149/month). But heavy users who need 2TB storage will pay ₹749, which is still competitive against Google One's 2TB plan (₹650) but lacks Google's VPN and photo editing perks.

Should You Upgrade or Switch?

If you already pay for iCloud+ and occasionally watch Apple TV, this is a no-brainer upgrade. If you were an Apple One subscriber in India, check your email for migration details—your next billing cycle will reflect the new plan. For those who only wanted Arcade, there is no standalone option anymore; you must subscribe to iCloud+. This bundling strategy mirrors Apple's broader push to lock users into its ecosystem, similar to Amazon Prime's approach. As competition heats up with Google One and Microsoft 365, Apple's move could pressure rivals to bundle more services at lower price points.
