PM Modi Challenges Fintech Sector to Scale Agentic AI and Global UPI

Key points
  • Agentic AI and quantum computing are the new pillars for Indian fintech infrastructure.
  • The government aims to leverage UPI to drastically reduce remittance costs for the diaspora.
  • Indian fintechs are encouraged to shift from domestic dominance to global service expansion.

The Next Frontier: Agentic AI and Quantum Infrastructure

Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s address at the Global Fintech Fest (GFF) 2026 signals a definitive pivot in India’s digital economy strategy. Beyond standard digitization, the focus has shifted toward Agentic AI—systems capable of autonomous decision-making in financial workflows—and quantum computing to bolster transactional security.

For the financial sector, this move addresses long-standing bottlenecks in risk assessment and fraud detection. By integrating quantum algorithms, institutions can mitigate the threat of advanced cyber-attacks that traditional encryption might fail to stop. Meanwhile, Agentic AI promises to transition banking apps from passive dashboards to active financial advisors capable of handling complex wealth management tasks for the average citizen.

Globalizing UPI and Reducing Remittance Friction

A core pillar of the Prime Minister’s mandate is the aggressive expansion of the Unified Payments Interface (UPI) beyond Indian borders. India currently leads the world in real-time retail payments, but the focus is now on the cross-border remittance market, where high transaction fees continue to erode the earnings of the Indian diaspora.

The integration of tokenization and real-time cross-border settlements seeks to bypass the legacy SWIFT network, which remains expensive and slow. By standardizing UPI-linked frameworks with international partners, India aims to lower the cost of sending money home, potentially saving households crores in aggregate annual fees.

Market Implications for Fintech Developers

For fintech founders and investors, the message is clear: move beyond the domestic saturation of payment aggregation. The government expects Indian firms to democratize financial services by providing low-cost credit and insurance to the underserved sectors across emerging markets.

  • Scalability: Transition from local payment gateways to international, protocol-agnostic financial layers.
  • Compliance: Adopt quantum-ready security frameworks to retain institutional trust.
  • Cost Efficiency: Leverage tokenization to minimize the data storage footprint and improve transaction speed.
  • Financial Inclusion: Focus on 'bottom-of-the-pyramid' access using AI-driven credit scoring models.

The call to action for the private sector is to move from being mere service providers to becoming architects of a global, inclusive financial ecosystem. As India positions itself as the laboratory for high-scale, low-cost fintech, the firms that master Agentic AI and quantum integration today will define the transactional standards of the next decade.
