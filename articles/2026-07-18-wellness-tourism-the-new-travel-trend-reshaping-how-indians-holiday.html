Wellness tourism: The new travel trend reshaping how Indians holiday

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Lifestyle Original source ↗
Key points
  • Wellness tourism is emerging as a major travel trend in India, driven by health-conscious travellers.
  • Destinations like Kerala, Rishikesh, and Himachal Pradesh are popular for yoga, Ayurveda, and detox retreats.
  • The trend reflects a shift from sightseeing to immersive, restorative experiences.

The rise of wellness-focused travel

Wellness tourism is quietly becoming one of the most talked-about travel trends in India. More travellers are now choosing holidays that prioritise physical and mental well-being over traditional sightseeing.

Industry experts say the shift is driven by a growing awareness of health and self-care. Instead of packing itineraries with monuments and museums, many are opting for retreats that offer yoga, meditation, Ayurvedic treatments, and detox programmes.

What makes wellness tourism different

Unlike a regular vacation, wellness tourism focuses on intentional experiences. Travellers seek out destinations that offer structured programmes — from morning yoga sessions to organic meals and spa therapies.

Kerala has long been a favourite for Ayurveda retreats. Rishikesh draws those looking for spiritual and yoga-based getaways. Himachal Pradesh, with its serene mountain settings, is also seeing a surge in wellness-focused stays.

Who is driving the trend

Young professionals and middle-aged travellers form the core of this market. Many are urban residents looking to escape the stress of city life. The trend is not limited to the wealthy — mid-range wellness packages are becoming more common.

Wellness tourism is also gaining traction among solo travellers and groups of friends. Some travel companies now offer curated wellness itineraries that include cooking classes, nature walks, and meditation sessions.

Local economies benefit

The rise of wellness tourism is creating new opportunities for local businesses. Homestays, small yoga centres, and Ayurvedic clinics are seeing increased demand. In places like Kerala and Himachal, this trend is helping to sustain traditional health practices.

Tourism boards are taking notice. Some states are promoting wellness circuits that combine health treatments with local culture and cuisine. The idea is to offer a holistic experience that goes beyond just relaxation.

What travellers should know

Before booking a wellness retreat, experts recommend checking the credentials of the instructors and therapists. Not all retreats offer the same quality of service. Reading reviews and asking for detailed itineraries can help avoid disappointments.

Pricing varies widely. A basic weekend retreat can cost a few thousand rupees, while luxury wellness resorts can run into lakhs. It is best to match the experience with personal goals — whether it is stress relief, weight management, or simply a break from routine.

As more Indians prioritise health, wellness tourism is likely to grow. The challenge for the industry will be to maintain authenticity while scaling up. For now, the trend shows no signs of slowing down.
