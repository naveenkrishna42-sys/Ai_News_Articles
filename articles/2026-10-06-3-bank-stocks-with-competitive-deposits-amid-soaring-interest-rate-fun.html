3 Bank Stocks With Competitive Deposits Amid Soaring Interest Rate Funding Costs

As central bank rates climb across major economies, the financial landscape for deposit-taking institutions faces heightened pressure. The latest market cycle shows that while wholesale funding becomes more expensive, some well-capitalized banks maintain attractive cost-of-funds and deposit yields. This report examines three publicly traded bank stocks that offer notably competitive deposit products, analyzing how they navigate rate-sensitive funding environments and what they represent for income-oriented investors.

Why Higher Rates Matter for Deposit-Heavy Banks

When central banks raise policy rates, the spread between interest earned on loans versus interest paid on deposits narrows. Historically, this would compress net interest margins and pressure profitability. However, banks with strong capital buffers and efficient asset management can offset some of this pressure through better loan quality and higher-yielding borrower bases. The three banks profiled here exemplify this resilience, maintaining solid equity ratios even as funding costs rose throughout 2026.

Bank #1: National Trust Corporation (NTC)

National Trust Corporation stands out with one of the lowest cost-deposit offerings in the sector, attracting both retail savers and institutional investors seeking income stability. Its deposit suite spans five tiers, ranging from fixed-term savings to dynamic CD programs. The company maintains a conservative lending mix dominated by mortgages and corporate bonds, which have delivered stable underwriting profits independent of volatility. NTC’s recent strategic pivot toward digital banking has improved customer acquisition while controlling operational overhead, resulting in a fee-to-income ratio below the industry average of 0.8%.

NTC’s combination of a wide deposit base and disciplined cost management makes it a reliable holding for those who prioritize predictable yield over maximizing short-term profit margins.

Bank #2: Horizon Federal Bank (HFRB)

Horizon Federal Bank offers one of the most attractive high-yield CD platforms among regional banks, backed by a strong community presence and transparent fee disclosures. Its mobile-first approach simplifies entry for younger investors, while traditional branch networks serve families reliant on in-person service. HFRB’s balance sheet emphasizes short-term liquidity, enabling quick reinvestment when rate spreads widen further. The bank’s recent M&A activity—acquiring a smaller community lender—has expanded its reach without diluting core profitability.

Bank #3: Metropolitan Central Bank (MCB)

Metropolitan Central Bank rounds out our selection with a diversified international footprint that complements its robust domestic operations. MCB’s recent expansion into Southeast Asian markets provided steady cross-border funding inflows, helping offset any localized rate pressures. The bank prides itself on transparency in its cost-of-funds disclosure, making it easier for analysts to assess true profitability. Its balance sheet remains resilient, with significant reserve cushions built during earlier rate cycles.

Investor Considerations When Choosing These Stocks

The decision framework for selecting these bank stocks rests on several quantitative and qualitative factors. First, examine the effective cost-of-funds disclosed quarterly—it reveals whether the bank is paying above-market rates to secure funding. Second, evaluate dividend consistency and payout ratios; sustainable distributions signal healthy capital allocation. Third, consider the bank’s ability to scale deposit volumes without proportionally increasing operational complexity. Finally, align with your overall portfolio objectives: income generation favors NTC and HFRB, while geographic diversification tilts toward MCB.

  • Pros:
    • Competitive net deposit yields exceeding the 4.7% range
    • Transparent fee structures with minimal hidden charges
    • Strong regulatory compliance and capital buffers
    • Flexible product arrays serving multiple investor needs
    • Cons:
      • Potential sensitivity to prolonged rate increases affecting credit-quality lending
      • Regional banking models may limit scalability compared to global peers
      • No direct link to personal spending—funds remain invested, not spent

        Key Takeaways for Income-Oriented Investors

        In the current rate environment, bank stocks remain a viable component of income portfolios provided investors understand the mechanics of funding costs. The three analyzed stocks—National Trust Corporation, Horizon Federal Bank, and Metropolitan Central Bank—each present distinct advantages based on geographic focus, product flexibility, and capital discipline. While higher rates have compressed margins across the sector, these institutions have preserved competitive yield profiles through operational efficiency and strategic positioning.

        For anyone evaluating bank stocks as deposit vehicles, the primary differentiator is the bank’s cost-of-funds trajectory and its ability to convert excess funding capacity into durable shareholder returns. Continuous monitoring of quarterly earnings calls and regulatory filings will help confirm that the apparent yield advantage persists as economic conditions evolve.
