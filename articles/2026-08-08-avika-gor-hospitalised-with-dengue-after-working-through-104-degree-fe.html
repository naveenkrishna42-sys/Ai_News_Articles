Avika Gor Hospitalised With Dengue After Working Through 104-Degree Fever

Actress Admitted After Dengue Diagnosis

Actress Avika Gor, best known for her role in the television series 'Balika Vadhu', has been hospitalised after testing positive for dengue. Her husband, Milind Chandwani, confirmed the news on social media, sharing that the actress had been working through a high fever before her condition worsened.

The 104-degree fever, which she reportedly continued to ignore, eventually led to her hospitalisation. Chandwani described her state as severe, noting that she was 'unable to move or eat' by the time she was admitted.

Worked Through Severe Symptoms

According to Chandwani's posts, Avika had been pushing herself to meet work commitments even as her body showed clear signs of distress. The decision to hospitalise came only after her condition became visibly critical.

Doctors at the hospital have since confirmed dengue, and she is currently under medical observation. The exact hospital where she is being treated has not been disclosed, and her family has requested privacy during this time.

Fans and Colleagues Rally Support

News of her hospitalisation has prompted an outpouring of concern from fans and colleagues in the television industry. Many have taken to social media to wish her a speedy recovery, with some recalling her iconic role as Anandi in the long-running show.

Avika, who transitioned from child stardom to a successful career in films and web series, has a significant following across India. Her husband's updates have been widely shared, with several entertainment portals picking up the story.

A Reminder of Health Risks

Health experts often warn against ignoring persistent high fever, which can be a symptom of vector-borne diseases like dengue. In many Indian cities, monsoon season brings a spike in such cases, and early detection is critical to avoid complications.

Avika's case serves as a stark example of the dangers of overworking during illness. While her condition is said to be stable now, the incident has sparked conversations about the pressures faced by actors who often continue shooting despite health issues.
