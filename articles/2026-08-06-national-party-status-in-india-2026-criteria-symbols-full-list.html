National Party Status in India 2026: Criteria, Symbols, Full List

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Politics Original source ↗
Key points
  • National party status is granted by the Election Commission of India based on specific electoral criteria.
  • Recognised national parties get a unique symbol and other privileges under election rules.
  • The list of national parties is updated periodically based on recent election results.

What Makes a Party 'National'?

The Election Commission of India (ECI) grants national party status to political parties that meet certain electoral thresholds. These criteria are laid out in the Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968, and are reviewed after every general election or state election cycle.

A party can be recognised as a national party if it is recognised as a state party in at least four states, or if it secures at least 6% of the valid votes polled in four or more states in a general election and wins at least four Lok Sabha seats. Alternatively, a party can qualify by winning at least 2% of the total Lok Sabha seats (that is, 11 seats) from at least three different states.

These rules ensure that only parties with a pan-India presence and consistent electoral performance earn the tag. The recognition brings with it a permanent election symbol, which is a crucial asset in a country where a large section of voters identify parties by their symbols.

Symbols and Privileges

Each recognised national party is allotted a unique symbol that is reserved exclusively for its candidates. For instance, the Congress party uses the hand symbol, the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) uses the lotus, and the Communist Party of India (Marxist) uses the hammer, sickle and star.

National parties also get benefits like free airtime on Doordarshan and All India Radio during elections, and they can have up to 40 star campaigners instead of the 20 allowed for unrecognised parties. They also need fewer proposers to file nomination papers.

The symbol is not just a visual aid; it is a political identity. In many rural areas, voters often cast their ballots based on the symbol rather than the candidate's name. Losing or gaining a symbol can significantly alter a party's electoral fortunes.

Latest List of National Parties (2026)

As of 2026, the ECI recognises the following parties as national parties: the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), the Indian National Congress (INC), the Communist Party of India (Marxist) (CPI-M), the Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP), the National People's Party (NPP), and the Aam Aadmi Party (AAP).

Some parties have seen their status change in recent reviews. For example, the Communist Party of India (CPI) lost its national party status in 2023 after failing to meet the required threshold in the 2019 elections. Similarly, the Nationalist Congress Party (NCP) and the Trinamool Congress (TMC) have been assessed in various cycles, with their status depending on their performance in specific state and national elections.

The ECI's list is not static. It is revised based on the latest electoral data, and parties can be upgraded or downgraded accordingly.

How the Criteria Are Applied

The ECI applies the criteria strictly based on the results of the last general election and the most recent state assembly elections. A party that fails to meet the threshold in one cycle can regain its status in the next, provided it performs well.

For instance, the AAP was recognised as a national party in 2023 after its strong showing in the 2022 Punjab assembly elections and its continued presence in Delhi and Goa. The party's symbol, the broom, has become synonymous with its anti-corruption campaign.

Similarly, the NPP, which is based in Meghalaya, became a national party in 2019 after winning seats in the Lok Sabha and being recognised as a state party in multiple northeastern states.

What to Watch Next

The next major review of national party status is likely to happen after the 2026 state elections and the next general election. Political observers will be watching whether any new parties, such as the TMC or the Samajwadi Party, can cross the threshold and join the list. The ECI's decisions will shape the electoral landscape for the coming years, as parties jostle for recognition and the coveted symbols that come with it.
