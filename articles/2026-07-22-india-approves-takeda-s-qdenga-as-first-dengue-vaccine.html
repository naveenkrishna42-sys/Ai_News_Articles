India Approves Takeda's Qdenga as First Dengue Vaccine

📅 2026-07-22 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • India's DCGI has approved Takeda's Qdenga (TAK-003) as the first dengue vaccine in the country.
  • The vaccine targets all four dengue serotypes and is administered in two doses.
  • Approval comes as India continues to report high dengue cases annually, with the monsoon season being a peak period.

Historic Approval for Dengue Prevention

India's drug regulator, the Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI), has approved Takeda's Qdenga (TAK-003) as the country's first dengue vaccine. The Japanese drugmaker's vaccine is now cleared for use in India, marking a significant step in the fight against the mosquito-borne disease.

The Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) gave the green light after reviewing clinical trial data, which showed the vaccine's efficacy against all four dengue serotypes. Qdenga is a live-attenuated vaccine administered in two doses, three months apart.

Targeting a Growing Public Health Threat

Dengue remains a major public health challenge in India, with lakhs of cases reported every year. The monsoon season often triggers outbreaks, overwhelming healthcare systems in several states. The approval of a vaccine is expected to reduce the burden on hospitals and clinics.

Qdenga has already been approved in several other countries, including the European Union and Indonesia. Takeda's vaccine is the first to be approved in India, where no other dengue vaccine was previously available.

How the Vaccine Works

Qdenga is designed to protect against all four serotypes of the dengue virus. Clinical trials conducted in endemic regions demonstrated its ability to reduce the incidence of dengue and prevent severe cases.

The vaccine is recommended for individuals aged four years and older. It is not approved for use in pregnant or breastfeeding women, and those with compromised immune systems.

Regulatory Process and Next Steps

The DCGI's approval follows a thorough evaluation of safety and efficacy data submitted by Takeda. The company has also committed to conducting post-marketing surveillance to monitor the vaccine's long-term performance in Indian populations.

Health officials have not yet announced a timeline for the nationwide rollout. Distribution and pricing details are expected to be finalised in the coming months.

India's decision to approve Qdenga comes as several other countries are also wrestling with dengue outbreaks. The World Health Organization has listed dengue as one of the top ten global health threats.

The vaccine's availability could be a game-changer, especially for children and young adults, who are most vulnerable to severe dengue. However, experts caution that vaccination must be paired with continued vector control measures for maximum impact.

The government is likely to prioritise high-burden states for the initial rollout. Takeda has said it will work with Indian authorities to ensure equitable access.

As India braces for another monsoon season, the approval offers a new tool in the public health arsenal. The focus now shifts to ensuring a smooth and effective vaccination campaign.
