Chaitra Navratri 2026: 5 Must-Visit Temples in Delhi for Divine Blessings

📅 2026-08-11 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • Chaitra Navratri 2026 begins, marking the nine-day worship of Goddess Durga.
  • Delhi's temples see a surge in devotees during the festival.
  • Five prominent temples offer unique spiritual experiences for visitors.

As the auspicious occasion of Chaitra Navratri commences, devotees across the national capital are preparing for nine days of fasting, prayer, and devotion. This springtime festival, which falls in the Hindu month of Chaitra, holds special significance as it marks the beginning of the new year in several regional calendars. For those seeking divine blessings in Delhi, the city's historic temples offer a serene escape from the urban rush.

The Spiritual Essence of Chaitra Navratri

Chaitra Navratri, unlike its autumn counterpart Sharadiya Navratri, is observed with quiet fervour. Devotees worship the nine forms of Goddess Durga, with each day dedicated to a different manifestation. The festival culminates in Ram Navami, the birth anniversary of Lord Rama, adding another layer of devotion to the period.

During these nine days, many households set up ghatas (sacred pots) and recite scriptures. Temple authorities in Delhi report a noticeable increase in footfall, with families flocking to offer prayers and participate in aarti ceremonies. The atmosphere is one of collective faith, where the city's diverse population comes together in worship.

Five Temples to Visit in Delhi

For those planning to immerse themselves in the festive spirit, here are five temples in Delhi that are particularly revered during Chaitra Navratri. Each offers a unique experience, from ancient architecture to vibrant community celebrations.

1. Jhandewalan Temple

Located in the heart of Delhi, the Jhandewalan Temple is dedicated to Goddess Jhandewali, an incarnation of Durga. The temple's towering idol and intricate carvings draw thousands of devotees daily. During Navratri, the temple is adorned with flowers and lights, and special bhajan sessions are held every evening.

2. Kalkaji Mandir

Situated near Nehru Place, Kalkaji Mandir is one of the oldest and most frequented temples in the city. Its black marble idol of Goddess Kali is believed to be self-manifested. The temple's courtyard fills with chants of 'Jai Mata Di' throughout the festival, and the atmosphere is particularly electric on the final day.

3. Chhatarpur Temple

Known as the largest temple complex in India, Chhatarpur Mandir is dedicated to Goddess Katyayani, a form of Durga. The temple's South Indian-style architecture stands out in the capital. Devotees often visit early in the morning to avoid crowds and enjoy the peaceful surroundings.

4. Maa Vaishno Devi Temple, Rohini

For those who cannot travel to Katra, the Maa Vaishno Devi Temple in Rohini offers a slice of that pilgrimage experience. The temple replicates the cave shrine in miniature form. During Navratri, the management organises prasad distribution and kirtans, drawing large neighbourhood crowds.

5. Sheetla Mata Temple, Gurugram

Although technically outside Delhi, this temple in nearby Gurugram is a popular destination for residents of the capital. Dedicated to Goddess Sheetla, who is believed to cure ailments, the temple sees special pujas for family well-being. It is particularly busy on the Ashtami (eighth day) when many perform the kanjak ritual, worshipping young girls as embodiments of the goddess.

Practical Tips for Devotees

Temple visits during Navratri require some planning. Authorities suggest arriving early, especially on weekends, to avoid long queues. Many temples have separate lines for men and women, and security has been heightened in recent years. Devotees are advised to carry minimal belongings and follow the dress codes that some temples enforce.

Also, while several temples offer prasad, some devotees prefer to bring their own offerings. It is customary to donate to the temple's charitable activities during this period, which many see as an essential part of the festival.

What to Watch For

As the nine days unfold, temple committees will announce special schedules for Ram Navami, which falls on the final day. Devotees should check individual temple websites or social media pages for updated timings and any changes to darshan procedures. The festival also marks the beginning of the new Samvat year, so many will also visit temples to seek blessings for prosperity in the months ahead.
