March: A month of cultural extravaganzas across India

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Religion Original source ↗
Key points
  • March features a series of cultural festivals including Holi, Navroz, and regional harvest events.
  • States like Maharashtra, Punjab, and Gujarat host major celebrations with music, dance, and food.
  • The month offers a mix of religious and secular events attracting local and international visitors.

March ushers in a season of celebrations

March is shaping up to be a month packed with cultural events across India. The calendar includes major festivals like Holi, celebrated largely in North India, and Navroz, the Parsi New Year. Several states also mark harvest festivals, blending religious traditions with community gatherings.

For many Indians, March is a time when winter gives way to spring. The weather is pleasant, making it ideal for outdoor events. From folk performances to elaborate processions, the month offers a glimpse into the country's diverse cultural fabric.

Holi leads the festive calendar

Holi, the festival of colours, falls in mid-March this year. Celebrated across Uttar Pradesh, Delhi, Rajasthan, and beyond, it involves people smearing coloured powder on each other, dancing, and sharing sweets. In Mathura and Vrindavan, the celebrations stretch over several days with special events at temples.

Local authorities in major cities have announced designated public spaces for Holi gatherings. Police advisories urge revellers to use natural colours and avoid water wastage. The festival also sees a spike in tourism, with hotels and travel operators reporting high bookings in places like Jaipur and Agra.

Navroz and regional harvest festivals

The Parsi community will observe Navroz, their New Year, in late March. Celebrations in Mumbai, Surat, and other cities with significant Parsi populations include visits to fire temples, family feasts, and cultural programmes. Community centres host events featuring traditional music and dance.

Several states also hold harvest festivals during this period. In Punjab, Baisakhi preparations begin in March, though the main event is in April. In Tamil Nadu, Pongal celebrations earlier in the year set the tone, but March sees local temple festivals and fairs in rural areas. Kerala’s Vishu also falls in mid-April, but March has numerous temple festivals with processions and folk art forms.

Art and music events draw crowds

Beyond religious festivals, March hosts art exhibitions and music concerts in metropolitan cities. Organisers in Delhi, Mumbai, and Bengaluru have lined up events featuring classical dance, theatre, and contemporary art. The month also includes literary festivals in smaller towns, often held in heritage venues.

The tourism department in several states has launched promotional campaigns around these events. Officials expect a rise in domestic travel, with many families planning short trips to attend specific festivals. Local businesses, from food vendors to handicraft sellers, prepare for increased footfall during weekends.

Looking ahead, the festival season continues into April with major events like Ugadi and Baisakhi. March sets the stage for these celebrations, offering a preview of India’s springtime cultural energy.
