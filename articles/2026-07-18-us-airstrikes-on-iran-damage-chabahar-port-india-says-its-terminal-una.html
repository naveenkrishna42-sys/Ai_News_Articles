US airstrikes on Iran damage Chabahar port; India says its terminal unaffected

📅 2026-07-18 📂 World Original source ↗
Key points
  • US airstrikes on Iran appear to have damaged Chabahar port.
  • India says no damage to its terminal at Chabahar.
  • Conflict has entered seventh night with attacks on civilian infrastructure.

The United States has launched a fresh wave of airstrikes on Iran, hitting targets including the strategic Chabahar port on the Gulf of Oman. The attacks mark the seventh consecutive night of hostilities between the two nations, with reports of damage to civilian infrastructure across several Iranian cities.

India, which operates a terminal at Chabahar as part of its connectivity project to Afghanistan and Central Asia, has said its facility has not been affected. Officials in New Delhi confirmed that the Indian-built terminal remains operational, though the overall condition of the port complex is still being assessed.

Chabahar port hit

Chabahar port, located in Iran's Sistan and Baluchistan province, is a critical node for India's trade route to Afghanistan and Central Asia, bypassing Pakistan. The strikes appear to have damaged parts of the port's infrastructure, though the full extent is not yet known.

India has invested heavily in the port, including the construction of a terminal for handling cargo. The project is seen as a strategic counterweight to China's Gwadar port in Pakistan, just 200 kilometres away.

India's response

In a statement, India's Ministry of External Affairs said there was no damage to its terminal at Chabahar. "Our terminal at the Shahid Beheshti port in Chabahar has not been impacted by the recent developments. We are in touch with Iranian authorities," the statement read.

The assurance came amid reports that the airstrikes had targeted several civilian sites, including bridges, airport runways and a railway station in and around Chabahar. The Times of India reported that the US attacks had specifically hit civilian infrastructure in Iran, raising concerns about the humanitarian impact.

Escalation fears

The conflict has escalated rapidly over the past week. Al Jazeera reported that the US and Iran have exchanged deadly attacks for seven nights, with both sides hitting infrastructure targets. India Today said fears of a wider escalation are mounting as the strikes continue.

ThePrint noted that the US campaign has entered its seventh night with no sign of de-escalation. Both nations have targeted key infrastructure, including power plants, oil refineries and transport hubs.

What to watch

With the Chabahar port damaged, India's connectivity to Afghanistan and Central Asia faces a potential disruption. New Delhi is expected to monitor the situation closely and may consider alternative routes. The coming days will reveal whether the US and Iran can step back from the brink or if the conflict widens further.
