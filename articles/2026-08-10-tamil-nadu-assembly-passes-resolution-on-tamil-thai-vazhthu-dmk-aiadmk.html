Tamil Nadu Assembly passes resolution on Tamil Thai Vazhthu; DMK, AIADMK back it

📅 2026-08-10 📂 Top Stories Original source ↗
Key points
  • Tamil Nadu Assembly unanimously passes resolution on Tamil Thai Vazhthu
  • CM Vijay says mother tongue is as pure as mother, calls it lifeline
  • DMK and AIADMK extend support to the TVK government's move

The Tamil Nadu Assembly on Monday unanimously adopted a resolution making 'Tamil Thai Vazhthu' (Invocation to Mother Tamil) mandatory as the first song at all state events. The move, piloted by Chief Minister Vijay, received rare backing from both the DMK and AIADMK, signalling a rare moment of political unity in the state.

Moving the resolution, CM Vijay said the mother tongue is not just a medium of communication but the very lifeline of the people. "Our mother tongue is as pure as our mother. It carries our emotions, our history, and our identity," he said, drawing applause from all benches.

Unanimous backing across party lines

The resolution was passed without a single dissenting voice. Leaders from the DMK and AIADMK, often at loggerheads over policy and power, rose to support the motion. The consensus underscored the emotional weight the issue carries in Tamil Nadu, where language politics has deep roots.

Political observers note that the support is significant given the TVK government's relatively recent assumption of power. The backing from the two principal opposition parties suggests that cultural and linguistic matters can still transcend partisan lines in the state.

What the resolution mandates

Under the adopted resolution, Tamil Thai Vazhthu must be sung first at all official state functions. This includes government ceremonies, school events, and public gatherings organised by state authorities. The song, composed by Manonmaniam Sundaranar, has long been a symbol of Tamil pride.

The directive is expected to be enforced across districts and departments. Officials have not yet detailed the penalties or mechanisms for non-compliance, but the government is expected to issue guidelines soon.

Context of the Centre's push

The resolution comes amid reports of the Central government promoting Vande Mataram at official events. While the state's move is not explicitly framed as a counter, the timing has drawn attention. CM Vijay did not directly reference the Centre's push in his speech, but his emphasis on "mother tongue first" was seen by many as a pointed response.

In Tamil Nadu, the two-anthem question has historically been sensitive. The state has its own official song, and successive governments have insisted on its pride of place at state functions. Monday's resolution cements that position further.

Reactions and political significance

DMK leaders called the resolution a reaffirmation of Tamil identity. AIADMK members said they stood with the government on this issue, setting aside their usual rivalry. The unanimity was welcomed by cultural outfits and Tamil scholars, who see it as a step towards preserving the language's pre-eminence.

Some analysts, however, caution that the real test lies in implementation. "Passing a resolution is one thing, ensuring it is followed uniformly at every event is another," said a senior political commentator. The government will need to build awareness among organisers and institutions.

For now, the Assembly's message is clear: in Tamil Nadu, Tamil comes first. The resolution also places the state in a delicate balance with the Centre's linguistic push, and how New Delhi responds will be closely watched.

Going forward, all eyes will be on the government's execution plan and whether the move sparks similar resolutions in other states. The next few months could reveal how this cultural assertion plays out in the wider national discourse.
