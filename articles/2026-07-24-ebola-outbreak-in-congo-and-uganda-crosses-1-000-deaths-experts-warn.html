Ebola Outbreak in Congo and Uganda Crosses 1,000 Deaths, Experts Warn

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Ebola deaths have crossed 1,000 in the ongoing outbreak in Congo and Uganda.
  • Experts are concerned about the virus spreading in densely populated camps.
  • Cross-border transmission between Congo and Uganda remains a major challenge.

The Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo and neighbouring Uganda has now claimed more than 1,000 lives, according to reports from global health agencies. The grim milestone underscores the severity of the epidemic, which has been raging for months despite international efforts to contain it.

Health officials in both countries are grappling with a virus that continues to find new footholds, particularly in overcrowded displacement camps. The death toll, confirmed by multiple news outlets, has prompted renewed calls for a coordinated regional response.

Why Experts Are Sounding the Alarm

Virologists and epidemiologists tracking the outbreak say the situation is different from previous flare-ups. The current strain has shown a higher rate of transmission in urban and semi-urban settings, making containment far more difficult.

One major point of concern is the spread within crowded camps for internally displaced persons in eastern Congo. These camps house hundreds of thousands of people with limited access to clean water, sanitation, and medical care. In such conditions, the virus can move quickly from person to person.

Experts have also flagged the risk of cross-border transmission. Uganda has already reported several confirmed cases linked to travellers from Congo, raising fears of a wider regional spread if border screening and community surveillance are not strengthened.

Stabilisation Signs in Congo, But No Room for Complacency

There are early indications that the outbreak may be stabilising in some parts of DR Congo. The China Daily Global Edition reported that local health authorities have observed a slight decline in new cases in certain hotspots. This has given some hope that response measures — including vaccination campaigns and contact tracing — are beginning to take effect.

However, officials caution that the situation remains fragile. New clusters continue to emerge, and the overall fatality rate remains high. The World Health Organization has not yet declared the outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern, but that assessment may change if the numbers do not trend downward consistently.

In Uganda, authorities have ramped up surveillance at border points and in high-risk districts. Health workers are being trained to identify symptoms quickly, and isolation units have been set up in several hospitals.

Life Inside the Camps: Fear and Desperation

Inside the camps, the mood is grim. Residents in affected zones have described scenes of fear and helplessness as the virus cuts through families. One camp resident was quoted by The New York Times as saying, "We will all die from Ebola here." Such sentiments reflect the deep anxiety among populations who feel trapped between the disease and a lack of adequate healthcare.

Mistrust of health workers and government authorities has also hampered response efforts. In some areas, teams conducting burials or vaccinations have faced hostility. Community engagement programmes are being scaled up to address these concerns, but progress is slow.

What Comes Next

The coming weeks will be critical. Health agencies are pushing for more funding to deploy vaccines and treatment supplies, especially to remote areas. Regional cooperation between Congo and Uganda will determine whether the outbreak can be contained or whether it spills further across borders.

Officials have not yet confirmed whether the death toll will continue to rise or if containment efforts will finally turn the tide. All eyes remain on the camps and border towns where the next chapter of this outbreak will be written.
