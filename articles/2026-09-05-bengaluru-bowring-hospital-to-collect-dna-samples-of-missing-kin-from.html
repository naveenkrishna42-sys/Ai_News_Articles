Bengaluru Bowring Hospital to Collect DNA Samples of Missing Kin from Nepal Floods

✍️ By TIVRA News Editorial Team 📅 2026-09-05 📂 Bengaluru Original source ↗
Key points
  • Bowring Hospital has been designated as the Bengaluru nodal centre for DNA collection in the Nepal floods missing-persons identification drive.
  • Relatives of suspected victims can provide reference swabs (buccal or blood) at the hospital's forensic medicine department during notified hours.
  • DNA profiles will be matched against samples collected from flood-affected zones in Nepal, coordinated through state and central forensic agencies.

Why Bengaluru's Bowring Hospital Is Central to the Identification Drive

Following devastating floods in Nepal, Bengaluru's Bowring Hospital has been designated as a DNA collection centre for families searching for missing relatives. The move brings forensic identification infrastructure directly to a city with a large migrant population and strong Nepali diaspora presence. The hospital's existing forensic medicine department, already equipped for medico-legal work, will handle the reference sample collection and chain-of-custody protocols required for cross-border identification.

For affected families in Karnataka, this eliminates the need to travel to Delhi or Kathmandu to lodge biological reference samples. State authorities have coordinated with the hospital to fast-track paperwork, recognising that DNA integrity and timely collection directly affect match probability.

How the DNA Sampling Process Works

The procedure follows standard forensic identification methodology used by accredited laboratories worldwide. Reference samples are typically collected via buccal (cheek) swabs or, where required, small blood draws from biological relatives — usually parents, children, or siblings of the missing person.

Step-by-Step Procedure at Bowring Hospital

  • Registration and Verification: Families must carry valid ID, proof of relationship, photographs of the missing person, and any available medical or dental records.
  • Informed Consent: A formal consent form is signed, authorising the hospital to store and transmit the DNA profile to the coordinating agency.
  • Sample Collection: Two buccal swabs are taken from the consenting relative under chain-of-custody supervision. Blood samples are only drawn if swabs are insufficient.
  • Lab Processing: Samples are sealed, barcoded, and sent to an accredited forensic lab for STR (Short Tandem Repeat) profiling, the global standard for human identification.
  • Cross-Matching: Profiles are compared with ante-mortem reference data or post-mortem samples collected in Nepal.

Real-World Highlights for Families

  • No Out-of-Pocket Cost: The exercise is funded under disaster relief provisions; families are not charged for sampling or profiling.
  • Confidentiality Maintained: Profiles are shared only with authorised agencies; data is not entered into any criminal database.
  • Higher Match Probability with Multiple Relatives: Providing samples from two or more close biological relatives dramatically improves match accuracy.
  • Travel Not Required to Kathmandu: Bengaluru-based registration saves thousands of rupees in travel and documentation effort for South Indian families.
  • Documentation Gaps Can Delay Process: Missing dental records or old photographs of the missing person can slow cross-matching significantly.

What Families Should Prepare Before Visiting

Relatives heading to Bowring Hospital should assemble all available identification material in advance. Clear recent photographs of the missing person, their Aadhaar or passport copies, and any old medical reports containing biometric identifiers accelerate the process. Where possible, two close biological relatives should accompany the primary applicant to improve the statistical weight of the reference sample.

Hospital authorities have urged families to avoid panic visits. A single coordinated visit with complete documentation is significantly more productive than multiple trips with partial paperwork. For those unable to travel, designated helpdesks are expected to coordinate sample collection through local police or forensic teams in district headquarters.

Limitations and Practical Challenges

DNA-based identification, while highly reliable, is constrained by the availability of post-mortem samples from flood-affected zones. In mass casualty events where bodies are recovered after prolonged exposure, degraded samples can reduce match confidence. Families should therefore maintain realistic expectations: a confirmed match provides closure, but inconclusive results do not necessarily mean the person is not among the deceased.

Additionally, the process is emotionally taxing. Counselling support has been made available at the collection centre, and families are advised to approach the exercise with patience, given that laboratory backlogs and cross-border coordination can extend timelines beyond the initial weeks of the disaster.
