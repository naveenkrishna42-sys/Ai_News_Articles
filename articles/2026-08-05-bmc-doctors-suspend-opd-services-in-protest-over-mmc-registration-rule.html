BMC doctors suspend OPD services in protest over MMC registration rule

📅 2026-08-05 📂 Mumbai Original source ↗
Key points
  • BMC doctors suspended routine OPD services in Mumbai
  • Protest against MMC registration for CCMP-qualified BHMS practitioners
  • Emergency services remain functional; talks with officials awaited

Mumbai's municipal doctors have pulled the plug on routine outpatient department (OPD) services, pressing their demand against a recent Maharashtra Medical Council (MMC) decision. The move, which took effect this morning, has left thousands of patients without access to non-emergency consultations at civic-run hospitals.

The protest stems from the MMC's approval to register practitioners who hold a Certificate Course in Medical Practice (CCMP) along with a Bachelor of Homeopathic Medicine and Surgery (BHMS) degree. Doctors argue this blurs the line between modern medicine and homeopathy, potentially compromising patient safety.

What triggered the strike

The Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) doctors are up in arms over a notification that allows CCMP-qualified BHMS practitioners to be registered with the MMC. This, they say, would permit them to prescribe allopathic drugs, a move that doctors claim is both legally and medically unsound.

"We are not against homeopathy," said a senior doctor at a major BMC hospital, speaking on condition of anonymity. "But allowing a short-term certificate course to override rigorous MBBS training is a dangerous precedent." The doctors have demanded an immediate rollback of the registration order.

Services hit, emergencies exempt

Routine OPDs across civic hospitals in the city have been suspended since morning. Patients arriving for scheduled check-ups were turned away, with notices pasted at entrances explaining the disruption. However, emergency services, including casualty wards and intensive care units, continue to function normally.

"My mother has diabetes and needed a regular check-up," said a frustrated relative at a hospital in central Mumbai. "We were told to come back tomorrow, but there's no clarity on when this will end." Civic officials have appealed to doctors to resume work, but talks have so far failed to break the deadlock.

What is CCMP and why it matters

The CCMP is a short-term bridge course designed to train homeopaths in basic allopathic practices, primarily for rural and underserved areas. The MMC's recent decision to register such practitioners has been seen by many in the medical fraternity as an erosion of standards.

"This is not about turf wars," said another doctor, who did not wish to be named. "It's about ensuring that whoever prescribes antibiotics or treats infections has the necessary training. A few months of coursework cannot substitute six years of medical education."

Patients caught in the crossfire

The disruption has hit the most vulnerable hardest. Daily wage workers, who rely on municipal hospitals for affordable care, are particularly affected. Many had taken time off work to attend appointments, only to be sent back.

"We have no other option," said a woman waiting outside a hospital in the eastern suburbs. "Private clinics are too expensive. The BMC must resolve this quickly." Civic authorities have said they are in touch with the protesting doctors' association and hope to reach a resolution soon.

Meanwhile, the state health department has remained silent on the issue, leaving BMC doctors and patients in limbo. The MMC has defended its decision, stating that CCMP-qualified practitioners are being registered under existing provisions, but the controversy shows no signs of cooling down.

As the standoff continues, all eyes are on the next round of talks between the BMC administration and the doctors' union. A breakthrough is expected within the next 48 hours, but if none comes, the doctors have hinted at an indefinite extension of the protest.
