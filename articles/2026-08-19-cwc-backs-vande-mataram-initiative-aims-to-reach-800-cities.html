CWC backs 'Vande Mataram' initiative, aims to reach 800 cities

📅 2026-08-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • CWC supports two-stanza 'Vande Mataram' initiative
  • Committee plans to expand student outreach to 800 cities
  • Congress discusses 'chanda chori' and paper leaks in strategy meeting
  • Kharge criticizes Modi government for 'destroying' students' future
  • Congress demands PM apology over Kharge's 'stage purification' remarks

CWC Supports 'Vande Mataram' Initiative

The Congress Working Committee (CWC) has given its backing to a two-stanza version of 'Vande Mataram', a patriotic song that holds significant cultural importance in India. This initiative is part of the party's broader efforts to engage with students across the country.

Expansion of Student Outreach

The CWC has set a target to expand its student outreach program to 800 cities. This move is aimed at strengthening the party's connection with the youth, who are considered to be the future of the nation. The initiative will involve various activities and programs designed to inspire and educate the students.

Discussions on 'Chanda Chori' and Paper Leaks

During a key strategy meeting, the Congress discussed issues related to 'chanda chori' and paper leaks. These topics have been a point of contention and have sparked debates within the party. The meeting aimed to find solutions and strategies to address these issues effectively.

Kharge's Criticism of Modi Government

Congress leader Mallikarjun Kharge has criticized the Modi government for what he termed as the 'destruction' of the future of students. He called upon the Congress to prepare a comprehensive roadmap to address the concerns of the youth. Kharge's remarks highlight the party's focus on the welfare and education of the students.

Congress Demands PM Apology

The Congress has demanded an apology from the Prime Minister over Kharge's remarks about 'stage purification'. The party has also announced plans to launch a grassroots campaign to address the issues of paper leaks and 'chanda chori'. This campaign is expected to mobilize the party's support base and gather public support for their cause.
