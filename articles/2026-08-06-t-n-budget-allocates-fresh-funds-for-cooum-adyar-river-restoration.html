T.N. Budget allocates fresh funds for Cooum, Adyar river restoration

📅 2026-08-06 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • Fresh allocation announced for river restoration projects
  • Focus on sewage treatment and desilting
  • Long-term plan to rejuvenate urban waterways

Budget boost for river rejuvenation

The Tamil Nadu government has announced a fresh push for the restoration of the Cooum and Adyar rivers in its latest budget, signalling a renewed commitment to cleaning the city's most polluted waterways. The allocation, revealed on Wednesday, aims to accelerate ongoing projects and introduce new measures to tackle sewage inflow and silt accumulation.

Officials say the funds will be directed towards completing pending desilting works, upgrading sewage treatment plants, and expanding the network of interconnected drains. The budget also proposes tighter monitoring of industrial effluents that have long choked the rivers.

Decades-old problem, new approach

Both rivers have been on the government's radar for years, with multiple restoration attempts yielding limited results. The new plan, however, integrates urban planning with environmental engineering, addressing not just the water quality but also the encroachments and solid waste that line the banks.

Chennai's rapid urbanisation has placed immense pressure on its waterways. The Cooum, in particular, has become synonymous with pollution, its dark, frothy waters a stark contrast to the city's skyline. The Adyar, though slightly better, still carries heavy loads of untreated sewage and plastic waste.

A multi-pronged strategy

The budget document outlines a multi-pronged strategy that includes bioremediation, installation of trash booms, and the creation of green buffer zones along the riverbanks. These measures are expected to complement the Chennai Rivers Restoration Trust's ongoing work, which has already seen some stretches show signs of recovery.

Residents living near the rivers have expressed cautious optimism, though many remain sceptical given past failures. "We have heard promises before," said a local activist, "but if the funds are actually released and work is monitored, there is hope."

Funding and implementation

The exact amount earmarked for the rivers has not been officially disclosed, but sources indicate it is a substantial increase over previous allocations. The government has assured that the funds will be released in phases, tied to specific milestones to ensure accountability.

Implementation will be overseen by a dedicated task force, which will coordinate between the public works department, Chennai Corporation, and the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board. Regular progress reports are expected to be made public, a move aimed at increasing transparency.

The budget also proposes public participation initiatives, including river-cleaning drives and citizen monitoring groups, to foster a sense of ownership among Chennaites.

What lies ahead

The success of this initiative will depend on the government's ability to execute on the ground and maintain political will. With the next assembly session scheduled for later this year, the opposition is likely to scrutinise the implementation closely, demanding timelines and measurable outcomes.

For now, the announcement marks a significant step forward, but the real test will be in the months ahead as work begins on the ground. The rivers, long neglected, may finally get the attention they deserve — if the promises translate into action.
