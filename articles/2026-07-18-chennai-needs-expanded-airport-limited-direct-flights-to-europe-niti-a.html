Chennai needs expanded airport, limited direct flights to Europe: NITI Aayog

📅 2026-07-18 📂 Chennai Original source ↗
Key points
  • NITI Aayog report stresses need for Chennai airport expansion to handle growing passenger traffic.
  • Chennai has limited direct flights to Europe, a disadvantage compared to other Indian metros.
  • Report recommends infrastructure upgrades to improve global connectivity and economic growth.

A new report from NITI Aayog has flagged Chennai's airport capacity constraints and its limited direct flight connectivity to Europe as key hurdles for the city's global ambitions. The policy think tank's analysis comes amid growing pressure on the city's aviation infrastructure.

Chennai International Airport, one of India's busiest, is struggling to keep pace with rising passenger numbers. The report notes that the airport needs urgent expansion to avoid congestion and flight delays.

Without significant upgrades, the city could lose out on business and tourism opportunities, especially as other metros ramp up their aviation capabilities.

Limited European links a concern

A major finding of the NITI Aayog report is Chennai's relatively sparse direct flight network to Europe. Unlike Delhi, Mumbai, or Bengaluru, Chennai has far fewer non-stop connections to major European hubs.

This limits options for business travelers and tourists alike. The report suggests that more direct routes to cities like London, Paris, and Frankfurt are needed to boost trade and investment flows.

The lack of direct flights also affects Chennai's appeal as a destination for European companies looking to set up operations in India. Officials have pointed out that improving connectivity could give the city a competitive edge in attracting foreign direct investment.

Infrastructure must catch up

The report calls for immediate steps to modernise Chennai International Airport. This includes expanding terminal capacity, adding more runways, and upgrading navigation systems to handle larger aircraft.

Currently, the airport handles over 20 million passengers annually, a number expected to grow significantly in the coming years. Without expansion, the facility could face severe overcrowding.

NITI Aayog has recommended a phased development plan. The first phase should focus on terminal expansion, while the second phase could involve building a new runway. The think tank has also stressed the need for better road and metro connectivity to the airport.

Economic implications

Chennai is a major hub for manufacturing, IT, and automotive industries. The report argues that better aviation infrastructure is directly linked to economic growth.

Every new direct flight route can open up trade corridors and make it easier for companies to do business. The city's port already handles a large volume of cargo, but air connectivity remains a weak link.

Industry bodies have echoed the NITI Aayog's concerns. They say that Chennai risks losing its competitive edge if airport expansion is delayed. The Tamil Nadu government has been pushing for faster approvals from the central government and the Airports Authority of India.

What happens next will depend on how quickly the state and central governments act on these recommendations. The report has put the spotlight firmly on Chennai's aviation gaps, and stakeholders will be watching for concrete steps in the coming months.
