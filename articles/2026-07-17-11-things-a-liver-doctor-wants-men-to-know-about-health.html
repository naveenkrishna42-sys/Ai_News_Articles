11 Things a Liver Doctor Wants Men to Know About Health

📅 2026-07-17 📂 Health Original source ↗
Key points
  • Fatty liver disease is common and often reversible with lifestyle changes.
  • Alcohol limits differ by gender; men should not exceed two drinks per day.
  • Many over-the-counter supplements can harm the liver.

Why Your Liver Deserves More Attention

For many men, the liver is an afterthought — until something goes wrong. But according to a leading liver specialist, waiting for symptoms is a mistake. The organ rarely complains until damage is significant.

The doctor, speaking to Esquire India, outlined 11 key insights every man should know. The message is straightforward: prevention and early action matter more than most realise.

Fatty Liver Is Not a Minor Issue

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) now affects a large number of men in India. It is closely linked to sedentary lifestyles and high-calorie diets. The condition often shows no symptoms in its early stages.

But the doctor warns that fatty liver can progress to inflammation, scarring, and even cirrhosis. The good news: it is reversible with weight loss, regular exercise, and a balanced diet.

Alcohol Limits Are Clear

Men often underestimate how much alcohol their liver can handle. The specialist recommends no more than two standard drinks per day. Binge drinking — even occasionally — puts the liver under severe stress.

For men with existing liver conditions, the advice is simple: zero alcohol. There is no safe level once the liver is compromised.

Supplements Can Do More Harm Than Good

Many men take vitamins, herbal remedies, and protein powders without a second thought. But the liver must process everything you ingest. Some supplements, especially those sold online without regulation, can cause drug-induced liver injury.

The doctor advises caution: avoid untested products, and always consult a physician before starting any new supplement.

Watch for These Signs

Early liver disease often has no symptoms. But certain signals should not be ignored. Unexplained fatigue, yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), dark urine, and swelling in the abdomen or legs all warrant a medical check-up.

Routine blood tests can catch problems early. The specialist recommends that men over 40 get liver function tests annually, especially if they have risk factors like diabetes or obesity.

Vaccinations and Medications Matter

Hepatitis B and C are major causes of liver disease in India. Vaccination for hepatitis B is safe and effective. Hepatitis C has no vaccine, but is curable with modern antiviral therapy.

Painkillers like paracetamol are another hidden risk. Never exceed the recommended dose, and avoid mixing paracetamol with alcohol.

What Comes Next

Liver health is largely in your hands. Small daily choices — what you eat, how much you move, what you drink — add up over time. The doctor's final word: get tested, stay informed, and don't wait for a warning that may come too late.
