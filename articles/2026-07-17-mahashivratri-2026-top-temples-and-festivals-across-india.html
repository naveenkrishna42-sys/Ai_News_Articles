Mahashivratri 2026: Top Temples and Festivals Across India

  • Kashi Vishwanath Temple in Varanasi, Somnath in Gujarat, and Mahakaleshwar in Ujjain are top destinations for Mahashivratri 2026.
  • The festival falls in late February or early March 2026, with night-long vigils and special abhishekams.
  • Regional celebrations include the Khajuraho Dance Festival and the fair at Mandi, Himachal Pradesh.

Observing Mahashivratri 2026

Mahashivratri, one of the most significant Hindu festivals dedicated to Lord Shiva, will be observed across India in 2026. Devotees mark the occasion with day-long fasts, night-long vigils (jaagran), and special rituals like abhishekam (ritual bathing of the Shiva lingam). The festival symbolises the marriage of Shiva and Parvati and is considered an auspicious time for spiritual reflection.

While the exact date depends on the lunar calendar, Mahashivratri typically falls in late February or early March. In 2026, preparations are already underway at major temples and pilgrimage sites. Here are some of the best places to experience the festival.

Kashi Vishwanath Temple, Varanasi

Varanasi’s Kashi Vishwanath Temple is among the most sacred Shiva shrines. On Mahashivratri, the temple sees an overwhelming crowd of devotees who queue for hours to offer milk, bilva leaves, and water to the Jyotirlinga. The narrow lanes of the old city are alive with chants of 'Har Har Mahadev'.

The temple management organises a special abhishekam throughout the night. Devotees believe a visit here on this night frees one from the cycle of rebirth. Security and crowd management are typically tightened for the festival.

Somnath Temple, Gujarat

The Somnath Temple in Prabhas Patan, Gujarat, is another prime destination. Known as the first of the twelve Jyotirlingas, it holds immense significance. On Mahashivratri, the temple hosts a grand aarti and a continuous recitation of Shiva mantras.

The nearby beach and the Somnath Trust set up temporary stalls for pilgrims. The state government usually arranges special trains and buses to manage the influx of visitors. The calm setting of the Arabian Sea adds to the spiritual atmosphere.

Mahakaleshwar Temple, Ujjain

In Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh, the Mahakaleshwar Temple is famous for its Bhasma Aarti, performed in the early morning. On Mahashivratri, this ritual attracts thousands. The temple remains open all night, and the main sanctum is decorated with flowers and lights.

Devotees from across central India throng the city. The local administration sets up temporary shelters and free food counters. The energy in Ujjain during this period is palpable, with processions and bhajan sessions continuing past midnight.

Regional Celebrations: Khajuraho and Mandi

Beyond the major Jyotirlingas, Mahashivratri is celebrated with unique local flavours. In Khajuraho, Madhya Pradesh, the annual Khajuraho Dance Festival coincides with the holy week. Classical dance performances are held against the backdrop of the UNESCO-listed temples, blending art and devotion.

In Mandi, Himachal Pradesh, a grand fair transforms the town. Known as the 'Mini Kumbh of the Himalayas,' it features a procession of over 200 deities carried in palanquins to the banks of the Beas River. The fair lasts several days and draws tourists along with pilgrims.

Other notable places include the Brihadeeswarar Temple in Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, and the Tungnath Temple in Uttarakhand. Each offers a distinct regional flavour, from temple architecture to local food offerings.

What to Watch

As the festival approaches, temple trusts and state tourism departments will release detailed schedules for darshan and special events. Devotees planning to visit should book accommodations well in advance. Live telecasts of major aartis are expected on religious channels, offering an alternative for those unable to travel.
