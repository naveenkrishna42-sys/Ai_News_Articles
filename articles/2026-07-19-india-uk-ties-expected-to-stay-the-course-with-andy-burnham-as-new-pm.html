India-UK ties expected to stay the course with Andy Burnham as new PM

📅 2026-07-19 📂 India Original source ↗
Key points
  • Andy Burnham assumes office as UK Prime Minister, with analysts predicting stable bilateral relations with India.
  • The India-UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement (CETA) is expected to provide a framework for enhanced trade.
  • Industry representatives express concerns over steel quotas in the CETA deal potentially constraining Indian exports.
  • The textile sector anticipates new opportunities as free trade agreements begin to take effect.

Andy Burnham has taken over as the new Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, and early signals from New Delhi and London point to a continuation of the steady arc in bilateral relations. The change in leadership comes at a time when the India-UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement, or CETA, is already reshaping the commercial landscape. Analysts say the broad contours of the partnership are unlikely to shift dramatically under Burnham.

Trade deal provides a solid foundation

The CETA agreement, which took years of negotiations, is now the central pillar of the economic relationship. It offers India preferential access to UK markets in several sectors while opening up British services to Indian firms. The deal has been described by both governments as a modern, ambitious pact that goes beyond tariffs.

For New Delhi, the agreement is a test of its competitive confidence. Indian industry has had to brace for greater competition in areas where UK firms have historically held an advantage. Yet, the government has maintained that the long-term gains from market access and technology transfer outweigh the short-term discomfort.

Textile sector poised for growth

The textile industry, one of India's traditional strengths, is expected to be a major beneficiary of the new trade architecture. With tariffs on Indian garments and fabrics set to drop significantly under CETA, exporters are gearing up to increase their footprint in the UK market. The Times of India has reported that the sector is now looking to "sew loose ends" as the free trade agreements kick in.

Industry bodies have welcomed the move, noting that the UK is a premium market for Indian textiles. However, they also caution that meeting quality and sustainability standards will be critical. The government has assured manufacturers of support in upgrading technology and compliance processes.

Steel quotas raise concerns

Not all sectors are celebrating. The steel industry has flagged potential trouble spots in the CETA deal. According to a report in Swarajya, industry representatives have warned that steel quotas built into the agreement may constrain Indian exports in key product categories. The quotas, they argue, are too restrictive and could limit the volume of Indian steel entering the UK market even as demand rises.

"The quotas are a bottleneck," one industry official said. "They don't reflect the true potential of Indian steel mills." The government is expected to take up the issue in the first round of review talks under the agreement. Negotiators on both sides have indicated a willingness to revisit the numbers if trade flows justify it.

Political continuity and strategic alignment

Burnham's ascent to 10 Downing Street had been anticipated for months. Known for his pragmatic approach, the former mayor of Greater Manchester has long expressed interest in deepening ties with India. His government is expected to maintain the strategic momentum built by his predecessor, particularly in defence, technology, and climate cooperation.

Diplomats in New Delhi say the personal rapport between Burnham and Indian leaders has been positive so far. The two sides are also likely to push forward with negotiations on a separate investment treaty and a migration and mobility partnership. These agreements, if concluded, could further strengthen the bilateral framework.

What lies ahead is a period of implementation. The CETA deal must now deliver on its promises, and both governments will be under pressure to show tangible results. For Indian exporters, the next few months will be crucial as they navigate the new rules of engagement with the UK market.
