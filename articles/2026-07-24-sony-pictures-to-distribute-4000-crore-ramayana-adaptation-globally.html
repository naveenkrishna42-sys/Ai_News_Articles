Sony Pictures to Distribute ₹4000 Crore 'Ramayana' Adaptation Globally

📅 2026-07-24 📂 Entertainment Original source ↗
Key points
  • Sony Pictures has secured international distribution rights for the upcoming 'Ramayana' adaptation.
  • The film's budget is reported to be around $500 million (approximately ₹4000 crore), making it one of the most expensive Indian productions.
  • The trailer launch has been postponed, with producer Namit Malhotra stating it will be released globally at a later date.

Mumbai: Sony Pictures has acquired the international distribution rights for the upcoming big-budget adaptation of the Indian epic 'Ramayana'. The film, which has been in production for several years, is one of the most ambitious projects in Indian cinema history.

A Mega-Budget Spectacle

Reports from multiple trade publications, including Variety and The Hollywood Reporter, confirm the deal. The film carries a production budget of approximately $500 million, translating to over ₹4000 crore. This makes it one of the most expensive films ever produced globally.

The project is being led by producer Namit Malhotra, known for his VFX company Prime Focus. The adaptation promises extensive visual effects work, aiming to bring the ancient saga to life on a scale never before seen in Indian cinema.

Star Cast and Trailer Delay

Ranbir Kapoor is set to play the lead role of Lord Ram in the film. The casting has generated significant buzz, though other key roles have not been officially confirmed in recent statements.

The trailer for the film was expected to launch recently. However, its release was postponed. Producer Namit Malhotra addressed the delay, stating, 'We will be launching our trailer globally at a later date.' Speculation in some media reports linked the postponement to student protests, but officials have not confirmed any connection.

Sony's International Push

The deal with Sony Pictures marks a significant step for the film. It ensures that 'Ramayana' will receive a wide international theatrical release, handled by one of Hollywood's major studios. This is a departure from the typical distribution model for large Indian films, which often rely on domestic studios for local release and smaller international partners for overseas markets.

Industry analysts view this as a signal that the producers are aiming for a global audience, not just the Indian diaspora. The budget and the scale of the VFX work suggest an attempt to compete with big-budget Hollywood epics.

The acquisition by Sony also indicates confidence in the project's commercial potential. The studio has a history of distributing large-scale spectacles and will likely leverage its global network to maximize the film's reach.

What to watch: The announcement of a new trailer date. With the global launch now pending, all eyes are on the producers and Sony Pictures to reveal the next marketing milestone for what could be a landmark film in Indian cinema's global ambitions.
